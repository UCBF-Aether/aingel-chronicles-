Baruch

Baruch was Jeremiah's scribe and companion — the man who wrote down the prophet's words, even when kings burned the scroll. This book, in six chapters, bears his name. It opens with the exiles in Babylon confessing their sins and praying for life under a foreign king, moves into a beautiful hymn praising Wisdom — who appeared on earth and lived among people — and closes, in chapter 6, with the Letter of Jeremiah, a sharp warning against the idols of Babylon. The text here is the King James Version of the Apocrypha, printed as received. Baruch stands in the Ethiopian canon this edition follows, as in the Catholic and Orthodox canons, and it belongs to the Jeremiah circle: in the Ethiopian ordering, the writings around Jeremiah — his laments, his scribe's book, and 4 Baruch — stay together as one family's testimony from the exile. Watch for chapter 3's hymn to Wisdom, and for the dry humour of the Letter, which points out that a god that must be dusted cannot be much of a god.
