Zechariah

Zechariah is one of the longest of the Twelve Prophets, with 14 chapters, and the most vision-filled. Its first half is a series of night visions — horsemen, horns, a man with a measuring line, a high priest in clean garments, a flying scroll — all encouraging a returned people as they rebuild Jerusalem and the Temple. Its second half turns more searching and more hopeful, speaking of a coming humble king riding on a donkey, a pierced one who is mourned, and a day when the Lord will be king over all the earth.

In this edition Zechariah is printed from the King James Version of 1611, the public-domain text as received. It stands in this canon as one of the Twelve, and as Haggai's close companion in the rebuilding story. The Ethiopian canon keeps it in that prophetic company, where its images have fed Christian hope for centuries.

Watch for the donkey-riding king in chapter 9, and for how many of these visions the Gospels later echo.
