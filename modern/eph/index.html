THE EPISTLE TO THE EPHESIANS

Ephesians

Ephesians is a letter the text addresses to the saints at Ephesus, naming Paul as its sender. Less focused on one local problem than many of his letters, it opens with a sweeping hymn of praise: chosen, redeemed, and sealed in Christ, believers from Israel and the nations are made one new people. The second half turns practical, urging unity, honest work, pure speech, loving families, and spiritual armor for a real unseen battle. In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, printed as received from the public domain. The Ethiopian Church keeps it in this canon among Paul's letters, where it serves as a kind of summary of the gospel's cosmic reach and daily walk. First-time readers often find it the most majestic of the shorter letters. Watch for the household instructions and the full armor of God in the final chapter.
