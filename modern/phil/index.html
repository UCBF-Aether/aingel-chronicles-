THE EPISTLE TO THE PHILIPPIANS

Philippians

Philippians is Paul's warm thank-you letter to the church at Philippi, the first congregation he founded in Europe according to Acts. The letter names Paul as sender, writing from imprisonment, yet joy runs through every page. He thanks them for their partnership and gifts, holds up Christ's humility in a famous hymn-like passage, warns against confidence in the flesh, and urges two quarreling women to agree. His own goal is simple: to know Christ, press forward, and rejoice whatever comes. In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, printed as received from the public domain. It belongs in this canon among Paul's letters, treasured by the Ethiopian Church with the rest of the New Testament writings. Short enough to read in one sitting, it repays a lifetime of rereading. Watch for the hymn to Christ's self-emptying and the peace that passes understanding.
