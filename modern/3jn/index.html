THE THIRD GENERAL EPISTLE OF JOHN

3 John

Third John is also a single chapter, a personal note from the elder to his friend Gaius. John praises Gaius for walking in the truth and for welcoming traveling brothers, even strangers, and supporting them on their way. Against that kindness stands Diotrephes, who loved to be first, refused the brothers, and stopped others who wanted to help. The letter is a small lesson in church character: imitate what is good, not what is evil, because the one who does good is from God.

In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, received as public-domain text. It stands in this canon because the Ethiopian Church keeps the full set of John's letters, and this one shows apostolic teaching at the level of one congregation and one friendship. Nothing here is grand theory; it is faithfulness in welcome, money, reputation, and daily conduct — the places where love becomes visible.

Watch for Gaius himself — a quiet example of hospitality that John holds up for every reader to copy.
