4 Baruch

Chapter 7

1 And Baruch arose and went forth from the tomb, and found the eagle sitting without.

2 And the eagle said to him: "Hail, Baruch, steward of the faith. And Baruch said to him: For you are chosen above all the birds of heaven, that you speak; by the brightness of your eyes it is manifest; and now show me what you do here."

3 And the eagle said to him: "I am sent here, that you may send by me every word which you will."

4 And Baruch said to him: "Can you indeed bring this word to Jeremiah in Babylon? And the eagle said to him: For this was I sent."

5 And Baruch took the book, and fifteen figs from the basket of figs which Abimelech brought, and bound them upon the neck of the eagle.

6 And he said: "To you I speak, O eagle, king of all birds: go in peace and in safety; carry the message for me; and be not like the raven which Noah sent forth, and it returned not again to him; but be like the dove which the third time brought word to Noah. So do you also take these good words to Jeremiah, and to them that are with him, the Israelites, that it may be well with you; and take this gladness to the people, the chosen of God."

7 And if all the birds surround you, and all the enemies of righteousness, desiring to kill you, be strong; and the Lord shall give you strength. And turn not aside to the right nor to the left, but as a flying arrow go straight; go in the strength of God.

8 And when Baruch had said this, the eagle flew with the book, and went into Babylon; and rested upon a post without the city, in a desert place; and abode there until Jeremiah passed by, and some of the people.

9 And they passed by there to bury a man that was dead; for Jeremiah had asked Nebuchadnezzar, saying: Give me a place where I may bury [my dead] of my people; and he gave it him.

10 And as they went weeping for him that was dead, they came before that eagle; and the eagle cried with a loud voice, saying: "To you I speak, Jeremiah, chosen of God: go and gather all the people, and bring them here, that they may hear the good message which I have brought."

11 And when he heard this, he glorified God; and then he gathered all the people, and their wives, and their children, and they came where the eagle was.

12 And that eagle came down upon the corpse, and it revived and lived; and this he did that they might believe. And all the people marvelled because of that which was done.

13 And they said: "Of a truth this is God, who appeared to our fathers in the wilderness by Moses; and in the likeness of an eagle he appeared to us, as it were a great eagle."

14 And the eagle said to Jeremiah, saying: "Come, loose this book, and read it to the people; and he read it to them."

15 And when the people heard it, they wept all together, and cast ashes upon their heads; and they said to Jeremiah: "Save us; what shall we do, that we may enter into our city?"

16 And Jeremiah arose and said to them: "All that you have heard in the book, so do; and he shall bring you into your city."

17 And Jeremiah wrote a book to Baruch, saying thus: My beloved son, be not weary in prayer, beseeching God for us; that he may lighten our way, until we come out from under the command of this sinful king.

18 And you are found righteous before God, who suffered you not to come with us, that you should not see the evil that is done to them, to the people in Babylon.

19 As a father who has an only son, and he is delivered up to be punished; and they that are with his father, even they that comfort him, cover their faces, that they may not see the father, who is consumed with grief.

20 Even so God had pity on you, and suffered you not to come to Babylon, that you should not see the affliction of the people.

21 For since we came into this land, we have had no rest from grief until this day, sixty and six years this day; when we go forth we find of the people some hanged by Nebuchadnezzar the king, weeping and saying: Have mercy upon us, O god of Sor.

22 And when I heard this word, I mourned and wept, because the hanged called upon a strange god, saying: "Have mercy upon us. And again I remembered the feasts which we kept in Jerusalem, before we were taken captive; and when I remembered, I entered into my house, crying and weeping."

23 And now make supplication to our God, where you are, you and Abimelech, for the people, that they may listen to my voice and to the word of my mouth, that they may go out from Persia.

24 And now I tell you: all the time that we have abode here, they have held us, saying: Sing us new songs from the hymns of Zion, the song of your God. And we say to them: How shall we sing to you, while we are in a strange land?

25 And when Jeremiah had thus written, he bound his book upon the neck of the eagle, and said to him: "Go in peace, and may the Lord keep you."

26 And the eagle went and flew, and brought the book to Baruch; and Baruch took it and read the book, and wept when he heard of the afflictions of the people and their tribulations.

27 And Jeremiah took those figs, and gave them to the sick that were among the people; and abode teaching them, that they should not do the deeds of the people of Babylon.
