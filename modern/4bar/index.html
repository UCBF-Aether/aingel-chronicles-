4 Baruch

4 Baruch, also called the Paralipomena of Jeremiah — the things left over from Jeremiah's story — is a nine-chapter tale from the fall of Jerusalem. When the city falls, Jeremiah and his scribe Baruch hide the temple vessels in the earth, an eagle carries a letter across the exile, and Abimelech, the boy sent to gather figs, sleeps sixty-six years beneath a tree — the whole exile in a single sleep — while Jeremiah himself waits in Babylon. It is part history, part legend, and wholly moving — a story about what survives when everything sacred seems lost. For this edition it was translated anew, directly from the Ge'ez; the Ge'ez prose has no verse divisions, so the verses here are editorial, following the turns of the story and its speeches. It stands in the Ethiopian canon as part of the Jeremiah circle, ordered with Jeremiah, Lamentations, and Baruch, where the Ethiopian church has kept it while most other traditions let it slip away. Watch for the sleeping boy, the figs that never faded, and the eagle's flight — this little book trusts that God keeps what his people cannot.
