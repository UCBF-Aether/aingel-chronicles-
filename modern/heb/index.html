THE EPISTLE TO THE HEBREWS

Hebrews

Hebrews is a stirring word of exhortation to Jewish believers tempted to drift back under pressure. Unusually, the letter never names its writer; tradition has often linked it to Paul's circle, but the text itself leaves the author unnamed, and its elegant style stands apart from the letters. Its argument is exalted and pastoral at once: Christ is greater than angels, greater than Moses, a high priest greater than Aaron, offering a better covenant and a once-for-all sacrifice. Famous faith heroes fill one whole chapter. In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, printed as received from the public domain. The Ethiopian Church keeps it in this canon after Paul's letters, where it bridges the old covenant world of temple and sacrifice to its fulfillment. Watch for the hall of faith and the closing picture of Mount Zion and the unshakable kingdom.
