Zephaniah

Zephaniah opens with one of the starkest phrases in the prophets: the day of the Lord. In 3 short chapters it sweeps from judgment on Judah's idolatry and pride, to warnings for the surrounding nations, to a tender ending — God rejoicing over his restored people with singing. The middle of the book calls the humble to seek righteousness and humility before that day comes, holding out a quiet hope of being sheltered.

In this edition Zephaniah is printed from the King James Version of 1611, the public-domain text as received. It is here as one of the Twelve Prophets, a collection the Ethiopian canon preserves complete. Its place in that sequence matters: after books that stress God's justice, Zephaniah lets the last note be joy, not ruin. The prophet's name is traditionally linked to a family line reaching back to King Hezekiah, which the opening verse itself traces.

Watch for how the book turns — the God who judges in chapter 1 is the God who sings over his people in chapter 3.
