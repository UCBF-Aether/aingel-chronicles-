THE SECOND EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS

2 Corinthians

Second Corinthians is Paul's most personal and emotional letter to that same Corinthian church. The text names Paul as sender, writing after a painful conflict, a sorrowful visit, and a severe letter that left both sides hurting. Here he defends his ministry, rejoices that many repented, urges generous giving for hungry believers in Jerusalem, and warns against impressive teachers who boast while tearing him down. Weakness becomes his surprising theme: God's power, he says, is made perfect in weakness. In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, printed as received from the public domain. It stands in this canon among Paul's letters, preserved by the Ethiopian Church with the rest of the apostolic writings. If you have ever served God while misunderstood, tired, or opposed, this letter will feel close to home. Watch for Paul's list of hardships and his account of a man caught up into paradise.
