Lamentations

Lamentations is five chapters of grief poetry written over the ruins of Jerusalem after Babylon destroyed the city and its temple. Nearly every poem is an acrostic, built on the letters of the Hebrew alphabet — grief given a strict form, as if the poet were working through sorrow letter by letter, A to Z. The speaker is the city herself, pictured as a widow sitting alone, and tradition credits Jeremiah, the weeping prophet, as the author. The text here is the King James Version, printed as received. In the Ethiopian canon this edition follows, Lamentations belongs to the Jeremiah circle, ordered with the writings that gather around that prophet's story. It belongs in the canon because scripture makes room for lament — not every prayer in the Bible is praise, and this book teaches God's people how to bring God their worst days honestly. Watch for the turn in the middle of chapter 3 — great is thy faithfulness — a shaft of hope rising out of the darkest book in the Bible.
