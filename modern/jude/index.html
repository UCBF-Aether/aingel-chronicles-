THE GENERAL EPISTLE OF JUDE

Jude

Jude is a single, intense chapter from Jude, the brother of James, urging believers to contend for the faith once delivered to the saints. False teachers had slipped in unnoticed, and Jude answers with a chain of examples from Israel's story, the angels, and Sodom, warning that grace is never a license to live carelessly. Most striking for readers of this edition, Jude quotes directly from 1 Enoch and speaks of Enoch's prophecy of judgment — a living thread between this tiny letter and the Enoch you can read in full earlier in this canon.

In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, received as public-domain text. Jude belongs here because the Ethiopian canon keeps it among the New Testament letters, and because its use of Enoch helps explain why Ethiopia treasured that book when others let it fall away. The letter ends not in fear but in praise to God who can keep you from falling.

Watch for the Enoch quotation in verses fourteen and fifteen, and for the mercy Jude still shows toward those who doubt.
