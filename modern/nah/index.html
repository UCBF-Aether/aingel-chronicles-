Nahum

Nahum is a fierce little book of 3 chapters, a poem of judgment against Nineveh, the Assyrian capital. Where Jonah showed Nineveh repenting and being spared, Nahum, speaking generations later in the story, announces that this violent empire will fall and not rise again. The language is vivid and unsparing: chariots flashing in the streets, a city that sold nations, a wound with no healing. For Judah, long pressed under Assyrian power, these words were comfort — the oppressor is not forever.

In this edition Nahum is printed from the King James Version of 1611, the public-domain text as received. It stands in this canon as one of the Twelve Prophets, kept together in the Ethiopian Bible just as in the common canon. Reading it beside Jonah matters. Together the two books hold both sides of the witness: mercy offered to enemies, and a firm end to cruelty that will not turn.

Watch for the opening portrait of God — slow to anger, great in power, and unwilling to let violence have the last word.
