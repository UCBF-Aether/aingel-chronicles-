THE BOOK OF JOB

Job

Job asks the hardest question in the Bible: why do good people suffer? Job is introduced as blameless and upright, yet he loses his wealth, his children, and his health in quick succession. For most of its 42 chapters, Job and his friends argue in magnificent poetry about whether suffering must be punishment, while Job insists on his honesty and longs to plead his case before God. At last God answers, not with explanations, but with a tour of the wild, ordered world. In this edition Job is printed from the King James Version of 1611, as received. It belongs in every canon, and here it stands among the wisdom books, giving suffering people words when easy answers fail. Watch for the shift from the friends' tidy speeches to God's questions, and for Job's quiet restoration at the end.
