Didascalia

Chapter 10

THAT THE BISHOP OUGHT TO EXAMINE AND TRY EVERY MATTER IN RIGHTEOUSNESS AND UPRIGHTNESS,

1 Be then pure as the first fathers, good and wise! And prudent; and put away from you (every) evil habit and wicked custom. Choose rather for yourselves the way of life. The bishop ought to walk in this way, and do as it befits, and turn aside from evildoing, and do good works, and heal the sick who desire to repent, and cast forth such as refuse to turn.

2 For there are many who seek pretexts, and lay snares and accusations through envy, as did the two elders against Susanna in Babylon, and as did the Egyptian woman against Joseph. But do not you, O man of God, as these do; but be good and merciful, and receive them that repent. And favor not the sinner, neither kill the just.

3 But whoever receives. Not the repentant, and brings not the sinner back is the father of anger, and in him is no peace. For in the place where anger is, there the Lord dwells not; but anger is the friend of Satan, who goes to and fro amongst false brothers—and in him is no peace—and causes trouble in the Church.

4 Understand, then, and follow not the way of fools who rejoice in evil, neither hearken to their voice, but beware of them, for there is no fear of God before their eyes, but rather revenge

5 And envy. For evil men accuse the good who are without sin, and raise against them an evil report, and bring them down into great trouble and affliction.

6 But you, O bishop, understand, and examine those who come to you to tell their cause, and judge between them with wisdom, discerning how and what the matter is. And if a man be found to have transgressed, admonish him yourself alone,!! As _ the Lord has commanded; take him with you alone, and speak to him, that he may turn to repentance.

7 And if he reject you,! Take with you one or two, and tell him of his sloth and folly, and teach him in meekness and wisdom; and it (that is, wisdom) dwells in the heart of the good, and rests in the mind of teachers.

8 And if he hearken to the word of you three, he has saved his soul and gained himself!; but if he refuse to hearken, tell it to the Church, and if he refuse (to hear) the Church, he shall be to you as a heathen and a tax collector.

9 Receive him not, and permit him not to enter the Church, nor reckon him with the Christians.” But those that repent of their sins, and turn to our Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God—let us receive their repentance.

10 I.,.] O bishop, help the sinners, and strengthen them by the word of the Lord, and despise them not, and refuse not to eat with them. For our Lord refused not to eat” with tax collectors and sinners and when the Pharisees said to His disciples, ‘‘ Therefore eats your master with sinners and tax collectors he answered them, The sick have need of the physician, and not the whole.

11 I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.’’ Therefore it is right that you visit them and comfort them, and eat with them in love, and be careful for their salvation, and help them, and say to them, Be strong, clap your hands and dance (with) your feet.

12 You ought then to console the sorrowful of heart, and bear patiently with the angry, lest they turn again to folly, for the passionate man is ever a fool.

13 But if any man turn, and show forth the fruits of repentance, immediately receive him, as the prodigal son who scattered all his father’s substance,

14 The sinner? Ought you not to rejoice with him, and to say to him, Because this your brother was dead, and lived again; and was lost, and is found? For God is merciful, and forgives the sin of him that has repented and done (that which is) good. Hear what David did when he sinned in the matter of the wife of Uriah.

15 After his sin he repented, and said, ‘Turn your face from my sin, and blot out all my transgression. Create for me a clean heart, O Lord, and renew a right spirit within me. And cast me not away from before your face, and take not your Holy Spirit from me.

16 Restore to me joy and your salvation and strengthen me with an excellent‘! Spirit.’’, And you also, O bishop, heal them as a wise physician, and as a faithful friend, who cares and seeks for the salvation of his neighbor. In like manner do you help and save, and heal from the wounds of sin those that turn to God, that they may be saved from condemnation.

17 If their disease be great, give to them the remedy which blots out uncleanness; and anoint them with the oil of life, which destroys the poison of the disease, that is, fasting and prayer; and bind up by prayer and supplication the defilement of the limbs of those that are broken.

18 And if, when you do all this, you are not able to heal them, do not be against them as a saw which cuts asunder, or as an axe which breaks in pieces, but rather as a wise physician who cuts with a lancet, and removes the foulness of the disease that is hidden within: and then shall the whole limb become sound.

19 But if you see a deceitful‘ man who refuses to turn to repentance, but lives in folly and error, cut him off from the Church. For it is said, “Remove all evil from you.” “ It is right that you admonish him, but it is right that you reckon him not an‘ enemy, for he is a member of yours. And

20 Respect not persons in judgment, and favor neither the rich nor the poor.

21 And you, O shepherds of the sheep and deacons, ought not to hearken to words of falsehood and accusation against an innocent man in whom is no sin, that you should put him forth from the Church, seeking occasions to receive bribes, and to accept persons; for those that do thus are the friends of the devil.

22 Know, then, that you shall be requited concerning this before the judgment-seat of the Lord, for it is written, saying, ‘You shall not kill the righteous with the sinner, nor take a bribe to destroy life: for a bribe blinds the ‘eyes of the wise, and perverts the word of righteousness.’’ Honor not a transgressor because of a bribe, and change not righteousness for falsehood, but take heed to yourselves,” and do not any (such) thing.

23 And judge not unrighteously, and do not be partakers with evil-doers. For it is said by Isaiah the prophet, ‘“ Woe to them that call good evil, and evil good; that call light darkness, and darkness light; that call sweet bitter, and bitter sweet. Woe to them that are wise to evil, and say, We are as the wise.’’ But if you pervert judgment by iniquity, know that you shall bring on yourselves grievous condemnation.

24 For our Lord says, “Even as you have judged, you shall be judged, and with the measure with which you

25 If you have understanding, and enquire into matters of judgment, you shall discern the evil, and the false, and the slanderers who would fain kill their neighbors by wrongdoing.

26 These are they that cause trouble, and observe words of vanity, and make snares for themselves by the words of their lips. Those that do thus put far away,” lest they destroy the Church of God. But if they turn and repent, and forsake their wickedness, and

27 ’ do good, let them be received that they ma enter into the Church, for they have received the Body or Christ. If a man be born having in his members aught superfluous, will he not cut off that which is superfluous in his members, that it is not a shame to him?

28 In like manner, you shepherds of the sheep, drive out from the Church of God the ungodly and unbelieving; for it is a shame if there are with you those that believe not in the name of Jesus Christ. Cut them off entirely from the Church; you are truly members of Christ and sons of the Church.

29 And if there is found in your midst a superfluous member, who has thoughts of evil, and goes to and fro among you with murder, and slander, and blasphemy—he that does thus has accomplished all the devices of Satan, and has blasphemed against the Church. Let those that do thus be put forth from the Church, and be requited according to their works, and entirely cut off from the people of the Lord.

30 Behold then the Church of the Lord is beautified, for the evil member has gone forth from her, that dwelt within her, troubling her. These are destroyers, haters of good, and lovers of wealth and vain praise, who are wise to evil, and scatter the sheep of the flock of Jesus Christ.

31 Bishops and people ought to judge in righteousness; for our Lord says, “If you walk in a crooked way, I also will walk with you in a crooked way.’’ And moreover somewhere it is said, ‘“ With a righteous man you will be righteous; and with a pure man you will be pure; and with the elect you will be elect; and with the crooked you will be crooked.” Walk, then, in the way of purity, that you may find honor and favor with the Lord.

32 Be of one heart and love one another: and let there be peace among you.

33 This honor befits a Christian people. Let not the layman judge, nor the elders, but rather such as are set in authority in the Church. For Satan brings snares on the servants of God from the ungodly, and teaches them reproaches and blasphemies. Is there not one among us of understanding that is able to judge between us in uprightness?

34 Do not allow, then, the heathen to know the mystery of your hidden things, and accept not against yourselves the testimony of those that believe not, but keep that which has been given to you.

35 For it is said, “Give that which is the King’s to the King, and that which is God’s to God.” ” But if you have a debt to pay, delay not to give, saying, To my brother a believer I will pay, but to him that believes not, I will not pay; but do as our Lord when He paid tribute.

36 And you, O servants of God, keep the commandments of Christ; and if you see among the brothers a quarrel or dispute, it is right for you to give word concerning them to those that are set in authority. If they are men of

37 Evil habits, and causers of trouble, and haters of good, and such as walk not in the right way—them that are such admonish and put to shame; but when they repent, receive them. And if you act thus, you have made peace between brothers and saved them from sin.

38 You ought to forgive and pardon the trespasses of your neighbors, even as the Lord commanded Peter, when he said to Him, If my neighbor sin against me, how often shall I forgive him? Seven times? And Jesus said to him, I say not to you, seven times, but, seventy times seven.

39 Thus, then, the Lord wills that His disciples should do, and that they should never let revenge dwell in their hearts, nor envy,” nor anger, nor evil desire, but that they should make peace between brothers that they may live in unity. For our Lord says, “Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called the sons of God.’’

40 You ought not then on the Christian Sabbath” to cause contention, but rather (to make) peace. Let judgment be given on other days. And it is right, O bishops, that presbyters and deacons should sit with you in judgment. And judge not with partiality or accepting of persons, but judge in righteousness and uprightness as servants of God.

41 And if there is anyone that has a suit with his fellow, let them come both of them together, and stand in the court, even as it is written in the law. Judge you” then in righteousness and in uprightness. Take heed and quickly set them at one, and make peace between them, before the judgment of death come forth from the bishop on him that has sinned.

42 For with you in the court of judgment is Christ the Son of God. And if any man say, Such a one has blasphemed against the name of God, and walks not in the way of righteousness, let not (your) judgment be given hastily, before his fellow appear.

43 And when they have come both of them judge between them. Il or the judgment which proceeds from you leads to life, and on the other hand it goes down to eternal condemnation.

44 Tor our Lord says, ‘Follow righteousness and uprightness.’’ But he whose sin is manifestly known, and has been justly brought up to judgment, shall be far from life eternal, and shall not see the glory of God, but shall be put to shame before the saints.

45 But if you judge wrongfully, you shall find condemnation and shall be requited according to your works, even as God requited the two elders who spoke falsely against Susanna. Beware then of speaking falsely, for he that speaks falsely shall find great condemnation.

46 Do not be as the men who were transgressors of the law, who spoke falsely against Naboth in Samaria; and as the council of the Jews who spoke falsely against Stephen the first martyr. They that bear witness must necessarily be faithful and meek men, in whom is no anger, pure and good and faithful, worshippers of God, in whom is no evil.

47 Such men are worthy to bear witness, (being) pure, in whom is no blemish, and their testimony is sure. But receive not the testimony of those that live not thus.

48 And though the testimony of many false witnesses agree with one consent, hear not their words, for it is said, ‘‘Let me have no fellowship with their assembly of blood.” And moreover it is commanded in the law, ‘‘And hearken not to a false accusation, nor sit with him that is unjust, lest you become an unjust witness; nor join yourself with a multitude to do iniquity.’’

49 And you also in like manner, if you judge not in righteousness and uprightness, shall find condemnation, and the scourge of God shall come upon you. But if you judge in righteousness and in uprightness, you shall find a good reward and life eternal.

50 Take heed, then, beloved brothers, and do good at all times, that you may find honor before God with the holy fathers, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob in the Kingdom of heaven in life eternal; for that is right before God.

51 Judge, then,"” in righteousness and peace without anger, for he that provokes his neighbor to anger without a cause, his portion shall be in Gehenna.

52 For it is said, “Before the sun set, cool your anger, and give not place to Satan. [For let him that stole, steal no more.] ’ And again David says, ‘Be angry and sin not.” Love one another, and remove evil from your hearts, and be at peace amongst yourselves. Hear, then, what our Master and our Lord and our Savior Jesus Christ says in the

53 Gospel, ‘‘When you bring your gift to the altar, and there remember that your brother has with which to reproach you, leave there your offering before the altar, and go, first be reconciled with your brother, and then, returning, offer your gift." The sacrifice of God is a meek spirit and a humble heart, prayer and thanksgiving; but if you provoke your neighbor to anger, and grieve his heart, God will not receive your sacrifice and your prayer.

54 You, beloved brothers, ought at all times to pray and make supplication, and utter your supplication to God, that He may remove from you indignation and anger.

55 Praise the lord continually at every season, and make mention of His name (which is) Most high, that He may remove from you hatred, and anger, and revenge, that you may be able to pray with a pure heart in which is no defilement; for the Lord has commanded us to love our enemies, and much more our friends.

56 And again it is said, “‘ Hate not any man, but some of them admonish, and some of them teach.” Neither hate you your brother with your whole heart, nor hear an accusation against him, nor find” occasion of stumbling because of him. And persecute not the stranger that dwells with you, for you were a

57 Stranger in the land of Egypt; and hate not an Edomite, for he is your brother. And David also says, “If I have requited them that requited me evil, let my enemies cause me to fall down naked.’’
