THE DIDASCALIA

Didascalia

The Didascalia, in forty-three chapters, is an early church-order work presented as the teaching of the apostles together. It instructs bishops above all — how to teach, judge fairly, receive the repentant, care for widows and orphans, and keep the church a place of mercy as well as order. Its tone is pastoral and often tender: a bishop should be a shepherd and physician of souls, slow to condemn and ready to restore. It helps you hear how the early church tried to practice forgiveness in real cases.

In this edition the text is J. M. Harden's The Ethiopic Didascalia, published in 1920, received as public-domain text and carefully repaired. The received scan had interleaved Harden's footnotes into the text itself; a multi-phase repair against his printed pages restored the body text, and every restored word is Harden's. The Didascalia belongs in this canon because the Ethiopian Church kept it as Scripture, in its Ethiopic form, when other churches treasured different versions or let it fade.

Watch for the bishop as physician — the image that softens every rule in this book.
