Didascalia

Chapter 13

📖 Study notes on this chapter — commentary from the public-domain greats, kept apart from the Scripture text.

THAT CHRISTIANS OUGHT NOT TO ENTER INTO THE CONGREGATION OF THE HEATHEN, NOR BE SPECTATORS IN THE THEATRE, NOR LISTEN TO (THEIR) SONGS

1 Beware, then, of deeds such as these, and take no part in the assembly of the Gentiles, and keep far from the devices of Satan, for the devil has no love toward God. For he who has fellowship with a singer to do the will of the enemy, shall be reckoned with the devil, and shall inherit a curse.

2 Keep far then from evil deeds, in which is no good thing, such are, play-acting, and worshipping of idols, and (consulting of) seers,’ and diviners, and augurs, and necromancers. Even as it is written, ‘And let there be no wickedness in Jacob, nor evil in Israel.”"? Depart then from such works as these which idolaters do to lead men astray: they are the works of Satan.

3 The faithful ought to separate themselves from the congregation of the ungodly, and not join in keeping a feast with them. And in like manner let us keep far from them, and not enter into their houses, nor eat with them, nor drink; for they are full of the deeds of Satan. Let us turn aside from worshipping of idols and from error, and their greetings’ and (their) vows to their gods.

4 You also, young men, perform without sloth for the Holy Church every duty that is fitting, and support yourselves by the fruit of your toil in purity, that you may need nothing, nor be a burden on the Church of God: so that we may be without care. But

5 In the preaching of the holy Gospel let us not neglect the labour of our hands, nor be slothful. For some of us are fishermen, and some of us tent-makers, and some tillers of the ground. Solomon says, “Turn to the ant, you sluggard, and when you see her way, imitate her, and become wiser than she.

6 For she, having no field, nor any that constrains her, and having no master, prepares in summer her food, and labors much in the time of harvest.

7 Or go to the bee, and learn how laborious she is, and how she makes her work good; of whose labour kings and people take for health; who is desired of all and had in honor, yet in strength is she weak; honouring wisdom she is advanced ().' How long, you sluggard, will you sit and sleep? When will you awake from sleep?

8 A while you sleep, and a while you sit, and a while you slumber, and a while you fold your hands on the breast: and then poverty shall come on you as an evil messenger, and want as a swift courier. But if you are not slothful, your harvest shall come” as a fountain of water, and poverty as a slow courier shall be far from you.

9 A foolish and lawless man walks in ways that are not good.” And again he says, “He that tills the ground shall be filled with bread.” And moreover he says, “The sluggard hides his hand in his bosom, and it troubles him to bring it to his mouth.” “He appeared wise to himself."’_ “A foolish sluggard folds his hands together, and eats his own flesh”; for the doings

10 Of a sluggard are without remedy. Whoever will not work, let him not eat, and let him not sit idly with you. Fear Him, then, at all times, and keep far from sloth, for the Lord our God hates all the slothful. Let not the slothful man sit with those that are obedient to God the Father, to whom be honor and glory for ever and ever. Amen.
