Haggai

Haggai is brief — just 2 chapters — and very practical. After the return from exile, the people have rebuilt their own homes but left God's house in ruins, and their harvests keep disappointing them. Through Haggai, God asks them to consider their ways, put first things first, and start rebuilding. They listen. Work begins, and the book carries promises that the glory of the new house will be greater, and that a chosen leader will be like a signet ring on God's hand.

In this edition Haggai is printed from the King James Version of 1611, the public-domain text as received. It belongs in this canon among the Twelve Prophets, the shorter prophetic books the Ethiopian Church keeps together. Its setting also ties it closely to Zechariah, its companion in this collection, who prophesied in the same rebuilding season. Haggai reminds ordinary, tired communities that small, faithful beginnings matter to God.

Watch for the repeated call to 'consider your ways' — a gentle, searching invitation to look honestly at your priorities.
