THE GENERAL EPISTLE OF JAMES

James

James is a practical letter opening with the name of James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ; tradition credits it to James the brother of the Lord, leader of the Jerusalem church. Addressed to the twelve tribes scattered abroad, it reads like wisdom teaching for everyday faith: endure trials, ask God for wisdom, control the tongue, care for orphans and widows, refuse favoritism toward the rich, and back up faith with works. Its proverbs are short, sharp, and memorable. In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, printed as received from the public domain. It stands in this canon among the general letters preserved by the Ethiopian Church, balancing Paul's teaching on faith with an insistence that real faith acts. First-time readers often underline half the book. Watch for the mirror image of a hearer who forgets what he saw.
