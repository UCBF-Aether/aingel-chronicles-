Mark

Mark is the shortest and fastest of the four Gospels, told in 16 chapters. It opens not with a birth but with John baptising and Jesus beginning — and then the action rarely slows. Again and again the word is 'immediately': healings, exorcisms, journeys, arguments, and parables crowd together as Jesus moves toward Jerusalem, suffering, and the cross. The ending is abrupt and trembling, with women fleeing the empty tomb in fear. Tradition associates the book with John Mark, writing down Peter's preaching.

In this edition Mark is printed from the King James Version of 1611, the public-domain text as received. It stands in this canon as the second Gospel, part of the fourfold Gospel the Ethiopian Church keeps at the heart of its New Testament. Its urgency suits a church that has always prized hearing the story whole and often.

Watch for the secret that keeps slipping out — demons and disciples naming who Jesus is, while he tells them to be silent.
