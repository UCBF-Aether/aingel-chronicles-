THE GOSPEL ACCORDING TO SAINT LUKE

Luke

Luke is the longest Gospel, told in 24 chapters, and the most deliberately ordered. Its opening says the writer investigated everything carefully to give his reader certainty, and the result is a spacious, compassionate telling: songs at Jesus's birth, parables found nowhere else — the Good Samaritan, the Prodigal Son, the Rich Man and Lazarus — a long journey to Jerusalem, and a risen Jesus who walks, eats, and explains the Scriptures. Tradition names the writer as Luke, the beloved physician and companion of Paul.

In this edition Luke is printed from the King James Version of 1611, the public-domain text as received. It belongs in this canon as the third Gospel, and its companion volume, Acts, follows later in this same New Testament. The Ethiopian Church keeps both together, so the story of Jesus flows without a break into the story of his witnesses.

Watch for the outsiders Luke keeps bringing to the centre — shepherds, widows, tax collectors, Samaritans, and the poor.
