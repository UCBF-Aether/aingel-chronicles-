Amos

Amos was not a professional prophet. He was a herdsman and a dresser of sycamore trees from Tekoa, in the south, sent north to a rich, comfortable kingdom that was rotting from inside. Over nine chapters his message lands like a hammer: God is not impressed by festivals and songs while the poor are sold for silver and the needy for a pair of shoes. Amos opens with judgments on nation after nation — and then the circle closes on Israel itself. His book holds the Bible's sharpest call for justice to roll down like waters. The text here is the King James Version, printed as received. Amos stands among the twelve minor prophets in the Ethiopian canon this edition follows, as in other canons, and it belongs because the canon will not let worship and injustice sit comfortably together. Watch for the plumb line vision in chapter 7, where God measures his people like a wall — and for Amos's blunt defence when a priest tells him to go home and prophesy somewhere else.
