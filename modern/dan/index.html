THE BOOK OF DANIEL

Daniel

Daniel stands in this canon in its fuller, Ethiopian form — 14 chapters, not twelve. The first six tell beloved stories: Daniel and his friends refusing the king's food, the fiery furnace, the lions' den, and a hand writing on a palace wall. Chapters 7 to 12 open into visions of beasts, kingdoms, and the end of days. Then come the chapters many Bibles move to an appendix. Within chapter 3 stand the Prayer of Azariah and the Song of the Three Children, sung inside the fire. Chapter 13 is Susanna, the story of a righteous woman saved by Daniel's sharp questions. Chapter 14 is Bel and the Dragon, where the prophet Habbacuc is carried by an angel to bring Daniel his dinner in the lions' den. The text here is the King James Version, with those additions restored from the King James Apocrypha. The Ethiopian canon never separated them, and this edition follows that ordering. Watch for how the book keeps one steady claim through every empire: God rules, and faithfulness outlasts kings.
