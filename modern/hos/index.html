Hosea

Hosea opens the twelve minor prophets with a book built on a marriage. Over 14 chapters, the prophet is told to marry a woman who will be unfaithful to him — and to keep loving her, and buy her back when she has fallen into slavery. That painful marriage becomes the book's living picture of God and Israel: a husband who will not stop pursuing a people who keep running to other gods. Tenderness and fury sit side by side here, sometimes in the same verse, and some of the most quoted lines in all the prophets come from Hosea's heartbreak. The text here is the King James Version, printed as received. Hosea stands first among the twelve in the Ethiopian canon this edition follows, as in other canons, setting the key for the whole collection — judgment spoken by a God whose anger never cancels his love. Watch for chapter 11, where God speaks like a father who taught his child to walk and cannot bear to give him up.
