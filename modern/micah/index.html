Micah

Micah came from a small country town, and over seven chapters he speaks to two capital cities at once — Samaria and Jerusalem — with the voice of a man who has watched the powerful rob the poor of their fields, their houses, and even their cloaks. His book swings between fierce judgment and radiant hope: cities will fall, yet a ruler will come from little Bethlehem; nations will beat their swords into ploughshares; and the book asks, in its most famous verse, what God actually requires — to do justly, love mercy, and walk humbly. The text here is the King James Version, printed as received. Micah stands among the twelve minor prophets in the Ethiopian canon this edition follows, as in other canons, a country prophet given a permanent place beside the great Isaiah, his contemporary. Watch for the Bethlehem promise in chapter 5 — the verse the Gospels reach for at Jesus's birth — and for the God in chapter 7 who casts sins into the depths of the sea.
