The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Obadiah 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The vision of Obadiah. Thus saith the Lord GOD concerning Edom; We have heard a rumour from the LORD, and an ambassador is sent among the heathen, Arise ye, and let us rise up against her in battle.

2 Behold, I have made thee small among the heathen: thou art greatly despised.

3 The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee, thou that dwellest in the clefts of the rock, whose habitation is high; that saith in his heart, Who shall bring me down to the ground?

4 Though thou exalt thyself as the eagle, and though thou set thy nest among the stars, thence will I bring thee down, saith the LORD.

5 If thieves came to thee, if robbers by night, (how art thou cut off!) would they not have stolen till they had enough? if the grapegatherers came to thee, would they not leave some grapes?

6 How are the things of Esau searched out! how are his hidden things sought up!

7 All the men of thy confederacy have brought thee even to the border: the men that were at peace with thee have deceived thee, and prevailed against thee; they that eat thy bread have laid a wound under thee: there is none understanding in him.

8 Shall I not in that day, saith the LORD, even destroy the wise men out of Edom, and understanding out of the mount of Esau?

9 And thy mighty men, O Teman, shall be dismayed, to the end that every one of the mount of Esau may be cut off by slaughter.

10 For thy violence against thy brother Jacob shame shall cover thee, and thou shalt be cut off for ever.

11 In the day that thou stoodest on the other side, in the day that the strangers carried away captive his forces, and foreigners entered into his gates, and cast lots upon Jerusalem, even thou wast as one of them.

12 But thou shouldest not have looked on the day of thy brother in the day that he became a stranger; neither shouldest thou have rejoiced over the children of Judah in the day of their destruction; neither shouldest thou have spoken proudly in the day of distress.

13 Thou shouldest not have entered into the gate of my people in the day of their calamity; yea, thou shouldest not have looked on their affliction in the day of their calamity, nor have laid hands on their substance in the day of their calamity;

14 Neither shouldest thou have stood in the crossway, to cut off those of his that did escape; neither shouldest thou have delivered up those of his that did remain in the day of distress.

15 For the day of the LORD is near upon all the heathen: as thou hast done, it shall be done unto thee: thy reward shall return upon thine own head.

16 For as ye have drunk upon my holy mountain, so shall all the heathen drink continually, yea, they shall drink, and they shall swallow down, and they shall be as though they had not been.

17 But upon mount Zion shall be deliverance, and there shall be holiness; and the house of Jacob shall possess their possessions.

18 And the house of Jacob shall be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame, and the house of Esau for stubble, and they shall kindle in them, and devour them; and there shall not be any remaining of the house of Esau; for the LORD hath spoken it.

19 And they of the south shall possess the mount of Esau; and they of the plain the Philistines: and they shall possess the fields of Ephraim, and the fields of Samaria: and Benjamin shall possess Gilead.

20 And the captivity of this host of the children of Israel shall possess that of the Canaanites, even unto Zarephath; and the captivity of Jerusalem, which is in Sepharad, shall possess the cities of the south.

21 And saviours shall come up on mount Zion to judge the mount of Esau; and the kingdom shall be the LORD’s.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This single chapter is a vision concerning Edom, the people descended from Esau, brother of Jacob. Edom's pride in its rock clefts and high dwelling is answered with a fall: its wealth will be searched out completely, its allies will betray it to the border, its wise men and mighty men will be cut off. The charge is violence against brother Jacob, standing aside while strangers carried away his forces and casting lots on Jerusalem, and the book widens from Edom to the day of the LORD upon all nations, then closes with deliverance on Mount Zion, where saviours will judge the mount of Esau and the kingdom will be the LORD's. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The book opens as a vision, that is, a prophecy revealed to Obadiah rather than imagined by him, and its subject is Edom, the neighbouring people descended from Esau who were both brothers and implacable enemies of Israel. JFB explains the 'we' as Obadiah and his people, the rumour as a report resting on the Lord's own authority and therefore certain, and the ambassador as a messenger sent to stir the nations — first Assyria, then Chaldea — against Edom, a summons Jeremiah later quotes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 2. Edom's reduction to insignificance is spoken of as already accomplished, because God's sentence makes it as sure as if done. JFB notes Edom then stretched from Dedan of Arabia to Bozrah in the north, and Jeremiah's echo of this verse confirms the reading: a people once considerable is made small and despised among the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Edom's pride deceived it, and its rock clefts and high dwelling fed that deception: a natural fortress felt like safety itself. Henry's warning is that God easily lays low those who magnify and exalt themselves, and carnal security only ripens men for ruin and makes the ruin worse when it comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. No height of self-exaltation escapes this sentence — not the eagle's flight, not a nest set among the stars. Henry groups these verses under pride humbled: the higher Edom climbs in its own heart, the more directly the Lord says, 'thence will I bring thee down.' Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Henry places these verses under Edom's wealth plundered, and the comparison makes the completeness plain: ordinary thieves steal until they have enough, and grapegatherers leave some grapes behind. Edom's stripping leaves nothing, for what thieves and gleaners spare, judgment takes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The cry of astonishment marks a total exposure: the things of Esau are searched out and even his hidden stores are sought up. Henry's heading for the passage is plunder, and his concise summary agrees — Edom shall be spoiled and brought down, with nothing kept back from the search. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Every support Edom trusted fails at once: its confederates escort it only as far as the border and abandon it, its friends at peace deceive and prevail against it, and those who ate its bread lay a wound under it. Henry's moral is exact — those who make flesh their trust arm it against themselves, and trusting men with whom one joins oneself may prove a wound and dishonour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The wisdom Edom boasted in — Teman was famed for it — is destroyed out of it, and understanding is taken from the mount of Esau. Henry reads this as just denial: God withholds the understanding to keep out of danger from those who would not use their understanding to keep out of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Even Edom's mighty men, the warriors it stayed itself on, are dismayed, so that every one of the mount of Esau is cut off by slaughter. Henry's summary covers it: all the enemies of God's church shall be disappointed in the very things they stay themselves on, and Edom's strength proves no stay at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The charge is named plainly: violence against brother Jacob, and the sentence matches it — shame shall cover Edom and it shall be cut off forever. Henry stresses that all violence is sin, but violence against God's people is far worse, and Edom's barbarous conduct toward Judah and Jerusalem is charged upon it here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Edom did not need to strike the blow to share the guilt: it stood on the other side while strangers carried away Jacob's forces, foreigners entered Jerusalem's gates, and lots were cast upon the city. Henry's rule applies — he who joins in with evildoers and abets their deeds shall be reckoned, and reckoned with, as one of them, and idle spectators of a neighbour's troubles have a great deal to answer for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. JFB reads 'looked on' as a malignant pleasure and a brutal stare at a brother's day of calamity, when he became a stranger banished as an alien from his own land. Edom should not have rejoiced over Judah's destruction nor spoken proudly — literally, made great the mouth — in the day of distress, for God sends heavy calamities on those who rejoice in their enemies' calamities, and Gill likewise dates the prophecy after Jerusalem's destruction by Nebuchadnezzar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 13. The indictment grows from looking to entering: Edom went into the gate of God's people in their calamity, gazed on their affliction, and laid hands on their substance. Henry's note is that by meddling with the spoil Edom became a partaker of the crime, an accessory after the fact, and those who think to enrich themselves by the ruins of God's people only impoverish themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Worse still, Edom stood in the crossway where roads met to cut off fugitives making their escape, and delivered up those who remained in the day of distress. Henry's account is blunt: Edom not only robbed its brethren but murdered them, intercepting those the Chaldean sword had spared and handing them back to destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The day of the Lord is near upon all the heathen, not Edom alone, and the measure returns exactly: as thou hast done, it shall be done unto thee, and thy reward shall return upon thine own head. Henry adds that though judgment begins at the house of God, it shall not end there, and the cup that went round to Israel now goes round to its oppressors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. As Edom and the nations drank in triumph upon God's holy mountain, so shall all the heathen drink continually of the same bitter cup, swallowing it down until they are as though they had never been. Henry's close of the passage holds both ends together: the troubles of the righteous will soon end, but those of the wicked will be eternal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The turn of the book is deliverance on Mount Zion, with holiness, where the house of Jacob shall possess its own possessions again. Henry's outline marks this as the second part of the prophecy: after the threatenings against Edom come gracious promises to Israel of restoration, reform, and victory over the Edomites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Matthew Henry (Concise), John Gill
  • Verse 18. The restored houses become the instruments of Edom's end: Jacob a fire, Joseph a flame, and Esau stubble kindled and devoured until no survivor remains of the house of Esau. Henry reads this as Israel victorious over the Edomites and master of their land, and Gill agrees that the Edomites' wasted land passes to those they wronged — 'for the Lord hath spoken it' makes it certain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, John Gill
  • Verse 19. Gill maps the restored borders in detail: those of the south seize Idumea lying next to them, those of the plain take Philistia — Azotus, Gaza, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron — and Israel repossesses the fields of Ephraim and Samaria, while Benjamin is enlarged to take in Gilead beyond Jordan. Some of this, Gill notes, was fulfilled in Maccabean times, but the full settlement looks to the latter day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill
  • Verse 20. The returning captives possess the Canaanite land as far as Zarephath, called Sarepta of Sidon, where Elijah once dwelt, and the captivity of Jerusalem in Sepharad possesses the cities of the south. Gill reads this as a greatly enlarged border in the latter day, or else as the enlargement of the church of Christ when the gospel spread through those parts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill
  • Verse 21. Saviours — deliverers raised up by God — come up on Mount Zion to judge the mount of Esau, and the book ends where it must: the kingdom shall be the Lord's. Henry's outline closes the prophecy here with the setting up of the kingdom of the Messiah by the bringing in of the great salvation, and Gill calls this the glorious prophecy of the kingdom of the Messiah with which the book concludes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, John Gill

Unfamiliar terms

  • Ephraim. the leading tribe of the northern kingdom, used in the prophets for the northern kingdom of Israel itself, as distinct from Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Samaria. the capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, standing in the prophets for the ten tribes as Jerusalem stands for Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • The day of the LORD. the day of God's visitation and judgment, pictured in the prophets as darkness, trumpet alarm, and distress, and also as deliverance for those who call on his name. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Teman. the Edomite region famed for wisdom, whose wise men and mighty men Obadiah says will be destroyed and dismayed. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Saviours. deliverers raised up by God, who in Obadiah's closing verse come up on Mount Zion to judge the mount of Esau. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
