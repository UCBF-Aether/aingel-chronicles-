The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 31 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 In thee, O LORD, do I put my trust; let me never be ashamed: deliver me in thy righteousness.

2 Bow down thine ear to me; deliver me speedily: be thou my strong rock, for an house of defence to save me.

3 For thou art my rock and my fortress; therefore for thy name’s sake lead me, and guide me.

4 Pull me out of the net that they have laid privily for me: for thou art my strength.

5 Into thine hand I commit my spirit: thou hast redeemed me, O LORD God of truth.

6 I have hated them that regard lying vanities: but I trust in the LORD.

7 I will be glad and rejoice in thy mercy: for thou hast considered my trouble; thou hast known my soul in adversities;

8 And hast not shut me up into the hand of the enemy: thou hast set my feet in a large room.

9 Have mercy upon me, O LORD, for I am in trouble: mine eye is consumed with grief, yea, my soul and my belly.

10 For my life is spent with grief, and my years with sighing: my strength faileth because of mine iniquity, and my bones are consumed.

11 I was a reproach among all mine enemies, but especially among my neighbours, and a fear to mine acquaintance: they that did see me without fled from me.

12 I am forgotten as a dead man out of mind: I am like a broken vessel.

13 For I have heard the slander of many: fear was on every side: while they took counsel together against me, they devised to take away my life.

14 But I trusted in thee, O LORD: I said, Thou art my God.

15 My times are in thy hand: deliver me from the hand of mine enemies, and from them that persecute me.

16 Make thy face to shine upon thy servant: save me for thy mercies’ sake.

17 Let me not be ashamed, O LORD; for I have called upon thee: let the wicked be ashamed, and let them be silent in the grave.

18 Let the lying lips be put to silence; which speak grievous things proudly and contemptuously against the righteous.

19 Oh how great is thy goodness, which thou hast laid up for them that fear thee; which thou hast wrought for them that trust in thee before the sons of men!

20 Thou shalt hide them in the secret of thy presence from the pride of man: thou shalt keep them secretly in a pavilion from the strife of tongues.

21 Blessed be the LORD: for he hath shewed me his marvellous kindness in a strong city.

22 For I said in my haste, I am cut off from before thine eyes: nevertheless thou heardest the voice of my supplications when I cried unto thee.

23 O love the LORD, all ye his saints: for the LORD preserveth the faithful, and plentifully rewardeth the proud doer.

24 Be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart, all ye that hope in the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Trust in the rock amid slander and encircling fear: the speaker commits his spirit, grieves in body and soul, is forgotten like a broken vessel, then turns on 'You are my God,' times in God's hand, goodness laid up for the fearing, hidden in God's presence, ending with courage for all who hope. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In you, Lord, I put my trust; let me never be ashamed; deliver me in your righteousness: trust, freedom from shame, and deliverance by God's righteousness open the psalm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Bow down your ear to me; deliver me speedily; be my strong rock, a house of defence to save me: a bowed ear, speedy deliverance, and a rock-house picture urgent, accessible refuge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For you are my rock and fortress; therefore for your name's sake lead me and guide me: God's nature and name are the reasons for leading, not David's worth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Pull me out of the net laid privily for me, for you are my strength: hidden nets need a stronger hand than the victim's to untangle them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Into your hand I commit my spirit; you have redeemed me, Lord God of truth: the words Christ uttered on the cross express a trust already complete — the spirit is deposited with the God of truth who redeems. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. I have hated those who regard lying vanities, but I trust in the Lord: hatred of idols and trust in God are two sides of one loyalty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. I will be glad and rejoice in your mercy, for you have considered my trouble and known my soul in adversities: mercy that considers trouble and knows the soul in adversity is personal, not general. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. You have not shut me up into the enemy's hand, but set my feet in a large room: liberty replaces confinement, and the large room is the opposite of the net. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Have mercy on me, Lord, for I am in trouble; my eye is consumed with grief, yea, my soul and my belly: grief reaches eye, soul, and body together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. My life is spent with grief, my years with sighing; my strength fails because of my iniquity, and my bones are consumed: sorrow is here traced partly to his own sin, which weakens strength itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. I was a reproach among all my enemies, especially among neighbours, and a fear to acquaintances; those who saw me without fled from me: reproach spreads from enemies to neighbours, and even friends flee the sight of suffering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. I am forgotten as a dead man out of mind, like a broken vessel: forgotten and broken — useless in men's account — is the depth of the humiliation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. I have heard the slander of many; fear was on every side; while they took counsel together against me, they devised to take away my life: slander, encircling fear, and a plot on his life combine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. But I trusted in you, Lord; I said, You are my God: the turn of the psalm is a single assertion of relation — You are my God — against all the preceding verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. My times are in your hand; deliver me from my enemies and persecutors: times, not merely this moment, are in God's hand, which is why deliverance can be asked with confidence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Make your face shine upon your servant; save me for your mercies' sake: the shining face is favour itself, and mercy, not merit, is its ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Let me not be ashamed, Lord, for I have called on you; let the wicked be ashamed and silent in the grave: shame is prayed to fall where it belongs — on the wicked, in death's silence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Let lying lips be put to silence, which speak grievous things proudly and contemptuously against the righteous: the slander of verse 13 is answered by the silencing of the slanderers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Oh how great is your goodness, laid up for those who fear you, wrought for those who trust in you before the sons of men! Goodness is both stored up and publicly worked out, before men's eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. You shall hide them in the secret of your presence from the pride of man, and keep them secretly in a pavilion from the strife of tongues: God's presence is a hiding-place from both pride and slanderous tongues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Blessed be the Lord, for he has shown me marvellous kindness in a strong city: kindness in a besieged, strong city — deliverance where escape looked impossible — is marvellous indeed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. For I said in my haste, I am cut off from before your eyes; nevertheless you heard my supplications when I cried: hasty despair is corrected by heard prayer — feeling said cut off, fact said heard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. O love the Lord, all his saints, for the Lord preserves the faithful and plentifully rewards the proud doer: preservation of the faithful and repayment of the proud are the two rules that justify love for God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart, all you who hope in the Lord: the psalm ends as exhortation to all hopers — courage is not self-made but God-strengthened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
