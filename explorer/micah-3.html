The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Micah 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And I said, Hear, I pray you, O heads of Jacob, and ye princes of the house of Israel; Is it not for you to know judgment?

2 Who hate the good, and love the evil; who pluck off their skin from off them, and their flesh from off their bones;

3 Who also eat the flesh of my people, and flay their skin from off them; and they break their bones, and chop them in pieces, as for the pot, and as flesh within the caldron.

4 Then shall they cry unto the LORD, but he will not hear them: he will even hide his face from them at that time, as they have behaved themselves ill in their doings.

5 Thus saith the LORD concerning the prophets that make my people err, that bite with their teeth, and cry, Peace; and he that putteth not into their mouths, they even prepare war against him.

6 Therefore night shall be unto you, that ye shall not have a vision; and it shall be dark unto you, that ye shall not divine; and the sun shall go down over the prophets, and the day shall be dark over them.

7 Then shall the seers be ashamed, and the diviners confounded: yea, they shall all cover their lips; for there is no answer of God.

8 But truly I am full of power by the spirit of the LORD, and of judgment, and of might, to declare unto Jacob his transgression, and to Israel his sin.

9 Hear this, I pray you, ye heads of the house of Jacob, and princes of the house of Israel, that abhor judgment, and pervert all equity.

10 They build up Zion with blood, and Jerusalem with iniquity.

11 The heads thereof judge for reward, and the priests thereof teach for hire, and the prophets thereof divine for money: yet will they lean upon the LORD, and say, Is not the LORD among us? none evil can come upon us.

12 Therefore shall Zion for your sake be plowed as a field, and Jerusalem shall become heaps, and the mountain of the house as the high places of the forest.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The heads of Jacob and princes of Israel are charged with hating good and loving evil, stripping the skin from the people and breaking their bones, while prophets divine for money and priests teach for hire, all leaning on the LORD and saying no evil can come. Therefore Zion will be plowed as a field, Jerusalem will become heaps, and the mountain of the house will be as the high places of the forest. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The heads of Jacob and princes of Israel are called to hear, for it is their business above all to know judgment. Henry notes how seldom wholesome truths reach the ears of those in high stations or authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. These rulers hate the good and love the evil, plucking the skin and flesh from the people they should protect. Men cannot expect to do ill and fare well; they will find done to them what they did to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. They eat the flesh of God's people, flaying their skin and chopping their bones as meat for the pot. Such cruelty to the people is the very behaviour that will be repaid in kind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Then they will cry to the Lord, but he will not hear them and will hide his face, as they behaved ill in their doings. Those who deceive others are preparing confusion for their own faces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The prophets who make God's people err cry "Peace" while they bite with their teeth, and prepare war against anyone who will not feed them. Their smooth words serve their appetites, not the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Therefore night will come on these prophets: no vision, no divining, the sun gone down over them and the day dark. The light they abused is taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The seers will be ashamed and the diviners confounded, all covering their lips, for there is no answer of God. Deceivers are left speechless when God stops answering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. But Micah is truly full of power by the Spirit of the Lord, with judgment and might, to declare Jacob's transgression and Israel's sin. Henry notes his strength was not from himself: ardent love for God and souls, and zeal against sin, kept him at his work despite difficulties, and those who act honestly may act boldly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Hear this, heads of Jacob and princes of Israel who abhor judgment and pervert all equity. Those who come to hear God's word must be willing to be told of their faults, and take it kindly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. They build up Zion with blood and Jerusalem with iniquity. Henry says Zion's walls owe no thanks to builders who raise them by bloodshed: the sin of man works not the righteousness of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Heads judge for reward, priests teach for hire, prophets divine for money, yet they lean on the Lord, saying none evil can come upon them. Henry distinguishes faith, which rests in the Lord as the soul's foundation, from presumption, which only leans on him as a prop to serve a turn; even good acts done for filthy lucre become abomination. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Therefore Zion will be plowed as a field for their sake, Jerusalem become heaps, and the mountain of the house as the high places of the forest. Henry notes this was exactly fulfilled at Jerusalem's destruction by the Romans: if sacred places are polluted by sin, they will be wasted by God's judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
