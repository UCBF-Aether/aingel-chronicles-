The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD sent Nathan unto David. And he came unto him, and said unto him, There were two men in one city; the one rich, and the other poor.

2 The rich man had exceeding many flocks and herds:

3 But the poor man had nothing, save one little ewe lamb, which he had bought and nourished up: and it grew up together with him, and with his children; it did eat of his own meat, and drank of his own cup, and lay in his bosom, and was unto him as a daughter.

4 And there came a traveller unto the rich man, and he spared to take of his own flock and of his own herd, to dress for the wayfaring man that was come unto him; but took the poor man’s lamb, and dressed it for the man that was come to him.

5 And David’s anger was greatly kindled against the man; and he said to Nathan, As the LORD liveth, the man that hath done this thing shall surely die:

6 And he shall restore the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing, and because he had no pity.

7 And Nathan said to David, Thou art the man. Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, I anointed thee king over Israel, and I delivered thee out of the hand of Saul;

8 And I gave thee thy master’s house, and thy master’s wives into thy bosom, and gave thee the house of Israel and of Judah; and if that had been too little, I would moreover have given unto thee such and such things.

9 Wherefore hast thou despised the commandment of the LORD, to do evil in his sight? thou hast killed Uriah the Hittite with the sword, and hast taken his wife to be thy wife, and hast slain him with the sword of the children of Ammon.

10 Now therefore the sword shall never depart from thine house; because thou hast despised me, and hast taken the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be thy wife.

11 Thus saith the LORD, Behold, I will raise up evil against thee out of thine own house, and I will take thy wives before thine eyes, and give them unto thy neighbour, and he shall lie with thy wives in the sight of this sun.

12 For thou didst it secretly: but I will do this thing before all Israel, and before the sun.

13 And David said unto Nathan, I have sinned against the LORD. And Nathan said unto David, The LORD also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die.

14 Howbeit, because by this deed thou hast given great occasion to the enemies of the LORD to blaspheme, the child also that is born unto thee shall surely die.

15 And Nathan departed unto his house. And the LORD struck the child that Uriah’s wife bare unto David, and it was very sick.

16 David therefore besought God for the child; and David fasted, and went in, and lay all night upon the earth.

17 And the elders of his house arose, and went to him, to raise him up from the earth: but he would not, neither did he eat bread with them.

18 And it came to pass on the seventh day, that the child died. And the servants of David feared to tell him that the child was dead: for they said, Behold, while the child was yet alive, we spake unto him, and he would not hearken unto our voice: how will he then vex himself, if we tell him that the child is dead?

19 But when David saw that his servants whispered, David perceived that the child was dead: therefore David said unto his servants, Is the child dead? And they said, He is dead.

20 Then David arose from the earth, and washed, and anointed himself, and changed his apparel, and came into the house of the LORD, and worshipped: then he came to his own house; and when he required, they set bread before him, and he did eat.

21 Then said his servants unto him, What thing is this that thou hast done? thou didst fast and weep for the child, while it was alive; but when the child was dead, thou didst rise and eat bread.

22 And he said, While the child was yet alive, I fasted and wept: for I said, Who can tell whether GOD will be gracious to me, that the child may live?

23 But now he is dead, wherefore should I fast? can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me.

24 And David comforted Bathsheba his wife, and went in unto her, and lay with her: and she bare a son, and he called his name Solomon: and the LORD loved him.

25 And he sent by the hand of Nathan the prophet; and he called his name Jedidiah, because of the LORD.

26 And Joab fought against Rabbah of the children of Ammon, and took the royal city.

27 And Joab sent messengers to David, and said, I have fought against Rabbah, and have taken the city of waters.

28 Now therefore gather the rest of the people together, and encamp against the city, and take it: lest I take the city, and it be called after my name.

29 And David gathered all the people together, and went to Rabbah, and fought against it, and took it.

30 And he took their king’s crown from off his head, the weight whereof was a talent of gold with the precious stones: and it was set on David’s head. And he brought forth the spoil of the city in great abundance.

31 And he brought forth the people that were therein, and put them under saws, and under harrows of iron, and under axes of iron, and made them pass through the brick-kiln: and thus did he unto all the cities of the children of Ammon. So David and all the people returned unto Jerusalem.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Nathan confronts David with the parable of the poor man's lamb, and David condemns himself out of his own mouth before Nathan names him: Thou art the man. David repents, but the child of Bathsheba dies, and after mourning David comforts Bathsheba; she bears Solomon, while Joab presses the war against Rabbah to its end. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Lord sends Nathan to David, and Nathan tells a parable of two men in one city, one rich and one poor. God will not suffer his people to lie still in sin, Henry notes, and by the parable Nathan draws from David a sentence against himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The rich man has exceeding many flocks and herds. His abundance is the setting for a theft that needs no excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The poor man has nothing save one little ewe lamb, bought and nourished up, growing with him and his children, eating his meat, drinking his cup, lying in his bosom as a daughter. The lamb's tenderness makes the coming wrong unmistakable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. A traveller comes to the rich man, who spares his own flock and herd but takes the poor man's lamb and dresses it for the wayfaring man. The injustice is plain precisely because the rich man loses nothing by sparing his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. David's anger is greatly kindled, and he swears the man who did this shall surely die. The king who cannot see his own sin judges another's with full severity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. He shall restore the lamb fourfold, because he did this and had no pity. David's sentence measures the crime by its heartlessness, not only by its loss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Nathan says, Thou art the man, and speaks the Lord's word: I anointed thee king over Israel and delivered thee from Saul. Prudence in reproof and faithfulness meet here, Henry notes: Nathan is plain, Thou art the man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. God gave David his master's house and wives, and the houses of Israel and Judah, and would have given more if that had been too little. The recital of gifts makes the sin not a want but a contempt of abundance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Wherefore hast thou despised the commandment of the Lord, to do evil in his sight, killing Uriah with the sword and taking his wife, slaying him with the sword of Ammon? God shows how much he hates sin even in his own people, and wherever he finds it he will not let it go unpunished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Therefore the sword shall never depart from David's house, because he despised God and took Uriah's wife. The punishment fits the crime's house: violence done in secret breeds violence at home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. God will raise up evil against David out of his own house, taking his wives before his eyes and giving them to his neighbour. The sentence is pronounced openly, answering a sin done secretly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Thou didst it secretly, but I will do this before all Israel and before the sun. Concealment, which David bought with murder, is answered with publicity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. David says to Nathan, I have sinned against the Lord, and Nathan answers, The Lord also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die. David says not a word to excuse himself, Henry notes, but freely owns it, and the true penitent is assured his sin is forgiven, not to die eternally nor be for ever put away from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Howbeit, because by this deed David gave great occasion to God's enemies to blaspheme, the child born to him shall surely die. Pardon does not cancel chastening: Henry warns that even where pardon is obtained, the Lord visits transgression with the rod, and the sins of professors furnish enemies with matter for reproach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Nathan departs, and the Lord strikes the child Uriah's wife bore to David, and it is very sick. For one momentary gratification of a vile lust, Henry observes, David had to endure many days and years of extreme distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. David besought God for the child, fasted, went in, and lay all night upon the earth. God gives leave to be earnest in prayer for particular blessings from trust in his power and general mercy, even without a particular promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The elders rise to lift him from the earth, but he will not, neither will he eat bread with them. His refusal of comfort while the child lives is the posture of a man pleading, not yet resigned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. On the seventh day the child dies, and David's servants fear to tell him, reasoning that while the child lived he would not be comforted. The servants' fear shows how deep David's grief has run. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. David sees his servants whisper, perceives the child is dead, asks, and they answer, He is dead. The truth he feared is confirmed by a whisper before it is spoken aloud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Then David rises from the earth, washes, anoints himself, changes his apparel, comes into the house of the Lord and worships, then eats at his own house. He patiently submits to God's will in the death of one child, and the way to have creature comforts restored, Henry says, is cheerfully to resign them to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. His servants ask why he fasted and wept while the child lived, but rises and eats now the child is dead. The reversal puzzles those who watched only the outward grief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. While the child was yet alive, David says, he fasted and wept, saying, Who can tell whether God will be gracious to me, that the child may live? Prayer while life remained was hope, not a bargain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. But now he is dead, wherefore should I fast? Can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me. Resignation replaces pleading, and grief takes the shape of a hope beyond death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. David comforts Bathsheba his wife, goes in to her, and she bears a son called Solomon, and the Lord loved him. God made up the loss to his advantage in the birth of another, Henry notes, and David, who penned Psalm 51, was willing to bear the shame of his sin ever before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. He sends by Nathan the prophet, and the child is called Jedidiah, because of the Lord. God by grace particularly owns and favours that son, ordering him to be called Beloved of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Joab fights against Rabbah of Ammon and takes the royal city. The war David neglected in Jerusalem is still being fought by his general, and now nears its end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Joab sends messengers saying he has fought against Rabbah and taken the city of waters. The report invites David to finish what his army began. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Joab urges David to gather the rest of the people, encamp against the city, and take it, lest Joab take it and it be called after his name. The general yields the honour of the capture to the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. David gathers all the people, goes to Rabbah, fights against it, and takes it. The king at last goes in person to the war he once stayed home from. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. He takes the king's crown, a talent of gold with precious stones, and it is set on David's head, with great spoil from the city. The enemy's crown becomes the victor's, the visible end of the Ammonite war. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. He brings out the people and puts them under saws, harrows, and axes of iron, and through the brick-kiln, doing so to all Ammon's cities. Henry reads this severity as a sign David's heart was not yet made soft by repentance: we are most compassionate and forgiving to others when we most feel our need of the Lord's forgiving love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
