The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.

2 And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.

3 And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

4 And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.

5 And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day.

6 And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters.

7 And God made the firmament, and divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament: and it was so.

8 And God called the firmament Heaven. And the evening and the morning were the second day.

9 And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so.

10 And God called the dry land Earth; and the gathering together of the waters called he Seas: and God saw that it was good.

11 And God said, Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself, upon the earth: and it was so.

12 And the earth brought forth grass, and herb yielding seed after his kind, and the tree yielding fruit, whose seed was in itself, after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

13 And the evening and the morning were the third day.

14 And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:

15 And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so.

16 And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also.

17 And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth,

18 And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that it was good.

19 And the evening and the morning were the fourth day.

20 And God said, Let the waters bring forth abundantly the moving creature that hath life, and fowl that may fly above the earth in the open firmament of heaven.

21 And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

22 And God blessed them, saying, Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let fowl multiply in the earth.

23 And the evening and the morning were the fifth day.

24 And God said, Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind: and it was so.

25 And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that it was good.

26 And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.

27 So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.

28 And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.

29 And God said, Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat.

30 And to every beast of the earth, and to every fowl of the air, and to every thing that creepeth upon the earth, wherein there is life, I have given every green herb for meat: and it was so.

31 And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In six days God orders a formless, watery world: light is separated from darkness, sky from waters, land and sea from one another, then plants, sun, moon and stars, sea and sky creatures, and land animals fill each realm. Humanity, male and female, is made last, in God's image, given rule over the other creatures and plants for food, and the whole is called very good. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The whole universe had a beginning, and God alone made it from nothing. Henry stresses that this first truth answers every claim that the world made itself or always existed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. At first the earth was a dark, formless, watery waste. JFB pictures the Spirit brooding over it, like a bird over eggs, ordering what was dead and disordered into a world fit for life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God's command alone brought light, clearing the gloom that covered the waters. Henry notes that light came by God's word before the sun is named, showing creation obeys his voice, not nature alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. God separated light from darkness, setting up their regular alternation. JFB explains this as day following night in order, not as a battle between two powers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The first day is counted from evening to morning, the ancient way of reckoning a day. This frames each day as a complete, ordered period under God's direction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The firmament is the expanse of sky and air set in the midst of the waters. JFB explains it as the atmosphere that lifts clouds above and leaves waters below. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God divided the waters above from those below by that expanse. The point is order: waters are given their place, not left as a threatening chaos. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. God named the expanse Heaven, the visible sky above the earth. Naming marks ownership and purpose: the sky is part of God's ordered creation, not a god itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The waters below were gathered into one place so dry land could appear. JFB describes seas, lakes and rivers forming as the waters rushed into hollows left for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. God named the dry land Earth and the gathered waters Seas, and saw that it was good. Henry notes that what God orders and names is fit for the life he will place on it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The earth was made to sprout plants, grasses and trees, each yielding seed after its kind. JFB stresses they grew at once by divine power, not by slow farming, and reproduce true to their kind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The land produced vegetation as commanded, each kind true to itself. The orderly fruitfulness of plants is presented as God's provision, built into creation from the start. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The third day closes with land, sea and plants all in place. The sequence matters: a home is prepared before its inhabitants arrive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Lights were set in the sky to divide day from night and mark seasons, days and years. Their job is to measure time and give light, not to rule human fate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Those lights also serve as lamps for the earth. Henry notes they are instruments under God, useful servants rather than objects of worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The sun is the greater light for the day and the moon the great light for the night, with the stars also made. JFB explains the moon counts as great because of its visible rule of the night, even though its light is borrowed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. God placed these lights in the sky to shine on the earth. Their position is deliberate: they are set where they can serve the world below. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They rule day and night and divide light from darkness. Henry calls them deputy rulers under God, whose best rule is simply giving light and doing good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The fourth day closes the ordering of the heavens. Sun, moon and stars now keep the rhythm of time that human life will live by. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The waters and sky were filled with living creatures, from small swarming life to great sea creatures and birds. JFB notes their rapid increase shows life made to thrive in its own element. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. God created the great sea creatures and every kind of bird and sea life. No creature, however large or strange, lies outside God's making. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. God blessed sea and sky creatures to be fruitful and multiply and fill their realms. Blessing here is God's gift of life and increase, not mere permission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The fifth day closes with waters and air teeming with life. The world is no longer empty: it is filling exactly as God commanded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The land was commanded to bring forth cattle, creeping things and wild beasts, each after its kind. JFB groups land animals into tame, wild and creeping kinds, all made in one day's work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. God made each kind of land animal and saw that it was good. Each creature is suited to its own way of life, which Henry takes as evidence of God's wisdom in fitting means to ends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Man was made last, by a special counsel, in God's image and likeness, to rule the other creatures. JFB stresses the image lies not in body or mere mind, but in moral uprightness: knowledge, righteousness and true holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. God created humanity male and female in his own image. The image is shared by both, and the verse repeats the point to show its weight: people bear a dignity no other creature carries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. God blessed humanity to multiply, fill the earth, subdue it and rule over sea, sky and land creatures. Henry and JFB read this as stewardship: rule under God, not ownership apart from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. God gave seed-bearing plants and fruit trees as food for people. Provision comes with creation itself: humanity is fed from what God has already made fruitful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Green plants were likewise given as food to beasts, birds and creeping things. The original order is one of provision for all living things, without predation named here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. God saw all he had made and called it very good, closing the sixth day. Henry contrasts this with our own works: God can look back on his work without shame, because it is exactly as he intended. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • firmament. The expanse of sky and air set in the midst of the waters, lifting clouds above and leaving waters below. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 1:6, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • dominion. Rule over the other creatures, read by the commentators as stewardship under God, not ownership apart from him. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 1:28, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — an open question, not a conclusion)

Genesis 1 and Genesis 2: two creation accounts

This section investigates a question readers often ask, it does not settle it. Genesis 1 tells creation in six ordered days, from light to humanity, with God speaking each realm into place and calling the whole very good. Genesis 2 returns to the same beginning in closer detail: man formed, a garden planted, animals named, and woman formed as companion, ending with man and wife unashamed. Interpreted Chapter text of Genesis 1 and Genesis 2; study notes for both chapters

The two accounts share the same God, the same created order, and the same humanity made for life with God. They differ in shape and focus: Genesis 1 moves in a broad, day-by-day sequence across the whole world, while Genesis 2 narrows to one garden, one man and one woman, and speaks of God forming and planting rather than only commanding. Genesis 2 also gives the seventh day's rest before turning back to the garden, where Genesis 1 closes on the sixth day. Interpreted Chapter text of Genesis 1 and Genesis 2

Whether the two chapters are two tellings of one event, a broad account followed by a detailed one, or something else is not established by the sources consulted for this page, and no direct dependence or shared authorship is implied here.
