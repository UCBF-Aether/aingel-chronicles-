The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — John 21 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 After these things Jesus shewed himself again to the disciples at the sea of Tiberias; and on this wise shewed he himself.

2 There were together Simon Peter, and Thomas called Didymus, and Nathanael of Cana in Galilee, and the sons of Zebedee, and two other of his disciples.

3 Simon Peter saith unto them, I go a fishing. They say unto him, We also go with thee. They went forth, and entered into a ship immediately; and that night they caught nothing.

4 But when the morning was now come, Jesus stood on the shore: but the disciples knew not that it was Jesus.

5 Then Jesus saith unto them, Children, have ye any meat? They answered him, No.

6 And he said unto them, Cast the net on the right side of the ship, and ye shall find. They cast therefore, and now they were not able to draw it for the multitude of fishes.

7 Therefore that disciple whom Jesus loved saith unto Peter, It is the Lord. Now when Simon Peter heard that it was the Lord, he girt his fisher’s coat unto him, (for he was naked,) and did cast himself into the sea.

8 And the other disciples came in a little ship; (for they were not far from land, but as it were two hundred cubits,) dragging the net with fishes.

9 As soon then as they were come to land, they saw a fire of coals there, and fish laid thereon, and bread.

10 Jesus saith unto them, Bring of the fish which ye have now caught.

11 Simon Peter went up, and drew the net to land full of great fishes, an hundred and fifty and three: and for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken.

12 Jesus saith unto them, Come and dine. And none of the disciples durst ask him, Who art thou? knowing that it was the Lord.

13 Jesus then cometh, and taketh bread, and giveth them, and fish likewise.

14 This is now the third time that Jesus shewed himself to his disciples, after that he was risen from the dead.

15 So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs.

16 He saith to him again the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my sheep.

17 He saith unto him the third time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me? And he said unto him, Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep.

18 Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdest thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest: but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not.

19 This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me.

20 Then Peter, turning about, seeth the disciple whom Jesus loved following; which also leaned on his breast at supper, and said, Lord, which is he that betrayeth thee?

21 Peter seeing him saith to Jesus, Lord, and what shall this man do?

22 Jesus saith unto him, If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee? follow thou me.

23 Then went this saying abroad among the brethren, that that disciple should not die: yet Jesus said not unto him, He shall not die; but, If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee?

24 This is the disciple which testifieth of these things, and wrote these things: and we know that his testimony is true.

25 And there are also many other things which Jesus did, the which, if they should be written every one, I suppose that even the world itself could not contain the books that should be written. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After these things Jesus shows himself again at the Sea of Tiberias: the disciples fish all night and catch nothing, until the stranger on shore tells them to cast on the right side and the net fills with a hundred and fifty-three great fishes (vv. 1–14). Over breakfast he asks Peter three times “lovest thou me?” and each time charges him “feed my sheep,” foretelling the death by which Peter will glorify God (vv. 15–19). Peter asks about the beloved disciple’s future and is answered “follow thou me”; John closes by testifying that this disciple wrote these things, and that the world itself could not contain the books if all Jesus did were written (vv. 20–25). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, John 21

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After these things Jesus showed himself again to the disciples at the Sea of Tiberias, and on this wise showed he himself, for Christ makes himself known to his people usually in his ordinances, but sometimes by his Spirit when they are employed in their business. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. There were together Simon Peter, and Thomas called Didymus, and Nathanael of Cana in Galilee, and the sons of Zebedee, and two other of his disciples, gathered in common conversation and common business. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Simon Peter said unto them, I go a fishing, and they said, We also go with thee, and they went forth, and entered into a ship immediately, and that night they caught nothing, for the hour for their entering upon action was not yet come, and they would maintain themselves rather than be burdensome to any. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. But when the morning was now come, Jesus stood on the shore, but the disciples knew not that it was Jesus, for Christ's time of making himself known is when his people are most at a loss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Then Jesus said unto them, Children, have ye any meat, and they answered him, No, for he knows the temporal wants of his people, and has promised not only grace sufficient, but food convenient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And he said unto them, Cast the net on the right side of the ship, and ye shall find, and they cast therefore, and now they were not able to draw it for the multitude of fishes, for there is nothing lost by observing Christ's orders, it is casting the net on the right side of the ship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Therefore that disciple whom Jesus loved said unto Peter, It is the Lord, and now when Simon Peter heard that it was the Lord, he girt his fisher's coat unto him, for he was naked, and did cast himself into the sea, for John, who had cleaved most closely to his Master in his sufferings, knew him soonest, and Peter was the most zealous and reached Christ first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And the other disciples came in a little ship, for they were not far from land, but as it were two hundred cubits, dragging the net with fishes, and such persons ought not to be blamed as worldly, for they, in their places, are as truly serving Christ as the others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. As soon then as they were come to land, they saw a fire of coals there, and fish laid thereon, and bread, for the Lord Jesus had provision ready for them, and we need not be curious whence it came, but may be comforted at Christ's care for his disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jesus said unto them, Bring of the fish which ye have now caught, joining their labour to his provision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Simon Peter went up, and drew the net to land full of great fishes, a hundred and fifty and three, and for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken, for the net of the gospel has enclosed multitudes, yet is as strong as ever to bring souls to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Jesus said unto them, Come and dine, and none of the disciples durst ask him, Who art thou, knowing that it was the Lord, for Jesus manifests himself to his people by doing that for them which none else can do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Jesus then came, and took bread, and gave them, and fish likewise, and latter favours are to bring to mind former favours, that eaten bread may not be forgotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. This is now the third time that Jesus showed himself to his disciples, after that he was risen from the dead, counting his appearances in order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. So when they had dined, Jesus said to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these, and he said unto him, Yea, Lord, thou knowest that I love thee, and he said unto him, Feed my lambs, for our Lord addressed Peter by his original name, as if he had forfeited that of Peter through denying him, and Peter now answered without professing to love Jesus more than others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He said to him again the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me, and he said, Yea, Lord, thou knowest that I love thee, and he said unto him, Feed my sheep, for we must not be surprised to have our sincerity called into question, when we ourselves have done that which makes it doubtful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. He said unto him the third time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me, and Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me, and he said, Lord, thou knowest all things, thou knowest that I love thee, and Jesus said unto him, Feed my sheep, for every remembrance of past sins, even pardoned sins, renews the sorrow of a true penitent, and Peter solemnly appealed to Christ as knowing all things, even the secrets of his heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Verily, verily, I say unto thee, When thou wast young, thou girdedst thyself, and walkedst whither thou wouldest, but when thou shalt be old, thou shalt stretch forth thy hands, and another shall gird thee, and carry thee whither thou wouldest not, for the sincerity of love to God must be brought to the test, and no one can be qualified to feed Christ's sheep who does not love the good Shepherd more than any earthly advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. This spoke he, signifying by what death he should glorify God, and when he had spoken this, he said unto him, Follow me, for it is the great concern of every good man, whatever death he dies, to glorify God in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Then Peter, turning about, saw the disciple whom Jesus loved following, which also leaned on his breast at supper, and said, Lord, which is he that betrayeth thee, recalling the beloved disciple's place at the table. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Peter seeing him said to Jesus, Lord, and what shall this man do, turning from his own duty to curiosity about another's future. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Jesus said unto him, If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee, follow thou me, for it is Christ's will that his disciples mind their own duty, and not be curious about future events, either as to themselves or others, and many things we are anxious about are nothing to us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Then went this saying abroad among the brethren, that that disciple should not die, yet Jesus said not unto him, He shall not die, but, If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee, for how little are unwritten traditions to be relied upon, and Scripture is its own interpreter, and Scripture language is the safest channel for Scripture truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. This is the disciple which testifies of these things, and wrote these things, and we know that his testimony is true, for the beloved disciple himself wrote this Gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And there are also many other things which Jesus did, the which, if they should be written every one, I suppose that even the world itself could not contain the books that should be written, for only a small part of the actions of Jesus had been written, but enough is recorded to direct our faith and regulate our practice, and we may look forward to a more complete knowledge in heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
