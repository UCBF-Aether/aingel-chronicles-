The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Habakkuk 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The burden which Habakkuk the prophet did see.

2 O LORD, how long shall I cry, and thou wilt not hear! even cry out unto thee of violence, and thou wilt not save!

3 Why dost thou shew me iniquity, and cause me to behold grievance? for spoiling and violence are before me: and there are that raise up strife and contention.

4 Therefore the law is slacked, and judgment doth never go forth: for the wicked doth compass about the righteous; therefore wrong judgment proceedeth.

5 Behold ye among the heathen, and regard, and wonder marvelously: for I will work a work in your days which ye will not believe, though it be told you.

6 For, lo, I raise up the Chaldeans, that bitter and hasty nation, which shall march through the breadth of the land, to possess the dwellingplaces that are not theirs.

7 They are terrible and dreadful: their judgment and their dignity shall proceed of themselves.

8 Their horses also are swifter than the leopards, and are more fierce than the evening wolves: and their horsemen shall spread themselves, and their horsemen shall come from far; they shall fly as the eagle that hasteth to eat.

9 They shall come all for violence: their faces shall sup up as the east wind, and they shall gather the captivity as the sand.

10 And they shall scoff at the kings, and the princes shall be a scorn unto them: they shall deride every strong hold; for they shall heap dust, and take it.

11 Then shall his mind change, and he shall pass over, and offend, imputing this his power unto his god.

12 Art thou not from everlasting, O LORD my God, mine Holy One? we shall not die. O LORD, thou hast ordained them for judgment; and, O mighty God, thou hast established them for correction.

13 Thou art of purer eyes than to behold evil, and canst not look on iniquity: wherefore lookest thou upon them that deal treacherously, and holdest thy tongue when the wicked devoureth the man that is more righteous than he?

14 And makest men as the fishes of the sea, as the creeping things, that have no ruler over them?

15 They take up all of them with the angle, they catch them in their net, and gather them in their drag: therefore they rejoice and are glad.

16 Therefore they sacrifice unto their net, and burn incense unto their drag; because by them their portion is fat, and their meat plenteous.

17 Shall they therefore empty their net, and not spare continually to slay the nations?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The burden Habakkuk saw begins as a complaint: how long shall I cry of violence and God will not save, while spoiling and strife are before me and the law is slacked and wrong judgment proceeds? God's answer startles him: he is raising up the Chaldeans, a bitter and hasty nation marching through the breadth of the land to seize dwellings not theirs, scoffing at kings and laughing at strongholds. The prophet's second complaint follows at once: God is of purer eyes than to behold evil, so why does he look on the treacherous while the wicked devours the man more righteous than himself, catching nations like fish in a net and sacrificing to that net? Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The burden which Habakkuk the prophet did see. The subject of the prophecy, Henry notes, is the destruction of Judah and Jerusalem for the people's sins, and the consolation of the faithful under national calamity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. O LORD, how long shall I cry, and thou wilt not hear, even cry out of violence, and thou wilt not save? The servants of the Lord are deeply afflicted, Henry observes, by seeing ungodliness and violence prevail, especially among those who profess the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Why dost thou show me iniquity and cause me to behold grievance, with spoiling and violence before me and strife and contention raised up? No man scrupled doing wrong to his neighbour, and the prophet longed for the world where holiness and love reign for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Therefore the law is slacked and judgment never goes forth, for the wicked compass the righteous and wrong judgment proceeds. When sentence against evil is not executed speedily, Henry warns, men's hearts become fully set in them to do evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Behold among the heathen and regard and wonder marvellously, for God will work a work in their days which they will not believe though told them. They were to notice what was going forward among the heathen by the Chaldeans, and consider themselves a nation to be scourged by them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. For lo, God raises up the Chaldeans, a bitter and hasty nation marching through the breadth of the land to possess dwellings not theirs. Most men, Henry notes, presume on continued prosperity or that calamities will not come in their days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. They are terrible and dreadful; their judgment and dignity proceed of themselves. The Chaldeans answer to no law but their own will, fierce and cruel and bearing down all before them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Their horses are swifter than leopards and more fierce than evening wolves, and their horsemen spread themselves, coming from far. The invader's speed and ferocity leave no room for escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. They shall come all for violence, their faces supping up as the east wind, gathering captivity as the sand. Nothing before them stands; they sweep up peoples like wind-blown dust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. They shall scoff at kings and princes shall be a scorn to them, deriding every stronghold, for they shall heap dust and take it. Fortress walls are no defence against an army that laughs at kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Then shall his mind change, and he shall pass over and offend, imputing this his power unto his god. It is the common offence of proud people, Henry observes, to take glory to themselves; the closing words of this section still give a glimpse of comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Art thou not from everlasting, O LORD my God, mine Holy One? We shall not die. However matters may be, God is the Lord our God, our Holy One, and the prophet will not entertain hard thoughts of him or of his service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Thou art of purer eyes than to behold evil, and canst not look on iniquity; why then lookest thou on the treacherous and holdest thy tongue when the wicked devours the man more righteous than he? God is holy and does not approve wickedness, Henry insists, and by this principle we must abide even when his providence seems not to agree with it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. And makest men as the fishes of the sea, as creeping things with no ruler over them? Left to the Chaldeans, peoples seem as defenceless as fish with no protector. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. They take up all of them with the angle, catch them in their net, and gather them in their drag, therefore they rejoice and are glad. Some victims are taken one by one, others caught in shoals, as in a net. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Therefore they sacrifice unto their net and burn incense unto their drag, because by them their portion is fat and their meat plenteous. They admire their own cleverness and contrivance, idolising themselves and sacrificing to the net because it is their own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Shall they therefore empty their net and not spare continually to slay the nations? Whatever advantages we possess, Henry concludes, we must give all the glory to God, for God will soon end successful and splendid robberies, and death and judgment shall make predators cease to prey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
