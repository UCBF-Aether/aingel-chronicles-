The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then said Solomon, The LORD hath said that he would dwell in the thick darkness.

2 But I have built an house of habitation for thee, and a place for thy dwelling for ever.

3 And the king turned his face, and blessed the whole congregation of Israel: and all the congregation of Israel stood.

4 And he said, Blessed be the LORD God of Israel, who hath with his hands fulfilled that which he spake with his mouth to my father David, saying,

5 Since the day that I brought forth my people out of the land of Egypt I chose no city among all the tribes of Israel to build an house in, that my name might be there; neither chose I any man to be a ruler over my people Israel:

6 But I have chosen Jerusalem, that my name might be there; and have chosen David to be over my people Israel.

7 Now it was in the heart of David my father to build an house for the name of the LORD God of Israel.

8 But the LORD said to David my father, Forasmuch as it was in thine heart to build an house for my name, thou didst well in that it was in thine heart:

9 Notwithstanding thou shalt not build the house; but thy son which shall come forth out of thy loins, he shall build the house for my name.

10 The LORD therefore hath performed his word that he hath spoken: for I am risen up in the room of David my father, and am set on the throne of Israel, as the LORD promised, and have built the house for the name of the LORD God of Israel.

11 And in it have I put the ark, wherein is the covenant of the LORD, that he made with the children of Israel.

12 And he stood before the altar of the LORD in the presence of all the congregation of Israel, and spread forth his hands:

13 For Solomon had made a brasen scaffold of five cubits long, and five cubits broad, and three cubits high, and had set it in the midst of the court: and upon it he stood, and kneeled down upon his knees before all the congregation of Israel, and spread forth his hands toward heaven.

14 And said, O LORD God of Israel, there is no God like thee in the heaven, nor in the earth; which keepest covenant, and shewest mercy unto thy servants, that walk before thee with all their hearts:

15 Thou which hast kept with thy servant David my father that which thou hast promised him; and spakest with thy mouth, and hast fulfilled it with thine hand, as it is this day.

16 Now therefore, O LORD God of Israel, keep with thy servant David my father that which thou hast promised him, saying, There shall not fail thee a man in my sight to sit upon the throne of Israel; yet so that thy children take heed to their way to walk in my law, as thou hast walked before me.

17 Now then, O LORD God of Israel, let thy word be verified, which thou hast spoken unto thy servant David.

18 But will God in very deed dwell with men on the earth? behold, heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain thee; how much less this house which I have built!

19 Have respect therefore to the prayer of thy servant, and to his supplication, O LORD my God, to hearken unto the cry and the prayer which thy servant prayeth before thee:

20 That thine eyes may be open upon this house day and night, upon the place whereof thou hast said that thou wouldest put thy name there; to hearken unto the prayer which thy servant prayeth toward this place.

21 Hearken therefore unto the supplications of thy servant, and of thy people Israel, which they shall make toward this place: hear thou from thy dwelling place, even from heaven; and when thou hearest, forgive.

22 If a man sin against his neighbour, and an oath be laid upon him to make him swear, and the oath come before thine altar in this house;

23 Then hear thou from heaven, and do, and judge thy servants, by requiting the wicked, by recompensing his way upon his own head; and by justifying the righteous, by giving him according to his righteousness.

24 And if thy people Israel be put to the worse before the enemy, because they have sinned against thee; and shall return and confess thy name, and pray and make supplication before thee in this house;

25 Then hear thou from the heavens, and forgive the sin of thy people Israel, and bring them again unto the land which thou gavest to them and to their fathers.

26 When the heaven is shut up, and there is no rain, because they have sinned against thee; yet if they pray toward this place, and confess thy name, and turn from their sin, when thou dost afflict them;

27 Then hear thou from heaven, and forgive the sin of thy servants, and of thy people Israel, when thou hast taught them the good way, wherein they should walk; and send rain upon thy land, which thou hast given unto thy people for an inheritance.

28 If there be dearth in the land, if there be pestilence, if there be blasting, or mildew, locusts, or caterpillers; if their enemies besiege them in the cities of their land; whatsoever sore or whatsoever sickness there be:

29 Then what prayer or what supplication soever shall be made of any man, or of all thy people Israel, when every one shall know his own sore and his own grief, and shall spread forth his hands in this house:

30 Then hear thou from heaven thy dwelling place, and forgive, and render unto every man according unto all his ways, whose heart thou knowest; (for thou only knowest the hearts of the children of men:)

31 That they may fear thee, to walk in thy ways, so long as they live in the land which thou gavest unto our fathers.

32 Moreover concerning the stranger, which is not of thy people Israel, but is come from a far country for thy great name’s sake, and thy mighty hand, and thy stretched out arm; if they come and pray in this house;

33 Then hear thou from the heavens, even from thy dwelling place, and do according to all that the stranger calleth to thee for; that all people of the earth may know thy name, and fear thee, as doth thy people Israel, and may know that this house which I have built is called by thy name.

34 If thy people go out to war against their enemies by the way that thou shalt send them, and they pray unto thee toward this city which thou hast chosen, and the house which I have built for thy name;

35 Then hear thou from the heavens their prayer and their supplication, and maintain their cause.

36 If they sin against thee, (for there is no man which sinneth not,) and thou be angry with them, and deliver them over before their enemies, and they carry them away captives unto a land far off or near;

37 Yet if they bethink themselves in the land whither they are carried captive, and turn and pray unto thee in the land of their captivity, saying, We have sinned, we have done amiss, and have dealt wickedly;

38 If they return to thee with all their heart and with all their soul in the land of their captivity, whither they have carried them captives, and pray toward their land, which thou gavest unto their fathers, and toward the city which thou hast chosen, and toward the house which I have built for thy name:

39 Then hear thou from the heavens, even from thy dwelling place, their prayer and their supplications, and maintain their cause, and forgive thy people which have sinned against thee.

40 Now, my God, let, I beseech thee, thine eyes be open, and let thine ears be attent unto the prayer that is made in this place.

41 Now therefore arise, O LORD God, into thy resting place, thou, and the ark of thy strength: let thy priests, O LORD God, be clothed with salvation, and let thy saints rejoice in goodness.

42 O LORD God, turn not away the face of thine anointed: remember the mercies of David thy servant.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives Solomon's blessing of the congregation and his long prayer of dedication, asking God to hear from heaven and ending with a plea to remember the mercies promised to David. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then said Solomon, The Lord hath said that he would dwell in the thick darkness. Henry notes the cloud of glory had filled the house, and Solomon improved the opportunity, addressing God as now peculiarly nigh at hand, with the house as the residence of that darkness in which God, who dwells in unapproachable light above, manifests himself below. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. But I have built an house of habitation for thee, and a place for thy dwelling for ever. Henry notes Solomon's highest end was God's glory and honour: the house was for the name of the Lord God of Israel, a house of habitation for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And the king turned his face and blessed the whole congregation of Israel, while all the congregation stood. Henry's chapter outline frames this as Solomon's solemn declaration to the satisfaction of the people and the honour of God, both of whom he blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And he said, Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, who hath with his hands fulfilled what he spake with his mouth to my father David. Henry notes Solomon declared his inducements, which justify and magnify the undertaking, chiefly that he acted in performance of the word God had spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Since the day God brought Israel out of Egypt, he chose no city to build a house in for his name, nor any man to be ruler over Israel. Henry's declaration traces God's choice through history: no city and no ruler was chosen until Jerusalem and David, in God's own time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. But I have chosen Jerusalem, that my name might be there, and have chosen David to be over my people Israel. Henry notes Solomon built in compliance with God's choice of Jerusalem as the city where he would record his name, fit to have a building excelling all the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Now it was in the heart of David my father to build a house for the name of the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes the project was David's, be it known to his honour, and it is no disparagement to pursue the good designs those before us laid and build upon their foundation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. But the Lord said to David, Forasmuch as it was in thine heart to build a house for my name, thou didst well in that it was in thine heart. Henry notes God approved David's intention though he permitted him not to execute it: thou didst well that it was in thy heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Notwithstanding thou shalt not build the house; but thy son which shall come forth out of thy loins, he shall build the house for my name. Henry notes the service was appointed to Solomon and the honour designed him by divine promise, so he did not act of his own head but was called of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The Lord therefore hath performed his word: I am risen up in the room of David, set on the throne of Israel as the Lord promised, and have built the house for the name of the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes those who see their call clear may go on with great satisfaction, for he who appoints the work appoints the workmen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And in it have I put the ark, wherein is the covenant of the Lord, which he made with the children of Israel. Henry's dedication deed is sealed here: the temple was appropriated to God's honour and service, with the ark of the covenant at its heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And he stood before the altar of the Lord in the presence of all the congregation, and spread forth his hands. Henry notes Solomon had signed and sealed the deed of dedication, and now prayed the consecration prayer, by which the temple became a figure of Christ, the great Mediator through whom we offer all prayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. For Solomon had made a bronze scaffold five cubits long, five broad and three high, set in the midst of the court; upon it he stood, kneeled down before all Israel, and spread forth his hands toward heaven. Henry's prayer posture is the same: standing before the altar, then kneeling with hands spread toward heaven, in the congregation's sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And said, O Lord God of Israel, there is no God like thee in heaven nor in earth, who keepest covenant and showest mercy to thy servants who walk before thee with all their hearts. Henry lays this down as doctrine: the God of Israel is of incomparable perfection, with no peer, and those who walk before him with all their hearts shall find him faithful and kind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Thou which hast kept with thy servant David my father what thou promisedst him, speaking with thy mouth and fulfilling with thine hand, as it is this day. Henry's doctrine applies: God is true to every word spoken, and his servants in sincerity shall find him as good as his word and better. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Now therefore, O Lord God of Israel, keep with David what thou promisedst, that there shall not fail a man to sit on Israel's throne, yet so that thy children take heed to walk in my law, as thou hast walked before me. Henry notes the prayer is warranted by the word: the prayer that will speed must be warranted by God's promise, here conditioned on walking in his law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Now then, O Lord God of Israel, let thy word be verified, which thou hast spoken unto thy servant David. Henry notes Solomon pleads the promise itself, that God's word be verified, as the ground of the whole dedication prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. But will God in very deed dwell with men on the earth? Behold, heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain thee; how much less this house which I have built! Henry lays this down as doctrine: God is infinite and immense, whom heaven of heavens cannot contain, and to whose felicity nothing is added by our utmost service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Have respect therefore to the prayer of thy servant, and to his supplication, O Lord my God, to hearken unto the cry and prayer thy servant prayeth before thee. Henry notes Solomon asked not that God help them whether they prayed or not, but that he help them in answer to their prayers, even as Christ's intercessions encourage rather than supersede our supplications. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. That thine eyes may be open upon this house day and night, upon the place whereof thou hast said thou wouldest put thy name there, to hearken unto the prayer thy servant prayeth toward this place. Henry notes this petition is pertinent because God himself had said he would put his name there; the prayer that will speed must be warranted by the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Hearken therefore unto the supplications of thy servant and thy people Israel, which they shall make toward this place: hear thou from thy dwelling place, even from heaven; and when thou hearest, forgive. Henry notes heaven is God's dwelling place still, not the temple, and forgiveness of sins makes way for all other answers to prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. If a man sin against his neighbour, and an oath be laid upon him to make him swear, and the oath come before thine altar in this house. Henry notes Solomon supposed controversies would be settled by appeal to God, laying an oath on the person whose testimony must decide, in reverence for an oath as old as conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Then hear thou from heaven, and do, and judge thy servants, by requiting the wicked and justifying the righteous according to his righteousness. Henry notes this petition asks God to give judgment according to equity on all appeals made to him, for God sits on the throne judging right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And if thy people Israel be put to the worse before the enemy, because they have sinned against thee, and shall return and confess thy name, and pray and make supplication before thee in this house. Henry notes Solomon expected troublesome times to come through sin, and supposed that in affliction those who had not called on God would seek him early and earnestly, confessing name and sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Then hear thou from the heavens, and forgive the sin of thy people Israel, and bring them again unto the land thou gavest to them and their fathers. Henry notes this petition asks God to return in mercy when his people repent, reform and seek him, building on God's repeated declarations of readiness to accept penitents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. When heaven is shut up and there is no rain, because they have sinned against thee, yet if they pray toward this place, and confess thy name, and turn from their sin when thou dost afflict them. Henry notes the same supposition: trouble drives to God those who said Depart, and in distress they will confess and turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Then hear thou from heaven, and forgive the sin of thy servants and thy people Israel, when thou hast taught them the good way wherein they should walk, and send rain upon thy land. Henry notes the petition pairs forgiveness with teaching the good way and restoring the land's rain, mercy following repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. If there be dearth, pestilence, blasting, mildew, locusts or caterpillars, if enemies besiege them, whatsoever sore or sickness there be. Henry notes Solomon supposed affliction in many forms would drive Israel to seek God, listing the troubles that would move a people to prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Then what prayer or supplication soever shall be made of any man, or of all thy people Israel, when every one shall know his own sore and his own grief, and shall spread forth his hands in this house. Henry's affliction case applies: each person knowing his own sore spreads his hands in prayer, and trouble teaches the prayer that prosperity did not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Then hear thou from heaven thy dwelling place, and forgive, and render unto every man according unto all his ways, whose heart thou knowest; for thou only knowest the hearts of the children of men. Henry lays this down as doctrine: God only knows hearts, all thoughts and affections naked before him, and he renders to every man according to his ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. That they may fear thee, to walk in thy ways, so long as they live in the land thou gavest unto our fathers. Henry's purpose of forgiveness applies: mercy received should lead to fearing God and walking in his ways in the land of promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Moreover concerning the stranger, not of thy people Israel, but come from a far country for thy great name's sake and thy mighty hand and stretched out arm, if they come and pray in this house. Henry notes Solomon supposed strangers would come from afar to worship Israel's God, considering how worthless the nations' gods were and what proofs Israel's God had given of being Lord of the whole earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Then hear thou from the heavens, even from thy dwelling place, and do according to all the stranger calleth to thee for, that all people of the earth may know thy name and fear thee, as Israel does, and may know this house is called by thy name. Henry notes the petition bids strangers welcome, for if there be one law in duty, why not one law in privilege for stranger and native-born? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. If thy people go out to war against their enemies by the way thou shalt send them, and they pray toward this city thou hast chosen and the house I have built for thy name. Henry notes Solomon prayed God would on all occasions own and plead Israel's cause against opposers, when they went out by his sending. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Then hear thou from the heavens their prayer and supplication, and maintain their cause. Henry notes this petition, repeated in verse 39, asks God to maintain his people's cause, for if they are the Israel of God, their cause is God's cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. If they sin against thee, for there is no man which sinneth not, and thou be angry and deliver them over before their enemies, and they carry them away captives unto a land far off or near. Henry lays this down as doctrine: there is no sinless perfection in this life, no man who does good and sins not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Yet if they bethink themselves in the land of captivity, and turn and pray unto thee there, saying, We have sinned, we have done amiss, and have dealt wickedly. Henry's repentance petition applies: in captivity itself, bethinking and confessing sin is the turning God waits for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. If they return to thee with all their heart and soul in the land of captivity, and pray toward their land, the city thou hast chosen, and the house I have built for thy name. Henry notes the petition asks God to return in mercy when his people return with all heart and soul and pray toward the chosen place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Then hear thou from the heavens, even from thy dwelling place, their prayer and supplications, and maintain their cause, and forgive thy people which have sinned against thee. Henry notes this combines the petitions: maintain their cause and forgive, for forgiveness makes way for every other answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Now, my God, let thine eyes be open, and let thine ears be attent unto the prayer made in this place. Henry's opening petition returns as the prayer's close: God's eyes open on the house day and night, and his ears attentive to prayer made there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Now therefore arise, O Lord God, into thy resting place, thou, and the ark of thy strength: let thy priests be clothed with salvation, and let thy saints rejoice in goodness. Henry notes Solomon borrowed these words from his father's psalm: that God take and keep possession of the temple as his resting place, that ministers be made public blessings clothed with salvation, and that saints rejoice in the goodness of God's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. O Lord God, turn not away the face of thine anointed: remember the mercies of David thy servant. Henry notes Solomon pleads his relation as God's anointed and God's covenant mercies to David, and we may plead the same with an eye to Christ, God's anointed, whose face is never turned away, and whose mercies are the sure mercies of David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

And if thy people Israel be put to the worse before the enemy, because they have sinned against thee, and shall return and confess thy name, and pray and make supplication before thee in this house. Henry notes Solomon expected troublesome times to come through sin, and supposed that in affliction those who had not called on God would seek him early and earnestly, confessing name and sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Then hear thou from the heavens, and forgive the sin of thy people Israel, and bring them again unto the land thou gavest to them and their fathers. Henry notes this petition asks God to return in mercy when his people repent, reform and seek him, building on God's repeated declarations of readiness to accept penitents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. When heaven is shut up and there is no rain, because they have sinned against thee, yet if they pray toward this place, and confess thy name, and turn from their sin when thou dost afflict them. Henry notes the same supposition: trouble drives to God those who said Depart, and in distress they will confess and turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Then hear thou from heaven, and forgive the sin of thy servants and thy people Israel, when thou hast taught them the good way wherein they should walk, and send rain upon thy land. Henry notes the petition pairs forgiveness with teaching the good way and restoring the land's rain, mercy following repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. If there be dearth, pestilence, blasting, mildew, locusts or caterpillars, if enemies besiege them, whatsoever sore or sickness there be. Henry notes Solomon supposed affliction in many forms would drive Israel to seek God, listing the troubles that would move a people to prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Then what prayer or supplication soever shall be made of any man, or of all thy people Israel, when every one shall know his own sore and his own grief, and shall spread forth his hands in this house. Henry's affliction case applies: each person knowing his own sore spreads his hands in prayer, and trouble teaches the prayer that prosperity did not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Then hear thou from heaven thy dwelling place, and forgive, and render unto every man according unto all his ways, whose heart thou knowest; for thou only knowest the hearts of the children of men. Henry lays this down as doctrine: God only knows hearts, all thoughts and affections naked before him, and he renders to every man according to his ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. That they may fear thee, to walk in thy ways, so long as they live in the land thou gavest unto our fathers. Henry's purpose of forgiveness applies: mercy received should lead to fearing God and walking in his ways in the land of promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Moreover concerning the stranger, not of thy people Israel, but come from a far country for thy great name's sake and thy mighty hand and stretched out arm, if they come and pray in this house. Henry notes Solomon supposed strangers would come from afar to worship Israel's God, considering how worthless the nations' gods were and what proofs Israel's God had given of being Lord of the whole earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Then hear thou from the heavens, even from thy dwelling place, and do according to all the stranger calleth to thee for, that all people of the earth may know thy name and fear thee, as Israel does, and may know this house is called by thy name. Henry notes the petition bids strangers welcome, for if there be one law in duty, why not one law in privilege for stranger and native-born? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. If thy people go out to war against their enemies by the way thou shalt send them, and they pray toward this city thou hast chosen and the house I have built for thy name. Henry notes Solomon prayed God would on all occasions own and plead Israel's cause against opposers, when they went out by his sending. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Then hear thou from the heavens their prayer and supplication, and maintain their cause. Henry notes this petition, repeated in verse 39, asks God to maintain his people's cause, for if they are the Israel of God, their cause is God's cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. If they sin against thee, for there is no man which sinneth not, and thou be angry and deliver them over before their enemies, and they carry them away captives unto a land far off or near. Henry lays this down as doctrine: there is no sinless perfection in this life, no man who does good and sins not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Yet if they bethink themselves in the land of captivity, and turn and pray unto thee there, saying, We have sinned, we have done amiss, and have dealt wickedly. Henry's repentance petition applies: in captivity itself, bethinking and confessing sin is the turning God waits for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. If they return to thee with all their heart and soul in the land of captivity, and pray toward their land, the city thou hast chosen, and the house I have built for thy name. Henry notes the petition asks God to return in mercy when his people return with all heart and soul and pray toward the chosen place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Then hear thou from the heavens, even from thy dwelling place, their prayer and supplications, and maintain their cause, and forgive thy people which have sinned against thee. Henry notes this combines the petitions: maintain their cause and forgive, for forgiveness makes way for every other answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Now, my God, let thine eyes be open, and let thine ears be attent unto the prayer made in this place. Henry's opening petition returns as the prayer's close: God's eyes open on the house day and night, and his ears attentive to prayer made there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Now therefore arise, O Lord God, into thy resting place, thou, and the ark of thy strength: let thy priests be clothed with salvation, and let thy saints rejoice in goodness. Henry notes Solomon borrowed these words from his father's psalm: that God take and keep possession of the temple as his resting place, that ministers be made public blessings clothed with salvation, and that saints rejoice in the goodness of God's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. O Lord God, turn not away the face of thine anointed: remember the mercies of David thy servant. Henry notes Solomon pleads his relation as God's anointed and God's covenant mercies to David, and we may plead the same with an eye to Christ, God's anointed, whose face is never turned away, and whose mercies are the sure mercies of David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
