The Text — Sirach 19 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 A labouring man that A is given to drunkenness shall not be rich: and he that contemneth small things shall fall by little and little.

2 Wine and women will make men of understanding to fall away: and he that cleaveth to harlots will become impudent.

3 Moths and worms shall have him to heritage, and a bold man shall be taken away.

4 He that is hasty to give credit is lightminded; and he that sinneth shall offend against his own soul.

5 Whoso taketh pleasure in wickedness shall be condemned: but he that resisteth pleasures crowneth his life.

6 He that can rule his tongue shall live without strife; and he that hateth babbling shall have less evil.

7 Rehearse not unto another that which is told unto thee, and thou shalt fare never the worse.

8 Whether it be to friend or foe, talk not of other men's lives; and if thou canst without offence, reveal them not.

9 For he heard and observed thee, and when time cometh he will hate thee.

10 If thou hast heard a word, let it die with thee; and be bold, it will not burst thee.

11 A fool travaileth with a word, as a woman in labour of a child.

12 As an arrow that sticketh in a man's thigh, so is a word within a fool's belly.

13 Admonish a friend, it may be he hath not done it: and if he have done it, that he do it no more.

14 Admonish thy friend, it may be he hath not said it: and if he have, that he speak it not again.

15 Admonish a friend: for many times it is a slander, and believe not every tale.

16 There is one that slippeth in his speech, but not from his heart; and who is he that hath not offended with his tongue?

17 Admonish thy neighbour before thou threaten him; and not being angry, give place to the law of the most High.

18 The fear of the Lord is the first step to be accepted [of him,] and wisdom obtaineth his love.

19 The knowledge of the commandments of the Lord is the doctrine of life: and they that do things that please him shall receive the fruit of the tree of immortality.

20 The fear of the Lord is all wisdom; and in all wisdom is the performance of the law, and the knowledge of his omnipotency.

21 If a servant say to his master, I will not do as it pleaseth thee; though afterward he do it, he angereth him that nourisheth him.

22 The knowledge of wickedness is not wisdom, neither at any time the counsel of sinners prudence.

23 There is a wickedness, and the same an abomination; and there is a fool wanting in wisdom.

24 He that hath small understanding, and feareth God, is better than one that hath much wisdom, and transgresseth the law of the most High.

25 There is an exquisite subtilty, and the same is unjust; and there is one that turneth aside to make judgment appear; and there is a wise man that justifieth in judgment.

26 There is a wicked man that hangeth down his head sadly; but inwardly he is full of deceit,

27 Casting down his countenance, and making as if he heard not: where he is not known, he will do thee a mischief before thou be aware.

28 And if for want of power he be hindered from sinning, yet when he findeth opportunity he will do evil.

29 A man may be known by his look, and one that hath understanding by his countenance, when thou meetest him.

30 A man's attire, and excessive laughter, and gait, shew what he is.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A labouring man given to drunkenness will not grow rich, and one who despises small things falls by little and little. The chapter distinguishes a slip of speech from a slip of heart, and closes by noting that attire, excessive laughter, and gait together show what a man is. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. A labouring man given to drunkenness will not be rich, and he who contemns small things will fall by little and little. Drunken labour cannot grow rich, and despising small things leads to falling gradually, little by little. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Wine and women will make men of understanding fall away, and he who cleaves to harlots will become impudent. Wine and women cause even understanding men to fall, and clinging to harlots breeds impudence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. Moths and worms will have him for heritage, and a bold man will be taken away. The end of such a man is moths and worms for heritage, and boldness itself is taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. He who is hasty to give credit is lightminded, and he who sins will offend against his own soul. Hasty belief is lightmindedness, and sin offends first against the sinner's own soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Whoever takes pleasure in wickedness will be condemned, but he who resists pleasures crowns his life. Pleasure in wickedness brings condemnation, while resisting pleasures crowns a life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. He who can rule his tongue will live without strife, and he who hates babbling will have less evil. A ruled tongue means life without strife, and hatred of babbling means less evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. Do not rehearse to another what is told to you, and you will fare never the worse. Keeping told things unrepeated is the way to fare no worse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Whether to friend or foe, do not talk of other men's lives, and if you can without offence, reveal them not. Other men's lives should not be talked of to friend or foe, and where silence is possible without offence, they should not be revealed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. For he heard and observed you, and when time comes he will hate you. The tale-bearer is heard and watched, and in time will be hated for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. If you have heard a word, let it die with you, and be bold, it will not burst you. A heard word should die with its hearer; kept silence will not burst a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. A fool travails with a word, as a woman in labour of a child. A fool labours over a word as a woman labours to bring forth a child, unable to keep it in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. As an arrow sticking in a man's thigh, so is a word within a fool's belly. A word in a fool's belly pains like an arrow in the thigh until it is out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. Admonish a friend; it may be he has not done it, and if he has, that he do it no more. Admonishing a friend rests on uncertainty: perhaps he did not do it, and if he did, admonition aims at stopping it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. Admonish your friend; it may be he has not said it, and if he has, that he speak it not again. The same rule holds for speech: admonish in case he did not say it, or to stop its repetition if he did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. Admonish a friend, for many times it is a slander, and do not believe every tale. Admonition is needed because reports are often slander, and not every tale should be believed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. There is one who slips in his speech, but not from his heart, and who is he who has not offended with his tongue? A slip of speech may not come from the heart, and no one has never offended with the tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. Admonish your neighbour before you threaten him, and not being angry, give place to the law of the Most High. Admonition should come before threat, without anger, leaving room for God's law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. The fear of the Lord is the first step to be accepted by him, and wisdom obtains his love. Fear of God is the first step toward acceptance, and wisdom obtains his love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. The knowledge of the commandments of the Lord is the doctrine of life, and those who do things that please him will receive the fruit of the tree of immortality. Knowledge of God's commandments is life's doctrine, and doing what pleases him brings the fruit of immortality's tree. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 20. The fear of the Lord is all wisdom, and in all wisdom is the performance of the law and the knowledge of his omnipotency. Fear of God is the whole of wisdom, and wisdom itself is law-keeping and knowledge of God's almighty power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 21. If a servant says to his master, I will not do as it pleases you, yet afterward he does it, he angers him who nourishes him. Even a servant who finally obeys angers his master by first refusing, since the master nourishes him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 22. The knowledge of wickedness is not wisdom, nor at any time is the counsel of sinners prudence. Knowing wickedness is not wisdom, and sinners' counsel is never prudence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 23. There is a wickedness, and the same is an abomination, and there is a fool wanting in wisdom. Some wickedness is outright abomination, and some folly is mere want of wisdom; the two must be distinguished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 24. He who has small understanding and fears God is better than one who has much wisdom and transgresses the law of the Most High. A little understanding with fear of God outvalues much wisdom joined to transgression. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 25. There is an exquisite subtlety, and the same is unjust, and there is one who turns aside to make judgment appear, and there is a wise man who justifies in judgment. Subtlety can be unjust, twisting judgment's appearance, while the truly wise man justifies rightly in judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 26. There is a wicked man who hangs down his head sadly, but inwardly he is full of deceit. A sad, bowed head can hide inward deceit in a wicked man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 27. Casting down his countenance and making as if he heard not, where he is not known he will do you a mischief before you are aware. The deceiver's downcast, deaf-seeming manner covers mischief done unawares where he is unknown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 28. If for want of power he is hindered from sinning, yet when he finds opportunity he will do evil. Hindrance by lack of power is not virtue; given opportunity, such a man will do evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 29. A man may be known by his look, and one who has understanding by his countenance, when you meet him. A man's look reveals him, and an understanding man's countenance shows itself at meeting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 30. A man's attire, excessive laughter, and gait show what he is. Dress, laughter, and walk together reveal a man's character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
