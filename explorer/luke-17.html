The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then said he unto the disciples, It is impossible but that offences will come: but woe unto him, through whom they come!

2 It were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and he cast into the sea, than that he should offend one of these little ones.

3 Take heed to yourselves: If thy brother trespass against thee, rebuke him; and if he repent, forgive him.

4 And if he trespass against thee seven times in a day, and seven times in a day turn again to thee, saying, I repent; thou shalt forgive him.

5 And the apostles said unto the Lord, Increase our faith.

6 And the Lord said, If ye had faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye might say unto this sycamine tree, Be thou plucked up by the root, and be thou planted in the sea; and it should obey you.

7 But which of you, having a servant plowing or feeding cattle, will say unto him by and by, when he is come from the field, Go and sit down to meat?

8 And will not rather say unto him, Make ready wherewith I may sup, and gird thyself, and serve me, till I have eaten and drunken; and afterward thou shalt eat and drink?

9 Doth he thank that servant because he did the things that were commanded him? I trow not.

10 So likewise ye, when ye shall have done all those things which are commanded you, say, We are unprofitable servants: we have done that which was our duty to do.

11 And it came to pass, as he went to Jerusalem, that he passed through the midst of Samaria and Galilee.

12 And as he entered into a certain village, there met him ten men that were lepers, which stood afar off:

13 And they lifted up their voices, and said, Jesus, Master, have mercy on us.

14 And when he saw them, he said unto them, Go shew yourselves unto the priests. And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed.

15 And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God,

16 And fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan.

17 And Jesus answering said, Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?

18 There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger.

19 And he said unto him, Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole.

20 And when he was demanded of the Pharisees, when the kingdom of God should come, he answered them and said, The kingdom of God cometh not with observation:

21 Neither shall they say, Lo here! or, lo there! for, behold, the kingdom of God is within you.

22 And he said unto the disciples, The days will come, when ye shall desire to see one of the days of the Son of man, and ye shall not see it.

23 And they shall say to you, See here; or, see there: go not after them, nor follow them.

24 For as the lightning, that lighteneth out of the one part under heaven, shineth unto the other part under heaven; so shall also the Son of man be in his day.

25 But first must he suffer many things, and be rejected of this generation.

26 And as it was in the days of Noe, so shall it be also in the days of the Son of man.

27 They did eat, they drank, they married wives, they were given in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, and the flood came, and destroyed them all.

28 Likewise also as it was in the days of Lot; they did eat, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded;

29 But the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all.

30 Even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed.

31 In that day, he which shall be upon the housetop, and his stuff in the house, let him not come down to take it away: and he that is in the field, let him likewise not return back.

32 Remember Lot’s wife.

33 Whosoever shall seek to save his life shall lose it; and whosoever shall lose his life shall preserve it.

34 I tell you, in that night there shall be two men in one bed; the one shall be taken, and the other shall be left.

35 Two women shall be grinding together; the one shall be taken, and the other left.

36 Two men shall be in the field; the one shall be taken, and the other left.

37 And they answered and said unto him, Where, Lord? And he said unto them, Wheresoever the body is, thither will the eagles be gathered together.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Offences, forgiveness seven times a day, and faith like a grain of mustard seed open the chapter, with the unprofitable servant who has only done his duty (vv. 1–10). Ten lepers are cleansed on the way to Jerusalem, but only one, a Samaritan, returns to give thanks (vv. 11–19). Asked when the kingdom of God comes, Jesus says it comes not with observation — it is within you — and describes the days of the Son of man as sudden as lightning and as Noah’s flood, warning that whoever seeks to save his life will lose it (vv. 20–37). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 17

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then said he unto the disciples, It is impossible but that offences will come, but woe unto him through whom they come! Henry notes it is no abatement of guilt, nor lessening of punishment, that offences will come; inevitability does not excuse the offender. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. It were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and he cast into the sea, than that he should offend one of these little ones. Drowning with a millstone is better than causing a little one to stumble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Take heed to yourselves: If thy brother trespass against thee, rebuke him, and if he repent, forgive him. Rebuke and forgiveness are paired duties, not alternatives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. If he trespass against thee seven times in a day, and seven times turn again saying, I repent, thou shalt forgive him. Henry notes faith in God's pardoning mercy enables us to get over the greatest difficulties in forgiving brethren, even sevenfold in a day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And the apostles said unto the Lord, Increase our faith. Forgiving seven times a day drives the apostles to pray for more faith, not for easier brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. If ye had faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye might say unto this sycamine tree, Be thou plucked up by the root, and be thou planted in the sea, and it should obey you. Henry notes as with God nothing is impossible, so all things are possible to him that can believe, and small faith, truly placed, moves what is rooted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. But which of you, having a servant plowing or feeding cattle, will say unto him when he comes from the field, Go and sit down to meat? The servant's field work does not earn immediate table service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And will not rather say, Make ready wherewith I may sup, and gird thyself, and serve me, till I have eaten and drunken, and afterward thou shalt eat and drink? Service continues after labour, without complaint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Does he thank that servant because he did the things commanded him? I trow not. Obedience commanded earns no special thanks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. So likewise ye, when ye shall have done all commanded you, say, We are unprofitable servants: we have done that which was our duty to do. Henry notes the Lord has such a property in every creature as no man can have in another; he cannot be in debt for services, nor do they deserve return from him, so deep humility is the disciples' need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. As he went to Jerusalem, he passed through the midst of Samaria and Galilee. The journey to Jerusalem passes through border country, where the next healing will find a Samaritan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. As he entered a certain village, there met him ten men that were lepers, which stood afar off. Leprosy keeps them at a distance, even from the healer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. They lifted up their voices, saying, Jesus, Master, have mercy on us. Henry notes a sense of spiritual leprosy should make us very humble when we draw near to Christ, and it is enough to refer ourselves to his compassions, for they fail not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. When he saw them, he said, Go show yourselves unto the priests. And it came to pass, as they went, they were cleansed. Henry notes we may look for God to meet us with mercy when found in the way of obedience; cleansing comes on the way, not before it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. One of them, when he saw he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God. The one who turns back does so loudly, glorifying God, not quietly slipping away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks, and he was a Samaritan. Henry notes only one returned to give thanks, and Christ noticed the one who distinguished himself: he was a Samaritan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Jesus answering said, Were there not ten cleansed? But where are the nine? The question counts the absent, not just the present. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger. The stranger alone gives glory, while the nine keep only the outward cure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And he said unto him, Arise, go thy way: thy faith has made thee whole. Henry notes the others only got the outward cure, he alone got the spiritual blessing, and it becomes us to be very humble in thanksgivings as well as in prayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. When demanded of the Pharisees when the kingdom of God should come, he answered, The kingdom of God comes not with observation. A date and a spectacle are refused as the kingdom's marks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Neither shall they say, Lo here! or, lo there! For behold, the kingdom of God is within you. Henry notes the kingdom was among the Jews, or rather within some of them, a spiritual kingdom set up in the heart by divine grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And he said unto the disciples, The days will come, when ye shall desire to see one of the days of the Son of man, and ye shall not see it. Desire for his days will outrun their presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And they shall say to you, See here, or, see there: go not after them, nor follow them. False locations of the Son of man are to be ignored, not investigated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. For as lightning, lightening out of one part under heaven, shines unto the other part under heaven, so shall also the Son of man be in his day. Lightning, not a rumour, is the image of his coming: sudden, visible, universal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. But first must he suffer many things, and be rejected of this generation. Before revelation comes rejection, and suffering precedes glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And as it was in the days of Noah, so shall it be also in the days of the Son of man. Noah's days become the pattern for the Son of man's days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. They did eat, drank, married wives, were given in marriage, until the day Noah entered the ark, and the flood came and destroyed them all. Ordinary life continued until the flood, and Henry notes sinners of every age go on securely in evil ways, remembering not their latter end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Likewise also as it was in the days of Lot: they did eat, drank, bought, sold, planted, builded. Lot's days add commerce and building to eating and marrying, all ordinary, all uninterrupted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. But the same day Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all. Deliverance and destruction fall on the same day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed. Henry notes what a dreadful surprise destruction will be to the secure and sensual, and thus shall it be when the Son of man is revealed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. In that day, he which shall be upon the housetop, and his stuff in the house, let him not come down to take it away, and he that is in the field, let him likewise not return back. Possessions must be abandoned without retrieval when the day comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Remember Lot's wife. Two words sum the warning: looking back is fatal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Whosoever shall seek to save his life shall lose it, and whosoever shall lose his life shall preserve it. Saving and losing are again reversed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. I tell you, in that night there shall be two men in one bed; the one shall be taken, and the other shall be left. The closest companionship does not guarantee the same fate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Two women shall be grinding together; the one shall be taken, and the other left. Shared labour, like shared bed, divides at the revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Two men shall be in the field; the one shall be taken, and the other left. Field, mill, and bed: every ordinary scene becomes a scene of separation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. And they answered, Where, Lord? And he said, Wheresoever the body is, thither will the eagles be gathered together. Henry notes wherever the wicked are, marked for eternal ruin, they shall be found by God's judgments, as eagles find the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
