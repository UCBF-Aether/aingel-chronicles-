The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 25 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover David and the captains of the host separated to the service of the sons of Asaph, and of Heman, and of Jeduthun, who should prophesy with harps, with psalteries, and with cymbals: and the number of the workmen according to their service was:

2 Of the sons of Asaph; Zaccur, and Joseph, and Nethaniah, and Asarelah, the sons of Asaph under the hands of Asaph, which prophesied according to the order of the king.

3 Of Jeduthun: the sons of Jeduthun; Gedaliah, and Zeri, and Jeshaiah, Hashabiah, and Mattithiah, six, under the hands of their father Jeduthun, who prophesied with a harp, to give thanks and to praise the LORD.

4 Of Heman: the sons of Heman: Bukkiah, Mattaniah, Uzziel, Shebuel, and Jerimoth, Hananiah, Hanani, Eliathah, Giddalti, and Romamtiezer, Joshbekashah, Mallothi, Hothir, and Mahazioth:

5 All these were the sons of Heman the king’s seer in the words of God, to lift up the horn. And God gave to Heman fourteen sons and three daughters.

6 All these were under the hands of their father for song in the house of the LORD, with cymbals, psalteries, and harps, for the service of the house of God, according to the king’s order to Asaph, Jeduthun, and Heman.

7 So the number of them, with their brethren that were instructed in the songs of the LORD, even all that were cunning, was two hundred fourscore and eight.

8 And they cast lots, ward against ward, as well the small as the great, the teacher as the scholar.

9 Now the first lot came forth for Asaph to Joseph: the second to Gedaliah, who with his brethren and sons were twelve:

10 The third to Zaccur, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

11 The fourth to Izri, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

12 The fifth to Nethaniah, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

13 The sixth to Bukkiah, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

14 The seventh to Jesharelah, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

15 The eighth to Jeshaiah, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

16 The ninth to Mattaniah, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

17 The tenth to Shimei, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

18 The eleventh to Azareel, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

19 The twelfth to Hashabiah, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

20 The thirteenth to Shubael, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

21 The fourteenth to Mattithiah, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

22 The fifteenth to Jeremoth, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

23 The sixteenth to Hananiah, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

24 The seventeenth to Joshbekashah, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

25 The eighteenth to Hanani, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

26 The nineteenth to Mallothi, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

27 The twentieth to Eliathah, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

28 The one and twentieth to Hothir, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

29 The two and twentieth to Giddalti, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

30 The three and twentieth to Mahazioth, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve:

31 The four and twentieth to Romamtiezer, he, his sons, and his brethren, were twelve.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter lists the musicians — the sons of Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun — divided by lot into twenty-four courses to prophesy with harps, psalteries, and cymbals. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Moreover David and the captains of the host separated to the service of the sons of Asaph, and of Heman, and of Jeduthun, who should prophesy with harps, with psalteries, and with cymbals. Henry notes singing God's praises is called prophesying, since the psalms were composed by prophets and intended for the church's edification, and it is called service and its singers workmen, because praise must be made a business. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Of the sons of Asaph: Zaccur, Joseph, Nethaniah, and Asarelah, under the hands of their father Asaph, which prophesied according to the order of the king. Henry notes fathers presided and children were under their father's hand, an example for parents to train children and juniors to submit to elders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Of Jeduthun: the sons of Jeduthun, Gedaliah, Zeri, Jeshaiah, Hashabiah, and Mattithiah, six, under their father Jeduthun, who prophesied with a harp, to give thanks and to praise the Lord. Henry notes the glory and honour of God were principally intended in all temple music, to give thanks and praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Of Heman: the sons of Heman, Bukkiah, Mattaniah, Uzziel, Shebuel, Jerimoth, Hananiah, Hanani, Eliathah, Giddalti, Romamti-ezer, Joshbekashah, Mallothi, Hothir, and Mahazioth. Henry counts Heman's fourteen sons among the twenty-four qualified heads, with Asaph's four and Jeduthun's six. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. All these were the sons of Heman the king's seer in the words of God, to lift up the horn. And God gave to Heman fourteen sons and three daughters. Henry notes Heman alone is called the king's seer, and one lifted up the horn, using wind-music among harps, psalteries, and cymbals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. All these were under the hands of their father for song in the house of the Lord, with cymbals, psalteries, and harps, for the service of the house of God, according to the king's order unto Asaph, Jeduthun, and Heman. Henry notes David acted as prophet in ordering divine institutions, an example to authority to promote religion, and that psalmody's intention is melody with the heart unto the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. So the number of them, with their brethren that were instructed in the songs of the Lord, even all that were cunning, was two hundred and fourscore and eight. Henry notes 288 skilful Levites were a competent number, yet small beside the 4,000 appointed to praise, the rest probably following as these led or presiding in country congregations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And they cast lots, ward against ward, as well the small as the great, the teacher as the scholar. Henry notes the lot was thrown impartially, small and great, teacher and scholar alike, to prevent strife for precedency, referring order to God rather than age or standing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
