The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — John 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 There was a man of the Pharisees, named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews:

2 The same came to Jesus by night, and said unto him, Rabbi, we know that thou art a teacher come from God: for no man can do these miracles that thou doest, except God be with him.

3 Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.

4 Nicodemus saith unto him, How can a man be born when he is old? can he enter the second time into his mother’s womb, and be born?

5 Jesus answered, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.

6 That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.

7 Marvel not that I said unto thee, Ye must be born again.

8 The wind bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit.

9 Nicodemus answered and said unto him, How can these things be?

10 Jesus answered and said unto him, Art thou a master of Israel, and knowest not these things?

11 Verily, verily, I say unto thee, We speak that we do know, and testify that we have seen; and ye receive not our witness.

12 If I have told you earthly things, and ye believe not, how shall ye believe, if I tell you of heavenly things?

13 And no man hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man which is in heaven.

14 And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up:

15 That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.

16 For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

17 For God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world; but that the world through him might be saved.

18 He that believeth on him is not condemned: but he that believeth not is condemned already, because he hath not believed in the name of the only begotten Son of God.

19 And this is the condemnation, that light is come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil.

20 For every one that doeth evil hateth the light, neither cometh to the light, lest his deeds should be reproved.

21 But he that doeth truth cometh to the light, that his deeds may be made manifest, that they are wrought in God.

22 After these things came Jesus and his disciples into the land of Judaea; and there he tarried with them, and baptized.

23 And John also was baptizing in Aenon near to Salim, because there was much water there: and they came, and were baptized.

24 For John was not yet cast into prison.

25 Then there arose a question between some of John’s disciples and the Jews about purifying.

26 And they came unto John, and said unto him, Rabbi, he that was with thee beyond Jordan, to whom thou barest witness, behold, the same baptizeth, and all men come to him.

27 John answered and said, A man can receive nothing, except it be given him from heaven.

28 Ye yourselves bear me witness, that I said, I am not the Christ, but that I am sent before him.

29 He that hath the bride is the bridegroom: but the friend of the bridegroom, which standeth and heareth him, rejoiceth greatly because of the bridegroom’s voice: this my joy therefore is fulfilled.

30 He must increase, but I must decrease.

31 He that cometh from above is above all: he that is of the earth is earthly, and speaketh of the earth: he that cometh from heaven is above all.

32 And what he hath seen and heard, that he testifieth; and no man receiveth his testimony.

33 He that hath received his testimony hath set to his seal that God is true.

34 For he whom God hath sent speaketh the words of God: for God giveth not the Spirit by measure unto him.

35 The Father loveth the Son, and hath given all things into his hand.

36 He that believeth on the Son hath everlasting life: and he that believeth not the Son shall not see life; but the wrath of God abideth on him.

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews, comes to Jesus by night and hears that unless a man is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God, and that the new birth is of water and of the Spirit, like wind blowing where it wills (vv. 1–13). The lifted-up serpent in the wilderness becomes the picture of the Son of man lifted up, “for God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son” (vv. 14–21). The chapter closes with John the Baptist’s last testimony: Jesus must increase, John must decrease, and he that believes on the Son has everlasting life (vv. 22–36). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, John 3

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Nicodemus, a Pharisee and ruler of the Jews, came to Jesus by night, afraid or ashamed to be seen with him, as many are when religion is out of fashion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He confessed that Jesus was a teacher come from God, since no man could do such miracles unless God were with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Jesus answered that unless a man is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God, for a new nature is the beginning of a new life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Nicodemus took the words literally, asking how a grown man could enter his mother's womb a second time, mistaking a spiritual birth for a bodily one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Jesus explained that the new birth is of water and of the Spirit, signified by baptism but wrought by the Spirit, without which no one enters the kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. That which is born of flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit, for a corrupt nature can only reproduce itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Nicodemus was not to marvel at the necessity, for God's holiness, our depravity, and the happiness set before us make the change no strange thing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The wind blows where it pleases and its sound is heard though its path is unseen; so is everyone born of the Spirit, whose causes are hidden but whose effects are plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Nicodemus still asked how these things could be, acknowledging his ignorance, which shows at least a desire to be better informed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jesus answered that a master of Israel ought to know these things, for the Old Testament itself taught a circumcised heart and a new spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He spoke of what he knew and testified of what he had seen, yet his testimony was not received by those who make spiritual things strange to themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. If earthly illustrations are not believed, heavenly things will be less so, and faith must receive what reason alone cannot prove. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. No man has ascended to heaven except he who came down from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven, revealing the Father's will from above. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of man be lifted up, that sinners stung by sin may look and live. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life, for the remedy is applied simply by looking in faith to Christ crucified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. God so loved the world that he gave his only begotten Son, a love so real and rich toward a worthless world that it should make us wonder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. God sent his Son not to condemn the world but that the world through him might be saved, for there is no salvation in any other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Whoever believes in him is not condemned, though once a great sinner, because he is not dealt with according to what his sins deserve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. This is the condemnation, that light came into the world and men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Everyone who does evil hates the light and avoids it, lest his deeds should be reproved, for Christ is hated because sin is loved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. But whoever does truth comes to the light, desiring God's will to be known and done, that his deeds may be shown as wrought in God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. After this Jesus and his disciples came into the land of Judea, where he stayed and baptised, his ministry growing while John's waned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. John was also baptising at Aenon near Salim, because there was much water there, and people came and were baptised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. John had not yet been cast into prison, and his public work still ran alongside the beginning of Jesus' ministry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. A dispute arose between John's disciples and the Jews about purifying, the kind of dispute that easily turns into rivalry over who baptises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. John's disciples came to him complaining that the one he had witnessed to was now baptising and all men were going to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. John answered that a man can receive nothing unless it is given him from heaven, and he was fully satisfied with the place and work assigned him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. He reminded them that he himself had said he was not the Christ but was sent before him, a forerunner, not the bridegroom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. He who has the bride is the bridegroom, and the friend of the bridegroom rejoices greatly at his voice, so John's joy was fulfilled in Christ's increase. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Christ must increase and John must decrease, for of his government and peace there will be no end while John's following lessens. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. He who comes from above is above all, while he who is of the earth speaks of the earth, for John was a sinful, mortal man and Jesus came from heaven as the Son of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Jesus testifies what he has seen and heard, yet no man receives his testimony, so widespread is the refusal of plain truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. But whoever receives his testimony sets his seal to this, that God is true, for Jesus' words are God's own words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. God gave the Spirit to Jesus not by measure, as to the prophets, but in all fullness, so his words carry divine authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The Father loves the Son and has given all things into his hand, placing the whole work of salvation under his authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Whoever believes on the Son has everlasting life, but whoever does not believe shall not see life, for the wrath of God abides on him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
