The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when Abram was ninety years old and nine, the LORD appeared to Abram, and said unto him, I am the Almighty God; walk before me, and be thou perfect.

2 And I will make my covenant between me and thee, and will multiply thee exceedingly.

3 And Abram fell on his face: and God talked with him, saying,

4 As for me, behold, my covenant is with thee, and thou shalt be a father of many nations.

5 Neither shall thy name any more be called Abram, but thy name shall be Abraham; for a father of many nations have I made thee.

6 And I will make thee exceeding fruitful, and I will make nations of thee, and kings shall come out of thee.

7 And I will establish my covenant between me and thee and thy seed after thee in their generations for an everlasting covenant, to be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee.

8 And I will give unto thee, and to thy seed after thee, the land wherein thou art a stranger, all the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession; and I will be their God.

9 And God said unto Abraham, Thou shalt keep my covenant therefore, thou, and thy seed after thee in their generations.

10 This is my covenant, which ye shall keep, between me and you and thy seed after thee; Every man child among you shall be circumcised.

11 And ye shall circumcise the flesh of your foreskin; and it shall be a token of the covenant betwixt me and you.

12 And he that is eight days old shall be circumcised among you, every man child in your generations, he that is born in the house, or bought with money of any stranger, which is not of thy seed.

13 He that is born in thy house, and he that is bought with thy money, must needs be circumcised: and my covenant shall be in your flesh for an everlasting covenant.

14 And the uncircumcised man child whose flesh of his foreskin is not circumcised, that soul shall be cut off from his people; he hath broken my covenant.

15 And God said unto Abraham, As for Sarai thy wife, thou shalt not call her name Sarai, but Sarah shall her name be.

16 And I will bless her, and give thee a son also of her: yea, I will bless her, and she shall be a mother of nations; kings of people shall be of her.

17 Then Abraham fell upon his face, and laughed, and said in his heart, Shall a child be born unto him that is an hundred years old? and shall Sarah, that is ninety years old, bear?

18 And Abraham said unto God, O that Ishmael might live before thee!

19 And God said, Sarah thy wife shall bear thee a son indeed; and thou shalt call his name Isaac: and I will establish my covenant with him for an everlasting covenant, and with his seed after him.

20 And as for Ishmael, I have heard thee: Behold, I have blessed him, and will make him fruitful, and will multiply him exceedingly; twelve princes shall he beget, and I will make him a great nation.

21 But my covenant will I establish with Isaac, which Sarah shall bear unto thee at this set time in the next year.

22 And he left off talking with him, and God went up from Abraham.

23 And Abraham took Ishmael his son, and all that were born in his house, and all that were bought with his money, every male among the men of Abraham’s house; and circumcised the flesh of their foreskin in the selfsame day, as God had said unto him.

24 And Abraham was ninety years old and nine, when he was circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin.

25 And Ishmael his son was thirteen years old, when he was circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin.

26 In the selfsame day was Abraham circumcised, and Ishmael his son.

27 And all the men of his house, born in the house, and bought with money of the stranger, were circumcised with him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God renews his covenant with ninety-nine-year-old Abram, renames him Abraham, father of many nations, and Sarai Sarah, and gives circumcision as the sign of the covenant for every male of the household. Isaac is promised as the covenant heir within the year, Ishmael is blessed, and Abraham circumcises his whole household the same day. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. At ninety-nine, God appeared to Abram as God Almighty, the all-sufficient God who pours out blessings, and commanded him to walk before him and be blameless, living always under God's eye. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke
  • Verse 2. God promised to make his covenant with Abram and multiply him exceedingly, renewing an old promise rather than making a new one, now that Abram was old and childless by Sarai. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Abram fell on his face in reverence, the posture of a man overwhelmed by God's nearness, and God continued to speak to him in that humble position. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The covenant was stated plainly: Abram would become the father of many nations, so the promise was never meant for one family alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. His name was changed from Abram to Abraham, father of a multitude, so that every time his name was spoken it would preach the promise back to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. God promised to make him exceedingly fruitful, with nations and kings descending from him, a greatness measured in peoples, not in wealth alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The covenant was everlasting, for Abraham and his offspring after him, with God himself as their God, which is the greatest part of the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. God gave the whole land of Canaan, where Abraham now lived as a stranger, as an everlasting possession, holding the present pilgrimage and the future inheritance together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Abraham and his offspring were to keep the covenant in their turn, so the promise carried a duty with it and was not a bare gift without response. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Every male child was to be circumcised, a sign cut into the flesh that marked the whole household as belonging to God's covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke
  • Verse 11. Circumcision was the token of the covenant between God and Abraham, a sign and seal of spiritual blessings promised, not the blessings themselves, as Clarke stresses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke
  • Verse 12. The rite was to be done at eight days old, because before that a child was counted unclean, and Clarke notes circumcision was understood as consecrating the person to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke
  • Verse 13. The sign applied to those born in the house and those bought with money alike, so the whole household, free and servant, stood inside the covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. An uncircumcised male would be cut off from his people for breaking the covenant, a severe warning that the sign was not optional for those claiming the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Sarai's name was changed to Sarah, princess, matching Abraham's new name and showing that the promise belonged to her as well, not to Abraham alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. God blessed Sarah and promised a son from her, with nations and kings of peoples coming from her, so the long-barren wife became the mother of the covenant line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Abraham fell on his face and laughed, asking whether a child could be born to a man of a hundred and a woman of ninety. Clarke and Henry read this as joyful wonder at an impossible thing, not scornful unbelief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke
  • Verse 18. Abraham pleaded that Ishmael might live before God, a father's natural wish that the son he already loved would not be set aside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. God answered that Sarah would bear Isaac, and the covenant would be established with him and his offspring, naming the child before he was conceived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Ishmael was not forgotten: God heard Abraham for him, promising blessing, fruitfulness, twelve princes, and a great nation, even though the covenant would run through Isaac. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The distinction was kept clear: the covenant itself would be established with Isaac, born to Sarah at the set time in the next year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. When God finished speaking, he went up from Abraham, leaving the old man with a promise that now had a name, a time, and a sign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. That very same day Abraham circumcised Ishmael and every male in his household, and Clarke notes that such a painful rite could only have been borne by men fully convinced it was God's command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke
  • Verse 24. Abraham himself was circumcised at ninety-nine, showing that the head of the household did not exempt himself from the sign he imposed on others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Ishmael was thirteen when circumcised, old enough to understand and consent, which underlines that his household's obedience was willing, not forced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Abraham and Ishmael were circumcised on the same day, father and son together entering the covenant sign without delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Every man of the house, born at home or bought from a foreigner, was circumcised with him, so the whole household was marked as God's people in one day's obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • circumcision. The sign cut into the flesh marking the household as belonging to God's covenant; Adam Clarke stresses it was a sign and seal of promised spiritual blessings, not the blessings themselves. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 17:10-11, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke
