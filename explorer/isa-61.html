The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 61 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me; because the LORD hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek; he hath sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that are bound;

2 To proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD, and the day of vengeance of our God; to comfort all that mourn;

3 To appoint unto them that mourn in Zion, to give unto them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness; that they might be called trees of righteousness, the planting of the LORD, that he might be glorified.

4 And they shall build the old wastes, they shall raise up the former desolations, and they shall repair the waste cities, the desolations of many generations.

5 And strangers shall stand and feed your flocks, and the sons of the alien shall be your plowmen and your vinedressers.

6 But ye shall be named the Priests of the LORD: men shall call you the Ministers of our God: ye shall eat the riches of the Gentiles, and in their glory shall ye boast yourselves.

7 For your shame ye shall have double; and for confusion they shall rejoice in their portion: therefore in their land they shall possess the double: everlasting joy shall be unto them.

8 For I the LORD love judgment, I hate robbery for burnt offering; and I will direct their work in truth, and I will make an everlasting covenant with them.

9 And their seed shall be known among the Gentiles, and their offspring among the people: all that see them shall acknowledge them, that they are the seed which the LORD hath blessed.

10 I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decketh himself with ornaments, and as a bride adorneth herself with her jewels.

11 For as the earth bringeth forth her bud, and as the garden causeth the things that are sown in it to spring forth; so the Lord GOD will cause righteousness and praise to spring forth before all the nations.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The Spirit of the Lord God is upon the anointed one, sent to preach good tidings to the meek, bind up the brokenhearted, and proclaim liberty to the captives. The chapter sets out the twofold work — mercy now, and the day of vengeance — and ends with Zion clothed in garments of salvation and a robe of righteousness, as righteousness and praise spring up before all nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me, because the Lord has anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek; he has sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that are bound. This chapter treats Messiah's offices and the restoration of Israel. Messiah announces His twofold commission to bring gospel mercy at His first coming, and judgments on unbelievers and comfort to Zion at His second coming, in verses 1 to 9; the language can be applied to Isaiah, comforting by his prophecies the exiles in Babylon, only in a subordinate sense. Is upon me, because hath anointed me is quoted by Jesus as His credentials in preaching, as in Luke. The Spirit is upon Me in preaching, because Jehovah hath anointed Me from the womb, as in Luke, and at baptism, with the Spirit without measure, and permanently abiding on Me, as in chapters 11 and John and Psalm 45, with 1 Kings and Exodus. Anointed as Messiah, Prophet, Priest, and King. Good tidings means as the word gospel means. The meek is rather, the poor, as Luke has it; that is, those afflicted with calamity, poor in circumstances and in spirit, as in Matthew. Proclaim liberty recalls John: language drawn from the deliverance of the Babylonian captives, to describe the deliverance from sin and death, as in Hebrews; also from the liberty proclaimed to all bond-servants in the year of jubilee, as in verse 2 and Leviticus and Jeremiah. Opening of the prison means the Hebrew rather is, the most complete opening, namely, of the eyes to them that are bound, that is, deliverance from prison, for captives are as it were blind in the darkness of prison, as in chapters 14, 35, and 42. So Luke and the Septuagint interpret it; Luke, under inspiration, adds to this, for the fuller explanation of the single clause in the Hebrew, to set at liberty them that are bruised; thus expressing the double opening implied; namely, that of the eyes, as in John, and that of the prison, as in Romans and Hebrews. His miracles were acted parables. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. To proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord, and the day of vengeance of our God; to comfort all that mourn. Acceptable year means the year of jubilee on which liberty was proclaimed to the captives, as in verse 1 and 2 Corinthians. Day of vengeance means the acceptable time of grace is a year; the time of vengeance but a day, as in chapters 34 and 63 and Malachi. Jesus in Luke closed the book before this clause; for the interval from His first to His second coming is the acceptable year; the day of vengeance will not be till He comes again, as in 2 Thessalonians. Our God means the saints call Him our God; for He cometh to avenge them, as in Revelation. All that mourn means the all seems to include the spiritual Israelite mourners, as well as the literal, who are in verse 3 called them that mourn in Zion, and to whom chapter 57 verse 18 refers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. To appoint unto them that mourn in Zion, to give unto them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness, that they might be called trees of righteousness, the planting of the Lord, that he might be glorified. To give means the double verb, with the one and the same accusative, imparts glowing vehemence to the style. Beauty for ashes means there is a play on the sound and meaning of the Hebrew words, peer, epher, literally, ornamental headdress or tiara, as in Ezekiel, worn in times of joy, instead of a headdress of ashes, cast on the head in mourning, as in 2 Samuel. Oil of joy means perfumed ointment was poured on the guests at joyous feasts, as in Psalms and Amos. On occasions of grief its use was laid aside, as in 2 Samuel. Garment of praise means bright-coloured garments, indicative of thankfulness, instead of those that indicate despondency, as sackcloth, as in John. Trees of righteousness means Hebrew, terebinth trees; symbolical of men strong in righteousness, instead of being, as heretofore, bowed down as a reed with sin and calamity, as in chapters 1 and 42, 1 Kings, Psalms, and Jeremiah. Planting of the Lord recalls chapter 60 verse 21. That he might be glorified recalls John. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They will build the old wastes, they will raise up the former desolations, and they will repair the waste cities, the desolations of many generations. Old wastes means Jerusalem and the cities of Judah which long lay in ruins, as in chapter 58 verse 12. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Strangers will stand and feed your flocks, and the sons of the alien will be your plowmen and your vinedressers. Stand means shall wait on you as servants, as in chapters 14 and 60. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But you will be named the Priests of the Lord; men will call you the Ministers of our God; you will eat the riches of the Gentiles, and in their glory will you boast yourselves. But ye means as contrasted with the strangers. Ye shall have no need to attend to your flocks and lands: strangers will do that for you; your exclusive business will be the service of Jehovah as His priests, as in Exodus, which remains yet to be realised; compare as to the spiritual Israel, chapters 66 and 1 Peter and Revelation. Ministers recalls Ezekiel. Eat riches of Gentiles recalls chapters 60. In their glory boast yourselves is rather, in their splendour ye shall be substituted in their stead; ye shall substitute yourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For your shame you will have double, and for confusion they will rejoice in their portion; therefore in their land they will possess the double; everlasting joy will be unto them. Double means instead of your past share, ye shall have not merely as much, but double as much reward, as in chapters 40 and Zechariah, compare the third clause in this verse. Confusion is rather, humiliation, or contumely. Rejoice means they shall celebrate with jubilation their portion. Transition from the second to the third person. In their land means marking the reference to literal Israel, not to the church at large. Everlasting joy recalls chapter 35. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For I the Lord love judgment, I hate robbery for burnt offering, and I will direct their work in truth, and I will make an everlasting covenant with them. Judgment means justice, which requires that I should restore My people, and give them double in compensation for their sufferings. Robbery for burnt offering is rather, from a different Hebrew root, the spoil of iniquity. So in Job. Hating, as I do, the rapine, combined with iniquity, perpetrated on My people by their enemies, I will vindicate Israel. Direct work in truth is rather, I will give them the reward of their work, as in chapters 40, 49, and 62, in faithfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Their seed will be known among the Gentiles, and their offspring among the people; all that see them will acknowledge them, that they are the seed which the Lord has blessed. Known means honourably; shall be illustrious, as in Psalm 67. People is rather, peoples. Seed blessed recalls chapter 65. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. I will greatly rejoice in the Lord, my soul will be joyful in my God, for he has clothed me with the garments of salvation, he has covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decks himself with ornaments, and as a bride adorns herself with her jewels. Zion in verse 3 gives thanks for God's returning favour, as in Luke and Habakkuk. Salvation, righteousness means inseparably connected together. The robe is a loose mantle thrown over the other parts of the dress, as in Psalms and Revelation. Decketh himself with ornaments is rather maketh himself a priestly headdress, that is, a magnificent headdress, such as was worn by the high priest, namely, a miter and a plate, or crown of gold worn in front of it, as Aquila, etc.; appropriate to the kingdom of priests, dedicated to the offering of spiritual sacrifices to God continually, as in Exodus and Revelation. Jewels is rather, ornaments in general. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. For as the earth brings forth her bud, and as the garden causes the things that are sown in it to spring forth, so the Lord God will cause righteousness and praise to spring forth before all the nations. This recalls chapters 45 and 55 and Psalms. Bud means the tender shoots. Praise recalls chapters 60 and 62. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
