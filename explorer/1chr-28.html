The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 28 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And David assembled all the princes of Israel, the princes of the tribes, and the captains of the companies that ministered to the king by course, and the captains over the thousands, and captains over the hundreds, and the stewards over all the substance and possession of the king, and of his sons, with the officers, and with the mighty men, and with all the valiant men, unto Jerusalem.

2 Then David the king stood up upon his feet, and said, Hear me, my brethren, and my people: As for me, I had in mine heart to build an house of rest for the ark of the covenant of the LORD, and for the footstool of our God, and had made ready for the building:

3 But God said unto me, Thou shalt not build an house for my name, because thou hast been a man of war, and hast shed blood.

4 Howbeit the LORD God of Israel chose me before all the house of my father to be king over Israel for ever: for he hath chosen Judah to be the ruler; and of the house of Judah, the house of my father; and among the sons of my father he liked me to make me king over all Israel:

5 And of all my sons, (for the LORD hath given me many sons,) he hath chosen Solomon my son to sit upon the throne of the kingdom of the LORD over Israel.

6 And he said unto me, Solomon thy son, he shall build my house and my courts: for I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father.

7 Moreover I will establish his kingdom for ever, if he be constant to do my commandments and my judgments, as at this day.

8 Now therefore in the sight of all Israel the congregation of the LORD, and in the audience of our God, keep and seek for all the commandments of the LORD your God: that ye may possess this good land, and leave it for an inheritance for your children after you for ever.

9 And thou, Solomon my son, know thou the God of thy father, and serve him with a perfect heart and with a willing mind: for the LORD searcheth all hearts, and understandeth all the imaginations of the thoughts: if thou seek him, he will be found of thee; but if thou forsake him, he will cast thee off for ever.

10 Take heed now; for the LORD hath chosen thee to build an house for the sanctuary: be strong, and do it.

11 Then David gave to Solomon his son the pattern of the porch, and of the houses thereof, and of the treasuries thereof, and of the upper chambers thereof, and of the inner parlours thereof, and of the place of the mercy seat,

12 And the pattern of all that he had by the spirit, of the courts of the house of the LORD, and of all the chambers round about, of the treasuries of the house of God, and of the treasuries of the dedicated things:

13 Also for the courses of the priests and the Levites, and for all the work of the service of the house of the LORD, and for all the vessels of service in the house of the LORD.

14 He gave of gold by weight for things of gold, for all instruments of all manner of service; silver also for all instruments of silver by weight, for all instruments of every kind of service:

15 Even the weight for the candlesticks of gold, and for their lamps of gold, by weight for every candlestick, and for the lamps thereof: and for the candlesticks of silver by weight, both for the candlestick, and also for the lamps thereof, according to the use of every candlestick.

16 And by weight he gave gold for the tables of shewbread, for every table; and likewise silver for the tables of silver:

17 Also pure gold for the fleshhooks, and the bowls, and the cups: and for the golden basons he gave gold by weight for every bason; and likewise silver by weight for every bason of silver:

18 And for the altar of incense refined gold by weight; and gold for the pattern of the chariot of the cherubims, that spread out their wings, and covered the ark of the covenant of the LORD.

19 All this, said David, the LORD made me understand in writing by his hand upon me, even all the works of this pattern.

20 And David said to Solomon his son, Be strong and of good courage, and do it: fear not, nor be dismayed: for the LORD God, even my God, will be with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee, until thou hast finished all the work for the service of the house of the LORD.

21 And, behold, the courses of the priests and the Levites, even they shall be with thee for all the service of the house of God: and there shall be with thee for all manner of workmanship every willing skilful man, for any manner of service: also the princes and all the people will be wholly at thy commandment.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells David's great assembly at Jerusalem, where he charges Solomon and the leaders, gives Solomon the pattern of the temple, and promises that God will be with him in the building. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David assembled all the princes of Israel, captains, stewards, mighty men, and valiant men, unto Jerusalem. Henry notes David, near his end and busier than ever like the Son of David, summoned the great men together to take leave, as Moses and Joshua did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Then David stood up upon his feet, and said, hear me, my brethren, and my people. Henry notes David rose from his chair in reverence to God and the assembly, calling them brethren whom he loved, not servants he commanded, humbled for his pride in numbers and dominion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. As for me, I had in mine heart to build an house of rest for the ark of the covenant of the Lord, and for the footstool of our God, and had made ready for the building: but God said unto me, thou shalt not build an house for my name, because thou hast been a man of war, and hast shed blood. Henry notes heaven is God's throne, earth and its temples but his footstool, and David's wars were enough work for one man: he must serve with the sword, another with line and plummet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Howbeit the Lord God of Israel chose me before all the house of my father to be king over Israel for ever: for he hath chosen Judah to be the ruler, and of the house of Judah, the house of my father, and among my father's sons he liked me to make me king over all Israel. Henry notes both titles were by divine choice, not primogeniture: Judah was not eldest, Jesse's house was not senior, David was youngest, yet God takes whom he likes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And of all my sons, for the Lord hath given me many sons, he hath chosen Solomon my son to sit upon the throne of the kingdom of the Lord over Israel. Henry notes Solomon, one of David's youngest, was chosen as likeliest to build the temple, wisest and best inclined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And he said unto me, Solomon thy son, he shall build my house and my courts, for I have chosen him to be my son, and I will be his father. Henry notes Solomon as type of Christ, the Son of love, founder and foundation of the gospel temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Moreover I will establish his kingdom for ever, if he be constant to do my commandments and my judgments, as at this day. Henry notes the promise is conditional for Solomon: if he continue towardly and good, his kingdom continues, otherwise not, and perseverance wears the crown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Now therefore in the sight of all Israel the congregation of the Lord, and in the audience of our God, keep and seek for all the commandments of the Lord your God, that ye may possess this good land, and leave it for an inheritance for your children after you for ever. Henry notes the charge was public, with God and congregation as witnesses, and its motive was happy possession and entail to children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And thou, Solomon my son, know thou the God of thy father, and serve him with a perfect heart and with a willing mind, for the Lord searcheth all hearts, and understandeth all the imaginations of the thoughts: if thou seek him, he will be found of thee, but if thou forsake him, he will cast thee off for ever. Henry notes Solomon must know and serve God with upright heart and willing mind, since God searches hearts, and God never casts off till men first cast him off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Take heed now, for the Lord hath chosen thee to build an house for the sanctuary: be strong, and do it. Henry notes the means prescribed: caution, take heed against evil, and courage, be strong and do it, fetching strength from divine grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Then David gave to Solomon his son the pattern of the porch, and of the houses thereof, and of the treasuries thereof, and of the upper chambers thereof, and of the inner parlours thereof, and of the place of the mercy seat. Henry notes David gave a model by the Spirit, as Moses had a pattern in the mount, because the temple, a type of Christ, must be framed by divine institution, not man's invention. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And the pattern of all that he had by the spirit, of the courts of the house of the Lord, and of all the chambers round about, of the treasuries of the house of God, and of the treasuries of the dedicated things. Henry notes the pattern covered courts, chambers, and treasuries, including a place to lay up Moses's tabernacle, signifying the Mosaic economy respectfully laid aside in fulness of time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Also for the courses of the priests and the Levites, and for all the work of the service of the house of the Lord, and for all the vessels of service in the house of the Lord. Henry notes David gave a table of priestly courses and patterns of service vessels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He gave of gold by weight for things of gold, for all instruments of all manner of service, silver also for all instruments of silver by weight, for all instruments of every kind of service. Henry notes David weighed exact quantities for each vessel, so none would be made less than the pattern. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Even the weight for the candlesticks of gold, and for their lamps of gold, by weight for every candlestick, and for the lamps thereof, and for the candlesticks of silver by weight, both for the candlestick, and also for the lamps thereof, according to the use of every candlestick. Henry notes the temple had ten golden candlesticks besides silver ones, where the tabernacle had one, furniture proportionable to the larger house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And by weight he gave gold for the tables of shewbread, for every table, and likewise silver for the tables of silver. Henry notes besides the showbread table there were ten other tables, plus silver tables, for the temple's larger service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Also pure gold for the fleshhooks, and the bowls, and the cups, and for the golden basons he gave gold by weight for every bason, and likewise silver by weight for every bason of silver. Henry includes fleshhooks, bowls, cups, and basins among the weighed vessels in the pattern. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And for the altar of incense refined gold by weight, and gold for the pattern of the chariot of the cherubims, that spread out their wings, and covered the ark of the covenant of the Lord. Henry notes incense gold was refined, purer than the rest, typical of Christ's pure intercession, and the great cherubim, wings wall to wall, are called a chariot, for angels are God's chariots. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. All this, said David, the Lord made me understand in writing by his hand upon me, even all the works of this pattern. Henry notes the pattern was given in writing by God's immediate hand, or as clearly represented to David's mind as if in writing, by the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And David said to Solomon his son, be strong and of good courage, and do it: fear not, nor be dismayed, for the Lord God, even my God, will be with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee, until thou hast finished all the work for the service of the house of the Lord. Henry notes David's encouragement: fear not opposition, charge, or miscarrying, for God who owned our fathers will go along with us and never leave us while he has work in us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And, behold, the courses of the priests and the Levites, even they shall be with thee for all the service of the house of God, and there shall be with thee for all manner of workmanship every willing skilful man, for any manner of service: also the princes and all the people will be wholly at thy commandment. Henry notes good men will help: priests and Levites to advise, willing and skilful workmen, and princes and people wholly at command, so good work goes on when all are hearty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
