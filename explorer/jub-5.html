The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 5 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And it came to pass when the children of men began to multiply on the face of the earth and daughters were born unto them, that the angels of God saw them on a certain year of this jubilee, that they were

2 beautiful to look upon ; and they took themselves wives of all whom they chose, and they bare unto them sons and they were giants.

3 And lawlessness increased on the earth and all flesh corrupted its way, alike men and cattle and beasts and birds and everything that walketh on the earth — all of them corrupted their ways and their orders, and they began to devour each other, and lawlessness increased on the earth and every imagination of the thoughts of all men (was) thus evil continually.

4 And God looked upon the earth, and behold it was corrupt, and all flesh had corrupted its orders, and all that were upon the earth had wrought all manner of evil before His eyes.

5 And He said : " I shall destroy man and all flesh upon the face of the earth which I have created." 5.

6 But Noah found grace before the eyes of the Lord. 6.

7 And against the angels whom He had sent upon the earth, He was exceedingly wroth, and He gave commandment to root them out of all their dominion, and He bade us to bind them in the depths of the earth, and behold they are bound in the midst of them, and are (kept) separate.

8 And against their sons went forth a command from before His face that they should be smitten with the sword, and be removed from under heaven.

9 And He said " My spirit will not always abide on man ; for they also are flesh and their days shall be one hundred and twenty years." 9.

10 And He sent His sword into their midst that each should slay his neighbour, and they began to slay each other till they all fell by the sword and were destroyed from the earth.

11 And their fathers were witnesses (of their destruction), and after this they were bound in the depths of the earth for ever, until the day of the great condemnation when judgment is executed on all those who have corrupted their ways and their works before

12 the Lord.

13 And He destroyed all from their places, and there was not left one of them whom He judged not according to all their wickedness.

14 And He made for all His works a new and righteous nature so that they should not sin in their whole nature for ever, but should be all righteous each in his kind always.

15 And the judgment of all is ordained and written on the heavenly tables in righteousness—even (the judgment of) all who depart from the path which is ordained for them to walk in ; and if they walk not therein, judgment is written down for every creature and for every kind.

16 And there is nothing in heaven or on earth, or in light or in darkness, or in Sheol or in the depth, or in the place of darkness (which is not judged) ; and all their judgments are ordained and written and engraved.

17 In regard to all He will judge, the great according to his greatness, and the small according to his smallness, and each according to his way.

18 And He is not one who will regard the person (of any), nor is He one who will receive gifts, if He saith that He will execute judgment on each : if one gave everything that is on the earth, He will not regard the gifts or the person (of any), nor accept anything at his hands, for He is a righteous judge.

19 And of the children of Israel it hath been written and ordained : If they turn to Him in righteousness, He will forgive all their transgressions and pardon all their sins.

20 It is written and ordained that He will show mercy to all who turn from all their guilt once each year.

21 And as for all those who corrupted their ways and their thoughts before the flood, no man's person was accepted save that of Noah alone ; for his person was accepted in behalf of his sons, whom (God) saved from the waters of the flood on his account ; for his heart was righteous in all his ways, according as it was commanded regarding him, and he had not departed from aught that was ordained for him.

22 And the Lord said that He would destroy everything which was upon the earth, both men and cattle, and beasts, and fowls of the air, and that which moveth on the earth.

23 The Building of the Ark ; the Flood.

24 And He commanded Noah to make him an ark, that he might save himself from the waters of the flood.

25 And Noah made the ark in all respects as He commanded him, in the twenty-seventh jubilee of years, in the fifth week in the fifth year (on the new moon of the first month).

26 And he entered in the sixth (year) thereof, in the second month, on the new moon of the second month, till the sixteenth; and he entered, and all that we brought to him, into the ark, and the Lord closed it from without on the seventeenth evening.

27 And the Lord opened flood-gates of heaven, And the mouths of the fountains of the great deep, seven mouths in number.

28 And the flood-gates began to pour down water

29 from the heaven forty days and forty nights, And the fountains of the deep also sent up waters, until the whole world was full of water.

30 And the waters increased upon the earth : Fifteen cubits did the waters rise above all the

31 high mountains,

32 And the ark was lift up above the earth, And it moved upon the face of the waters. 2.

33 And the water prevailed on the face of the earth five months — one hundred and fifty days.

34 And the ark went and rested on the top of Lubar, one of the mountains of Ararat. 29.

35 And (on the new moon) in the fourth month the fountains of the great deep were closed and the flood-gates of heaven were restrained; and on the new moon of the seventh month all the mouths of the abysses of the earth were opened, and the water began to descend into the deep below.

36 And on the new moon of the tenth month the tops of the mountains were seen, and on the new moon of the first month the earth became visible. 31.

37 And the waters disappeared from above the earth in the fifth week in the seventh year thereof, and on the seventeenth day in the second month the earth was dry.

38 And on the twenty-seventh thereof he opened the ark, and sent forth from it beasts, and cattle, and birds, and every moving thing.

39 Noah Sacrifice ; God's Covenant with him

40 Instructions to Moses about eating of Blood, the Feast of Weeks, etc., and Division of the Year.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: When the children of men began to multiply and daughters were born to them, the angels of God saw them in a certain year of this jubilee, beautiful to look upon. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. When the children of men began to multiply and daughters were born to them, the angels of God saw them in a certain year of this jubilee, beautiful to look upon. Jubilees reads the Genesis 6 account as angels, the Watchers of chapter 4, whose descent for instruction turns to desire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 2. They took themselves wives of all whom they chose, and they bare sons, and they were giants. The union of Watchers and women produces giants, the violent offspring whose destruction the rest of the chapter orders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 3. Lawlessness increased and all flesh corrupted its way, men, cattle, beasts, birds and everything walking on earth, all corrupting their ways and orders, devouring each other, with every imagination of men's thoughts evil continually. JFB on Genesis 6:5-6 and 6:11 describes a world filled with violence, men doing what was right in their own eyes with no fear of God, until God, in language suited to our nature, is said to repent and turn from mercy to judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. God looked on the earth, and behold it was corrupt, and all flesh had corrupted its orders, and all on earth had wrought all manner of evil before his eyes. The divine inspection confirms the corruption: no order, human or animal, is left uncorrupted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God said he would destroy man and all flesh on the face of the earth which he had created. JFB on Genesis 6:17-22 notes the flood was brought by God as punishment for enormous wickedness, and the repetition of the announcement establishes its certainty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But Noah found grace before the eyes of the Lord. JFB on Genesis 6:8 reads grace as favour, and stresses the awful state of things when only one man, or one family, of piety and virtue remained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Against the angels he had sent on earth God was exceedingly wroth, commanding them to be rooted out of all their dominion, and bidding the angels bind them in the depths of the earth, where they are bound in the midst and kept separate. The Watchers' judgment is binding until the great condemnation, as verse 11 states. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. Against their sons went forth a command from before his face that they should be smitten with the sword and removed from under heaven. The giants, not only their fathers, are sentenced: removal from under heaven ends their dominion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. God said his spirit would not always abide on man, for they also are flesh and their days shall be one hundred and twenty years. JFB on Genesis 6:3 explains the Spirit had striven through Enoch, Noah and perhaps other prophets preaching repentance, but the generation was incorrigible, and the 120 years are a long respite while corruption gradually reached its height. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. God sent his sword into their midst that each should slay his neighbour, and they began to slay each other until they all fell by the sword and were destroyed from the earth. The giants perish by mutual slaughter before the flood, judged by the sword they lived by. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. Their fathers were witnesses of their destruction, and after this they were bound in the depths of the earth forever, until the day of the great condemnation when judgment is executed on all who corrupted their ways and works before the Lord. The bound Watchers watch their sons perish, then wait bound for the final judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. This verse completes the judgment sentence of verse 11: the condemnation falls before the Lord, on all who corrupted their ways and works. No corruption escapes the coming judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. God destroyed all from their places, and there was not left one of them whom he judged not according to all their wickedness. The destruction is total and proportioned: each is judged according to the whole of his wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. God made for all his works a new and righteous nature, so that they should not sin in their whole nature forever, but should be all righteous each in his kind always. After judgment comes renewal: the post-flood world receives a nature ordered to righteousness by kind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. The judgment of all is ordained and written on the heavenly tables in righteousness, even the judgment of all who depart from the path ordained for them, and if they walk not therein, judgment is written down for every creature and every kind. The heavenly tables hold not only laws but judgments, recorded in advance for every departure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. There is nothing in heaven or on earth, in light or darkness, in Sheol or in the depth, or in the place of darkness, which is not judged, and all their judgments are ordained and written and engraved. Judgment reaches every realm, including Sheol and the deep, and it is engraved, not merely spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. In regard to all he will judge, the great according to his greatness and the small according to his smallness, and each according to his way. Judgment is proportioned to stature and to conduct alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. God is not one who regards the person of any, nor one who receives gifts: if he says he will execute judgment on each, then if one gave everything on earth he will not regard gifts or person nor accept anything at his hands, for he is a righteous judge. Impartiality is the ground of the whole judgment section: no bribe and no rank alters the sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. Of the children of Israel it has been written and ordained: if they turn to him in righteousness, he will forgive all their transgressions and pardon all their sins. The judgment section turns to Israel's hope: turning in righteousness brings full forgiveness, as chapter 1 promised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. It is written and ordained that he will show mercy to all who turn from all their guilt once each year. Repentance has a fixed annual season of mercy, the yearly turning that answers the yearly record of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. Of all who corrupted their ways and thoughts before the flood, no man's person was accepted save Noah alone, for his person was accepted on behalf of his sons, whom God saved from the flood on his account, for his heart was righteous in all his ways as commanded, and he had not departed from anything ordained for him. JFB on Genesis 6:9 calls Noah just and perfect, not absolutely sinless, but living by faith, just, and sincere in his desire to do God's will; Jubilees adds that his righteousness covered his sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The Lord said he would destroy everything on the earth, men, cattle, beasts, fowls of the air, and movers on the earth. JFB on Genesis 7:21 notes it is a uniform principle in divine judgments to include everything connected with the sinful objects of wrath, while goodness is mingled with severity in preserving the one righteous family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. This unit is the section heading for the narrative that follows: the building of the ark and the flood. It divides the judgment discourse from the flood history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. God commanded Noah to make an ark, that he might save himself from the waters of the flood. JFB on Genesis 6:14-16 explains the ark as a hollow chest of gopher, probably cypress, wood, with rooms or small cabins, pitched within and without with bitumen to make it watertight, an immense house-like structure designed not to sail but only to float. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Noah made the ark in all respects as commanded, in the twenty-seventh jubilee of years, in the fifth week, in the fifth year, on the new moon of the first month. JFB on Genesis 6:22 notes Noah began without delay and faithfully followed the divine directions at every step; Jubilees dates the finished obedience to month and year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. He entered in the sixth year, in the second month, on the new moon of the second month until the sixteenth, and he entered with all that was brought to him into the ark, and the Lord closed it from without on the seventeenth evening. JFB on Genesis 7:1, 9 and 16 notes Noah entered in implicit faith, the creatures were led by a divine impulse two and two, and the Lord shutting him in meant Noah had become the special object of divine care while the season of grace ended for those without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The Lord opened the flood-gates of heaven and the mouths of the fountains of the great deep, seven mouths in number. The flood comes from above and below at once, and Jubilees counts seven mouths for the deep's fountains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. This verse opens the flood's onset: the flood-gates began to pour down water. The sentence is completed by the next verse's forty days and the deep's rising waters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. From heaven the water fell forty days and forty nights, and the fountains of the deep also sent up waters, until the whole world was full of water. Above and below join until the world itself is full, leaving no dry refuge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. The waters increased on the earth: fifteen cubits did the waters rise above all the high mountains. JFB on Genesis 7:20 computes fifteen cubits as twenty-two and a half feet above the highest hills, language inconsistent with a merely partial deluge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. This verse completes the measurement: the fifteen cubits rose above all the high mountains. The flood's depth is stated against the highest ground, not the valleys. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The ark was lifted up above the earth and moved on the face of the waters. JFB on Genesis 7:17 notes the ark seems to have been raised so gradually as to be scarcely perceptible to its occupants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. The water prevailed on the face of the earth five months, one hundred and fifty days. JFB on Genesis 7:24 notes the 150 days are five months, a lengthened continuance designed to manifest God's stern displeasure at sin and sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. The ark went and rested on the top of Lubar, one of the mountains of Ararat. JFB on Genesis 8:4 reads 'rested' as a calm and gentle motion, and Ararat as Armenia; Jubilees names the particular peak, Lubar, where the ark grounded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. On the new moon of the fourth month the fountains of the deep were closed and the flood-gates of heaven restrained, and on the new moon of the seventh month all the mouths of the abysses were opened and the water began to descend into the deep below. The recession is dated by new moons, as the flood's onset was, keeping the book's calendar over the waters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. On the new moon of the tenth month the tops of the mountains were seen, and on the new moon of the first month the earth became visible. JFB on Genesis 8:5 notes the decrease was exceedingly slow and gradual, the waters' return taking nearly twice as long as their rise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. The waters disappeared from above the earth in the fifth week, in the seventh year, and on the seventeenth day in the second month the earth was dry. The drying is fixed to week, year and day, closing the flood's dated account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. On the twenty-seventh of that month he opened the ark and sent forth from it beasts, cattle, birds and every moving thing. JFB on Genesis 8:15-16 notes they went forth in the most orderly manner, the human occupants first, then each species after their kinds, literally according to their families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. This unit is the section heading for the next part of the story: Noah's sacrifice and God's covenant with him. It points forward to the sacrifice and covenant teaching that opens chapter 6. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. This unit is the section heading for the teaching that follows: instructions to Moses about eating blood, the feast of weeks, and the division of the year. The headings show the book's didactic shape: history carries halakhah, and the flood story will yield laws for Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

JFB on Genesis 6:5-6 and 6:11 describes a world filled with violence, men doing what was right in their own eyes with no fear of God, until God, in language suited to our nature, is said to repent and turn from mercy to judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Of all who corrupted their ways and thoughts before the flood, no man's person was accepted save Noah alone, for his person was accepted on behalf of his sons, whom God saved from the flood on his account, for his heart was righteous in all his ways as commanded, and he had not departed from anything ordained for him. JFB on Genesis 6:9 calls Noah just and perfect, not absolutely sinless, but living by faith, just, and sincere in his desire to do God's will; Jubilees adds that his righteousness covered his sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Noah made the ark in all respects as commanded, in the twenty-seventh jubilee of years, in the fifth week, in the fifth year, on the new moon of the first month. JFB on Genesis 6:22 notes Noah began without delay and faithfully followed the divine directions at every step; Jubilees dates the finished obedience to month and year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. The ark went and rested on the top of Lubar, one of the mountains of Ararat. JFB on Genesis 8:4 reads 'rested' as a calm and gentle motion, and Ararat as Armenia; Jubilees names the particular peak, Lubar, where the ark grounded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 4. God looked on the earth, and behold it was corrupt, and all flesh had corrupted its orders, and all on earth had wrought all manner of evil before his eyes. The divine inspection confirms the corruption: no order, human or animal, is left uncorrupted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God said he would destroy man and all flesh on the face of the earth which he had created. JFB on Genesis 6:17-22 notes the flood was brought by God as punishment for enormous wickedness, and the repetition of the announcement establishes its certainty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But Noah found grace before the eyes of the Lord. JFB on Genesis 6:8 reads grace as favour, and stresses the awful state of things when only one man, or one family, of piety and virtue remained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. God said his spirit would not always abide on man, for they also are flesh and their days shall be one hundred and twenty years. JFB on Genesis 6:3 explains the Spirit had striven through Enoch, Noah and perhaps other prophets preaching repentance, but the generation was incorrigible, and the 120 years are a long respite while corruption gradually reached its height. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The Lord said he would destroy everything on the earth, men, cattle, beasts, fowls of the air, and movers on the earth. JFB on Genesis 7:21 notes it is a uniform principle in divine judgments to include everything connected with the sinful objects of wrath, while goodness is mingled with severity in preserving the one righteous family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. God commanded Noah to make an ark, that he might save himself from the waters of the flood. JFB on Genesis 6:14-16 explains the ark as a hollow chest of gopher, probably cypress, wood, with rooms or small cabins, pitched within and without with bitumen to make it watertight, an immense house-like structure designed not to sail but only to float. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. He entered in the sixth year, in the second month, on the new moon of the second month until the sixteenth, and he entered with all that was brought to him into the ark, and the Lord closed it from without on the seventeenth evening. JFB on Genesis 7:1, 9 and 16 notes Noah entered in implicit faith, the creatures were led by a divine impulse two and two, and the Lord shutting him in meant Noah had become the special object of divine care while the season of grace ended for those without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The waters increased on the earth: fifteen cubits did the waters rise above all the high mountains. JFB on Genesis 7:20 computes fifteen cubits as twenty-two and a half feet above the highest hills, language inconsistent with a merely partial deluge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. This verse completes the measurement: the fifteen cubits rose above all the high mountains. The flood's depth is stated against the highest ground, not the valleys. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The ark was lifted up above the earth and moved on the face of the waters. JFB on Genesis 7:17 notes the ark seems to have been raised so gradually as to be scarcely perceptible to its occupants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. The water prevailed on the face of the earth five months, one hundred and fifty days. JFB on Genesis 7:24 notes the 150 days are five months, a lengthened continuance designed to manifest God's stern displeasure at sin and sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. On the new moon of the tenth month the tops of the mountains were seen, and on the new moon of the first month the earth became visible. JFB on Genesis 8:5 notes the decrease was exceedingly slow and gradual, the waters' return taking nearly twice as long as their rise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. On the twenty-seventh of that month he opened the ark and sent forth from it beasts, cattle, birds and every moving thing. JFB on Genesis 8:15-16 notes they went forth in the most orderly manner, the human occupants first, then each species after their kinds, literally according to their families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
