The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Sirach 5 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Set thy heart upon thy goods; and say not, I have enough for my life.

2 Follow not thine own mind and thy strength, to walk in the ways of thy heart:

3 And say not, Who shall controul me for my works? for the Lord will surely revenge thy pride.

4 Say not, I have sinned, and what harm hath happened unto me? for the Lord is longsuffering, he will in no wise let thee go.

5 Concerning propitiation, be not without fear to add sin unto sin:

6 And say not His mercy is great; he will be pacified for the multitude of my sins: for mercy and wrath come from him, and his indignation resteth upon sinners.

7 Make no tarrying to turn to the Lord, and put not off from day to day: for suddenly shall the wrath of the Lord come forth, and in thy security thou shalt be destroyed, and perish in the day of vengeance.

8 Set not thine heart upon goods unjustly gotten, for they shall not profit thee in the day of calamity.

9 Winnow not with every wind, and go not into every way: for so doth the sinner that hath a double tongue.

10 Be stedfast in thy understanding; and let thy word be the same.

11 Be swift to hear; and let thy life be sincere; and with patience give answer.

12 If thou hast understanding, answer thy neighbour; if not, lay thy hand upon thy mouth.

13 Honour and shame is in talk: and the tongue of man is his fall.

14 Be not called a whisperer, and lie not in wait with thy tongue: for a foul shame is upon the thief, and an evil condemnation upon the double tongue.

15 Be not ignorant of any thing in a great matter or a small.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Do not set your heart on wealth, least of all wealth unjustly gotten, which will fail in the day of calamity, and do not presume on God's mercy as a licence to keep sinning. Self-control in speech and careful attention to matters great and small run through the chapter's warnings. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Do not set your heart upon your goods, nor say you have enough for your life. Wealth must not become the heart's anchor, and a claim of self-sufficiency is itself a danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Do not follow your own mind and strength to walk in the ways of your heart. Self-will, trusted as a guide, leads a person wherever appetite points. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. Do not say, who shall control me for my works, for the Lord will surely avenge your pride. Boasting that no one can call you to account invites the Lord's own reckoning with pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. Do not say, I have sinned, and what harm has happened to me, for the Lord is longsuffering but will in no way let you go. Delay of punishment is God's patience, not his permission, and it never means escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Concerning propitiation, do not be without fear while adding sin to sin. Presuming on forgiveness while piling up sins is a fearless folly, not faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. Do not say his mercy is great and he will be pacified for the multitude of my sins, for mercy and wrath both come from him, and his indignation rests on sinners. God's mercy cannot be quoted against his wrath; both belong to him, and wrath rests on persistent sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. Do not tarry to turn to the Lord, nor put it off from day to day, for suddenly his wrath will come forth, and in your security you will be destroyed and perish in the day of vengeance. Delay in repentance is perilous because judgment arrives suddenly, catching the secure unprepared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Do not set your heart upon goods unjustly gotten, for they will not profit you in the day of calamity. Ill-gotten wealth fails exactly when it is most needed, in calamity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. Do not winnow with every wind, nor go into every way, for so does the sinner with a double tongue. Being blown about by every opinion and taking every road is the mark of a double-tongued sinner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Be steadfast in your understanding, and let your word be the same. A settled mind shows itself in consistent speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. Be swift to hear, let your life be sincere, and with patience give answer. Quick listening, a sincere life, and a patient answer belong together as one discipline of speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. If you have understanding, answer your neighbour; if not, lay your hand upon your mouth. Answer only from real understanding; where it is lacking, silence is the right response. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. Honour and shame are in talk, and a man's tongue is his fall. Speech is where honour is won or lost, and the tongue is the usual instrument of a fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. Do not be called a whisperer, nor lie in wait with your tongue, for foul shame is upon the thief and evil condemnation upon the double tongue. Secret slander and lying in wait with words bring shame like theft and condemnation for double speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. Do not be ignorant of anything in a great matter or a small. Carelessness about details, great or small, is itself a fault to be avoided. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
