The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the six and thirtieth year of the reign of Asa Baasha king of Israel came up against Judah, and built Ramah, to the intent that he might let none go out or come in to Asa king of Judah.

2 Then Asa brought out silver and gold out of the treasures of the house of the LORD and of the king’s house, and sent to Benhadad king of Syria, that dwelt at Damascus, saying,

3 There is a league between me and thee, as there was between my father and thy father: behold, I have sent thee silver and gold; go, break thy league with Baasha king of Israel, that he may depart from me.

4 And Benhadad hearkened unto king Asa, and sent the captains of his armies against the cities of Israel; and they smote Ijon, and Dan, and Abelmaim, and all the store cities of Naphtali.

5 And it came to pass, when Baasha heard it, that he left off building of Ramah, and let his work cease.

6 Then Asa the king took all Judah; and they carried away the stones of Ramah, and the timber thereof, wherewith Baasha was building; and he built therewith Geba and Mizpah.

7 And at that time Hanani the seer came to Asa king of Judah, and said unto him, Because thou hast relied on the king of Syria, and not relied on the LORD thy God, therefore is the host of the king of Syria escaped out of thine hand.

8 Were not the Ethiopians and the Lubims a huge host, with very many chariots and horsemen? yet, because thou didst rely on the LORD, he delivered them into thine hand.

9 For the eyes of the LORD run to and fro throughout the whole earth, to shew himself strong in the behalf of them whose heart is perfect toward him. Herein thou hast done foolishly: therefore from henceforth thou shalt have wars.

10 Then Asa was wroth with the seer, and put him in a prison house; for he was in a rage with him because of this thing. And Asa oppressed some of the people the same time.

11 And, behold, the acts of Asa, first and last, lo, they are written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.

12 And Asa in the thirty and ninth year of his reign was diseased in his feet, until his disease was exceeding great: yet in his disease he sought not to the LORD, but to the physicians.

13 And Asa slept with his fathers, and died in the one and fortieth year of his reign.

14 And they buried him in his own sepulchres, which he had made for himself in the city of David, and laid him in the bed which was filled with sweet odours and divers kinds of spices prepared by the apothecaries’ art: and they made a very great burning for him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Asa's later years: Baasha's fortifying of Ramah, Asa's payment to Syria for help, the seer Hanani's rebuke, Asa's anger and imprisonment of the seer, and his death. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the six and thirtieth year of Asa's reign Baasha king of Israel came up against Judah, and built Ramah, to let none go out or come in to Asa. Henry notes the date's difficulty against Kings, where Baasha died in Asa's twenty-sixth year, and suggests the thirty-sixth year of Judah's kingdom from Rehoboam, coinciding with Asa's sixteenth, while Josephus places it in his twenty-sixth, perhaps by a transcriber's mistake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Then Asa brought out silver and gold from the treasures of the house of the Lord and the king's house, and sent to Benhadad king of Syria at Damascus, saying. Henry notes taking temple silver and gold for carnal politics was a great aggravation: must the temple be plundered, when he had better have brought gifts and prayers to God's house to engage God on his side? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. There is a league between me and thee, as between my father and thy father: behold, I have sent thee silver and gold; go, break thy league with Baasha king of Israel, that he may depart from me. Henry notes Asa did not do well to make a league with a heathen king and boast of it, nor to hire Benhadad to break his league with Baasha, for the public faith of kings must not be made so cheap, and Asa relied on Syria's league more than on God's covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And Benhadad hearkened unto Asa, and sent his army captains against Israel's cities, and they smote Ijon, Dan, Abelmaim, and all Naphtali's store cities. Henry notes it was a sin in Benhadad to break his league merely for a bribe, and a sin in Asa to move him to it, and Asa had to answer for the mischief Benhadad's army did unjustly, for those who draw others to sin know not where it will end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And it came to pass, when Baasha heard it, that he left off building Ramah, and let his work cease. Henry notes the project succeeded as diversion: Benhadad obliged Baasha to leave Ramah and defend his own country northward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Then Asa took all Judah, and they carried away Ramah's stones and timber, wherewith Baasha was building, and he built therewith Geba and Mizpah. Henry notes Asa seized the opportunity not only to demolish Baasha's fortifications but to convert the materials to his own use, building Geba and Mizpah from them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And at that time Hanani the seer came to Asa, and said, Because thou hast relied on the king of Syria, and not relied on the Lord thy God, therefore is the host of Syria escaped out of thine hand. Henry notes the greatest fault charged was relying on Syria and not on God, thinking God could not or would not help unless Benhadad was on his side, and God is much displeased when an arm of flesh is relied on more than his power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Were not the Ethiopians and Lubims a huge host, with very many chariots and horsemen? Yet, because thou didst rely on the Lord, he delivered them into thine hand. Henry notes Asa acted against his own experience: he of all men had no reason to distrust God, who had triumphed over a threatening enemy when he relied on the Lord, and experiences of God's goodness aggravate distrust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. For the eyes of the Lord run to and fro throughout the whole earth, to show himself strong in behalf of them whose heart is perfect toward him. Herein thou hast done foolishly: therefore from henceforth thou shalt have wars. Henry notes Asa acted against his knowledge of providence, whose eye runs to and fro, quick-sighted and far-reaching, governing for his people's good, and against his interest, losing the chance to check Syria and incurring wars henceforth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Then Asa was wroth with the seer, and put him in a prison house, for he was in a rage with him because of this thing. And Asa oppressed some of the people the same time. Henry notes even a good man, impatient of reproof, flew into rage, jailed the prophet in stocks, and oppressed people who owned the prophet, weakening himself, for persecutors are tyrants, and let him that thinks he stands take heed lest he fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And behold, the acts of Asa, first and last, lo, they are written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel. Henry's conclusion of Asa's reign applies: his acts first and last stand written in the kings' book, closing a reign whose latter end was less pleasing than its beginning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And Asa in the thirty-ninth year of his reign was diseased in his feet, until his disease was exceeding great, yet in his disease he sought not to the Lord, but to the physicians. Henry notes two years before death Asa was diseased in his feet, gout in a high degree, as if God put him in stocks for putting the prophet in stocks, and his sin was not using physicians but trusting them and expecting from them what only God could give. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And Asa slept with his fathers, and died in the one and fortieth year of his reign. Henry notes Asa's death followed his disease, closing a long and once-good reign that ended in distrust and impatience of reproof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And they buried him in his own sepulchres, which he had made for himself in the city of David, and laid him in the bed filled with sweet odours and divers spices prepared by the apothecaries' art, and they made a very great burning for him. Henry notes the magnificent funeral was likely the people's respect, not Asa's vanity, remembering eminent piety and usefulness despite blemishes, for faults should be buried in graves while services are remembered over them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Investigation (editorial — a question, not a conclusion)

This section investigates a question; it does not conclude it. The study note for verse 1 observes a difficulty: this chapter dates Baasha's attack on Judah to the thirty-sixth year of Asa's reign, while Kings places Baasha's death in Asa's twenty-sixth year.

Parallels. Both accounts concern Baasha of Israel against Asa of Judah. Differences. The years do not align as regnal years: Henry suggests the thirty-sixth year counts from the kingdom's division under Rehoboam, coinciding with Asa's sixteenth year, while Josephus places the attack in Asa's twenty-sixth year, perhaps by a transcriber's mistake.

Which reading is correct is not established by the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
