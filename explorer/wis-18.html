The Text — Wisdom of Solomon 18 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Nevertheless thy saints had a very great light, whose voice they hearing, and not seeing their shape, because they also had not suffered the same things, they counted them happy.

2 But for that they did not hurt them now, of whom they had been wronged before, they thanked them, and besought them pardon for that they had been enemies.

3 Instead whereof thou gavest them a burning pillar of fire, both to be a guide of the unknown journey, and an harmless sun to entertain them honourably.

4 For they were worthy to be deprived of light and imprisoned in darkness, who had kept thy sons shut up, by whom the uncorrupt light of the law was to be given unto the world.

5 And when they had determined to slay the babes of the saints, one child being cast forth, and saved, to reprove them, thou tookest away the multitude of their children, and destroyedst them altogether in a mighty water.

6 Of that night were our fathers certified afore, that assuredly knowing unto what oaths they had given credence, they might afterwards be of good cheer.

7 So of thy people was accepted both the salvation of the righteous, and destruction of the enemies.

8 For wherewith thou didst punish our adversaries, by the same thou didst glorify us, whom thou hadst called.

9 For the righteous children of good men did sacrifice secretly, and with one consent made a holy law, that the saints should be like partakers of the same good and evil, the fathers now singing out the songs of praise.

10 But on the other side there sounded an ill according cry of the enemies, and a lamentable noise was carried abroad for children that were bewailed.

11 The master and the servant were punished after one manner; and like as the king, so suffered the common person.

12 So they all together had innumerable dead with one kind of death; neither were the living sufficient to bury them: for in one moment the noblest offspring of them was destroyed.

13 For whereas they would not believe any thing by reason of the enchantments; upon the destruction of the firstborn, they acknowledged this people to be the sons of God.

14 For while all things were in quiet silence, and that night was in the midst of her swift course,

15 Thine Almighty word leaped down from heaven out of thy royal throne, as a fierce man of war into the midst of a land of destruction,

16 And brought thine unfeigned commandment as a sharp sword, and standing up filled all things with death; and it touched the heaven, but it stood upon the earth.

17 Then suddenly visions of horrible dreams troubled them sore, and terrors came upon them unlooked for.

18 And one thrown here, and another there, half dead, shewed the cause of his death.

19 For the dreams that troubled them did foreshew this, lest they should perish, and not know why they were afflicted.

20 Yea, the tasting of death touched the righteous also, and there was a destruction of the multitude in the wilderness: but the wrath endured not long.

21 For then the blameless man made haste, and stood forth to defend them; and bringing the shield of his proper ministry, even prayer, and the propitiation of incense, set himself against the wrath, and so brought the calamity to an end, declaring that he was thy servant.

22 So he overcame the destroyer, not with strength of body, nor force of arms, but with a word subdued him that punished, alleging the oaths and covenants made with the fathers.

23 For when the dead were now fallen down by heaps one upon another, standing between, he stayed the wrath, and parted the way to the living.

24 For in the long garment was the whole world, and in the four rows of the stones was the glory of the fathers graven, and thy Majesty upon the daidem of his head.

25 Unto these the destroyer gave place, and was afraid of them: for it was enough that they only tasted of the wrath.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

While Egypt lay in darkness, Israel had great light and a pillar of burning fire to guide an unknown journey. The night of the firstborn brought both the salvation of the righteous and the destruction of their adversaries, as God's almighty word leaped from heaven like a fierce warrior with a sharp sword. The destroyer was stayed by the blameless man standing between the dead and the living, with prayer and incense, not with force of arms. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock

Haydock notes Israel had great light, and hearing Egypt's voice without seeing shape, counted them happy for not suffering the same. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock

Haydock observes Israel thanked Egypt for not hurting them now, and sought pardon for former enmity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. Instead thereof you gave them a burning pillar of fire, to be a guide of their unknown journey and a harmless sun to entertain them honourably. Haydock notes the pillar: guide for an unknown journey, harmless sun for honourable entertainment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. They were worthy to be deprived of light and imprisoned in darkness, who had kept your sons shut up, by whom the incorruptible light of the law was to be given to the world. Haydock's justice is exact: those who shut up the law's bearers are shut up in darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock

When they had determined to slay the babes of the saints, one child being cast forth and saved, to reprove them you took away the multitude of their children, and destroyed them altogether in a mighty water. Haydock notes the reproof: one child saved, a multitude of theirs taken by water. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. That night was known beforehand to our fathers, that assuredly knowing what oaths they had believed, they might be of good cheer. Haydock observes the night was foreknown to the fathers, so that known oaths gave them cheer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. So your people received the salvation of the righteous and destruction of the enemies. Haydock notes one night brings both: salvation and destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. As you punished the adversaries, so you glorified us whom you called. Haydock's correspondence is that punishment and glorification are one act's two sides. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. The good children of good men offered sacrifices secretly, and with one consent made a holy law, that the saints should be like partakers of the same good and evil, the fathers now singing out the songs of praise. Haydock notes the secret Passover: one law, shared good and evil, fathers singing praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. The enemies, on the other side, made a cry, and the sound of lamentation was heard, and the wailing for dead children was heard on every side. Haydock contrasts praise with lamentation for dead children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. The same punishment befell servant and master, and the king suffered the same as the common person. Haydock stresses equality in punishment: servant, master, and king alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. All died together, and the number of deaths could not be counted, for the living were not sufficient to bury them. Haydock notes the dead outnumbered the living buriers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. For all that they would not believe before by their enchantments, upon the destruction of the firstborn they confessed this people to be God's son. Haydock observes enchantments failed to persuade, but the firstborn's destruction forced confession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock

  • Verse 15. Your Almighty word leaped down from heaven out of your royal throne, as a fierce man of war into the midst of a land of destruction. Haydock pictures the Almighty word leaping as a warrior into a doomed land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. It brought an unfeigned commandment as a sharp sword, and standing up filled all things with death, touching heaven yet walking on earth. Haydock notes the sword touches heaven and walks earth, filling all with death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. Then suddenly visions of horrible dreams troubled them sore, and fears came upon them unlooked for. Haydock observes dreams and unlooked-for fears precede the stroke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. Half dead with fear, they confessed the cause of their death. Haydock notes the half-dead confess the cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. The dreams that troubled them foreshowed this, lest they should perish without knowing why they were afflicted. Haydock's point is that foreknowledge by dreams leaves no excuse of ignorance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. The tasting of death touched the righteous also, and there was a disturbance in the wilderness, but the wrath endured not long. Haydock notes death touched the righteous too, but wrath did not endure long. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. For the blameless man made haste and stood forth to defend them, bringing the shield of his proper ministry, prayer and propitiation of incense, and set himself against the wrath, putting an end to the calamity, showing he was your servant. Haydock reads Aaron's intercession: prayer and incense as shield, ending calamity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. He overcame the destroyer, not with strength of body nor force of arms, but by the word subdued him who punished, alleging the oaths and covenants made with the fathers. Haydock stresses the victory was by word and covenant, not by arms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 23. When the dead were already fallen in heaps one upon another, he stood in the midst and cut off the wrath, parting the way to the living. Haydock pictures Aaron standing between dead and living, cutting off wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 24. In the long garment was the whole world, and in the four rows of stones was the glory of the fathers, and your Majesty upon the diadem of his head. Haydock notes the high priest's garment bears the world, the fathers' glory, and God's Majesty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 25. To these the destroyer gave place, and was afraid of them, for the trial of wrath sufficed. Haydock closes: the destroyer gives place, for wrath's trial sufficed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
