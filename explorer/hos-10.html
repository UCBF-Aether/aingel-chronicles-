The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hosea 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Israel is an empty vine, he bringeth forth fruit unto himself: according to the multitude of his fruit he hath increased the altars; according to the goodness of his land they have made goodly images.

2 Their heart is divided; now shall they be found faulty: he shall break down their altars, he shall spoil their images.

3 For now they shall say, We have no king, because we feared not the LORD; what then should a king do to us?

4 They have spoken words, swearing falsely in making a covenant: thus judgment springeth up as hemlock in the furrows of the field.

5 The inhabitants of Samaria shall fear because of the calves of Bethaven: for the people thereof shall mourn over it, and the priests thereof that rejoiced on it, for the glory thereof, because it is departed from it.

6 It shall be also carried unto Assyria for a present to king Jareb: Ephraim shall receive shame, and Israel shall be ashamed of his own counsel.

7 As for Samaria, her king is cut off as the foam upon the water.

8 The high places also of Aven, the sin of Israel, shall be destroyed: the thorn and the thistle shall come up on their altars; and they shall say to the mountains, Cover us; and to the hills, Fall on us.

9 O Israel, thou hast sinned from the days of Gibeah: there they stood: the battle in Gibeah against the children of iniquity did not overtake them.

10 It is in my desire that I should chastise them; and the people shall be gathered against them, when they shall bind themselves in their two furrows.

11 And Ephraim is as an heifer that is taught, and loveth to tread out the corn; but I passed over upon her fair neck: I will make Ephraim to ride; Judah shall plow, and Jacob shall break his clods.

12 Sow to yourselves in righteousness, reap in mercy; break up your fallow ground: for it is time to seek the LORD, till he come and rain righteousness upon you.

13 Ye have plowed wickedness, ye have reaped iniquity; ye have eaten the fruit of lies: because thou didst trust in thy way, in the multitude of thy mighty men.

14 Therefore shall a tumult arise among thy people, and all thy fortresses shall be spoiled, as Shalman spoiled Betharbel in the day of battle: the mother was dashed in pieces upon her children.

15 So shall Bethel do unto you because of your great wickedness: in a morning shall the king of Israel utterly be cut off.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Israel is an empty vine, fruiting only for itself, multiplying altars as its fruit multiplies, with a divided heart that will see its altars broken and its images spoiled. The calf of Bethaven will be carried to Assyria as a present to king Jareb, Samaria's king will be cut off like foam on water, and yet the chapter still urges: sow in righteousness, reap in mercy, break up the fallow ground, for it is time to seek the LORD. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Israel is an empty vine, he brings forth fruit unto himself, according to the multitude of his fruit he has increased the altars, according to the goodness of his land they have made goodly images, because the vine, fruitful only for itself, turns every blessing into another altar and every good land into another image. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Their heart is divided, now shall they be found faulty, he shall break down their altars, he shall spoil their images, because a divided heart cannot stand, and God himself will break the altars and spoil the images it built. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For now they shall say, we have no king, because we feared not the Lord, what then should a king do to us, because when the king is lost, the people confess that a king who did not fear God could do them no good anyway. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They have spoken words, swearing falsely in making a covenant, thus judgment springs up as hemlock in the furrows of the field, because false oaths in covenants make judgment grow like poisonous hemlock across the furrows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The inhabitants of Samaria shall fear because of the calves of Bethaven, for the people thereof shall mourn over it, and the priests thereof that rejoiced on it, for its glory, because it is departed from it, because the calf of Bethel, once the glory of the shrine, will depart, and priests who rejoiced in it will mourn with the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. It shall be also carried unto Assyria for a present to king Jareb, Ephraim shall receive shame, and Israel shall be ashamed of his own counsel, because the idol itself will be carried as tribute to Assyria, and the counsel that trusted it will end in shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. As for Samaria, her king is cut off as the foam upon the water, because the king, like foam on a stream, is carried off without resistance and without trace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The high places also of Aven, the sin of Israel, shall be destroyed, the thorn and the thistle shall come up on their altars, and they shall say to the mountains, cover us, and to the hills, fall on us, because the shrines of iniquity will be overgrown with thorns, and the people will prefer burial under mountains to the sight of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. O Israel, thou hast sinned from the days of Gibeah, there they stood, the battle in Gibeah against the children of iniquity did not overtake them, because the sin that began at Gibeah has continued unbroken, and the battle that should have overtaken evildoers there did not overtake them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. It is in my desire that I should chastise them, and the people shall be gathered against them, when they shall bind themselves in their two furrows, because God will gather the nations against Israel for chastisement, binding them as oxen are bound in their furrows for the yoke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And Ephraim is as a heifer that is taught, and loves to tread out the corn, but I passed over upon her fair neck, I will make Ephraim to ride, Judah shall plough, and Jacob shall break his clods, because Ephraim, a heifer taught only to tread out corn and eat, will be put to the yoke and made to plough, with Judah alongside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Sow to yourselves in righteousness, reap in mercy, break up your fallow ground, for it is time to seek the Lord, till he come and rain righteousness upon you, because the fallow heart must be broken up and sown with righteousness, and seeking the Lord is the work of the season until he rains righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Ye have ploughed wickedness, ye have reaped iniquity, ye have eaten the fruit of lies, because thou didst trust in thy way, in the multitude of thy mighty men, because the harvest answers the sowing, and trust in one's own way and in many mighty men reaps the fruit of lies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Therefore shall a tumult arise among thy people, and all thy fortresses shall be spoiled, as Shalman spoiled Betharbel in the day of battle, the mother was dashed in pieces upon her children, because the spoiler's tumult will wreck every fortress, as Shalman wrecked Beth-arbel, where mothers were dashed with their children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. So shall Bethel do unto you because of your great wickedness, in a morning shall the king of Israel utterly be cut off, because Bethel, the house of the calf, will do to Israel what it has always done, and the king will be cut off at morning, suddenly, at the day's beginning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Ephraim. the leading tribe of the northern kingdom, used in the prophets for the northern kingdom of Israel itself, as distinct from Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Bethaven. Hosea's name for the corrupt shrine at Bethel, the calf-shrine whose glory departs when the calf is carried to Assyria. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Samaria. the capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, standing in the prophets for the ten tribes as Jerusalem stands for Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
