The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 30 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Woe to the rebellious children, saith the LORD, that take counsel, but not of me; and that cover with a covering, but not of my spirit, that they may add sin to sin:

2 That walk to go down into Egypt, and have not asked at my mouth; to strengthen themselves in the strength of Pharaoh, and to trust in the shadow of Egypt!

3 Therefore shall the strength of Pharaoh be your shame, and the trust in the shadow of Egypt your confusion.

4 For his princes were at Zoan, and his ambassadors came to Hanes.

5 They were all ashamed of a people that could not profit them, nor be an help nor profit, but a shame, and also a reproach.

6 The burden of the beasts of the south: into the land of trouble and anguish, from whence come the young and old lion, the viper and fiery flying serpent, they will carry their riches upon the shoulders of young asses, and their treasures upon the bunches of camels, to a people that shall not profit them.

7 For the Egyptians shall help in vain, and to no purpose: therefore have I cried concerning this, Their strength is to sit still.

8 Now go, write it before them in a table, and note it in a book, that it may be for the time to come for ever and ever:

9 That this is a rebellious people, lying children, children that will not hear the law of the LORD:

10 Which say to the seers, See not; and to the prophets, Prophesy not unto us right things, speak unto us smooth things, prophesy deceits:

11 Get you out of the way, turn aside out of the path, cause the Holy One of Israel to cease from before us.

12 Wherefore thus saith the Holy One of Israel, Because ye despise this word, and trust in oppression and perverseness, and stay thereon:

13 Therefore this iniquity shall be to you as a breach ready to fall, swelling out in a high wall, whose breaking cometh suddenly at an instant.

14 And he shall break it as the breaking of the potters’ vessel that is broken in pieces; he shall not spare: so that there shall not be found in the bursting of it a sherd to take fire from the hearth, or to take water withal out of the pit.

15 For thus saith the Lord GOD, the Holy One of Israel; In returning and rest shall ye be saved; in quietness and in confidence shall be your strength: and ye would not.

16 But ye said, No; for we will flee upon horses; therefore shall ye flee: and, We will ride upon the swift; therefore shall they that pursue you be swift.

17 One thousand shall flee at the rebuke of one; at the rebuke of five shall ye flee: till ye be left as a beacon upon the top of a mountain, and as an ensign on an hill.

18 And therefore will the LORD wait, that he may be gracious unto you, and therefore will he be exalted, that he may have mercy upon you: for the LORD is a God of judgment: blessed are all they that wait for him.

19 For the people shall dwell in Zion at Jerusalem: thou shalt weep no more: he will be very gracious unto thee at the voice of thy cry; when he shall hear it, he will answer thee.

20 And though the Lord give you the bread of adversity, and the water of affliction, yet shall not thy teachers be removed into a corner any more, but thine eyes shall see thy teachers:

21 And thine ears shall hear a word behind thee, saying, This is the way, walk ye in it, when ye turn to the right hand, and when ye turn to the left.

22 Ye shall defile also the covering of thy graven images of silver, and the ornament of thy molten images of gold: thou shalt cast them away as a menstruous cloth; thou shalt say unto it, Get thee hence.

23 Then shall he give the rain of thy seed, that thou shalt sow the ground withal; and bread of the increase of the earth, and it shall be fat and plenteous: in that day shall thy cattle feed in large pastures.

24 The oxen likewise and the young asses that ear the ground shall eat clean provender, which hath been winnowed with the shovel and with the fan.

25 And there shall be upon every high mountain, and upon every high hill, rivers and streams of waters in the day of the great slaughter, when the towers fall.

26 Moreover the light of the moon shall be as the light of the sun, and the light of the sun shall be sevenfold, as the light of seven days, in the day that the LORD bindeth up the breach of his people, and healeth the stroke of their wound.

27 Behold, the name of the LORD cometh from far, burning with his anger, and the burden thereof is heavy: his lips are full of indignation, and his tongue as a devouring fire:

28 And his breath, as an overflowing stream, shall reach to the midst of the neck, to sift the nations with the sieve of vanity: and there shall be a bridle in the jaws of the people, causing them to err.

29 Ye shall have a song, as in the night when a holy solemnity is kept; and gladness of heart, as when one goeth with a pipe to come into the mountain of the LORD, to the mighty One of Israel.

30 And the LORD shall cause his glorious voice to be heard, and shall shew the lighting down of his arm, with the indignation of his anger, and with the flame of a devouring fire, with scattering, and tempest, and hailstones.

31 For through the voice of the LORD shall the Assyrian be beaten down, which smote with a rod.

32 And in every place where the grounded staff shall pass, which the LORD shall lay upon him, it shall be with tabrets and harps: and in battles of shaking will he fight with it.

33 For Tophet is ordained of old; yea, for the king it is prepared; he hath made it deep and large: the pile thereof is fire and much wood; the breath of the LORD, like a stream of brimstone, doth kindle it.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Woe to the rebellious children who send ambassadors to Egypt for help instead of seeking the Lord: Egypt's strength is a shadow and a confusion, and the alliance only adds sin to sin. God waits to be gracious to those who wait for him, and the chapter ends with Tophet prepared — a burning pile kindled by the breath of the Lord — for the Assyrian king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Woe to the rebellious children, says the Lord, who take counsel, but not of me, and who cover with a covering, but not of my spirit, that they may add sin to sin. Chapters 30–32 refer probably to the summer of 714 B.C., with Jewish ambassadors now on their way to Egypt to seek aid against Assyria, and Isaiah denounces reliance on Egypt rather than Jehovah, for God had prohibited such alliances and it was a leading part of Jewish polity that they be a separate people. Take counsel is rather execute counsels, and cover with a covering is to wrap themselves in reliances disloyal toward Jehovah, answering to seeking to hide deeply their counsel from the Lord — or literally, to pour out libations, by which leagues were made, so they make a league not suggested by God's Spirit. That they may add sin to sin speaks of consequence as intention, so reckless were they: one sin entails another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Who walk to go down into Egypt, and have not asked at God's mouth, to strengthen themselves in the strength of Pharaoh, and to trust in the shadow of Egypt! Walk means they are now setting out, namely their ambassadors, and Pharaoh is the generic name of Egypt's kings, as Caesar was at Rome, meaning king, with Phra, the sun, as hieroglyphic symbol and title; the shadow is an image from shelter against heat, meaning protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Therefore the strength of Pharaoh will be your shame, and the trust in the shadow of Egypt your confusion. Shame means disappointment, for Egypt, weakened by its internal dissensions, can give no solid help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For his princes were at Zoan, and his ambassadors came to Hanes. His is Judah's, and the princes are already arrived at Zoan on their errand to Pharaoh, while the ambassadors have come to Hanes, west of the Nile in central Egypt, Egyptian Hnes, Greek Heracleopolis, the seat of a reigning prince at the time, as was Zoan, hence the ambassadors go to both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. They were all ashamed of a people that could not profit them, nor be a help nor profit, but a shame, and also a reproach. As Jeremiah says, they were ashamed of a people that could not profit them, for Egypt's help proves shame and reproach rather than profit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The burden of the beasts of the south: into the land of trouble and anguish, from whence come the young and old lion, the viper and fiery flying serpent, they will carry their riches upon the shoulders of young asses, and their treasures upon the bunches of camels, to a people that will not profit them. The burden is the prophecy as to the beasts, or Isaiah sees in vision the ambassadors' beasts burdened with rich presents travelling southwards to Egypt and exclaims at the burden of treasure; the land of trouble is the desert between Palestine and Egypt, destitute of water and abounding in dangerous animals, the flying serpent a species springing like a dart from trees upon its prey, and the riches are carried, rather presented, as presents to Egypt on full-grown asses and camels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For the Egyptians will help in vain, and to no purpose; therefore I have cried concerning this: Their strength is to sit still. Egypt is vanity, and to no purpose will they help; strength is in Hebrew Rahab, a designation for Egypt implying haughty fierceness, so the phrase is, I call her Arrogance that sits still, for she who boasted of the help she would give, when it came to the test, sat still, as in chapter 36. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Now go, write it before them in a table, and note it in a book, that it may be for the time to come for ever and ever. The table is a tablet set in public containing the prophecy in briefer form, to be read by all, and the book is a parchment roll containing the prophecy in full for distant posterity, whose truth will be seen hereafter when the event comes to pass; for ever and ever is rather, for a testimony forever, for testimony is often joined to perpetuity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. That this is a rebellious people, lying children, children that will not hear the law of the Lord. Lying means unfaithful to Jehovah, whose covenant they had taken on them as His adopted children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Who say to the seers: See not; and to the prophets: Prophesy not unto us right things, speak unto us smooth things, prophesy deceits. See not means, as you now do, foretelling misfortune, and prophesy not right things was not avowedly requested, but their conduct virtually expressed it: no man professedly wishes to be deceived, but many seek a kind of teaching which is deceit, and the Jews desired success to be foretold as the issue of their Egyptian league, though ill had been announced by God's prophet — these constituted the deceits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Get out of the way, turn aside out of the path, cause the Holy One of Israel to cease from before us. Get out of the way means depart from the true way of religion, and cause to cease means let us hear no more of His name, for God's holiness is what troubles sinners most. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Therefore thus says the Holy One of Israel: Because you despise this word, and trust in oppression and perverseness, and stay thereon. Isaiah so little yields to their wicked prejudices that he repeats the very name and truth they disliked; this word is Isaiah's exhortation to reliance on Jehovah, oppression is whereby they levied the treasures sent to conciliate Egypt, and perverseness is relying on Egypt rather than Jehovah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Therefore this iniquity will be to you as a breach ready to fall, swelling out in a high wall, whose breaking comes suddenly at an instant. The image is from a curve swelling out in a wall: when it gives way, it causes the downfall of the whole wall, so their Egyptian policy will collapse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He will break it as the breaking of the potters' vessel that is broken in pieces, and he will not spare, so that there will not be found in the bursting of it a sherd to take fire from the hearth, or to take water withal out of the pit. He is the enemy, or rather God, and it is the Jewish state, fragile as a potter's vessel, whose bursting leaves not even a sherd large enough to take up a live coal or water from a cistern; the swell of the wall is at first imperceptible and gradual, but at last comes to the crisis, so the decay of the Jewish state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. For thus says the Lord God, the Holy One of Israel: In returning and rest you will be saved; in quietness and in confidence will be your strength, and you would not. Returning and rest mean turning back from the embassy to Egypt and ceasing from warlike preparations, and quietness answers to waiting for God, but they would not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. But you said: No, for we will flee upon horses, therefore you will flee; and, We will ride upon the swift, therefore will they that pursue you be swift. Flee here means not as fugitives but we will speed our course against the Assyrians by help of cavalry supplied by Egypt, expressly against the Mosaic law; and they will flee before their enemies, for their sin and its punishment correspond. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. One thousand will flee at the rebuke of one; at the rebuke of five you will flee, till you are left as a beacon upon the top of a mountain, and as an ensign on a hill. One thousand at once, or as one man, will flee at the rebuke — the battle cry — of one, and at the rebuke of five all will flee so utterly that even two will not be left together, but each one will be as solitary as a signal staff or a banner on a hill, erected to rally a nation in war; the remnant left will be beacons warning all men of God's justice and the truth of His threatenings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. And therefore the Lord will wait, that he may be gracious unto you, and therefore he will be exalted, that he may have mercy upon you, for the Lord is a God of judgment; blessed are all they that wait for him. Therefore — on account of their perverseness — Jehovah will delay to be gracious, or wait, delay in punishing, to give time for repentance, or yet therefore, because the distress will lead the Jews to repentance and so Jehovah will pity them; he will be exalted, so men will have more elevated views of God's mercy, or he will rise up to pity them, or be far removed, not immediately descending to punish, in order to have mercy. Judgment means justice and faithfulness to His covenant, and wait compares with verse 15: wait for His times of having mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. For the people will dwell in Zion at Jerusalem, and you will weep no more; he will be very gracious unto you at the voice of your cry, and when he hears it, he will answer you. The restoration from Babylon only typifies the full accomplishment of verses 18–33: they will weep no more, as in chapter 25, and at the voice of their cry, as in chapters 26 and Jeremiah 29, God will answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. And though the Lord give you the bread of adversity, and the water of affliction, yet your teachers will not be removed into a corner any more, but your eyes will see your teachers. Rather, The Lord will give the bread of adversity — He will not deny food enough to save them in adversity — and the water of affliction, yet their teachers will not hide themselves any more, but will be openly received with reverence, in contrast to times when teachers were forced to hide from persecution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Your ears will hear a word behind you, saying: This is the way, walk in it, when you turn to the right hand, and when you turn to the left. The word behind is conscience, guided by the Holy Spirit, directing them to the way whenever they turn aside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. You will defile also the covering of your graven images of silver, and the ornament of your molten images of gold, and cast them away as a menstruous cloth, and say unto it: Get you hence. The images are formed of wood or potter's clay and covered with silver, and Hezekiah and afterwards Josiah defiled them, casting them away as unclean, as in chapter 2. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Then he will give the rain of your seed, that you will sow the ground withal, and bread of the increase of the earth, and it will be fat and plenteous; in that day your cattle will feed in large pastures. The rain is rather rain for your seed, for physical prosperity accompanies national piety, especially under the Old Testament: the early rain fell soon after sowing in October or November, the latter rain in spring before ripening, and both were needed for a good harvest; the increase is the produce, and fat is bread made of the best wheat flour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The oxen likewise and the young asses that ear the ground will eat clean provender, which has been winnowed with the shovel and with the fan. Ear means till, and asses were employed in tillage as well as oxen; clean is rather salted provender, for sweet provender is as bread to camels and salted provender as confectionery, so the very cattle will share the coming felicity, or else well-fermented maslin of grain, beans, vetches, hay, and salt. Winnowed means not as usually given to cattle before separation from chaff, for grain will be so abundant it will be given winnowed, with the shovel throwing grain up in the wind and the fan winnowing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. There will be upon every high mountain, and upon every high hill, rivers and streams of waters in the day of the great slaughter, when the towers fall. Even the otherwise barren hills will then be well-watered, and the day is when the disobedient among the Jews are slain, as foretold in verse 16: the towers are mighty men, or else the towers of Sennacherib or of Babylon, types of all God's people's enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Moreover the light of the moon will be as the light of the sun, and the light of the sun will be sevenfold, as the light of seven days, in the day that the Lord binds up the breach of his people, and heals the stroke of their wound. The image from the heavenly bodies expresses the increase of spiritual light and felicity, and sevenfold implies the perfection of that felicity, seven being the sacred number, also to be literally fulfilled hereafter in the heavenly city; the breach is the wound or calamity sent by God on account of their sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Behold, the name of the Lord comes from far, burning with his anger, and the burden thereof is heavy; his lips are full of indignation, and his tongue as a devouring fire. The Lord is Jehovah Himself, represented as a storm approaching and ready to burst over the Assyrians; the burden is heavy, literally grievousness is the flame, the flame darting from Him is grievous, or the uprising cloud is grievous, the gathering cloud gradually rising till it bursts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. His breath, as an overflowing stream, will reach to the midst of the neck, and sift the nations with the sieve of vanity, and there will be a bridle in the jaws of the people, causing them to err. The stream reaches to the neck, the most extreme danger, yet as the head, the capital of Judah, was to be spared, so the head, the sovereign of Assyria, Sennacherib, should escape; the sieve of vanity is rather the winnowing fan of destruction, and the bridle in the jaws is as prisoners are represented in Assyrian inscriptions, causing the peoples, the various races composing the Assyrian armies, to err. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. You will have a song, as in the night when a holy solemnity is kept, and gladness of heart, as when one goes with a pipe to come into the mountain of the Lord, to the mighty One of Israel. The solemnity is as in the passover night when they celebrate deliverance from Egypt, so they will celebrate rescue from Assyrian bondage; going with a pipe, or flute, recalls going up to Jerusalem at the three feasts with music and gladness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The Lord will cause his glorious voice to be heard, and will show the lighting down of his arm, with the indignation of his anger, and with the flame of a devouring fire, with scattering, and tempest, and hailstones. Jehovah's glorious voice, raised against the enemy, is mentioned again in contrast to the music with which His people come to worship Him, and the lighting down of His arm is the descent of His arm in striking, with a scattering blast or inundation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. For through the voice of the Lord the Assyrian will be beaten down, who smote with a rod. The Assyrian rod which beat will itself be beaten, and that by the mere voice of the Lord, an unseen divine agency. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. In every place where the grounded staff passes, which the Lord will lay upon him, it will be with tabrets and harps, and in battles of shaking he will fight with it. Grounded is rather decreed, appointed, and the staff is the avenging rod; every passing through, or infliction, of the appointed rod will be with tabrets, accompanied with joy on the part of the rescued peoples, and in battles of shaking, the shock of battles, God will fight with it, namely Assyria. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. For Tophet is ordained of old; yes, for the king it is prepared; he has made it deep and large; the pile thereof is fire and much wood; the breath of the Lord, like a stream of brimstone, does kindle it. Tophet is literally a place of abomination, the valley of the sons of Hinnom southeast of Jerusalem, where Israel offered human sacrifices to Moloch by fire, hence a place of burning, and latterly Gehenna, the receptacle of the city's refuse, consumed by constantly burning fires, hence hell, the place of torment. In the former sense it fitly symbolises the funeral pyre of the Assyrian army, not that it actually perished there, for the Hebrews buried their dead, but the heathen Assyrians are to be burnt as a mark of ignominy; in the latter sense Tophet is the receptacle prepared for the devil, antitype to the king of chapter 14, and his angels, and unbelieving men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Tophet. Literally a place of abomination, the note on verse 33 says: the valley of the sons of Hinnom south-east of Jerusalem, where children were offered by fire to Molech, and so an image of burning judgment. Interpreted Study note on verse v33, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 13. Therefore this iniquity will be to you as a breach ready to fall, swelling out in a high wall, whose breaking comes suddenly at an instant. The image is from a curve swelling out in a wall: when it gives way, it causes the downfall of the whole wall, so their Egyptian policy will collapse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He will break it as the breaking of the potters' vessel that is broken in pieces, and he will not spare, so that there will not be found in the bursting of it a sherd to take fire from the hearth, or to take water withal out of the pit. He is the enemy, or rather God, and it is the Jewish state, fragile as a potter's vessel, whose bursting leaves not even a sherd large enough to take up a live coal or water from a cistern; the swell of the wall is at first imperceptible and gradual, but at last comes to the crisis, so the decay of the Jewish state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. For thus says the Lord God, the Holy One of Israel: In returning and rest you will be saved; in quietness and in confidence will be your strength, and you would not. Returning and rest mean turning back from the embassy to Egypt and ceasing from warlike preparations, and quietness answers to waiting for God, but they would not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. But you said: No, for we will flee upon horses, therefore you will flee; and, We will ride upon the swift, therefore will they that pursue you be swift. Flee here means not as fugitives but we will speed our course against the Assyrians by help of cavalry supplied by Egypt, expressly against the Mosaic law; and they will flee before their enemies, for their sin and its punishment correspond. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. One thousand will flee at the rebuke of one; at the rebuke of five you will flee, till you are left as a beacon upon the top of a mountain, and as an ensign on a hill. One thousand at once, or as one man, will flee at the rebuke — the battle cry — of one, and at the rebuke of five all will flee so utterly that even two will not be left together, but each one will be as solitary as a signal staff or a banner on a hill, erected to rally a nation in war; the remnant left will be beacons warning all men of God's justice and the truth of His threatenings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. And therefore the Lord will wait, that he may be gracious unto you, and therefore he will be exalted, that he may have mercy upon you, for the Lord is a God of judgment; blessed are all they that wait for him. Therefore — on account of their perverseness — Jehovah will delay to be gracious, or wait, delay in punishing, to give time for repentance, or yet therefore, because the distress will lead the Jews to repentance and so Jehovah will pity them; he will be exalted, so men will have more elevated views of God's mercy, or he will rise up to pity them, or be far removed, not immediately descending to punish, in order to have mercy. Judgment means justice and faithfulness to His covenant, and wait compares with verse 15: wait for His times of having mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. For the people will dwell in Zion at Jerusalem, and you will weep no more; he will be very gracious unto you at the voice of your cry, and when he hears it, he will answer you. The restoration from Babylon only typifies the full accomplishment of verses 18–33: they will weep no more, as in chapter 25, and at the voice of their cry, as in chapters 26 and Jeremiah 29, God will answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. And though the Lord give you the bread of adversity, and the water of affliction, yet your teachers will not be removed into a corner any more, but your eyes will see your teachers. Rather, The Lord will give the bread of adversity — He will not deny food enough to save them in adversity — and the water of affliction, yet their teachers will not hide themselves any more, but will be openly received with reverence, in contrast to times when teachers were forced to hide from persecution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Your ears will hear a word behind you, saying: This is the way, walk in it, when you turn to the right hand, and when you turn to the left. The word behind is conscience, guided by the Holy Spirit, directing them to the way whenever they turn aside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. You will defile also the covering of your graven images of silver, and the ornament of your molten images of gold, and cast them away as a menstruous cloth, and say unto it: Get you hence. The images are formed of wood or potter's clay and covered with silver, and Hezekiah and afterwards Josiah defiled them, casting them away as unclean, as in chapter 2. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Then he will give the rain of your seed, that you will sow the ground withal, and bread of the increase of the earth, and it will be fat and plenteous; in that day your cattle will feed in large pastures. The rain is rather rain for your seed, for physical prosperity accompanies national piety, especially under the Old Testament: the early rain fell soon after sowing in October or November, the latter rain in spring before ripening, and both were needed for a good harvest; the increase is the produce, and fat is bread made of the best wheat flour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The oxen likewise and the young asses that ear the ground will eat clean provender, which has been winnowed with the shovel and with the fan. Ear means till, and asses were employed in tillage as well as oxen; clean is rather salted provender, for sweet provender is as bread to camels and salted provender as confectionery, so the very cattle will share the coming felicity, or else well-fermented maslin of grain, beans, vetches, hay, and salt. Winnowed means not as usually given to cattle before separation from chaff, for grain will be so abundant it will be given winnowed, with the shovel throwing grain up in the wind and the fan winnowing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. There will be upon every high mountain, and upon every high hill, rivers and streams of waters in the day of the great slaughter, when the towers fall. Even the otherwise barren hills will then be well-watered, and the day is when the disobedient among the Jews are slain, as foretold in verse 16: the towers are mighty men, or else the towers of Sennacherib or of Babylon, types of all God's people's enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Moreover the light of the moon will be as the light of the sun, and the light of the sun will be sevenfold, as the light of seven days, in the day that the Lord binds up the breach of his people, and heals the stroke of their wound. The image from the heavenly bodies expresses the increase of spiritual light and felicity, and sevenfold implies the perfection of that felicity, seven being the sacred number, also to be literally fulfilled hereafter in the heavenly city; the breach is the wound or calamity sent by God on account of their sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Behold, the name of the Lord comes from far, burning with his anger, and the burden thereof is heavy; his lips are full of indignation, and his tongue as a devouring fire. The Lord is Jehovah Himself, represented as a storm approaching and ready to burst over the Assyrians; the burden is heavy, literally grievousness is the flame, the flame darting from Him is grievous, or the uprising cloud is grievous, the gathering cloud gradually rising till it bursts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. His breath, as an overflowing stream, will reach to the midst of the neck, and sift the nations with the sieve of vanity, and there will be a bridle in the jaws of the people, causing them to err. The stream reaches to the neck, the most extreme danger, yet as the head, the capital of Judah, was to be spared, so the head, the sovereign of Assyria, Sennacherib, should escape; the sieve of vanity is rather the winnowing fan of destruction, and the bridle in the jaws is as prisoners are represented in Assyrian inscriptions, causing the peoples, the various races composing the Assyrian armies, to err. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. You will have a song, as in the night when a holy solemnity is kept, and gladness of heart, as when one goes with a pipe to come into the mountain of the Lord, to the mighty One of Israel. The solemnity is as in the passover night when they celebrate deliverance from Egypt, so they will celebrate rescue from Assyrian bondage; going with a pipe, or flute, recalls going up to Jerusalem at the three feasts with music and gladness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The Lord will cause his glorious voice to be heard, and will show the lighting down of his arm, with the indignation of his anger, and with the flame of a devouring fire, with scattering, and tempest, and hailstones. Jehovah's glorious voice, raised against the enemy, is mentioned again in contrast to the music with which His people come to worship Him, and the lighting down of His arm is the descent of His arm in striking, with a scattering blast or inundation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. For through the voice of the Lord the Assyrian will be beaten down, who smote with a rod. The Assyrian rod which beat will itself be beaten, and that by the mere voice of the Lord, an unseen divine agency. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. In every place where the grounded staff passes, which the Lord will lay upon him, it will be with tabrets and harps, and in battles of shaking he will fight with it. Grounded is rather decreed, appointed, and the staff is the avenging rod; every passing through, or infliction, of the appointed rod will be with tabrets, accompanied with joy on the part of the rescued peoples, and in battles of shaking, the shock of battles, God will fight with it, namely Assyria. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. For Tophet is ordained of old; yes, for the king it is prepared; he has made it deep and large; the pile thereof is fire and much wood; the breath of the Lord, like a stream of brimstone, does kindle it. Tophet is literally a place of abomination, the valley of the sons of Hinnom southeast of Jerusalem, where Israel offered human sacrifices to Moloch by fire, hence a place of burning, and latterly Gehenna, the receptacle of the city's refuse, consumed by constantly burning fires, hence hell, the place of torment. In the former sense it fitly symbolises the funeral pyre of the Assyrian army, not that it actually perished there, for the Hebrews buried their dead, but the heathen Assyrians are to be burnt as a mark of ignominy; in the latter sense Tophet is the receptacle prepared for the devil, antitype to the king of chapter 14, and his angels, and unbelieving men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
