The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Sirach 32 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 If thou be made the master [of a feast,] lift not thyself up, but be among them as one of the rest; take diligent care for them, and so sit down.

2 And when thou hast done all thy office, take thy place, that thou mayest be merry with them, and receive a crown for thy well ordering of the feast.

3 Speak, thou that art the elder, for it becometh thee, but with sound judgment; and hinder not musick.

4 Pour not out words where there is a musician, and shew not forth wisdom out of time.

5 A concert of musick in a banquet of wine is as a signet of carbuncle set in gold.

6 As a signet of an emerald set in a work of gold, so is the melody of musick with pleasant wine.

7 Speak, young man, if there be need of thee: and yet scarcely when thou art twice asked.

8 Let thy speech be short, comprehending much in few words; be as one that knoweth and yet holdeth his tongue.

9 If thou be among great men, make not thyself equal with them; and when ancient men are in place, use not many words.

10 Before the thunder goeth lightning; and before a shamefaced man shall go favour.

11 Rise up betimes, and be not the last; but get thee home without delay.

12 There take thy pastime, and do what thou wilt: but sin not by proud speech.

13 And for these things bless him that made thee, and hath replenished thee with his good things.

14 Whoso feareth the Lord will receive his discipline; and they that seek him early shall find favour.

15 He that seeketh the law shall be filled therewith: but the hypocrite will be offended thereat.

16 They that fear the Lord shall find judgment, and shall kindle justice as a light.

17 A sinful man will not be reproved, but findeth an excuse according to his will.

18 A man of counsel will be considerate; but a strange and proud man is not daunted with fear, even when of himself he hath done without counsel.

19 Do nothing without advice; and when thou hast once done, repent not.

20 Go not in a way wherein thou mayest fall, and stumble not among the stones.

21 Be not confident in a plain way.

22 And beware of thine own children.

23 In every good work trust thy own soul; for this is the keeping of the commandments.

24 He that believeth in the Lord taketh heed to the commandment; and he that trusteth in him shall fare never the worse.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

If made master of a feast, do not lift yourself up but be among the guests as one of the rest and take diligent care for them. For all such enjoyments, bless the Maker who replenished you with good things, and remember that belief in the Lord shows itself in heeding the commandment. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. If you are made master of a feast, do not lift yourself up, but be among them as one of the rest; take diligent care for them, and so sit down. A feast-master should serve humbly among the guests, caring diligently before sitting down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. When you have done all your office, take your place, that you may be merry with them, and receive a crown for your well ordering of the feast. After the work is done, merriment follows, and good ordering earns a crown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. Speak, you who are the elder, for it becomes you, but with sound judgment, and do not hinder music. Elders should speak, as befits them, with sound judgment, without stopping the music. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

  • Verse 5. A concert of music in a banquet of wine is as a signet of carbuncle set in gold. Music at a wine banquet is like a carbuncle signet set in gold, precious ornament. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. As a signet of an emerald set in a work of gold, so is the melody of music with pleasant wine. Melody with pleasant wine is like an emerald signet in gold work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. Speak, young man, if there is need of you, and yet scarcely when you are twice asked. A young man should speak only if needed, and scarcely even when twice asked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Let your speech be short, comprehending much in few words; be as one who knows and yet holds his tongue. Youthful speech should be short, much in few words, like one who knows yet holds his tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. If you are among great men, do not make yourself equal with them, and when ancient men are in place, do not use many words. Among the great, equality must not be claimed, and before ancients, words must be few. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

  • Verse 12. There take your pastime, and do what you will, but do not sin by proud speech. At home, pastime and freedom are allowed, but not sin by proud speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

  • Verse 16. Those who fear the Lord will find judgment, and will kindle justice as a light. The God-fearing find judgment and kindle justice like a light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. A sinful man will not be reproved, but finds an excuse according to his will. The sinful man refuses reproof and invents excuses to suit his will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. A man of counsel will be considerate, but a strange and proud man is not daunted with fear, even when he has done without counsel. Counsel makes a man considerate, while the strange, proud man fears nothing even after acting without counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. Do nothing without advice, and when you have once done, do not repent. Nothing should be done without advice, and once done on advice, it should not be repented of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 20. Do not go in a way where you may fall, and do not stumble among the stones. Dangerous ways and stony paths must be avoided. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 21. Do not be confident in a plain way. Even a plain way should not breed overconfidence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 22. Beware of your own children. Even one's own children require wariness, since family can also endanger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 23. In every good work trust your own soul, for this is the keeping of the commandments. In every good work, trusting one's own soul is the keeping of the commandments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 24. He who believes in the Lord takes heed to the commandment, and he who trusts in him will fare never the worse. Belief in God shows in heeding commandments, and trust in him never fares worse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
