The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And as they spake unto the people, the priests, and the captain of the temple, and the Sadducees, came upon them,

2 Being grieved that they taught the people, and preached through Jesus the resurrection from the dead.

3 And they laid hands on them, and put them in hold unto the next day: for it was now eventide.

4 Howbeit many of them which heard the word believed; and the number of the men was about five thousand.

5 And it came to pass on the morrow, that their rulers, and elders, and scribes,

6 And Annas the high priest, and Caiaphas, and John, and Alexander, and as many as were of the kindred of the high priest, were gathered together at Jerusalem.

7 And when they had set them in the midst, they asked, By what power, or by what name, have ye done this?

8 Then Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, said unto them, Ye rulers of the people, and elders of Israel,

9 If we this day be examined of the good deed done to the impotent man, by what means he is made whole;

10 Be it known unto you all, and to all the people of Israel, that by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom ye crucified, whom God raised from the dead, even by him doth this man stand here before you whole.

11 This is the stone which was set at nought of you builders, which is become the head of the corner.

12 Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.

13 Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.

14 And beholding the man which was healed standing with them, they could say nothing against it.

15 But when they had commanded them to go aside out of the council, they conferred among themselves,

16 Saying, What shall we do to these men? for that indeed a notable miracle hath been done by them is manifest to all them that dwell in Jerusalem; and we cannot deny it.

17 But that it spread no further among the people, let us straitly threaten them, that they speak henceforth to no man in this name.

18 And they called them, and commanded them not to speak at all nor teach in the name of Jesus.

19 But Peter and John answered and said unto them, Whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye.

20 For we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard.

21 So when they had further threatened them, they let them go, finding nothing how they might punish them, because of the people: for all men glorified God for that which was done.

22 For the man was above forty years old, on whom this miracle of healing was shewed.

23 And being let go, they went to their own company, and reported all that the chief priests and elders had said unto them.

24 And when they heard that, they lifted up their voice to God with one accord, and said, Lord, thou art God, which hast made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and all that in them is:

25 Who by the mouth of thy servant David hast said, Why did the heathen rage, and the people imagine vain things?

26 The kings of the earth stood up, and the rulers were gathered together against the Lord, and against his Christ.

27 For of a truth against thy holy child Jesus, whom thou hast anointed, both Herod, and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles, and the people of Israel, were gathered together,

28 For to do whatsoever thy hand and thy counsel determined before to be done.

29 And now, Lord, behold their threatenings: and grant unto thy servants, that with all boldness they may speak thy word,

30 By stretching forth thine hand to heal; and that signs and wonders may be done by the name of thy holy child Jesus.

31 And when they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were assembled together; and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they spake the word of God with boldness.

32 And the multitude of them that believed were of one heart and of one soul: neither said any of them that ought of the things which he possessed was his own; but they had all things common.

33 And with great power gave the apostles witness of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus: and great grace was upon them all.

34 Neither was there any among them that lacked: for as many as were possessors of lands or houses sold them, and brought the prices of the things that were sold,

35 And laid them down at the apostles’ feet: and distribution was made unto every man according as he had need.

36 And Joses, who by the apostles was surnamed Barnabas, (which is, being interpreted, The son of consolation,) a Levite, and of the country of Cyprus,

37 Having land, sold it, and brought the money, and laid it at the apostles’ feet.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After the healing, Peter and John are arrested, questioned by the council about the power behind the miracle, and boldly answer that the man stands whole by the name of Jesus, in whom alone there is salvation. Threatened and released, they pray with the believers, and the community shares its possessions freely, Barnabas giving a notable example. Interpreted Overview of this chapter's text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. As they spake to the people, the priests, the captain of the temple and the Sadducees came upon them. Henry's general point for the passage is that the harmless and useful servants of Christ are often troubled for their work of faith and labour of love, while wicked men escape, yet obeying Christ's precepts brings his support. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. They were grieved that the apostles taught the people and preached through Jesus the resurrection from the dead. Henry explains what was at stake: the resurrection preached through Jesus includes all the happiness of the future state, to be had through him only, so miserable is the case of those to whom the glory of Christ's kingdom is a grief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They laid hands on them and put them in hold until the next day, for it was now eventide. Henry notes that to this day reading the Scriptures, social prayer and religious conversation meet with frowns and checks, but if we obey the precepts of Christ, he will support us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Set in the midst, they were asked by what power or by what name they had done this. Henry answers with Peter's own answer: the miracle had been wrought by the name, or power, of Jesus of Nazareth, the Messiah whom the rulers had crucified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Then Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, said unto them, Ye rulers of the people and elders of Israel. Henry stresses the filling: Peter would have all understand the miracle was by the name of Jesus, and this confirmed the apostles' testimony to his resurrection, which proved him to be the Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Be it known unto you all that by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom ye crucified, whom God raised from the dead, doth this man stand here before you whole. Henry presses the alternative: these rulers must either be saved by that Jesus whom they had crucified, or perish for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Neither is there salvation in any other, for there is none other name under heaven given among men whereby we must be saved. Henry generalizes the claim: the name of Jesus is given to men of every age and nation as that whereby alone believers are saved from the wrath to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. When they saw the boldness of Peter and John and perceived they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled and took knowledge that they had been with Jesus. Henry turns this into a rule for followers of Christ: act so that all who converse with them may take knowledge they have been with Jesus, which makes them holy, heavenly, spiritual and cheerful, and raises them above this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Beholding the man which was healed standing with them, they could say nothing against it. Henry's point for the council's deliberations fits here first: they cannot say the doctrine is false or dangerous or of any ill tendency, and are ashamed to own the true reason for opposing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Commanded to go aside out of the council, the apostles left the rulers conferring among themselves. Henry diagnoses their care: all of it is that the doctrine of Christ spread not among the people, because it testifies against their hypocrisy, wickedness and tyranny. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. What shall we do to these men, for a notable miracle hath been done by them and we cannot deny it? Henry repeats the rulers' own admission as the key: the miracle is manifest to all in Jerusalem, yet their concern is only containment, not truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. But that it spread no further among the people, let us straitly threaten them that they speak henceforth to no man in this name. Henry reads the threat as the rulers' whole policy: they cannot refute the work, so they aim only to stop its spread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. They called them and commanded them not to speak at all nor teach in the name of Jesus. Henry contrasts threatenings with promises: those who know how to put a just value upon Christ's promises know how to put just contempt upon the world's threatenings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Peter and John answered, Whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye. Henry's rule is to guide conduct by the fixed standard of truth, not by the shifting opinions and fancies of men, and to beware the vain attempt to serve two masters, God and the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. For we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard. Henry explains the apostles' refusal: they look with concern on perishing souls who cannot escape eternal ruin but by Jesus Christ, and therefore are faithful in warning and showing the right way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Being let go, they went to their own company and reported all that the chief priests and elders had said. Henry observes that Christ's followers do best in company, provided it is their own company, and that it encourages God's servants in doing and suffering work to serve the God who made all things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. They lifted up their voice to God with one accord: Lord, thou art God, which hast made heaven and earth and the sea and all that in them is. Henry draws the comfort: they serve the God who made all things and therefore has the disposal of all events, and the Scriptures must be fulfilled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Who by the mouth of thy servant David hast said, Why did the heathen rage, and the people imagine vain things? Henry treats the prayer as resting on fulfilled Scripture: what David spoke is now coming to pass, and the servants' confidence rests on a God whose word is being kept. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. For of a truth against thy holy child Jesus, whom thou hast anointed, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles and the people of Israel, were gathered together. Henry explains the anointing: Jesus was anointed to be a Saviour, therefore it was determined he should be a sacrifice to make atonement for sin, though sin is not the less evil for God's bringing good out of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. For to do whatsoever thy hand and thy counsel determined before to be done. Henry holds the two truths together: the sacrifice was determined by God's counsel, yet the evil of the deeds is not lessened because God brings good out of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And now, Lord, behold their threatenings, and grant unto thy servants that with all boldness they may speak thy word. Henry notes what they do not pray: not that troubles may be prevented, but that they may go on with cheerfulness and courage in their work and duty, not fearing the face of man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. By stretching forth thine hand to heal, and that signs and wonders may be done by the name of thy holy child Jesus. Henry generalizes the prayer: those who desire divine aid and encouragement may depend upon having them, and ought to go forth and go on in the strength of the Lord God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. When they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were assembled, and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost and spake the word of God with boldness. Henry reads the shaking as God's sign of acceptance, given that their faith might be established and unshaken, with greater degrees of his Spirit enabling bold speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The multitude of them that believed were of one heart and of one soul, neither said any of them that aught of the things he possessed was his own, but they had all things common. Henry calls this love the blessed fruit of Christ's dying precept and dying prayer, possible because they sat so loose to the wealth of this world, dead to it and expecting to be stripped of all for cleaving to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. With great power gave the apostles witness of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and great grace was upon them all. Henry says the doctrine preached was the resurrection of Christ, a matter of fact which, duly explained, was a summary of all the duties, privileges and comforts of Christians, with evident fruits of grace in all they said and did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Neither was there any among them that lacked, for as many as were possessors of lands or houses sold them and brought the prices of the things sold. Henry explains the sharing: they did not take away others' property but were indifferent to their own, having in affection forsaken all for Christ, so that care was taken for everyone's supply. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And laid them down at the apostles' feet, and distribution was made unto every man according as he had need. Henry adds the stewardship rule: great care ought to be taken in distributing public charity, that it be given to such as have need, especially those reduced to want for well-doing and for the testimony of a good conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Joses, surnamed by the apostles Barnabas, the son of consolation, a Levite of Cyprus, is named for this generous charity. Henry notes he was one designed to be a preacher of the gospel, and so disentangled himself from the affairs of this life, a disposition that gives the testimony very great power upon others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Having land, Barnabas sold it and brought the money and laid it at the apostles' feet. Henry presents him as the particular example of the common life: when such generous dispositions prevail and are exercised according to the circumstances of the times, the testimony has very great power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • Sadducees. The priestly party in the council who opposed the apostles' preaching of resurrection through Jesus. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • straitly threaten. To threaten strictly or severely; the council's order that the apostles speak no more in Jesus' name. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • captain of the temple. The officer in charge of the temple guard, who came upon the apostles with the priests and Sadducees. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
