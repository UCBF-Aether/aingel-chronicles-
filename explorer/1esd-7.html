The Text — 1 Esdras 7 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Then Sisinnes the governor of Celosyria and Phenice, and Sathrabuzanes, with their companions following the commandments of king Darius,

2 Did very carefully oversee the holy works, assisting the ancients of the Jews and governors of the temple.

3 And so the holy works prospered, when Aggeus and Zacharias the prophets prophesied.

4 And they finished these things by the commandment of the Lord God of Israel, and with the consent of Cyrus, Darius, and Artexerxes, kings of Persia.

5 And thus was the holy house finished in the three and twentieth day of the month Adar, in the sixth year of Darius king of the Persians

6 And the children of Israel, the priests, and the Levites, and others that were of the captivity, that were added unto them, did according to the things written in the book of Moses.

7 And to the dedication of the temple of the Lord they offered an hundred bullocks two hundred rams, four hundred lambs;

8 And twelve goats for the sin of all Israel, according to the number of the chief of the tribes of Israel.

9 The priests also and the Levites stood arrayed in their vestments, according to their kindreds, in the service of the Lord God of Israel, according to the book of Moses: and the porters at every gate.

10 And the children of Israel that were of the captivity held the passover the fourteenth day of the first month, after that the priests and the Levites were sanctified.

11 They that were of the captivity were not all sanctified together: but the Levites were all sanctified together.

12 And so they offered the passover for all them of the captivity, and for their brethren the priests, and for themselves.

13 And the children of Israel that came out of the captivity did eat, even all they that had separated themselves from the abominations of the people of the land, and sought the Lord.

14 And they kept the feast of unleavened bread seven days, making merry before the Lord,

15 For that he had turned the counsel of the king of Assyria toward them, to strengthen their hands in the works of the Lord God of Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

The governors obey Darius's decree speedily, the elders prosper through the prophesying of Haggai and Zechariah, and the house is finished and dedicated with great joy, with offerings including twelve he-goats for all Israel. The passover is kept, the priests and Levites set in their courses, and the chapter closes noting that the Lord had turned the counsel of the king of Assyria toward them to strengthen their hands. Unfamiliar terms

  • He-goats. the twelve goats offered at the dedication, one for each tribe of Israel (verse 8). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Celosyria and Phenice. the province governed by Sisinnes, who carries out Darius's decree (verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. JFB notes Tatnai and his companions did speedily what the decree commanded, a concurrence of favorable events accelerating the restoration and infusing new spirit and energy into the workmen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. JFB notes the governor and his companions furthered the work speedily, and the workmen labored with unabating assiduity till the temple was brought to completion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. JFB notes the prophets' appeals had given a new impulse to the building, and that the concurrence of favorable events under Darius accelerated the work to completion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. JFB notes the temple's foundation was laid in April 536 B.C. and it was completed on February 21, 515 B.C., twenty-one years after it was begun, by the commandment of God and with the consent of the Persian kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. JFB notes the temple was completed on February 21, 515 B.C., being twenty-one years after its foundation was laid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. JFB notes the dedication was kept with demonstrations of the liveliest joy, since the aged who had wept at the laying of the foundation were most, if not all, now dead, and all rejoiced at the completion of this national undertaking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. JFB notes the twelve he-goats correspond to the dedication of the tabernacle, as in Numbers 7:87 and 8:17. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. JFB notes the priests were set in their divisions and the Levites in their courses as written in the book of Moses, and that although David arranged the courses by families, it was Moses who assigned to priests and Levites their rights, privileges, stations, and duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. JFB notes those who had separated themselves from the filthiness of the heathen had given satisfactory evidence of being true proselytes, not only renouncing impure idolatrous worship but undergoing circumcision, a condition indispensable to participation in the Passover. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. JFB notes the religious joy characterizing the feast was testified by lively gratitude to God, whose overruling power and converting grace had produced so marvelous a change in the hearts of mighty potentates, disposing them, pagans though they were, to aid the cause and provide for the worship of the true God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. JFB notes the Lord turned the heart of the king of Assyria, that is, the king of the Persian empire which now included Assyria's possessions and surpassed its glory, and that his favorable disposition secured the Jews peace, prosperity, and the privileges of their own religion during the rest of his reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
