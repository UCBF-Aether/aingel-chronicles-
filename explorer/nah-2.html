The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Nahum 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 He that dasheth in pieces is come up before thy face: keep the munition, watch the way, make thy loins strong, fortify thy power mightily.

2 For the LORD hath turned away the excellency of Jacob, as the excellency of Israel: for the emptiers have emptied them out, and marred their vine branches.

3 The shield of his mighty men is made red, the valiant men are in scarlet: the chariots shall be with flaming torches in the day of his preparation, and the fir trees shall be terribly shaken.

4 The chariots shall rage in the streets, they shall justle one against another in the broad ways: they shall seem like torches, they shall run like the lightnings.

5 He shall recount his worthies: they shall stumble in their walk; they shall make haste to the wall thereof, and the defence shall be prepared.

6 The gates of the rivers shall be opened, and the palace shall be dissolved.

7 And Huzzab shall be led away captive, she shall be brought up, and her maids shall lead her as with the voice of doves, tabering upon their breasts.

8 But Nineveh is of old like a pool of water: yet they shall flee away. Stand, stand, shall they cry; but none shall look back.

9 Take ye the spoil of silver, take the spoil of gold: for there is none end of the store and glory out of all the pleasant furniture.

10 She is empty, and void, and waste: and the heart melteth, and the knees smite together, and much pain is in all loins, and the faces of them all gather blackness.

11 Where is the dwelling of the lions, and the feedingplace of the young lions, where the lion, even the old lion, walked, and the lion’s whelp, and none made them afraid?

12 The lion did tear in pieces enough for his whelps, and strangled for his lionesses, and filled his holes with prey, and his dens with ravin.

13 Behold, I am against thee, saith the LORD of hosts, and I will burn her chariots in the smoke, and the sword shall devour thy young lions: and I will cut off thy prey from the earth, and the voice of thy messengers shall no more be heard.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

He that dashes in pieces is come up before Nineveh, and the chapter stages the assault in present tense: shields made red, valiant men in scarlet, chariots like flaming torches raging in the streets, river gates opened, the palace dissolved, and Huzzab led away captive with her maids mourning like doves. Nineveh, once like a pool of water, flees while spoil of silver and gold is taken, and the lion's den where prey was torn for whelps and lionesses stands empty, because the LORD of hosts is against her. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He that dashes in pieces is come up before thy face, keep the munition, watch the way, make thy loins strong, fortify thy power mightily, the dasher being God's battle axe, the Medo-Babylonian army under Cyaxares and Nabopolassar, and the advice to defend being ironical, since all defence will be in vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. For the LORD has turned away the excellency of Jacob, as the excellency of Israel, for the emptiers have emptied them out and marred their vine branches, the time for Nineveh's overthrow being ripe because Judah and Israel have been sufficiently chastised, and the Assyrian rod, having done its work, is to be thrown into the fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The shield of his mighty men is made red, valiant men are in scarlet, chariots shall be with flaming torches in the day of his preparation, and fir trees shall be terribly shaken, the Medo-Babylonian general's men dyeing bull's-hide shields red lest blood be perceived, wearing crimson tunics, and their chariots flashing like torches with glitter of steel weapons, their fir-tree lances brandished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The chariots shall rage in the streets, they shall jostle one against another in the broad ways, they shall seem like torches, they shall run like lightnings, driven in furious haste through large open spaces in Nineveh's suburbs, flashing in the sun with rapid violence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He shall recount his worthies, they shall stumble in their walk, they shall make haste to the wall, and the defence shall be prepared, the Assyrian king reviewing his nobles and choosing the bravest for the walls in vain, stumbling through fear and hurry, while the covering machine of the besiegers advances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The gates of the rivers shall be opened, and the palace shall be dissolved, the Tigris river wall being 4,530 yards long with moats and dams that the foe turned to a dry bed, and in the earlier capture the river by flood breaking walls twenty furlongs, fire and water being the means of destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Huzzab shall be led away captive, she shall be brought up, and her maids shall lead her as with the voice of doves, tabering upon their breasts, Nineveh personified as a queen long standing in supreme prosperity, now laid bare and stripped, her people going up to Babylon, her handmaids moaning dove-like and beating their breasts as tambourines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. But Nineveh is of old like a pool of water, yet they shall flee away, Stand, stand, shall they cry, but none shall look back, though Nineveh has from earliest times been defended by surrounding water, her inhabitants flee panic-stricken, and patriotic cries to stand go unheeded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Take the spoil of silver, take the spoil of gold, for there is none end of the store and glory out of all the pleasant furniture, the conquerors being summoned to plunder riches from tribute and merchandise, gold and silver being taken for spoil before palaces were fired, as the ruins confirm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. She is empty, and void, and waste, and the heart melts, and knees smite together, and much pain is in all loins, and faces gather blackness, literally emptiness, emptiedness, devastation, a climax of substantives intensifying gloom, faces growing pale. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Where is the dwelling of the lions, and the feedingplace of the young lions, where the lion, even the old lion, walked, and the lion's whelp, and none made them afraid, Nineveh being the seat of rapacious warriors typified by lions, frequent in Assyrian sculpture, full of spoils as a lion's den, and the question expressing wonder that its very site could not be found. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The lion did tear in pieces enough for his whelps, and strangled for his lionesses, and filled his holes with prey, and his dens with ravin, the Assyrian lion filling its dens with prey of different kinds from the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Behold, I am against thee, says the LORD of hosts, and I will burn her chariots in the smoke, and the sword shall devour thy young lions, and I will cut off thy prey from the earth, and the voice of thy messengers shall no more be heard, chariots burnt entirely into smoke, prey cut off from the earth, and royal messengers no longer heard exacting tribute in the provinces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Nineveh. the great Assyrian city, three days' journey across, to which Jonah is sent and whose repentance spares it in Jonah 3, and whose fall Nahum announces. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Huzzab. read in the study note for Nahum 2 as Nineveh herself personified as a queen long standing in supreme prosperity, now led away captive. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
