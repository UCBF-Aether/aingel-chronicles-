The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the twelfth year of Ahaz king of Judah began Hoshea the son of Elah to reign in Samaria over Israel nine years.

2 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, but not as the kings of Israel that were before him.

3 Against him came up Shalmaneser king of Assyria; and Hoshea became his servant, and gave him presents.

4 And the king of Assyria found conspiracy in Hoshea: for he had sent messengers to So king of Egypt, and brought no present to the king of Assyria, as he had done year by year: therefore the king of Assyria shut him up, and bound him in prison.

5 Then the king of Assyria came up throughout all the land, and went up to Samaria, and besieged it three years.

6 In the ninth year of Hoshea the king of Assyria took Samaria, and carried Israel away into Assyria, and placed them in Halah and in Habor by the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes.

7 For so it was, that the children of Israel had sinned against the LORD their God, which had brought them up out of the land of Egypt, from under the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt, and had feared other gods,

8 And walked in the statutes of the heathen, whom the LORD cast out from before the children of Israel, and of the kings of Israel, which they had made.

9 And the children of Israel did secretly those things that were not right against the LORD their God, and they built them high places in all their cities, from the tower of the watchmen to the fenced city.

10 And they set them up images and groves in every high hill, and under every green tree:

11 And there they burnt incense in all the high places, as did the heathen whom the LORD carried away before them; and wrought wicked things to provoke the LORD to anger:

12 For they served idols, whereof the LORD had said unto them, Ye shall not do this thing.

13 Yet the LORD testified against Israel, and against Judah, by all the prophets, and by all the seers, saying, Turn ye from your evil ways, and keep my commandments and my statutes, according to all the law which I commanded your fathers, and which I sent to you by my servants the prophets.

14 Notwithstanding they would not hear, but hardened their necks, like to the neck of their fathers, that did not believe in the LORD their God.

15 And they rejected his statutes, and his covenant that he made with their fathers, and his testimonies which he testified against them; and they followed vanity, and became vain, and went after the heathen that were round about them, concerning whom the LORD had charged them, that they should not do like them.

16 And they left all the commandments of the LORD their God, and made them molten images, even two calves, and made a grove, and worshipped all the host of heaven, and served Baal.

17 And they caused their sons and their daughters to pass through the fire, and used divination and enchantments, and sold themselves to do evil in the sight of the LORD, to provoke him to anger.

18 Therefore the LORD was very angry with Israel, and removed them out of his sight: there was none left but the tribe of Judah only.

19 Also Judah kept not the commandments of the LORD their God, but walked in the statutes of Israel which they made.

20 And the LORD rejected all the seed of Israel, and afflicted them, and delivered them into the hand of spoilers, until he had cast them out of his sight.

21 For he rent Israel from the house of David; and they made Jeroboam the son of Nebat king: and Jeroboam drave Israel from following the LORD, and made them sin a great sin.

22 For the children of Israel walked in all the sins of Jeroboam which he did; they departed not from them;

23 Until the LORD removed Israel out of his sight, as he had said by all his servants the prophets. So was Israel carried away out of their own land to Assyria unto this day.

24 And the king of Assyria brought men from Babylon, and from Cuthah, and from Ava, and from Hamath, and from Sepharvaim, and placed them in the cities of Samaria instead of the children of Israel: and they possessed Samaria, and dwelt in the cities thereof.

25 And so it was at the beginning of their dwelling there, that they feared not the LORD: therefore the LORD sent lions among them, which slew some of them.

26 Wherefore they spake to the king of Assyria, saying, The nations which thou hast removed, and placed in the cities of Samaria, know not the manner of the God of the land: therefore he hath sent lions among them, and, behold, they slay them, because they know not the manner of the God of the land.

27 Then the king of Assyria commanded, saying, Carry thither one of the priests whom ye brought from thence; and let them go and dwell there, and let him teach them the manner of the God of the land.

28 Then one of the priests whom they had carried away from Samaria came and dwelt in Bethel, and taught them how they should fear the LORD.

29 Howbeit every nation made gods of their own, and put them in the houses of the high places which the Samaritans had made, every nation in their cities wherein they dwelt.

30 And the men of Babylon made Succothbenoth, and the men of Cuth made Nergal, and the men of Hamath made Ashima,

31 And the Avites made Nibhaz and Tartak, and the Sepharvites burnt their children in fire to Adrammelech and Anammelech, the gods of Sepharvaim.

32 So they feared the LORD, and made unto themselves of the lowest of them priests of the high places, which sacrificed for them in the houses of the high places.

33 They feared the LORD, and served their own gods, after the manner of the nations whom they carried away from thence.

34 Unto this day they do after the former manners: they fear not the LORD, neither do they after their statutes, or after their ordinances, or after the law and commandment which the LORD commanded the children of Jacob, whom he named Israel;

35 With whom the LORD had made a covenant, and charged them, saying, Ye shall not fear other gods, nor bow yourselves to them, nor serve them, nor sacrifice to them:

36 But the LORD, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt with great power and a stretched out arm, him shall ye fear, and him shall ye worship, and to him shall ye do sacrifice.

37 And the statutes, and the ordinances, and the law, and the commandment, which he wrote for you, ye shall observe to do for evermore; and ye shall not fear other gods.

38 And the covenant that I have made with you ye shall not forget; neither shall ye fear other gods.

39 But the LORD your God ye shall fear; and he shall deliver you out of the hand of all your enemies.

40 Howbeit they did not hearken, but they did after their former manner.

41 So these nations feared the LORD, and served their graven images, both their children, and their children’s children: as did their fathers, so do they unto this day.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Hoshea's conspiracy and Assyria's invasion end Israel: Samaria falls after three years, the people are carried to Assyria, and the chapter explains why, they served idols, rejected the prophets, and walked in other nations' statutes. Foreign peoples resettled in Samaria worship the LORD alongside their own gods, fearing the LORD and serving their graven images. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the twelfth year of Ahaz king of Judah, Hoshea son of Elah began to reign over Israel in Samaria for nine years. JFB reconciles this with the earlier date: Hoshea conspired against Pekah in Pekah's twentieth year, was acknowledged king two years later, and his reign became tranquil and prosperous in Ahaz's twelfth year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Hoshea did evil in the LORD's sight, but not as Israel's kings before him. JFB explains that unlike predecessors from Jeroboam onward, he neither established Baal's rites nor compelled adherence to calf worship, acting as a constitutional king in these respects, yet the nation, demoralized by nineteen idolatrous princes, still faced angry Providence's judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Against Hoshea came up Shalmaneser king of Assyria, and Hoshea became his servant and gave him presents. JFB identifies Shalmaneser with Shalman and with Isaiah's Sargon, whose name has recently been traced on Ninevite monuments in an expedition against a king of Samaria read as Hoshea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Assyria's king found conspiracy in Hoshea, for he had sent messengers to So king of Egypt and brought no present yearly as before, so the king shut him up and bound him in prison. JFB explains Hoshea, after paying tribute several years, withheld it to throw off the yoke, hoping Egypt's famous Ethiopian king Sabaco would help resist Assyria. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Assyria's king came up throughout the land, went up to Samaria, and besieged it three years. JFB notes Shalmaneser, marching against Hoshea, scoured Israel, besieged the capital, and took the king himself, imprisoning him for life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. In Hoshea's ninth year Assyria's king took Samaria, carried Israel away to Assyria, and placed them in Halah, on the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes. JFB renders the places as Halah, the Chabor river of Gozan, and the cities of the Medes, identifying Halah with Calah near Nineveh and the Chabor as a river still bearing the name Khabour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. This befell because Israel had sinned against the LORD their God, who brought them from Egypt, and had feared other gods. JFB heads this long section "Samaria taken, and Israel for their sins carried captive," giving a full and impressive vindication of God's procedure against his highly privileged but rebellious and apostate people, whose patience he had exhausted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Assyria's king brought men from Babylon, Cuthah, Ava, Hamath, and Sepharvaim, and placed them in Samaria's cities instead of Israel's children, and they possessed Samaria and dwelt in its cities. JFB notes this was not Shalmaneser but Esarhaddon, who ordered colonies from his provinces to occupy the vacated places, and not all Israelites were removed — a remnant of the poor remained, mingling with the colonists. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. At the beginning of their dwelling there, they feared not the LORD, so the LORD sent lions among them that slew some of them. JFB explains the land had long been infested with lions, which multiplied as the colonists were too few to replenish it, and the colonists recognized the attacks as judgment from the God of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. They told Assyria's king that the nations he had placed in Samaria's cities knew not the manner of the land's God, so the God of the land sent lions to slay them. JFB notes they petitioned the Assyrian court to send Jewish priests to instruct them in serving him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Assyria's king commanded that one of the priests carried away from Samaria be carried back to dwell there and teach them the manner of the land's God. JFB notes the king sent one of the exiled priests, who established himself at Bethel and taught them how to fear the LORD, though as a calf-worshipping priest his instructions would be neither sound nor efficient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. One of the priests carried away from Samaria came and dwelt in Bethel, teaching them how they should fear the LORD. JFB notes he taught orally rather than from a written book, and, being no Levite but a calf priest, his teaching was imperfect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Howbeit every nation made gods of their own and put them in the houses of the high places the Samaritans had made, each nation in its cities. JFB explains that though instructed in Israel's God's worship, the colonists did not suppose him the only God, but combined his worship with their own gods, forming a promiscuous society with a variety of idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The men of Babylon made Succothbenoth, the men of Cuth made Nergal, and the men of Hamath made Ashima. JFB explains Succothbenoth as the tents or booths of the daughters used in impure Babylonian rites, Nergal as the planet Mars, god of war, in the form of a cock according to Jewish writers, and Ashima as an entirely bald he-goat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. The Avites made Nibhaz and Tartak, and the Sepharvites burnt their children in fire to Adrammelech and Anammelech, Sepharvaim's gods. JFB explains Nibhaz as a dog, Tartak as an ass or a planet of ill omen, probably Saturn, Adrammelech as Molech or the sun in the form of a mule or peacock, and Anammelech as a hare or goat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. So they feared the LORD and made for themselves priests of the lowest of them from the high places, who sacrificed for them there. JFB's account of the colonists' mongrel doctrine covers this mixture: they acknowledged Israel's God while keeping their own gods, forming a mongrel race and a mongrel doctrine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. They feared the LORD and served their own gods, after the manner of the nations whence they were carried away. JFB notes the prevailing character of society about Samaria became heathen, not Israelite, as Assyrian colonists became masters of the land and intermarried with the remnant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Unto this day they do after the former manners: they fear not the LORD, neither do they after his statutes, ordinances, law, or commandment. JFB dates this "unto this day" to the Babylonian exile, when the book was composed, and calls their religion a strange medley of God's service and idols — the first settlement of the people afterwards called Samaritans, after Israel's kingdom, which had lasted three hundred fifty-six years, was overthrown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
