The Text — Jubilees 34 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the sixth year of this week of this forty-fourth jubilee Jacob sent his sons to pasture

2 their sheep, and his servants with them, to the pastures of Shechem. And the seven kings of the Amorites assembled themselves together against them, to slay them, hiding themselves under the trees, and to take their cattle as a prey.

3 And Jacob and Levi and Judah and Joseph were in the house with Isaac their father; for his spirit was sorrowful, and they could not leave him : and Benjamin was the youngest, and for this reason remained with his father.

4 And there came the king[s] of Taphu

5 and the king[s] of 'Aresa, and the king[s] of Seragan, and the king[s] of Selo, and the king[s] of Ga'as, and the king of Bethoron, and the king of Ma'anisakir, and all those who dwell in these mountains (and) who dwell in the woods in the land of Canaan.

6 And they announced this to Jacob saying : " Behold, the kings of the Amorites have surrounded thy sons, and plundered their herds." 6.

7 And he arose from his house, he and his three sons and all the servants of his father, and his own servants, and he went against them with six thousand men, who carried swords.

8 And he slew them in the pastures of Shechem, and pursued those who fled, and he slew them with the edge of the sword, and he slew 'Aresa and Taphu and Saregan and Selo and 'Amanisakir and Ga[ga]'as, and he recovered his herds.

9 And he prevailed over them, and imposed tribute on them that they should pay him tribute, five fruit products of their land, and he built Robel and Tamnatares. 9.

10 And he returned in peace, and made peace with them, and they became his servants, until the day that he and his sons went down into Egypt. 10.

11 And in the seventh year of this week he sent Joseph to learn about the welfare of his brothers from his house to the land of Shechem, and he found them in the land of Dothan.

12 And they dealt treacherously with him, and formed a plot against him to slay him, but changing their minds,

13 they sold him to Ishmaelite merchants, and they brought him down into Egypt, and they sold him to Potiphar, the eunuch of Pharaoh, the chief of the cooks, priest of the city of 'Elew.

14 And the sons of Jacob slaughtered a kid, and dipped the coat of Joseph in the blood, and sent (it) to Jacob their father on the tenth of the seventh month.

15 And he mourned all that night, for they had brought it to him in the evening, and he became feverish with mourning for his death, and he said : " An evil beast hath devoured Joseph " ; and all the members of his house [mourned with him that day, and they] were grieving and mourning with him all that day.

16 And his sons and his daughter rose up to comfort him, but he refused to be comforted for his son.

17 And on that day Bilhah heard that Joseph had perished, and she died mourning him, and she was living in Qafratef, and Dinah also, his daughter, died after Joseph had perished.

18 And there came these three mournings upon Israel in one month.

19 And they buried Bilhah over against the tomb of Rachel, and Dinah also, his daughter, they buried there.

20 And he mourned for Joseph one year, and did not cease, for he said " Let me go down to the grave mourning for my son." 18.

21 For this reason it is ordained for the children of Israel that they should afflict themselves on the tenth of the seventh month — on the day that the news which made him weep for Joseph came to Jacob his father — that they should make atonement for themselves thereon with a young goat on the tenth of the seventh month,

22 once a year, for their sins; for they had grieved the affection of their father regarding Joseph his son.

23 And this day hath been ordained that they should grieve thereon for their sins, and for all their transgressions and for all their errors, so that they might cleanse themselves on that day once a year.

24 The Wives of Jacob's Sons.

25 And after Joseph perished, the sons of Jacob took unto themselves wives.

26 The name of Reuben's wife is 'Ada ; and the name of Simeon's wife is 'Adiba'a, and the name of Levi's wife is Melka, of the daughters of Aram, of the seed of the sons of Terah ; and the name of Judah's wife, Betasu'el, a Canaanite ; and the name of Issachar's wife, Hezaqa ; and the name of Zabulon's wife, Ni'iman ; and the name of Dan's wife, 'Egla ; and the name of Naphtali's wife, Rasu'u, of Mesopotamia ; and the name of Gad's wife, Maka ; and the name of Asher's wife, 'Ijona ; and the name of Joseph's wife, Asenath, the Egyptian ; and the name of Benjamin's wife, 'Ijasaka. And Simeon repented, and took a second wife from Mesopotamia as his brothers.

27 Rebecca's Last Admonitions and Death

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 11. In the seventh year of this week Jacob sent Joseph to learn about the welfare of his brothers from his house to Shechem, and he found them in Dothan. Henry notes how readily Joseph waited on his father's orders, and that the best beloved children should be the most ready to obey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Joseph's brothers dealt treacherously with him and formed a plot against him to slay him, but then changed their minds. Henry stresses how deliberate they were against him, resolving on his death from malice aforethought and in cold blood, because their father loved him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The brothers sold Joseph to Ishmaelite merchants, who brought him down into Egypt and sold him to Potiphar, Pharaoh's eunuch and chief of the cooks. Henry notes that the brethren were wonderfully restrained from murdering him, and that their selling him was as wonderfully turned to God's praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The sons slaughtered a kid, dipped Joseph's coat in the blood, and sent it to Jacob on the tenth of the seventh month. Henry warns that when Satan has taught men one sin, he teaches them to conceal it with another, hiding theft and murder with lying and false oaths. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Jacob mourned all that night, for the coat was brought in the evening, and he became feverish with mourning, saying an evil beast had devoured Joseph, while all his house grieved with him all that day. Henry bids the reader suppose the agony of poor Jacob, hastily concluding from the bloody coat that Joseph was rent in pieces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Jacob's sons and daughter rose up to comfort him, but he refused to be comforted for his son. Henry notes they were miserable, hypocritical comforters, who might have comforted him at once by telling the truth, and that Jacob refused to be comforted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Jacob mourned for Joseph one year without ceasing, saying let me go down to the grave mourning for my son. Henry observes that great affection to any creature prepares for so much the greater affliction when it is taken from us, and that undue love commonly ends in undue grief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
