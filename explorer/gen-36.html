The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 36 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now these are the generations of Esau, who is Edom.

2 Esau took his wives of the daughters of Canaan; Adah the daughter of Elon the Hittite, and Aholibamah the daughter of Anah the daughter of Zibeon the Hivite;

3 And Bashemath Ishmael’s daughter, sister of Nebajoth.

4 And Adah bare to Esau Eliphaz; and Bashemath bare Reuel;

5 And Aholibamah bare Jeush, and Jaalam, and Korah: these are the sons of Esau, which were born unto him in the land of Canaan.

6 And Esau took his wives, and his sons, and his daughters, and all the persons of his house, and his cattle, and all his beasts, and all his substance, which he had got in the land of Canaan; and went into the country from the face of his brother Jacob.

7 For their riches were more than that they might dwell together; and the land wherein they were strangers could not bear them because of their cattle.

8 Thus dwelt Esau in mount Seir: Esau is Edom.

9 And these are the generations of Esau the father of the Edomites in mount Seir:

10 These are the names of Esau’s sons; Eliphaz the son of Adah the wife of Esau, Reuel the son of Bashemath the wife of Esau.

11 And the sons of Eliphaz were Teman, Omar, Zepho, and Gatam, and Kenaz.

12 And Timna was concubine to Eliphaz Esau’s son; and she bare to Eliphaz Amalek: these were the sons of Adah Esau’s wife.

13 And these are the sons of Reuel; Nahath, and Zerah, Shammah, and Mizzah: these were the sons of Bashemath Esau’s wife.

14 And these were the sons of Aholibamah, the daughter of Anah the daughter of Zibeon, Esau’s wife: and she bare to Esau Jeush, and Jaalam, and Korah.

15 These were dukes of the sons of Esau: the sons of Eliphaz the firstborn son of Esau; duke Teman, duke Omar, duke Zepho, duke Kenaz,

16 Duke Korah, duke Gatam, and duke Amalek: these are the dukes that came of Eliphaz in the land of Edom; these were the sons of Adah.

17 And these are the sons of Reuel Esau’s son; duke Nahath, duke Zerah, duke Shammah, duke Mizzah: these are the dukes that came of Reuel in the land of Edom; these are the sons of Bashemath Esau’s wife.

18 And these are the sons of Aholibamah Esau’s wife; duke Jeush, duke Jaalam, duke Korah: these were the dukes that came of Aholibamah the daughter of Anah, Esau’s wife.

19 These are the sons of Esau, who is Edom, and these are their dukes.

20 These are the sons of Seir the Horite, who inhabited the land; Lotan, and Shobal, and Zibeon, and Anah,

21 And Dishon, and Ezer, and Dishan: these are the dukes of the Horites, the children of Seir in the land of Edom.

22 And the children of Lotan were Hori and Hemam; and Lotan’s sister was Timna.

23 And the children of Shobal were these; Alvan, and Manahath, and Ebal, Shepho, and Onam.

24 And these are the children of Zibeon; both Ajah, and Anah: this was that Anah that found the mules in the wilderness, as he fed the asses of Zibeon his father.

25 And the children of Anah were these; Dishon, and Aholibamah the daughter of Anah.

26 And these are the children of Dishon; Hemdan, and Eshban, and Ithran, and Cheran.

27 The children of Ezer are these; Bilhan, and Zaavan, and Akan.

28 The children of Dishan are these; Uz, and Aran.

29 These are the dukes that came of the Horites; duke Lotan, duke Shobal, duke Zibeon, duke Anah,

30 Duke Dishon, duke Ezer, duke Dishan: these are the dukes that came of Hori, among their dukes in the land of Seir.

31 And these are the kings that reigned in the land of Edom, before there reigned any king over the children of Israel.

32 And Bela the son of Beor reigned in Edom: and the name of his city was Dinhabah.

33 And Bela died, and Jobab the son of Zerah of Bozrah reigned in his stead.

34 And Jobab died, and Husham of the land of Temani reigned in his stead.

35 And Husham died, and Hadad the son of Bedad, who smote Midian in the field of Moab, reigned in his stead: and the name of his city was Avith.

36 And Hadad died, and Samlah of Masrekah reigned in his stead.

37 And Samlah died, and Saul of Rehoboth by the river reigned in his stead.

38 And Saul died, and Baalhanan the son of Achbor reigned in his stead.

39 And Baalhanan the son of Achbor died, and Hadar reigned in his stead: and the name of his city was Pau; and his wife’s name was Mehetabel, the daughter of Matred, the daughter of Mezahab.

40 And these are the names of the dukes that came of Esau, according to their families, after their places, by their names; duke Timnah, duke Alvah, duke Jetheth,

41 Duke Aholibamah, duke Elah, duke Pinon,

42 Duke Kenaz, duke Teman, duke Mibzar,

43 Duke Magdiel, duke Iram: these be the dukes of Edom, according to their habitations in the land of their possession: he is Esau the father of the Edomites.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This is the genealogy of Esau, who is Edom: his wives, sons, and the chiefs and kings who ruled Edom before any king ruled Israel. Verses 4, 5, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14 are name lists carried in the Text panel, on which the study notes are silent. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. This chapter opens the generations, that is, the history of the leading men and events, of Esau, who is Edom. JFB traces the name Edom to his red colour at birth, his craving for the red pottage, and the fierce character of his descendants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Esau took wives from Canaan's daughters, Adah and Aholibamah, named here with their fathers. JFB notes Esau's wives appear under different names elsewhere, since women in that age often bore two names, especially on moving to a new settlement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Bashemath, Ishmael's daughter and Nebajoth's sister, was Esau's third wife. JFB identifies her with Mahalath, since both stand in the same relation to Ishmael and Nebajoth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Esau took his whole household, cattle, and substance and went to a country away from his brother Jacob. JFB reads the move as providence at work: Esau's vast temporal prosperity, fulfilling the promise to him, was itself the occasion of his leaving Canaan and making way for Jacob's return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Their riches were too great for them to dwell together, and the land where they sojourned could not bear both households' cattle. Outward prosperity, not quarrel alone, forced the separation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Thus Esau dwelt in Mount Seir, and the verse repeats that Esau is Edom. JFB notes Seir was divinely assigned as his possession, while Henry contrasts Seir in hand with Canaan held only in promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The dukes of Esau's sons are now listed, beginning with Eliphaz's line. JFB explains that duke here does not mean a British peer, but a tribal head like a sheik, emir, or Highland chieftain, with fourteen such heads flourishing at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. This verse names the dukes that came from Eliphaz, Esau's firstborn, in the land of Edom. The list shows Edom organised by tribes named for Esau's sons, each under its own chief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. This verse names the dukes that came from Reuel, son of Bashemath, in Edom. The same tribal pattern repeats in a second branch of Esau's family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. This verse names the dukes that came from Aholibamah, daughter of Anah. All three wives' lines thus produced recognised tribal chiefs in Edom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The verse sums up the preceding lists: these are the sons of Esau, who is Edom, and these are their dukes. The summary closes the account of Esau's own descendants before turning to the older inhabitants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The chapter now lists the sons of Seir the Horite, the native inhabitants of the land. JFB notes these native dukes were incorporated with the Edomite race, so Edom's chiefs were not all of Esau's blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. This verse lists Lotan's children, with his sister Timna, among the Horite families. The detail preserves the native lineages that were absorbed into Edom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. This verse lists Shobal's children among the Horites. Each family is recorded because its head later appears as a duke in the land of Seir. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. This verse lists Zibeon's children, Ajah and Anah. The genealogy keeps the Horite branches distinct before their chiefs are named together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. This Anah is singled out as the one who found the mules, or in several ancient versions the water springs, in the wilderness while feeding his father's asses. JFB notes that such a discovery was distinction enough among a pastoral people to earn a permanent mention. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. This verse lists Anah's children, Dishon and Aholibamah. The names link the Horite stock to Esau's household through Aholibamah, Esau's wife. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. This verse lists Dishon's children among the Horites. The record moves branch by branch through Seir's seven sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. This verse lists Ezer's children among the Horites. The lists are brief because their purpose is to account for the dukes that follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. This verse lists Dishan's children among the Horites, completing the seven branches of Seir. The full Horite stock is now before the reader. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. These are the dukes that came from the Horites, named by their fathers, in the land of Seir. JFB's point stands over the whole list: native chiefs held rank alongside Esau's descendants in Edom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. This verse continues the Horite dukes, naming Dishon, Ezer, and Dishan among their dukes. The second half of the list completes the native chiefs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. The chapter now lists the kings who reigned in Edom before any king reigned over Israel. JFB stresses that royal power was not built on the ruins of the dukedoms but existed at the same time as them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Bela son of Beor reigned in Edom, from the city Dinhabah, opening the king list. Henry notes the pomp of Edom's kings would later try Israel's faith while they were slaves in Egypt, since God's people had to wait for God's time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. When Bela died, Jobab son of Zerah of Bozrah reigned in his stead. The succession shows Edom's kingship passing from ruler to ruler, not by a single dynasty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. When Jobab died, Husham of the land of Temani reigned in his stead. Each king is tied to a place, grounding the list in Edom's geography. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. When Husham died, Hadad son of Bedad, who struck Midian in Moab, reigned from Avith. The military note is the one deed remembered for this king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. When Hadad died, Samlah of Masrekah reigned in his stead. The bare succession continues the record of kings before Israel had a king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. When Samlah died, Saul of Rehoboth by the river reigned in his stead. The river town marks another centre of Edomite rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. When Saul died, Baal-hanan son of Achbor reigned in his stead. The list's steady rhythm underlines how long Edom had kings while Israel waited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. When Baal-hanan died, Hadar reigned from Pau, with his wife Mehetabel named. The final king's household detail closes the king list on a personal note. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. The chapter closes by recapitulating the dukes of Esau by families, places, and names. JFB notes this is a second listing, now arranged by residence rather than by descent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. This verse names dukes Timnah, Alvah, and Jetheth by their places. The recapitulation maps Edom's chiefs to the districts they held. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. This verse names dukes Aholibamah, Elah, and Pinon by their places. The place-names show Edom settled and divided, not merely a wandering clan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. This verse names dukes Kenaz, Teman, Mibzar, Magdiel, and Iram, completing the list. The chapter ends where it began: he is Esau, the father of the Edomites, now shown as a settled people with chiefs and kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • duke. A clan chief: the note compares such a head to a sheik, emir or Highland chieftain. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 36:15, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
