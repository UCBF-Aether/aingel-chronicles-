The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 32 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered.

2 Blessed is the man unto whom the LORD imputeth not iniquity, and in whose spirit there is no guile.

3 When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long.

4 For day and night thy hand was heavy upon me: my moisture is turned into the drought of summer. Selah.

5 I acknowledge my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Selah.

6 For this shall every one that is godly pray unto thee in a time when thou mayest be found: surely in the floods of great waters they shall not come nigh unto him.

7 Thou art my hiding place; thou shalt preserve me from trouble; thou shalt compass me about with songs of deliverance. Selah.

8 I will instruct thee and teach thee in the way which thou shalt go: I will guide thee with mine eye.

9 Be ye not as the horse, or as the mule, which have no understanding: whose mouth must be held in with bit and bridle, lest they come near unto thee.

10 Many sorrows shall be to the wicked: but he that trusteth in the LORD, mercy shall compass him about.

11 Be glad in the LORD, and rejoice, ye righteous: and shout for joy, all ye that are upright in heart.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The blessedness of pardon: silence over sin rotted the bones and dried life like a summer drought, and confession brought forgiveness at once. The psalm becomes a lesson, pray while God may be found, be guided by God's eye rather than bit and bridle, and end glad and shouting for joy. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered: blessedness here is not prosperity but pardon — transgression forgiven and sin covered are its two faces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord imputes not iniquity, and in whose spirit there is no guile: non-imputation and a guileless spirit together describe the justified man, as Paul expounds in Romans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day: unconfessed sin rotted him inwardly, and the roaring was pain without confession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Day and night your hand was heavy on me; my moisture turned into summer drought: God's heavy hand dried up his inward life like a summer drought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. I acknowledged my sin to you, and did not hide my iniquity; I said, I will confess my transgressions to the Lord, and you forgave the iniquity of my sin: confession, resolved and spoken, met immediate forgiveness — the turning point of the psalm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For this shall every godly one pray to you in a time when you may be found; surely in floods of great waters they shall not come near him: David's experience becomes a pattern — pray while God may be found, and floods cannot reach the praying man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. You are my hiding place; you preserve me from trouble and compass me about with songs of deliverance: God himself is the hiding place, and deliverance itself becomes song around him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. I will instruct you and teach you in the way you shall go; I will guide you with my eye: God now speaks as teacher, guiding not by force but by the eye — a look, for the willing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Be not as the horse or mule, which have no understanding, whose mouth must be held with bit and bridle: the untaught need force; the godly should need only God's eye. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Many sorrows shall be to the wicked, but he who trusts in the Lord, mercy shall compass him about: sorrows surround the wicked, mercy surrounds the trusting — the two compassings contrast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Be glad in the Lord and rejoice, you righteous, and shout for joy, all upright in heart: the psalm that began with pardon ends in gladness and shouting joy for the righteous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
