The Text — Judith 2 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 And in the eighteenth year, the two and twentieth day of the first month, there was talk in the house of Nabuchodonosor king of the Assyrians that he should, as he said, avenge himself on all the earth.

2 So he called unto him all his officers, and all his nobles, and communicated with them his secret counsel, and concluded the afflicting of the whole earth out of his own mouth.

3 Then they decreed to destroy all flesh, that did not obey the commandment of his mouth.

4 And when he had ended his counsel, Nabuchodonosor king of the Assyrians called Holofernes the chief captain of his army, which was next unto him, and said unto him.

5 Thus saith the great king, the lord of the whole earth, Behold, thou shalt go forth from my presence, and take with thee men that trust in their own strength, of footmen an hundred and twenty thousand; and the number of horses with their riders twelve thousand.

6 And thou shalt go against all the west country, because they disobeyed my commandment.

7 And thou shalt declare unto that they prepare for me earth and water: for I will go forth in my wrath against them and will cover the whole face of the earth with the feet of mine army, and I will give them for a spoil unto them:

8 So that their slain shall fill their valleys and brooks and the river shall be filled with their dead, till it overflow:

9 And I will lead them captives to the utmost parts of all the earth.

10 Thou therefore shalt go forth. and take beforehand for me all their coasts: and if they will yield themselves unto thee, thou shalt reserve them for me till the day of their punishment.

11 But concerning them that rebel, let not thine eye spare them; but put them to the slaughter, and spoil them wheresoever thou goest.

12 For as I live, and by the power of my kingdom, whatsoever I have spoken, that will I do by mine hand.

13 And take thou heed that thou transgress none of the commandments of thy lord, but accomplish them fully, as I have commanded thee, and defer not to do them.

14 Then Holofernes went forth from the presence of his lord, and called ail the governors and captains, and the officers of the army of Assur;

15 And he mustered the chosen men for the battle, as his lord had commanded him, unto an hundred and twenty thousand, and twelve thousand archers on horseback;

16 And he ranged them, as a great army is ordered for the war.

17 And he took camels and asses for their carriages, a very great number; and sheep and oxen and goats without number for their provision:

18 And plenty of victual for every man of the army, and very much gold and silver out of the king's house.

19 Then he went forth and all his power to go before king Nabuchodonosor in the voyage, and to cover all the face of the earth westward with their chariots, and horsemen, and their chosen footmen.

20 A great number also sundry countries came with them like locusts, and like the sand of the earth: for the multitude was without number.

21 And they went forth of Nineve three days' journey toward the plain of Bectileth, and pitched from Bectileth near the mountain which is at the left hand of the upper Cilicia.

22 Then he took all his army, his footmen, and horsemen and chariots, and went from thence into the hill country;

23 And destroyed Phud and Lud, and spoiled all the children of Rasses, and the children of Israel, which were toward the wilderness at the south of the land of the Chellians.

24 Then he went over Euphrates, and went through Mesopotamia, and destroyed all the high cities that were upon the river Arbonai, till ye come to the sea.

25 And he took the borders of Cilicia, and killed all that resisted him, and came to the borders of Japheth, which were toward the south, over against Arabia.

26 He compassed also all the children of Madian, and burned up their tabernacles, and spoiled their sheepcotes.

27 Then he went down into the plain of Damascus in the time of wheat harvest, and burnt up all their fields, and destroyed their flocks and herds, also he spoiled their cities, and utterly wasted their countries, and smote all their young men with the edge of the sword.

28 Therefore the fear and dread of him fell upon all the inhabitants of the sea coasts, which were in Sidon and Tyrus, and them that dwelt in Sur and Ocina, and all that dwelt in Jemnaan; and they that dwelt in Azotus and Ascalon feared him greatly.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

In the eighteenth year, on the twenty-second day of the first month, Nabuchodonosor resolves to avenge himself on all the earth for the nations that refused him, and sends Holofernes, chief captain of his army, westward with chariots, horsemen, and chosen footmen to cover the face of the earth. City after city is taken, and fear and dread fall upon all the inhabitants of the sea coasts, from Sidon and Tyre to Azotus and Jemnaan. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Holofernes. the chief captain of Nabuchodonosor's army, commissioned to avenge the king on all the earth (verse 4). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • First month. Nisan, in spring, according to the study note on verse 1. Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Douay text reads "thirteenth" year here, while the Greek reads 18th, matching this corpus's "eighteenth year"; the month is Nisan, in spring. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. The "secret" counsel is literally "mystery" (Tobit 12:7) — prying into a prince's hidden counsels is illicit, as Tacitus notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. The counsellors' decree to destroy all flesh that did not obey reflects what Sallust called the old and deep-rooted cause of war, "the thirst of empire and riches"; the Greek adds that they judged all flesh must be destroyed of those who had not complied with his order, but God laughs at the vain designs of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. Holofernes, chief captain of the army, is in the Greek "the second after himself" in the kingdom; Casaubon suspects this drunkard was the king in history who lost Cappadocia by attempting to introduce the dissolute feasts of Bacchus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. The demand for "earth and water" was an acknowledgment of dominion, signifying that all necessaries of life belonged to the king; this custom prevailed in Persia (Herodotus iv. 123). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. The Greek adds corn "for every man, in abundance," for fear provisions should be carried off in the countries invaded — the army's victual was gathered in great abundance, with much gold and silver out of the king's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. The Greek adds to the army's description "a mixed multitude," not bearing arms (Exodus 12:38) — the host that went forth with Holofernes covered the earth like locusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. The Greek reads that they "proceeded from Ninive, three days' journey, to the plain of Bektileth," or Bagdania in Cappadocia, between Mount Argaeus and Taurus; Strabo says Argaeus is the highest mountain in the country, from which the seas of Cilicia and the Euxine may be seen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 24. Mambre is in the Greek "Abrona, or Arbonai" — that is, from the river Chaboras to the Persian Gulf, or sea — marking the sweep through Mesopotamia past the high cities on the river. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 25. Japheth is Joppe, now Jaffa; the Greek adds that he "cut in pieces all who opposed him; and he came to the borders of Japheth, which are on the south, over-against Arabia." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 26. On Madian, the Greek reads that he "surrounded and burnt their tents, and plundered their folds for cattle"; Madian lay to the east of the Dead Sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 27. The rapidity of these conquests was astonishing, all done in six or seven weeks, though Holofernes probably sent his lieutenants into different parts at the same time; the Greek adds that he "burnt all their fields, and destroyed their flocks and herds, and plundered their cities, and put to the sword all their young men." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 28. The Greek adds to the lands seized with fear "on the sea-shore, in Sidon, Ascalon," and the like — the dread of Holofernes fell upon all the inhabitants of the sea coasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock

Connections to other passages

  • The study note for verse 2 connects this passage to Tobit 12:7. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • The study note for verse 19 connects this passage to Exodus 12:38. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
