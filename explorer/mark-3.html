The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he entered again into the synagogue; and there was a man there which had a withered hand.

2 And they watched him, whether he would heal him on the sabbath day; that they might accuse him.

3 And he saith unto the man which had the withered hand, Stand forth.

4 And he saith unto them, Is it lawful to do good on the sabbath days, or to do evil? to save life, or to kill? But they held their peace.

5 And when he had looked round about on them with anger, being grieved for the hardness of their hearts, he saith unto the man, Stretch forth thine hand. And he stretched it out: and his hand was restored whole as the other.

6 And the Pharisees went forth, and straightway took counsel with the Herodians against him, how they might destroy him.

7 But Jesus withdrew himself with his disciples to the sea: and a great multitude from Galilee followed him, and from Judaea,

8 And from Jerusalem, and from Idumaea, and from beyond Jordan; and they about Tyre and Sidon, a great multitude, when they had heard what great things he did, came unto him.

9 And he spake to his disciples, that a small ship should wait on him because of the multitude, lest they should throng him.

10 For he had healed many; insomuch that they pressed upon him for to touch him, as many as had plagues.

11 And unclean spirits, when they saw him, fell down before him, and cried, saying, Thou art the Son of God.

12 And he straitly charged them that they should not make him known.

13 And he goeth up into a mountain, and calleth unto him whom he would: and they came unto him.

14 And he ordained twelve, that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach,

15 And to have power to heal sicknesses, and to cast out devils:

16 And Simon he surnamed Peter;

17 And James the son of Zebedee, and John the brother of James; and he surnamed them Boanerges, which is, The sons of thunder:

18 And Andrew, and Philip, and Bartholomew, and Matthew, and Thomas, and James the son of Alphaeus, and Thaddaeus, and Simon the Canaanite,

19 And Judas Iscariot, which also betrayed him: and they went into an house.

20 And the multitude cometh together again, so that they could not so much as eat bread.

21 And when his friends heard of it, they went out to lay hold on him: for they said, He is beside himself.

22 And the scribes which came down from Jerusalem said, He hath Beelzebub, and by the prince of the devils casteth he out devils.

23 And he called them unto him, and said unto them in parables, How can Satan cast out Satan?

24 And if a kingdom be divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.

25 And if a house be divided against itself, that house cannot stand.

26 And if Satan rise up against himself, and be divided, he cannot stand, but hath an end.

27 No man can enter into a strong man’s house, and spoil his goods, except he will first bind the strong man; and then he will spoil his house.

28 Verily I say unto you, All sins shall be forgiven unto the sons of men, and blasphemies wherewith soever they shall blaspheme:

29 But he that shall blaspheme against the Holy Ghost hath never forgiveness, but is in danger of eternal damnation.

30 Because they said, He hath an unclean spirit.

31 There came then his brethren and his mother, and, standing without, sent unto him, calling him.

32 And the multitude sat about him, and they said unto him, Behold, thy mother and thy brethren without seek for thee.

33 And he answered them, saying, Who is my mother, or my brethren?

34 And he looked round about on them which sat about him, and said, Behold my mother and my brethren!

35 For whosoever shall do the will of God, the same is my brother, and my sister, and mother.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jesus heals a man with a withered hand on the Sabbath, and the Pharisees at once plot with the Herodians against him (vv. 1–6). Crowds press on him by the sea, and he appoints the twelve on a mountain, giving their names (vv. 7–19). When scribes from Jerusalem say he casts out devils by Beelzebub, he answers with the divided kingdom and the strong man, and warns about blasphemy against the Holy Ghost (vv. 20–30). The chapter ends with his mother and brothers outside, and his answer that whoever does the will of God is his brother, sister, and mother (vv. 31–35). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 3

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He entered again into the synagogue, and there was a man with a withered hand. The synagogue, a place of worship, becomes the stage for a sabbath controversy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. They watched him, whether he would heal on the sabbath, that they might accuse him. Their watching was not curiosity but a trap, seeking evidence against a healer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He said to the man with the withered hand, Stand forth. Christ brings the case into the open, refusing to heal in secret where malice could deny it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He asked them, is it lawful to do good on the sabbath, or evil, to save life, or to kill? They held their peace. To forbid a good deed on the sabbath is to choose the evil of neglect, and silence betrayed their conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Looking round on them with anger, grieved for the hardness of their hearts, he said, Stretch forth thine hand, and it was restored whole as the other. Christ's anger is holy grief at hardness, and his command gives the power it requires. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The Pharisees went forth and straightway took counsel with the Herodians against him, how they might destroy him. Enemies who agreed on nothing else united against Christ, and a miracle of mercy provoked a plot of murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Jesus withdrew with his disciples to the sea, and a great multitude from Galilee followed him, and from Judea. He avoided the plot without abandoning his work, and the crowds followed him out of the towns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. People also came from Jerusalem, Idumaea, beyond Jordan, and about Tyre and Sidon, a great multitude, when they heard what great things he did. Fame drew Gentile borders as well as Jewish towns to the healer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. He told his disciples a small ship should wait on him because of the multitude, lest they throng him. The boat was both a pulpit and a protection against a crushing crowd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For he had healed many, so that they pressed upon him to touch him, as many as had plagues. Suffering crowds sought even a touch, and their pressing showed the scale of his healings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Unclean spirits, when they saw him, fell down before him and cried, Thou art the Son of God. Even devils confessed his sonship, though their confession was forced and unwelcome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. He straitly charged them that they should not make him known. Christ would not have his name proclaimed by devils, whose testimony would dishonour the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He went up into a mountain and called to him whom he would, and they came to him. The call to apostleship was by his sovereign choice, not by their application. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He ordained twelve, that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach. Companionship came before commission: they must first learn Christ before they preach him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They were also given power to heal sicknesses and to cast out devils. Miracles were to confirm their preaching, as they had confirmed his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Simon he surnamed Peter, marking the rock-like steadfastness he would need and, by grace, show. A new name marked a new calling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. James son of Zebedee and John his brother he surnamed Boanerges, the sons of thunder. Their vehement, earnest spirit suited the thunder of gospel preaching, once grace had tamed it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Then followed Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, and Simon the Canaanite. The twelve were ordinary men of varied trades and tempers, chosen to show that the work was God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. And Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed him; and they went into a house. The traitor stands in the list without disguise, a warning that nearness to Christ without grace saves no one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The multitude came together again, so that they could not so much as eat bread. The press of ministry left no leisure even for meals, a pattern of self-denying service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. When his friends heard of it, they went out to lay hold on him, for they said, He is beside himself. Even his own kin mistook zeal for madness, and sought to restrain what they could not understand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The scribes from Jerusalem said, He has Beelzebub, and by the prince of devils casts out devils. Unable to deny the miracles, his enemies ascribed them to Satan, the worst charge malice could invent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. He called them to him and said in parables, How can Satan cast out Satan? Christ answers slander calmly, by reason, inviting them to see the absurdity of their charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. If a kingdom be divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand. A power at war with itself destroys itself, and Satan would not destroy his own kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. If a house be divided against itself, that house cannot stand. The same reason holds in smaller societies: internal war is ruin, not strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. If Satan rise up against himself and be divided, he cannot stand, but has an end. To cast out devils by Satan would be Satan ending his own reign, which he will not do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. No man can enter a strong man's house and spoil his goods, except he first bind the strong man, and then he will spoil his house. Christ's casting out devils proves he has bound Satan, the strong man, and is plundering his house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Verily, all sins shall be forgiven to the sons of men, and blasphemies with which they blaspheme. No sin, however great, is beyond pardon for the penitent, and this promise is meant to encourage return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. But he that blasphemes against the Holy Ghost has never forgiveness, but is in danger of eternal damnation. Wilful, settled ascribing of the Spirit's work to Satan hardens the heart past repentance, and so past pardon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. He said this because they said, He has an unclean spirit. Their charge was itself the blasphemy in view: calling the Spirit's manifest work the devil's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Then his brethren and his mother came, standing without, and sent to him, calling him. Family ties sought him in the midst of his work, and the crowd became their messenger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The multitude sat about him and said, behold, thy mother and brethren without seek for thee. Those sitting at his feet reported the interruption, not knowing how he would answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. He answered, Who is my mother, or my brethren? The question does not dishonour his mother; it lifts the hearers to a higher kinship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Looking round on those who sat about him, he said, Behold my mother and my brethren! Disciples seated to learn are counted his nearest family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. For whosoever shall do the will of God, the same is my brother, and my sister, and mother. Obedience, not blood, makes kinship with Christ, and that kinship is open to all who do God's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Boanerges (v. 17): The surname Jesus gives James and John, which the chapter text itself glosses as “The sons of thunder.” Documented Standard corpus, Mark 3:17
