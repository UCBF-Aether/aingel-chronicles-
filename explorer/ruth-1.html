The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ruth 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now it came to pass in the days when the judges ruled, that there was a famine in the land. And a certain man of Bethlehemjudah went to sojourn in the country of Moab, he, and his wife, and his two sons.

2 And the name of the man was Elimelech, and the name of his wife Naomi, and the name of his two sons Mahlon and Chilion, Ephrathites of Bethlehemjudah. And they came into the country of Moab, and continued there.

3 And Elimelech Naomi’s husband died; and she was left, and her two sons.

4 And they took them wives of the women of Moab; the name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the other Ruth: and they dwelled there about ten years.

5 And Mahlon and Chilion died also both of them; and the woman was left of her two sons and her husband.

6 Then she arose with her daughters in law, that she might return from the country of Moab: for she had heard in the country of Moab how that the LORD had visited his people in giving them bread.

7 Wherefore she went forth out of the place where she was, and her two daughters in law with her; and they went on the way to return unto the land of Judah.

8 And Naomi said unto her two daughters in law, Go, return each to her mother’s house: the LORD deal kindly with you, as ye have dealt with the dead, and with me.

9 The LORD grant you that ye may find rest, each of you in the house of her husband. Then she kissed them; and they lifted up their voice, and wept.

10 And they said unto her, Surely we will return with thee unto thy people.

11 And Naomi said, Turn again, my daughters: why will ye go with me? are there yet any more sons in my womb, that they may be your husbands?

12 Turn again, my daughters, go your way; for I am too old to have an husband. If I should say, I have hope, if I should have an husband also to night, and should also bear sons;

13 Would ye tarry for them till they were grown? would ye stay for them from having husbands? nay, my daughters; for it grieveth me much for your sakes that the hand of the LORD is gone out against me.

14 And they lifted up their voice, and wept again: and Orpah kissed her mother in law; but Ruth clave unto her.

15 And she said, Behold, thy sister in law is gone back unto her people, and unto her gods: return thou after thy sister in law.

16 And Ruth said, Intreat me not to leave thee, or to return from following after thee: for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God:

17 Where thou diest, will I die, and there will I be buried: the LORD do so to me, and more also, if ought but death part thee and me.

18 When she saw that she was stedfastly minded to go with her, then she left speaking unto her.

19 So they two went until they came to Bethlehem. And it came to pass, when they were come to Bethlehem, that all the city was moved about them, and they said, Is this Naomi?

20 And she said unto them, Call me not Naomi, call me Mara: for the Almighty hath dealt very bitterly with me.

21 I went out full and the LORD hath brought me home again empty: why then call ye me Naomi, seeing the LORD hath testified against me, and the Almighty hath afflicted me?

22 So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter in law, with her, which returned out of the country of Moab: and they came to Bethlehem in the beginning of barley harvest.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the days of the judges a famine drives Elimelech, Naomi and their sons Mahlon and Chilion from Bethlehem-judah to sojourn in Moab, where Elimelech and then both sons die, leaving Naomi a widow with two Moabite daughters-in-law. Hearing that the LORD has visited his people with bread, Naomi sets out for home, urging Orpah and Ruth to return to their mothers' houses; Orpah kisses her and goes back, but Ruth cleaves to her with the great declaration, where thou goest I will go, thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God, and only death shall part us. They come to Bethlehem at barley harvest, the city is moved, and Naomi asks to be called Mara, bitterness, for she went out full and the LORD has brought her home empty. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the days of the judges a famine drove a Bethlehem family to sojourn in Moab. Henry warns that trying to escape a cross laid in our way by changing place seldom mends matters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Elimelech, Naomi, and their sons Mahlon and Chilion were Ephrathites of Bethlehemjudah who settled in Moab. JFB notes the names mean, respectively, "My God is king" and "fair or pleasant." Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Elimelech died in Moab, leaving Naomi a widow with her two sons. Henry draws the general lesson that death continually mars all our outward comforts, which are of short continuance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The sons married Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth, and lived there about ten years. JFB notes these marriages violated the Mosaic law, and Jewish writers regarded the young men's early deaths as judgments for those unlawful connections. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Then both sons also died, and Naomi was left without husband or sons. Henry observes that the removal which was meant to provide for the family ended in the wasting of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Hearing that the LORD had visited his people in giving them bread, Naomi arose with her daughters-in-law to return from Moab. JFB notes the aged widow longed to enjoy again the privileges of Israel once the famine had ceased. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Naomi set out from Moab toward the land of Judah with her two daughters-in-law. Henry remarks that when death comes into a family it ought to reform what is amiss there, and earth is made bitter so that heaven may be made dear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Naomi urged each daughter-in-law to return to her mother's house, praying the LORD would deal kindly with them as they had dealt with the dead. JFB explains the mother's house as the women's own apartments in Eastern custom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Naomi prayed the LORD would grant each of them rest in the house of a husband, then kissed them and they wept aloud. JFB glosses this rest as a life of tranquillity, free from the peculiar troubles of widowhood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Both daughters-in-law answered that they would surely return with Naomi to her people. Henry notes Naomi dismissed them with prayer, as friends parting in love should part. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Naomi asked whether she had any more sons in her womb who could become their husbands. JFB explains the allusion to the ancient custom, later sanctioned in the law of Moses, by which a younger son married his deceased brother's widow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Naomi pressed them to turn back, saying she was too old to have a husband and had no hope of sons for them. JFB calls this dissuasion wise: it prevented hopes that could not be realized and tested whether their conversion to the true religion was sincere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Naomi added that it grieved her for their sakes that the hand of the LORD had gone out against her. JFB explains that she was so reduced in circumstances she could not bear to subject them to privation with her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Orpah kissed her mother-in-law and went back, but Ruth clave to Naomi. Henry contrasts them: Orpah loved Naomi but not enough to leave Moab, while many likewise value Christ yet will not forsake other things for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Naomi pointed out that Ruth's sister-in-law had gone back to her people and to her gods, and urged Ruth to return after her. Henry frames this as the moment of decision between Moab and the God of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Ruth answered with her great declaration: where Naomi goes she will go, Naomi's people will be her people, and Naomi's God her God. Henry calls this an example of grace inclining the soul to choose the better part. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Ruth swore that only death would part her from Naomi, calling down the LORD's judgment on herself if she did otherwise. Henry notes the power of such resolution: it silences temptation, while an unsteady mind stands like a door half open. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Seeing Ruth was steadfastly minded to go with her, Naomi stopped urging her. Henry observes that Naomi could desire no more than this solemn declaration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. When the two came to Bethlehem, all the city was moved about them and asked, "Is this Naomi?" JFB notes the painful contrast between her former flourishing state and her present condition as a forlorn widow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Naomi asked to be called Mara, bitterness, because the Almighty had dealt very bitterly with her. Henry glosses Naomi as "pleasant" and Mara as "bitter," marking her as a woman of a sorrowful spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Naomi said she went out full and the LORD had brought her home empty, testifying against her and afflicting her. Henry adds that she owned the affliction came from God, and that it is affliction rightly borne, not affliction itself, that does us good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Naomi returned with Ruth the Moabitess at the beginning of barley harvest. JFB dates this to the end of March, and Henry notes that though Naomi came home empty and childless, believers have a fulness of which they can never be emptied. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words in this chapter that a modern reader may stumble on. They explain the chapter text; they are not Scripture.

  • sojourn. to stay for a time as a stranger, not to settle permanently Interpreted Editorial gloss, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes
  • Mara. bitterness, the name Naomi asks to be called instead of Naomi, pleasant Interpreted Editorial gloss, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Connections to other passages

  • The levirate custom: Jamieson-Fausset-Brown explains Naomi's question about sons in her womb as an allusion to the ancient custom, later sanctioned in the law of Moses, by which a younger son married his deceased brother's widow. See the law of the brother's duty in Deuteronomy 25. Interpreted Study note on Ruth 1:11, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
