The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 146 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Praise ye the LORD. Praise the LORD, O my soul.

2 While I live will I praise the LORD: I will sing praises unto my God while I have any being.

3 Put not your trust in princes, nor in the son of man, in whom there is no help.

4 His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish.

5 Happy is he that hath the God of Jacob for his help, whose hope is in the LORD his God:

6 Which made heaven, and earth, the sea, and all that therein is: which keepeth truth for ever:

7 Which executeth judgment for the oppressed: which giveth food to the hungry. The LORD looseth the prisoners:

8 The LORD openeth the eyes of the blind: the LORD raiseth them that are bowed down: the LORD loveth the righteous:

9 The LORD preserveth the strangers; he relieveth the fatherless and widow: but the way of the wicked he turneth upside down.

10 The LORD shall reign for ever, even thy God, O Zion, unto all generations. Praise ye the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A psalm that begins and ends with praise from the speaker's own soul: while he lives he will praise the LORD and sing to his God while he has any being. The warning at its centre is against trusting princes or the son of man, in whom there is no help: his breath goes forth, he returns to his earth, and in that very day his thoughts perish. Happy instead is he whose help is the God of Jacob and whose hope is in the LORD his God — maker of heaven, earth, sea and all therein, keeper of truth for ever, executor of judgment for the oppressed, giver of food to the hungry, looser of prisoners, opener of blind eyes, raiser of the bowed down, lover of the righteous, preserver of strangers, reliever of fatherless and widow, while the way of the wicked he turns upside down. The LORD shall reign for ever, Zion's God unto all generations. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
