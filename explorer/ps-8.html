The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 8 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 O LORD, our Lord, how excellent is thy name in all the earth! who hast set thy glory above the heavens.

2 Out of the mouth of babes and sucklings hast thou ordained strength because of thine enemies, that thou mightest still the enemy and the avenger.

3 When I consider thy heavens, the work of thy fingers, the moon and the stars, which thou hast ordained;

4 What is man, that thou art mindful of him? and the son of man, that thou visitest him?

5 For thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honour.

6 Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet:

7 All sheep and oxen, yea, and the beasts of the field;

8 The fowl of the air, and the fish of the sea, and whatsoever passeth through the paths of the seas.

9 O LORD our Lord, how excellent is thy name in all the earth!

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Wonder at creation becomes worship: God's name is excellent in all the earth, and though man is small beside moon and stars, he is crowned with glory and given dominion over all creatures. The psalm closes as it opened, in praise. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Heading note: the text panel prints the verses only, without a heading. The name David in the verse notes (verse 3) comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. Authorship and date remain Unresolved (see Trace). Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The psalm opens in adoration: the Lord's name is excellent in all the earth, and his glory is set above the heavens. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Out of babes and sucklings God ordains strength, stilling enemy and avenger: weak praise confounds proud opposition, as Christ himself applied this verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Considering the heavens, the work of God's fingers, moon and stars ordained by him, David is struck by the order and vastness of creation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. What is man, that God is mindful of him, and the son of man, that he visits him? Human smallness makes divine attention wonderful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Man is made a little lower than the angels and crowned with glory and honour: dignity is God's gift, not man's achievement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Dominion over the works of God's hands is given to man, with all things under his feet: creation is entrusted, not abandoned, to human rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Sheep and oxen and beasts of the field are named as the subjects of that dominion, the familiar creatures of daily life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Fowl of the air, fish of the sea, and all that pass through the paths of the seas complete the picture: no realm of creation lies outside man's stewardship, and the psalm's fullest sense, as Hebrews shows, is in Christ, the true Son of man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The psalm closes as it opened, with the excellent name of the Lord in all the earth: wonder at creation returns to worship of the Creator. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
