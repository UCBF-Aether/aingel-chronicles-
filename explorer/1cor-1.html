The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Corinthians 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Paul, called to be an apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God, and Sosthenes our brother,

2 Unto the church of God which is at Corinth, to them that are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, with all that in every place call upon the name of Jesus Christ our Lord, both theirs and ours:

3 Grace be unto you, and peace, from God our Father, and from the Lord Jesus Christ.

4 I thank my God always on your behalf, for the grace of God which is given you by Jesus Christ;

5 That in every thing ye are enriched by him, in all utterance, and in all knowledge;

6 Even as the testimony of Christ was confirmed in you:

7 So that ye come behind in no gift; waiting for the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ:

8 Who shall also confirm you unto the end, that ye may be blameless in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.

9 God is faithful, by whom ye were called unto the fellowship of his Son Jesus Christ our Lord.

10 Now I beseech you, brethren, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye all speak the same thing, and that there be no divisions among you; but that ye be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment.

11 For it hath been declared unto me of you, my brethren, by them which are of the house of Chloe, that there are contentions among you.

12 Now this I say, that every one of you saith, I am of Paul; and I of Apollos; and I of Cephas; and I of Christ.

13 Is Christ divided? was Paul crucified for you? or were ye baptized in the name of Paul?

14 I thank God that I baptized none of you, but Crispus and Gaius;

15 Lest any should say that I had baptized in mine own name.

16 And I baptized also the household of Stephanas: besides, I know not whether I baptized any other.

17 For Christ sent me not to baptize, but to preach the gospel: not with wisdom of words, lest the cross of Christ should be made of none effect.

18 For the preaching of the cross is to them that perish foolishness; but unto us which are saved it is the power of God.

19 For it is written, I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and will bring to nothing the understanding of the prudent.

20 Where is the wise? where is the scribe? where is the disputer of this world? hath not God made foolish the wisdom of this world?

21 For after that in the wisdom of God the world by wisdom knew not God, it pleased God by the foolishness of preaching to save them that believe.

22 For the Jews require a sign, and the Greeks seek after wisdom:

23 But we preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews a stumblingblock, and unto the Greeks foolishness;

24 But unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God.

25 Because the foolishness of God is wiser than men; and the weakness of God is stronger than men.

26 For ye see your calling, brethren, how that not many wise men after the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, are called:

27 But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty;

28 And base things of the world, and things which are despised, hath God chosen, yea, and things which are not, to bring to nought things that are:

29 That no flesh should glory in his presence.

30 But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption:

31 That, according as it is written, He that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul writes to a divided Corinthian church that boasts in rival leaders, and answers their divisions with the cross: Christ is not divided, and the preaching of the cross, foolishness to the world, is God's power and wisdom. God chose the foolish, weak, and despised things of the world to shame the wise and strong, so that no flesh should glory, but whoever glories should glory in the Lord. Interpreted Overview of this chapter's text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul calls himself an apostle by God's will, not by his own merit or choice — the same will that grounds his authority over the Corinthian church is also a reason for humility. Sosthenes, named beside him, was likely the former synagogue ruler of Corinth, now a brother whose standing would add weight to the letter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Paul still calls the divided Corinthian church the church of God, since it held evangelical doctrine, baptism, and the Lord's Supper; apparent purity is not the test of a true church. Its members are sanctified, set apart as holy by union with Christ, and called saints by God's calling, not by their own achievement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Grace and peace are wished to the church from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, with peace peculiarly needed in a church torn by dissension. The blessing follows the order of grace first, then peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Paul opens with praise rather than reproof, putting the causes for thanksgiving first so as not to discourage the church and to appeal to its better self. His thanks are for the grace given them in Christ Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Corinthians were enriched in utterance and knowledge — in the preaching of the word and in apprehending it. Paul congratulates them on these very gifts before he deals with their abuse of them, winning goodwill before correction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The testimony about Christ was confirmed among the Corinthians by God's work through Paul's preaching, the miracles accompanying it, and their own acceptance of the gospel by the Spirit's inward power. Their faith itself was the seal of the message. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The church came behind in no gift, though no single believer had every gift — different persons held different gifts. Their eager waiting for Christ's coming exercised faith, hope, and love together, and was the crowning proof of their enrichment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. God himself will confirm the believers to the end, so that they stand blameless in the day of Christ. Now is the day of work and trial; then will be the day of Christ and of his glory in his saints, after which there is no more danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. God is faithful to his promises, and has called believers according to his purpose into fellowship with Jesus Christ — to be fellow heirs with him, sons of God and heirs of glory. Christ's name recurs so often in this epistle precisely to draw the Corinthians away from party admiration of teachers to Christ alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Paul urges them, since they already have knowledge, utterance, and hope, to maintain love as well. They are to speak the same thing, without splits or breaches, and to be perfectly joined together — the word pictures healing a wound or mending a rent — in mind and judgment, in what they believe and in what they decide to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Word of their contentions reached Paul from Chloe's household, people trusted by both Paul and the Corinthians. Paul names the family to show his charge rests on authority, but names no individuals, so as not to stir hatred against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Each believer was boasting, 'I am of Paul,' 'I of Apollos,' 'I of Cephas,' 'I of Christ' — not necessarily fixed parties, but a party spirit attaching to favorite teachers. Paul will not be flattered even by those using his own name, since that dishonors Christ; Apollos, for his part, discouraged such preference and would not revisit just then. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Christ cannot be divided into parts, one under one leader and another under another, for all belong to him, the one Head. Nor was Paul crucified for them or they baptized into his name — the cross claims believers for Christ as their redeemer, and baptism dedicates them to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Paul thanks God that he baptized only Crispus, the former synagogue ruler, and Gaius, his host at Corinth. Baptizing was chiefly the deacons' work of instruction and administration, while the apostles' work was to preach, found churches, and superintend them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. God so ordered it that no one could say Paul had baptized people into his own name. The restraint was providential, not a reason Paul had in mind at the time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Paul also baptized the household of Stephanas, the firstfruits of Achaia among the first converts there. Such households likely included children, and the history of the church favors infant baptism as the usage from the earliest ages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Christ sent Paul to preach the gospel, not to baptize — not to depreciate baptism, which he exalts elsewhere, but because the apostles' peculiar work was preaching and founding churches. He preached not with philosophical reasoning dressed in oratory, lest human eloquence empty the cross of Christ, the sum of the gospel, of its power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The message of the cross seems foolishness to those on the way to perishing, because they prefer human wisdom of words to the doctrine of the cross. To those on the way to salvation it is the power of God — his mighty instrument of salvation and the highest exhibition of his power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Quoting Isaiah, Paul says God himself will destroy the wisdom of the wise and bring the understanding of the prudent to nothing. Worldly wisdom perishes not by accident but by God's own act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Where is the wise man, the Jewish scribe, or the Greek disputer now? God has shown this age's philosophy to be folly, because it lacks faith in Christ crucified, and he has not used its help in converting and saving men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. In God's wise arrangement, the world by its own philosophy never came to know God, whatever other knowledge it gained — nature's highest gifts cannot make the moral day dawn, any more than stars and moon can make it day. So it pleased God to save believers through preaching that the world calls foolishness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Jews demanded signs from heaven as direct tokens that Jesus was Messiah, while Greeks sought a philosophical demonstration of Christianity. Christ instead demands faith on the ground of his word and reasonable evidence that the revelation is his — Christianity begins by satisfying the heart longing for forgiveness, not by solving every intellectual difficulty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Paul and Apollos preached Christ crucified — the Greek marks not just the fact of crucifixion but the permanent character Christ now bears as Savior. To Jews this was a stumbling-block and to Gentiles foolishness, yet a religion so seemingly contemptible in origin could not have succeeded unless it were divine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. To those effectually called, Jew and Greek alike, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God. The cross, a slave's death that scandalized Jews looking for a temporal Messiah, meets every reasonable demand for a sign; and Christ, received beyond the offense of the cross, is wisdom in the highest degree. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. What men call the foolishness of God — his plan of salvation — is wiser than men, and what they call his weakness — Christ crucified through weakness, yet living by God's power — is stronger than men. God perfects strength out of the weakness of his servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Consider your calling: not many wise by human study, not many mighty, not many noble were called. The stress falls on the weakness of the instruments God used, so that the church's expansion is a progressive victory of the lowly over the lofty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. God chose the foolish things of the world to shame the wise, and the weak things to shame the mighty. The repeated 'chosen' marks the gracious deliberateness of his purpose: he effects through his instruments, without human wisdom, what the worldly wise cannot effect — bringing men to salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. God chose the lowborn and despised, things regarded as nothing, to bring to nothing the things that are. The clause gathers up the foolish, weak, and base as one class of nothings in the world's eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. No flesh may glory in God's presence. Human greatness, like the flower of the field, is beautiful but frail, and those who boast in it are put to shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Believers are in Christ not of themselves but of God, by living union with him. From God, Christ has been made to them wisdom — wise unto salvation where once they were fools — and righteousness, the ground of justification, and sanctification by his Spirit, and redemption, the final deliverance of the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. As Jeremiah wrote, whoever glories must glory in the Lord, not in flesh or in the world. Paul joins humility with the elevating consciousness of true dignity in Christ, against both self-glory and slavish self-abasement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • Cephas. The Aramaic name for Peter, one of the leaders whose name some Corinthians claimed as their party badge. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • foolishness of preaching. Paul's phrase for the world's verdict on the preached cross, which is in fact God's power. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
