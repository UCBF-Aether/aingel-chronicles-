The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Corinthians 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now concerning spiritual gifts, brethren, I would not have you ignorant.

2 Ye know that ye were Gentiles, carried away unto these dumb idols, even as ye were led.

3 Wherefore I give you to understand, that no man speaking by the Spirit of God calleth Jesus accursed: and that no man can say that Jesus is the Lord, but by the Holy Ghost.

4 Now there are diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit.

5 And there are differences of administrations, but the same Lord.

6 And there are diversities of operations, but it is the same God which worketh all in all.

7 But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal.

8 For to one is given by the Spirit the word of wisdom; to another the word of knowledge by the same Spirit;

9 To another faith by the same Spirit; to another the gifts of healing by the same Spirit;

10 To another the working of miracles; to another prophecy; to another discerning of spirits; to another divers kinds of tongues; to another the interpretation of tongues:

11 But all these worketh that one and the selfsame Spirit, dividing to every man severally as he will.

12 For as the body is one, and hath many members, and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body: so also is Christ.

13 For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit.

14 For the body is not one member, but many.

15 If the foot shall say, Because I am not the hand, I am not of the body; is it therefore not of the body?

16 And if the ear shall say, Because I am not the eye, I am not of the body; is it therefore not of the body?

17 If the whole body were an eye, where were the hearing? If the whole were hearing, where were the smelling?

18 But now hath God set the members every one of them in the body, as it hath pleased him.

19 And if they were all one member, where were the body?

20 But now are they many members, yet but one body.

21 And the eye cannot say unto the hand, I have no need of thee: nor again the head to the feet, I have no need of you.

22 Nay, much more those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are necessary:

23 And those members of the body, which we think to be less honourable, upon these we bestow more abundant honour; and our uncomely parts have more abundant comeliness.

24 For our comely parts have no need: but God hath tempered the body together, having given more abundant honour to that part which lacked.

25 That there should be no schism in the body; but that the members should have the same care one for another.

26 And whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it; or one member be honoured, all the members rejoice with it.

27 Now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular.

28 And God hath set some in the church, first apostles, secondarily prophets, thirdly teachers, after that miracles, then gifts of healings, helps, governments, diversities of tongues.

29 Are all apostles? are all prophets? are all teachers? are all workers of miracles?

30 Have all the gifts of healing? do all speak with tongues? do all interpret?

31 But covet earnestly the best gifts: and yet shew I unto you a more excellent way.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

About spiritual gifts, Paul insists the same Spirit gives diverse gifts, the same Lord diverse administrations, and the same God diverse operations, all for the common good. The church is one body with many members, none dispensable, from apostles and prophets to helps and tongues, and Paul closes by urging earnest desire for the best gifts, while showing a more excellent way, which the next chapter names as love. Interpreted Overview of this chapter's text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Concerning spiritual gifts — the signs of the Spirit's continued presence in the church, Christ's body as the body complements the head — Paul would not have them ignorant, despite their boasts of knowledge. He sets forth in order the unity of the body, the variety of its members and functions, the grand principle of love for rightly exercising the gifts, and then the comparison of gifts with one another. Ordinary and extraordinary gifts alike flow from the indwelling Spirit, and extraordinary gifts did not even prove their possessors were in a safe state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When they were Gentiles, they were carried away to dumb idols, blindly transported at the will of false guides, led at random without any definite principle, as heathen oracles led their votaries. This past explains why they needed instruction about spiritual gifts: when blind, they went to the dumb, in contrast to the living God who speaks in the believer by his Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The test of inspiration is the rejection or confession of Jesus as Lord. No one speaking in the Spirit of God calls Jesus accursed, as Jews and Gentiles treated him, and no one can say Jesus is Lord except in the Holy Spirit — 'say' meaning a spiritual and believing confession of the historical, living God-man, acknowledging oneself his servant, since 'Lord' is the Septuagint's rendering of Jehovah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. There are diversities of gifts — varieties of spiritual endowments peculiar to the several members — but the same Spirit. The Holy Trinity appears in the passage: the Spirit here, Christ in the ministrations, and the Father in the operations, with gifts, administrations, and operations corresponding respectively to the Three. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. There are differences of administrations, the various functions and services performed by those having the gifts, but the same Lord, whom the Spirit glorifies by these ministrations. Gifts, administrations, and operations form an ascending climax from endowment to service to effect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. There are diversities of operations, the actual effects resulting from gifts and administrations through the universally operative power of the one Father, who works all in all — all the gifts in all the persons who possess them, by his Spirit working. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The manifestation of the Spirit is given to each member severally, varying in each individual though all gifts flow from the one God, Lord, and Spirit, and it is given with a view to the profit of the whole body, not of the individual alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. To one is given by the Spirit the word of wisdom — ready utterance of wisdom, new revelations of divine wisdom in redemption, as contrasted with human philosophy — and to another the word of knowledge, ready utterance supernaturally imparted of truths already revealed. Wisdom penetrates deeper than knowledge and belongs to prophets, while knowledge belongs to teachers and relates to things to be done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. To another is given faith by the same Spirit — not faith in doctrines but miracle-faith, confidence by the Spirit's impulse that God would enable the performance of any required miracle — and to another gifts of healing, in the plural, for different kinds of disease needing different kinds of healing. Saving faith differs in object, not in principle, from this faith: both rely on God, and saving faith saves by the merits of its object, not its own merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. To another the working of miracles of special and extraordinary power, such as raising the dead or inflicting death by a word, distinct from healings that might in time be effected by human skill. To another prophecy, here the inspired disclosure of the future, depending on faith and ranking next to apostles. To another discerning of spirits, distinguishing God's Spirit from an evil spirit or unaided human spirit. To another diverse kinds of tongues, various languages and also a spiritual language uttered in ecstasy, and to another the interpretation of tongues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. All these are worked by one and the same Spirit, dividing to every man severally as he will. The distribution is sovereign, not according to human preference. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. As the body, though one, has many members, and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body, so also is Christ — the whole Christ, head and body. Unity, not unvarying uniformity, is the law of God in grace as in nature; the many members compose an organic whole and none can be dispensed with. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. By one Spirit we are all baptized into one body, whether Jews or Greeks, bond or free, and all made to drink of one Spirit. Baptism's designed effect is realized when not frustrated by man's unfaithfulness, and the drinking of one Spirit alludes indirectly to the Lord's Supper, as Spirit, water, and blood combine the two outward signs with the inward grace signified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The body is not one member but many, and the analogy illustrates the mutual dependence of the various members in the one body, the church — like the fable of the belly and the members told by Menenius Agrippa to the seceding commons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. If the foot says, 'Because I am not the hand, I am not of the body,' it is not therefore not of the body. The humbler members ought not to disparage themselves: the foot and hand represent active life, and each compares himself with those nearest him in gifts, not those far superior. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. If the ear says, 'Because I am not the eye, I am not of the body,' it is not therefore not of the body. The ear and eye represent contemplative life, and the ear speaks of the nobler eye as the foot speaks of the hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. If the whole body were an eye, where would the hearing be? If the whole were hearing, where would the smelling be? Superior as the eye is, it would not do if it were the sole member to the exclusion of the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. But now God has set the members, every one of them, in the body as it has pleased him. Each member is severally placed by God's own appointment, not by self-selection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. If all were one member, where would the body be? A body by its very idea has many members, so oneness of member would destroy the body itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. But now there are many members, yet one body — many mutually dependent members, not a heap of disconnected parts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The eye cannot say to the hand, 'I have no need of you,' nor the head to the feet, 'I have no need of you.' The higher cannot dispense with the lower members. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Those members of the body which seem more feeble — more susceptible of injury, like the brain, belly, and eye — are necessary. Their very feebleness calls forth greater care for their preservation, as being felt necessary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Those members we think less honorable, we bestow more abundant honor on — putting shoes on the feet and clothes to cover the belly — and our uncomely parts have more abundant comeliness, since even the poorest cover those secret parts though unclad elsewhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Our comely parts have no need of such honor, but God has tempered the body together on the principle of mutual compensation, giving more abundant honor to the part that lacked, the deficient part. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. This tempering is so that there should be no schism in the body — no disunion, referring to the divisions at Corinth — but that the members should have the same care one for another, in behalf of one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Whether one member suffers, all the members suffer with it — when a thorn enters the heel the whole body feels it, the back bends, the hands come forward to draw it out, the head stoops and the eyes gaze intently. Or if one member is honored, all the members rejoice with it, as when the head is crowned the whole man feels honored. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. You are the body of Christ, and members in particular — severally members of it. Each church is in miniature what the whole aggregate of churches is collectively, the body of Christ, and its components are members each in his assigned place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. God has set some in the church, first apostles — above even the prophets, and not merely the Twelve, but others such as Barnabas — secondarily prophets, thirdly teachers, who taught truths already revealed while prophets made new revelations, after that miracles, ranked below teachers since teaching is more edifying though less dazzling, then gifts of healings, helps, governments, and diversities of tongues. Helps are lower ministrations, such as deacons relieving the poor and helpers with time and means; governments are higher, presbyters or bishops guiding the helm of the church, ranked with lower functions despite outward status because occupied with external things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? Are all workers of miracles? Surely not. The rhetorical questions establish that gifts are distributed severally, not universally. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Have all the gifts of healing? Do all speak with tongues? Do all interpret? Surely not — the same distribution holds for the remaining gifts, and no member holds them all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Covet earnestly the best — the greatest — gifts, emulously desiring them by prayer, watchfulness, and cultivation of faculties, not in discontented coveting, since the Spirit divides as he will. And yet, besides this, Paul will show a still more excellent way to desire: the way of love, which includes faith and hope and bears the Spirit's fruits, and is more to be desired than gifts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.
