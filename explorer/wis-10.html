Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Wisdom of Solomon 10 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 She preserved the first formed father of the world, that was created alone, and brought him out of his fall,

2 And gave him power to rule all things.

3 But when the unrighteous went away from her in his anger, he perished also in the fury wherewith he murdered his brother.

4 For whose cause the earth being drowned with the flood, wisdom again preserved it, and directed the course of the righteous in a piece of wood of small value.

5 Moreover, the nations in their wicked conspiracy being confounded, she found out the righteous, and preserved him blameless unto God, and kept him strong against his tender compassion toward his son.

6 When the ungodly perished, she delivered the righteous man, who fled from the fire which fell down upon the five cities.

7 Of whose wickedness even to this day the waste land that smoketh is a testimony, and plants bearing fruit that never come to ripeness: and a standing pillar of salt is a monument of an unbelieving soul.

8 For regarding not wisdom, they gat not only this hurt, that they knew not the things which were good; but also left behind them to the world a memorial of their foolishness: so that in the things wherein they offended they could not so much as be hid.

9 Rut wisdom delivered from pain those that attended upon her.

10 When the righteous fled from his brother's wrath she guided him in right paths, shewed him the kingdom of God, and gave him knowledge of holy things, made him rich in his travels, and multiplied the fruit of his labours.

11 In the covetousness of such as oppressed him she stood by him, and made him rich.

12 She defended him from his enemies, and kept him safe from those that lay in wait, and in a sore conflict she gave him the victory; that he might know that goodness is stronger than all.

13 When the righteous was sold, she forsook him not, but delivered him from sin: she went down with him into the pit,

14 And left him not in bonds, till she brought him the sceptre of the kingdom, and power against those that oppressed him: as for them that had accused him, she shewed them to be liars, and gave him perpetual glory.

15 She delivered the righteous people and blameless seed from the nation that oppressed them.

16 She entered into the soul of the servant of the Lord, and withstood dreadful kings in wonders and signs;

17 Rendered to the righteous a reward of their labours, guided them in a marvellous way, and was unto them for a cover by day, and a light of stars in the night season;

18 Brought them through the Red sea, and led them through much water:

19 But she drowned their enemies, and cast them up out of the bottom of the deep.

20 Therefore the righteous spoiled the ungodly, and praised thy holy name, O Lord, and magnified with one accord thine hand, that fought for them.

21 For wisdom opened the mouth of the dumb, and made the tongues of them that cannot speak eloquent.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Wisdom is traced through the early history: she preserved the first-formed man, delivered the world through the flood, found the righteous man amid confounded nations, delivered him from fire, guided the fugitive, stood by the oppressed, delivered the sold man to a sceptre, and led the righteous people through the Red Sea while drowning their enemies. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. She preserved the first formed father of the world, created alone, and brought him out of his fall. Haydock notes wisdom's first work: preserving Adam and raising him from his fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. And gave him power to rule all things. Haydock observes that dominion, lost by the fall, is restored in part through wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. But when the unrighteous went away from her in anger, he perished in the fury with which he murdered his brother. Haydock's contrast is Cain, who left wisdom and perished in fratricide's fury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. When the earth was drowned because of him, wisdom again preserved it, and directed the course of the righteous in a piece of wood of small value. Haydock notes the ark: a small piece of wood, directed by wisdom, preserves the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. Moreover, when nations were confounded in wicked consent, she knew the righteous and preserved him blameless to God, and kept him strong against his tender compassion toward his son. Haydock reads this as Abraham, kept blameless amid confounded nations and strong in the trial of his son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. She delivered the righteous man who fled from fire descending on the five cities, whose wickedness is witnessed by smoking land, plants bearing fruit that never ripens, and a standing pillar of salt as a monument of an unbelieving soul. Haydock notes Lot's deliverance and the smoking land as witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. The five cities' wickedness left a smoking land, unripe fruit, and a salt pillar for an unbelieving soul. Haydock observes the monuments remain as warnings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. For regarding not wisdom, they got not only this hurt, that they knew not good things, but also left a memorial of their foolishness to the world, so their faults could not pass unnoticed. Haydock's point is double hurt: ignorance of good, and a lasting memorial of folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. Wisdom delivers from pain those who attend to her. Haydock's summary is that attendance on wisdom is deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. When the righteous fled from his brother's wrath, she guided him in right paths, showed him God's kingdom, and gave him knowledge of holy things, making him rich in labours and fruitful. Haydock reads this as Jacob, guided in flight and enriched in labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. In the covetousness of those who oppressed him, she stood by him and made him rich. Haydock notes wisdom stood by Jacob against oppressors and made him rich. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. She defended him from enemies and kept him safe from those who lay in wait, and in a sore conflict gave him victory, that he might know godliness is stronger than all. Haydock observes the victory's lesson: godliness is stronger than all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. When the righteous was sold, she forsook him not, but delivered him from sin; she went down with him into the pit, and left him not in bonds, till she brought him the sceptre of the kingdom and power against those who oppressed him. Haydock reads this as Joseph, accompanied into pit and prison until the sceptre. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. She delivered him from those who devised against him and made liars of his accusers. Haydock notes wisdom's vindication: accusers proved liars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. She delivered the holy people and blameless seed from a nation that oppressed them. Haydock's turn is to Israel in Egypt, delivered as a holy people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. She entered into the soul of the Lord's servant, and withstood dreadful kings with wonders and signs. Haydock notes wisdom entered Moses and withstood Pharaoh with wonders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. She rendered to the righteous the reward of their labours, guided them in a marvellous way, and was a cover by day and a light of stars in the night. Haydock pictures the Exodus guidance: cover by day, starlight by night. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. She brought them through the Red Sea and led them through much water. Haydock's point is the sea passage itself, led by wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. But she drowned their enemies, and cast them up out of the sea's bottom. Haydock notes the reversal: the same waters that bore Israel drowned Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. Therefore the righteous spoiled the ungodly, and praised your holy name, O Lord, and magnified with one accord your victorious hand. Haydock observes that spoil turns to praise of God's victorious hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. Wisdom opened the mouth of the dumb, and made the tongues of children eloquent. Haydock closes the chapter: wisdom gives speech to the dumb and eloquence to children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
