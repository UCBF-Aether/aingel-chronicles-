The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then they told David, saying, Behold, the Philistines fight against Keilah, and they rob the threshingfloors.

2 Therefore David enquired of the LORD, saying, Shall I go and smite these Philistines? And the LORD said unto David, Go, and smite the Philistines, and save Keilah.

3 And David’s men said unto him, Behold, we be afraid here in Judah: how much more then if we come to Keilah against the armies of the Philistines?

4 Then David enquired of the LORD yet again. And the LORD answered him and said, Arise, go down to Keilah; for I will deliver the Philistines into thine hand.

5 So David and his men went to Keilah, and fought with the Philistines, and brought away their cattle, and smote them with a great slaughter. So David saved the inhabitants of Keilah.

6 And it came to pass, when Abiathar the son of Ahimelech fled to David to Keilah, that he came down with an ephod in his hand.

7 And it was told Saul that David was come to Keilah. And Saul said, God hath delivered him into mine hand; for he is shut in, by entering into a town that hath gates and bars.

8 And Saul called all the people together to war, to go down to Keilah, to besiege David and his men.

9 And David knew that Saul secretly practised mischief against him; and he said to Abiathar the priest, Bring hither the ephod.

10 Then said David, O LORD God of Israel, thy servant hath certainly heard that Saul seeketh to come to Keilah, to destroy the city for my sake.

11 Will the men of Keilah deliver me up into his hand? will Saul come down, as thy servant hath heard? O LORD God of Israel, I beseech thee, tell thy servant. And the LORD said, He will come down.

12 Then said David, Will the men of Keilah deliver me and my men into the hand of Saul? And the LORD said, They will deliver thee up.

13 Then David and his men, which were about six hundred, arose and departed out of Keilah, and went whithersoever they could go. And it was told Saul that David was escaped from Keilah; and he forbare to go forth.

14 And David abode in the wilderness in strong holds, and remained in a mountain in the wilderness of Ziph. And Saul sought him every day, but God delivered him not into his hand.

15 And David saw that Saul was come out to seek his life: and David was in the wilderness of Ziph in a wood.

16 And Jonathan Saul’s son arose, and went to David into the wood, and strengthened his hand in God.

17 And he said unto him, Fear not: for the hand of Saul my father shall not find thee; and thou shalt be king over Israel, and I shall be next unto thee; and that also Saul my father knoweth.

18 And they two made a covenant before the LORD: and David abode in the wood, and Jonathan went to his house.

19 Then came up the Ziphites to Saul to Gibeah, saying, Doth not David hide himself with us in strong holds in the wood, in the hill of Hachilah, which is on the south of Jeshimon?

20 Now therefore, O king, come down according to all the desire of thy soul to come down; and our part shall be to deliver him into the king’s hand.

21 And Saul said, Blessed be ye of the LORD; for ye have compassion on me.

22 Go, I pray you, prepare yet, and know and see his place where his haunt is, and who hath seen him there: for it is told me that he dealeth very subtilly.

23 See therefore, and take knowledge of all the lurking places where he hideth himself, and come ye again to me with the certainty, and I will go with you: and it shall come to pass, if he be in the land, that I will search him out throughout all the thousands of Judah.

24 And they arose, and went to Ziph before Saul: but David and his men were in the wilderness of Maon, in the plain on the south of Jeshimon.

25 Saul also and his men went to seek him. And they told David; wherefore he came down into a rock, and abode in the wilderness of Maon. And when Saul heard that, he pursued after David in the wilderness of Maon.

26 And Saul went on this side of the mountain, and David and his men on that side of the mountain: and David made haste to get away for fear of Saul; for Saul and his men compassed David and his men round about to take them.

27 But there came a messenger unto Saul, saying, Haste thee, and come; for the Philistines have invaded the land.

28 Wherefore Saul returned from pursuing after David, and went against the Philistines: therefore they called that place Selahammahlekoth.

29 And David went up from thence, and dwelt in strong holds at Engedi.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

David, hearing the Philistines fight against Keilah and rob the threshingfloors, enquires of the LORD twice and saves the city with a great slaughter, bringing away their cattle, though the city will soon prove ready to betray him. Abiathar, escaped from Nob, brings the ephod to Keilah, and David uses it at once: Saul, thinking David shut in by gates and bars, musters to besiege him, and David asks whether Saul will come down and whether Keilah's men will deliver him up, and the LORD answers they will, so David and about six hundred men depart whithersoever they can go. In the wilderness of Ziph Saul seeks him every day but God delivers him not into his hand; Jonathan comes into the wood and strengthens his hand in God, promising, thou shalt be king over Israel and I shall be next unto thee, and they make a covenant before the LORD. The Ziphites betray David's hiding place on the hill of Hachilah, Saul demands certainty of every lurking place and will search him out through all the thousands of Judah, and David and Saul end on opposite sides of a mountain in Maon, Saul compassing him round about, until a messenger calls Saul away to a Philistine invasion, and the place is called Selahammahlekoth; David goes up to the strongholds of Engedi. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. They told David, Behold, the Philistines fight against Keilah and rob the threshingfloors. When princes persecute God's people, Henry notes, let them expect vexation on all sides: if Saul fights against David, the Philistines fight against Saul's country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Therefore David enquired of the LORD, Shall I go and smite these Philistines? And the LORD said, Go, smite the Philistines, and save Keilah. David considered himself the protector of the land, Henry says; thus did the Saviour Jesus, and left us an example. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. David's men said, Behold, we be afraid here in Judah; how much more then if we come to Keilah against the Philistine armies? The fugitive band's fear was plain, and David did not dismiss it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Then David enquired of the LORD yet again, and the LORD answered, Arise, go down to Keilah, for I will deliver the Philistines into thine hand. The second enquiry confirmed the first with a promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. So David and his men went to Keilah, fought the Philistines, brought away their cattle, smote them with a great slaughter, and saved the inhabitants of Keilah. Those unlike David, Henry observes, sullenly decline to do good if they are not rewarded for services; David saved a city that would soon betray him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. When Abiathar son of Ahimelech fled to David to Keilah, he came down with an ephod in his hand. The escaped priest brought the means of enquiring of God, and David would use it at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. It was told Saul that David was come to Keilah, and Saul said, God hath delivered him into mine hand, for he is shut in, by entering a town with gates and bars. The walled town that David had saved looked to Saul like a trap. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Saul called all the people together to war, to go down to Keilah to besiege David and his men. The king mustered Israel against the man who had just saved one of its cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. David knew Saul secretly practised mischief against him, and said to Abiathar the priest, Bring hither the ephod. David applied to his great Protector for direction, Henry notes; no sooner was the ephod brought than he made use of it. We have the Scriptures in our hands, Henry says — let us take advice from them in doubtful cases: say, Bring hither the Bible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Then said David, O LORD God of Israel, thy servant hath certainly heard that Saul seeketh to come to Keilah, to destroy the city for my sake. David's address to God was very solemn and very particular, Henry observes, and God allows us to be so in our addresses: Lord, direct me in this matter, about which I am now at a loss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. David asked whether the men of Keilah would deliver him up and whether Saul would come down, and the LORD answered that he would come down. The first question was answered directly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Then said David, Will the men of Keilah deliver me and my men into Saul's hand? And the LORD said, They will deliver thee up. Well might David complain, Henry says, that his enemies rewarded him evil for good, and that for his love they were his adversaries; Christ was used thus basely. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Then David and his men, about six hundred, arose and departed out of Keilah, and went whithersoever they could go, and when Saul heard David was escaped, he forbore to go forth. God knows not only what will be, Henry notes, but what would be if it were not hindered, and therefore knows how to deliver the godly out of temptation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. David abode in the wilderness in strongholds, and remained in a mountain in the wilderness of Ziph, and Saul sought him every day, but God delivered him not into his hand. David made no attempt against Saul, Henry observes; he kept God's way, waited God's time, and was content to secure himself in woods and wildernesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. David saw that Saul was come out to seek his life, and David was in the wilderness of Ziph in a wood. The hunt continued into the woods of Ziph. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Jonathan Saul's son arose and went to David into the wood, and strengthened his hand in God. We find Jonathan comforting David, Henry says: as a pious friend, he directed him to God, the Foundation of his comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. He said, Fear not, for Saul my father's hand shall not find thee, and thou shalt be king over Israel, and I shall be next unto thee, and that also Saul my father knoweth. As a self-denying friend, Henry notes, Jonathan took pleasure in the prospect of David's advancement to the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. They two made a covenant before the LORD, and David abode in the wood, and Jonathan went to his house. As a constant friend, Henry observes, Jonathan renewed his friendship with him; our covenant with God should be often renewed, and therein our communion with him kept up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Then came the Ziphites to Saul to Gibeah, saying, Doth not David hide himself with us in strongholds in the wood, in the hill of Hachilah, south of Jeshimon? The men of Ziph betrayed the hiding place to the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Now therefore, O king, come down according to all the desire of thy soul, and our part shall be to deliver him into the king's hand. The betrayers offered themselves as the king's hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Saul said, Blessed be ye of the LORD, for ye have compassion on me. In the midst of his wickedness, Henry notes, Saul affected to speak the language of piety; such expressions, without suitable effects, can only amuse or deceive those who hear and use them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Go, prepare yet, and know and see his place where his haunt is, and who hath seen him there, for it is told me that he dealeth very subtilly. Saul demanded certainty before he moved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. See therefore, and take knowledge of all the lurking places where he hideth himself, and come again to me with the certainty, and I will go with you, and if he be in the land, I will search him out throughout all the thousands of Judah. The net was to close over every thousand of Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. They arose and went to Ziph before Saul, but David and his men were in the wilderness of Maon, in the plain south of Jeshimon. The betrayers went ahead, but David had already moved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Saul and his men went to seek him, and they told David, wherefore he came down into a rock and abode in the wilderness of Maon, and when Saul heard that, he pursued after David in Maon. The pursuit shifted to the rock country of Maon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Saul went on this side of the mountain, and David and his men on that side, and David made haste to get away for fear of Saul, for Saul and his men compassed David and his men round about to take them. This mountain was an emblem of Divine Providence coming between David and the destroyer, Henry observes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. But there came a messenger unto Saul, saying, Haste thee, and come, for the Philistines have invaded the land. At the very moment the net closed, an invasion called the hunter away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Wherefore Saul returned from pursuing after David and went against the Philistines; therefore they called that place Selahammahlekoth. Sooner than his promise shall fail, Henry says, God will commission Philistines to effect our escape at the very moment when our case appears most desperate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. David went up from thence and dwelt in strongholds at Engedi. The fugitive moved on to the cliffs above the Dead Sea, safe for another season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
