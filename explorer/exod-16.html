The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And they took their journey from Elim, and all the congregation of the children of Israel came unto the wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after their departing out of the land of Egypt.

2 And the whole congregation of the children of Israel murmured against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness:

3 And the children of Israel said unto them, Would to God we had died by the hand of the LORD in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the flesh pots, and when we did eat bread to the full; for ye have brought us forth into this wilderness, to kill this whole assembly with hunger.

4 Then said the LORD unto Moses, Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no.

5 And it shall come to pass, that on the sixth day they shall prepare that which they bring in; and it shall be twice as much as they gather daily.

6 And Moses and Aaron said unto all the children of Israel, At even, then ye shall know that the LORD hath brought you out from the land of Egypt:

7 And in the morning, then ye shall see the glory of the LORD; for that he heareth your murmurings against the LORD: and what are we, that ye murmur against us?

8 And Moses said, This shall be, when the LORD shall give you in the evening flesh to eat, and in the morning bread to the full; for that the LORD heareth your murmurings which ye murmur against him: and what are we? your murmurings are not against us, but against the LORD.

9 And Moses spake unto Aaron, Say unto all the congregation of the children of Israel, Come near before the LORD: for he hath heard your murmurings.

10 And it came to pass, as Aaron spake unto the whole congregation of the children of Israel, that they looked toward the wilderness, and, behold, the glory of the LORD appeared in the cloud.

11 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

12 I have heard the murmurings of the children of Israel: speak unto them, saying, At even ye shall eat flesh, and in the morning ye shall be filled with bread; and ye shall know that I am the LORD your God.

13 And it came to pass, that at even the quails came up, and covered the camp: and in the morning the dew lay round about the host.

14 And when the dew that lay was gone up, behold, upon the face of the wilderness there lay a small round thing, as small as the hoar frost on the ground.

15 And when the children of Israel saw it, they said one to another, It is manna: for they wist not what it was. And Moses said unto them, This is the bread which the LORD hath given you to eat.

16 This is the thing which the LORD hath commanded, Gather of it every man according to his eating, an omer for every man, according to the number of your persons; take ye every man for them which are in his tents.

17 And the children of Israel did so, and gathered, some more, some less.

18 And when they did mete it with an omer, he that gathered much had nothing over, and he that gathered little had no lack; they gathered every man according to his eating.

19 And Moses said, Let no man leave of it till the morning.

20 Notwithstanding they hearkened not unto Moses; but some of them left of it until the morning, and it bred worms, and stank: and Moses was wroth with them.

21 And they gathered it every morning, every man according to his eating: and when the sun waxed hot, it melted.

22 And it came to pass, that on the sixth day they gathered twice as much bread, two omers for one man: and all the rulers of the congregation came and told Moses.

23 And he said unto them, This is that which the LORD hath said, To morrow is the rest of the holy sabbath unto the LORD: bake that which ye will bake to day, and seethe that ye will seethe; and that which remaineth over lay up for you to be kept until the morning.

24 And they laid it up till the morning, as Moses bade: and it did not stink, neither was there any worm therein.

25 And Moses said, Eat that to day; for to day is a sabbath unto the LORD: to day ye shall not find it in the field.

26 Six days ye shall gather it; but on the seventh day, which is the sabbath, in it there shall be none.

27 And it came to pass, that there went out some of the people on the seventh day for to gather, and they found none.

28 And the LORD said unto Moses, How long refuse ye to keep my commandments and my laws?

29 See, for that the LORD hath given you the sabbath, therefore he giveth you on the sixth day the bread of two days; abide ye every man in his place, let no man go out of his place on the seventh day.

30 So the people rested on the seventh day.

31 And the house of Israel called the name thereof Manna: and it was like coriander seed, white; and the taste of it was like wafers made with honey.

32 And Moses said, This is the thing which the LORD commandeth, Fill an omer of it to be kept for your generations; that they may see the bread wherewith I have fed you in the wilderness, when I brought you forth from the land of Egypt.

33 And Moses said unto Aaron, Take a pot, and put an omer full of manna therein, and lay it up before the LORD, to be kept for your generations.

34 As the LORD commanded Moses, so Aaron laid it up before the Testimony, to be kept.

35 And the children of Israel did eat manna forty years, until they came to a land inhabited; they did eat manna, until they came unto the borders of the land of Canaan.

36 Now an omer is the tenth part of an ephah.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the wilderness of Sin the people murmur for bread, and God rains manna in the morning and brings quails in the evening. Israel gathers a daily portion, double before the Sabbath, and none on the Sabbath itself, learning that the bread is the LORD's gift and not their own storing. A pot of manna is kept as a memorial for later generations. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. They journeyed from Elim and came to the wilderness of Sin, between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after departing Egypt. Henry notes the provisions brought from Egypt were spent by the middle of the second month, and want became the occasion of murmuring. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The whole congregation murmured against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness. Henry observes it is no new thing for the greatest kindness to be basely represented as the greatest injury, and none talk more absurdly than murmurers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They said, Would to God we had died by the hand of the LORD in Egypt, when we sat by the flesh pots and ate bread to the full, for ye have brought us out to kill this assembly with hunger. Henry notes the absurdity: they had not plenty in Egypt, nor could they fear dying for want while they had flocks and herds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The LORD said to Moses, Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you, and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them whether they will walk in my law. Henry observes God promised a speedy and constant supply, and tried whether they would trust him and rest satisfied with the bread of the day in its day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. On the sixth day they would prepare what they brought in, and it would be twice as much as they gathered daily. Henry notes the double portion was itself a provision for the sabbath, teaching them to prepare beforehand for holy rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Moses and Aaron told all Israel, At even ye shall know that the LORD hath brought you out of Egypt. Henry observes the evening meal would settle the question the murmuring had raised: their deliverer was the LORD, not Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. In the morning they would see the glory of the LORD, for he heard their murmurings against the LORD, and what were Moses and Aaron that they murmured against them? Henry notes the rebuke: when we begin to fret, we ought to consider that God hears all our murmurings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Moses said the LORD would give them flesh in the evening and bread in the morning to the full, and their murmurings were not against Moses and Aaron but against the LORD. Henry observes the distinction mattered: to murmur against God's servants for God's providence is to murmur against God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Moses told Aaron to say to the congregation, Come near before the LORD, for he hath heard your murmurings. Henry notes the summons was gracious even in rebuke: the murmurers were called near, not cast off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. As Aaron spoke to the whole congregation, they looked toward the wilderness, and behold, the glory of the LORD appeared in the cloud. Henry observes the glory answered the summons at once, that Israel might know their God had heard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The LORD spake unto Moses, renewing the promise after the glory appeared. Henry notes God spoke again to confirm what the cloud had shown, that the people might have both sight and word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. I have heard the murmurings of Israel, said the LORD: at even ye shall eat flesh, and in the morning ye shall be filled with bread, and ye shall know that I am the LORD your God. Henry observes the twofold lesson: when God plagued Egypt it was to make them know he was their Lord, and when he provided for Israel it was to make them know he was their God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. At even the quails came up and covered the camp, and in the morning the dew lay round about the camp. Henry notes the quails came at evening and were caught with ease, as many as they needed, a sudden supply for a sudden want. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. When the dew was gone up, there lay upon the wilderness a small round thing, as small as the hoar frost. Henry observes the manna appeared when the dew was gone, small and round like coriander seed, in colour like pearls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. When Israel saw it they said one to another, It is manna, for they wist not what it was, and Moses said, This is the bread which the LORD hath given you to eat. Henry notes they called it Manhu, What is this? and took it as the portion God had allotted them, with thankfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. This is the thing the LORD commanded: Gather of it every man according to his eating, an omer for every man, according to the number of persons, every man for them which are in his tent. Henry observes God's bounty left room for man's duty: they must not eat till they have gathered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Israel did so and gathered, some more, some less. Henry notes the gathering itself taught diligence: with quietness working, and eating their own bread, not the bread of idleness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. When they meted it with an omer, he that gathered much had nothing over, and he that gathered little had no lack, every man according to his eating. Henry observes the lesson of contentment: he who gathers much has nothing over, and he who gathers little has no lack, for there is less disproportion in enjoyment than in possession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Moses said, Let no man leave of it till the morning. Henry notes the command taught dependence on providence: they were to sleep quietly with no bread in their tents, trusting God to bring daily bread with the day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Notwithstanding, some hearkened not and left of it until the morning, and it bred worms and stank, and Moses was wroth with them. Henry observes the folly of hoarding: manna laid up distrustfully bred worms, and what is covetously spared proves most wasted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. They gathered it every morning, every man according to his eating, and when the sun waxed hot, it melted. Henry notes the morning discipline: the gift had its hour, and those who let slip the morning of opportunity might find it too late to gather. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. On the sixth day they gathered twice as much bread, two omers for one, and all the rulers of the congregation came and told Moses. Henry observes the double quantity on the sixth day was the sign of the sabbath provision, and the rulers reported what they did not yet understand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Moses said, This is what the LORD hath said: Tomorrow is the rest of the holy sabbath unto the LORD; bake what ye will bake, seethe what ye will seethe, and lay up what remaineth until the morning. Henry notes the sabbath was known before Sinai, from the beginning, and God took care that his people should be no losers by keeping it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. They laid it up till the morning as Moses bade, and it did not stink, neither was there any worm therein. Henry observes the contrast: kept against a command it stank, kept by a command it was sweet and good, for everything is sanctified by the word of God and prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Moses said, Eat that today, for today is a sabbath unto the LORD, and today ye shall not find it in the field. Henry notes the sabbath meal came from yesterday's gathering: rest was provided for, not improvised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Six days ye shall gather it, but on the seventh day, which is the sabbath, in it there shall be none. Henry observes the rhythm was fixed: six days of gathering, one of rest, and the manna itself preached the week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Some of the people went out on the seventh day to gather, and found none. Henry notes the disobedience proved the miracle: on the seventh day God did not send the manna, therefore they must not expect it nor go out to gather. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The LORD said unto Moses, How long refuse ye to keep my commandments and my laws? Henry observes the question rebuked not ignorance but refusal: the sabbath had been shown by double portions and empty fields, yet some still went out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. See, for the LORD hath given you the sabbath, therefore he giveth you on the sixth day the bread of two days; abide every man in his place, let no man go out of his place on the seventh day. Henry notes the sabbath was a gift, not a burden, and the double bread was the proof that none would lose by serving God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. So the people rested on the seventh day. Henry observes the rest finally came, after rebuke and demonstration, and the camp learned to keep the day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. The house of Israel called the name thereof Manna, and it was like coriander seed, white, and the taste of it was like wafers made with honey. Henry notes it was pleasant and wholesome food, unlike anything found before or since, ground or beaten and made into cakes and baked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Moses said, This is what the LORD commandeth: Fill an omer of it to be kept for your generations, that they may see the bread wherewith I fed you in the wilderness, when I brought you out of Egypt. Henry observes eaten bread must not be forgotten: God's miracles and mercies are to be had in remembrance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Moses told Aaron to take a pot and put an omer full of manna therein, and lay it up before the LORD to be kept for their generations. Henry notes the word of God is the manna by which souls are nourished, and the hidden manna is the comfort of the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. As the LORD commanded Moses, so Aaron laid it up before the Testimony, to be kept. Henry observes the memorial was placed before the Testimony itself, that the bread of the wilderness might stand beside the law of the covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Israel did eat manna forty years, until they came to a land inhabited; they did eat manna until they came unto the borders of Canaan. Henry notes it continued wherever they went for forty years, and ceased when they arrived in Canaan, a provision exactly fitted to the pilgrimage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Now an omer is the tenth part of an ephah. Henry observes the measure is defined for the reader, that the daily portion might be known: enough for each, and none had too much. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
