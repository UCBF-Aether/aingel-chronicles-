The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ecclesiastes 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The words of the Preacher, the son of David, king in Jerusalem.

2 Vanity of vanities, saith the Preacher, vanity of vanities; all is vanity.

3 What profit hath a man of all his labour which he taketh under the sun?

4 One generation passeth away, and another generation cometh: but the earth abideth for ever.

5 The sun also ariseth, and the sun goeth down, and hasteth to his place where he arose.

6 The wind goeth toward the south, and turneth about unto the north; it whirleth about continually, and the wind returneth again according to his circuits.

7 All the rivers run into the sea; yet the sea is not full; unto the place from whence the rivers come, thither they return again.

8 All things are full of labour; man cannot utter it: the eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.

9 The thing that hath been, it is that which shall be; and that which is done is that which shall be done: and there is no new thing under the sun.

10 Is there any thing whereof it may be said, See, this is new? it hath been already of old time, which was before us.

11 There is no remembrance of former things; neither shall there be any remembrance of things that are to come with those that shall come after.

12 I the Preacher was king over Israel in Jerusalem.

13 And I gave my heart to seek and search out by wisdom concerning all things that are done under heaven: this sore travail hath God given to the sons of man to be exercised therewith.

14 I have seen all the works that are done under the sun; and, behold, all is vanity and vexation of spirit.

15 That which is crooked cannot be made straight: and that which is wanting cannot be numbered.

16 I communed with mine own heart, saying, Lo, I am come to great estate, and have gotten more wisdom than all they that have been before me in Jerusalem: yea, my heart had great experience of wisdom and knowledge.

17 And I gave my heart to know wisdom, and to know madness and folly: I perceived that this also is vexation of spirit.

18 For in much wisdom is much grief: and he that increaseth knowledge increaseth sorrow.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The Preacher opens with his thesis: everything done under the sun is vanity. Generations pass while sun, wind and rivers repeat their courses, memory fades, and even the search for wisdom ends in grief, because human wisdom cannot straighten what is crooked. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The speaker is the Preacher, a son of David and king in Jerusalem, whom Henry identifies as Solomon writing late in life, as one made sensible of his own folly and recording his experience for the benefit of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Vanity of vanities is the text of the whole sermon: everything in this world, considered apart from God, is empty, unable to satisfy the soul or to answer its true end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Labour under the sun yields no lasting profit, because all it gains cannot supply the wants of the soul, atone for its sins, or stand it in stead at death and judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. One generation passes and another comes, while the earth abides, so that man is brief against a world that outlasts him and keeps its course without him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The sun rises and sets and hastens back to its place, a picture of ceaseless motion that never arrives at rest, like man's own restless pursuits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The wind blows in shifting circuits and returns again, constant only in its inconstancy, as all earthly things are in perpetual flux and reflux. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. All rivers run into the sea, yet the sea is never full, because the waters return again to their sources; so the soul, like the sea, is never filled by outward streams of prosperity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. All things are full of labour beyond what words can utter, and the senses are soon tired yet still craving, the eye never satisfied with seeing nor the ear filled with hearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. What has been shall be again, and there is nothing new under the sun, because men's hearts, corruptions, desires and complaints remain the same in every age. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. When something is called new, it has already existed in ages before, and only seems new because the memory of former times has perished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. There is no remembrance of former things, nor will there be of things to come, so that even things and persons once thought very great are forgotten, a warning against expecting happiness from fame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The Preacher speaks from his own station, having been king over Israel in Jerusalem, so his verdict on earthly things rests on trial made from the highest place, not on hearsay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He set his heart to seek out by wisdom all that is done under heaven, and found the search itself a sore travail given to men, weariness to flesh and mind alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Having surveyed all the works done under the sun, he judges them all vanity and vexation of spirit, feeding the mind without feeding the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. What is crooked cannot be made straight, and what is wanting cannot be numbered, because human wisdom can neither redress the disorders of the world nor reckon up its defects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He communed with his heart that he had attained greatness and wisdom beyond all before him in Jerusalem, and that his heart had great experience of wisdom and knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. He gave his heart to know wisdom and also madness and folly, and perceived that even this pursuit was vexation of spirit, since it exposed more of man's wickedness and misery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. In much wisdom is much grief, and he that increases knowledge increases sorrow, because clearer sight of the world brings clearer sight of its vanity and of sin, the cause of it all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words in this chapter that modern readers may trip over. They are editorial explanation, not Scripture.
