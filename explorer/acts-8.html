The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Saul was consenting unto his death. And at that time there was a great persecution against the church which was at Jerusalem; and they were all scattered abroad throughout the regions of Judaea and Samaria, except the apostles.

2 And devout men carried Stephen to his burial, and made great lamentation over him.

3 As for Saul, he made havock of the church, entering into every house, and haling men and women committed them to prison.

4 Therefore they that were scattered abroad went every where preaching the word.

5 Then Philip went down to the city of Samaria, and preached Christ unto them.

6 And the people with one accord gave heed unto those things which Philip spake, hearing and seeing the miracles which he did.

7 For unclean spirits, crying with loud voice, came out of many that were possessed with them: and many taken with palsies, and that were lame, were healed.

8 And there was great joy in that city.

9 But there was a certain man, called Simon, which beforetime in the same city used sorcery, and bewitched the people of Samaria, giving out that himself was some great one:

10 To whom they all gave heed, from the least to the greatest, saying, This man is the great power of God.

11 And to him they had regard, because that of long time he had bewitched them with sorceries.

12 But when they believed Philip preaching the things concerning the kingdom of God, and the name of Jesus Christ, they were baptized, both men and women.

13 Then Simon himself believed also: and when he was baptized, he continued with Philip, and wondered, beholding the miracles and signs which were done.

14 Now when the apostles which were at Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent unto them Peter and John:

15 Who, when they were come down, prayed for them, that they might receive the Holy Ghost:

16 (For as yet he was fallen upon none of them: only they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.)

17 Then laid they their hands on them, and they received the Holy Ghost.

18 And when Simon saw that through laying on of the apostles’ hands the Holy Ghost was given, he offered them money,

19 Saying, Give me also this power, that on whomsoever I lay hands, he may receive the Holy Ghost.

20 But Peter said unto him, Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money.

21 Thou hast neither part nor lot in this matter: for thy heart is not right in the sight of God.

22 Repent therefore of this thy wickedness, and pray God, if perhaps the thought of thine heart may be forgiven thee.

23 For I perceive that thou art in the gall of bitterness, and in the bond of iniquity.

24 Then answered Simon, and said, Pray ye to the LORD for me, that none of these things which ye have spoken come upon me.

25 And they, when they had testified and preached the word of the Lord, returned to Jerusalem, and preached the gospel in many villages of the Samaritans.

26 And the angel of the Lord spake unto Philip, saying, Arise, and go toward the south unto the way that goeth down from Jerusalem unto Gaza, which is desert.

27 And he arose and went: and, behold, a man of Ethiopia, an eunuch of great authority under Candace queen of the Ethiopians, who had the charge of all her treasure, and had come to Jerusalem for to worship,

28 Was returning, and sitting in his chariot read Esaias the prophet.

29 Then the Spirit said unto Philip, Go near, and join thyself to this chariot.

30 And Philip ran thither to him, and heard him read the prophet Esaias, and said, Understandest thou what thou readest?

31 And he said, How can I, except some man should guide me? And he desired Philip that he would come up and sit with him.

32 The place of the scripture which he read was this, He was led as a sheep to the slaughter; and like a lamb dumb before his shearer, so opened he not his mouth:

33 In his humiliation his judgment was taken away: and who shall declare his generation? for his life is taken from the earth.

34 And the eunuch answered Philip, and said, I pray thee, of whom speaketh the prophet this? of himself, or of some other man?

35 Then Philip opened his mouth, and began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus.

36 And as they went on their way, they came unto a certain water: and the eunuch said, See, here is water; what doth hinder me to be baptized?

37 And Philip said, If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest. And he answered and said, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God.

38 And he commanded the chariot to stand still: and they went down both into the water, both Philip and the eunuch; and he baptized him.

39 And when they were come up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught away Philip, that the eunuch saw him no more: and he went on his way rejoicing.

40 But Philip was found at Azotus: and passing through he preached in all the cities, till he came to Caesarea.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After Stephen's death a great persecution scatters the Jerusalem believers, and Philip preaches in Samaria, where Simon the sorcerer professes faith but is rebuked for trying to buy the gift of God. Philip is then sent to an Ethiopian official reading Isaiah on the road to Gaza, explains the passage as speaking of Jesus, and baptizes him before being carried away to Azotus. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Saul was consenting unto Stephen's death, and at that time there was a great persecution against the church at Jerusalem, scattering all but the apostles through Judea and Samaria. Henry's principle for the scattering is that persecution must not drive us from our work, yet it may send us to work elsewhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Therefore they that were scattered abroad went every where preaching the word. Henry says wherever the established believer is driven, he carries the knowledge of the gospel and makes known the preciousness of Christ in every place, and where a simple desire of doing good influences the heart, it is impossible to shut a man out from all opportunities of usefulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Then Philip went down to the city of Samaria and preached Christ unto them. Henry's opening point for Samaria is that as far as the gospel prevails, evil spirits are dislodged, particularly unclean spirits, and Philip's preaching of Christ is the prevailing power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The people with one accord gave heed unto those things which Philip spake, hearing and seeing the miracles which he did. Henry stresses the depth of their attention: the people not only gave heed to what Philip said, but were fully convinced that it was of God and not of men, and gave up themselves to be directed thereby. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. For unclean spirits, crying with loud voice, came out of many that were possessed with them, and many taken with palsies and that were lame were healed. Henry reads these cures as the gospel dislodging evil spirits, and notes the distempers named are the most difficult to be cured by nature and most expressive of the disease of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. There was great joy in that city. Henry's arc for Samaria runs from the gospel prevailing and evil spirits dislodged to this joy: where Christ's power displaces the old bondage, the city rejoices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. But there was a certain man called Simon, which beforetime in the same city used sorcery and bewitched the people of Samaria, giving out that himself was some great one. Henry quotes the people's verdict on him, This man is the great power of God, and adds, See how ignorant and thoughtless people mistake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. To whom they all gave heed, from the least to the greatest, saying, This man is the great power of God. Henry's warning is the same mistake from the other side: ignorant and thoughtless people mistook a sorcerer for the great power of God, until divine grace brought them to Christ, who is Truth itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And to him they had regard, because that of long time he had bewitched them with sorceries. Henry notes the long habit behind the regard: pride, ambition and desire after grandeur have always caused abundance of mischief, both to the world and to the church, and long bewitchment made the mistake feel natural. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. But when they believed Philip preaching the things concerning the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, they were baptized, both men and women. Henry contrasts Simon's hold with this: how strong is the power of divine grace, by which they were brought to Christ, who is Truth itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Then Simon himself believed also, and when he was baptized, he continued with Philip and wondered, beholding the miracles and signs which were done. Henry warns from Simon's case: even bad men, whose hearts still go after covetousness, may come before God as his people come and for a time continue with them, and many wonder at the proofs of divine truths who never experience their power, for the gospel may have a common operation on a soul where it never produced inward holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Now when the apostles at Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent unto them Peter and John. Henry draws encouragement from what follows for prayer: we may pray to God to give the renewing graces of the Holy Ghost to all for whose spiritual welfare we are concerned, for that includes all blessings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Who, when they were come down, prayed for them, that they might receive the Holy Ghost. Henry's encouragement is the same prayer: ask God for the renewing graces of the Holy Ghost for those whose spiritual welfare concerns us, since that gift includes all blessings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. For as yet he was fallen upon none of them: only they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. Henry marks the distinction: the Holy Ghost was as yet fallen upon none of these converts in the extraordinary powers conveyed by the descent of the Spirit on the day of Pentecost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Then laid they their hands on them, and they received the Holy Ghost. Henry adds the caution: no man can give the Holy Spirit by the laying on of his hands, but we should use our best endeavours to instruct those for whom we pray. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. When Simon saw that through laying on of the apostles' hands the Holy Ghost was given, he offered them money. Henry diagnoses Simon: he was ambitious to have the honour of an apostle, but cared not at all to have the spirit and disposition of a Christian, and was more desirous to gain honour to himself than to do good to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Saying, Give me also this power, that on whomsoever I lay hands, he may receive the Holy Ghost. Henry exposes the error: Simon esteemed the wealth of this world as if it would answer for things relating to the other life, and would purchase the pardon of sin, the gift of the Holy Ghost, and eternal life, a condemning error that could by no means consist with a state of grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. But Peter said unto him, Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money. Henry's rule for money temptation fits: when tempted with money to do evil, see what a perishing thing money is, and scorn it, and think not that Christianity is a trade to live by in this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Thou hast neither part nor lot in this matter, for thy heart is not right in the sight of God. Henry states the ground: our hearts are what they are in the sight of God, who cannot be deceived, and if they are not right in his sight, our religion is vain and will stand us in no stead, for a proud and covetous heart cannot be right with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Repent therefore of this thy wickedness, and pray God, if perhaps the thought of thine heart may be forgiven thee. Henry lists what must be repented: much wickedness in the thought of the heart, its false notions and corrupt affections and wicked projects, which must be repented of or we are undone, but it shall be forgiven upon our repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. For I perceive that thou art in the gall of bitterness, and in the bond of iniquity. Henry's diagnosis is that it is possible for a man to continue under the power of sin, yet to put on a form of godliness, and Simon's bitterness and bondage show that form without inward holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Then answered Simon, and said, Pray ye to the Lord for me, that none of these things which ye have spoken come upon me. Henry is careful about the doubt here: the doubt is of the sincerity of Simon's repentance, not of his pardon, if his repentance was sincere, and he prays for a faith unlike Simon's, which made him wonder only and did not sanctify his heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The angel of the Lord spake unto Philip, saying, Arise, and go toward the south unto the way that goeth down from Jerusalem unto Gaza, which is desert. Henry's lesson is that Philip was directed to go to a desert, and sometimes God opens a door of opportunity to his ministers in very unlikely places. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And he arose and went, and behold, a man of Ethiopia, an eunuch of great authority under Candace queen of the Ethiopians, who had the charge of all her treasure, and had come to Jerusalem for to worship. Henry's travelling rule fits the meeting: we should study to do good to those we come into company with by travelling, and not be so shy of all strangers, for as to those of whom we know nothing else, we know this, that they have souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Was returning, and sitting in his chariot read Esaias the prophet. Henry praises the redeemed time: it is wisdom for men of business to redeem time for holy duties, filling up every minute with something which will turn to a good account, and in reading the word we should often pause to inquire of whom and of what the sacred writers spake, especially with our thoughts employed about the Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Then the Spirit said unto Philip, Go near, and join thyself to this chariot. Henry's desert-road lesson continues: God opens a door of opportunity in unlikely places, and ministers should study to do good to those they come into company with by travelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Philip ran thither to him, and heard him read the prophet Esaias, and said, Understandest thou what thou readest? Henry's reading rule is embodied in the question: in reading the word of God we should often pause to inquire of whom and of what the sacred writers spake, but especially let our thoughts be employed about the Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And he said, How can I, except some man should guide me? And he desired Philip that he would come up and sit with him. Henry's encouragement for seekers fits: those who seek the truth and employ their time in searching the Scriptures will be sure to reap advantages, and the eunuch's honest need of a guide is the beginning of that advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The place of the scripture which he read was this, He was led as a sheep to the slaughter, and like a lamb dumb before his shearer, so opened he not his mouth. Henry says the Ethiopian was convinced by the teaching of the Holy Spirit of the exact fulfilment of the Scripture in this suffering servant passage, and was made to understand the nature of the Messiah's kingdom and salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. And the eunuch answered Philip, and said, I pray thee, of whom speaketh the prophet this? of himself, or of some other man? Henry's reading rule is the eunuch's own question: pause to inquire of whom and of what the sacred writers spake, and here the question opens the way to the Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Then Philip opened his mouth, and began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus. Henry says the Ethiopian was convinced by the teaching of the Holy Spirit of the exact fulfilment of the Scripture, was made to understand the nature of the Messiah's kingdom and salvation, and desired to be numbered among the disciples of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And as they went on their way, they came unto a certain water, and the eunuch said, See, here is water; what doth hinder me to be baptized? Henry reads the desire behind the question: having been convinced of the Scripture's fulfilment and of the Messiah's salvation, the eunuch desired to be numbered among the disciples of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. And Philip said, If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest. And he answered and said, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God. Henry insists this avowal must be understood as expressing simple reliance on Christ for salvation and unreserved devotion to him, and urges us not to be satisfied till we get faith, as the Ethiopian did, by diligent study of the Scriptures and the teaching of the Spirit, fixed as a principle in our hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. And he commanded the chariot to stand still, and they went down both into the water, both Philip and the eunuch, and he baptized him. Henry's sequence is avowal then baptism, and he adds the necessary warning: though baptized in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, with water, it is not enough without the baptism of the Holy Ghost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. And when they were come up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught away Philip, that the eunuch saw him no more, and he went on his way rejoicing. Henry says that as soon as he was baptized, the Spirit took Philip from him so that he saw him no more, but this tended to confirm his faith, and when the inquirer after salvation becomes acquainted with Jesus and his gospel, he will go on his way rejoicing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry says the Ethiopian was convinced by the teaching of the Holy Spirit of the exact fulfilment of the Scripture, was made to understand the nature of the Messiah's kingdom and salvation, and desired to be numbered among the disciples of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And as they went on their way, they came unto a certain water, and the eunuch said, See, here is water; what doth hinder me to be baptized? Henry reads the desire behind the question: having been convinced of the Scripture's fulfilment and of the Messiah's salvation, the eunuch desired to be numbered among the disciples of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. And Philip said, If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest. And he answered and said, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God. Henry insists this avowal must be understood as expressing simple reliance on Christ for salvation and unreserved devotion to him, and urges us not to be satisfied till we get faith, as the Ethiopian did, by diligent study of the Scriptures and the teaching of the Spirit, fixed as a principle in our hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. And he commanded the chariot to stand still, and they went down both into the water, both Philip and the eunuch, and he baptized him. Henry's sequence is avowal then baptism, and he adds the necessary warning: though baptized in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, with water, it is not enough without the baptism of the Holy Ghost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. And when they were come up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught away Philip, that the eunuch saw him no more, and he went on his way rejoicing. Henry says that as soon as he was baptized, the Spirit took Philip from him so that he saw him no more, but this tended to confirm his faith, and when the inquirer after salvation becomes acquainted with Jesus and his gospel, he will go on his way rejoicing.
