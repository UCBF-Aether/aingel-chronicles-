The Text — Jubilees 37 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And on the day that Isaac the father of Jacob and Esau died, the sons of Esau heard that Isaac had given the portion of the elder to his younger son Jacob and they were very angry.

2 And they strove with their father, saying : " Why hath thy father given Jacob the portion of the elder and passed over thee, although thou art the elder and Jacob the younger? ' 3.

3 And he said unto them " Because I sold my birthright to Jacob for a small mess of lentils ; and on the day my father sent me to hunt and catch and bring him something that he should eat and bless me, he came with guile and brought my father food and drink, and my father blessed him and put me under his hand.

4 And now our father hath caused us to swear, me and him, that we shall not mutually devise evil, either against his brother, and that we

5 shall continue in love and in peace each with his brother and not make our ways corrupt." 5.

6 And they said unto him, " We shall not hearken unto thee to make peace with him ; for our strength is greater than his strength, and we are more powerful than he ; we shall go against him and slay him, and destroy him and his sons.

7 And if thou wilt not go with us, we shall do hurt to thee also.

8 And now hearken unto us : Let us send to Aram and Philistia and Moab

9 and Ammon and let us choose for ourselves chosen men who are ardent for battle, and let us go against him and do battle with him, and let us exterminate him from the earth before he groweth strong." 7.

10 And their father said unto them, " Do not go and do not make war with him lest ye fall before him." 8.

11 And they said unto him, " This too, is exactly thy mode of action from thy youth until this day, and thou art putting thy neck under his yoke.

12 We shall not hearken to these words." 9.

13 And they sent to Aram, and to 'Aduram to the friend of their father, and they hired along with them one thousand fighting men, chosen men of war.

14 And there came to them from Moab and from the children of Ammon, those who were hired, one thousand chosen men, and from Philistia, one thousand chosen men of war, and from Edom and from the Horites one thousand chosen fighting men, and from the Kittim mighty men of war.

15 And they said unto their father : " Go forth with them and lead them, else we shall slay thee." 12.

16 And he was filled with wrath and indignation on seeing that his sons were forcing him to go before (them) to lead them against Jacob his brother.

17 But afterward he remembered all the evil which lay hidden in his heart against Jacob his brother ; and he remembered not the oath which he had sworn to his father and to his mother that he would devise no evil all his days against Jacob his brother.

18 And notwithstanding all this, Jacob knew not that they were coming against him to battle, and he was mourning for Leah, his wife, until they approached very near to the tower with four thousand warriors and chosen men of war.

19 And the men of Hebron sent to him saying, " Behold thy brother hath come against thee, to fight thee, with four thousand girt with the sword, and they carry shields and weapons " ; for they loved Jacob more than Esau.

20 So they told him ; for Jacob was a more liberal and merciful man

21 than Esau.

22 But Jacob would not believe until they came very near to the tower.

23 And he closed the gates of the tower ; and he stood on the battlements and spake to his brother Esau and said, " Noble is the comfort wherewith thou hast come to comfort me for my wife who hath died.

24 Is this the oath that thou didst swear to thy father and again to thy mother before they died?

25 Thou hast broken the oath, and on the moment that thou didst swear to thy father wast thou condemned." 18.

26 And then Esau answered and said unto him, " Neither the children of men nor the beasts of the earth have any oath of righteousness which in swearing they have sworn (an oath valid) for ever ; but every day they devise evil one against another, and how each may slay his adversary and foe.

27 And thou dost hate me and my children for ever.

28 And there is no observing the tie of brotherhood with thee.

29 Hear these words which I declare unto thee,

30 If the boar can change its skin and make its bristles as soft as wool,

31 Or if it can cause horns to sprout forth on its head like the horns of a stag or of a sheep,

32 Then shall I observe the tie of brotherhood with thee.

33 [And if the breasts separated themselves from their mother; for thou hast not been a brother to me.]

34 And if the wolves make peace with the lambs so

35 as not to devour or do them violence, And if their hearts are towards them for good, Then there will be peace in my heart towards

36 thee.

37 And if the lion becometh the friend of the ox and

38 maketh peace with him,

39 And if he is bound under one yoke with him and

40 plougheth with him, Then shall I make peace with thee.

41 And when the raven becometh white as the raza Then know that I have loved thee

42 And shall make peace with thee.

43 Thou shalt be rooted out, And thy sons shall be rooted out, And there shall be no peace for thee."

44 And when Jacob saw that he was (so) evilly disposed towards him with his heart, and with all his soul as to slay him, and that he had come springing like the wild boar which cometh upon the spear that pierceth and killeth it, and recoileth not from it ; Then he spake to his own and to his servants that they should attack him and all his companions.

45 The War between Jacob and Esau at the Tower of Hebron.

46 The Death of Esau and Overthrow of his Forces.

How to read the labels

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: On the day Isaac died, the sons of Esau heard that Isaac had given the portion of the elder to Jacob, and they were very angry. Jacob said Esau had broken the oath, and that from the moment he swore to his father he was condemned. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. On the day Isaac died, the sons of Esau heard that Isaac had given the portion of the elder to Jacob, and they were very angry. The anger of the next generation reopens the grievance the fathers had settled by oath, and Henry's warning applies: men may fret at God's counsels, but cannot change them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Esau's sons strove with their father, asking why Isaac had given Jacob the elder's portion and passed over Esau, though Esau was elder and Jacob younger. The question repeats Esau's own old complaint, now carried by his sons against the father's settlement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Esau answered that he had sold his birthright to Jacob for a small mess of lentils, and that on the day Isaac sent him hunting, Jacob came with guile, brought food and drink, and Isaac blessed him and put him under his hand. Henry's account agrees on both facts: Esau sold his title for what was of no value, and Jacob gained the blessing by deceit that God nevertheless overruled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Esau added that their father Isaac had caused them to swear, he and Jacob, that they would not mutually devise evil against his brother. The oath of the previous chapter is pleaded here as the standing peace between the houses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The oath bound the brothers to continue in love and peace with each other and not to make their ways corrupt. The terms are those of Isaac's charge: affection, righteousness, and prosperity together, or destruction for the breaker. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Esau was filled with wrath and indignation at seeing his sons force him to lead them against Jacob his brother. The father's anger shows he knew the war was wrong, even as he was dragged into it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Afterwards Esau remembered all the evil hidden in his heart against Jacob, and forgot the oath sworn to his father and mother that he would devise no evil against Jacob all his days. Henry's portrait of Esau fits: he bore malice on account of the blessing, in the way of Cain, and old malice, once remembered, drowned the oath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Jacob stood on the battlements and spoke to Esau, saying noble was the comfort with which he had come to comfort him for his dead wife. The bitter irony names the truth: an army is no comfort for a mourner, and the visit's pretext is exposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Jacob asked whether this was the oath Esau had sworn to his father and again to his mother before they died. The double oath, to father and to mother, is the measure of the breach: both parents' settlements are broken at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Jacob said Esau had broken the oath, and that from the moment he swore to his father he was condemned. The condemnation follows Isaac's own sanction: the brother who devises evil falls into his brother's hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
