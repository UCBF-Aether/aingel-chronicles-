The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Lamentations 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 How is the gold become dim! how is the most fine gold changed! the stones of the sanctuary are poured out in the top of every street.

2 The precious sons of Zion, comparable to fine gold, how are they esteemed as earthen pitchers, the work of the hands of the potter!

3 Even the sea monsters draw out the breast, they give suck to their young ones: the daughter of my people is become cruel, like the ostriches in the wilderness.

4 The tongue of the sucking child cleaveth to the roof of his mouth for thirst: the young children ask bread, and no man breaketh it unto them.

5 They that did feed delicately are desolate in the streets: they that were brought up in scarlet embrace dunghills.

6 For the punishment of the iniquity of the daughter of my people is greater than the punishment of the sin of Sodom, that was overthrown as in a moment, and no hands stayed on her.

7 Her Nazarites were purer than snow, they were whiter than milk, they were more ruddy in body than rubies, their polishing was of sapphire:

8 Their visage is blacker than a coal; they are not known in the streets: their skin cleaveth to their bones; it is withered, it is become like a stick.

9 They that be slain with the sword are better than they that be slain with hunger: for these pine away, stricken through for want of the fruits of the field.

10 The hands of the pitiful women have sodden their own children: they were their meat in the destruction of the daughter of my people.

11 The LORD hath accomplished his fury; he hath poured out his fierce anger, and hath kindled a fire in Zion, and it hath devoured the foundations thereof.

12 The kings of the earth, and all the inhabitants of the world, would not have believed that the adversary and the enemy should have entered into the gates of Jerusalem.

13 For the sins of her prophets, and the iniquities of her priests, that have shed the blood of the just in the midst of her,

14 They have wandered as blind men in the streets, they have polluted themselves with blood, so that men could not touch their garments.

15 They cried unto them, Depart ye; it is unclean; depart, depart, touch not: when they fled away and wandered, they said among the heathen, They shall no more sojourn there.

16 The anger of the LORD hath divided them; he will no more regard them: they respected not the persons of the priests, they favoured not the elders.

17 As for us, our eyes as yet failed for our vain help: in our watching we have watched for a nation that could not save us.

18 They hunt our steps, that we cannot go in our streets: our end is near, our days are fulfilled; for our end is come.

19 Our persecutors are swifter than the eagles of the heaven: they pursued us upon the mountains, they laid wait for us in the wilderness.

20 The breath of our nostrils, the anointed of the LORD, was taken in their pits, of whom we said, Under his shadow we shall live among the heathen.

21 Rejoice and be glad, O daughter of Edom, that dwellest in the land of Uz; the cup also shall pass through unto thee: thou shalt be drunken, and shalt make thyself naked.

22 The punishment of thine iniquity is accomplished, O daughter of Zion; he will no more carry thee away into captivity: he will visit thine iniquity, O daughter of Edom; he will discover thy sins.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

How is the gold become dim, and the most fine gold changed — the precious sons of Zion, once comparable to fine gold, are now esteemed as earthen pitchers. The chapter dwells on siege hunger, worse than the sword, on mothers, princes and prophets, and on the fall of a city no king of the earth believed could be entered. It ends with Zion's punishment accomplished and Edom warned that her cup will come to her too. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

  • Verse 6. For the punishment of the daughter of God's people's iniquity is greater than Sodom's sin, which was overthrown in a moment without hands staying on her, Jerusalem's prolonged ruin being the heavier judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Her Nazarites were purer than snow, whiter than milk, more ruddy than rubies, their polishing of sapphire, consecrated beauty once conspicuous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Now their visage is blacker than coal, unknown in the streets, skin cleaving to bones, withered like wood, the change from splendor to starvation complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Those slain by the sword are better than those slain by hunger, for the hungry pine away, stricken through for want of the fruits of the field, a slow death worse than a swift one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The hands of pitiful women have sodden their own children, who were their meat in the destruction of the daughter of God's people, the horrors of the siege reaching their most unnatural depth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The Lord has accomplished his fury, poured out fierce anger, and kindled a fire in Zion that devoured its foundations, judgment carried through to its determined end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The kings of the earth and all the world's inhabitants would not have believed adversary and enemy could enter Jerusalem's gates, the city's fall surprising even outsiders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. It came for the sins of her prophets and the iniquities of her priests, who shed the blood of the just in her midst, nothing ripening a people for ruin faster than the sins of priests and prophets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. They have wandered as blind men in the streets, polluted with blood so men could not touch their garments, guilt making them untouchable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Men cried to them, depart, it is unclean, depart, depart, touch not, and when they fled and wandered, nations said they shall no more sojourn there, the defiled leaders driven out as lepers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The anger of the Lord has divided them, and he will no more regard them, since they respected not priests and favored not elders, divine vengeance pursuing even the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. As for the people, their eyes failed for vain help, watching for a nation that could not save them, trust in Egypt or any ally proving empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Their steps were hunted so they could not go in the streets, their end near, their days fulfilled, for their end had come, no street safe from pursuers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Their persecutors were swifter than eagles of heaven, pursuing on mountains and laying wait in the wilderness, escape cut off on every side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The breath of their nostrils, the Lord's anointed, was taken in their pits, he of whom they said, under his shadow we shall live among the heathen, yet only the true anointed King is the life of souls, under whose shadow believers may safely live. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Rejoice and be glad, daughter of Edom dwelling in Uz, for the cup shall also pass to thee, thou shalt be drunken and make thyself naked, Edom's triumph being temporary and her turn coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The punishment of Zion's iniquity is accomplished, and she shall no more be carried into captivity, while God will visit Edom's iniquity and discover her sins, an end put to Zion's troubles, not the full punishment deserved but what God determined, and Edom standing for all the church's enemies whose doom approaches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
