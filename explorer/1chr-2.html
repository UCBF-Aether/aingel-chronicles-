The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 These are the sons of Israel; Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun,

2 Dan, Joseph, and Benjamin, Naphtali, Gad, and Asher.

3 The sons of Judah; Er, and Onan, and Shelah: which three were born unto him of the daughter of Shua the Canaanitess. And Er, the firstborn of Judah, was evil in the sight of the LORD; and he slew him.

4 And Tamar his daughter in law bore him Pharez and Zerah. All the sons of Judah were five.

5 The sons of Pharez; Hezron, and Hamul.

6 And the sons of Zerah; Zimri, and Ethan, and Heman, and Calcol, and Dara: five of them in all.

7 And the sons of Carmi; Achar, the troubler of Israel, who transgressed in the thing accursed.

8 And the sons of Ethan; Azariah.

9 The sons also of Hezron, that were born unto him; Jerahmeel, and Ram, and Chelubai.

10 And Ram begat Amminadab; and Amminadab begat Nahshon, prince of the children of Judah;

11 And Nahshon begat Salma, and Salma begat Boaz,

12 And Boaz begat Obed, and Obed begat Jesse,

13 And Jesse begat his firstborn Eliab, and Abinadab the second, and Shimma the third,

14 Nethaneel the fourth, Raddai the fifth,

15 Ozem the sixth, David the seventh:

16 Whose sisters were Zeruiah, and Abigail. And the sons of Zeruiah; Abishai, and Joab, and Asahel, three.

17 And Abigail bare Amasa: and the father of Amasa was Jether the Ishmeelite.

18 And Caleb the son of Hezron begat children of Azubah his wife, and of Jerioth: her sons are these; Jesher, and Shobab, and Ardon.

19 And when Azubah was dead, Caleb took unto him Ephrath, which bare him Hur.

20 And Hur begat Uri, and Uri begat Bezaleel.

21 And afterward Hezron went in to the daughter of Machir the father of Gilead, whom he married when he was threescore years old; and she bare him Segub.

22 And Segub begat Jair, who had three and twenty cities in the land of Gilead.

23 And he took Geshur, and Aram, with the towns of Jair, from them, with Kenath, and the towns thereof, even threescore cities. All these belonged to the sons of Machir the father of Gilead.

24 And after that Hezron was dead in Calebephratah, then Abiah Hezron’s wife bare him Ashur the father of Tekoa.

25 And the sons of Jerahmeel the firstborn of Hezron were, Ram the firstborn, and Bunah, and Oren, and Ozem, and Ahijah.

26 Jerahmeel had also another wife, whose name was Atarah; she was the mother of Onam.

27 And the sons of Ram the firstborn of Jerahmeel were, Maaz, and Jamin, and Eker.

28 And the sons of Onam were, Shammai, and Jada. And the sons of Shammai; Nadab and Abishur.

29 And the name of the wife of Abishur was Abihail, and she bare him Ahban, and Molid.

30 And the sons of Nadab; Seled, and Appaim: but Seled died without children.

31 And the sons of Appaim; Ishi. And the sons of Ishi; Sheshan. And the children of Sheshan; Ahlai.

32 And the sons of Jada the brother of Shammai; Jether, and Jonathan: and Jether died without children.

33 And the sons of Jonathan; Peleth, and Zaza. These were the sons of Jerahmeel.

34 Now Sheshan had no sons, but daughters. And Sheshan had a servant, an Egyptian, whose name was Jarha.

35 And Sheshan gave his daughter to Jarha his servant to wife; and she bare him Attai.

36 And Attai begat Nathan, and Nathan begat Zabad,

37 And Zabad begat Ephlal, and Ephlal begat Obed,

38 And Obed begat Jehu, and Jehu begat Azariah,

39 And Azariah begat Helez, and Helez begat Eleasah,

40 And Eleasah begat Sisamai, and Sisamai begat Shallum,

41 And Shallum begat Jekamiah, and Jekamiah begat Elishama.

42 Now the sons of Caleb the brother of Jerahmeel were, Mesha his firstborn, which was the father of Ziph; and the sons of Mareshah the father of Hebron.

43 And the sons of Hebron; Korah, and Tappuah, and Rekem, and Shema.

44 And Shema begat Raham, the father of Jorkoam: and Rekem begat Shammai.

45 And the son of Shammai was Maon: and Maon was the father of Bethzur.

46 And Ephah, Caleb’s concubine, bare Haran, and Moza, and Gazez: and Haran begat Gazez.

47 And the sons of Jahdai; Regem, and Jotham, and Gesham, and Pelet, and Ephah, and Shaaph.

48 Maachah, Caleb’s concubine, bare Sheber, and Tirhanah.

49 She bare also Shaaph the father of Madmannah, Sheva the father of Machbenah, and the father of Gibea: and the daughter of Caleb was Achsa.

50 These were the sons of Caleb the son of Hur, the firstborn of Ephratah; Shobal the father of Kirjathjearim.

51 Salma the father of Bethlehem, Hareph the father of Bethgader.

52 And Shobal the father of Kirjathjearim had sons; Haroeh, and half of the Manahethites.

53 And the families of Kirjathjearim; the Ithrites, and the Puhites, and the Shumathites, and the Mishraites; of them came the Zareathites, and the Eshtaulites,

54 The sons of Salma; Bethlehem, and the Netophathites, Ataroth, the house of Joab, and half of the Manahethites, the Zorites.

55 And the families of the scribes which dwelt at Jabez; the Tirathites, the Shimeathites, and Suchathites. These are the Kenites that came of Hemath, the father of the house of Rechab.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter lists the sons of Israel, then follows Judah's line in detail — through Perez to David's family, and through Caleb and Jerahmeel — ending with the scribes who lived at Jabez. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These are the sons of Israel, and Henry notes the twelve are named here as the illustrious number celebrated throughout the Bible, the twelve tribes descending from twelve patriarchs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Dan, Joseph, Benjamin, Naphtali, Gad, and Asher complete the twelve. Henry stresses the covenant was entailed on their seed by free grace, not works, though several of the patriarchs were personally blemished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Er, Judah's firstborn, was evil in the sight of the Lord, and the Lord slew him. Henry holds him up as proof that even the best families may have members who are blemishes, cut off in the beginning of their days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Tamar, Judah's daughter-in-law, bore him Pharez and Zerah, making five sons of Judah in all. Henry names this incest plainly among the dishonourable branches of an otherwise illustrious tree. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The sons of Zerah include Ethan, Heman, Calcol, and Dara, whom Henry calls very wise and good, the glory of their father's house, against whose wisdom Solomon's wisdom was later measured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Achar, of Carmi, is called the troubler of Israel, who transgressed in the accursed thing. Henry cites him as another blemish in Judah's line, troubling Israel by taking what was devoted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Ram begat Amminadab, and Amminadab begat Nahshon, prince of the children of Judah. Henry notes Nahshon led the van when the camp was formed in the wilderness, a post of honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Nahshon begat Salma, and Salma begat Boaz. Henry notes Salma, or Salmon, held the same post of honour when Israel entered Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Jesse begat Eliab his firstborn, Abinadab second, and Shimma third. Henry keeps Jesse's family particularly for David, the seventh son, and the Son of David who is a rod out of Jesse's stem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. David is the seventh son of Jesse, the rod out of the stem of Jesse. Henry keeps this account particularly for the sake of David, and of the Son of David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. David's sisters were Zeruiah and Abigail, whose sons were his great commanders: Abishai, Joab, and Asahel from Zeruiah. Henry notes three of the four commanders went down slain, though they had been the terror of the mighty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Abigail bore Amasa, whose father was Jether the Ishmaelite. Henry counts Amasa among David's commanders from his sisters' families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Caleb son of Hezron begat children of Azubah his wife and of Jerioth. Henry notes few persons in this Caleb paragraph are mentioned elsewhere, yet Judah's exact genealogies show special providence for clearing the genealogy of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Hur begat Uri, and Uri begat Bezaleel, the head-workman in building the tabernacle. Henry singles Bezaleel out from a paragraph whose persons are otherwise little mentioned elsewhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Hezron married Machir's daughter when he was sixty years old, and she bore Segub. Henry notes Hezron's great desire for posterity in the family of Pharez, from whom the Messiah was to descend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Segub begat Jair, who had twenty-three cities in Gilead. Henry notes Jair's achievements were later than the conquest, and that Hezron's family increased in Egypt as other oppressed families did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. After Hezron died in Caleb-ephratah, his wife Abiah bore him Ashur, father of Tekoa. Henry notes the puzzle that Hezron, who went down to Egypt, is said to have died in Canaan, suggesting some family members visited Canaan before the bondage grew extreme. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Seled, son of Nadab, died without children. Henry groups him with others in this pedigree who died childless, urging sufferers not to think their case new or singular, since providence orders families by its own sovereignty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Jether, son of Jada, died without children. Henry again notes childlessness in this line as a matter of providence, not of singularity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Sheshan had no sons but daughters, and had an Egyptian servant named Jarha. Henry raises the question of Sheshan's only daughter's marriage to his servant in the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Sheshan gave his daughter to Jarha his servant to wife, and she bore Attai. Henry argues that if this is praised, the Egyptian must have become a proselyte to the Jewish religion and eminent for wisdom and virtue, since neither parentage nor servitude should bar preferment for a convert who does worthily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. These were the sons of Caleb son of Hur, including Shobal, father of Kirjath-jearim. Henry notes several pedigrees here terminate in a place, because those places fell to their lot in the division of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. Salma was father of Bethlehem, afterwards David's city, and Hareph father of Beth-gader. Henry reads father of a place as lord or founder of it by lot in the division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. The families of scribes dwelt at Jabez, the Tirathites, Shimeathites, and Suchathites, Kenites of Hemath's house. Henry rejoices to find families of scribes among these great families, keeping up scripture-learning and teaching the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
