The Text — Genesis 45 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Joseph could not refrain himself before all them that stood by him; and he cried, Cause every man to go out from me. And there stood no man with him, while Joseph made himself known unto his brethren.

2 And he wept aloud: and the Egyptians and the house of Pharaoh heard.

3 And Joseph said unto his brethren, I am Joseph; doth my father yet live? And his brethren could not answer him; for they were troubled at his presence.

4 And Joseph said unto his brethren, Come near to me, I pray you. And they came near. And he said, I am Joseph your brother, whom ye sold into Egypt.

5 Now therefore be not grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that ye sold me hither: for God did send me before you to preserve life.

6 For these two years hath the famine been in the land: and yet there are five years, in the which there shall neither be earing nor harvest.

7 And God sent me before you to preserve you a posterity in the earth, and to save your lives by a great deliverance.

8 So now it was not you that sent me hither, but God: and he hath made me a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house, and a ruler throughout all the land of Egypt.

9 Haste ye, and go up to my father, and say unto him, Thus saith thy son Joseph, God hath made me lord of all Egypt: come down unto me, tarry not:

10 And thou shalt dwell in the land of Goshen, and thou shalt be near unto me, thou, and thy children, and thy children’s children, and thy flocks, and thy herds, and all that thou hast:

11 And there will I nourish thee; for yet there are five years of famine; lest thou, and thy household, and all that thou hast, come to poverty.

12 And, behold, your eyes see, and the eyes of my brother Benjamin, that it is my mouth that speaketh unto you.

13 And ye shall tell my father of all my glory in Egypt, and of all that ye have seen; and ye shall haste and bring down my father hither.

14 And he fell upon his brother Benjamin’s neck, and wept; and Benjamin wept upon his neck.

15 Moreover he kissed all his brethren, and wept upon them: and after that his brethren talked with him.

16 And the fame thereof was heard in Pharaoh’s house, saying, Joseph’s brethren are come: and it pleased Pharaoh well, and his servants.

17 And Pharaoh said unto Joseph, Say unto thy brethren, This do ye; lade your beasts, and go, get you unto the land of Canaan;

18 And take your father and your households, and come unto me: and I will give you the good of the land of Egypt, and ye shall eat the fat of the land.

19 Now thou art commanded, this do ye; take you wagons out of the land of Egypt for your little ones, and for your wives, and bring your father, and come.

20 Also regard not your stuff; for the good of all the land of Egypt is yours.

21 And the children of Israel did so: and Joseph gave them wagons, according to the commandment of Pharaoh, and gave them provision for the way.

22 To all of them he gave each man changes of raiment; but to Benjamin he gave three hundred pieces of silver, and five changes of raiment.

23 And to his father he sent after this manner; ten asses laden with the good things of Egypt, and ten she asses laden with corn and bread and meat for his father by the way.

24 So he sent his brethren away, and they departed: and he said unto them, See that ye fall not out by the way.

25 And they went up out of Egypt, and came into the land of Canaan unto Jacob their father,

26 And told him, saying, Joseph is yet alive, and he is governor over all the land of Egypt. And Jacob’s heart fainted, for he believed them not.

27 And they told him all the words of Joseph, which he had said unto them: and when he saw the wagons which Joseph had sent to carry him, the spirit of Jacob their father revived:

28 And Israel said, It is enough; Joseph my son is yet alive: I will go and see him before I die.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Joseph can no longer restrain himself and reveals himself to his brothers, weeping aloud. He forgives them, reads God's hand in their selling him, and sends them to bring Jacob and the whole household to Egypt, where Pharaoh himself offers them the best of the land. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Joseph can no longer restrain himself before all who stand by, and he sends every man out, so his brothers' past will not become known to his household or to Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He weeps aloud, in the vehement Eastern manner of expressing overwhelming feeling, so loudly that Pharaoh's house hears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. His announcement, I am Joseph, leaves his brothers troubled and unable to answer, astonished, confounded, and shrinking from the brother they had wronged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He bids them come near and repeats that he is Joseph their brother whom they sold, speaking not to wound them but to convince them of his identity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He tells them not to be grieved or angry with themselves, since God sent him before them to preserve life, tracing an overruling providence without excusing their crime. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Two years of famine have passed and five remain with neither ploughing nor harvest, a drought that stopped the Nile's overflow and left the land unfit for seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God sent Joseph ahead to preserve a posterity and save lives by a great deliverance, so his exile is reframed as a rescue mission for the whole family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. It was not they who sent him, Joseph insists, but God, who made him a father to Pharaoh, that is, vizier, provider, and benefactor, as well as lord of Pharaoh's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They are to hurry to Jacob with the news that God has made Joseph lord of all Egypt, and to bring their father down without delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Jacob is to dwell in Goshen, near Joseph, with his sons, flocks, and all he has, so the family will be kept together under Joseph's care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Joseph promises to nourish his father there through the five remaining famine years, lest he and his household come to poverty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Joseph points out that their own eyes and Benjamin's eyes see that it is his mouth speaking, so the message rests on sight and voice, not report alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They are to tell Jacob of all Joseph's glory in Egypt and all they have seen, and to hasten their father down, making the brothers themselves witnesses to Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Joseph falls on Benjamin's neck and weeps, and Benjamin weeps on his, a sudden change from condemned criminal to fondled brother that tears alone can relieve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He kisses all his brothers and weeps over them, and afterwards they talk with him, since forgiveness shown by embrace does more than words alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The news reaches Pharaoh's house that Joseph's brothers have come, and it pleases Pharaoh and his servants, who welcome the family of their benefactor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Pharaoh himself invites the family to migrate, telling the brothers to load their beasts and go to Canaan, a liberality that honours Joseph's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Pharaoh offers them the good of Egypt, that they may eat its fat, so their settlement is to be generous, not merely tolerated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Wagons are commanded for their little ones and wives and for bringing their father, a novelty in Palestine, where wheeled carriages were almost unknown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. They are told not to regard their household stuff, since the good of all Egypt is theirs, so nothing need hold them back from the move. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The sons of Israel do as commanded, and Joseph gives them wagons by Pharaoh's order and provision for the way, so the journey home is fully supplied. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Each man receives changes of raiment, a customary gift of honour, while Benjamin receives five changes and three hundred pieces of silver, a fond partiality Joseph now feels safe to show. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. To his father Joseph sends ten asses laden with Egypt's good things and ten she-asses with corn, bread, and meat for the way, a large and liberal token of filial love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. As he sends them away he warns them not to fall out by the way, a caution greatly needed, since the journey would revive their part in selling Joseph and soon bring it to Jacob's knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. They go up out of Egypt to Canaan, up because the Hebrews counted northward travel as going up, and the return journey is now a homecoming with news. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. They tell Jacob that Joseph is yet alive and is governor over all Egypt, and Jacob's heart faints, because he cannot believe them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. They repeat all Joseph's words, and when Jacob sees the wagons Joseph sent to carry him, his spirit revives, since the wagons are visible proof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Jacob answers that it is enough, Joseph is alive, and he will go and see him before he dies, so the father's long grief turns to a single purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
