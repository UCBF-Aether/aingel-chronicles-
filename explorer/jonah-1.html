The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jonah 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the word of the LORD came unto Jonah the son of Amittai, saying,

2 Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry against it; for their wickedness is come up before me.

3 But Jonah rose up to flee unto Tarshish from the presence of the LORD, and went down to Joppa; and he found a ship going to Tarshish: so he paid the fare thereof, and went down into it, to go with them unto Tarshish from the presence of the LORD.

4 But the LORD sent out a great wind into the sea, and there was a mighty tempest in the sea, so that the ship was like to be broken.

5 Then the mariners were afraid, and cried every man unto his god, and cast forth the wares that were in the ship into the sea, to lighten it of them. But Jonah was gone down into the sides of the ship; and he lay, and was fast asleep.

6 So the shipmaster came to him, and said unto him, What meanest thou, O sleeper? arise, call upon thy God, if so be that God will think upon us, that we perish not.

7 And they said every one to his fellow, Come, and let us cast lots, that we may know for whose cause this evil is upon us. So they cast lots, and the lot fell upon Jonah.

8 Then said they unto him, Tell us, we pray thee, for whose cause this evil is upon us; What is thine occupation? and whence comest thou? what is thy country? and of what people art thou?

9 And he said unto them, I am an Hebrew; and I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, which hath made the sea and the dry land.

10 Then were the men exceedingly afraid, and said unto him. Why hast thou done this? For the men knew that he fled from the presence of the LORD, because he had told them.

11 Then said they unto him, What shall we do unto thee, that the sea may be calm unto us? for the sea wrought, and was tempestuous.

12 And he said unto them, Take me up, and cast me forth into the sea; so shall the sea be calm unto you: for I know that for my sake this great tempest is upon you.

13 Nevertheless the men rowed hard to bring it to the land; but they could not: for the sea wrought, and was tempestuous against them.

14 Wherefore they cried unto the LORD, and said, We beseech thee, O LORD, we beseech thee, let us not perish for this man’s life, and lay not upon us innocent blood: for thou, O LORD, hast done as it pleased thee.

15 So they took up Jonah, and cast him forth into the sea: and the sea ceased from her raging.

16 Then the men feared the LORD exceedingly, and offered a sacrifice unto the LORD, and made vows.

17 Now the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The word of the LORD comes to Jonah son of Amittai: arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry against it. Instead Jonah rises to flee to Tarshish, pays his fare at Joppa, and goes down into the ship, until a great wind raises a tempest that threatens to break it. The sailors cast lots, the lot falls on Jonah, and though they row hard to bring the ship to land, they at last cast him into the sea, the sea ceases from raging, the men fear the LORD and offer sacrifice, and a great fish prepared by the LORD swallows Jonah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The word of the Lord came to Jonah son of Amittai, an honour in itself: God gave his prophet a commission. Henry notes Jonah means a dove, a fitting name for God's people, who should be harmless as doves, and Amittai means my truth, for God's prophets should be sons of truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jonah must arise and go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry against it, because its wickedness had come up before God. Henry stresses Nineveh's greatness in people, wealth and power, yet great cities as well as great men stand under God's government and judgment, and its sin was a bold, open affront. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Instead of rising to go to Nineveh, Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish, away from the presence of the Lord and the spirit of prophecy that pressed him to the work. Henry warns that providence may seem to favour a wrong course, a ship ready to sail, but the ready way is not always the right way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. God sent out a great wind and a mighty tempest, so that the ship was likely to be broken. Henry calls the storm a pursuer sent after Jonah to fetch him back to God and his duty, and counts it mercy to be reclaimed when we go astray, even by a tempest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The mariners, afraid, cried every man to his god and cast the wares overboard to lighten the ship, valuing life above wealth. Henry contrasts their alarm with Jonah, the one concerned, lying fast asleep below, unawakened by the noise without or the guilt within. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The shipmaster roused Jonah with a just chiding, What meanest thou, O sleeper, urging him to arise and call upon his God, if so be God would think upon them and they perish not. Henry calls this seasonable reproof the first step toward Jonah's recovery, and notes that those who hope to share a common mercy should contribute their prayers toward it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Concluding the storm was divine justice arresting some enormous crime aboard, the mariners cast lots to know for whose cause the evil was upon them. Henry notes they appealed to God's disposal of the lot, and that God has many ways of bringing concealed sins and deserting servants to light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Questioned calmly rather than attacked, Jonah was asked for whose cause the evil had come, what his occupation was, and of what people he was. Henry notes their compassion toward a discovered offender, and that finding the cause of trouble requires descending to particulars in diligent search. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Jonah answered that he was a Hebrew who feared the Lord, the God of heaven who made the sea and the dry land. Henry notes he owned his religion as his calling, condemning his own folly in fleeing from such a God, and pointing the mariners from their many gods to the one living and true God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The men were exceedingly afraid when they learned Jonah had fled from the presence of the Lord. Henry notes their fear doubled: the tempest came from an offended God, and if a prophet was so pursued for one offence, they feared what would become of themselves for many. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. With the sea still growing more tempestuous, the mariners asked Jonah himself what they should do to him that the sea might be calm. Henry notes they treated him tenderly as a penitent, and that discovering sin as the cause of trouble is not enough: the sin itself must be confessed, detested and forsaken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Jonah read his own doom: take me up and cast me into the sea, and the sea would be calm. Henry reads this as a true penitent's language, desiring that none but himself should suffer for his sin, and submitting even to a sentence of death, as a type of Christ giving himself that the storm might cease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The mariners rowed hard to bring the ship to land and spare Jonah, but they could not, for the sea wrought against them. Henry notes they rowed against the wind of God's vengeance and the tide of his counsels, and that it is vain to seek safety any way other than by destroying the sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Before casting Jonah over, the mariners cried to the Lord, no longer to their many gods, begging that innocent blood not be laid on them. Henry notes their dread of blood-guiltiness, especially a prophet's blood, and their plea that God himself had done as it pleased him in wind and lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. They took up Jonah and cast him into the sea, and the sea ceased from its raging. Henry's application is that the sin that raises the storm must itself be cast forth and drowned, and then God, by pardoning mercy, casts our iniquities into the depths of the sea as well. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The men then feared the Lord exceedingly, and offered sacrifice and made vows to him. Henry reads this as confirmation in the true God: his power in raising and laying the tempest, his justice on his own servant, and his goodness in saving them moved them to worship him only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The Lord had prepared a great fish to swallow Jonah, and he was three days and three nights in its belly. Henry stresses the fish swallowed him not to devour but to protect him, a monument of mercy, a preparation for preaching to Nineveh, and a type of Christ three days in the heart of the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Tarshish. the distant port Jonah sails for from Joppa, in the opposite direction from Nineveh, fleeing from the presence of the LORD. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Nineveh. the great Assyrian city, three days' journey across, to which Jonah is sent and whose repentance spares it in Jonah 3, and whose fall Nahum announces. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
