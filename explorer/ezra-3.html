The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ezra 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when the seventh month was come, and the children of Israel were in the cities, the people gathered themselves together as one man to Jerusalem.

2 Then stood up Jeshua the son of Jozadak, and his brethren the priests, and Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and his brethren, and builded the altar of the God of Israel, to offer burnt offerings thereon, as it is written in the law of Moses the man of God.

3 And they set the altar upon his bases; for fear was upon them because of the people of those countries: and they offered burnt offerings thereon unto the LORD, even burnt offerings morning and evening.

4 They kept also the feast of tabernacles, as it is written, and offered the daily burnt offerings by number, according to the custom, as the duty of every day required;

5 And afterward offered the continual burnt offering, both of the new moons, and of all the set feasts of the LORD that were consecrated, and of every one that willingly offered a freewill offering unto the LORD.

6 From the first day of the seventh month began they to offer burnt offerings unto the LORD. But the foundation of the temple of the LORD was not yet laid.

7 They gave money also unto the masons, and to the carpenters; and meat, and drink, and oil, unto them of Zidon, and to them of Tyre, to bring cedar trees from Lebanon to the sea of Joppa, according to the grant that they had of Cyrus king of Persia.

8 Now in the second year of their coming unto the house of God at Jerusalem, in the second month, began Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and Jeshua the son of Jozadak, and the remnant of their brethren the priests and the Levites, and all they that were come out of the captivity unto Jerusalem; and appointed the Levites, from twenty years old and upward, to set forward the work of the house of the LORD.

9 Then stood Jeshua with his sons and his brethren, Kadmiel and his sons, the sons of Judah, together, to set forward the workmen in the house of God: the sons of Henadad, with their sons and their brethren the Levites.

10 And when the builders laid the foundation of the temple of the LORD, they set the priests in their apparel with trumpets, and the Levites the sons of Asaph with cymbals, to praise the LORD, after the ordinance of David king of Israel.

11 And they sang together by course in praising and giving thanks unto the LORD; because he is good, for his mercy endureth for ever toward Israel. And all the people shouted with a great shout, when they praised the LORD, because the foundation of the house of the LORD was laid.

12 But many of the priests and Levites and chief of the fathers, who were ancient men, that had seen the first house, when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes, wept with a loud voice; and many shouted aloud for joy:

13 So that the people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people: for the people shouted with a loud shout, and the noise was heard afar off.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the seventh month the people gather as one man to Jerusalem, and Jeshua with the priests and Zerubbabel with his brethren build the altar of the God of Israel on its bases, though fear is on them because of the peoples round about. Burnt offerings are resumed and the feasts kept, and then the foundation of the LORD’s temple is laid amid shouting for joy and weeping aloud, so the sound is heard afar off. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the seventh month, with the people settled in their cities, they gathered as one man to Jerusalem. Henry notes the unity: the feast month that once saw the tabernacle's dedication now sees a scattered people act together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Jeshua son of Jozadak with his fellow priests, and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel with his brethren, built the altar of the God of Israel. Priest and governor act together, and the altar comes before the temple, as atonement comes before building. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They set the altar on its bases, though fear was on them because of the peoples of the countries round about. JFB notes worship was resumed under threat, and morning and evening offerings began at once, as the law required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They kept the feast of tabernacles as written, with daily burnt offerings by number as each day required. Henry notes the return to the written calendar: exile had interrupted the feasts, but not the law that ordered them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Afterwards they offered the continual burnt offering, the new-moon offerings, all the set feasts of the LORD, and every freewill offering. The full round of worship is restored, not only the great feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. From the first day of the seventh month they began to offer burnt offerings, though the temple foundation was not yet laid. Henry stresses the point: sacrifice did not wait for a finished house, because the altar itself was God's appointed place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Money went to masons and carpenters, and meat, drink and oil to Tyre and Sidon for cedar from Lebanon to Joppa, under Cyrus's grant. JFB notes the parallel with Solomon's building, now on a humbler scale and by a foreign king's leave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. In the second year, in the second month, Zerubbabel, Jeshua, the priests, Levites and all the returned exiles began, appointing Levites from twenty years old upward to set forward the work. Henry notes the careful organisation: the work is entrusted to a tested age of Levites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Jeshua with his sons and brethren, Kadmiel and his sons, the sons of Judah, and the sons of Henadad with their brethren the Levites, stood together to set forward the workmen. The overseers are named in families, showing the whole Levite body engaged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. When the builders laid the foundation, priests in apparel stood with trumpets and Levites, sons of Asaph, with cymbals, to praise the LORD after David's ordinance. JFB notes worship and work are joined: the foundation is laid to music, as David ordered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They sang by course, praising and giving thanks because he is good, for his mercy endures for ever toward Israel, and all the people shouted greatly. Henry notes the ancient refrain is sung over a new beginning, linking restoration to the old covenant mercies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Many ancient priests, Levites and chiefs who had seen the first house wept loudly when this foundation was laid before their eyes, while many shouted for joy. JFB notes the tears were not mere grief: they remembered a glory this second house could not yet match. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The people could not discern the shout of joy from the weeping, for the shout was loud and heard afar off. Henry notes joy and sorrow mingled at every true restoration, since what is regained still bears the marks of what was lost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
