The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 29 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Furthermore David the king said unto all the congregation, Solomon my son, whom alone God hath chosen, is yet young and tender, and the work is great: for the palace is not for man, but for the LORD God.

2 Now I have prepared with all my might for the house of my God the gold for things to be made of gold, and the silver for things of silver, and the brass for things of brass, the iron for things of iron, and wood for things of wood; onyx stones, and stones to be set, glistering stones, and of divers colours, and all manner of precious stones, and marble stones in abundance.

3 Moreover, because I have set my affection to the house of my God, I have of mine own proper good, of gold and silver, which I have given to the house of my God, over and above all that I have prepared for the holy house.

4 Even three thousand talents of gold, of the gold of Ophir, and seven thousand talents of refined silver, to overlay the walls of the houses withal:

5 The gold for things of gold, and the silver for things of silver, and for all manner of work to be made by the hands of artificers. And who then is willing to consecrate his service this day unto the LORD?

6 Then the chief of the fathers and princes of the tribes of Israel and the captains of thousands and of hundreds, with the rulers of the king’s work, offered willingly,

7 And gave for the service of the house of God of gold five thousand talents and ten thousand drams, and of silver ten thousand talents, and of brass eighteen thousand talents, and one hundred thousand talents of iron.

8 And they with whom precious stones were found gave them to the treasure of the house of the LORD, by the hand of Jehiel the Gershonite.

9 Then the people rejoiced, for that they offered willingly, because with perfect heart they offered willingly to the LORD: and David the king also rejoiced with great joy.

10 Wherefore David blessed the LORD before all the congregation: and David said, Blessed be thou, LORD God of Israel our father, for ever and ever.

11 Thine, O LORD is the greatness, and the power, and the glory, and the victory, and the majesty: for all that is in the heaven and in the earth is thine; thine is the kingdom, O LORD, and thou art exalted as head above all.

12 Both riches and honour come of thee, and thou reignest over all; and in thine hand is power and might; and in thine hand it is to make great, and to give strength unto all.

13 Now therefore, our God, we thank thee, and praise thy glorious name.

14 But who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able to offer so willingly after this sort? for all things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee.

15 For we are strangers before thee, and sojourners, as were all our fathers: our days on the earth are as a shadow, and there is none abiding.

16 O LORD our God, all this store that we have prepared to build thee an house for thine holy name cometh of thine hand, and is all thine own.

17 I know also, my God, that thou triest the heart, and hast pleasure in uprightness. As for me, in the uprightness of mine heart I have willingly offered all these things: and now have I seen with joy thy people, which are present here, to offer willingly unto thee.

18 O LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel, our fathers, keep this for ever in the imagination of the thoughts of the heart of thy people, and prepare their heart unto thee:

19 And give unto Solomon my son a perfect heart, to keep thy commandments, thy testimonies, and thy statutes, and to do all these things, and to build the palace, for the which I have made provision.

20 And David said to all the congregation, Now bless the LORD your God. And all the congregation blessed the LORD God of their fathers, and bowed down their heads, and worshipped the LORD, and the king.

21 And they sacrificed sacrifices unto the LORD, and offered burnt offerings unto the LORD, on the morrow after that day, even a thousand bullocks, a thousand rams, and a thousand lambs, with their drink offerings, and sacrifices in abundance for all Israel:

22 And did eat and drink before the LORD on that day with great gladness. And they made Solomon the son of David king the second time, and anointed him unto the LORD to be the chief governor, and Zadok to be priest.

23 Then Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king instead of David his father, and prospered; and all Israel obeyed him.

24 And all the princes, and the mighty men, and all the sons likewise of king David, submitted themselves unto Solomon the king.

25 And the LORD magnified Solomon exceedingly in the sight of all Israel, and bestowed upon him such royal majesty as had not been on any king before him in Israel.

26 Thus David the son of Jesse reigned over all Israel.

27 And the time that he reigned over Israel was forty years; seven years reigned he in Hebron, and thirty and three years reigned he in Jerusalem.

28 And he died in a good old age, full of days, riches, and honour: and Solomon his son reigned in his stead.

29 Now the acts of David the king, first and last, behold, they are written in the book of Samuel the seer, and in the book of Nathan the prophet, and in the book of Gad the seer,

30 With all his reign and his might, and the times that went over him, and over Israel, and over all the kingdoms of the countries.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the people's freewill offerings for the temple, David's prayer of blessing, Solomon's anointing as king, and David's death after reigning forty years over Israel. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Furthermore David said unto all the congregation, Solomon my son, whom alone God hath chosen, is yet young and tender, and the work is great, for the palace is not for man, but for the Lord God. Henry notes David pressed the congregation to contribute, urging Solomon's youth and chosen status, and that the palace was for the Lord, not man, so all hands should help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Now I have prepared with all my might for the house of my God the gold, silver, brass, iron, wood, onyx, glistering and divers coloured stones, precious stones, and marble in abundance. Henry notes David did not throw the burden on others: he had prepared with all his might, making the work his business. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Moreover, because I have set my affection to the house of my God, I have of mine own proper good, of gold and silver, which I have given to the house of my God, over and above all that I have prepared for the holy house. Henry notes David gave from his own share because he loved God's habitation, not to atone or be seen, but purely from affection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Even three thousand talents of gold, of the gold of Ophir, and seven thousand talents of refined silver, to overlay the walls of the houses withal. Henry records David's own gift, 3,000 talents of gold and 7,000 of refined silver, for beautifying the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The gold for things of gold, and the silver for things of silver, and for all manner of work to be made by the hands of artificers. And who then is willing to consecrate his service this day unto the Lord? Henry notes the call to consecrate service this day: fill our hands to the Lord, serve him only, liberally, willingly, and speedily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Then the chief of the fathers and princes of the tribes of Israel, and the captains of thousands and hundreds, with the rulers of the king's work, offered willingly. Henry notes though persuaded, they offered willingly, with a perfect heart, from a good principle and sincere respect to God's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And gave for the service of the house of God of gold five thousand talents and ten thousand drams, and of silver ten thousand talents, and of brass eighteen thousand talents, and one hundred thousand talents of iron. Henry notes they gave like princes of Israel, generously, in sums befitting their station. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And they with whom precious stones were found gave them to the treasure of the house of the Lord, by the hand of Jehiel the Gershonite. Henry includes precious stones in the willing contributions, handed to Jehiel for the treasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Then the people rejoiced, for that they offered willingly, because with perfect heart they offered willingly to the Lord, and David the king also rejoiced with great joy. Henry notes the people rejoiced in the opportunity and prospect, and David rejoiced to see his successor surrounded by men well affected to God's house, a reviving sight for a man leaving the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Wherefore David blessed the Lord before all the congregation, and David said, blessed be thou, Lord God of Israel our father, for ever and ever. Henry notes David blessed God not only in his closet but before the congregation, as an aged saint whose heart enlarged in praise near death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Thine, O Lord, is the greatness, and the power, and the glory, and the victory, and the majesty, for all that is in the heaven and in the earth is thine, thine is the kingdom, O Lord, and thou art exalted as head above all. Henry adores God's infinite perfections in himself, his sovereign dominion as owner of heaven and earth, and his headship above all kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Both riches and honour come of thee, and thou reignest over all, and in thine hand is power and might, and in thine hand it is to make great, and to give strength unto all. Henry notes princes must learn they merited nothing by generosity: riches and honour came from God, and what they returned was a small part of what they received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Now therefore, our God, we thank thee, and praise thy glorious name. Henry notes the more we do for God, the more indebted we are for the honour of service and grace enabling us: servants have reason to thank God, not God to thank servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. But who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able to offer so willingly after this sort? For all things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee. Henry notes David, most honourable then in the world, wondered God took notice of him, and confessed offerings were but rent from God's own gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. For we are strangers before thee, and sojourners, as were all our fathers: our days on the earth are as a shadow, and there is none abiding. Henry notes even David, great, good, useful, and old, put himself foremost among those whose days are a shadow: vain, dark, transient, with no abiding expectation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. O Lord our God, all this store that we have prepared to build thee an house for thy holy name cometh of thine hand, and is all thine own. Henry repeats the acknowledgment: we have it from God as free gift, bound to use it for him, and every good thought and work must be referred to his grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. I know also, my God, that thou triest the heart, and hast pleasure in uprightness. As for me, in the uprightness of mine heart I have willingly offered all these things, and now have I seen with joy thy people, which are present here, to offer willingly unto thee. Henry notes David's comfort that God tries hearts and approves uprightness, neither proud of his own work nor envious of others'. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. O Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel, our fathers, keep this for ever in the imagination of the thoughts of the heart of thy people, and prepare their heart unto thee. Henry prays God keep good thoughts in people's hearts forever, confirming resolutions when David is gone, and preparing hearts for the temple privilege. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And give unto Solomon my son a perfect heart, to keep thy commandments, thy testimonies, and thy statutes, and to do all these things, and to build the palace, for the which I have made provision. Henry notes David prays not for riches, greatness, or learning, but an honest, perfect heart, to keep commandments and build with a single eye, since church-building without obedience does not save. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And David said to all the congregation, now bless the Lord your God. And all the congregation blessed the Lord God of their fathers, and bowed down their heads, and worshipped the Lord, and the king. Henry notes the assembly concurred by bowing heads, and honoured the king as God's instrument, honouring God in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And they sacrificed sacrifices unto the Lord, and offered burnt offerings unto the Lord, on the morrow after that day, even a thousand bullocks, a thousand rams, and a thousand lambs, with their drink offerings, and sacrifices in abundance for all Israel. Henry notes burnt offerings wholly consumed and peace offerings testified generous gratitude for the good posture of public affairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And did eat and drink before the Lord on that day with great gladness. And they made Solomon the son of David king the second time, and anointed him unto the Lord to be the chief governor, and Zadok to be priest. Henry notes they feasted on peace offerings before the Lord, showing they would be no poorer for liberal contributions, and Solomon was anointed a second time, with Zadok as priest in Abiathar's room. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Then Solomon sat on the throne of the Lord as king instead of David his father, and prospered, and all Israel obeyed him. Henry notes Israel's throne is called the Lord's throne, since God founded and filled it by immediate direction, and Solomon, following divine guidance, prospered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And all the princes, and the mighty men, and all the sons likewise of king David, submitted themselves unto Solomon the king. Henry notes even David's sons, whose seniority gave prior title, submitted, God inclining hearts so Solomon's reign began peaceably. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And the Lord magnified Solomon exceedingly in the sight of all Israel, and bestowed upon him such royal majesty as had not been on any king before him in Israel. Henry notes God honours those who honour him: Solomon's presence and deeds commanded respect beyond any predecessor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Thus David the son of Jesse reigned over all Israel. Henry introduces David's summary: forty years, as Moses, Othniel, Deborah, Gideon, Eli, Samuel, Saul, and later Solomon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And the time that he reigned over Israel was forty years: seven years reigned he in Hebron, and thirty and three years reigned he in Jerusalem. Henry records seven years in Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem, completing forty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And he died in a good old age, full of days, riches, and honour, and Solomon his son reigned in his stead. Henry notes David died loaded with days, riches, and honour, yet satiated: he had enough of this world and was willing to die, finding satisfaction only in God's lovingkindness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Now the acts of David the king, first and last, behold, they are written in the book of Samuel the seer, and in the book of Nathan the prophet, and in the book of Gad the seer. Henry notes fuller accounts lay in Samuel's, Nathan's, and Gad's records, then in being but now lost, and authentic church histories, though not sacred, may still be well used. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. With all his reign and his might, and the times that went over him, and over Israel, and over all the kingdoms of the countries. Henry closes with the times that went over David, Israel, and all kingdoms, the full scope of those lost records. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
