The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 72 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Give the king thy judgments, O God, and thy righteousness unto the king’s son.

2 He shall judge thy people with righteousness, and thy poor with judgment.

3 The mountains shall bring peace to the people, and the little hills, by righteousness.

4 He shall judge the poor of the people, he shall save the children of the needy, and shall break in pieces the oppressor.

5 They shall fear thee as long as the sun and moon endure, throughout all generations.

6 He shall come down like rain upon the mown grass: as showers that water the earth.

7 In his days shall the righteous flourish; and abundance of peace so long as the moon endureth.

8 He shall have dominion also from sea to sea, and from the river unto the ends of the earth.

9 They that dwell in the wilderness shall bow before him; and his enemies shall lick the dust.

10 The kings of Tarshish and of the isles shall bring presents: the kings of Sheba and Seba shall offer gifts.

11 Yea, all kings shall fall down before him: all nations shall serve him.

12 For he shall deliver the needy when he crieth; the poor also, and him that hath no helper.

13 He shall spare the poor and needy, and shall save the souls of the needy.

14 He shall redeem their soul from deceit and violence: and precious shall their blood be in his sight.

15 And he shall live, and to him shall be given of the gold of Sheba: prayer also shall be made for him continually; and daily shall he be praised.

16 There shall be an handful of corn in the earth upon the top of the mountains; the fruit thereof shall shake like Lebanon: and they of the city shall flourish like grass of the earth.

17 His name shall endure for ever: his name shall be continued as long as the sun: and men shall be blessed in him: all nations shall call him blessed.

18 Blessed be the LORD God, the God of Israel, who only doeth wondrous things.

19 And blessed be his glorious name for ever: and let the whole earth be filled with his glory; Amen, and Amen.

20 The prayers of David the son of Jesse are ended.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The king's psalm, and the second book's close: judgments and righteousness for the king and the king's son, judging the poor, saving needy children, breaking the oppressor, like rain on mown grass, dominion sea to sea, kings of Tarshish, Sheba, and Seba bringing gifts, all nations serving, the needy delivered and blood precious in his sight, name enduring as the sun, men blessed in him. Doxology to the God who only does wondrous things, and the colophon: the prayers of David son of Jesse are ended. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Heading note: the text panel prints the verses only, without a heading. The identification in verse 1 as Solomon's accession psalm, and the David colophon in verse 20, comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. Authorship and date remain Unresolved (see Trace). Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Give the king your judgments, O God, and your righteousness unto the king's son: judgments and righteousness for the king and the king's son are prayed for — Solomon's accession psalm, and, in its fullest sense, Christ's reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He shall judge your people with righteousness, and your poor with judgment: righteous judgment of the people, judgment for the poor — the king's first work is justice for the lowly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The mountains shall bring peace to the people, and the little hills, by righteousness: mountains and little hills bringing peace by righteousness — the whole land's fruitfulness follows just rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He shall judge the poor of the people, he shall save the children of the needy, and shall break in pieces the oppressor: judging the poor, saving needy children, breaking the oppressor — the threefold work of the just king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. They shall fear you as long as the sun and moon endure, throughout all generations: fear of God as long as sun and moon endure, through all generations — the reign's duration is cosmic. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He shall come down like rain upon the mown grass, as showers that water the earth: rain on mown grass, showers watering earth — the king's coming is gentle, refreshing, and life-giving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. In his days shall the righteous flourish, and abundance of peace so long as the moon endures: righteous flourishing, abundance of peace while the moon endures — prosperity and peace are the reign's climate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He shall have dominion also from sea to sea, and from the river unto the ends of the earth: sea-to-sea dominion, river to earth's ends — the kingdom is universal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They who dwell in the wilderness shall bow before him, and his enemies shall lick the dust: wilderness dwellers bowing, enemies licking dust — submission reaches the wildest and the proudest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The kings of Tarshish and of the isles shall bring presents; the kings of Sheba and Seba shall offer gifts: Tarshish and isles, Sheba and Seba bringing presents and gifts — distant kings pay tribute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Yea, all kings shall fall down before him; all nations shall serve him: all kings falling down, all nations serving — the tribute becomes universal worship and service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. For he shall deliver the needy when he cries, the poor also, and him who has no helper: delivering the crying needy, the poor, the helperless — the reason for universal service is mercy to the helpless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He shall spare the poor and needy, and shall save the souls of the needy: sparing and saving the souls of the poor and needy — mercy reaches the soul, not only the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He shall redeem their soul from deceit and violence, and precious shall their blood be in his sight: redemption from deceit and violence, blood precious in his sight — the oppressed are valued, not expendable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And he shall live, and to him shall be given of the gold of Sheba; prayer also shall be made for him continually, and daily shall he be praised: living, Sheba's gold given, continual prayer, daily praise — the king's life is sustained by gifts, prayer, and praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. There shall be a handful of corn in the earth upon the top of the mountains; the fruit thereof shall shake like Lebanon, and they of the city shall flourish like grass of the earth: a handful of corn on mountain tops fruiting like Lebanon, city dwellers flourishing like grass — abundance from small beginnings, under the just king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. His name shall endure for ever; his name shall be continued as long as the sun, and men shall be blessed in him; all nations shall call him blessed: name enduring for ever, continued as long as the sun, men blessed in him, all nations calling him blessed — the Abrahamic promise fulfilled in the King. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Blessed be the Lord God, the God of Israel, who only does wondrous things: the doxology blesses the Lord God of Israel, who only does wonders — the second book of Psalms closes here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. And blessed be his glorious name for ever, and let the whole earth be filled with his glory; Amen, and Amen: glorious name blessed for ever, whole earth filled with glory, double Amen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The prayers of David the son of Jesse are ended: the colophon notes David's prayers ended — the second book closes with David's name, though later psalms bear his name too, as collected and ordered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
