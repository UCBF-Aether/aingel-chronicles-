The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Colossians 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ sitteth on the right hand of God.

2 Set your affection on things above, not on things on the earth.

3 For ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God.

4 When Christ, who is our life, shall appear, then shall ye also appear with him in glory.

5 Mortify therefore your members which are upon the earth; fornication, uncleanness, inordinate affection, evil concupiscence, and covetousness, which is idolatry:

6 For which things’ sake the wrath of God cometh on the children of disobedience:

7 In the which ye also walked some time, when ye lived in them.

8 But now ye also put off all these; anger, wrath, malice, blasphemy, filthy communication out of your mouth.

9 Lie not one to another, seeing that ye have put off the old man with his deeds;

10 And have put on the new man, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him:

11 Where there is neither Greek nor Jew, circumcision nor uncircumcision, Barbarian, Scythian, bond nor free: but Christ is all, and in all.

12 Put on therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, longsuffering;

13 Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye.

14 And above all these things put on charity, which is the bond of perfectness.

15 And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to the which also ye are called in one body; and be ye thankful.

16 Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom; teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord.

17 And whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God and the Father by him.

18 Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as it is fit in the Lord.

19 Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter against them.

20 Children, obey your parents in all things: for this is well pleasing unto the Lord.

21 Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged.

22 Servants, obey in all things your masters according to the flesh; not with eyeservice, as menpleasers; but in singleness of heart, fearing God;

23 And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men;

24 Knowing that of the Lord ye shall receive the reward of the inheritance: for ye serve the Lord Christ.

25 But he that doeth wrong shall receive for the wrong which he hath done: and there is no respect of persons.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

If raised with Christ, seek the things above, Paul says: put to death sexual immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire and greed, and put off anger, malice and lying, putting on compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience and, above all, love. Let Christ's word dwell richly, do everything in his name, and let households, wives, husbands, children, fathers, servants and masters, live the same pattern. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. If believers have risen with Christ, they are to seek the things above, where Christ sits at the right hand of God, making heaven their scope and aim rather than living as they list. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. They are to set their affection on things above, not on things on the earth, since heaven and earth are contrary to one another and both cannot be followed together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Believers are dead to sin, its dominion broken and its power gradually subdued by grace, and their life is hid with Christ in God, safe with one whom they have not yet seen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. When Christ, who is their life, shall appear, they also shall appear with him in glory, at the general assembling of all the redeemed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They are therefore to mortify their members upon the earth, killing and suppressing corrupt workings as weeds or vermin that spread and destroy, making no provision for carnal indulgences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. For the sake of such sins the wrath of God comes on the children of disobedience, which is why mortification is necessary, since sins not killed will kill us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The Colossians had themselves once walked in these sins when they lived in them, but the gospel changes the higher as well as the lower powers of the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Now they are to put off all anger, wrath, malice, blasphemy, and filthy communication, keeping continual opposition to all corrupt workings and avoiding occasions of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. They are not to lie to one another, seeing they have put off the old man with his deeds, and Henry notes the duty of holiness rests on everyone because Christ is the Christian's All. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They have put on the new man, renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him, as the gospel supports right reason and conscience over appetite and passion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. In this new man there is neither Greek nor Jew, circumcision nor uncircumcision, Barbarian, Scythian, bond nor free, since no difference of country or condition exempts anyone from the duty of holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. As the elect of God, holy and beloved, they are to put on bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, and meekness, being lowly and compassionate towards all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. They are to forbear and forgive one another, even where quarrels arise, imitating the forgiveness through which they themselves are saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Above all these things they are to put on charity, which is the bond of perfectness, binding the other graces together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The peace of God is to rule in their hearts, to which they are called in one body, and thanksgiving helps make them agreeable to all men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The word of Christ is to dwell in them richly in all wisdom, teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, since the soul prospers when full of the Scriptures and the grace of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Whatever they do in word or deed, they are to do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father, in believing dependence on him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Wives are to submit to their own husbands, as is fit in the Lord, a submission not to a severe lord or stern tyrant but to a husband engaged to affectionate duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Husbands must love their wives with tender and faithful affection, and not be bitter against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Children are to obey their parents in all things, for this is well pleasing unto the Lord, and dutiful children are the most likely to prosper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Fathers are not to provoke their children to anger, lest they be discouraged, since parents must be tender as well as children obedient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Servants are to obey their masters in all things consistent with duty to God, not with eyeservice as menpleasers, but in singleness of heart, fearing God, just and faithful even when out of their master's eye. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Whatever they do, they are to do it heartily, as to the Lord and not unto men, with diligence and cheerfulness rather than idleness or discontent at God's providence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. They shall receive from the Lord the reward of the inheritance, for in serving their masters according to Christ's command they serve the Lord Christ himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. He that does wrong shall receive for the wrong he has done, with no respect of persons, since the righteous Judge will deal justly between master and servant alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
