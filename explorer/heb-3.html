The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hebrews 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Wherefore, holy brethren, partakers of the heavenly calling, consider the Apostle and High Priest of our profession, Christ Jesus;

2 Who was faithful to him that appointed him, as also Moses was faithful in all his house.

3 For this man was counted worthy of more glory than Moses, inasmuch as he who hath builded the house hath more honour than the house.

4 For every house is builded by some man; but he that built all things is God.

5 And Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were to be spoken after;

6 But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end.

7 Wherefore (as the Holy Ghost saith, To day if ye will hear his voice,

8 Harden not your hearts, as in the provocation, in the day of temptation in the wilderness:

9 When your fathers tempted me, proved me, and saw my works forty years.

10 Wherefore I was grieved with that generation, and said, They do alway err in their heart; and they have not known my ways.

11 So I sware in my wrath, They shall not enter into my rest.)

12 Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God.

13 But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.

14 For we are made partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence stedfast unto the end;

15 While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation.

16 For some, when they had heard, did provoke: howbeit not all that came out of Egypt by Moses.

17 But with whom was he grieved forty years? was it not with them that had sinned, whose carcases fell in the wilderness?

18 And to whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not?

19 So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Consider Jesus, the writer urges, faithful as a Son over God's house, where Moses was faithful as a servant within it. Then comes the warning drawn from the wilderness generation, who heard God's voice, hardened their hearts in provocation, and were shut out of rest: readers must exhort one another daily and hold fast, lest unbelief do the same to them. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Holy brethren, partakers of the heavenly calling, are to consider Christ Jesus — the Apostle of our profession, the Messenger sent by God, the great Revealer of the faith and hope we profess. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Christ was faithful to him that appointed him, as Moses was faithful in all his house — yet Moses' faithfulness was but a type of Christ's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Christ is counted worthy of more glory than Moses, inasmuch as he who built the house has more honour than the house itself: Christ is Master and Maker of his church, not merely a servant in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Every house is built by some man, but he that built all things is God — so the church, as God's house, belongs to Christ its builder and Owner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were to be spoken after — a faithful servant, pointing forward to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. But Christ as a Son over his own house — whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end; setting out well is not enough without stedfast perseverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Wherefore, as the Holy Ghost says, "To day if ye will hear his voice" — the Spirit's warning comes with present urgency, for to-morrow is not ours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Harden not your hearts, as in the provocation, in the day of temptation in the wilderness — hardening of heart is the spring of all other sins, and days of temptation become days of provocation when we depend wholly on God yet rebel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Your fathers tempted me, proved me, and saw my works forty years — long privilege did not cure unbelief; the sins of others, especially of our relations, should be warnings to us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Wherefore I was grieved with that generation, and said, They do alway err in their heart, and they have not known my ways — sin, especially by God's professing people, not only provokes God but grieves him, though he waits long to be gracious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. So I sware in my wrath, They shall not enter into my rest — sin long persisted in will at last make God's wrath discover itself in destroying the impenitent, and there is no resting under the wrath of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God — all who would get safe to heaven must look about them, for distrust soon leads to desertion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day, lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin — none, even the strongest, needs no help from other Christians, and sin appears fair but is vile, promising much and performing nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. For we are made partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence stedfast unto the end — perseverance in faith is the best evidence of the sincerity of our faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation — hearing the word often is a means of salvation, yet if not hearkened to it exposes us more to Divine wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. For some, when they had heard, did provoke: howbeit not all that came out of Egypt by Moses — outward privilege did not save the provokers; we must pray to be numbered with true believers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. But with whom was he grieved forty years? Was it not with them that had sinned, whose carcases fell in the wilderness? — unbelief left a whole generation short of the promised land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. And to whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not? — their exclusion was the fruit of unbelief, not of any failure in God's promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief — as our obedience follows according to the power of our faith, so our sins and want of care follow according to the prevailing of unbelief in us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
