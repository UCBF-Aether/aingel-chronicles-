The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 31 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Avenge the children of Israel of the Midianites: afterward shalt thou be gathered unto thy people.

3 And Moses spake unto the people, saying, Arm some of yourselves unto the war, and let them go against the Midianites, and avenge the LORD of Midian.

4 Of every tribe a thousand, throughout all the tribes of Israel, shall ye send to the war.

5 So there were delivered out of the thousands of Israel, a thousand of every tribe, twelve thousand armed for war.

6 And Moses sent them to the war, a thousand of every tribe, them and Phinehas the son of Eleazar the priest, to the war, with the holy instruments, and the trumpets to blow in his hand.

7 And they warred against the Midianites, as the LORD commanded Moses; and they slew all the males.

8 And they slew the kings of Midian, beside the rest of them that were slain; namely, Evi, and Rekem, and Zur, and Hur, and Reba, five kings of Midian: Balaam also the son of Beor they slew with the sword.

9 And the children of Israel took all the women of Midian captives, and their little ones, and took the spoil of all their cattle, and all their flocks, and all their goods.

10 And they burnt all their cities wherein they dwelt, and all their goodly castles, with fire.

11 And they took all the spoil, and all the prey, both of men and of beasts.

12 And they brought the captives, and the prey, and the spoil, unto Moses, and Eleazar the priest, and unto the congregation of the children of Israel, unto the camp at the plains of Moab, which are by Jordan near Jericho.

13 And Moses, and Eleazar the priest, and all the princes of the congregation, went forth to meet them without the camp.

14 And Moses was wroth with the officers of the host, with the captains over thousands, and captains over hundreds, which came from the battle.

15 And Moses said unto them, Have ye saved all the women alive?

16 Behold, these caused the children of Israel, through the counsel of Balaam, to commit trespass against the LORD in the matter of Peor, and there was a plague among the congregation of the LORD.

17 Now therefore kill every male among the little ones, and kill every woman that hath known man by lying with him.

18 But all the women children, that have not known a man by lying with him, keep alive for yourselves.

19 And do ye abide without the camp seven days: whosoever hath killed any person, and whosoever hath touched any slain, purify both yourselves and your captives on the third day, and on the seventh day.

20 And purify all your raiment, and all that is made of skins, and all work of goats’ hair, and all things made of wood.

21 And Eleazar the priest said unto the men of war which went to the battle, This is the ordinance of the law which the LORD commanded Moses;

22 Only the gold, and the silver, the brass, the iron, the tin, and the lead,

23 Every thing that may abide the fire, ye shall make it go through the fire, and it shall be clean: nevertheless it shall be purified with the water of separation: and all that abideth not the fire ye shall make go through the water.

24 And ye shall wash your clothes on the seventh day, and ye shall be clean, and afterward ye shall come into the camp.

25 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

26 Take the sum of the prey that was taken, both of man and of beast, thou, and Eleazar the priest, and the chief fathers of the congregation:

27 And divide the prey into two parts; between them that took the war upon them, who went out to battle, and between all the congregation:

28 And levy a tribute unto the Lord of the men of war which went out to battle: one soul of five hundred, both of the persons, and of the beeves, and of the asses, and of the sheep:

29 Take it of their half, and give it unto Eleazar the priest, for an heave offering of the LORD.

30 And of the children of Israel’s half, thou shalt take one portion of fifty, of the persons, of the beeves, of the asses, and of the flocks, of all manner of beasts, and give them unto the Levites, which keep the charge of the tabernacle of the LORD.

31 And Moses and Eleazar the priest did as the LORD commanded Moses.

32 And the booty, being the rest of the prey which the men of war had caught, was six hundred thousand and seventy thousand and five thousand sheep,

33 And threescore and twelve thousand beeves,

34 And threescore and one thousand asses,

35 And thirty and two thousand persons in all, of women that had not known man by lying with him.

36 And the half, which was the portion of them that went out to war, was in number three hundred thousand and seven and thirty thousand and five hundred sheep:

37 And the LORD’s tribute of the sheep was six hundred and threescore and fifteen.

38 And the beeves were thirty and six thousand; of which the LORD’s tribute was threescore and twelve.

39 And the asses were thirty thousand and five hundred; of which the LORD’s tribute was threescore and one.

40 And the persons were sixteen thousand; of which the LORD’s tribute was thirty and two persons.

41 And Moses gave the tribute, which was the LORD’s heave offering, unto Eleazar the priest, as the LORD commanded Moses.

42 And of the children of Israel’s half, which Moses divided from the men that warred,

43 (Now the half that pertained unto the congregation was three hundred thousand and thirty thousand and seven thousand and five hundred sheep,

44 And thirty and six thousand beeves,

45 And thirty thousand asses and five hundred,

46 And sixteen thousand persons;)

47 Even of the children of Israel’s half, Moses took one portion of fifty, both of man and of beast, and gave them unto the Levites, which kept the charge of the tabernacle of the LORD; as the LORD commanded Moses.

48 And the officers which were over thousands of the host, the captains of thousands, and captains of hundreds, came near unto Moses:

49 And they said unto Moses, Thy servants have taken the sum of the men of war which are under our charge, and there lacketh not one man of us.

50 We have therefore brought an oblation for the LORD, what every man hath gotten, of jewels of gold, chains, and bracelets, rings, earrings, and tablets, to make an atonement for our souls before the LORD.

51 And Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold of them, even all wrought jewels.

52 And all the gold of the offering that they offered up to the LORD, of the captains of thousands, and of the captains of hundreds, was sixteen thousand seven hundred and fifty shekels.

53 (For the men of war had taken spoil, every man for himself.)

54 And Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold of the captains of thousands and of hundreds, and brought it into the tabernacle of the congregation, for a memorial for the children of Israel before the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the war against Midian, commanded as vengeance for the matter of Peor. Twelve thousand men, a thousand from each tribe, go out with Phinehas and the holy instruments and trumpets, kill the five kings of Midian and Balaam son of Beor, burn their cities, and bring back captives, cattle, and spoil. The spoil is divided equally between the soldiers and the congregation, with a tribute to the LORD from each half, one in five hundred from the soldiers and one in fifty from the people, given to Eleazar and the Levites. The officers then bring a gold offering, and the count shows not one soldier missing. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. Avenge Israel of the Midianites: afterward you shall be gathered to your people. JFB notes the Midianites, a semi-nomad people descended from Abraham and Keturah east and southeast of Moab, were the principal instigators of the seduction scheme that entrapped Israel into idolatry and licentiousness, hoping the Lord would withdraw his protection, and God spoke of avenging Israel while Moses spoke of avenging the Lord, since dishonour had been done to God and injury to his people, whose cause was identical. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Moses spoke to the people, saying, arm some of yourselves to the war, and let them go against the Midianites, and avenge the Lord of Midian. JFB notes this order came but a short time before Moses' death, and the announcement of that approaching event seems to have accelerated rather than retarded his warlike preparations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Of every tribe a thousand, throughout all the tribes of Israel, shall you send to the war. The quota was equal from every tribe, to prevent mutual jealousy or strife in a war that concerned them all alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. So there were delivered out of the thousands of Israel, a thousand of every tribe, twelve thousand armed for war. JFB notes delivered means drafted or chosen, and considering the enemy's numerical force this was a small quota, but the design was to exercise their faith and animate them for the approaching invasion of Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Moses sent them to the war, a thousand of every tribe, them and Phinehas son of Eleazar the priest, to the war, with the holy instruments, and the trumpets to blow in his hand. JFB notes the priest's presence was necessary to preside over the Levites and inflame the combatants' courage by sacred services and counsels, and the holy instruments must mean the trumpets, since neither ark nor Urim and Thummim were carried to battle until a later period. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They warred against the Midianites, as the Lord commanded Moses, and they slew all the males. JFB notes this accorded with the divine order in such cases, but the destruction appears only partial, limited to those near the Hebrew camp who had been accomplices in the Baal-peor plot, while a large portion of Midianites were absent on pastoral wanderings or saved themselves by flight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They slew the kings of Midian, beside the rest of them that were slain, namely Evi, Rekem, Zur, Hur, and Reba, five kings of Midian: Balaam also son of Beor they slew with the sword. JFB notes the kings each held absolute power within his own city or district and were also called dukes or princes of Sihon, Zur was Cozbi's father, and Balaam, who on dismissal set out for Mesopotamia but remained among the Midianites to incite them and watch his wicked counsel work, or returned in greed to demand his reward, was an object of merited vengeance, since to attempt defeating prophecies he himself had uttered was desperate wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The children of Israel took all the women of Midian captives, and their little ones, and took the spoil of all their cattle, and all their flocks, and all their goods. The first spoils of the war were its people and livestock, taken as captives and prey before the cities were burned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They burnt all their cities wherein they dwelt, and all their goodly castles, with fire. The burning of cities and castles completed the destruction of Midian's places of refuge, so that the guilty nation lost its homes as well as its kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. They took all the spoil, and all the prey, both of men and of beasts. Spoil and prey together, men and beasts, were gathered from the field before being brought to Moses for division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. They brought the captives, and the prey, and the spoil, to Moses, and Eleazar the priest, and to the congregation of Israel, to the camp at the plains of Moab, which are by Jordan near Jericho. The war's fruits were not kept by the army but brought to Moses, Eleazar and the congregation at the plains of Moab, for judgment and division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Moses, and Eleazar the priest, and all the princes of the congregation, went forth to meet them without the camp. JFB notes they went out partly as a token of respect and congratulation on the victory, partly to see how the Lord's commands had been executed, and partly to prevent defilement of the camp by blood-stained warriors entering it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Moses was wroth with the officers of the host, with the captains over thousands, and captains over hundreds, which came from the battle. JFB notes his displeasure, though it looks like fierce temper, arose from a pious and enlightened regard to Israel's best interests, since no order had been given for slaughtering the women, who in ancient war were commonly reserved for slaves, but by their antecedent conduct the Midianite women had forfeited all claims to mild treatment, and the sacred character and avowed object of the war made their slaughter necessary without any special order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Moses said to them, have you saved all the women alive? The question exposed the error: the very women whose seduction had caused the plague had been spared, leaving the root of the sin alive in the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Behold, these caused Israel, through the counsel of Balaam, to commit trespass against the Lord in the matter of Peor, and there was a plague among the congregation of the Lord. Henry notes the Midianites' wicked wiles were Balaam's projects, and it was just that he should perish with them, since the counsel that caused the trespass and the plague was his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Now therefore kill every male among the little ones, and kill every woman that has known man by lying with him. JFB notes it was designed to be a war of extermination, such as God himself had ordered against the people of Canaan, whom the Midianites equalled in the enormity of their wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. But all the women children, that have not known a man by lying with him, keep alive for yourselves. Henry notes the sword of war should spare women and children, but the sword of justice knows no distinction but guilty or not guilty, and in this war the women were the worst criminals, while the female children spared, brought up among Israelites, would not tempt them to idolatry, and were kept not for sinful purposes but for slaves, a custom everywhere practised in former times as to captives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Do you abide without the camp seven days: whosoever has killed any person, and whosoever has touched any slain, purify both yourselves and your captives on the third day, and on the seventh day. JFB notes that though Israel had taken the field in obedience to God's command, they had become defiled by contact with the dead, and a purification was required as the law prescribed, extended to dress, houses, tents and everything a dead body had lain on or blood-stained hands had touched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Purify all your raiment, and all that is made of skins, and all work of goats' hair, and all things made of wood. The purification reached everything the warriors and captives owned, since the spoil had been used by Midianites and now came into Israelite possession, and it was fit that it should be purified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Eleazar the priest said to the men of war which went to the battle, this is the ordinance of the law which the Lord commanded Moses: only the gold, and the silver, the brass, the iron, the tin, and the lead. Eleazar, not Moses, gave the men of war the ordinance for the spoil, since the priesthood administered the law of purification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Only the gold, and the silver, the brass, the iron, the tin, and the lead. Six metals were named, covering every metal the spoil could contain, so that no material escaped the ordinance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Every thing that may abide the fire, you shall make it go through the fire, and it shall be clean: nevertheless it shall be purified with the water of separation: and all that abides not the fire you shall make go through the water. Fire purified what could bear fire, and the water of separation purified the rest, a double cleansing for goods that had belonged to idolaters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. You shall wash your clothes on the seventh day, and you shall be clean, and afterward you shall come into the camp. Henry notes the Israelites had to purify themselves according to the law and abide without the camp seven days, though they had contracted no moral guilt, the war being just and lawful and commanded by God, so that God would preserve in their minds a dread and detestation of shedding blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Take the sum of the prey that was taken, both of man and of beast, you, and Eleazar the priest, and the chief fathers of the congregation. JFB notes the captives and cattle, first lumped together according to ancient usage, were divided into two equal parts, one to the people at large who had sustained a common injury and were all liable to serve, and the other to the combatants who had encountered the labours and perils of war. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Divide the prey into two parts: between them that took the war upon them, who went out to battle, and between all the congregation. The division was equal between soldiers and congregation, though the soldiers had borne the war's labours and perils. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Levy a tribute to the Lord of the men of war which went out to battle: one soul of five hundred, both of the persons, and of the beeves, and of the asses, and of the sheep. From both parts a deduction was taken for the sanctuary as a thank offering to God for preservation and victory, and the soldiers' rate was one in five hundred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Take it of their half, and give it to Eleazar the priest, for a heave offering of the Lord. The soldiers' tribute went to Eleazar the priest as the Lord's heave offering, the priesthood receiving what the army owed to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. And of Israel's half, you shall take one portion of fifty, of the persons, of the beeves, of the asses, and of the flocks, of all manner of beasts, and give them to the Levites, which keep the charge of the tabernacle of the Lord. Henry notes whatever we have, God justly claims a part, and out of the people's share God required one in fifty, but out of the soldiers' share only one in five hundred, since the less opportunity we have of honouring God with personal services, the more we should give in money or value. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Moses and Eleazar the priest did as the Lord commanded Moses. The division was carried out exactly as commanded, with Moses and Eleazar together administering it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And the booty, being the rest of the prey which the men of war had caught, was six hundred thousand and seventy thousand and five thousand sheep. JFB tabulates the remaining booty after the slain captives and the army's support were deducted: sheep six hundred and seventy-five thousand, beeves seventy-two thousand, asses sixty-one thousand, persons thirty-two thousand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. And threescore and twelve thousand beeves. The beeves numbered seventy-two thousand, the second great herd of the booty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. And threescore and one thousand asses. The asses numbered sixty-one thousand, completing the livestock of the spoil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. And thirty and two thousand persons in all, of women that had not known man by lying with him. The persons numbered thirty-two thousand, all women who had not known man, the survivors kept alive under Moses' order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. And the half, which was the portion of them that went out to war, was in number three hundred thousand and seven and thirty thousand and five hundred sheep. The soldiers' half of the sheep was three hundred and thirty-seven thousand five hundred, exactly half the total. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. And the Lord's tribute of the sheep was six hundred and threescore and fifteen. One in five hundred of the soldiers' sheep came to six hundred and seventy-five, the Lord's tribute from that half. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. And the beeves were thirty and six thousand, of which the Lord's tribute was threescore and twelve. The soldiers' beeves were thirty-six thousand, and their tribute seventy-two. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. And the asses were thirty thousand and five hundred, of which the Lord's tribute was threescore and one. The soldiers' asses were thirty thousand five hundred, and their tribute sixty-one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. And the persons were sixteen thousand, of which the Lord's tribute was thirty and two persons. The soldiers' persons were sixteen thousand, and their tribute thirty-two. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Moses gave the tribute, which was the Lord's heave offering, to Eleazar the priest, as the Lord commanded Moses. The soldiers' tribute was handed to Eleazar exactly as commanded, closing the army's account with the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. And of Israel's half, which Moses divided from the men that warred. The congregation's half was then divided from the soldiers' half, so that the people's share stood apart for its own deduction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. Now the half that pertained to the congregation was three hundred thousand and thirty thousand and seven thousand and five hundred sheep. The congregation's sheep matched the soldiers', three hundred and thirty-seven thousand five hundred, the equal division holding exactly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. And thirty and six thousand beeves. The congregation's beeves matched the soldiers' at thirty-six thousand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. And thirty thousand asses and five hundred. The congregation's asses matched the soldiers' at thirty thousand five hundred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. And sixteen thousand persons. The congregation's persons matched the soldiers' at sixteen thousand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. Even of Israel's half, Moses took one portion of fifty, both of man and of beast, and gave them to the Levites, which kept the charge of the tabernacle of the Lord, as the Lord commanded Moses. The congregation's deduction at one in fifty went to the Levites who kept the tabernacle's charge, a far heavier rate than the soldiers' one in five hundred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. And the officers which were over thousands of the host, the captains of thousands, and captains of hundreds, came near to Moses. The officers came to Moses after the division, with a report that turned the victory into an offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. And they said to Moses, your servants have taken the sum of the men of war which are under our charge, and there lacks not one man of us. JFB calls a victory untarnished by the loss of a single Israelite soldier an astonishing miracle, clearly betokening the direct interposition of Heaven, and Henry adds the success was remarkable, so small a company overcoming such multitudes, but still more wonderful that not one was slain or missing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. We have therefore brought an oblation for the Lord, what every man has gotten, of jewels of gold, chains, and bracelets, rings, earrings, and tablets, to make an atonement for our souls before the Lord. JFB notes the oblation was partly an atonement or reparation for their error in sparing the women, though it could possess no expiatory virtue, and partly a tribute of gratitude for the stupendous service rendered them, consisting of spoil acquired by individual valour, not divided like the prey, each soldier retaining it in lieu of pay, and offered by the captains alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold of them, even all wrought jewels. Moses and Eleazar received the captains' gold, every wrought jewel, for the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. And all the gold of the offering that they offered up to the Lord, of the captains of thousands, and of the captains of hundreds, was sixteen thousand seven hundred and fifty shekels. The gold totalled sixteen thousand seven hundred and fifty shekels, the captains' offering measured and recorded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. For the men of war had taken spoil, every man for himself. The note explains why only captains offered: the men of war had taken spoil each for himself, and the captains' offering represented the army's gratitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold of the captains of thousands and of hundreds, and brought it into the tabernacle of the congregation, for a memorial for the children of Israel before the Lord. Henry notes the officers thus confessed that instead of claiming a reward for their service, they needed forgiveness of much that had been amiss, and desired to be thankful for the preservation of their lives, which might justly have been taken away, and the gold stood in the tabernacle as a memorial for Israel before the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

JFB notes his displeasure, though it looks like fierce temper, arose from a pious and enlightened regard to Israel's best interests, since no order had been given for slaughtering the women, who in ancient war were commonly reserved for slaves, but by their antecedent conduct the Midianite women had forfeited all claims to mild treatment, and the sacred character and avowed object of the war made their slaughter necessary without any special order.   • Verse 15. Moses said to them, have you saved all the women alive? The question exposed the error: the very women whose seduction had caused the plague had been spared, leaving the root of the sin alive in the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

JFB notes it was designed to be a war of extermination, such as God himself had ordered against the people of Canaan, whom the Midianites equalled in the enormity of their wickedness. JFB notes that though Israel had taken the field in obedience to God's command, they had become defiled by contact with the dead, and a purification was required as the law prescribed, extended to dress, houses, tents and everything a dead body had lain on or blood-stained hands had touched. JFB notes the captives and cattle, first lumped together according to ancient usage, were divided into two equal parts, one to the people at large who had sustained a common injury and were all liable to serve, and the other to the combatants who had encountered the labours and perils of war. The first spoils of the war were its people and livestock, taken as captives and prey before the cities were burned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

The burning of cities and castles completed the destruction of Midian's places of refuge, so that the guilty nation lost its homes as well as its kings.   • Verse 11. They took all the spoil, and all the prey, both of men and of beasts. Spoil and prey together, men and beasts, were gathered from the field before being brought to Moses for division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

The war's fruits were not kept by the army but brought to Moses, Eleazar and the congregation at the plains of Moab, for judgment and division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Behold, these caused Israel, through the counsel of Balaam, to commit trespass against the Lord in the matter of Peor, and there was a plague among the congregation of the Lord. Henry notes the Midianites' wicked wiles were Balaam's projects, and it was just that he should perish with them, since the counsel that caused the trespass and the plague was his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. But all the women children, that have not known a man by lying with him, keep alive for yourselves. Henry notes the sword of war should spare women and children, but the sword of justice knows no distinction but guilty or not guilty, and in this war the women were the worst criminals, while the female children spared, brought up among Israelites, would not tempt them to idolatry, and were kept not for sinful purposes but for slaves, a custom everywhere practised in former times as to captives.   • Verse 20. Purify all your raiment, and all that is made of skins, and all work of goats' hair, and all things made of wood. The purification reached everything the warriors and captives owned, since the spoil had been used by Midianites and now came into Israelite possession, and it was fit that it should be purified.   • Verse 22. Only the gold, and the silver, the brass, the iron, the tin, and the lead. Six metals were named, covering every metal the spoil could contain, so that no material escaped the ordinance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry notes the Israelites had to purify themselves according to the law and abide without the camp seven days, though they had contracted no moral guilt, the war being just and lawful and commanded by God, so that God would preserve in their minds a dread and detestation of shedding blood. Henry notes whatever we have, God justly claims a part, and out of the people's share God required one in fifty, but out of the soldiers' share only one in five hundred, since the less opportunity we have of honouring God with personal services, the more we should give in money or value.
