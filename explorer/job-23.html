The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Job answered and said,

2 Even to day is my complaint bitter: my stroke is heavier than my groaning.

3 Oh that I knew where I might find him! that I might come even to his seat!

4 I would order my cause before him, and fill my mouth with arguments.

5 I would know the words which he would answer me, and understand what he would say unto me.

6 Will he plead against me with his great power? No; but he would put strength in me.

7 There the righteous might dispute with him; so should I be delivered for ever from my judge.

8 Behold, I go forward, but he is not there; and backward, but I cannot perceive him:

9 On the left hand, where he doth work, but I cannot behold him: he hideth himself on the right hand, that I cannot see him:

10 But he knoweth the way that I take: when he hath tried me, I shall come forth as gold.

11 My foot hath held his steps, his way have I kept, and not declined.

12 Neither have I gone back from the commandment of his lips; I have esteemed the words of his mouth more than my necessary food.

13 But he is in one mind, and who can turn him? and what his soul desireth, even that he doeth.

14 For he performeth the thing that is appointed for me: and many such things are with him.

15 Therefore am I troubled at his presence: when I consider, I am afraid of him.

16 For God maketh my heart soft, and the Almighty troubleth me:

17 Because I was not cut off before the darkness, neither hath he covered the darkness from my face.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Job answers Eliphaz a third time by searching for the God who seems withdrawn: oh that I knew where I might find him, that I might come even to his seat and order my cause before him. He looks forward and backward, left and right, and cannot behold him — yet he holds that God knows the way he takes, and that when he is tried he shall come forth as gold. The same decree that steadies him terrifies him, for God performs the thing appointed for him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Job answers Eliphaz a third time, appealing from his friends to God's just judgment. The chapter is Job's search for the God who seems withdrawn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Even today Job's complaint is bitter, and his stroke heavier than his groaning. JFB notes today may imply the debate ran more than one day, and the stroke is God's hand upon him, so heavy he cannot relieve himself adequately by groaning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Oh that Job knew where he might find God, that he might come even to his seat! JFB notes the same wish as before, with seat as a well-prepared throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Job would order his cause before God and fill his mouth with arguments. JFB explains order as stating methodically, and filling as having abundance of arguments to adduce. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Job would know the words God would answer him, and understand what he would say unto him. JFB notes the emphasis: it little matters what man says of him, if only he knows what God judges of him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Will God plead against Job with his great power? No, but he would put strength in him. JFB notes the objection: Job does not wish God to plead with omnipotence, but to attend to him as an ordinary judge, giving a patient hearing in divine knowledge of his innocence, with strength as in Jacob's wrestling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. There the righteous might dispute with God, and Job would be delivered forever from his judge. JFB renders there as then, if God would attend: the result of the dispute would be acknowledgment as righteous, delivered from suspicion of guilt by his judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Behold, Job goes forward, but God is not there, and backward, but he cannot perceive him. JFB explains forward and backward as east and west, Hebrew geographers facing sunrise, so before means east and behind west. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. On the left hand, where God works, Job cannot behold him, and he hides himself on the right hand so Job cannot see him. JFB renders left as north, where God's glorious works are especially seen, and right as south, the unexplored region then thought uninhabitable for heat, where God hides himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. But God knows the way that Job takes; when he has tried him, Job shall come forth as gold. JFB notes Job corrects himself: the omniscient One already knows his inward way, though for inscrutable cause he hides himself, and if he tries the cause Job shall come forth as gold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Job's foot has held his steps, his way he has kept and not declined. JFB explains held as fast by God's steps, the law as a way with God going before as guide, in whose footsteps Job must tread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Job has not gone back from the commandment of God's lips, esteeming his words more than his necessary food. JFB renders esteemed as laid up as treasure found, alluding to Eliphaz's counsel to lay up God's words, which Job says he has already done, more than his appointed portion of food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But God is in one mind, and who can turn him? What his soul desires, even that he does. JFB notes that notwithstanding Job's innocence, God is unaltered in purpose, with soul as will, and sovereignty: one great purpose, nothing haphazard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. For God performs the thing appointed for Job, and many such things are with him. JFB notes God has yet many more such ills in store, though hidden in his breast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Therefore Job is troubled at God's presence; when he considers, he is afraid of him. JFB notes God's decrees, impossible to resist and leaving us in the dark about what comes next, are calculated to fill the mind with holy awe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. For God makes Job's heart soft, and the Almighty troubles him. JFB renders soft as faint, melted courage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Because Job was not cut off before the darkness, nor has God covered the darkness from his face. JFB explains it as not taken away by death from the evil to come, and cut off in the Arabic sense as brought to the land of silence, his complaint hushed in death, while instead of covering the cloud of evil from his face, God covers him with it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Where the speakers disagree

Eliphaz, in his third speech, charges Job with specific sins — taking pledges, stripping the naked, withholding bread and water. Job does not answer the charges item by item, because, he says, he cannot find God to lay his cause before him at all; yet he holds that God knows his way and that when tried he shall come forth as gold. The two speeches pass each other: accusation on one side, absence on the other. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
