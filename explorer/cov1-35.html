The Text — 1 Book of the Covenant 35 (J. Cooper & A. J. Maclean, The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1902), public domain (archive.org cu31924029296170))

1 Let him be in everything as the eye of the Church, with fear admonishing, so that he may be an example to! the people of piety Let him admonish thus:

*ADMONITION OF THE DEACON*

2 Let us arise.

3 Let every one know his place.

4 Let the catechumens depart.

5 [Lift] wp the eyes of your hearts.

6 The angels are looking on.

7 Let us beseech in concord.

8 Let no fornicator, no wrathful man? [remain]; if one who is a servant of evil be [here], let him withdraw.

9 our Lord and our God and our Saviour Jesus Christ.

10 When the presbyter or bishop beyinneth the prayer, let the people pray and kneel.

11 Then let the deacon say thus:

12 For the peace which is from heaven let us beseech, that the Lord in His mercy may give us peace.

13 For our faith let us beseech, that the Lord may grant unto us to keep truly unto the end the faith which is in Him.

14 For harmony and concord let us beseech, that the Lord may keep us together in concord of the Spirit.

15 For patience let us beseech, that the Lord may bestow [upon us patience unto the end in all afflictions.

16 For the Apostles let us beseech, that the Lord may grant to us to please Him, as they also pleased Him, and may make us worthy of their inheritance.

17 For the holy prophets let us beseech, that the Lord may number us with them.

18 For the holy confessors let us beseech, that the Lord God may grant us to fulfil [our course] with the same mind [as they].

19 For the bishop let us beseech, that our Lord may grant him to us for length of days’ in faith, rightly dividing the word of truth, and standing at the head of the Church purely and without blame.

20 For the presbyterate let us beseech, that the Lord may not take away from them the spirit of the presbyterate, but bestow on them earnestness and piety until the end.

21 For the deacons let us beseech, that the Lord may grant unto them to run a perfect course, and to perfect holiness, and that He may remember their work and their love.

22 For the presbyteresses let us beseech, that the Lord may hear their supplications and keep their hearts perfectly in the grace of the Spirit and help their work.

23 For the subdeacons, readers, deaconesses let us beseech, that the Lord may grant to them to receive a reward in patience.

24 For the faithful laymen let us beseech, that the Lord may grant unto them to keep the faith perfectly.

25 For the catechumens let us beseech, that the Lord may grant unto them to be counted worthy of the laver of forgiveness, and may sanctify them with the seal of holiness.

26 For the kingdom let us beseech, that the Lord may bestow upon it tranquillity.

27 For the exalted powers let us beseech, that the Lord may grant to them prudence and the fear of Him.

28 For all the world let us beseech, that the Lord may provide for each one such things as are meet.

29 For those who travel by sea, and those who go on journeys let us beseech, that the Lord may guide them with the right hand of mercy.

30 For those who are persecuted let us beseech, that the Lord may grant to them patience and knowledge, and may bestow on them also a completed labour.

31 For those who have fallen asleep from the Church let us beseech, that the Lord may bestow upon them a place of rest.

32 For those who have fallen let us beseech, that the Lord may not remember their follies unto them, but moderate [His] threats unto them.

33 And let us all also, who need prayer, beseech that the Lord may protect and keep us with the peaceful Spirit.

34 Let us persuade and beseech the Lord, that He may receive our prayers.

35 After the deacon commemorateth, let the bishop make a sign with his hand.

36 Let the deacon say: Let us arise in the Holy Ghost, that, being made wise, we may grow in His grace, boasting in His Name ; being built on the foundation of the Apostles,’ let us beg [and] beseech the Lord that, being persuaded, He may receive our prayers.

37 Then let the bishop complete [the prayer]. And let the people say: Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The deacon is to be in everything as the eye of the Church, with fear admonishing, so that he may be an example to the people of piety, and he is to admonish thus: Let us arise; Let every one know his place; Let the catechumens depart; Lift up the eyes of your hearts; The angels are looking on; Let us beseech in concord; Let no fornicator, no wrathful man remain, and if one who is a servant of evil be here, let him withdraw — in the name of our Lord and our God and our Saviour Jesus Christ. When the presbyter or bishop begins the prayer, the people pray and kneel. Then the deacon says the long litany of beseeching: for the peace from heaven, that the Lord in His mercy may give us peace; for our faith, that the Lord may grant us to keep truly to the end the faith in Him; for harmony and concord, that the Lord may keep us together in concord of the Spirit; for patience, that the Lord may bestow patience to the end in all afflictions; for the Apostles, that the Lord may grant us to please Him as they pleased Him and make us worthy of their inheritance; for the holy prophets, that the Lord may number us with them; for the holy confessors, that the Lord God may grant us to fulfil our course with the same mind as they; for the bishop, that the Lord may grant him to us for length of days in faith, rightly dividing the word of truth and standing at the head of the Church purely and without blame; for the presbyterate, that the Lord may not take away from them the spirit of the presbyterate but bestow earnestness and piety until the end; for the deacons, that the Lord may grant them to run a perfect course and perfect holiness, and remember their work and love; for the presbyteresses, that the Lord may hear their supplications and keep their hearts perfectly in the grace of the Spirit and help their work; for the subdeacons, readers and deaconesses, that the Lord may grant them to receive a reward in patience; for the faithful laymen, that the Lord may grant them to keep the faith perfectly; for the catechumens, that the Lord may grant them to be counted worthy of the laver of forgiveness and sanctify them with the seal of holiness; for the kingdom, that the Lord may bestow tranquillity on it; for the exalted powers, that the Lord may grant them prudence and the fear of Him; for all the world, that the Lord may provide for each one such things as are meet; for those who travel by sea and go on journeys, that the Lord may guide them with the right hand of mercy; for the persecuted, that the Lord may grant them patience and knowledge and bestow a completed labour; for those fallen asleep from the Church, that the Lord may bestow a place of rest; for those who have fallen, that the Lord may not remember their follies to them but moderate His threats to them; and for all of us who need prayer, that the Lord may protect and keep us with the peaceful Spirit — persuading and beseeching the Lord that He may receive our prayers. After the deacon commemorates, the bishop makes a sign with his hand; the deacon says, Let us arise in the Holy Ghost, that being made wise we may grow in His grace, boasting in His Name, being built on the foundation of the Apostles, and beg and beseech the Lord that, being persuaded, He may receive our prayers; then the bishop completes the prayer, and the people say Amen.
