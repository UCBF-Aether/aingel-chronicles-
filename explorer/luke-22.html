The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the feast of unleavened bread drew nigh, which is called the Passover.

2 And the chief priests and scribes sought how they might kill him; for they feared the people.

3 Then entered Satan into Judas surnamed Iscariot, being of the number of the twelve.

4 And he went his way, and communed with the chief priests and captains, how he might betray him unto them.

5 And they were glad, and covenanted to give him money.

6 And he promised, and sought opportunity to betray him unto them in the absence of the multitude.

7 Then came the day of unleavened bread, when the passover must be killed.

8 And he sent Peter and John, saying, Go and prepare us the passover, that we may eat.

9 And they said unto him, Where wilt thou that we prepare?

10 And he said unto them, Behold, when ye are entered into the city, there shall a man meet you, bearing a pitcher of water; follow him into the house where he entereth in.

11 And ye shall say unto the goodman of the house, The Master saith unto thee, Where is the guestchamber, where I shall eat the passover with my disciples?

12 And he shall shew you a large upper room furnished: there make ready.

13 And they went, and found as he had said unto them: and they made ready the passover.

14 And when the hour was come, he sat down, and the twelve apostles with him.

15 And he said unto them, With desire I have desired to eat this passover with you before I suffer:

16 For I say unto you, I will not any more eat thereof, until it be fulfilled in the kingdom of God.

17 And he took the cup, and gave thanks, and said, Take this, and divide it among yourselves:

18 For I say unto you, I will not drink of the fruit of the vine, until the kingdom of God shall come.

19 And he took bread, and gave thanks, and brake it, and gave unto them, saying, This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me.

20 Likewise also the cup after supper, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you.

21 But, behold, the hand of him that betrayeth me is with me on the table.

22 And truly the Son of man goeth, as it was determined: but woe unto that man by whom he is betrayed!

23 And they began to enquire among themselves, which of them it was that should do this thing.

24 And there was also a strife among them, which of them should be accounted the greatest.

25 And he said unto them, The kings of the Gentiles exercise lordship over them; and they that exercise authority upon them are called benefactors.

26 But ye shall not be so: but he that is greatest among you, let him be as the younger; and he that is chief, as he that doth serve.

27 For whether is greater, he that sitteth at meat, or he that serveth? is not he that sitteth at meat? but I am among you as he that serveth.

28 Ye are they which have continued with me in my temptations.

29 And I appoint unto you a kingdom, as my Father hath appointed unto me;

30 That ye may eat and drink at my table in my kingdom, and sit on thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel.

31 And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat:

32 But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.

33 And he said unto him, Lord, I am ready to go with thee, both into prison, and to death.

34 And he said, I tell thee, Peter, the cock shall not crow this day, before that thou shalt thrice deny that thou knowest me.

35 And he said unto them, When I sent you without purse, and scrip, and shoes, lacked ye any thing? And they said, Nothing.

36 Then said he unto them, But now, he that hath a purse, let him take it, and likewise his scrip: and he that hath no sword, let him sell his garment, and buy one.

37 For I say unto you, that this that is written must yet be accomplished in me, And he was reckoned among the transgressors: for the things concerning me have an end.

38 And they said, Lord, behold, here are two swords. And he said unto them, It is enough.

39 And he came out, and went, as he was wont, to the mount of Olives; and his disciples also followed him.

40 And when he was at the place, he said unto them, Pray that ye enter not into temptation.

41 And he was withdrawn from them about a stone’s cast, and kneeled down, and prayed,

42 Saying, Father, if thou be willing, remove this cup from me: nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done.

43 And there appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him.

44 And being in an agony he prayed more earnestly: and his sweat was as it were great drops of blood falling down to the ground.

45 And when he rose up from prayer, and was come to his disciples, he found them sleeping for sorrow,

46 And said unto them, Why sleep ye? rise and pray, lest ye enter into temptation.

47 And while he yet spake, behold a multitude, and he that was called Judas, one of the twelve, went before them, and drew near unto Jesus to kiss him.

48 But Jesus said unto him, Judas, betrayest thou the Son of man with a kiss?

49 When they which were about him saw what would follow, they said unto him, Lord, shall we smite with the sword?

50 And one of them smote the servant of the high priest, and cut off his right ear.

51 And Jesus answered and said, Suffer ye thus far. And he touched his ear, and healed him.

52 Then Jesus said unto the chief priests, and captains of the temple, and the elders, which were come to him, Be ye come out, as against a thief, with swords and staves?

53 When I was daily with you in the temple, ye stretched forth no hands against me: but this is your hour, and the power of darkness.

54 Then took they him, and led him, and brought him into the high priest’s house. And Peter followed afar off.

55 And when they had kindled a fire in the midst of the hall, and were set down together, Peter sat down among them.

56 But a certain maid beheld him as he sat by the fire, and earnestly looked upon him, and said, This man was also with him.

57 And he denied him, saying, Woman, I know him not.

58 And after a little while another saw him, and said, Thou art also of them. And Peter said, Man, I am not.

59 And about the space of one hour after another confidently affirmed, saying, Of a truth this fellow also was with him: for he is a Galilaean.

60 And Peter said, Man, I know not what thou sayest. And immediately, while he yet spake, the cock crew.

61 And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.

62 And Peter went out, and wept bitterly.

63 And the men that held Jesus mocked him, and smote him.

64 And when they had blindfolded him, they struck him on the face, and asked him, saying, Prophesy, who is it that smote thee?

65 And many other things blasphemously spake they against him.

66 And as soon as it was day, the elders of the people and the chief priests and the scribes came together, and led him into their council, saying,

67 Art thou the Christ? tell us. And he said unto them, If I tell you, ye will not believe:

68 And if I also ask you, ye will not answer me, nor let me go.

69 Hereafter shall the Son of man sit on the right hand of the power of God.

70 Then said they all, Art thou then the Son of God? And he said unto them, Ye say that I am.

71 And they said, What need we any further witness? for we ourselves have heard of his own mouth.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

As Passover draws near, the chief priests plot, Judas bargains to betray Jesus, and the Passover is prepared (vv. 1–13). At supper Jesus gives bread as his body and the cup as the new testament in his blood, predicts betrayal and Peter’s denial, and teaches that the greatest must be as the youngest and as one who serves (vv. 14–38). In Gethsemane he prays, is strengthened by an angel, and is arrested with a kiss (vv. 39–53); Peter denies him three times before the cock crows, and Jesus is mocked, blindfolded, and brought before the council, where he confesses himself the Son of God (vv. 54–71). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 22

Henry notes Christ knew all men and had wise and holy ends in taking Judas as disciple, yet how one who knew Christ so well came to betray him is told here: Satan entered into Judas.   • Verse 6. He promised, and sought opportunity to betray him in the absence of the multitude. Opportunity sought is absence of the multitude, whose presence restrains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Then came the day of unleavened bread, when the passover must be killed. The day of killing the passover arrives, and the Lamb's own killing approaches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 10. Behold, when ye are entered into the city, there shall a man meet you, bearing a pitcher of water; follow him into the house where he enters in. Henry notes those who go upon Christ's word need not fear disappointment, and according to orders given, the disciples got all ready. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Ye shall say unto the goodman of the house, The Master says unto thee, Where is the guestchamber, where I shall eat the passover with my disciples? The Master's question claims the room. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. He shall show you a large upper room furnished: there make ready. A large furnished upper room awaits, already prepared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. They went, and found as he had said unto them, and they made ready the passover. Finding as said, they make ready. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. When the hour was come, he sat down, and the twelve apostles with him. The hour, not merely the day, governs the sitting down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. With desire I have desired to eat this passover with you before I suffer. Henry notes Jesus bids this passover welcome and desired it, though he knew sufferings would follow, because it was in order to his Father's glory and man's redemption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. For I say unto you, I will not any more eat thereof, until it be fulfilled in the kingdom of God. This passover is his last until fulfilment in the kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. He took the cup, and gave thanks, and said, Take this, and divide it among yourselves. The cup is shared among them, divided, not drunk alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. For I say unto you, I will not drink of the fruit of the vine, until the kingdom of God shall come. Henry notes he takes leave of all passovers, signifying doing away with ceremonial ordinances, of which passover was earliest and chief, because now in God's kingdom the substance was come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. He took bread, gave thanks, broke it, and gave unto them, saying, This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me. Henry notes the supper is a sign and memorial of Christ already come, who by dying delivered us, and the breaking of his body as sacrifice is brought to remembrance by breaking of bread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Likewise also the cup after supper, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you. Henry notes the shedding of blood, by which atonement was made, is represented by the wine, and nothing is more nourishing to the soul than the doctrine of Christ's atonement and assurance of interest in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. But behold, the hand of him that betrays me is with me on the table. The betrayer's hand shares the table with the betrayed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And truly the Son of man goes, as it was determined, but woe unto that man by whom he is betrayed! Determination does not remove woe from the betrayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. They began to enquire among themselves, which of them it was that should do this thing. The table turns to self-enquiry, each fearing himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. There was also a strife among them, which of them should be accounted the greatest. Henry notes how unbecoming worldly ambition of being greatest is to a follower of Jesus, who took the form of a servant and humbled himself to the cross's death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The kings of the Gentiles exercise lordship over them, and they that exercise authority upon them are called benefactors. Gentile lordship and its flattering title are the pattern rejected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. But ye shall not be so, but he that is greatest among you, let him be as the younger, and he that is chief, as he that does serve. Greatness is redefined as youth and service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. For whether is greater, he that sits at meat, or he that serves? Is not he that sits at meat? But I am among you as he that serves. The greater by custom sits, but Christ among them serves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Ye are they which have continued with me in my temptations. Continuance through temptations is credited to the disputing disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And I appoint unto you a kingdom, as my Father has appointed unto me. Appointment passes from Father to Son to disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. That ye may eat and drink at my table in my kingdom, and sit on thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel. Table and thrones together: fellowship and judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan has desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat. Henry notes in the way to happiness we must expect to be assaulted and sifted by Satan, who if he cannot destroy will try to disgrace or distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not, and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren. Henry notes if believers were left to themselves they would fall, but they are kept by God's power and Christ's prayer; and Peter, converted, must strengthen brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And he said, Lord, I am ready to go with thee, both into prison, and to death. Readiness is professed for prison and death together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. And he said, I tell thee, Peter, the cock shall not crow this day, before thou shalt thrice deny that thou knowest me. Henry warns nothing more certainly forebodes a fall than self-confidence with disregard to warnings and contempt of danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. When I sent you without purse, scrip, and shoes, lacked ye anything? And they said, Nothing. The earlier mission lacked nothing, proving provision without purse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. But now, he that has a purse, let him take it, and likewise his scrip, and he that has no sword, let him sell his garment, and buy one. Henry notes a very great change of circumstances approaches: friends will not be as kind as before, enemies more fierce, so purse and scrip are now needed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. For I say unto you, that this that is written must yet be accomplished in me, And he was reckoned among the transgressors, for the things concerning me have an end. Henry notes the apostles understood real weapons, but he spoke only of spiritual warfare's weapons, the sword of the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. And they said, Lord, behold, here are two swords. And he said, It is enough. Two swords are enough to close the discussion, not to arm a revolt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. He came out, and went, as he was wont, to the mount of Olives, and his disciples also followed him. Custom leads him to Olivet, even on this night. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. When he was at the place, he said unto them, Pray that ye enter not into temptation. The garden's first word is prayer against temptation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. He was withdrawn from them about a stone's cast, and kneeled down, and prayed. Withdrawal by a stone's cast, kneeling, praying: the agony begins in posture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Saying, Father, if thou be willing, remove this cup from me: nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done. Henry notes every description of his state of mind proves the tremendous nature of the assault and his perfect foreknowledge of its terrors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. There appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him. Henry notes this as one of three things peculiar to Luke: an angel strengthening him, part of his humiliation to be strengthened by a ministering spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Being in an agony he prayed more earnestly, and his sweat was as it were great drops of blood falling down to the ground. Henry notes the second and third peculiar things: in agony he prayed more earnestly, and his sweat was as great drops of blood, showing the travail of his soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. When he rose up from prayer, and was come to his disciples, he found them sleeping for sorrow. Sorrow, not indifference, sleeps the disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. And said, Why sleep ye? Rise and pray, lest ye enter into temptation. Henry warns unless we watch and pray always, we may be drawn in the day into sins we in the morning most resolved against. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. While he yet spoke, behold a multitude, and he that was called Judas, one of the twelve, went before them, and drew near unto Jesus to kiss him. Henry notes nothing is a greater affront to Jesus than to be betrayed by professed followers who say they love him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. But Jesus said, Judas, betrayest thou the Son of man with a kiss? The kiss, sign of love, is named as the betrayal's instrument. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. When they which were about him saw what would follow, they said, Lord, shall we smite with the sword? The disciples ask for sword action at the arrest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. One of them smote the servant of the high priest, and cut off his right ear. The sword asked for is used at once, on the high priest's servant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. And Jesus answered, Suffer ye thus far. And he touched his ear, and healed him. Henry notes Jesus gave an illustrious example of doing good to those that hate us, as afterwards of praying for those that despitefully use us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Then Jesus said unto the chief priests, captains of the temple, and elders, which were come to him, Be ye come out, as against a thief, with swords and staves? The arrest's manner is questioned: swords and staves, as against a thief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. When I was daily with you in the temple, ye stretched forth no hands against me, but this is your hour, and the power of darkness. Henry notes Christ was willing to wait for triumphs till warfare was accomplished, and the hour and power of darkness were short, as wicked triumphs always will be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. Then took they him, and led him, and brought him into the high priest's house. And Peter followed afar off. Following afar off begins Peter's fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. When they had kindled a fire in the midst of the hall, and were set down together, Peter sat down among them. The fire's circle becomes the place of denial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. But a certain maid beheld him as he sat by the fire, and earnestly looked upon him, and said, This man was also with him. A maid's earnest look names him as with Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 57. And he denied him, saying, Woman, I know him not. Henry notes Peter's fall was denying he knew Christ and was his disciple, disowning him because of distress and danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 58. After a little while another saw him, and said, Thou art also of them. And Peter said, Man, I am not. The second denial follows after a little while, to another witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 59. About the space of one hour after, another confidently affirmed, Of a truth this fellow also was with him, for he is a Galilaean. An hour later, Galilean speech confirms what Peter denies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 60. And Peter said, Man, I know not what thou sayest. And immediately, while he yet spoke, the cock crew. The third denial is cut short by the cock's crow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 61. And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. Henry lists the look's sixfold character: convincing, chiding, expostulating, compassionate, directing, and significant, conveying grace to enable repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 62. And Peter went out, and wept bitterly. Henry notes grace works in and by the word, bringing it to mind and setting it home on conscience, giving the soul its happy turn; and it was not the mere look, but divine grace with it, that restored Peter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 63. And the men that held Jesus mocked him, and smote him. Mocking and smiting follow Peter's weeping, in the same house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 64. When they had blindfolded him, they struck him on the face, and asked, Prophesy, who is it that smote thee? Blindfolded prophecy is demanded as a game. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 65. And many other things blasphemously spoke they against him. Mockery multiplies into many blasphemous speeches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 66. As soon as it was day, the elders, chief priests, and scribes came together, and led him into their council, saying, Art thou the Christ? Tell us. Daylight brings the council and its direct question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 67. Art thou the Christ? Tell us. And he said, If I tell you, ye will not believe. The answer names their unbelief before their verdict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 68. And if I also ask you, ye will not answer me, nor let me go. Questions from him would meet neither answer nor release. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 69. Hereafter shall the Son of man sit on the right hand of the power of God. The future seat at God's right hand is the proof offered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 70. Then said they all, Art thou then the Son of God? And he said, Ye say that I am. Henry notes he owns himself Son of God, though he knew he should suffer for it, and upon this they ground his condemnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 71. And they said, What need we any further witness? For we ourselves have heard of his own mouth. His own mouth becomes their witness, and they need no further. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Investigation (editorial — a parallel examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a possible parallel. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

Supper, arrest, and denial — Luke 22, Mark 14, and John 18

Luke 22:14–54, Mark 14:22–50, and John 18:1–14 agree on the sequence: a final supper, prayer in a garden place, Judas leading a multitude, Jesus taken, and Peter following afar off. The differences are in emphasis: Luke alone records “this do in remembrance of me,” the dispute about greatness answered by service, and an angel strengthening Jesus; Mark records the hymn sung before the Mount of Olives and Peter’s denial in the high priest’s courtyard; John records no bread-and-cup words at supper but footwashing (John 13), and at the arrest Jesus’ “I am he” felling the band and his being led to Annas first. How these accounts combine historically is not settled by the sources consulted for this page.
