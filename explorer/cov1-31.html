Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After ordination the presbyter is to be constant at the altar, making prayers laboriously without ceasing, sometimes resting alone in some house from the things of the Lord's house but not ceasing or diminishing one hour from prayers. He is to fast three days each week all the year — that he may be perfected in intelligence, and moreover according to his strength — not wandering about and going hither and thither with every spirit, but doing everything with energy. If it be revealed to a presbyter or bishop to speak, let him speak; if not, let him not neglect and despise his work. If it be revealed to him to visit his parishes and speak the word, let him go; if not, let him entreat God with supplication, and if revealed to speak, let him speak, always taking the burden and load of Him who was crucified for him and praying for all the people. A presbyter or bishop is not to be anxious about food or raiment, for God takes thought and cares for His own in the things He knows; if, receiving food or clothing from one person, he is told he should receive also from another, it suffices to receive from the first alone, and only so far as is fitting and as he needs, not to excess. In firmness of faith he is always to be unchangeable, for such God desires; he is to prove the heart of each one, lest evil kept and buried within make him a stranger to the grace of God. He is not to neglect or despise those who do good works through teaching, but to watch for signs in them — sighs, weeping, earnest conversations, silence, sadness, patience, humble bowing of the head — and weeping and groaning best train and cause suffering. Their work is watching, continence, fasting, quietness, unceasing prayer, meditation, faith, meekness, philanthropy, labour, weariness, love, subjection, goodness, gravity and every work of light. The signs of those who do not bring forth the fruits of life are sloth, love of pleasure, eyes wandering in all directions, disobedience, complaining, restlessness and a laziness that will not move; their work is gluttony, debauchery, anger, unbelief, idle and unseasonable laughter, confusion, neglect, error, disturbance, wantonness, love of gains, love of money, envy, contention, drunkenness, high-mindedness, vain talking, love of praise and every work of darkness. He is to recognise such products, speak to those who are worthy, and not waste time on those who do not receive his teaching, for those who sow on earth without fruits shall reap miseries. As is right and fitting, he is to go about to the houses of the sick with the deacon and visit them, consider and say fitting and proper things, especially to the faithful, and exhort that the sick who are poor be helped by the Church, so that those who do deeds of kindness may enter into the joy of their Lord. He is to confirm those newly become catechumens with prophetical and evangelical utterances, with the word of teaching, and not neglect his prayers, for he is the figure of the archangels — but let him know that God did not spare the angels who sinned. He is to fast, and if proper receive of the cup, wine sufficing him only as much as in his judgment profits and helps him, lest the drink given for healing he receive to his loss; in sickness let him eat herbs and fish and have care for his work. In everything the priest is to be an example to the faithful of the work of holiness, and the presbyter is to praise and give thanks in the same way as the bishop.
