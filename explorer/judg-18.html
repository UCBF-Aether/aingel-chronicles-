The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In those days there was no king in Israel: and in those days the tribe of the Danites sought them an inheritance to dwell in; for unto that day all their inheritance had not fallen unto them among the tribes of Israel.

2 And the children of Dan sent of their family five men from their coasts, men of valour, from Zorah, and from Eshtaol, to spy out the land, and to search it; and they said unto them, Go, search the land: who when they came to mount Ephraim, to the house of Micah, they lodged there.

3 When they were by the house of Micah, they knew the voice of the young man the Levite: and they turned in thither, and said unto him, Who brought thee hither? and what makest thou in this place? and what hast thou here?

4 And he said unto them, Thus and thus dealeth Micah with me, and hath hired me, and I am his priest.

5 And they said unto him, Ask counsel, we pray thee, of God, that we may know whether our way which we go shall be prosperous.

6 And the priest said unto them, Go in peace: before the LORD is your way wherein ye go.

7 Then the five men departed, and came to Laish, and saw the people that were therein, how they dwelt careless, after the manner of the Zidonians, quiet and secure; and there was no magistrate in the land, that might put them to shame in any thing; and they were far from the Zidonians, and had no business with any man.

8 And they came unto their brethren to Zorah and Eshtaol: and their brethren said unto them, What say ye?

9 And they said, Arise, that we may go up against them: for we have seen the land, and, behold, it is very good: and are ye still? be not slothful to go, and to enter to possess the land.

10 When ye go, ye shall come unto a people secure, and to a large land: for God hath given it into your hands; a place where there is no want of any thing that is in the earth.

11 And there went from thence of the family of the Danites, out of Zorah and out of Eshtaol, six hundred men appointed with weapons of war.

12 And they went up, and pitched in Kirjathjearim, in Judah: wherefore they called that place Mahanehdan unto this day: behold, it is behind Kirjathjearim.

13 And they passed thence unto mount Ephraim, and came unto the house of Micah.

14 Then answered the five men that went to spy out the country of Laish, and said unto their brethren, Do ye know that there is in these houses an ephod, and teraphim, and a graven image, and a molten image? now therefore consider what ye have to do.

15 And they turned thitherward, and came to the house of the young man the Levite, even unto the house of Micah, and saluted him.

16 And the six hundred men appointed with their weapons of war, which were of the children of Dan, stood by the entering of the gate.

17 And the five men that went to spy out the land went up, and came in thither, and took the graven image, and the ephod, and the teraphim, and the molten image: and the priest stood in the entering of the gate with the six hundred men that were appointed with weapons of war.

18 And these went into Micah’s house, and fetched the carved image, the ephod, and the teraphim, and the molten image. Then said the priest unto them, What do ye?

19 And they said unto him, Hold thy peace, lay thine hand upon thy mouth, and go with us, and be to us a father and a priest: is it better for thee to be a priest unto the house of one man, or that thou be a priest unto a tribe and a family in Israel?

20 And the priest’s heart was glad, and he took the ephod, and the teraphim, and the graven image, and went in the midst of the people.

21 So they turned and departed, and put the little ones and the cattle and the carriage before them.

22 And when they were a good way from the house of Micah, the men that were in the houses near to Micah’s house were gathered together, and overtook the children of Dan.

23 And they cried unto the children of Dan. And they turned their faces, and said unto Micah, What aileth thee, that thou comest with such a company?

24 And he said, Ye have taken away my gods which I made, and the priest, and ye are gone away: and what have I more? and what is this that ye say unto me, What aileth thee?

25 And the children of Dan said unto him, Let not thy voice be heard among us, lest angry fellows run upon thee, and thou lose thy life, with the lives of thy household.

26 And the children of Dan went their way: and when Micah saw that they were too strong for him, he turned and went back unto his house.

27 And they took the things which Micah had made, and the priest which he had, and came unto Laish, unto a people that were at quiet and secure: and they smote them with the edge of the sword, and burnt the city with fire.

28 And there was no deliverer, because it was far from Zidon, and they had no business with any man; and it was in the valley that lieth by Bethrehob. And they built a city, and dwelt therein.

29 And they called the name of the city Dan, after the name of Dan their father, who was born unto Israel: howbeit the name of the city was Laish at the first.

30 And the children of Dan set up the graven image: and Jonathan, the son of Gershom, the son of Manasseh, he and his sons were priests to the tribe of Dan until the day of the captivity of the land.

31 And they set them up Micah’s graven image, which he made, all the time that the house of God was in Shiloh.

  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The Danites, still seeking an inheritance, send five spies who lodge at Micah's house, recognise the young Levite's voice, and ask him to enquire of God for their way. Finding Laish dwelling careless, quiet and secure, far from Zidon with no deliverer, they return, and six hundred armed Danites march back through Mount Ephraim, steal Micah's graven image, ephod, teraphim and molten image, and persuade the priest to go with them as father and priest to a tribe rather than to one man's house. Laish is smitten, burned and rebuilt as Dan, and Micah's image is set up there all the time the house of God is in Shiloh. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

  • Verse 1. In those days there was no king in Israel, and the Danites sought an inheritance to dwell in, since their lot had not yet fallen to them, and Henry notes one family of Dan, whose lot included distant Laish, had sponged upon their brethren rather than go far to provide for themselves, until necessity aroused them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Dan sent five valiant men from Zorah and Eshtaol to spy out the land, and when they came to Mount Ephraim they lodged at Micah's house, and Henry notes it is prudent to look before leaping, and Dan had both the serpent's subtlety and the lion's whelp's courage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. By Micah's house they knew the young Levite's voice, turned in, and asked who brought him there, what he did, and what he had there, recognising by voice a man they had known in his rambles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. He told them Micah had hired him and he was his priest, explaining the business and encouragement of his strange preferment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They asked him to ask counsel of God whether their way would be prosperous, and Henry notes their carelessness: they would not have enquired of the Lord at all if the Levite's mention of teraphim had not put it into their heads, and they had passed by Shiloh without enquiring of God's high priest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The priest said, Go in peace, for before the Lord is your way, and Henry notes he humoured the consultation so well they believed he had an answer from God, while true care should be that our way be such as God approves, and then we may go in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The five men came to Laish and saw its people dwelling careless, quiet, and secure after the manner of the Zidonians, with no magistrate to put them to shame, far from Zidon, and with no business with any man, and Henry calls it a place ill governed and ill guarded, and therefore an easy prey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. They returned to their brethren at Zorah and Eshtaol, who asked what they said, and the report that follows was the spies' answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. They urged, Arise, let us go up against them, for the land is very good, and they chided their brethren's sloth, and Henry notes the spies were not evil spies in this, representing the place as desirable and attainable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. When they went they would come to a secure people and a large land God had given into their hands, a place lacking nothing, and Henry applies it: heaven is a very good land God has given into our hands, and we must not be slothful in laying hold on eternal life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Six hundred Danites appointed with weapons of war went from Zorah and Eshtaol, and Henry notes this was but a hundredth part of the tribe, which had numbered above sixty-four thousand, showing how little public spirit yet appeared in Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. They pitched in Kirjath-jearim in Judah, and called the place Mahaneh-dan, the camp of Dan, unto this day, military encampments being then so rare that the night's resting place kept the name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. They passed on to Mount Ephraim and came to Micah's house, where the march paused and the second half of the story begins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The five spies told their brethren that in these houses there were an ephod, teraphim, a graven image, and a molten image, and bade them consider what to do, and Henry notes they thought the gods worth stealing, if masters of these gods they might prosper and master Laish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. They turned aside to the young Levite's house at Micah's and saluted him, while the six hundred stood by the gate, so that the priest was kept in talk while the theft was done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The six hundred appointed with weapons stood by the entering of the gate, a show of force that kept the priest occupied and the household overawed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The five spies went in and took the graven image, the ephod, the teraphim, and the molten image, while the priest stood at the gate with the six hundred, and Henry asks how gods that could not keep themselves from being stolen should protect those who stole them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. When they fetched the carved image, ephod, teraphim, and molten image from Micah's house, the priest asked what they were doing, and Henry notes the sorry priest, sauntering at the gate, had let his treasure go, and the Danites' idolatry began in theft, breaking the eighth commandment to break the second. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. They told him to hold his peace, lay his hand on his mouth, and go with them to be father and priest to a tribe and family rather than to one man's house, and Henry notes they tempted him with better preferment, more dependants, sacrifices, and fees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The priest's heart was glad, and he took the ephod, teraphim, and graven image and went in the midst of the people, and Henry notes a little persuasion served his rambling fancy, covetousness, and ambition, and that if ten shekels won him, eleven would lose him, for the hireling flees because he is a hireling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. They turned and departed, putting the little ones, cattle, and carriage before them, so confident of success that families and effects marched in the van. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. When they were a good way from Micah's house, the men of the neighbouring houses gathered and overtook the Danites, mustering to recover the stolen gods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. They cried to the Danites, who turned and asked Micah what ailed him that he came with such a company, the rear guard turning to face the pursuers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Micah said they had taken away the gods he had made and the priest, and asked what he had left, and Henry notes the folly of calling gods what he himself had made, and the spiritual warning that whatever we say What have we more of, we have made an idol. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The Danites answered that he should not let his voice be heard, lest angry fellows run upon him and he lose his life and his household's, justifying robbery with a threat of murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The Danites went their way, and Micah, seeing they were too strong for him, turned back to his house, and Henry notes he had not courage to venture his life for gods he knew could not protect him, and if the loss cured him of loving idols, it was unspeakable gain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. They took Micah's things and his priest, came to Laish, to a quiet and secure people, smote them with the sword, and burnt the city with fire, and Henry notes many are brought to destruction by security, since Satan gains advantage when we are careless and off our watch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. There was no deliverer, because Laish was far from Zidon and had no business with any man, and it lay in the valley by Beth-rehob, and they built a city and dwelt there, rebuilding what they had burned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. They called the city Dan, after Dan their father, though its first name was Laish, and Henry notes the name witnessed that, though far separated, they were Danites by birth, and we should likewise preserve the remembrance of our relation to God's Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The children of Dan set up the graven image, and Jonathan son of Gershom, son of Manasseh, he and his sons were priests to Dan until the day of the captivity, so that the stolen idolatry gained a settled priesthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. They set up Micah's graven image all the time the house of God was in Shiloh, and Henry's chapter heading states the point plainly: how great a matter a little fire kindles, when idolatry creeps from one family into a tribe and gains settlement in a city of note. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
