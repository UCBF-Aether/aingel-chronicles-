The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 When Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses’ father in law, heard of all that God had done for Moses, and for Israel his people, and that the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt;

2 Then Jethro, Moses’ father in law, took Zipporah, Moses’ wife, after he had sent her back,

3 And her two sons; of which the name of the one was Gershom; for he said, I have been an alien in a strange land:

4 And the name of the other was Eliezer; for the God of my father, said he, was mine help, and delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh:

5 And Jethro, Moses’ father in law, came with his sons and his wife unto Moses into the wilderness, where he encamped at the mount of God:

6 And he said unto Moses, I thy father in law Jethro am come unto thee, and thy wife, and her two sons with her.

7 And Moses went out to meet his father in law, and did obeisance, and kissed him; and they asked each other of their welfare; and they came into the tent.

8 And Moses told his father in law all that the LORD had done unto Pharaoh and to the Egyptians for Israel’s sake, and all the travail that had come upon them by the way, and how the LORD delivered them.

9 And Jethro rejoiced for all the goodness which the LORD had done to Israel, whom he had delivered out of the hand of the Egyptians.

10 And Jethro said, Blessed be the LORD, who hath delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians, and out of the hand of Pharaoh, who hath delivered the people from under the hand of the Egyptians.

11 Now I know that the LORD is greater than all gods: for in the thing wherein they dealt proudly he was above them.

12 And Jethro, Moses’ father in law, took a burnt offering and sacrifices for God: and Aaron came, and all the elders of Israel, to eat bread with Moses’ father in law before God.

13 And it came to pass on the morrow, that Moses sat to judge the people: and the people stood by Moses from the morning unto the evening.

14 And when Moses’ father in law saw all that he did to the people, he said, What is this thing that thou doest to the people? why sittest thou thyself alone, and all the people stand by thee from morning unto even?

15 And Moses said unto his father in law, Because the people come unto me to enquire of God:

16 When they have a matter, they come unto me; and I judge between one and another, and I do make them know the statutes of God, and his laws.

17 And Moses’ father in law said unto him, The thing that thou doest is not good.

18 Thou wilt surely wear away, both thou, and this people that is with thee: for this thing is too heavy for thee; thou art not able to perform it thyself alone.

19 Hearken now unto my voice, I will give thee counsel, and God shall be with thee: Be thou for the people to God-ward, that thou mayest bring the causes unto God:

20 And thou shalt teach them ordinances and laws, and shalt shew them the way wherein they must walk, and the work that they must do.

21 Moreover thou shalt provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness; and place such over them, to be rulers of thousands, and rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens:

22 And let them judge the people at all seasons: and it shall be, that every great matter they shall bring unto thee, but every small matter they shall judge: so shall it be easier for thyself, and they shall bear the burden with thee.

23 If thou shalt do this thing, and God command thee so, then thou shalt be able to endure, and all this people shall also go to their place in peace.

24 So Moses hearkened to the voice of his father in law, and did all that he had said.

25 And Moses chose able men out of all Israel, and made them heads over the people, rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens.

26 And they judged the people at all seasons: the hard causes they brought unto Moses, but every small matter they judged themselves.

27 And Moses let his father in law depart; and he went his way into his own land.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jethro brings Moses' wife Zipporah and their two sons back to him at the mount of God, hears all the LORD has done, and offers sacrifice and rejoices. Seeing Moses judge the people alone from morning to evening, Jethro advises him to appoint able, God-fearing men as rulers over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens, keeping only the great matters for himself. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When Jethro, priest of Midian and Moses's father-in-law, heard of all God had done for Moses and Israel, and that the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt, he set out to join them. Henry notes Jethro came to rejoice with Moses in Israel's happiness, and to bring his family to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jethro took Zipporah, Moses's wife, after he had sent her back. Henry observes Moses must have his family with him, that while he ruled the church of God, his own house might be in order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Her two sons came with her, of whom one was named Gershom, for Moses had said, I have been an alien in a strange land. Henry notes the name kept Moses's exile before him even in his new calling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The name of the other was Eliezer, for the God of my father, said Moses, was mine help, and delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh. Henry observes the second name recorded the deliverance: God had been a help against Pharaoh's sword. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Jethro came with his sons and his wife unto Moses into the wilderness, where he encamped at the mount of God. Henry notes the meeting place mattered: the family was reunited at the very mountain where God had called Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Jethro sent word to Moses, I thy father-in-law Jethro am come unto thee, and thy wife, and her two sons with her. Henry observes the message was simple and affectionate: the father-in-law came not to inspect but to join. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Moses went out to meet his father-in-law, and did obeisance, and kissed him, and they asked each other of their welfare, and came into the tent. Henry notes the courtesy: the lawgiver bowed to his elder, and welfare was asked before business began. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Moses told his father-in-law all that the LORD had done unto Pharaoh and the Egyptians for Israel's sake, and all the travail that had come upon them by the way, and how the LORD delivered them. Henry observes conversation concerning God's wondrous works is good and edifies, and Moses told the whole story without sparing the hardships. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Jethro rejoiced for all the goodness which the LORD had done to Israel, whom he had delivered out of the hand of the Egyptians. Henry notes standers-by were sometimes more affected by God's favours to Israel than many were who received them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jethro said, Blessed be the LORD, who hath delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians and out of the hand of Pharaoh, and delivered the people from under the hand of the Egyptians. Henry notes Jethro gave the glory to Israel's God: whatever we have the joy of, God must have the praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Now I know that the LORD is greater than all gods, for in the thing wherein they dealt proudly he was above them. Henry observes the confession was grounded in the plagues: Egypt's gods were proud, and the LORD proved above them in the very thing of their pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Jethro took a burnt offering and sacrifices for God, and Aaron came, and all the elders of Israel, to eat bread with Moses's father-in-law before God. Henry notes mutual friendship is sanctified by joint worship, and the feast was temperate: they ate bread, even manna, and though a Gentile, Jethro was welcome to the bread from heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. On the morrow Moses sat to judge the people, and the people stood by Moses from the morning unto the evening. Henry observes the great zeal and toil of Moses as magistrate: having redeemed Israel, he was now their lawgiver and judge, and the meanest Israelite was welcome to bring his cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. When Moses's father-in-law saw all that he did to the people, he said, What is this thing that thou doest to the people? Why sittest thou thyself alone, and all the people stand by thee from morning unto even? Henry notes Jethro's question was not criticism but concern: the work was too much for one man, and would make justice tiresome to the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Moses answered, Because the people come unto me to enquire of God. Henry observes Moses kept to his business from morning to night with great care and kindness, for the people came not only for judgment but to enquire of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. When they have a matter, they come unto me, and I judge between one and another, and I do make them know the statutes of God and his laws. Henry notes the twofold work: deciding causes and teaching statutes, so that judgment and instruction went together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Moses's father-in-law said, The thing that thou doest is not good. Henry observes the plain speech of a friend: there may be over-doing even in well-doing, and wisdom directs us neither to content ourselves with less than our duty nor to task ourselves beyond our strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Thou wilt surely wear away, both thou, and this people that is with thee, for this thing is too heavy for thee, and thou art not able to perform it thyself alone. Henry notes the warning covered both judge and people: the judge would break, and the people would weary waiting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Hearken now unto my voice, said Jethro, I will give thee counsel, and God shall be with thee: Be thou for the people to God-ward, that thou mayest bring the causes unto God. Henry observes the counsel began with Moses's highest work: to stand between the people and God, and carry their causes upward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Thou shalt teach them ordinances and laws, and shalt show them the way wherein they must walk, and the work that they must do. Henry notes the second work was teaching: a people who knew the law would need the judge less often. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Moreover thou shalt provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness, and place such over them to be rulers of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. Henry notes the character required: good sense and courage, piety that fears God, truthfulness, and hatred of bribes, for the fear of God best fortifies a man against temptations to injustice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Let them judge the people at all seasons, and every great matter they shall bring unto thee, but every small matter they shall judge themselves, so shall it be easier for thyself, and they shall bear the burden with thee. Henry notes great men should not only study to be useful themselves, but contrive to make others useful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. If thou shalt do this thing, and God command thee so, then thou shalt be able to endure, and all this people shall also go to their place in peace. Henry observes the promise was conditional on God's command as well as Jethro's counsel: human advice must be tried by divine approval. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. So Moses hearkened to the voice of his father-in-law, and did all that he had said. Henry notes Moses did not despise this advice: those are not wise who think themselves too wise to be counselled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Moses chose able men out of all Israel, and made them heads over the people, rulers of thousands, of hundreds, of fifties, and of tens. Henry observes the plan was put into effect at once, with graded courts reaching every part of the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. They judged the people at all seasons, and the hard causes they brought unto Moses, but every small matter they judged themselves. Henry notes the system worked as designed: ordinary justice near at hand, and only the hard causes rising to Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Moses let his father-in-law depart, and he went his way into his own land. Henry closes on the parting: counsel given, received, and put to work, and the counsellor returning home, his visit complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
