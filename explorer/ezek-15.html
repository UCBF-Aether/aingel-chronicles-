The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ezekiel 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the word of the LORD came unto me, saying,

2 Son of man, what is the vine tree more than any tree, or than a branch which is among the trees of the forest?

3 Shall wood be taken thereof to do any work? or will men take a pin of it to hang any vessel thereon?

4 Behold, it is cast into the fire for fuel; the fire devoureth both the ends of it, and the midst of it is burned. Is it meet for any work?

5 Behold, when it was whole, it was meet for no work: how much less shall it be meet yet for any work, when the fire hath devoured it, and it is burned?

6 Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD; As the vine tree among the trees of the forest, which I have given to the fire for fuel, so will I give the inhabitants of Jerusalem.

7 And I will set my face against them; they shall go out from one fire, and another fire shall devour them; and ye shall know that I am the LORD, when I set my face against them.

8 And I will make the land desolate, because they have committed a trespass, saith the Lord GOD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A short parable asks what the wood of a vine is good for. Among forest trees it is fit for nothing — not even a pin to hang a vessel on — and once burned at both ends it is fit for even less. So, the chapter says, Jerusalem, unfruitful as a vine, is given to the fire. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The word of the LORD comes with a similitude applied to Jerusalem, an unfruitful vine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. What is the vine tree more than any tree, or a branch among forest trees, if it bears no fruit? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Shall wood be taken from it for any work, or a pin to hang a vessel on? No, for fruit is the vine's sole use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Cast into the fire for fuel, both ends devoured and the midst burned, is it meet for any work? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. When whole it was meet for no work, how much less when fire has devoured and burned it? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. As the vine among forest trees given to fire for fuel, so God will give Jerusalem's inhabitants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. God will set his face against them, going out from one fire only to be devoured by another, and they shall know he is the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. God will make the land desolate, because they have committed a trespass, an unfruitful profession ending in destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
