The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Nehemiah 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And all the people gathered themselves together as one man into the street that was before the water gate; and they spake unto Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses, which the LORD had commanded to Israel.

2 And Ezra the priest brought the law before the congregation both of men and women, and all that could hear with understanding, upon the first day of the seventh month.

3 And he read therein before the street that was before the water gate from the morning until midday, before the men and the women, and those that could understand; and the ears of all the people were attentive unto the book of the law.

4 And Ezra the scribe stood upon a pulpit of wood, which they had made for the purpose; and beside him stood Mattithiah, and Shema, and Anaiah, and Urijah, and Hilkiah, and Maaseiah, on his right hand; and on his left hand, Pedaiah, and Mishael, and Malchiah, and Hashum, and Hashbadana, Zechariah, and Meshullam.

5 And Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people; (for he was above all the people;) and when he opened it, all the people stood up:

6 And Ezra blessed the LORD, the great God. And all the people answered, Amen, Amen, with lifting up their hands: and they bowed their heads, and worshipped the LORD with their faces to the ground.

7 Also Jeshua, and Bani, and Sherebiah, Jamin, Akkub, Shabbethai, Hodijah, Maaseiah, Kelita, Azariah, Jozabad, Hanan, Pelaiah, and the Levites, caused the people to understand the law: and the people stood in their place.

8 So they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading.

9 And Nehemiah, which is the Tirshatha, and Ezra the priest the scribe, and the Levites that taught the people, said unto all the people, This day is holy unto the LORD your God; mourn not, nor weep. For all the people wept, when they heard the words of the law.

10 Then he said unto them, Go your way, eat the fat, and drink the sweet, and send portions unto them for whom nothing is prepared: for this day is holy unto our LORD: neither be ye sorry; for the joy of the LORD is your strength.

11 So the Levites stilled all the people, saying, Hold your peace, for the day is holy; neither be ye grieved.

12 And all the people went their way to eat, and to drink, and to send portions, and to make great mirth, because they had understood the words that were declared unto them.

13 And on the second day were gathered together the chief of the fathers of all the people, the priests, and the Levites, unto Ezra the scribe, even to understand the words of the law.

14 And they found written in the law which the LORD had commanded by Moses, that the children of Israel should dwell in booths in the feast of the seventh month:

15 And that they should publish and proclaim in all their cities, and in Jerusalem, saying, Go forth unto the mount, and fetch olive branches, and pine branches, and myrtle branches, and palm branches, and branches of thick trees, to make booths, as it is written.

16 So the people went forth, and brought them, and made themselves booths, every one upon the roof of his house, and in their courts, and in the courts of the house of God, and in the street of the water gate, and in the street of the gate of Ephraim.

17 And all the congregation of them that were come again out of the captivity made booths, and sat under the booths: for since the days of Jeshua the son of Nun unto that day had not the children of Israel done so. And there was very great gladness.

18 Also day by day, from the first day unto the last day, he read in the book of the law of God. And they kept the feast seven days; and on the eighth day was a solemn assembly, according unto the manner.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

All the people gather as one man before the water gate and ask Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses. Ezra reads from morning until midday, the Levites explain the sense so the people understand, and weeping turns to holy joy, with portions sent to those who have nothing prepared. The next day the leaders learn the law of booths, and the people build booths and keep the feast seven days, with daily reading and a solemn assembly on the eighth day. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Water gate. the gate before whose street the people gathered to hear the law read (verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Booths (tabernacles). shelters of branches kept for seven days as the law commands, on rooftops, in courts, and in the streets (verses 15-17). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Feast of trumpets. the occasion of the gathering, at the start of the seventh month, according to the study note on verse 1. Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. All the people gathered as one man to the street before the water gate and asked Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses. JFB notes the occasion was the feast of trumpets at the start of the seventh month, the civil new year, and that the people's strong desire to hear the word read showed a greatly improved religious feeling after the neglect of captivity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Ezra the priest brought the law before the congregation of men and women and all who could hear with understanding, on the first day of the seventh month. JFB notes Ezra had returned with Nehemiah in a subordinate capacity and had devoted himself to producing a complete edition of the Scriptures, reviving the public reading the law required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He read before the water gate from morning until midday, and the ears of all the people were attentive to the book. JFB stresses the labour of the reading, lightened by the priests who shared it, and the people's sustained attention as the mark of the occasion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Ezra stood on a pulpit of wood made for the purpose, with six men at his right hand and seven at his left. JFB explains the pulpit was a raised scaffold broad enough for fourteen to stand on, and that the priests' presence showed their agreement with the truth declared and their share in reading and expounding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. When Ezra opened the book in sight of all the people, they all stood up. JFB notes they stood either from respect for God's word or because the reading was prefaced by solemn prayer, concluded by a general "Amen, Amen." Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Ezra blessed the LORD, the great God, and all the people answered "Amen, Amen" with lifted hands, bowed their heads, and worshipped with their faces to the ground. JFB ties the standing posture and the Amen to the solemn prayer that opened the reading. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Jeshua, Bani, Sherebiah, and the other Levites caused the people to understand the law, while the people stood in their place. JFB notes commentators differ on whether the Levites translated sentence by sentence into Chaldee, the exiles' vernacular, or explained what Ezra had read to a people grown ignorant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They read in the book of the law of God distinctly, gave the sense, and caused the people to understand the reading. JFB's discussion of this work of interpretation covers exactly this verse: the Levites either rendered the Hebrew into the spoken dialect or expounded its meaning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Nehemiah the Tirshatha, Ezra, and the teaching Levites told the weeping people that the day was holy to the LORD, so they should not mourn or weep. JFB explains their tears: the law's denunciations brought their national sins to remembrance, yet a sacred festival called for joy and thankfulness, not only sorrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They were told to eat the fat, drink the sweet, and send portions to those for whom nothing was prepared, for the day was holy and the joy of the LORD was their strength. JFB notes that sharing portions let poorer brethren join the public rejoicing, as the law directed for festivals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The Levites stilled the people, saying to hold their peace, for the day was holy, and not to be grieved. JFB places this calming word inside the same comfort: penitential sorrow was not to swallow the joy proper to a sacred festival. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The people went their way to eat, drink, send portions, and make great mirth, because they had understood the words declared to them. JFB ties the mirth to understanding: the joy followed the sense of the law being opened to them, not mere feasting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. On the second day the chiefs of the fathers, priests, and Levites gathered to Ezra to understand the words of the law. JFB's account of the feast that follows shows this second-day study as the source of the discovery: they returned to the text to learn what it required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. They found written in the law commanded by Moses that Israel should dwell in booths in the feast of the seventh month. JFB refers this observance to Leviticus 23 and Deuteronomy 16, the appointed feast of tabernacles now revived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They were to publish in all their cities and in Jerusalem that the people should go to the mount and fetch olive, pine, myrtle, palm, and thick-tree branches to make booths, as written. JFB treats this proclamation as the practical beginning of the feast-keeping described in the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The people went out, brought branches, and made booths on rooftops, in courts, in the courts of God's house, and in the streets of the water gate and the gate of Ephraim. JFB points to Leviticus 23:34 and Deuteronomy 16:13 as the pattern they followed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The whole congregation of returned exiles made booths and sat under them, with very great gladness, as had not been done since the days of Jeshua son of Nun. JFB clarifies this does not mean the feast had been wholly neglected since then, but that popular joy had never risen so high since Israel's entrance into Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Day by day, from the first to the last day, the book of the law of God was read, the feast was kept seven days, and on the eighth day there was a solemn assembly according to the manner. JFB notes this daily reading went beyond what was enjoined, arising from the exuberant zeal of the time, and that the eighth day was the last and great day of the feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages

  • The study note for verse 16 connects this passage to Leviticus 23:34. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • The study note for verse 16 connects this passage to Deuteronomy 16:13. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
