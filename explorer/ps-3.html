The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 3 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Lord, how are they increased that trouble me! many are they that rise up against me.

2 Many there be which say of my soul, There is no help for him in God. Selah.

3 But thou, O LORD, art a shield for me; my glory, and the lifter up of mine head.

4 I cried unto the LORD with my voice, and he heard me out of his holy hill. Selah.

5 I laid me down and slept; I awaked; for the LORD sustained me.

6 I will not be afraid of ten thousands of people, that have set themselves against me round about.

7 Arise, O LORD; save me, O my God: for thou hast smitten all mine enemies upon the cheek bone; thou hast broken the teeth of the ungodly.

8 Salvation belongeth unto the LORD: thy blessing is upon thy people. Selah.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Surrounded by enemies who taunt that God will not help, the speaker trusts God as shield and lifter of his head, sleeps and wakes kept by God, and ends with a wider truth: salvation belongs to the Lord, and his blessing rests on his people. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David, fleeing Absalom, counts his troubles increased: many rise against him, and the danger is personal and pressing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. His enemies add taunts to threats, saying God will not help him — the hardest trial, when foes attack faith itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. But God is his shield, his glory, and the lifter of his head: defence, honour, and restored courage are all found in the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. His cry was heard from God's holy hill; past answers to prayer become the ground of present trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He slept and woke sustained by the Lord: ordinary rest, in danger, is itself a testimony to God's keeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Therefore ten thousands massed around him cannot terrify him; numbers count for nothing against God's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He prays for God to arise and save, recalling past blows struck against his enemies — the cheek smitten and the teeth of the wicked broken, their power to bite removed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Salvation belongs to the Lord alone, and his blessing rests on his people: the psalm ends where it must, in a general truth wider than David's own case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
