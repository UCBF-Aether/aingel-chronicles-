The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the king sent, and they gathered unto him all the elders of Judah and of Jerusalem.

2 And the king went up into the house of the LORD, and all the men of Judah and all the inhabitants of Jerusalem with him, and the priests, and the prophets, and all the people, both small and great: and he read in their ears all the words of the book of the covenant which was found in the house of the LORD.

3 And the king stood by a pillar, and made a covenant before the LORD, to walk after the LORD, and to keep his commandments and his testimonies and his statutes with all their heart and all their soul, to perform the words of this covenant that were written in this book. And all the people stood to the covenant.

4 And the king commanded Hilkiah the high priest, and the priests of the second order, and the keepers of the door, to bring forth out of the temple of the LORD all the vessels that were made for Baal, and for the grove, and for all the host of heaven: and he burned them without Jerusalem in the fields of Kidron, and carried the ashes of them unto Bethel.

5 And he put down the idolatrous priests, whom the kings of Judah had ordained to burn incense in the high places in the cities of Judah, and in the places round about Jerusalem; them also that burned incense unto Baal, to the sun, and to the moon, and to the planets, and to all the host of heaven.

6 And he brought out the grove from the house of the LORD, without Jerusalem, unto the brook Kidron, and burned it at the brook Kidron, and stamped it small to powder, and cast the powder thereof upon the graves of the children of the people.

7 And he brake down the houses of the sodomites, that were by the house of the LORD, where the women wove hangings for the grove.

8 And he brought all the priests out of the cities of Judah, and defiled the high places where the priests had burned incense, from Geba to Beersheba, and brake down the high places of the gates that were in the entering in of the gate of Joshua the governor of the city, which were on a man’s left hand at the gate of the city.

9 Nevertheless the priests of the high places came not up to the altar of the LORD in Jerusalem, but they did eat of the unleavened bread among their brethren.

10 And he defiled Topheth, which is in the valley of the children of Hinnom, that no man might make his son or his daughter to pass through the fire to Molech.

11 And he took away the horses that the kings of Judah had given to the sun, at the entering in of the house of the LORD, by the chamber of Nathanmelech the chamberlain, which was in the suburbs, and burned the chariots of the sun with fire.

12 And the altars that were on the top of the upper chamber of Ahaz, which the kings of Judah had made, and the altars which Manasseh had made in the two courts of the house of the LORD, did the king beat down, and brake them down from thence, and cast the dust of them into the brook Kidron.

13 And the high places that were before Jerusalem, which were on the right hand of the mount of corruption, which Solomon the king of Israel had builded for Ashtoreth the abomination of the Zidonians, and for Chemosh the abomination of the Moabites, and for Milcom the abomination of the children of Ammon, did the king defile.

14 And he brake in pieces the images, and cut down the groves, and filled their places with the bones of men.

15 Moreover the altar that was at Bethel, and the high place which Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin, had made, both that altar and the high place he brake down, and burned the high place, and stamped it small to powder, and burned the grove.

16 And as Josiah turned himself, he spied the sepulchres that were there in the mount, and sent, and took the bones out of the sepulchres, and burned them upon the altar, and polluted it, according to the word of the LORD which the man of God proclaimed, who proclaimed these words.

17 Then he said, What title is that that I see? And the men of the city told him, It is the sepulchre of the man of God, which came from Judah, and proclaimed these things that thou hast done against the altar of Bethel.

18 And he said, Let him alone; let no man move his bones. So they let his bones alone, with the bones of the prophet that came out of Samaria.

19 And all the houses also of the high places that were in the cities of Samaria, which the kings of Israel had made to provoke the Lord to anger, Josiah took away, and did to them according to all the acts that he had done in Bethel.

20 And he slew all the priests of the high places that were there upon the altars, and burned men’s bones upon them, and returned to Jerusalem.

21 And the king commanded all the people, saying, Keep the passover unto the LORD your God, as it is written in the book of this covenant.

22 Surely there was not holden such a passover from the days of the judges that judged Israel, nor in all the days of the kings of Israel, nor of the kings of Judah;

23 But in the eighteenth year of king Josiah, wherein this passover was holden to the LORD in Jerusalem.

24 Moreover the workers with familiar spirits, and the wizards, and the images, and the idols, and all the abominations that were spied in the land of Judah and in Jerusalem, did Josiah put away, that he might perform the words of the law which were written in the book that Hilkiah the priest found in the house of the LORD.

25 And like unto him was there no king before him, that turned to the LORD with all his heart, and with all his soul, and with all his might, according to all the law of Moses; neither after him arose there any like him.

26 Notwithstanding the LORD turned not from the fierceness of his great wrath, wherewith his anger was kindled against Judah, because of all the provocations that Manasseh had provoked him withal.

27 And the LORD said, I will remove Judah also out of my sight, as I have removed Israel, and will cast off this city Jerusalem which I have chosen, and the house of which I said, My name shall be there.

28 Now the rest of the acts of Josiah, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

29 In his days Pharaohnechoh king of Egypt went up against the king of Assyria to the river Euphrates: and king Josiah went against him; and he slew him at Megiddo, when he had seen him.

30 And his servants carried him in a chariot dead from Megiddo, and brought him to Jerusalem, and buried him in his own sepulchre. And the people of the land took Jehoahaz the son of Josiah, and anointed him, and made him king in his father’s stead.

31 Jehoahaz was twenty and three years old when he began to reign; and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Hamutal, the daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.

32 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, according to all that his fathers had done.

33 And Pharaohnechoh put him in bands at Riblah in the land of Hamath, that he might not reign in Jerusalem; and put the land to a tribute of an hundred talents of silver, and a talent of gold.

34 And Pharaohnechoh made Eliakim the son of Josiah king in the room of Josiah his father, and turned his name to Jehoiakim, and took Jehoahaz away: and he came to Egypt, and died there.

35 And Jehoiakim gave the silver and the gold to Pharaoh; but he taxed the land to give the money according to the commandment of Pharaoh: he exacted the silver and the gold of the people of the land, of every one according to his taxation, to give it unto Pharaohnechoh.

36 Jehoiakim was twenty and five years old when he began to reign; and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Zebudah, the daughter of Pedaiah of Rumah.

37 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, according to all that his fathers had done.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Josiah reads the covenant to all the people, who pledge themselves to it, and then carries out the most thorough reform in the books, smashing high places, Baal vessels, and the Topheth, defiling Bethel's altar, and keeping a Passover like none since the judges. Yet the LORD does not turn from his anger over Manasseh, and Josiah dies at Megiddo against Pharaoh Necho. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The king sent and gathered to him all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem. JFB notes Josiah, not content with his own promised security, desired to avert the threatened calamities from his kingdom, and convened the elders, placing himself at their head with the collective inhabitants, in solemn procession to the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The king went up into the LORD's house with all Judah's men and Jerusalem's inhabitants, priests, prophets, and all people small and great, and read in their ears all the words of the book of the covenant found in the LORD's house. JFB notes he ordered the book of the law read to the assembled audience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The king stood by a pillar and made a covenant before the LORD, to walk after him and keep his commandments, testimonies, and statutes with all heart and soul, and all the people stood to the covenant. JFB explains the people agreed to the proposals and assented to what was required, covenanting with unanimous concurrence to adhere steadfastly to the LORD's commandments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The king commanded Hilkiah and the priests of the second order and door-keepers to bring out of the LORD's temple all vessels made for Baal, the grove, and the host of heaven, and he burned them outside Jerusalem in the Kidron fields, carrying the ashes to Bethel. JFB explains the whole apparatus of idol worship was consigned to flames as the law required, in the Kidron valley east and south of the city, and Josiah, outstripping his reforming predecessors in zeal, had the ashes conveyed to Bethel to associate horror with that polluted place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Josiah put down the idolatrous priests whom Judah's kings had ordained to burn incense in the high places and cities of Judah, and those who burned incense to Baal, the sun, the moon, the planets, and all the host of heaven. JFB explains the idolatrous priests, Hebrew chemarim or "scorched" fire-worshippers distinguished by a girdle, and notes Baal-shemesh worship was pure star-worship borrowed from the old Assyrians, not worship by images. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He brought out the grove from the LORD's house, outside Jerusalem to the Kidron brook, burned it there, stamped it to powder, and cast the powder on the graves of the children of the people. JFB identifies the grove as Asherah, the mystic tree placed by Manasseh in the temple, removed after his conversion but replaced by Amon, which Josiah took to Kidron, burnt, ground to powder, and strewed on the graves of those who had sacrificed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He broke down the houses of the sodomites by the LORD's house, where the women wove hangings for the grove. JFB explains these were not solid houses but tents, Succoth-benoth or booths of young women devoted to Asherah's service, where they made embroidered hangings and gave themselves to revelry and lust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Josiah brought all the priests out of Judah's cities and defiled the high places from Geba to Beersheba, breaking down the high places at the gates, including the one at the entrance of Joshua the governor's gate. JFB explains many Levites, finding temple worship abolished and offerings alienated under Manasseh and Amon, had officiated on high places, and Josiah brought them to Jerusalem, debarred them from temple service as defiled, but gave them an allowance from temple revenues; Geba to Beersheba meant all parts of the kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Nevertheless the priests of the high places came not up to the LORD's altar in Jerusalem, but ate unleavened bread among their brethren. JFB notes Josiah regarded them as defiled and debarred them from temple service, while giving them an allowance like lame and disabled priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Josiah defiled Topheth in the valley of Hinnom's children, so no man might make his son or daughter pass through the fire to Molech. JFB explains Topheth took its name from Toph, a drum, since Jewish writers held children's terrified cries in that idolatrous horror were drowned by the instrument. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. He took away the horses Judah's kings had given to the sun, at the LORD's house entrance by Nathanmelech the chamberlain's chamber in the suburbs, and burned the sun's chariots with fire. JFB explains sun-worshippers dedicated horses to the divinity, supposing the sun drawn in a chariot by horses, and idolatrous kings rode them each morning from the temple's east gate to salute the rising sun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. He broke down the altars on the top of Ahaz's upper chamber, which Judah's kings had made, and the altars Manasseh had made in the LORD's house's two courts, beating them down and casting their dust into the Kidron brook. JFB notes altars were reared on flat roofs where host-of-heaven worshippers burnt incense, Ahaz on his palace's upper chamber and Manasseh on the temple roof, and Josiah demolished both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He defiled the high places before Jerusalem, on the right hand of the mount of corruption, which Solomon had built for Ashtoreth, Chemosh, and Milcom. JFB explains the Mount of Olives' central summit was the Mount of Corruption, named for idol temples built there, and Josiah defiled rather than destroyed these high places, since the superstitious people still regarded the spot as consecrated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He broke in pieces the images, cut down the groves, and filled their places with men's bones. JFB notes every monument of idolatry in his dominion he likewise destroyed, defiling their sites by strewing dead men's bones, since a carcass rendered persons and places unclean to Jews and heathens alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Moreover the altar at Bethel and the high place Jeroboam son of Nebat had made, who made Israel sin, Josiah broke down, burned the high place, stamped it to powder, and burned the grove. JFB notes Josiah, not satisfied with purging his own dominion, toured Samaria's cities and the ten tribes' former territory, destroying high-place altars and temples and consigning Asherim to flames. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. As Josiah turned, he spied the sepulchres in the mount, sent and took the bones out of them, burned them on the altar, and polluted it according to the LORD's word proclaimed by the man of God. JFB notes this act had been predicted three hundred twenty-six years before Josiah's birth, with his name expressly mentioned and the very place, one of the Bible's most remarkable prophecies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Josiah asked what title he saw, and the city's men told him it was the sepulchre of the man of God who had come from Judah and proclaimed these things done against Bethel's altar. JFB notes the king's attention was arrested by a conspicuous tombstone bearing an inscription, and the informants were not Assyrian colonists, who knew nothing of the ancient transactions, but old people allowed to remain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Josiah said, "Let him alone; let no man move his bones," so they let his bones alone with the bones of the prophet who came out of Samaria. JFB's account of the tomb inquiry covers this sparing of the man of God's sepulchre. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Josiah also took away all the houses of the high places in Samaria's cities, which Israel's kings had made to provoke the LORD, and did to them according to all he had done in Bethel. JFB notes his tour of inspection through Samaria destroyed the high places' altars and temples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He slew all the priests of the high places there upon the altars, burned men's bones upon them, and returned to Jerusalem. JFB notes he put the high-place priests to death and showed horror at idolatry by ransacking idolatrous priests' sepulchres and strewing their burnt bones' ashes on the altars before demolishing them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The king commanded all the people to keep the passover unto the LORD their God, as written in the book of the covenant. JFB notes it was observed with great solemnity, attended not only by his subjects but by the remnant people from Israel, who may have heard the law read and procured a copy, stimulated to better observance by Jerusalem's solemn transactions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Surely no such passover had been held from the days of the judges who judged Israel, nor in all the days of Israel's and Judah's kings. JFB presents this passover's solemnity, attended by Judah and Israel's remnant, as unprecedented since the judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. But in Josiah's eighteenth year this passover was held to the LORD in Jerusalem. JFB's account places this great passover in the eighteenth year of the reformation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Moreover Josiah put away the workers with familiar spirits, the wizards, the images, the idols, and all abominations seen in Judah and Jerusalem, to perform the law's words written in the book Hilkiah found. JFB's section on destroying idolatry covers this sweeping purge of occult and idolatrous objects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Like Josiah there was no king before him who turned to the LORD with all his heart, soul, and might, according to all Moses' law, nor any like him after. JFB's account of the pious and patriotic king presents this wholehearted turning as the crown of his reformation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Notwithstanding, the LORD turned not from the fierceness of his great wrath, kindled against Judah because of all Manasseh's provocations. JFB explains the people's reformation was acquiescence from submission to royal will, while they secretly hankered after suppressed idolatries, their hearts not right toward God, so God, seeing no genuine repentance, allowed his decree for the kingdom's subversion to take fatal effect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The LORD said he would remove Judah also out of his sight, as he had removed Israel, and cast off Jerusalem, the city he had chosen, and the house of which he said, "My name shall be there." JFB notes God's decree for subversion, pronounced against Manasseh's sins, was allowed to take fatal effect despite Josiah's reform. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

JFB explains the people's reformation was acquiescence from submission to royal will, while they secretly hankered after suppressed idolatries, their hearts not right toward God, so God, seeing no genuine repentance, allowed his decree for the kingdom's subversion to take fatal effect.
