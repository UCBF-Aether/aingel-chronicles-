The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 63 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Who is this that cometh from Edom, with dyed garments from Bozrah? this that is glorious in his apparel, travelling in the greatness of his strength? I that speak in righteousness, mighty to save.

2 Wherefore art thou red in thine apparel, and thy garments like him that treadeth in the winefat?

3 I have trodden the winepress alone; and of the people there was none with me: for I will tread them in mine anger, and trample them in my fury; and their blood shall be sprinkled upon my garments, and I will stain all my raiment.

4 For the day of vengeance is in mine heart, and the year of my redeemed is come.

5 And I looked, and there was none to help; and I wondered that there was none to uphold: therefore mine own arm brought salvation unto me; and my fury, it upheld me.

6 And I will tread down the people in mine anger, and make them drunk in my fury, and I will bring down their strength to the earth.

7 I will mention the lovingkindnesses of the LORD, and the praises of the LORD, according to all that the LORD hath bestowed on us, and the great goodness toward the house of Israel, which he hath bestowed on them according to his mercies, and according to the multitude of his lovingkindnesses.

8 For he said, Surely they are my people, children that will not lie: so he was their Saviour.

9 In all their affliction he was afflicted, and the angel of his presence saved them: in his love and in his pity he redeemed them; and he bare them, and carried them all the days of old.

10 But they rebelled, and vexed his holy Spirit: therefore he was turned to be their enemy, and he fought against them.

11 Then he remembered the days of old, Moses, and his people, saying, Where is he that brought them up out of the sea with the shepherd of his flock? where is he that put his holy Spirit within him?

12 That led them by the right hand of Moses with his glorious arm, dividing the water before them, to make himself an everlasting name?

13 That led them through the deep, as an horse in the wilderness, that they should not stumble?

14 As a beast goeth down into the valley, the Spirit of the LORD caused him to rest: so didst thou lead thy people, to make thyself a glorious name.

15 Look down from heaven, and behold from the habitation of thy holiness and of thy glory: where is thy zeal and thy strength, the sounding of thy bowels and of thy mercies toward me? are they restrained?

16 Doubtless thou art our father, though Abraham be ignorant of us, and Israel acknowledge us not: thou, O LORD, art our father, our redeemer; thy name is from everlasting.

17 O LORD, why hast thou made us to err from thy ways, and hardened our heart from thy fear? Return for thy servants’ sake, the tribes of thine inheritance.

18 The people of thy holiness have possessed it but a little while: our adversaries have trodden down thy sanctuary.

19 We are thine: thou never barest rule over them; they were not called by thy name.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Who is this coming from Edom, with garments dyed red from Bozrah, glorious in apparel and mighty to save? The avenger has trodden the winepress alone, and the chapter turns to prayer: recalling God's mercy in the exodus, confessing that the people are clay in the Potter's hand, and pleading that God, who once bore rule over them, will not hide himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Who is this that comes from Edom, with dyed garments from Bozrah? this that is glorious in his apparel, travelling in the greatness of his strength? I that speak in righteousness, mighty to save. This chapter treats Messiah coming as the avenger, in answer to His people's prayers. Messiah, approaching Jerusalem after having avenged His people on His and their enemies, is represented under imagery taken from the destruction of Edom, the type of the last and most bitter foes of God and His people, as in chapter 34. Who means the question of the prophet in prophetic vision. Dyed means scarlet with blood, as in verses 2 and 3 and Revelation. Bozrah recalls chapter 34. Travelling is rather, stately; literally, throwing back the head. Speak in righteousness means answer of Messiah. I, who have in faithfulness given a promise of deliverance, am now about to fulfil it. Rather, speak of righteousness, as in chapters 45 and 46; salvation being meant as the result of His righteousness. Save means the same Messiah that destroys the unbeliever saves the believer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Why are you red in your apparel, and your garments like him that treads in the winefat? The prophet asks why His garments are dyed and red. Winefat is rather, the wine-press, wherein the grapes were trodden with the feet; the juice would stain the garment of him who trod them, as in Revelation. The image was appropriate, as the country round Bozrah abounded in grapes. This final blow inflicted by Messiah and His armies, as in Revelation, shall decide His claim to the kingdoms usurped by Satan, and by the beast, to whom Satan delegates his power. It will be a day of judgment to the hostile Gentiles, as His first coming was a day of judgment to the unbelieving Jews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. I have trodden the winepress alone, and of the people there was none with me, for I will tread them in my anger, and trample them in my fury, and their blood will be sprinkled upon my garments, and I will stain all my raiment. Reply of Messiah. For the image, see Lamentations. He treads the wine-press here not as a sufferer, but as an inflicter of vengeance. Will tread, shall be, will stain are rather preterites, I trod, trampled, was sprinkled, I stained. Blood is literally, spirited juice of the grape, pressed out by treading. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For the day of vengeance is in my heart, and the year of my redeemed is come. Is is rather, was. This assigns the reason why He has thus destroyed the foe, as in Zephaniah. My redeemed means my people to be redeemed. Day, year means here, as in chapters 34 and 61, the time of vengeance is described as a day; that of grace and of recompense to the redeemed, as a year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. I looked, and there was none to help, and I wondered that there was none to uphold, therefore my own arm brought salvation unto me, and my fury, it upheld me. The same words as in chapter 59 verse 16, except that there it is His righteousness, here it is His fury, which is said to have upheld Him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. I will tread down the people in my anger, and make them drunk in my fury, and I will bring down their strength to the earth. Made them drunk means the same image occurs in chapters 51 and Psalm 75 and Jeremiah. Will bring down strength to earth is rather, I spilled their life-blood, the same Hebrew words as in verse 3, on the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. I will mention the lovingkindnesses of the Lord, and the praises of the Lord, according to all that the Lord has bestowed on us, and the great goodness toward the house of Israel, which he has bestowed on them according to his mercies, and according to the multitude of his lovingkindnesses. Israel's penitential confession and prayer for restoration, as in Psalm 102, extending from verse 7 to chapter 64 verse 12. Loving-kindnesses, praises, mercies, loving-kindnesses means the plurals and the repetitions imply that language is inadequate to express the full extent of God's goodness. Us means the dispersed Jews at the time just preceding their final restoration. House of Israel means of all ages; God was good not merely to the Jews now dispersed, but to Israel in every age of its history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For he said, Surely they are my people, children that will not lie, so he was their Saviour. He means Jehovah said, that is, thought, in choosing them as His covenant-people; so said, as in Psalm 95. Not that God was ignorant that the Jews would not keep faith with Him; but God is here said, according to human modes of thought to say within Himself what He might naturally have expected, as the result of His goodness to the Jews; thus the enormity of their unnatural perversity is the more vividly set forth. Lie means prove false to Me, as in Psalm 44. So means in virtue of His having chosen them, He became their Saviour. So the therefore, as in Jeremiah. His eternal choice is the ground of His actually saving men, as in Ephesians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. In all their affliction he was afflicted, and the angel of his presence saved them; in his love and in his pity he redeemed them, and he bore them, and carried them all the days of old. He was afflicted means English Version reads the Hebrew as the Keri, margin, does, There was affliction to Him. But the Chetib, text, reads, There was no affliction, the change in Hebrew being only of one letter; that is, In all their affliction there was no, utterly overwhelming, affliction; or, for Hardly had an affliction befallen them, when the angel of His presence saved them; or, as best suits the parallelism, In all their straits there was no straitness in His goodness to them, as in Judges, Micah, and 2 Corinthians. Angel of his presence is literally, of His face, that is, who stands before Him continually; Messiah, as in Exodus and Proverbs, language applicable to no creature, as in Exodus and Numbers and Malachi. Bare them recalls chapters 46 and 40 and Exodus and Deuteronomy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. But they rebelled, and vexed his holy Spirit, therefore he was turned to be their enemy, and he fought against them. Vexed means grieved, as in Psalms and Acts and Ephesians and Hebrews. He fought is rather, He it was that fought, namely, the angel of His presence, as in Lamentations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Then he remembered the days of old, Moses, and his people, saying, Where is he that brought them up out of the sea with the shepherd of his flock? where is he that put his holy Spirit within him? Remembered means notwithstanding their perversity, He forgot not His covenant of old; therefore He did not wholly forsake them, as in Leviticus and Psalm 106; the Jews make this their plea with God, that He should not now forsake them. Saying means God is represented, in human language, mentally speaking of Himself and His former acts of love to Israel, as His ground for pitying them notwithstanding their rebellion. Sea means Red Sea. Shepherd means Moses; or if the Hebrew be read plural, shepherds, Moses, Aaron, and the other leaders, as in Psalm 77. Put Spirit within him is Hebrew, in the inward parts of him, that is, Moses; or it refers to the flock, in the midst of his people, as in Numbers and Nehemiah and Haggai. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. That led them by the right hand of Moses with his glorious arm, dividing the water before them, to make himself an everlasting name? The right hand of Moses was but the instrument; the arm of God was the real mover, as in Exodus. Dividing the water recalls Nehemiah and Psalm 78. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. That led them through the deep, as a horse in the wilderness, that they should not stumble? Deep is literally, the tossing and roaring sea. Wilderness is rather, the open plain, wherein there is no obstacle to cause a horse in its course the danger of stumbling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. As a beast goes down into the valley, the Spirit of the Lord caused him to rest, so did you lead your people, to make yourself a glorious name. Rest means image from a herd led down from the hills to a fertile and well-watered valley, as in Psalm 23; so God's Spirit caused Israel to rest in the promised land after their weary wanderings. To make name recalls verse 12 and 2 Samuel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Look down from heaven, and behold from the habitation of your holiness and of your glory; where is your zeal and your strength, the sounding of your bowels and of your mercies toward me? are they restrained? Here begins a fervent appeal to God to pity Israel now on the ground of His former benefits. Habitation of holiness recalls chapters 57 and Deuteronomy and 2 Chronicles and Psalms. Zeal, strength means evinced formerly for Thy people. Sounding of bowels means thine emotions of compassion, as in chapters 16 and Jeremiah and Hosea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Doubtless you are our father, though Abraham be ignorant of us, and Israel acknowledge us not; you, O Lord, are our father, our redeemer; your name is from everlasting. Father means of Israel, by right not merely of creation, but also of electing adoption, as in chapter 64 and Deuteronomy and 1 Chronicles. Though Abraham, Israel means it had been the besetting temptation of the Jews to rest on the mere privilege of their descent from faithful Abraham and Jacob, as in Matthew and John; now at last they renounce this, to trust in God alone as their Father, notwithstanding all appearances to the contrary. Even though Abraham, our earthly father, on whom we have prided ourselves, disown us, Thou wilt not, as in chapters 49 and Psalm 27. Isaac is not mentioned, because not all his posterity was admitted to the covenant, whereas all Jacob's was; Abraham is specified because he was the first father of the Jewish race. Everlasting means an argument why He should help them, namely, because of His everlasting immutability. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. O Lord, why have you made us to err from your ways, and hardened our heart from your fear? Return for your servants' sake, the tribes of your inheritance. Made us to err means that is, suffer us to err and to be hardened in our heart. They do not mean to deny their own blameworthiness, but confess that through their own fault God gave them over to a reprobate mind, as in chapters 6 and Psalm 119 and Romans. Return recalls Numbers and Psalm 90. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The people of your holiness have possessed it but a little while; our adversaries have trodden down your sanctuary. Holiness means Israel dedicated as holy unto God, as in chapter 62 and Deuteronomy. Possessed means namely, the Holy Land, or Thy sanctuary, taken from the following clause, which is parallel to this, as in chapters 64 and Psalm 74. Thy means an argument why God should help them; their cause is His cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. We are yours; you never bore rule over them; they were not called by your name. Never is rather, We are Thine from of old; Thou barest not rule over them. Some translate, We for long have been as those over whom Thou hast not ruled, who are not called by Thy name; for long thus stands in contrast to but a little while, verse 18. But the analogy of verse 18 makes it likely that the first clause in this verse refers to the Jews, and the second to their foes, as English Version and Barnes translate it. The Jews' foes are aliens who have unjustly intruded into the Lord's heritage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Bozrah / winepress. Bozrah in Edom, and the winepress image is literal in the note on verse 2: grapes trodden with the feet, staining the treader's garments — here, garments dyed with the blood of judgment. Interpreted Study note on verse 2, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
