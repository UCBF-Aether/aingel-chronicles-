Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 At that time, saith the LORD, they shall bring out the bones of the kings of Judah, and the bones of his princes, and the bones of the priests, and the bones of the prophets, and the bones of the inhabitants of Jerusalem, out of their graves:

2 And they shall spread them before the sun, and the moon, and all the host of heaven, whom they have loved, and whom they have served, and after whom they have walked, and whom they have sought, and whom they have worshipped: they shall not be gathered, nor be buried; they shall be for dung upon the face of the earth.

3 And death shall be chosen rather than life by all the residue of them that remain of this evil family, which remain in all the places whither I have driven them, saith the LORD of hosts.

4 Moreover thou shalt say unto them, Thus saith the LORD; Shall they fall, and not arise? shall he turn away, and not return?

5 Why then is this people of Jerusalem slidden back by a perpetual backsliding? they hold fast deceit, they refuse to return.

6 I hearkened and heard, but they spake not aright: no man repented him of his wickedness, saying, What have I done? every one turned to his course, as the horse rusheth into the battle.

7 Yea, the stork in the heaven knoweth her appointed times; and the turtle and the crane and the swallow observe the time of their coming; but my people know not the judgment of the LORD.

8 How do ye say, We are wise, and the law of the LORD is with us? Lo, certainly in vain made he it; the pen of the scribes is in vain.

9 The wise men are ashamed, they are dismayed and taken: lo, they have rejected the word of the LORD; and what wisdom is in them?

10 Therefore will I give their wives unto others, and their fields to them that shall inherit them: for every one from the least even unto the greatest is given to covetousness, from the prophet even unto the priest every one dealeth falsely.

11 For they have healed the hurt of the daughter of my people slightly, saying, Peace, peace; when there is no peace.

12 Were they ashamed when they had committed abomination? nay, they were not at all ashamed, neither could they blush: therefore shall they fall among them that fall: in the time of their visitation they shall be cast down, saith the LORD.

13 I will surely consume them, saith the LORD: there shall be no grapes on the vine, nor figs on the fig tree, and the leaf shall fade; and the things that I have given them shall pass away from them.

14 Why do we sit still? assemble yourselves, and let us enter into the defenced cities, and let us be silent there: for the LORD our God hath put us to silence, and given us water of gall to drink, because we have sinned against the LORD.

15 We looked for peace, but no good came; and for a time of health, and behold trouble!

16 The snorting of his horses was heard from Dan: the whole land trembled at the sound of the neighing of his strong ones; for they are come, and have devoured the land, and all that is in it; the city, and those that dwell therein.

17 For, behold, I will send serpents, cockatrices, among you, which will not be charmed, and they shall bite you, saith the LORD.

18 When I would comfort myself against sorrow, my heart is faint in me.

19 Behold the voice of the cry of the daughter of my people because of them that dwell in a far country: Is not the LORD in Zion? is not her king in her? Why have they provoked me to anger with their graven images, and with strange vanities?

20 The harvest is past, the summer is ended, and we are not saved.

21 For the hurt of the daughter of my people am I hurt; I am black; astonishment hath taken hold on me.

22 Is there no balm in Gilead; is there no physician there? why then is not the health of the daughter of my people recovered?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Judgment here reaches even the dead, whose bones are brought out of their graves, while the living refuse to return. The people cling to error like birds that know their seasons yet do not know the Lord's judgment, and the chapter closes with Jeremiah's famous unanswered question: is there no balm in Gilead, no physician there, that the health of his people is not recovered? What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. At that time the bones of Judah's kings, princes, priests, prophets, and inhabitants shall be brought out of their graves. Henry notes that though no real hurt can be done to a dead body, disgrace to the remains of wicked persons may alarm those yet alive, reminding us that divine justice and punishments extend beyond the grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The bones shall be spread before the sun, moon, and all the host of heaven, whom they loved, served, walked after, sought, and worshipped. Henry's point is the exposure: the very gods they served will look down on the disgrace of their worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Death shall be chosen rather than life by all the residue of this evil family remaining in the places where God has driven them. Henry urges the lesson: whatever befalls us here, let us humble ourselves before God and seek his mercy before death becomes preferable to life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Shall they fall and not arise? Shall he turn away and not return? Henry begins the account of the ruin with reason: sin is backsliding, going back from the way that leads to life toward destruction, yet the fallen refuse the rising that reason itself demands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Why has Jerusalem slidden back by a perpetual backsliding, holding fast deceit and refusing to return? Henry notes the people would not act in the affairs of their souls with common prudence: they hold deceit fast rather than let it go. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. God hearkened and heard, but they spoke not aright: no man repented of his wickedness, saying, 'What have I done?' Every one ran his course like a horse rushing into battle. Henry observes they would not attend to conscience: true repentance begins in serious inquiry as to what we have done, from conviction that we have done amiss, and they never took that first step. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The stork in heaven knows her appointed times, and the turtle, crane, and swallow observe the time of their coming, but God's people do not know the judgment of the Lord. Henry notes they would not attend to the ways of providence nor understand God's voice in them, and know not how to improve the seasons of grace; unless taught by the Spirit, the instinct of brutes is a surer guide than their supposed wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. How do ye say, 'We are wise, and the law of the Lord is with us'? Lo, certainly in vain made he it; the pen of the scribes is in vain. Henry notes they would not attend to the written word: many enjoy abundance of the means of grace, Bibles and ministers, but have them in vain, and will soon be ashamed of their devices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The wise men are ashamed, dismayed, and taken, for they have rejected the word of the Lord, and what wisdom is in them? Henry identifies the pretenders to wisdom as the priests and false prophets, who flattered people in sin and so flattered them into destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. God will give their wives to others and their fields to inheritors, for from the least to the greatest every one is given to covetousness, and from prophet to priest every one deals falsely. Henry's judgment fits the cause: a people given to covetousness and false dealing lose wives, fields, and all they grasped. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. They have healed the hurt of God's people slightly, saying, 'Peace, peace,' when there is no peace. Henry warns that selfish teachers may promise peace when there is no peace, silencing fears and complaints with 'All is well,' and thus men encourage each other in committing evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Were they ashamed when they committed abomination? Nay, they were not at all ashamed, neither could they blush, therefore they shall fall among the fallen and be cast down at God's visitation. Henry notes that in the day of visitation such flattered sinners will have no refuge to flee unto. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. God will surely consume them: no grapes on the vine, no figs on the fig tree, the leaf withered, and what he gave them passed away from them. Henry's ruin is total consumption: comforts, fruit, and gifts all pass away from a people who would not attend to reason, conscience, providence, or word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Why do we sit still? Assemble and enter the defenced cities, and be silent there, for the Lord has doomed us to perish and given us water of gall to drink, because we have sinned against him. Henry notes that at length they begin to see the hand of God lifted up: the defenced cities are not refuge but the place to perish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. We looked for peace, but no good came, and for a time of health, but behold trouble. Henry observes that when God appears against us, everything that is against us appears formidable: peace hoped for becomes trouble found. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The snorting of his horses was heard from Dan, and the whole land trembled at the neighing of his strong ones, for they come to devour the land, the city, and those dwelling in it. Henry's alarm is the invasion itself, formidable once God's hand is seen in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Behold, God will send serpents and cockatrices among them, which will not be charmed, and they shall bite. Henry's point is that no charming will turn this judgment: the serpents cannot be charmed, as the flatterers' 'peace' could not charm God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. When Jeremiah would comfort himself against sorrow, his heart is faint within him. Henry's lamentation begins here: the prophet who warned faithfully faints under the sorrow he foresaw. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Behold the cry of the daughter of God's people from a far country: 'Is not the Lord in Zion? Is not her King in her?' And God answers, 'Why have they provoked me with carved images and strange vanities?' Henry notes salvation is found only in the Lord, and the cry from afar meets its cause: idols provoked the King who seemed absent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. The harvest is past, the summer is ended, and we are not saved. Henry warns that the present moment should be seized: seasons of grace unimproved end in this cry, when harvest and summer are gone and salvation has not come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. For the hurt of the daughter of God's people Jeremiah is hurt; he is black, and astonishment has taken hold on him. Henry's prophet shares the wound: hurt for hurt, black as a mourner, astonished at the misery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Is there no balm in Gilead? Is there no physician there? Why then is not the health of God's people recovered? Henry answers yes: God is able to help and heal, the blood of Christ is balm in Gilead and his Spirit the all-sufficient Physician, so the people may be healed but will not; thus men die unpardoned and unchanged, for they will not come to Christ to be saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
