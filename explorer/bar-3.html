The Text — Baruch 3 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 O Lord Almighty, God of Israel, the soul in anguish the troubled spirit, crieth unto thee.

2 Hear, O Lord, and have mercy; ar thou art merciful: and have pity upon us, because we have sinned before thee.

3 For thou endurest for ever, and we perish utterly.

4 O Lord Almighty, thou God of Israel, hear now the prayers of the dead Israelites, and of their children, which have sinned before thee, and not hearkened unto the voice of thee their God: for the which cause these plagues cleave unto us.

5 Remember not the iniquities of our forefathers: but think upon thy power and thy name now at this time.

6 For thou art the Lord our God, and thee, O Lord, will we praise.

7 And for this cause thou hast put thy fear in our hearts, to the intent that we should call upon thy name, and praise thee in our captivity: for we have called to mind all the iniquity of our forefathers, that sinned before thee.

8 Behold, we are yet this day in our captivity, where thou hast scattered us, for a reproach and a curse, and to be subject to payments, according to all the iniquities of our fathers, which departed from the Lord our God.

9 Hear, Israel, the commandments of life: give ear to understand wisdom.

10 How happeneth it Israel, that thou art in thine enemies' land, that thou art waxen old in a strange country, that thou art defiled with the dead,

11 That thou art counted with them that go down into the grave?

12 Thou hast forsaken the fountain of wisdom.

13 For if thou hadst walked in the way of God, thou shouldest have dwelled in peace for ever.

14 Learn where is wisdom, where is strength, where is understanding; that thou mayest know also where is length of days, and life, where is the light of the eyes, and peace.

15 Who hath found out her place? or who hath come into her treasures ?

16 Where are the princes of the heathen become, and such as ruled the beasts upon the earth;

17 They that had their pastime with the fowls of the air, and they that hoarded up silver and gold, wherein men trust, and made no end of their getting?

18 For they that wrought in silver, and were so careful, and whose works are unsearchable,

19 They are vanished and gone down to the grave, and others are come up in their steads.

20 Young men have seen light, and dwelt upon the earth: but the way of knowledge have they not known,

21 Nor understood the paths thereof, nor laid hold of it: their children were far off from that way.

22 It hath not been heard of in Chanaan, neither hath it been seen in Theman.

23 The Agarenes that seek wisdom upon earth, the merchants of Meran and of Theman, the authors of fables, and searchers out of understanding; none of these have known the way of wisdom, or remember her paths.

24 O Israel, how great is the house of God! and how large is the place of his possession!

25 Great, and hath none end; high, and unmeasurable.

26 There were the giants famous from the beginning, that were of so great stature, and so expert in war.

27 Those did not the Lord choose, neither gave he the way of knowledge unto them:

28 But they were destroyed, because they had no wisdom, and perished through their own foolishness.

29 Who hath gone up into heaven, and taken her, and brought her down from the clouds?

30 Who hath gone over the sea, and found her, and will bring her for pure gold?

31 No man knoweth her way, nor thinketh of her path.

32 But he that knoweth all things knoweth her, and hath found her out with his understanding: he that prepared the earth for evermore hath filled it with fourfooted beasts:

33 He that sendeth forth light, and it goeth, calleth it again, and it obeyeth him with fear.

34 The stars shined in their watches, and rejoiced: when he calleth them, they say, Here we be; and so with cheerfulness they shewed light unto him that made them.

35 This is our God, and there shall none other be accounted of in comparison of him

36 He hath found out all the way of knowledge, and hath given it unto Jacob his servant, and to Israel his beloved.

37 Afterward did he shew himself upon earth, and conversed with men.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter opens in anguish — the soul in anguish and the troubled spirit crying for mercy — and then turns to the great hymn of wisdom: wisdom is not found among merchants, kings, giants or the wise of the earth, nor bought with gold, but is the book of God's commandments, given to Jacob and Israel. It closes with the striking line that afterward he showed himself upon earth and conversed with men. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 3. To remain for ever is asked as a plea, whether so short-lived a creature is an object worthy of eternal wrath, with Job and the Psalms urging the same brevity of man against the length of anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. The dead of Israel are the banished, or the ancient patriarchs, whose names are urged as a motive, as Moses pleaded them, and people in sin and misery seem dead, yet may revive by grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. Think upon thy hand means save us by thy power, and for thy name's sake, as Josue pleaded, so the prayer asks God to act for his own hand and name, not for the people's merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. The offence is in the Greek a debt, since parents might sell their children, and God threatens the rebellious with being forced to borrow, so captivity itself is described as a debt owed for the iniquities of the fathers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. Hear opens the second part, in which God replies, and the Church has long read this passage as holy scripture on the eves of Easter and Pentecost, since it is an instruction in true wisdom, found in God alone and in the people to whom he communicates it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. Grown old is read by some as growing, since five years had not elapsed, yet the exiles already knew their captivity would be long, and defiled with the dead means they were as if buried, dwelling among defilements as if near a dead body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. Peace belongs only to observers of the law, for there is none for the wicked, and for ever is read by many as upon the land, conformably to the Greek, so the promise of dwelling in peace is tied to walking in God's way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. The treasures are the place of wisdom, and the question, who has found her place, exposes how few are truly wise and virtuous, since the great men of the world missed their aim, while Israel received the law, yet shows little fruit of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. The princes of the nations are those of Assyria and like empires, who esteemed themselves lords of nature and delighted in hunting, with beasts and birds subject to them, yet none of their dominion found wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. The works are unsearchable in the sense that the hoarders shall not find the fruit of their works, since silver and gold heaped without end yield nothing that lasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. The young men are heirs of those conquerors, equally in the dark, so the wisdom the fathers missed is missed by the sons as well, and the way of knowledge is not inherited with the empire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. Theman is the capital city of Edom, where Eliphaz was king, and the Phoenicians, so famous for industry, had no idea of true wisdom, no more than the Ismaelites, so neither commerce nor royal counsel found her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 23. Agar is the mother of the Ismaelites, and Merrha probably in Arabia, while the tellers of fables may be reproached or commended, since those nations delighted in enigmatical discourses, but instructors of mankind deserve praise, not those who devise false gods for lucre. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 24. The possession is all the world, which belongs to God, yet how few partake of his wisdom, so the vastness of God's house only sharpens the question of why so few enter into its wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 26. The giants are those before and after the deluge, renowned and expert in war, yet Noe and Israel were preferred before them, since God chose not the giants and they did not find the way of knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 29. The clouds are invoked because no human industry can procure wisdom from above, since God alone dispenses it, and his grace makes the discovery easy, as the yoke is made easy in the Gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 32. Evermore is literally in the eternal time, or so long ago, designed to continue while kingdoms change, so the earth prepared by God outlasts every empire that rises upon it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 33. Trembling describes the sun, which stops, goes back, or withdraws its light at God's command, as at Josue's bidding and at the passion, so light itself obeys with trembling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 34. The watches are the stations of the stars, which are like God's soldiers, shining with cheerfulness when called, and answering, here we are, to him that made them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 37. The verse is read by most of the Fathers as the incarnation, since afterwards he was seen upon earth and conversed with men, and the prophets speak of things to come as past to express certainty, while Christ, as the wisdom of God, is the same wisdom given to Jacob and Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
