The Text — Josippon 15 (BL EAP704/2/3 — Ge'ez codex of the Zena Ayhud, Christian redaction, copied 1595–1605)

1 ...and he was also like Adam; and he lived, when he was created — there being in them shame that is known from her — [?]; and she was like the angels, for the light of their faces covered their flesh.

2 And when He created Eve, He wrought a wondrous work for that Adam: He took from Adam; He created the completion of her bones from his sides, without [?], that He might show that He is Almighty to take from her; He is the bone that is closely joined; He maketh from her another completion in her sides, as He created for her the completion; and she was made like Adam; and He worketh from one another, for He is Almighty over every work.

3 And know this saying: that the woman hath the bone of her side added unto the man until now, as the wise men, the interpreters, have spoken; and it was confirmed together with the Book of the LORD in their [?].

4 For Satan, when he had finished questioning all the beasts that stood firm, and had come unto the garden, and saw Adam and Eve standing firm, without [?], and they had glory and a voice, all of them were amazed. He knew in his heart that they were of the company of the hosts of the angels, being within the compass of the beasts; for his thought was set against this. And then he said in himself: I know [?], lest it come to pass as he destroyed the hosts, and they rise up and cast down her order, and [?] remain [?] unto the ages.

5 And I know also that the LORD is righteous and [?].

6 Judgment; and there is no respect of persons with Him.

7 And these, when they are lifted up like me, He will cast them down, as He cast me down, when I was lifted up against Him and became like [?].

8 And when they do these things in envy, and when they do them like me, He will cast them down, as He cast them down like me; and if I make them do my work, He will overshadow me with a pretext of mine own [?]; for it befits me to take a pretext against them(?) until they do it of their own desire, without compulsion, and in the doing, that a pretext of their own trespass may be fitting against them.

9 And then he hid his spirit from them within the serpent, and spake unto them from it, saying: Why did God forbid you, that ye should say: Of all the trees that are in the garden shall ye eat? He said this saying because he supposed in his heart that God had laid a commandment upon them, that they might decline by their commandments.

10 As He did with us concerning the glory and the holiness.

11 And I saw not [?] in their hiding, nor doth aught command them that they should be kept from them, except the trees: at the time He commanded them not to eat of any of them. And concerning this thought which he thought concerning the judgment of God, which we have mentioned before: at the time He commanded them not to eat of that which is not written(?) in the Book of the Teaching, and [?] from all the beasts.

12 And Eve said: God hath not forbidden us to eat of all the trees; but He forbade us the eating of the fruit of the tree; and He said unto us: Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, for in the day that ye eat of it, ye shall surely die.

13 And Satan said: It is not that ye shall die when ye eat of it; but God knoweth that ye, when ye eat of it, shall become angels, according to His likeness.

14 And then she longed to become like an angel, and she ate of it, and gave it unto Adam [?] that he might become like her.

15 And he ate of it.

16 And when God stripped them of the garment of light, as He stripped the Devil of it, and cast them not down as He cast him down into the earth, that they might be(?) in judgment, according to His judgment of the Devil, they knew that they had sinned in likeness, and were likened unto him in themselves; and they heard from the serpent, and believed him, and desired from the serpent to become angels.

17 And they deceived them in their deeds, and they were not perfected, nor filled with all wisdom; for the Book of the Teaching saith: God brought unto Adam all the beasts and the wild beasts, to see what he would call them, and what they should be unto all of them.

18 And the names which he gave them have firmness unto the ages; God showed thereby in truth that Adam was filled with all wisdom.

19 And therefore God bare witness that He created Eve like Adam in every work; and she was not [?], but wise.

20 And therefore this is named, that which followeth against the Devil and the serpent: By what pretext did he come into the garden? This is it: that God brought unto Adam every beast, and the serpent was subtle above every beast, and the serpent was subtle above all; He left them in their thought in the garden, that they might guard it. And by this Satan found a pretext whereby to beguile them, for he saw them rejoicing in it greatly and [?].

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Adam and Eve after their making are the subject, with shame known from the woman, the trees they were commanded not to eat, and the question that follows against the Devil and the serpent: by what pretext did he come into the garden? The chapter's wording is fragmentary, and the figure like Adam appears amid its broken lines. Interpreted Chapter text as printed above; study-notes file for this chapter is silent
