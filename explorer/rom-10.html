The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Romans 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Brethren, my heart’s desire and prayer to God for Israel is, that they might be saved.

2 For I bear them record that they have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge.

3 For they being ignorant of God’s righteousness, and going about to establish their own righteousness, have not submitted themselves unto the righteousness of God.

4 For Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth.

5 For Moses describeth the righteousness which is of the law, That the man which doeth those things shall live by them.

6 But the righteousness which is of faith speaketh on this wise, Say not in thine heart, Who shall ascend into heaven? (that is, to bring Christ down from above:)

7 Or, Who shall descend into the deep? (that is, to bring up Christ again from the dead.)

8 But what saith it? The word is nigh thee, even in thy mouth, and in thy heart: that is, the word of faith, which we preach;

9 That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.

10 For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.

11 For the scripture saith, Whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed.

12 For there is no difference between the Jew and the Greek: for the same Lord over all is rich unto all that call upon him.

13 For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.

14 How then shall they call on him in whom they have not believed? and how shall they believe in him of whom they have not heard? and how shall they hear without a preacher?

15 And how shall they preach, except they be sent? as it is written, How beautiful are the feet of them that preach the gospel of peace, and bring glad tidings of good things!

16 But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Esaias saith, Lord, who hath believed our report?

17 So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.

18 But I say, Have they not heard? Yes verily, their sound went into all the earth, and their words unto the ends of the world.

19 But I say, Did not Israel know? First Moses saith, I will provoke you to jealousy by them that are no people, and by a foolish nation I will anger you.

20 But Esaias is very bold, and saith, I was found of them that sought me not; I was made manifest unto them that asked not after me.

21 But to Israel he saith, All day long I have stretched forth my hands unto a disobedient and gainsaying people.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul's heart's desire for Israel is salvation, but he says they have zeal without knowledge, seeking their own righteousness rather than submitting to God's. Righteousness by faith is near, in the mouth and heart, confessed with the mouth and believed in the heart, and faith comes by hearing the preached word; yet Israel, hearing, remains disobedient, while God stretches out his hands all day long to them. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Brethren, Paul's heart's desire and prayer to God for Israel is that they might be saved. Henry notes that doctrine about Israel never cools Paul's prayer for Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He bears them record that they have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge. JFB stresses that zeal without knowledge is dangerous, not commendable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Being ignorant of God's righteousness and seeking to establish their own, they have not submitted to God's righteousness. Henry observes that self-righteousness is a rival righteousness, not a supplement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes. JFB notes that Christ ends the law as a way of righteousness, not as a revelation of God's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Moses describes the righteousness of the law, that the man who does those things shall live by them. Henry observes that law-righteousness demands perfect doing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The righteousness of faith speaks otherwise: do not say in your heart, who shall ascend into heaven, to bring Christ down. JFB notes that faith does not need to fetch Christ from afar; he has already come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Nor, who shall descend into the deep, to bring Christ up from the dead. Henry observes that faith does not need to raise Christ again; he is already risen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart, the word of faith Paul preaches. JFB stresses the nearness of the gospel: it is to be believed and confessed, not fetched from afar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. If you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus, and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you shall be saved. Henry notes that confession and belief together mark the saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. With the heart man believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation. JFB stresses that inward faith and outward confession belong together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Scripture says, whoever believes on him shall not be ashamed. Henry observes that the promise is open to whoever, without exception of nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. There is no difference between Jew and Greek, for the same Lord over all is rich to all who call on him. JFB notes that one Lord's riches are open to every caller. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Whoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved. Henry stresses that calling on the Lord is the plain, open door of salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. How shall they call on him in whom they have not believed? How shall they believe in him of whom they have not heard? How shall they hear without a preacher? JFB notes the chain that makes preaching necessary to faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. How shall they preach except they be sent? As it is written, how beautiful are the feet of those who preach the gospel of peace and bring glad tidings of good things. Henry observes that the preacher's feet are beautiful because of the message they carry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Not all obeyed the gospel, for Isaiah says, Lord, who has believed our report? JFB notes that unbelief in the face of preaching fulfils, not frustrates, prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God. Henry stresses that the word preached, not wonders or visions, is faith's ordinary means. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Have they not heard? Yes, their sound went into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world. JFB notes that Israel cannot plead ignorance of the message. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Did not Israel know? Moses says, I will provoke you to jealousy by a people who are no people, and by a foolish nation I will anger you. Henry observes that Gentile blessing was meant to stir Israel to jealousy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Isaiah is very bold and says, I was found by those who did not seek me; I was made manifest to those who did not ask for me. JFB notes the boldness: God found those who were not even looking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. To Israel he says, all day long I have stretched forth my hands to a disobedient and gainsaying people. Henry observes that God's hands stayed open all day to a people who talked back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • zeal without knowledge. Paul's diagnosis of Israel: earnest for God, but not according to knowledge of God's righteousness. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • confess with thy mouth. The open acknowledgment of Jesus as Lord, joined in the chapter to believing in the heart that God raised him. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • beautiful feet. The image from Isaiah, quoted by Paul, of messengers bringing glad tidings of good things. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Connections to other passages

  • Isaiah's question, 'Lord, who hath believed our report?', which Paul cites for Israel's unbelief in the face of preaching. Interpreted Study note for verse 16, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
