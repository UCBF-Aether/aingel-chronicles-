The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 114 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 When Israel went out of Egypt, the house of Jacob from a people of strange language;

2 Judah was his sanctuary, and Israel his dominion.

3 The sea saw it, and fled: Jordan was driven back.

4 The mountains skipped like rams, and the little hills like lambs.

5 What ailed thee, O thou sea, that thou fleddest? thou Jordan, that thou wast driven back?

6 Ye mountains, that ye skipped like rams; and ye little hills, like lambs?

7 Tremble, thou earth, at the presence of the Lord, at the presence of the God of Jacob;

8 Which turned the rock into a standing water, the flint into a fountain of waters.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A vivid, short retelling of the Exodus: when Israel went out of Egypt, the house of Jacob from a people of strange language, Judah became God's sanctuary and Israel his dominion. The sea saw it and fled, Jordan was driven back, mountains skipped like rams and little hills like lambs. The psalm then turns the picture into questions — what ailed the sea that it fled, Jordan that it was driven back, the mountains that they skipped? — and answers with trembling: the earth trembles at the presence of the God of Jacob, who turned rock into standing water and flint into a fountain of waters. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry insists time must not wear out the sense of mercy: Israel marched out with honour, not stolen away or driven out, from a barbarous people of strange language, having kept their own tongue pure as an earnest of deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry reads Judah and Israel as God's sanctuary and dominion: he framed their civil and sacred constitution, set up his sanctuary with special tokens of his presence, and ruled as their King — and what is his sanctuary must submit to his laws, as Christ redeems us to bring us into God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry says the sea fled because it saw Judah as God's sanctuary and Israel his dominion: God was at the head of that people, so the waters must give way contrary to their nature when he speaks the word, for their rescue and their enemies' ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry treats Sinai's shaking as proof that the same power which fixed fluid waters also shakes stable mountains: before God the bulkiest hills are as manageable as rams and lambs to a shepherd, and their trembling should shame men's stupidity before his glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry makes the psalmist put the question poetically to sea and Jordan, then answer it: the miracle was real, not by nature's power, merciful in kind — for Israel's salvation — and surprising, that nature's laws should be dispensed with to serve God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry asks the mountains the same question and answers it alike: they skipped at the presence of the Lord, and he who made hills skip can dissipate the strength of the proudest enemies and make them tremble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry bids the whole earth tremble at the presence of the God of Jacob, since the earth lies under the curse for man's sin; before that presence not only mountains but the earth itself may well tremble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry closes with provision matching protection: the almighty power that turned waters into a wall for Israel turned the flinty rock into a standing fountain, and that rock was Christ, a fountain of living waters from whom his Israel receives grace for grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
