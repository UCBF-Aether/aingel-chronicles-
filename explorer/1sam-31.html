The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 31 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the Philistines fought against Israel: and the men of Israel fled from before the Philistines, and fell down slain in mount Gilboa.

2 And the Philistines followed hard upon Saul and upon his sons; and the Philistines slew Jonathan, and Abinadab, and Melchishua, Saul’s sons.

3 And the battle went sore against Saul, and the archers hit him; and he was sore wounded of the archers.

4 Then said Saul unto his armourbearer, Draw thy sword, and thrust me through therewith; lest these uncircumcised come and thrust me through, and abuse me. But his armourbearer would not; for he was sore afraid. Therefore Saul took a sword, and fell upon it.

5 And when his armourbearer saw that Saul was dead, he fell likewise upon his sword, and died with him.

6 So Saul died, and his three sons, and his armourbearer, and all his men, that same day together.

7 And when the men of Israel that were on the other side of the valley, and they that were on the other side Jordan, saw that the men of Israel fled, and that Saul and his sons were dead, they forsook the cities, and fled; and the Philistines came and dwelt in them.

8 And it came to pass on the morrow, when the Philistines came to strip the slain, that they found Saul and his three sons fallen in mount Gilboa.

9 And they cut off his head, and stripped off his armour, and sent into the land of the Philistines round about, to publish it in the house of their idols, and among the people.

10 And they put his armour in the house of Ashtaroth: and they fastened his body to the wall of Bethshan.

11 And when the inhabitants of Jabeshgilead heard of that which the Philistines had done to Saul;

12 All the valiant men arose, and went all night, and took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons from the wall of Bethshan, and came to Jabesh, and burnt them there.

13 And they took their bones, and buried them under a tree at Jabesh, and fasted seven days.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The Philistines fight against Israel on Mount Gilboa, the men of Israel flee and fall slain, the Philistines follow hard on Saul and slay his sons Jonathan, Abinadab and Melchishua, and the archers hit Saul and wound him sore. Saul tells his armourbearer to thrust him through lest the uncircumcised abuse him, the armourbearer will not for fear, and Saul takes a sword and falls upon it, and his armourbearer falls likewise upon his sword and dies with him, so that Saul, his three sons, his armourbearer and all his men die that same day together. The men of Israel beyond the valley and beyond Jordan forsake their cities and the Philistines dwell in them; next day the strippers find Saul and his sons on Gilboa, cut off his head, strip his armour, publish it in the house of their idols and among the people, put his armour in the house of Ashtaroth, and fasten his body to the wall of Bethshan. When Jabesh-gilead, the city Saul delivered in the first fruit of his reign, hears what the Philistines have done to Saul, all the valiant men rise, go all night, take the bodies from the wall, bring them to Jabesh, burn them there, bury the bones under a tree, and fast seven days, and the notes close by observing that Scripture makes no mention of what became of the souls of Saul and his sons after death, but of their bodies only, and secret things belong not to us. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Philistines fought against Israel, and the men of Israel fled before the Philistines and fell down slain in Mount Gilboa. The doom pronounced at Endor fell on the mountain where Saul had pitched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The Philistines followed hard upon Saul and his sons, and slew Jonathan, Abinadab, and Melchishua, Saul's sons. The king's house fell with the king's army; Jonathan, the covenant friend, died in his father's battle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The battle went sore against Saul, and the archers hit him, and he was sore wounded of the archers. The wounded king could neither resist nor flee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Saul told his armourbearer to thrust him through lest the uncircumcised abuse him, but the armourbearer would not for fear, so Saul took a sword and fell upon it. Saul, sorely wounded, expressed no concern about his never-dying soul, Henry observes, but only desired that the Philistines might not insult him or put him to pain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. When his armourbearer saw that Saul was dead, he fell likewise upon his sword and died with him. The servant followed his master even in self-destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. So Saul died, and his three sons, and his armourbearer, and all his men, that same day together. We cannot judge of the spiritual or eternal state of any by the manner of their death, Henry warns, for in that there is one event to the righteous and to the wicked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. When the men of Israel on the other side of the valley and beyond Jordan saw that Israel fled and that Saul and his sons were dead, they forsook the cities and fled, and the Philistines came and dwelt in them. The kingdom emptied before the conquerors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. On the morrow, when the Philistines came to strip the slain, they found Saul and his three sons fallen in Mount Gilboa. The strippers found the king among the stripped. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. They cut off his head, stripped off his armour, and sent into the land of the Philistines round about, to publish it in the house of their idols and among the people. The defeat was proclaimed as a triumph of Philistine gods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. They put his armour in the house of Ashtaroth, and fastened his body to the wall of Bethshan. The king's armour became a temple trophy, and his body a wall display. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. When the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead heard of what the Philistines had done to Saul — the city Saul had delivered in the first fruit of his reign now heard of his disgrace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. All the valiant men arose, went all night, took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons from the wall of Bethshan, and came to Jabesh, and burnt them there. The men of the rescued city risked a night march to rescue the fallen king's body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. They took their bones, buried them under a tree at Jabesh, and fasted seven days. Scripture makes no mention of what became of the souls of Saul and his sons after death, Henry notes, but of their bodies only, and secret things belong not to us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
