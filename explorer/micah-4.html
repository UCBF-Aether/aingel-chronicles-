The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Micah 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 But in the last days it shall come to pass, that the mountain of the house of the LORD shall be established in the top of the mountains, and it shall be exalted above the hills; and people shall flow unto it.

2 And many nations shall come, and say, Come, and let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, and to the house of the God of Jacob; and he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths: for the law shall go forth of Zion, and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.

3 And he shall judge among many people, and rebuke strong nations afar off; and they shall beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruninghooks: nation shall not lift up a sword against nation, neither shall they learn war any more.

4 But they shall sit every man under his vine and under his fig tree; and none shall make them afraid: for the mouth of the LORD of hosts hath spoken it.

5 For all people will walk every one in the name of his god, and we will walk in the name of the LORD our God for ever and ever.

6 In that day, saith the LORD, will I assemble her that halteth, and I will gather her that is driven out, and her that I have afflicted;

7 And I will make her that halted a remnant, and her that was cast far off a strong nation: and the LORD shall reign over them in mount Zion from henceforth, even for ever.

8 And thou, O tower of the flock, the strong hold of the daughter of Zion, unto thee shall it come, even the first dominion; the kingdom shall come to the daughter of Jerusalem.

9 Now why dost thou cry out aloud? is there no king in thee? is thy counsellor perished? for pangs have taken thee as a woman in travail.

10 Be in pain, and labour to bring forth, O daughter of Zion, like a woman in travail: for now shalt thou go forth out of the city, and thou shalt dwell in the field, and thou shalt go even to Babylon; there shalt thou be delivered; there the LORD shall redeem thee from the hand of thine enemies.

11 Now also many nations are gathered against thee, that say, Let her be defiled, and let our eye look upon Zion.

12 But they know not the thoughts of the LORD, neither understand they his counsel: for he shall gather them as the sheaves into the floor.

13 Arise and thresh, O daughter of Zion: for I will make thine horn iron, and I will make thy hoofs brass: and thou shalt beat in pieces many people: and I will consecrate their gain unto the LORD, and their substance unto the Lord of the whole earth.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

But in the last days the mountain of the house of the LORD will be established above the hills, and many nations will flow to it to learn God's ways, beating swords into plowshares and spears into pruninghooks, each sitting under his own vine and fig tree with none to make them afraid. The chapter looks from present exile in Babylon to deliverance there, and to the LORD reigning in Zion for ever. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the last days the mountain of the Lord's house will be established above the mountains, and peoples will flow to it. Henry reads this as a precious promise of the gospel church, which Christ himself will build upon a rock, to be more and more fulfilled because he who promised is faithful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Many nations will come, urging one another up to the mountain of the Lord, to be taught his ways and walk in his paths, for the law goes out from Zion. Henry's exhortation fits here: stir one another up to attend God's ordinances, that we may learn his ways and receive his law written on our hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. He will judge among many peoples and rebuke strong nations afar off, and they will beat swords into plowshares and spears into pruninghooks, learning war no more. Henry notes the nations have not yet so submitted to the Prince of Peace; this peace belongs to the kingdom of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Each will sit under his own vine and fig tree, and none will make them afraid, for the Lord of hosts has spoken it. The promise is certain because it rests on God's own word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. While all peoples walk each in the name of his god, God's people will walk in the name of the Lord their God for ever and ever. The Gentiles once worshipped idols; in the promised period God's people cleave to the Lord with full purpose of heart and delight in doing his will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. In that day the Lord will assemble her that halts and gather her that is driven out and afflicted. Henry explains "halteth" as walking not according to God's word, and the gathering of the Babylonian captives as an earnest of the church's healing, purifying and prospering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Her that halted will become a remnant, and her that was cast far off a strong nation, and the Lord will reign over them in Mount Zion for ever. Christ's reign continues until it is succeeded by the everlasting kingdom of heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. To the tower of the flock, the stronghold of the daughter of Zion, the first dominion will come: the kingdom will come to the daughter of Jerusalem. The glorious church for God set up in the world is the fulfilment of this first dominion restored. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Why does Zion cry aloud, as if she had no king and her counsellor had perished, in pangs like a woman in travail? Henry's counsel for distress fits here: believers should cry aloud under distress with the prayer of faith, not with despondency. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Daughter of Zion must labour like a woman in travail: she will go out of the city, dwell in the field, and go even to Babylon, and there she will be delivered and redeemed from her enemies. The Babylonian captivity itself becomes the place of deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Now many nations gather against Zion, saying let her be defiled and let their eye look upon her. They assemble to rejoice in her calamities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. But they do not know the Lord's thoughts or understand his counsel: he gathers them as sheaves into the threshing floor. Their assembly against Zion is, unknown to them, their own gathering for judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Arise and thresh, daughter of Zion, with a horn of iron and hoofs of brass, beating many peoples in pieces and consecrating their gain to the Lord of the whole earth. When God has conquering work for his people, Henry notes, he furnishes them with strength and ability for it; nothing yet in the Jewish church's history has matched this prediction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
