The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Micah 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Woe is me! for I am as when they have gathered the summer fruits, as the grapegleanings of the vintage: there is no cluster to eat: my soul desired the firstripe fruit.

2 The good man is perished out of the earth: and there is none upright among men: they all lie in wait for blood; they hunt every man his brother with a net.

3 That they may do evil with both hands earnestly, the prince asketh, and the judge asketh for a reward; and the great man, he uttereth his mischievous desire: so they wrap it up.

4 The best of them is as a brier: the most upright is sharper than a thorn hedge: the day of thy watchmen and thy visitation cometh; now shall be their perplexity.

5 Trust ye not in a friend, put ye not confidence in a guide: keep the doors of thy mouth from her that lieth in thy bosom.

6 For the son dishonoureth the father, the daughter riseth up against her mother, the daughter in law against her mother in law; a man’s enemies are the men of his own house.

7 Therefore I will look unto the LORD; I will wait for the God of my salvation: my God will hear me.

8 Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy: when I fall, I shall arise; when I sit in darkness, the LORD shall be a light unto me.

9 I will bear the indignation of the LORD, because I have sinned against him, until he plead my cause, and execute judgment for me: he will bring me forth to the light, and I shall behold his righteousness.

10 Then she that is mine enemy shall see it, and shame shall cover her which said unto me, Where is the LORD thy God? mine eyes shall behold her: now shall she be trodden down as the mire of the streets.

11 In the day that thy walls are to be built, in that day shall the decree be far removed.

12 In that day also he shall come even to thee from Assyria, and from the fortified cities, and from the fortress even to the river, and from sea to sea, and from mountain to mountain.

13 Notwithstanding the land shall be desolate because of them that dwell therein, for the fruit of their doings.

14 Feed thy people with thy rod, the flock of thine heritage, which dwell solitarily in the wood, in the midst of Carmel: let them feed in Bashan and Gilead, as in the days of old.

15 According to the days of thy coming out of the land of Egypt will I shew unto him marvellous things.

16 The nations shall see and be confounded at all their might: they shall lay their hand upon their mouth, their ears shall be deaf.

17 They shall lick the dust like a serpent, they shall move out of their holes like worms of the earth: they shall be afraid of the LORD our God, and shall fear because of thee.

18 Who is a God like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity, and passeth by the transgression of the remnant of his heritage? he retaineth not his anger for ever, because he delighteth in mercy.

19 He will turn again, he will have compassion upon us; he will subdue our iniquities; and thou wilt cast all their sins into the depths of the sea.

20 Thou wilt perform the truth to Jacob, and the mercy to Abraham, which thou hast sworn unto our fathers from the days of old.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Woe is me, says the prophet, for the good man has perished from the earth and the upright are gone, every man hunting his brother with a net, while princes, judges, and great men conspire together. Yet the prophet resolves to look to the LORD and wait for the God of his salvation, and the book ends with the question that becomes its praise: who is a God like unto thee, pardoning iniquity, delighting in mercy, casting all their sins into the depths of the sea, and keeping the promises sworn to Jacob and Abraham. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Woe is me, says the prophet: he is like one after the summer fruits are gathered and the vintage gleaned, with no cluster to eat, though his soul desired the firstripe fruit. Henry reads this as the prophet bemoaning that he lived among a people ripening apace for ruin, in which many good persons would suffer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The good man has perished from the earth and none is upright: all lie in wait for blood, hunting every man his brother with a net. Universal corruption shows itself in neighbours preying on neighbours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. They do evil with both hands earnestly: prince asks, judge asks for a reward, and the great man utters his mischievous desire, and together they wrap it up. Corruption is a joint work of rulers, judges and powerful men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The best of them is as a brier, the most upright sharper than a thorn hedge; the day of the watchmen and of visitation comes, and now will be their perplexity. Even the best of a corrupt people prick and wound. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Trust not in a friend, put no confidence in a guide, and keep the doors of your mouth from her who lies in your bosom. Men had no comfort or satisfaction, Henry notes, in their own families or nearest relations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The son dishonours the father, the daughter rises against her mother, the daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law, and a man's enemies are the men of his own house. Contempt and violation of domestic duties are a sad symptom of universal corruption, and those undutiful to their parents are never likely to come to good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Therefore I will look to the Lord; I will wait for the God of my salvation: my God will hear me. The prophet saw no safety or comfort but in looking to the Lord and waiting on God his salvation, and under trials we should look continually to our Divine Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Rejoice not against me, O my enemy: when I fall, I shall arise; when I sit in darkness, the Lord will be a light to me. Though cast down, Henry says, we are not destroyed; we may join hope in God's mercy with submission to his correction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. I will bear the indignation of the Lord because I have sinned against him, until he pleads my cause and executes judgment for me, bringing me forth to the light where I shall behold his righteousness. Those truly penitent for sin see great reason to be patient under affliction, and when we complain of the badness of the times we ought to complain against ourselves for the badness of our hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Then the enemy who said, "Where is the Lord thy God?" will see it and be covered with shame, and will be trodden down as the mire of the streets. Though enemies triumph and insult, they will be silenced and put to shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. In the day that your walls are to be built, the decree will be far removed. Though Zion's walls may long be in ruins, there will come a day when they are repaired. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. In that day he will come to you from Assyria and the fortified cities, from fortress to river, from sea to sea and mountain to mountain. Israel will come from all the remote parts, not turning back for discouragements, and no hindrances can prevent the favours the Lord intends for his church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Notwithstanding, the land will be desolate because of those who dwell in it, for the fruit of their doings. Deliverance does not cancel the desolation sin earned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Feed your people with your rod, the flock of your heritage dwelling solitarily in the wood, in the midst of Carmel, and let them feed in Bashan and Gilead as in days of old. Henry applies the prayer spiritually to Christ, the great Shepherd of the sheep, to take care of his church and go before them while they are in this world as in a wood, in it but not of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. According to the days of coming out of Egypt, God will show marvellous things. God promises, in answer to prayer, to repeat the miracles of former ages for his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The nations will see and be confounded at all their might, laying hand on mouth with ears deaf. As sin brought them into bondage, God's pardoning their sin brings them out, and the nations are silenced before it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. They will lick the dust like a serpent and move out of their holes like worms of the earth, afraid of the Lord our God and fearing because of his people. Enemies who seemed to prevail are humbled to the dust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Who is a God like you, pardoning iniquity and passing by the transgression of the remnant of his heritage, not retaining anger for ever because he delights in mercy? All who find pardoning mercy, Henry says, cannot but wonder at that mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. He will turn again and have compassion on us, subduing our iniquities and casting all their sins into the depths of the sea. When the Lord takes away the guilt of sin, he breaks its power so it may not have dominion; and he casts sins not near the shore, where they may appear again, but into the depth, never to rise again, forgiving all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. He will perform the truth to Jacob and the mercy to Abraham, sworn to the fathers from days of old. These engagements relate to Christ and the success of the gospel to the end of time, the future restoration of Israel, and the final prevailing of true religion in all lands; not one jot or tittle of his promised truth and mercy will fall to the ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
