The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And I saw another sign in heaven, great and marvellous, seven angels having the seven last plagues; for in them is filled up the wrath of God.

2 And I saw as it were a sea of glass mingled with fire: and them that had gotten the victory over the beast, and over his image, and over his mark, and over the number of his name, stand on the sea of glass, having the harps of God.

3 And they sing the song of Moses the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying, Great and marvellous are thy works, Lord God Almighty; just and true are thy ways, thou King of saints.

4 Who shall not fear thee, O Lord, and glorify thy name? for thou only art holy: for all nations shall come and worship before thee; for thy judgments are made manifest.

5 And after that I looked, and, behold, the temple of the tabernacle of the testimony in heaven was opened:

6 And the seven angels came out of the temple, having the seven plagues, clothed in pure and white linen, and having their breasts girded with golden girdles.

7 And one of the four beasts gave unto the seven angels seven golden vials full of the wrath of God, who liveth for ever and ever.

8 And the temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God, and from his power; and no man was able to enter into the temple, till the seven plagues of the seven angels were fulfilled.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Seven angels appear with the seven last plagues, in which the wrath of God is filled up, while those who have gotten victory over the beast, his image, his mark and the number of his name stand on a sea of glass mingled with fire, with harps, singing the song of Moses and the song of the Lamb — great and marvellous are thy works, just and true are thy ways. The temple of the tabernacle of the testimony in heaven is then opened, the seven angels come out clothed in pure white linen with golden girdles, one of the living creatures gives them seven golden vials full of God's wrath, and the temple fills with smoke from God's glory and power so that no one can enter until the plagues are fulfilled. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John saw another sign in heaven, great and marvellous: seven angels having the seven last plagues, for in them is filled up the wrath of God. Henry notes the work was to finish the destruction of antichrist, since Babylon's sins were filled up and she should now find the full measure of God's vindictive wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. John saw as it were a sea of glass mingled with fire, and those who had gotten the victory over the beast, and over his image, and over his mark, and over the number of his name, standing on the sea of glass, having the harps of God. Henry notes the sea may figure this brittle world, the gospel covenant like the temple's brazen sea, or the Red Sea congealed while Israel passed, with fire mingled as God's wrath against Pharaoh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They sing the song of Moses the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying, "Great and marvellous are thy works, Lord God Almighty; just and true are thy ways, thou King of saints." Henry notes they extol the greatness of God's works and the justice and truth of his ways, both in delivering his people and destroying their enemies, rejoicing in hope of what was not yet accomplished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They ask, "Who shall not fear thee, O Lord, and glorify thy name? for thou only art holy: for all nations shall come and worship before thee; for thy judgments are made manifest." Henry notes they call upon all nations to render God the fear, glory, and worship due to such a discovery of his truth and justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. After that John looked, and the temple of the tabernacle of the testimony in heaven was opened. Henry notes the allusion to the holiest of all, where the mercy-seat covered the ark of the testimony: God was fulfilling the prophecies and promises of his word, answering his people's prayers, and avenging the quarrel of his Son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The seven angels came out of the temple, having the seven plagues, clothed in pure and white linen, and having their breasts girded with golden girdles. Henry notes this was the high priests' habit when enquiring of God: the angels act under divine appointment, preparing a sacrifice to the Lord, ministers of divine justice doing everything in a pure and holy manner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. One of the four beasts gave unto the seven angels seven golden vials full of the wrath of God, who lives for ever and ever. Henry notes the vials were received from a minister of the true church, in answer to the prayers of ministers and people, to avenge their cause, and that God's wrath was divided into seven parts to fall successively on the antichristian party. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God and from his power, and no man was able to enter into the temple till the seven plagues of the seven angels were fulfilled. Henry notes antichrist's interests were so interwoven with civil interests that his destruction would shock the whole world, interrupting sabbaths and assemblies for a time, until the work done, the churches would have rest and the temple be opened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Vials. Golden bowls full of the wrath of God, given to the seven angels for the seven last plagues. Interpreted Study notes, verses 1 and 7, Matthew Henry
  • Song of Moses and of the Lamb. The victors' song on the sea of glass, joining deliverance remembered from Moses with the Lamb's work. Interpreted Study note, verse 3, Matthew Henry
