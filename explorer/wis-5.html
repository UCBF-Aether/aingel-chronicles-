Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Wisdom of Solomon 5 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Then shall the righteous man stand in great boldness before the face of such as have afflicted him, and made no account of his labours.

2 When they see it, they shall be troubled with terrible fear, and shall be amazed at the strangeness of his salvation, so far beyond all that they looked for.

3 And they repenting and groaning for anguish of spirit shall say within themselves, This was he, whom we had sometimes in derision, and a proverb of reproach:

4 We fools accounted his life madness, and his end to be without honour:

5 How is he numbered among the children of God, and his lot is among the saints!

6 Therefore have we erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness hath not shined unto us, and the sun of righteousness rose not upon us.

7 We wearied ourselves in the way of wickedness and destruction: yea, we have gone through deserts, where there lay no way: but as for the way of the Lord, we have not known it.

8 What hath pride profited us? or what good hath riches with our vaunting brought us?

9 All those things are passed away like a shadow, and as a post that hasted by;

10 And as a ship that passeth over the waves of the water, which when it is gone by, the trace thereof cannot be found, neither the pathway of the keel in the waves;

11 Or as when a bird hath flown through the air, there is no token of her way to be found, but the light air being beaten with the stroke of her wings and parted with the violent noise and motion of them, is passed through, and therein afterwards no sign where she went is to be found;

12 Or like as when an arrow is shot at a mark, it parteth the air, which immediately cometh together again, so that a man cannot know where it went through:

13 Even so we in like manner, as soon as we were born, began to draw to our end, and had no sign of virtue to shew; but were consumed in our own wickedness.

14 For the hope of the Godly is like dust that is blown away with the wind; like a thin froth that is driven away with the storm; like as the smoke which is dispersed here and there with a tempest, and passeth away as the remembrance of a guest that tarrieth but a day.

15 But the righteous live for evermore; their reward also is with the Lord, and the care of them is with the most High.

16 Therefore shall they receive a glorious kingdom, and a beautiful crown from the Lord's hand: for with his right hand shall he cover them, and with his arm shall he protect them.

17 He shall take to him his jealousy for complete armour, and make the creature his weapon for the revenge of his enemies.

18 He shall put on righteousness as a breastplate, and true judgment instead of an helmet.

19 He shall take holiness for an invincible shield.

20 His severe wrath shall he sharpen for a sword, and the world shall fight with him against the unwise.

21 Then shall the right aiming thunderbolts go abroad; and from the clouds, as from a well drawn bow, shall they fly to the mark.

22 And hailstones full of wrath shall be cast as out of a stone bow, and the water of the sea shall rage against them, and the floods shall cruelly drown them.

23 Yea, a mighty wind shall stand up against them, and like a storm shall blow them away: thus iniquity shall lay waste the whole earth, and ill dealing shall overthrow the thrones of the mighty.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The righteous stand in boldness before their former afflicters, who repent too late and confess they counted his life madness. Pride, riches and pleasure have passed like a shadow, a ship's wake, a bird's flight and an arrow's parting air. The righteous live for ever with their reward in the Lord, while creation itself, armed with breastplate, helmet, sword, thunderbolts and hail, fights against the ungodly. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then the righteous man shall stand in great boldness before those who afflicted him and made no account of his labours. Haydock's scene is the reversal: the afflicted stands, the afflicters answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. When they see it, they shall be troubled with terrible fear and amazed at the strangeness of his salvation, so far beyond what they looked for. Haydock notes their amazement: salvation comes in a form they never expected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. Repenting and groaning for anguish of spirit, they shall say within themselves: this was he whom we had sometimes in derision and a proverb of reproach. Haydock observes that derision turns to recognition too late. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. We fools esteemed his life madness and his end without honour. Haydock's point is that the world's verdict on the righteous is confessed as folly by the world itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. How is he numbered among God's children, and his lot among the saints? Haydock notes the question that undoes their former contempt: the despised man is counted a son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. Therefore we have erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness has not shined on us, nor the sun of understanding risen upon us. Haydock reads this as their own confession of the darkness they chose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. We wearied ourselves in the way of wickedness and destruction, and walked through hard deserts, but the Lord's way we have not known. Haydock observes that the path of wickedness wearies, while the Lord's way was never even sought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. What has pride profited us, or what good has riches with our vaunting brought us? Haydock notes the profit-and-loss account of pride: nothing remains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. All those things are passed away like a shadow, and as a post that hastens by. Haydock's image is speed: pride's goods pass like a runner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. As a ship passing through waves leaves no trace of its way, nor any path in the waves. Haydock notes the ship's wake vanishes, as does the ungodly life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. As a bird flying through air leaves no token of its way, but the air, beaten by wings and parted by force, is passed through with no sign after. Haydock's point is that even the swiftest, proudest passage leaves nothing behind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. As an arrow shot at a mark parts the air, which immediately closes, so that its passage is unknown. Haydock observes the arrow's path closes behind it, as the ungodly life closes without trace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. So we, as soon as we were born, began to draw to our end, and had no sign of virtue to show. Haydock notes the sum: a life that leaves no sign of virtue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. The hope of the ungodly is like dust blown away with wind, like thin froth driven by a storm, like smoke scattered by wind, and as the remembrance of a guest who tarried but a day. Haydock's four images all say the same: such hope does not endure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. But the righteous live for evermore; their reward is with the Lord, and the Most High cares for them. Haydock's contrast is the chapter's hinge: the perishing hope against the living righteous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. Therefore they shall receive a glorious kingdom and a beautiful crown from the Lord's hand, for with his right hand he shall cover them, and with his arm protect them. Haydock notes the crown and covering are God's own gift and defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. He shall take his zeal as complete armour and arm the creature for revenge on his enemies. Haydock pictures God arming creation itself as his instrument. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. He shall put on righteousness as a breastplate, and true judgment instead of a helmet. Haydock observes that God's armour is moral: righteousness and judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. He shall take holiness for an invincible shield. Haydock notes holiness as defence that cannot be pierced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. His severe wrath shall he sharpen for a sword, and the world shall fight with him against the unwise. Haydock's point is that creation joins its Maker against the unwise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. Then shall rightly aimed thunderbolts go abroad, and from the clouds, as from a well-drawn bow, they shall fly to the mark. Haydock notes judgment's aim is unerring. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. Hailstones full of wrath shall be cast as out of a stone bow, the sea shall rage against them, and floods shall cruelly drown them. Haydock pictures sea and flood as fellow-combatants in judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 23. A mighty wind shall stand up against them, and like a storm shall carry them away; thus iniquity shall lay waste the whole earth, and ill-dealing shall overthrow the thrones of the mighty. Haydock's close is that iniquity wastes the earth it ruled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
