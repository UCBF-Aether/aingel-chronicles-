2 And Adam named them all by their respective names, and as he called them, so was their name.

3 And on these five days Adam saw all these, male and female, according to every kind that was on the earth, but he was alone and found no help-

7 And He awaked Adam out of his sleep and on awaking he rose on the sixth day, and He brought her to him, and he knew her, and said unto her : " This is now bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she will be called [my] wife; because she was taken from her husband.

8 Therefore shall man and wife be one, and therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and cleave unto his wife, and they shall be one flesh.

10 And after Adam had completed forty days in the land where he had been created, we brought him into the Garden of Eden to till and keep it, but his wife they brought in on the eightieth day, and after this she entered into the Garden of Eden.

13 But in the case of a female child she shall remain in her uncleanness two weeks of days, according to the first two weeks, and sixty-six days in the blood of her purification, and they will be in all eighty days." 12.

15 Therefore, there was ordained regarding her who beareth a male or a female child the statute of those days that she should touch no hallowed thing, nor enter into the sanctuary until these days for the male or female child are accomplished.

16 This is the law and testimony which was written down for Israel, in order that they should observe (it) all the days.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: On the six days of the second week the angels brought to Adam, by God's word, all beasts, cattle, birds, movers on earth and movers in water, by kinds and types: beasts on the first day, cattle on the second, birds on the third, earth-movers on the fourth, water-movers on the fifth. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. On the six days of the second week the angels brought to Adam, by God's word, all beasts, cattle, birds, movers on earth and movers in water, by kinds and types: beasts on the first day, cattle on the second, birds on the third, earth-movers on the fourth, water-movers on the fifth. JFB on Genesis 2:19 notes God brought chiefly the animals in Adam's neighbourhood to be subservient to his use; Jubilees orders the showing across five days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Adam named them all by their respective names, and as he called them so was their name. JFB explains that Adam's perception and intelligence were supernaturally enlarged to know the characters, habits and uses of each species brought to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. On these five days Adam saw all these, male and female, by every kind on earth, but he was alone and found no helpmeet for him. JFB on Genesis 2:20 explains the design: each class came with its mate of the same nature, while Adam alone had no companion, so his want was made sensible to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. This verse completes the previous one: no helpmeet was found for Adam. The lack is the hinge on which the making of woman turns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Lord said it is not good that man should be alone, and proposed to make a helpmeet for him. JFB on Genesis 2:18 notes that amid plenty and delights Adam was conscious of feelings he could not gratify, and the remedy is a companion, not more creatures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The Lord caused a deep sleep to fall on Adam, took one rib from among his ribs as the origin of the woman, built up flesh in its stead, and built the woman. JFB on Genesis 2:21 reads the deep sleep as probably an ecstasy or trance like the prophets' visions, and repeats the saying that she was taken not from his head to surpass him nor from his feet to be trampled on, but from his side to be equal to him and near his heart to be dear to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Adam awoke on the sixth day, she was brought to him, and he said, 'This is now bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she will be called wife, because she was taken from her husband.' JFB on Genesis 2:23 notes 'woman' is in Hebrew 'man-ess,' marking her derivation from man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Therefore man and wife shall be one, and a man shall leave his father and mother, cleave to his wife, and they shall be one flesh. JFB on Genesis 2:24 appeals to our Lord's use of the passage as the divine institution of marriage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Adam was created in the first week and the rib, his wife, shown to him in the second week, and for this reason the commandment was given to keep in defilement seven days for a male and twice seven for a female. Charles's thesis is on display: the creation order itself grounds the purification law, which the book treats as written on the heavenly tables before Sinai. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. After Adam completed forty days in the land where he had been created, he was brought into the Garden of Eden to till and keep it, but his wife was brought in on the eightieth day and only then entered Eden. The forty and eighty days match the purification periods of the next verses: Eden's entry already follows the law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. This verse introduces the reason the commandment is written on the heavenly tables regarding her that gives birth, quoted in full in the next verses. The heavenly tables are the book's divine statute book, of which the Mosaic law is the earthly reproduction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. If she bears a male, she remains in uncleanness seven days according to the first week of days, and thirty-three days in the blood of her purifying, touching no hallowed thing nor entering the sanctuary until those days are accomplished. The seven-plus-thirty-three pattern mirrors Adam's forty days before entering Eden, the holier place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. For a female child she remains in uncleanness two weeks of days, according to the first two weeks, and sixty-six days in the blood of her purification, eighty days in all. The doubling matches the wife's eightieth-day entry into Eden in verse 10. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. When she had completed these eighty days she was brought into the Garden of Eden, for it is holier than all the earth besides and every tree planted in it is holy. Eden's superior holiness is why entry follows purification, as the sanctuary will later require. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. Therefore the statute was ordained for her who bears a male or female child: touch no hallowed thing nor enter the sanctuary until the days for the male or female child are accomplished. The Eden pattern is restated as a general statute for Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. This is the law and testimony written down for Israel, to be observed all the days. The chapter's refrain is Charles's central claim: what happened in Eden is already law, written down before Sinai for Israel to keep always. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. In the first week of the first jubilee Adam and his wife were in the Garden of Eden for seven years, tilling and keeping it, given work and instructed in everything suitable for tillage. JFB on Genesis 2:15 notes the garden work was both pleasant employment and probation, the garden itself a temple where man worshipped; Jubilees extends the stay to seven years of instructed tillage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He tilled the garden, naked and not knowing it, not ashamed, protecting the garden from birds, beasts and cattle, and gathering its fruit. Nakedness without shame marks innocence, and 'keeping' the garden includes guarding it, as JFB notes on Genesis 2:15. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. This verse continues the garden life: Adam gathered the fruit, ate, and put aside the residue for himself and his wife. After the completion of the seven years exactly, in the second month on the seventeenth day, the serpent came and approached the woman. The precise dating is the book's habit: even the fall is fixed in its jubilee calendar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. The serpent said to the woman, 'Hath God commanded you, saying, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?' JFB on Genesis 3:1 explains the serpent was real but the instrument of a higher agent, Satan, who insinuated doubt about God's will and attacked the woman alone, unfortified by her husband's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. She answered that of all the garden's fruit God said 'Eat,' but of the tree in the midst God said neither eat nor touch it, lest they die. JFB on Genesis 3:2 notes she extolled their liberty and declared the prohibition certain, yet her 'lest ye die' already softens God's 'ye shall surely die,' as if the fruit were merely poisonous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The serpent answered, 'Ye shall not surely die, for God knows that on the day ye eat, your eyes will be opened, and ye will be as gods, knowing good and evil.' JFB on Genesis 3:4-5 notes the tempter assured impunity and promised benefits, concealing that opened eyes would mean a direful experience of good and evil, the misery of a sinful condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The woman saw the tree was agreeable and pleasant to the eye and its fruit good for food, and she took and ate. JFB on Genesis 3:6 traces the pattern of every temptation: outward attraction, inward commotion, the triumph of passionate desire, ending in ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. When she had first covered her shame with fig-leaves, she gave to Adam and he ate, and his eyes were opened and he saw he was naked. JFB notes the fall of Eve was soon followed by that of Adam, and opened eyes here meant shame, not godhead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. He took fig-leaves, sewed them together, made an apron, and covered his shame. The first human covering is sewn from the garden itself, a hasty answer to a newly known nakedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. God cursed the serpent and was wroth with it forever. JFB on Genesis 3:14 distinguishes the doom on the material serpent, cursed above all creatures, from the doom on the spiritual seducer, whose power would be wholly destroyed by the offspring of those he deceived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. God was wroth with the woman because she hearkened to the serpent's voice and ate, saying he would greatly multiply her sorrow and pains, she would bring forth children in sorrow, her return would be to her husband, and he would rule over her. JFB on Genesis 3:16 reads this as her doom as wife and mother: from helpmeet partner to humble subjection, with pain of body and distress of mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. To Adam God said that because he hearkened to his wife's voice and ate of the forbidden tree, the ground is cursed for his sake, bringing thorns and thistles, and he will eat bread in the sweat of his face until he returns to the earth from which he was taken, for earth he is and to earth he will return. JFB on Genesis 3:17-19 notes livelihood that was easy and pleasant now requires painful, persevering exertion, and man became mortal, his body changed toward dissolution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. He made coats of skin for them, clothed them, and sent them forth from the Garden of Eden. Judgment comes with provision: God himself clothes them before expelling them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. On the day Adam went forth from the garden he offered as a sweet savour frankincense, galbanum, stacte and spices in the morning with the rising of the sun, from the day he covered his shame, and on that day the mouth of all beasts, cattle, birds and movers was closed so they could no longer speak, for they had all spoken with one lip and one tongue. Jubilees makes Adam the first offerer, keeping sacrifice before Sinai, and dates the silencing of animal speech to the expulsion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. God sent out of Eden all flesh that was in it, and all flesh was scattered by kinds and types to the places created for them. The garden's creatures are dispersed to their appointed places, matching the ordered kinds of chapter 2. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. To Adam alone did God give the wherewithal to cover his shame, of all the beasts and cattle. The coats of skin are Adam's alone, setting the first man apart from the beasts whose speech was just taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. On this account it is prescribed on the heavenly tables, for all who know the judgment of the law, that they should cover their shame and not uncover themselves as the Gentiles uncover themselves. Eden's clothing becomes a standing statute of modesty, written before Sinai and binding on those who know the law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. On the new moon of the fourth month Adam and his wife went forth from the Garden of Eden. The departure, like the temptation, is fixed to the book's calendar: a new moon in the fourth month. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. They dwelt in the land of Elda, in the land of their creation. Exile ends where creation began: they settle in the very land Adam was formed from, outside the garden but not outside God's ordering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. Adam called his wife's name Eve. The naming closes the garden account and opens the life outside it, in the land of Elda where the first family will now live. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. They had no son until the first jubilee, and after this he knew her. The book's chronology delays the first birth a full jubilee, so that the generations that follow can be counted in its weeks and jubilees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 28. God cursed the serpent and was wroth with it forever. JFB on Genesis 3:14 distinguishes the doom on the material serpent, cursed above all creatures, from the doom on the spiritual seducer, whose power would be wholly destroyed by the offspring of those he deceived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. God was wroth with the woman because she hearkened to the serpent's voice and ate, saying he would greatly multiply her sorrow and pains, she would bring forth children in sorrow, her return would be to her husband, and he would rule over her. JFB on Genesis 3:16 reads this as her doom as wife and mother: from helpmeet partner to humble subjection, with pain of body and distress of mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. To Adam God said that because he hearkened to his wife's voice and ate of the forbidden tree, the ground is cursed for his sake, bringing thorns and thistles, and he will eat bread in the sweat of his face until he returns to the earth from which he was taken, for earth he is and to earth he will return. JFB on Genesis 3:17-19 notes livelihood that was easy and pleasant now requires painful, persevering exertion, and man became mortal, his body changed toward dissolution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. He made coats of skin for them, clothed them, and sent them forth from the Garden of Eden. Judgment comes with provision: God himself clothes them before expelling them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

R. H. Charles

  • Verse 9. Adam was created in the first week and the rib, his wife, shown to him in the second week, and for this reason the commandment was given to keep in defilement seven days for a male and twice seven for a female. Charles's thesis is on display: the creation order itself grounds the purification law, which the book treats as written on the heavenly tables before Sinai. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. After Adam completed forty days in the land where he had been created, he was brought into the Garden of Eden to till and keep it, but his wife was brought in on the eightieth day and only then entered Eden. The forty and eighty days match the purification periods of the next verses: Eden's entry already follows the law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. This verse introduces the reason the commandment is written on the heavenly tables regarding her that gives birth, quoted in full in the next verses. The heavenly tables are the book's divine statute book, of which the Mosaic law is the earthly reproduction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. If she bears a male, she remains in uncleanness seven days according to the first week of days, and thirty-three days in the blood of her purifying, touching no hallowed thing nor entering the sanctuary until those days are accomplished. The seven-plus-thirty-three pattern mirrors Adam's forty days before entering Eden, the holier place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. For a female child she remains in uncleanness two weeks of days, according to the first two weeks, and sixty-six days in the blood of her purification, eighty days in all. The doubling matches the wife's eightieth-day entry into Eden in verse 10. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. When she had completed these eighty days she was brought into the Garden of Eden, for it is holier than all the earth besides and every tree planted in it is holy. Eden's superior holiness is why entry follows purification, as the sanctuary will later require. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. Therefore the statute was ordained for her who bears a male or female child: touch no hallowed thing nor enter the sanctuary until the days for the male or female child are accomplished. The Eden pattern is restated as a general statute for Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. This is the law and testimony written down for Israel, to be observed all the days. The chapter's refrain is Charles's central claim: what happened in Eden is already law, written down before Sinai for Israel to keep always. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. This verse continues the garden life: Adam gathered the fruit, ate, and put aside the residue for himself and his wife. After the completion of the seven years exactly, in the second month on the seventeenth day, the serpent came and approached the woman. The precise dating is the book's habit: even the fall is fixed in its jubilee calendar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. On the day Adam went forth from the garden he offered as a sweet savour frankincense, galbanum, stacte and spices in the morning with the rising of the sun, from the day he covered his shame, and on that day the mouth of all beasts, cattle, birds and movers was closed so they could no longer speak, for they had all spoken with one lip and one tongue. Jubilees makes Adam the first offerer, keeping sacrifice before Sinai, and dates the silencing of animal speech to the expulsion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. God sent out of Eden all flesh that was in it, and all flesh was scattered by kinds and types to the places created for them. The garden's creatures are dispersed to their appointed places, matching the ordered kinds of chapter 2. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. To Adam alone did God give the wherewithal to cover his shame, of all the beasts and cattle. The coats of skin are Adam's alone, setting the first man apart from the beasts whose speech was just taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. On this account it is prescribed on the heavenly tables, for all who know the judgment of the law, that they should cover their shame and not uncover themselves as the Gentiles uncover themselves. Eden's clothing becomes a standing statute of modesty, written before Sinai and binding on those who know the law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. On the new moon of the fourth month Adam and his wife went forth from the Garden of Eden. The departure, like the temptation, is fixed to the book's calendar: a new moon in the fourth month. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. They dwelt in the land of Elda, in the land of their creation. Exile ends where creation began: they settle in the very land Adam was formed from, outside the garden but not outside God's ordering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. Adam called his wife's name Eve. The naming closes the garden account and opens the life outside it, in the land of Elda where the first family will now live. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. They had no son until the first jubilee, and after this he knew her. The book's chronology delays the first birth a full jubilee, so that the generations that follow can be counted in its weeks and jubilees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
