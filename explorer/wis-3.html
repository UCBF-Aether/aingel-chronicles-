The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Wisdom of Solomon 3 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and there shall no torment touch them.

2 In the sight of the unwise they seemed to die: and their departure is taken for misery,

3 And their going from us to be utter destruction: but they are in peace.

4 For though they be punished in the sight of men, yet is their hope full of immortality.

5 And having been a little chastised, they shall be greatly rewarded: for God proved them, and found them worthy for himself.

6 As gold in the furnace hath he tried them, and received them as a burnt offering.

7 And in the time of their visitation they shall shine, and run to and fro like sparks among the stubble.

8 They shall judge the nations, and have dominion over the people, and their Lord shall reign for ever.

9 They that put their trust in him shall understand the truth: and such as be faithful in love shall abide with him: for grace and mercy is to his saints, and he hath care for his elect.

10 But the ungodly shall be punished according to their own imaginations, which have neglected the righteous, and forsaken the Lord.

11 For whoso despiseth wisdom and nurture, he is miserable, and their hope is vain, their labours unfruitful, and their works unprofitable:

12 Their wives are foolish, and their children wicked:

13 Their offspring is cursed. Wherefore blessed is the barren that is undefiled, which hath not known the sinful bed: she shall have fruit in the visitation of souls.

14 And blessed is the eunuch, which with his hands hath wrought no iniquity, nor imagined wicked things against God: for unto him shall be given the special gift of faith, and an inheritance in the temple of the Lord more acceptable to his mind.

15 For glorious is the fruit of good labours: and the root of wisdom shall never fall away.

16 As for the children of adulterers, they shall not come to their perfection, and the seed of an unrighteous bed shall be rooted out.

17 For though they live long, yet shall they be nothing regarded: and their last age shall be without honour.

18 Or, if they die quickly, they have no hope, neither comfort in the day of trial.

19 For horrible is the end of the unrighteous generation.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The souls of the righteous are in God's hand and no torment touches them, though to the unwise they seemed to die and their departure looked like destruction. They are in peace, their hope full of immortality, tried like gold in the furnace and destined to shine and judge. The ungodly, by contrast, are punished by their own imaginations, and their offspring comes to nothing. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The souls of the righteous are in God's hand, and no torment shall touch them. Haydock's reading is that death cannot reach what God himself holds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. In the sight of the unwise they seemed to die, and their departure was taken for misery. Haydock notes the unwise judge by sight alone, mistaking departure for destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. Their going from us was taken for utter destruction, but they are in peace. Haydock stresses the correction: what looks like ruin is peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. Though in men's sight they were punished, their hope is full of immortality. Haydock observes that apparent punishment and real hope stand together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. Having been a little chastised, they shall be greatly rewarded, for God proved them and found them worthy of himself. Haydock reads chastisement here as proving, not rejection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. As gold in the furnace he tried them, and received them as a burnt offering. Haydock notes the furnace image: trial purifies rather than consumes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. In the time of their visitation they shall shine and run to and fro like sparks among stubble. Haydock's point is that visitation, dreaded by the ungodly, becomes the righteous' shining. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. They shall judge nations and have dominion over peoples, and their Lord shall reign for ever. Haydock observes the reversal: the judged become judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. Those who put trust in God shall understand truth, and the faithful in love shall abide with him, for grace and mercy belong to his saints. Haydock stresses that abiding with God is the portion of trust and love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. The ungodly shall be punished according to their imaginations, for they neglected the righteous and forsook the Lord. Haydock notes punishment fits the imagination that devised evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. Whoever despises wisdom and nurture is miserable, and their hope is vain, their labours unfruitful, their works unprofitable. Haydock's point is that contempt for wisdom empties every enterprise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. Their wives are foolish and their children wicked. Haydock reads this as the household fruit of a wisdom despised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. Their offspring is cursed. Haydock notes the curse follows the parents' rejection of God into the next generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. Blessed is the barren that is undefiled, who has not known the sinful bed; she shall have fruit in the visitation of souls. Haydock observes that fruitfulness is counted by faithfulness, not by children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. The fruit of good labours is glorious, and the root of understanding cannot fail. Haydock stresses that understanding, once rooted, does not wither. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. As for the children of adulterers, they shall not come to perfection, and the seed of an unlawful bed shall be rooted out. Haydock reads this as the cutting off of a line begun in sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. Though they live long, they shall be nothing regarded, and their last age shall be without honour. Haydock notes length of life without honour is no blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. If they die quickly, they have no hope, nor comfort in the day of trial. Haydock's point is that a short life leaves the same emptiness when hope is absent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. The end of the unrighteous generation is dreadful. Haydock sums the chapter: a generation that forsakes wisdom ends in dread, while the righteous, who seemed to die, are in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
