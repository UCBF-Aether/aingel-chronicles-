Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 9 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 I will praise thee, O LORD, with my whole heart; I will shew forth all thy marvellous works.

2 I will be glad and rejoice in thee: I will sing praise to thy name, O thou most High.

3 When mine enemies are turned back, they shall fall and perish at thy presence.

4 For thou hast maintained my right and my cause; thou satest in the throne judging right.

5 Thou hast rebuked the heathen, thou hast destroyed the wicked, thou hast put out their name for ever and ever.

6 O thou enemy, destructions are come to a perpetual end: and thou hast destroyed cities; their memorial is perished with them.

7 But the LORD shall endure for ever: he hath prepared his throne for judgment.

8 And he shall judge the world in righteousness, he shall minister judgment to the people in uprightness.

9 The LORD also will be a refuge for the oppressed, a refuge in times of trouble.

10 And they that know thy name will put their trust in thee: for thou, LORD, hast not forsaken them that seek thee.

11 Sing praises to the LORD, which dwelleth in Zion: declare among the people his doings.

12 When he maketh inquisition for blood, he remembereth them: he forgetteth not the cry of the humble.

13 Have mercy upon me, O LORD; consider my trouble which I suffer of them that hate me, thou that liftest me up from the gates of death:

14 That I may shew forth all thy praise in the gates of the daughter of Zion: I will rejoice in thy salvation.

15 The heathen are sunk down in the pit that they made: in the net which they hid is their own foot taken.

16 The LORD is known by the judgment which he executeth: the wicked is snared in the work of his own hands. Higgaion. Selah.

17 The wicked shall be turned into hell, and all the nations that forget God.

18 For the needy shall not alway be forgotten: the expectation of the poor shall not perish for ever.

19 Arise, O LORD; let not man prevail: let the heathen be judged in thy sight.

20 Put them in fear, O LORD: that the nations may know themselves to be but men. Selah.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Wholehearted praise for God's past judgments, enemies turned back, the wicked's memory erased, the Lord enthroned for ever as refuge for the oppressed, joined to a present plea for mercy, so that deliverance may be praised in Zion and the nations may learn they are but men. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David praises with his whole heart and shows forth all God's marvellous works: praise is wholehearted and specific, recounting deeds, not vague feeling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He is glad and rejoices in God, singing praise to the name of the Most High: joy and song belong together in worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. His enemies are turned back, falling and perishing at God's presence: their defeat is ascribed to God's appearing, not to David's sword alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. God has maintained David's right and cause, sitting on the throne judging right: the Judge has heard the case and decided justly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The heathen are rebuked, the wicked destroyed, their name put out for ever: judgment erases even the memory of proud power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The enemy's destructions are ended perpetually, their cities destroyed, their memorial perished: what they built to last is forgotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But the Lord endures for ever and has prepared his throne for judgment: human empires pass, the divine throne does not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He judges the world in righteousness and ministers judgment in uprightness: the standard is God's own character, applied without partiality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The Lord is a refuge for the oppressed, a refuge in times of trouble: the afflicted have somewhere to flee, and it is a person, not a place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Those who know God's name will trust him, for he has not forsaken those who seek him: knowledge of God, proved by experience, breeds trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Sing praises to the Lord who dwells in Zion and declare his doings among the people: worship in Zion must become witness to the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. When God makes inquisition for blood he remembers the afflicted and does not forget the cry of the humble: murder and oppression do not escape his account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. David now pleads for mercy in his own trouble from haters, asking the God who lifts him from the gates of death to consider him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. That he may show forth God's praise in the gates of Zion and rejoice in salvation: deliverance is sought so that it may be publicly praised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The heathen sink in the pit they made, their own foot taken in the hidden net: their secret devices trap themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The Lord is known by the judgment he executes, and the wicked is snared in his own hands' work: God's character becomes visible in retribution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The wicked shall be turned into hell, and all nations that forget God: forgetfulness of God is itself the road to ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Yet the needy shall not always be forgotten, nor the poor's expectation perish for ever: delay is not abandonment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Arise, O Lord, let not man prevail; let the heathen be judged in God's sight, that power may be measured against justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Put them in fear, that the nations may know themselves to be but men: the lesson of judgment is humility — proud nations are mortal after all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
