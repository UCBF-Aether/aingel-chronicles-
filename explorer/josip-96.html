Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter argues one nature after the union by the soul-and-body analogy (when flesh is named, the soul is not separated, and vice versa), cites John of the Golden Mouth and Peter of Alexandria on the Nativity, and pronounces anathemas on those who say God was not born or that a man was created and dwelt upon. It then turns to the aftermath of Chalcedon, slayings, plunder, and the reigns of Leo the Great, his son, and Anastasius, with Severus of Antioch named.
