The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Nehemiah 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the rulers of the people dwelt at Jerusalem: the rest of the people also cast lots, to bring one of ten to dwell in Jerusalem the holy city, and nine parts to dwell in other cities.

2 And the people blessed all the men, that willingly offered themselves to dwell at Jerusalem.

3 Now these are the chief of the province that dwelt in Jerusalem: but in the cities of Judah dwelt every one in his possession in their cities, to wit, Israel, the priests, and the Levites, and the Nethinims, and the children of Solomon’s servants.

4 And at Jerusalem dwelt certain of the children of Judah, and of the children of Benjamin. Of the children of Judah; Athaiah the son of Uzziah, the son of Zechariah, the son of Amariah, the son of Shephatiah, the son of Mahalaleel, of the children of Perez;

5 And Maaseiah the son of Baruch, the son of Colhozeh, the son of Hazaiah, the son of Adaiah, the son of Joiarib, the son of Zechariah, the son of Shiloni.

6 All the sons of Perez that dwelt at Jerusalem were four hundred threescore and eight valiant men.

7 And these are the sons of Benjamin; Sallu the son of Meshullam, the son of Joed, the son of Pedaiah, the son of Kolaiah, the son of Maaseiah, the son of Ithiel, the son of Jesaiah.

8 And after him Gabbai, Sallai, nine hundred twenty and eight.

9 And Joel the son of Zichri was their overseer: and Judah the son of Senuah was second over the city.

10 Of the priests: Jedaiah the son of Joiarib, Jachin.

11 Seraiah the son of Hilkiah, the son of Meshullam, the son of Zadok, the son of Meraioth, the son of Ahitub, was the ruler of the house of God.

12 And their brethren that did the work of the house were eight hundred twenty and two: and Adaiah the son of Jeroham, the son of Pelaliah, the son of Amzi, the son of Zechariah, the son of Pashur, the son of Malchiah.

13 And his brethren, chief of the fathers, two hundred forty and two: and Amashai the son of Azareel, the son of Ahasai, the son of Meshillemoth, the son of Immer,

14 And their brethren, mighty men of valour, an hundred twenty and eight: and their overseer was Zabdiel, the son of one of the great men.

15 Also of the Levites: Shemaiah the son of Hashub, the son of Azrikam, the son of Hashabiah, the son of Bunni;

16 And Shabbethai and Jozabad, of the chief of the Levites, had the oversight of the outward business of the house of God.

17 And Mattaniah the son of Micha, the son of Zabdi, the son of Asaph, was the principal to begin the thanksgiving in prayer: and Bakbukiah the second among his brethren, and Abda the son of Shammua, the son of Galal, the son of Jeduthun.

18 All the Levites in the holy city were two hundred fourscore and four.

19 Moreover the porters, Akkub, Talmon, and their brethren that kept the gates, were an hundred seventy and two.

20 And the residue of Israel, of the priests, and the Levites, were in all the cities of Judah, every one in his inheritance.

21 But the Nethinims dwelt in Ophel: and Ziha and Gispa were over the Nethinims.

22 The overseer also of the Levites at Jerusalem was Uzzi the son of Bani, the son of Hashabiah, the son of Mattaniah, the son of Micha. Of the sons of Asaph, the singers were over the business of the house of God.

23 For it was the king’s commandment concerning them, that a certain portion should be for the singers, due for every day.

24 And Pethahiah the son of Meshezabeel, of the children of Zerah the son of Judah, was at the king’s hand in all matters concerning the people.

25 And for the villages, with their fields, some of the children of Judah dwelt at Kirjatharba, and in the villages thereof, and at Dibon, and in the villages thereof, and at Jekabzeel, and in the villages thereof,

26 And at Jeshua, and at Moladah, and at Bethphelet,

27 And at Hazarshual, and at Beersheba, and in the villages thereof,

28 And at Ziklag, and at Mekonah, and in the villages thereof,

29 And at Enrimmon, and at Zareah, and at Jarmuth,

30 Zanoah, Adullam, and in their villages, at Lachish, and the fields thereof, at Azekah, and in the villages thereof. And they dwelt from Beersheba unto the valley of Hinnom.

31 The children also of Benjamin from Geba dwelt at Michmash, and Aija, and Bethel, and in their villages.

32 And at Anathoth, Nob, Ananiah,

33 Hazor, Ramah, Gittaim,

34 Hadid, Zeboim, Neballat,

35 Lod, and Ono, the valley of craftsmen.

36 And of the Levites were divisions in Judah, and in Benjamin.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

The rulers already dwell in Jerusalem, but the city needs inhabitants, so the rest of the people cast lots to bring one in ten to live in the holy city, while those who willingly offer themselves are blessed. The chapter then lists who dwells in Jerusalem, priests, Levites, porters, singers, and temple servants, and the villages of Judah and Benjamin where the rest settle. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Cast lots. drawing lots to choose, here one person in ten to dwell in Jerusalem (verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Holy city. Jerusalem, so called as the city of the temple, where stricter living and greater danger were expected (study note on verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The rulers dwelt in Jerusalem the holy city, while the rest of the people cast lots to bring one in ten to dwell there. Henry explains the reluctance: stricter living was expected in the holy city, it was the post of danger most hated by neighbours, and country living paid better; yet the rulers' example would draw others, for the mighty are magnetic. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The people blessed all the men who willingly offered themselves to dwell at Jerusalem. Henry notes they praised them, prayed for them, and praised God for them, and that God and man bless those who are public blessings, which should encourage zeal in doing good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. At Jerusalem dwelt certain of the children of Judah and of Benjamin. Henry notes the city lay partly in each tribe's lot, mostly Benjamin's, which is why Judah's families there were fewer but Benjamin's more numerous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. All the sons of Perez who dwelt at Jerusalem were four hundred sixty-eight valiant men. Henry stresses their character: though fewer than Benjamin's, the men of Judah were valiant and fit to defend the city, Judah keeping its ancient character of a lion's whelp, bold and daring. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The sons of Benjamin in Jerusalem begin with Sallu son of Meshullam, traced through Joed, Pedaiah, Kolaiah, Maaseiah, Ithiel, and Jesaiah. Henry counts Benjamin's households as the larger share of the city's new population. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. After Sallu came Gabbai and Sallai, nine hundred twenty-eight in all. Henry contrasts the totals: 468 of Judah against 928 of Benjamin, small beginnings that would grow much more populous before the Saviour's time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Joel son of Zichri was their overseer, and Judah son of Senuah was second over the city. Henry notes good order within is as necessary as fortification without: a people need good magistrates as well as good soldiers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The brethren who did the work of the house numbered eight hundred twenty-two. Henry counts the temple workers: 822 of one priestly family, 242 of another, and 128 of another, observing it was well that those labourers were not few. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Their brethren, chief of the fathers, numbered two hundred forty-two. Henry includes this family among the priests settled at Jerusalem, where their business lay, since the temple was there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Their brethren, mighty men of valour, numbered one hundred twenty-eight, overseen by Zabdiel, son of one of the great men. Henry notes valour was necessary because the priesthood was not only a work requiring might but a warfare, and that it was no disparagement for a great man's son to be in the priesthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Shabbethai and Jozabad, chief Levites, had oversight of the outward business of the house of God. Henry explains the priests managed the inward temple business, while these Levites oversaw secular concerns, collecting contributions and providing materials, without which the inward work would have starved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Mattaniah son of Micha, son of Zabdi, son of Asaph, was the principal to begin the thanksgiving in prayer, with Bakbukiah second and Abda among his brethren. Henry notes he was likely a skilled singer chosen as precentor, and that thanksgiving belongs with prayer, done decently and in order, one beginning and the rest following. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. All the Levites in the holy city were two hundred eighty-four. Henry notes this was few in comparison, because much of the Levites' work was teaching the good knowledge of God up and down the country, for which they were to be scattered in Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The porters, Akkub, Talmon, and their brethren who kept the gates, numbered one hundred seventy-two. Henry counts them with the Levites attending at Jerusalem, while the rest served elsewhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The residue of Israel, priests, and Levites were in all the cities of Judah, each in his inheritance. Henry stresses the balance: Jerusalem must be replenished, yet not so as to drain the country, for the king himself is served of the field and both city and village dwellers are needful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The Nethinims dwelt in Ophel, with Ziha and Gispa over them. Henry notes they were the posterity of the Gibeonites, posted on the wall near the temple because they did its servile work and had to be ready to attend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Uzzi son of Bani oversaw the Levites at Jerusalem, and the sons of Asaph, the singers, were over the business of the house of God. Henry notes the Levites, though dispersed through Judah's cities, had this Jerusalem overseer to direct them and take cognizance of their conduct, and that ingenious singers with leisure between services looked after temple repairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. It was the king's commandment concerning the singers that a certain portion should be theirs, due every day. Henry notes the Persian king's kindness to their office, allotting them a maintenance besides what belonged to them as Levites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Pethahiah son of Meshezabeel, of the children of Zerah son of Judah, was at the king's hand in all matters concerning the people. Henry explains his office: to settle controversies between the king's officers and subjects, see the king's dues paid in, and the temple allowance paid out, and counts it happy that one of the Jews held the post. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Of the Levites there were divisions in Judah and in Benjamin. Henry closes the chapter with these Levite divisions among both tribes, completing the account of the villages inhabited by the residue of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
