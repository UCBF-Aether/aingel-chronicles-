The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the LORD: and the LORD delivered them into the hand of Midian seven years.

2 And the hand of Midian prevailed against Israel: and because of the Midianites the children of Israel made them the dens which are in the mountains, and caves, and strong holds.

3 And so it was, when Israel had sown, that the Midianites came up, and the Amalekites, and the children of the east, even they came up against them;

4 And they encamped against them, and destroyed the increase of the earth, till thou come unto Gaza, and left no sustenance for Israel, neither sheep, nor ox, nor ass.

5 For they came up with their cattle and their tents, and they came as grasshoppers for multitude; for both they and their camels were without number: and they entered into the land to destroy it.

6 And Israel was greatly impoverished because of the Midianites; and the children of Israel cried unto the LORD.

7 And it came to pass, when the children of Israel cried unto the LORD because of the Midianites,

8 That the LORD sent a prophet unto the children of Israel, which said unto them, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, I brought you up from Egypt, and brought you forth out of the house of bondage;

9 And I delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians, and out of the hand of all that oppressed you, and drave them out from before you, and gave you their land;

10 And I said unto you, I am the LORD your God; fear not the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye dwell: but ye have not obeyed my voice.

11 And there came an angel of the LORD, and sat under an oak which was in Ophrah, that pertained unto Joash the Abiezrite: and his son Gideon threshed wheat by the winepress, to hide it from the Midianites.

12 And the angel of the LORD appeared unto him, and said unto him, The LORD is with thee, thou mighty man of valour.

13 And Gideon said unto him, Oh my Lord, if the LORD be with us, why then is all this befallen us? and where be all his miracles which our fathers told us of, saying, Did not the LORD bring us up from Egypt? but now the LORD hath forsaken us, and delivered us into the hands of the Midianites.

14 And the LORD looked upon him, and said, Go in this thy might, and thou shalt save Israel from the hand of the Midianites: have not I sent thee?

15 And he said unto him, Oh my Lord, wherewith shall I save Israel? behold, my family is poor in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father’s house.

16 And the LORD said unto him, Surely I will be with thee, and thou shalt smite the Midianites as one man.

17 And he said unto him, If now I have found grace in thy sight, then shew me a sign that thou talkest with me.

18 Depart not hence, I pray thee, until I come unto thee, and bring forth my present, and set it before thee. And he said, I will tarry until thou come again.

19 And Gideon went in, and made ready a kid, and unleavened cakes of an ephah of flour: the flesh he put in a basket, and he put the broth in a pot, and brought it out unto him under the oak, and presented it.

20 And the angel of God said unto him, Take the flesh and the unleavened cakes, and lay them upon this rock, and pour out the broth. And he did so.

21 Then the angel of the LORD put forth the end of the staff that was in his hand, and touched the flesh and the unleavened cakes; and there rose up fire out of the rock, and consumed the flesh and the unleavened cakes. Then the angel of the LORD departed out of his sight.

22 And when Gideon perceived that he was an angel of the LORD, Gideon said, Alas, O LORD God! for because I have seen an angel of the LORD face to face.

23 And the LORD said unto him, Peace be unto thee; fear not: thou shalt not die.

24 Then Gideon built an altar there unto the LORD, and called it Jehovahshalom: unto this day it is yet in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

25 And it came to pass the same night, that the LORD said unto him, Take thy father’s young bullock, even the second bullock of seven years old, and throw down the altar of Baal that thy father hath, and cut down the grove that is by it:

26 And build an altar unto the LORD thy God upon the top of this rock, in the ordered place, and take the second bullock, and offer a burnt sacrifice with the wood of the grove which thou shalt cut down.

27 Then Gideon took ten men of his servants, and did as the LORD had said unto him: and so it was, because he feared his father’s household, and the men of the city, that he could not do it by day, that he did it by night.

28 And when the men of the city arose early in the morning, behold, the altar of Baal was cast down, and the grove was cut down that was by it, and the second bullock was offered upon the altar that was built.

29 And they said one to another, Who hath done this thing? And when they enquired and asked, they said, Gideon the son of Joash hath done this thing.

30 Then the men of the city said unto Joash, Bring out thy son, that he may die: because he hath cast down the altar of Baal, and because he hath cut down the grove that was by it.

31 And Joash said unto all that stood against him, Will ye plead for Baal? will ye save him? he that will plead for him, let him be put to death whilst it is yet morning: if he be a god, let him plead for himself, because one hath cast down his altar.

32 Therefore on that day he called him Jerubbaal, saying, Let Baal plead against him, because he hath thrown down his altar.

33 Then all the Midianites and the Amalekites and the children of the east were gathered together, and went over, and pitched in the valley of Jezreel.

34 But the Spirit of the LORD came upon Gideon, and he blew a trumpet; and Abiezer was gathered after him.

35 And he sent messengers throughout all Manasseh; who also was gathered after him: and he sent messengers unto Asher, and unto Zebulun, and unto Naphtali; and they came up to meet them.

36 And Gideon said unto God, If thou wilt save Israel by mine hand, as thou hast said,

37 Behold, I will put a fleece of wool in the floor; and if the dew be on the fleece only, and it be dry upon all the earth beside, then shall I know that thou wilt save Israel by mine hand, as thou hast said.

38 And it was so: for he rose up early on the morrow, and thrust the fleece together, and wringed the dew out of the fleece, a bowl full of water.

39 And Gideon said unto God, Let not thine anger be hot against me, and I will speak but this once: let me prove, I pray thee, but this once with the fleece; let it now be dry only upon the fleece, and upon all the ground let there be dew.

40 And God did so that night: for it was dry upon the fleece only, and there was dew on all the ground.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Israel again does evil and Midian, Amalek and the east raid every harvest for seven years, until a prophet rehearses the exodus and the broken command before any deliverer comes. Gideon, threshing wheat in hiding by the winepress, is greeted as a mighty man of valour, doubts, is given the sign of fire on the rock and builds an altar called Jehovah-shalom; by night he throws down his father's altar of Baal, is renamed Jerubbaal, and is confirmed by the two signs of the fleece, wet and then dry, before he gathers an army. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Israel again did evil, and the Lord delivered them to Midian seven years, and Henry states the standing rule of the book: let all that sin expect to suffer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Midian's hand prevailed so that Israel made dens in the mountains, caves, and strongholds, and Henry reads this hiding as the effect of a guilty conscience, since sin dispirits men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Whenever Israel had sown, Midian, Amalek, and the children of the east came up against them, timing their raids to steal the harvest itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The invaders encamped against Israel and destroyed the increase of the earth, leaving no sustenance for man or beast, so that what Israel had prepared for Baal was justly taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They came with their cattle and tents like grasshoppers for multitude, with innumerable camels, and entered to destroy the land, a human locust plague. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Israel was greatly impoverished, and cried to the Lord, and Henry notes that affliction again made them cry to the God they had scarcely spoken to in prosperity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. When they cried, God sent not at once a deliverer but a prophet to teach them, and Henry calls faithful ministers a token that God has mercy in store for a land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The prophet rehearsed the exodus: the Lord brought Israel up from Egypt and out of the house of bondage, grounding the rebuke in remembered grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. God had delivered them from Egyptians and all oppressors, driven out their enemies, and given them the land, so that their present misery could not be blamed on God's failure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The prophet closed with the command they had broken, to fear no gods of the Amorites, and Henry notes that real repentance chiefly laments sin as disobedience to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The angel of the Lord sat under the oak at Ophrah while Gideon threshed wheat by the winepress to hide it from Midian, a brave and active man in obscurity, stirred up to something great. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The angel greeted Gideon as a mighty man of valour with the Lord with him, and Henry quotes Bishop Hall: while God calls Gideon valiant, he makes him so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Gideon answered with the people's complaint, asking where the Lord's miracles were and why Israel was forsaken and delivered to Midian, since his weak faith could not reconcile God's presence with Israel's distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The Lord answered by commissioning him directly, to go in his might and save Israel, telling him that the sending itself was enough warrant to act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Gideon pleaded his family's poverty in Manasseh and his own least place in his father's house, and Henry notes that God delights to advance the humble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The Lord promised, I will be with thee, and thou shalt smite Midian as one man, so that the assurance rested on God's presence, not on Gideon's standing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Gideon asked for a sign that it was truly God who talked with him, desiring his weak faith to be confirmed before he acted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. He asked the visitor not to depart until he brought his present, holding the heavenly guest with the hospitality of one who suspects he entertains more than a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Gideon prepared a kid and unleavened cakes and brought them to the oak, an offering of food that would be transformed into something far greater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The angel told him to lay the flesh and cakes on the rock and pour out the broth, arranging the gift as a sacrifice rather than a meal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The angel touched the offering with his staff, fire rose from the rock and consumed it, and the angel departed, showing he was not a man needing meat but the Son of God to be served by sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Seeing the angel depart, Gideon feared he would die for having seen the angel face to face, since a message from heaven is a terror to sinful man who scarcely dares expect good tidings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The Lord spoke peace to him, fear not, thou shalt not die, and God's own word steadied the courage that had just failed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Gideon built an altar there and called it Jehovah-shalom, the Lord is peace, a monument in Ophrah to the word that had calmed him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. That night God ordered Gideon to take his father's bullock, throw down Baal's altar, cut down the grove, and Henry stresses the order: he must make peace with God before making war on Midian, and not merely cease worshipping but throw the altar down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. He was to build an altar to the Lord on the rock in orderly fashion and offer the second bullock with the grove's wood, replacing idolatry with true sacrifice on the very site of the offence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Gideon took ten servants and did it by night, fearing his father's household and the men of the city, yet doing it nonetheless, and Henry bids us do our duty and trust God with our safety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. In the morning the city found Baal's altar cast down, the grove cut, and the bullock offered on the new altar, so that the secret act of the night confronted the whole town at dawn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. The men asked who had done this, and learned it was Gideon son of Joash, the reformer now named and exposed to the city's anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The men demanded that Joash bring out his son to die for breaking Baal's altar, turning the town's loyalty to its idol into a demand for blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Joash answered by challenging Baal's defenders, asking whether they would plead for Baal and declaring that whoever pleaded for him should die by morning, since a god who cannot avenge himself needs no human advocate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. From that day Gideon was called Jerubbaal, let Baal plead against him, because he had thrown down Baal's altar, and the nickname itself became a standing challenge to the idol. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Midian, Amalek, and the east gathered and pitched in Jezreel, so that the reformed town immediately faced the foreign army its reformation had been preparing it to meet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The Spirit of the Lord clothed Gideon, he blew a trumpet, and Abiezer gathered to him, the timid thresher now visibly animated by the Spirit for public action. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Messengers went to Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali, and they came up to meet Gideon, widening the muster beyond his own clan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Gideon asked God to confirm that Israel would be saved by his hand as he had said, returning to signs even after the altar and the trumpet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. He laid a fleece on the threshing floor, asking that dew fall only on the fleece while the ground stayed dry, a sign that God could distinguish a small Israel from the vast floor of Midian. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. In the morning it was so, and Gideon wrung a bowl full of water from the fleece, receiving the first sign in full measure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Gideon asked God not to be angry if he proved once more, this time with the fleece dry and the ground wet, desiring the dew of grace both for himself in particular and for all Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. God did so that night, reversing the sign exactly, and Henry notes that the means of grace still come in different measures, as in one congregation one soul is the moistened fleece and another the dry ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
