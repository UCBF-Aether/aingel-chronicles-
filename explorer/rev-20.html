The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And I saw an angel come down from heaven, having the key of the bottomless pit and a great chain in his hand.

2 And he laid hold on the dragon, that old serpent, which is the Devil, and Satan, and bound him a thousand years,

3 And cast him into the bottomless pit, and shut him up, and set a seal upon him, that he should deceive the nations no more, till the thousand years should be fulfilled: and after that he must be loosed a little season.

4 And I saw thrones, and they sat upon them, and judgment was given unto them: and I saw the souls of them that were beheaded for the witness of Jesus, and for the word of God, and which had not worshipped the beast, neither his image, neither had received his mark upon their foreheads, or in their hands; and they lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years.

5 But the rest of the dead lived not again until the thousand years were finished. This is the first resurrection.

6 Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with him a thousand years.

7 And when the thousand years are expired, Satan shall be loosed out of his prison,

8 And shall go out to deceive the nations which are in the four quarters of the earth, Gog, and Magog, to gather them together to battle: the number of whom is as the sand of the sea.

9 And they went up on the breadth of the earth, and compassed the camp of the saints about, and the beloved city: and fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them.

10 And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet are, and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

11 And I saw a great white throne, and him that sat on it, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away; and there was found no place for them.

12 And I saw the dead, small and great, stand before God; and the books were opened: and another book was opened, which is the book of life: and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works.

13 And the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them: and they were judged every man according to their works.

14 And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death.

15 And whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

An angel with the key of the bottomless pit and a great chain binds the dragon, that old serpent, the Devil and Satan, for a thousand years, while thrones are given to those who reign with Christ, and blessed and holy is he who has part in the first resurrection, over whom the second death has no power. When the thousand years expire Satan is loosed, deceives Gog and Magog, and compasses the beloved city, but fire from heaven devours them and the devil is cast into the lake of fire. Then the great white throne appears, the dead small and great stand before God, books are opened and the book of life, and whosoever is not found written in the book of life is cast into the lake of fire, with death and hell themselves. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John saw an angel come down from heaven, having the key of the bottomless pit and a great chain in his hand. Henry notes the angel is very probably none other than the Lord Jesus Christ, who alone has power to bind the strong man armed, cast him out, and spoil his goods, having the powers of heaven and the keys of hell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He laid hold on the dragon, that old serpent, which is the Devil, and Satan, and bound him a thousand years. Henry notes neither the dragon's strength nor the serpent's subtlety could rescue him from Christ's hands: he caught hold and kept his hold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He cast him into the bottomless pit, shut him up, and set a seal upon him, that he should deceive the nations no more, till the thousand years should be fulfilled: and after that he must be loosed a little season. Henry notes Christ shuts and none can open, seals by his authority, and even devils cannot break his lock and seal, though the church's trials were not yet over. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. John saw thrones, and judgment was given to those who sat on them, and the souls of those beheaded for the witness of Jesus and for the word of God, who had not worshipped the beast or his image nor received his mark, lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years. Henry notes the honoured are all who suffered for Christ and kept clear of pagan and papal idolatry, raised and restored to life, given thrones and judgment, reigning with Christ in glorious conformity to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. But the rest of the dead lived not again until the thousand years were finished. This is the first resurrection. Henry notes this first resurrection belongs only to those who served and suffered for Christ, while the wicked shall not be raised and restored to power again until Satan is let loose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with him a thousand years. Henry notes none can be blessed but the holy, and all the holy shall be blessed, secured from the second death, the death of the soul, eternal separation from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. When the thousand years are expired, Satan shall be loosed out of his prison. Henry notes the restraints laid for a long time are at length taken off, for while this world lasts Satan's power will not be wholly destroyed, only limited and lessened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He shall go out to deceive the nations which are in the four quarters of the earth, Gog and Magog, to gather them together to battle: the number of whom is as the sand of the sea. Henry notes no sooner is Satan loose than he falls to his old work, deceiving nations as to both the cause and the issue, and raising a mighty army from all parts of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. They went up on the breadth of the earth, and compassed the camp of the saints about, and the beloved city: and fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them. Henry notes the saints' army lay encamped about the spiritual Jerusalem, compassed by a superior enemy, until God in an extraordinary and immediate manner fought this last and decisive battle for his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet are, and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever. Henry notes the grand enemy is now cast into hell with his two great officers, tyranny and idolatry, not for any term of time but for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. John saw a great white throne, and him that sat on it, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away, and there was found no place for them. Henry notes the throne is very glorious and perfectly just, and the Judge is the Lord Jesus Christ, putting on such majesty and terror that the whole frame of nature dissolves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. John saw the dead, small and great, stand before God, and the books were opened, and another book was opened, which is the book of life: and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works. Henry notes the books are God's omniscience, the sinner's conscience, and the scriptures, the statute-book of heaven, the touchstone by which hearts and lives are tried. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The sea gave up the dead which were in it, and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them: and they were judged every man according to their works. Henry notes none are so mean but they have talents to account for, and none so great as to avoid this court's jurisdiction, since the grave, hell, and sea all surrender their dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death. Henry notes those who made a covenant with death and an agreement with hell shall be condemned with their infernal confederates, cast into the lake of fire as not entitled to eternal life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire. Henry notes those justified and acquitted by the gospel shall then be justified by the Judge and enter eternal life, having nothing more to fear from death, hell, or wicked men, for these are all destroyed together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Thousand years. The period of the dragon's binding and of the reign of those on thrones; Henry's note stresses that neither the dragon's strength nor subtlety could free him from Christ's hold. Interpreted Study notes, verses 2 and 4, Matthew Henry
  • First resurrection. The resurrection blessed and holy, over which the second death has no power, in contrast to the rest of the dead who live not again until the thousand years are finished. Interpreted Study notes, verses 5–6, Matthew Henry
  • Book of life. The book opened alongside the books of deeds at the great white throne; whosoever is not found written in it is cast into the lake of fire. Interpreted Study notes, verses 12 and 15, Matthew Henry
