The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 65 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Praise waiteth for thee, O God, in Sion: and unto thee shall the vow be performed.

2 O thou that hearest prayer, unto thee shall all flesh come.

3 Iniquities prevail against me: as for our transgressions, thou shalt purge them away.

4 Blessed is the man whom thou choosest, and causest to approach unto thee, that he may dwell in thy courts: we shall be satisfied with the goodness of thy house, even of thy holy temple.

5 By terrible things in righteousness wilt thou answer us, O God of our salvation; who art the confidence of all the ends of the earth, and of them that are afar off upon the sea:

6 Which by his strength setteth fast the mountains; being girded with power:

7 Which stilleth the noise of the seas, the noise of their waves, and the tumult of the people.

8 They also that dwell in the uttermost parts are afraid at thy tokens: thou makest the outgoings of the morning and evening to rejoice.

9 Thou visitest the earth, and waterest it: thou greatly enrichest it with the river of God, which is full of water: thou preparest them corn, when thou hast so provided for it.

10 Thou waterest the ridges thereof abundantly: thou settlest the furrows thereof: thou makest it soft with showers: thou blessest the springing thereof.

11 Thou crownest the year with thy goodness; and thy paths drop fatness.

12 They drop upon the pastures of the wilderness: and the little hills rejoice on every side.

13 The pastures are clothed with flocks; the valleys also are covered over with corn; they shout for joy, they also sing.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Praise waiting in Zion, vows performed, to the Hearer of prayer to whom all flesh shall come. Iniquities are purged, the chosen are brought to dwell in God's courts and satisfied with his house, terrible answers in righteousness come from the God who sets mountains fast and stills seas and peoples' tumult. Then the visited earth is watered, ridges softened, the year crowned with goodness, pastures clothed with flocks and valleys with corn shouting for joy. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Praise waits for you, O God, in Zion, and unto you shall the vow be performed: praise waiting in Zion, vows performed — worship in God's house is both expectation and payment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. O you who hear prayer, unto you shall all flesh come: the Hearer of prayer draws all flesh — prayer is universal in its invitation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Iniquities prevail against me; as for our transgressions, you shall purge them away: prevailing iniquities, personal and collective, are answered by God's purging away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Blessed is the man whom you choose, and cause to approach unto you, that he may dwell in your courts; we shall be satisfied with the goodness of your house, even of your holy temple: chosen, caused to approach, dwelling in courts, satisfied with the house's goodness — approach is God's gift, and satisfaction its fruit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. By terrible things in righteousness will you answer us, O God of our salvation, who are the confidence of all the ends of the earth, and of them afar off upon the sea: terrible things in righteousness are God's answers, and he is confidence to earth's ends and distant seas. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Which by his strength sets fast the mountains, being girded with power: mountains set fast by strength, girded with power — creation's firmness is God's might. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Which stills the noise of the seas, the noise of their waves, and the tumult of the people: stilled seas, stilled waves, stilled tumult of peoples — nature's and nations' noise alike obey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They also who dwell in the uttermost parts are afraid at your tokens; you make the outgoings of morning and evening to rejoice: uttermost dwellers fear at God's tokens, yet morning and evening outgoings rejoice — fear and joy meet in God's signs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. You visit the earth and water it; you greatly enrich it with the river of God, which is full of water; you prepare them corn, when you have so provided for it: visiting, watering, enriching with God's full river, preparing corn — providence is personal visitation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. You water the ridges abundantly, settle the furrows, make it soft with showers, bless the springing thereof: ridges watered, furrows settled, earth softened, springing blessed — the husbandry is God's own handiwork. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. You crown the year with your goodness, and your paths drop fatness: the year crowned with goodness, paths dropping fatness — the year's end wears goodness as a crown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They drop upon the pastures of the wilderness, and the little hills rejoice on every side: fatness drops on wilderness pastures, little hills rejoice on every side — even the wild and low places share the bounty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The pastures are clothed with flocks, the valleys also are covered over with corn; they shout for joy, they also sing: flocks clothing pastures, corn covering valleys, shouting and singing — creation itself joins the praise of verse 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
