The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 51 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Have mercy upon me, O God, according to thy lovingkindness: according unto the multitude of thy tender mercies blot out my transgressions.

2 Wash me throughly from mine iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin.

3 For I acknowledge my transgressions: and my sin is ever before me.

4 Against thee, thee only, have I sinned, and done this evil in thy sight: that thou mightest be justified when thou speakest, and be clear when thou judgest.

5 Behold, I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me.

6 Behold, thou desirest truth in the inward parts: and in the hidden part thou shalt make me to know wisdom.

7 Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.

8 Make me to hear joy and gladness; that the bones which thou hast broken may rejoice.

9 Hide thy face from my sins, and blot out all mine iniquities.

10 Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.

11 Cast me not away from thy presence; and take not thy holy spirit from me.

12 Restore unto me the joy of thy salvation; and uphold me with thy free spirit.

13 Then will I teach transgressors thy ways; and sinners shall be converted unto thee.

14 Deliver me from bloodguiltiness, O God, thou God of my salvation: and my tongue shall sing aloud of thy righteousness.

15 O Lord, open thou my lips; and my mouth shall shew forth thy praise.

16 For thou desirest not sacrifice; else would I give it: thou delightest not in burnt offering.

17 The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.

18 Do good in thy good pleasure unto Zion: build thou the walls of Jerusalem.

19 Then shalt thou be pleased with the sacrifices of righteousness, with burnt offering and whole burnt offering: then shall they offer bullocks upon thine altar.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The great penitential psalm: mercy according to lovingkindness, blotting according to multiplied mercies, washing thorough, sin ever before him, sin chiefly against God, shapen in iniquity. Hyssop purging, whiter than snow, a clean heart created rather than repaired, joy restored, then teaching transgressors. The sacrifices God wants are a broken spirit, widening at last to Zion's walls and sacrifices of righteousness. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

The name David, and the Nathan and Bathsheba occasion named in verse 1 (which the note says Gill's introduction records), comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Have mercy upon me, O God, according to your lovingkindness; according to the multitude of your tender mercies blot out my transgressions: mercy according to lovingkindness, blotting according to multiplied tender mercies — the measures of pardon match the multitude of sin. This is David's psalm after Nathan came concerning Bathsheba, as Gill's introduction records. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Gill invoked in the note text itself, not in the note’s listed sources
  • Verse 2. Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin: thorough washing and cleansing are asked, for sin long lain upon him, as Gill notes, needs much washing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Gill invoked in the note text itself, not in the note’s listed sources
  • Verse 3. For I acknowledge my transgressions, and my sin is ever before me: acknowledgment before God and man, with sin ever before his face, is the penitent's state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Against you, you only, have I sinned, and done this evil in your sight, that you might be justified when you speak, and clear when you judge: all sin against creatures is chiefly against God the lawgiver, and confession justifies God's sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Behold, I was shapen in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me: original sin is confessed — shapen and conceived in it, before any act of his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Behold, you desire truth in the inward parts, and in the hidden part you shall make me to know wisdom: God desires truth inwardly, and wisdom is to be known in the hidden part — the opposite of his secret sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow: hyssop, the cleansing herb of the law, and washing whiter than snow picture complete purification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Make me to hear joy and gladness, that the bones which you have broken may rejoice: broken bones — the frame crushed by conviction — shall rejoice at hearing joy and gladness again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Hide your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities: God's face hidden from sins, and all iniquities blotted, are prayed for together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me: creation, not repair, is asked — a clean heart created and a right spirit renewed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Cast me not away from your presence, and take not your holy spirit from me: casting away from presence and taking of the Holy Spirit are the feared losses, remembered in Saul's fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and uphold me with your free spirit: joy of salvation restored, and a free, willing spirit upholding — joy and willingness return together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Then will I teach transgressors your ways, and sinners shall be converted to you: restored, he will teach transgressors, and sinners shall turn — his fall becomes others' instruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Deliver me from bloodguiltiness, O God, you God of my salvation, and my tongue shall sing aloud of your righteousness: bloodguiltiness — Uriah's blood — is named, and deliverance from it will make his tongue sing God's righteousness aloud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. O Lord, open my lips, and my mouth shall show forth your praise: opened lips are God's work before praise is man's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. For you desire not sacrifice, else would I give it; you delight not in burnt offering: sacrifice and burnt offering, even if given, are not what God delights in from an unbroken heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise: a broken spirit and contrite heart are God's sacrifices — and these he will not despise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Do good in your good pleasure unto Zion; build the walls of Jerusalem: the penitent's prayer widens to Zion and Jerusalem's walls — personal pardon and public welfare together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Then shall you be pleased with the sacrifices of righteousness, with burnt offering and whole burnt offering; then shall they offer bullocks upon your altar: once the heart is right, sacrifices of righteousness please God again, and bullocks are offered on a restored altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
