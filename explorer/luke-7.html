The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now when he had ended all his sayings in the audience of the people, he entered into Capernaum.

2 And a certain centurion’s servant, who was dear unto him, was sick, and ready to die.

3 And when he heard of Jesus, he sent unto him the elders of the Jews, beseeching him that he would come and heal his servant.

4 And when they came to Jesus, they besought him instantly, saying, That he was worthy for whom he should do this:

5 For he loveth our nation, and he hath built us a synagogue.

6 Then Jesus went with them. And when he was now not far from the house, the centurion sent friends to him, saying unto him, Lord, trouble not thyself: for I am not worthy that thou shouldest enter under my roof:

7 Wherefore neither thought I myself worthy to come unto thee: but say in a word, and my servant shall be healed.

8 For I also am a man set under authority, having under me soldiers, and I say unto one, Go, and he goeth; and to another, Come, and he cometh; and to my servant, Do this, and he doeth it.

9 When Jesus heard these things, he marvelled at him, and turned him about, and said unto the people that followed him, I say unto you, I have not found so great faith, no, not in Israel.

10 And they that were sent, returning to the house, found the servant whole that had been sick.

11 And it came to pass the day after, that he went into a city called Nain; and many of his disciples went with him, and much people.

12 Now when he came nigh to the gate of the city, behold, there was a dead man carried out, the only son of his mother, and she was a widow: and much people of the city was with her.

13 And when the Lord saw her, he had compassion on her, and said unto her, Weep not.

14 And he came and touched the bier: and they that bare him stood still. And he said, Young man, I say unto thee, Arise.

15 And he that was dead sat up, and began to speak. And he delivered him to his mother.

16 And there came a fear on all: and they glorified God, saying, That a great prophet is risen up among us; and, That God hath visited his people.

17 And this rumour of him went forth throughout all Judaea, and throughout all the region round about.

18 And the disciples of John shewed him of all these things.

19 And John calling unto him two of his disciples sent them to Jesus, saying, Art thou he that should come? or look we for another?

20 When the men were come unto him, they said, John Baptist hath sent us unto thee, saying, Art thou he that should come? or look we for another?

21 And in that same hour he cured many of their infirmities and plagues, and of evil spirits; and unto many that were blind he gave sight.

22 Then Jesus answering said unto them, Go your way, and tell John what things ye have seen and heard; how that the blind see, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, to the poor the gospel is preached.

23 And blessed is he, whosoever shall not be offended in me.

24 And when the messengers of John were departed, he began to speak unto the people concerning John, What went ye out into the wilderness for to see? A reed shaken with the wind?

25 But what went ye out for to see? A man clothed in soft raiment? Behold, they which are gorgeously apparelled, and live delicately, are in kings’ courts.

26 But what went ye out for to see? A prophet? Yea, I say unto you, and much more than a prophet.

27 This is he, of whom it is written, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face, which shall prepare thy way before thee.

28 For I say unto you, Among those that are born of women there is not a greater prophet than John the Baptist: but he that is least in the kingdom of God is greater than he.

29 And all the people that heard him, and the publicans, justified God, being baptized with the baptism of John.

30 But the Pharisees and lawyers rejected the counsel of God against themselves, being not baptized of him.

31 And the Lord said, Whereunto then shall I liken the men of this generation? and to what are they like?

32 They are like unto children sitting in the marketplace, and calling one to another, and saying, We have piped unto you, and ye have not danced; we have mourned to you, and ye have not wept.

33 For John the Baptist came neither eating bread nor drinking wine; and ye say, He hath a devil.

34 The Son of man is come eating and drinking; and ye say, Behold a gluttonous man, and a winebibber, a friend of publicans and sinners!

35 But wisdom is justified of all her children.

36 And one of the Pharisees desired him that he would eat with him. And he went into the Pharisee’s house, and sat down to meat.

37 And, behold, a woman in the city, which was a sinner, when she knew that Jesus sat at meat in the Pharisee’s house, brought an alabaster box of ointment,

38 And stood at his feet behind him weeping, and began to wash his feet with tears, and did wipe them with the hairs of her head, and kissed his feet, and anointed them with the ointment.

39 Now when the Pharisee which had bidden him saw it, he spake within himself, saying, This man, if he were a prophet, would have known who and what manner of woman this is that toucheth him: for she is a sinner.

40 And Jesus answering said unto him, Simon, I have somewhat to say unto thee. And he saith, Master, say on.

41 There was a certain creditor which had two debtors: the one owed five hundred pence, and the other fifty.

42 And when they had nothing to pay, he frankly forgave them both. Tell me therefore, which of them will love him most?

43 Simon answered and said, I suppose that he, to whom he forgave most. And he said unto him, Thou hast rightly judged.

44 And he turned to the woman, and said unto Simon, Seest thou this woman? I entered into thine house, thou gavest me no water for my feet: but she hath washed my feet with tears, and wiped them with the hairs of her head.

45 Thou gavest me no kiss: but this woman since the time I came in hath not ceased to kiss my feet.

46 My head with oil thou didst not anoint: but this woman hath anointed my feet with ointment.

47 Wherefore I say unto thee, Her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much: but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little.

48 And he said unto her, Thy sins are forgiven.

49 And they that sat at meat with him began to say within themselves, Who is this that forgiveth sins also?

50 And he said to the woman, Thy faith hath saved thee; go in peace.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A centurion’s servant is healed at a distance, on the strength of a faith Jesus says he has not found even in Israel (vv. 1–10). At Nain he stops a funeral procession and raises a widow’s only son (vv. 11–17). John the Baptist, from prison, sends disciples to ask whether Jesus is the one to come; Jesus answers by pointing to what the blind, lame, leprous, deaf, and dead have received, and then praises John as more than a prophet (vv. 18–35). The chapter ends in a Pharisee’s house, where a sinful woman washes Jesus’ feet with tears and anoints them, and hears that her sins are forgiven because she loved much (vv. 36–50). What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When he had ended his sayings before the people, he entered Capernaum. Teaching gives way to a test of faith in that same town. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. A centurion's servant, dear to him, was sick and ready to die. Henry notes servants should study to endear themselves to masters, and masters ought to take particular care of servants when sick. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Hearing of Jesus, the centurion sent elders of the Jews, beseeching him to come and heal his servant. Henry urges that we may still, by faithful and fervent prayer, apply to Christ when sickness is in our families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The elders besought him instantly, saying he was worthy for whom he should do this. Intercessors plead the centurion's worth, while the centurion himself will plead unworthiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. For he loves our nation, and has built us a synagogue. Henry notes building places for worship is a good work, an instance of love to God and his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Jesus went with them, but not far from the house the centurion sent friends, saying, Lord, trouble not thyself, for I am not worthy that thou shouldest enter under my roof. Worthiness claimed by others is disowned by the man himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Neither thought I myself worthy to come unto thee; but say in a word, and my servant shall be healed. Distance is no barrier to a word of command, and humility asks for the word, not the visit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. I also am a man under authority, having soldiers under me; I say, Go, and he goes; Come, and he comes; Do this, and he does it. The soldier reasons from command to command: as his word rules men, Christ's word rules disease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Jesus marvelled at him and said to the following people, I have not found so great faith, no, not in Israel. Henry notes the Lord was pleased with the centurion's faith, and never fails to answer the expectations of faith that honours his power and love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry urges that when dead souls are raised by divine power with the gospel, we must glorify God and see it as a gracious visit to his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 20. When the men came, they repeated John's question: Art thou he that should come, or look we for another? The messengers deliver the question exactly as sent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 30. But the Pharisees and lawyers rejected God's counsel against themselves, being not baptized of him. Refusal of John's baptism was refusal of God's counsel itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 33. John came neither eating bread nor drinking wine, and ye say, he has a devil. Austerity is called madness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The Son of man is come eating and drinking, and ye say, behold a glutton and winebibber, a friend of publicans and sinners! The opposite manner is condemned with the opposite insult. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. But wisdom is justified of all her children. Henry's counsel is to prove ourselves children of Wisdom by attending God's word, and adoring mysteries that infidels and Pharisees deride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. One Pharisee desired him to eat with him, and he went into the Pharisee's house and sat down to meat. Jesus accepts the table of a critic, and the scene that follows will test host and guest alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. A woman in the city, a sinner, knowing Jesus sat at meat in the Pharisee's house, brought an alabaster box of ointment. Henry notes none can truly perceive how precious Christ is, and the glory of the gospel, except the broken-hearted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Standing at his feet behind him weeping, she washed his feet with tears, wiped them with her hair, kissed them, and anointed them with ointment. Tears, hair, kisses, ointment: repentance expresses itself without words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. The Pharisee thought within himself, if he were a prophet he would know who and what manner of woman touches him, for she is a sinner. Henry notes the Pharisee, instead of rejoicing in tokens of her repentance, confined his thoughts to her former bad character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Jesus answered, Simon, I have somewhat to say unto thee; and he said, Master, say on. The unspoken thought is answered aloud, and Simon must listen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. A creditor had two debtors, one owing five hundred pence, the other fifty. The parable sets up a simple arithmetic of debt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. When they had nothing to pay, he frankly forgave them both. Tell me, which will love him most? Henry notes sin is a debt, all are debtors, some greater, but every debt is more than we can pay, and God is ready to forgive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Simon answered, I suppose he to whom he forgave most; and Jesus said, thou hast rightly judged. The Pharisee judges the parable rightly, and thereby judges the woman's case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Turning to the woman, Jesus told Simon, I entered thine house, thou gavest no water for my feet, but she washed my feet with tears and wiped them with her hair. Henry notes Christ forced Simon to acknowledge that the greater sinner, pardoned, ought to show the greater love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Thou gavest me no kiss, but this woman since I came in has not ceased to kiss my feet. Courtesy withheld by the host is supplied, exceeded, by the sinner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. My head with oil thou didst not anoint, but this woman has anointed my feet with ointment. The contrast is complete: host's neglect against guest's devotion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much; but to whom little is forgiven, the same loves little. Henry's rule follows: the more we express sorrow for sin and love to Christ, the clearer evidence we have of forgiveness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. And he said unto her, Thy sins are forgiven. The word spoken to Simon about her becomes a word spoken to her herself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. They that sat at meat began to say within themselves, who is this that forgives sins also? The table now asks the same question the scribes asked over the paralytic. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. He said to the woman, Thy faith has saved thee; go in peace. Henry closes with the wonderful change grace makes on a sinner's heart and life, and on her state before God, by full remission through faith in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry notes Christ forced Simon to acknowledge that the greater sinner, pardoned, ought to show the greater love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Thou gavest me no kiss, but this woman since I came in has not ceased to kiss my feet. Courtesy withheld by the host is supplied, exceeded, by the sinner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. My head with oil thou didst not anoint, but this woman has anointed my feet with ointment. The contrast is complete: host's neglect against guest's devotion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much; but to whom little is forgiven, the same loves little. Henry's rule follows: the more we express sorrow for sin and love to Christ, the clearer evidence we have of forgiveness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. And he said unto her, Thy sins are forgiven. The word spoken to Simon about her becomes a word spoken to her herself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. They that sat at meat began to say within themselves, who is this that forgives sins also? The table now asks the same question the scribes asked over the paralytic. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. He said to the woman, Thy faith has saved thee; go in peace. Henry closes with the wonderful change grace makes on a sinner's heart and life, and on her state before God, by full remission through faith in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
