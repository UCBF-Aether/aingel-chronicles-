The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ephesians 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Be ye therefore followers of God, as dear children;

2 And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour.

3 But fornication, and all uncleanness, or covetousness, let it not be once named among you, as becometh saints;

4 Neither filthiness, nor foolish talking, nor jesting, which are not convenient: but rather giving of thanks.

5 For this ye know, that no whoremonger, nor unclean person, nor covetous man, who is an idolater, hath any inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and of God.

6 Let no man deceive you with vain words: for because of these things cometh the wrath of God upon the children of disobedience.

7 Be not ye therefore partakers with them.

8 For ye were sometimes darkness, but now are ye light in the Lord: walk as children of light:

9 (For the fruit of the Spirit is in all goodness and righteousness and truth;)

10 Proving what is acceptable unto the Lord.

11 And have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather reprove them.

12 For it is a shame even to speak of those things which are done of them in secret.

13 But all things that are reproved are made manifest by the light: for whatsoever doth make manifest is light.

14 Wherefore he saith, Awake thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall give thee light.

15 See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise,

16 Redeeming the time, because the days are evil.

17 Wherefore be ye not unwise, but understanding what the will of the Lord is.

18 And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;

19 Speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord;

20 Giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ;

21 Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God.

22 Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord.

23 For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body.

24 Therefore as the church is subject unto Christ, so let the wives be to their own husbands in every thing.

25 Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;

26 That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word,

27 That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish.

28 So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself.

29 For no man ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church:

30 For we are members of his body, of his flesh, and of his bones.

31 For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and they two shall be one flesh.

32 This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church.

33 Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Be imitators of God as beloved children and walk in love as Christ loved, Paul says, leaving behind sexual immorality, impurity, greed and shameful talk. Once darkness, readers are now light: walk wisely, use the time well, be filled with the Spirit rather than wine, singing and giving thanks, and let husbands and wives mirror Christ and the church. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. As dear children forgiven by God, believers are to be followers, imitators, of God, resembling him especially in love and pardoning goodness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. They are to walk in love as Christ loved them and gave himself as an offering and sacrifice to God, a sweet-smelling savor, in whose sacrifice his love triumphs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Fornication, all uncleanness, and covetousness must not even be named among saints, since such sins are wholly unbecoming to their profession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Neither filthiness, foolish talking, nor jesting is fitting; instead there should be giving of thanks, cheerfulness expressed as becomes Christians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. No whoremonger, unclean person, or covetous man, who is an idolater, has any inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and of God, since a covetous man makes a god of his money. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Let no one deceive them with vain words, for because of these things God's wrath comes on the children of disobedience, and light talk about sin must not be made light of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Therefore they must not be partakers with such people, since sharing in others' sins by commendation, counsel, consent, or concealment brings a share in their plagues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Once they were darkness, but now they are light in the Lord, so they must walk as children of light, grace having wrought a mighty change in their souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The fruit of the Spirit, shown in all goodness, righteousness, and truth, is the evidence of that change from darkness to light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Children of light prove what is acceptable to the Lord, testing their conduct by his will rather than by the world's approval. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. They are to have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather reprove them, since not reproving them is itself a kind of fellowship with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. It is a shame even to speak of the secret deeds of darkness, and a good man will be ashamed to name what wicked men are not ashamed to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. All things reproved are made manifest by the light, for whatever makes manifest is light, exposing sin as a breach of God's holy law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Hence the call: awake, sleeper, arise from the dead, and Christ shall give you light, as prophets and apostles called the dead in sin to arise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. They must therefore walk circumspectly, not as fools but as wise, since care and caution are a remedy against sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Redeeming the time, because the days are evil: time is a talent from God, misspent when not used according to his design, and lost time calls for doubled diligence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. They must not be unwise, but understand what the will of the Lord is, since ignorance of duty and neglect of the soul show the greatest folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. They must not be drunk with wine, wherein is excess, but be filled with the Spirit, since drunkenness never goes alone and only ends in making sorrows more felt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Spirit-filled believers speak to themselves in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in their hearts to the Lord, with reason to sing for joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. They give thanks always for all things to God the Father in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, never lacking matter for thanks even in trials. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. They submit themselves to one another in the fear of God, each fulfilling duties to others for God's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Wives are to submit to their own husbands as unto the Lord, a submission including honor and obedience from a principle of love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church, and he is the savior of the body, the pattern for headship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. As the church is subject to Christ, so wives are to be subject to their husbands in everything, within the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Husbands are to love their wives even as Christ loved the church and gave himself for it, a love sincere, pure, and constant despite her failures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Christ gave himself that he might sanctify and cleanse the church with the washing of water by the word, delivering it from guilt, pollution, and dominion of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. His aim is to present the church to himself glorious, without spot or wrinkle, holy and without blemish, a perfection reached only in glory, though only the sanctified now will be glorified then. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. So husbands ought to love their wives as their own bodies, since he who loves his wife loves himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. No man ever hated his own flesh, but nourishes and cherishes it, even as the Lord cherishes the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. For believers are members of Christ's body, of his flesh, and of his bones, joined to him in a real union. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. For this cause a man shall leave father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall be one flesh, the words of Adam spoken literally of marriage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. This is a great mystery, but Paul speaks concerning Christ and the church, marriage being a type of that union. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. In sum, each husband is to love his wife as himself, and the wife is to reverence her husband, since all duties of marriage are included in unity and love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
