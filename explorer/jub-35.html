The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 35 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the first year of the first week of the forty-fifth jubilee Rebecca called Jacob, her son, and commanded him regarding his father and regarding his brother' that he should honour them all the days of his life.

2 And Jacob said : " I will do

3 everything as thou hast commanded me ; for this thing will be honour and greatness to me, and righteousness before the Lord, that I should honour them.

4 And thou too, mother, knowest from the time I was born until this day, all my deeds and all that is in my heart, that I always think good concerning all.

5 And how should I not do this thing which thou hast commanded me, that I should honour my father and my brother !

6 Tell me, mother, what perversity hast thou seen in me and I shall turn away from it, and mercy will be upon me."

7 And she said unto him : " My son, I have not seen in thee all my days any perverse but (only) upright deeds.

8 And yet I shall tell thee the truth, my son : I shall die this year, and I shall not survive this year in my life; for I have seen in a dream the day of my death, that I should not live beyond a hundred and fifty-five years : and behold I have completed all the days of my life which I am to live." 7.

9 And Jacob laughed at the words of his mother, because his mother had said unto him that she should die ; and she was sitting opposite to him in possession of her strength, and she was not infirm in her strength ; for she went in and out and saw, and her teeth were strong, and no ailment had touched her all the days of her life.

10 And Jacob said unto her : " Blessed am I, mother, if my days approach the days of thy life, and my strength remain with me thus as thy strength : and thou wilt not die, for thou art jesting idly with me regarding thy death." 9.

11 And she went in to Isaac and said unto him : " One petition I make unto thee : make Esau swear that he will not injure Jacob, nor pursue him with enmity ; for thou knowest Esau's thoughts that they are perverse from his youth, and there is no goodness in him ; for he desireth after thy death to kill him.

12 And thou knowest all that he hath done since the day Jacob his brother went to Haran until this day ; how he hath forsaken us with his whole heart, and hath done evil to us; thy flocks he hath taken to himself, and carried off all thy

13 possessions from before thy face.

14 And when we implored and besought him for what was our own, he did as a man who was taking pity on us.

15 And he is bitter against thee because thou didst bless Jacob thy perfect and upright son ; for there is no evil but only goodness in him, and since he came from Haran unto this day he hath not robbed us of aught, for he bringeth us everything in its season always, and rejoiceth with all his heart when we take at his hands, and he blesseth us, and hath not parted from us since he came from Haran until this day, and he remaineth with us continually at home honouring us." 13.

16 And Isaac said unto her : "I, too, know and see the deeds of Jacob who is with us, how that with all his heart he honoureth us ; but I loved Esau formerly more than Jacob, because he was the first-born ; but now I love Jacob more than Esau, for he hath done manifold evil deeds, and there is no righteousness in him, for all his ways are unrighteousness and violence, [and there is no righteousness around him].

17 And now my heart is troubled because of all his deeds, and neither he nor his seed is to be saved, for they are those who will be destroyed from the earth, and who will be rooted out from under heaven, for he hath forsaken the God of Abraham and gone after his wives and after their uncleanness and after their error, he and his children.

18 And thou dost bid me make him swear that he will not slay Jacob, his brother ; even if he swear he will not abide by his oath, and he will not do good but evil only.

19 But if he desireth to slay Jacob, his brother, into Jacob's hands will he be given, and he will not escape from his hands, [for he will descend into his hands.]

20 And fear thou not on account of Jacob ; for the guardian of Jacob is great and powerful and honoured, and praised more than the guardian of Esau." 18.

21 And Rebecca sent

22 and called Esau, and he came to her, and she said unto him : " I have a petition, my son, to make unto thee, and do thou promise to do it, my son."

23 And he said : " I will do everything that thou sayest unto me, and I will not refuse thy petition."

24 And she said unto him : " I ask you that the day I die, thou wilt take me in and bury me near Sarah, thy father's mother, and that thou and Jacob will love each other, and that neither will desire evil against the other, but mutual love only, and (so) ye will prosper, my sons, and be honoured in the midst of the land, and no enemy will rejoice over you, and ye will be a blessing and a mercy in the eyes of all those that love you." 21.

25 And he said : "I will do all that thou hast told me, and I shall bury thee on the day thou diest near Sarah, my father's mother, as thou hast desired that her bones may be near thy bones.

26 And Jacob, my brother, also, I shall love above all flesh ; for I have not a brother in all the earth but him only : and this is no great merit for me if I love him ; for he is my brother, and we were sown together in thy body, and together came we forth from thy womb, and if I do not love my brother, whom shall I love?

27 And I, myself, beg thee to exhort Jacob concerning me and concerning my sons, for I know that he will assuredly be king over me and my sons, for on the day my father blessed him he made him the higher and me the lower.

28 And I swear unto thee that I shall love him, and not desire evil against him all the days of my life but good only."

29 And he sware unto her regarding all this matter.

30 And she called Jacob before the eyes of Esau, and gave him commandment according to the words which she had spoken to Esau.

31 And he said : " I shall do thy pleasure; believe me that no evil will proceed from me or from my sons against Esau, and I shall be first in naught save in love only." 27.

32 And they ate and drank, she and her sons that night, and she died, three jubilees and one week and one year old, on that night, and her two sons, Esau and

33 Jacob, buried her in the double cave near Sarah, their father's mother.

34 Isaac's Last Words and Admonitions : his Death.

35 The Death of Leah.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the first year of the first week of the forty-fifth jubilee Rebecca called Jacob and commanded him regarding his father and his brother, that he should honour them all the days of his life. The mother's last charge is honour: the household that began in deception is to end in honour kept for life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jacob answered that he would do everything as his mother commanded him. The ready promise answers Rebecca's charge with obedience, the same readiness Henry finds in Jacob's service to his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Jacob said that honouring his father and brother would be honour and greatness to him, and righteousness before the Lord. Honour is not servility here but the righteousness of a son who keeps his place in the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Jacob told his mother that she knew, from his birth until that day, all his deeds and all that was in his heart, and that he always thought good concerning all. The appeal to a lifetime known by his mother answers any doubt about his intentions toward his brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Jacob asked how he could fail to do what she commanded, to honour his father and his brother. The question turns the charge into a certainty: honour is what he already intends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Jacob asked his mother to tell him what perversity she had seen in him, so that he might turn away from it and mercy might be upon him. The offer of correction shows a conscience willing to be searched, even by the mother who once led him into deceit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Rebecca answered that she had seen in Jacob all her days no perverse deeds, but only upright ones. The testimony clears the son whom she had once tempted: the years since the blessing have proved his uprightness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Rebecca went in to Isaac with one petition: that he make Esau swear not to injure Jacob nor pursue him with enmity. Henry's account of Esau's malice explains the fear: Esau bore malice on account of the blessing and aimed to prevent Jacob's seed from having the dominion by taking his life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Rebecca reminded Isaac that Esau's thoughts had been perverse from his youth, with no goodness in him, and that he desired after Isaac's death to kill Jacob. The petition looks past Isaac's lifetime to the danger that would open at his death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Rebecca recalled all Esau had done since Jacob went to Haran: he had forsaken his parents with his whole heart, done them evil, taken Isaac's flocks, and carried off all his possessions. The record of wrongs is the ground for asking an oath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. When his parents implored and besought Esau for what was their own, he acted as a man taking pity on them. The pity of a son toward his own parents' property shows how far the house had been divided. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Esau was bitter against Isaac because he had blessed Jacob, his perfect and upright son, and Jacob since his return from Haran had robbed them of nothing, bringing everything in its season, rejoicing when they took from his hands, blessing them, and remaining at home honouring them. The contrast between the brothers is drawn in deeds: one took, the other gave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Isaac answered that he too knew Jacob's deeds, how with all his heart he honoured his parents, but that he had loved Esau formerly more than Jacob because he was first-born, and now loved Jacob more than Esau, for Esau had done manifold evil deeds with no righteousness in him. Henry notes the same change in Isaac: the father who once set his affection on Esau against God's word now sees the sons as their deeds have shown them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Isaac warned that even if Esau swore, he would not abide by his oath and would do evil only, not good. The warning is realistic about Esau's character: an oath from a man who keeps no faith binds nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Isaac told Rebecca not to fear for Jacob, for Jacob's guardian is great, powerful, honoured, and praised more than Esau's guardian. Henry's account of Jacob's life agrees: angels guarded him, God kept him wherever he went, and his enemies were made to be at peace with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Rebecca sent and called Esau, and he came to her. The mother who once sent Jacob away in secret now summons Esau openly, to settle the brothers' peace before her death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Rebecca told Esau she had a petition and asked him to promise to do it. The petition is the same in substance as the one laid on Isaac: peace between the brothers, secured before the parents die. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Esau answered that he would do everything his mother said and would not refuse her petition. The ready promise, whatever its later worth, is the son's honour to his mother in her last days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Rebecca asked that on the day of her death Esau take her in and bury her near Sarah, his father's mother, and that he and Jacob love each other, neither desiring evil against the other, but mutual love only, so that they would prosper and be honoured, with no enemy rejoicing over them. The burial beside Sarah joins the mother to the matriarchs, and the love between brothers is the condition of the family's honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Esau promised to bury his mother near Sarah on the day of her death, as she desired, so that her bones might lie near Sarah's bones. The promise of burial is the son's last service, and it binds him to act with Jacob when the day comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Esau promised to love Jacob his brother above all flesh, for he had no other brother in all the earth, and they were sown together in their mother's body and came forth together from her womb. The twin bond is pleaded as the reason love is not a favour but a debt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Esau begged Rebecca to exhort Jacob concerning him and his sons, for he knew Jacob would assuredly be king over him and his sons, since on the day Isaac blessed him he made Jacob the higher and himself the lower. The acknowledgment accepts the blessing's order at last: Jacob is lord, and Esau knows it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Esau swore that he would love Jacob and desire no evil against him all the days of his life, but good only. The oath is the counterpart of the one Rebecca sought from Isaac's side, a sworn peace between the brothers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Esau swore to his mother regarding all this matter, sealing the promise. The oath stands as the last formal act between mother and elder son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Rebecca called Jacob before Esau's eyes and gave him commandment according to the words she had spoken to Esau. The command was given openly, so that both brothers heard the same charge and neither could claim ignorance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Jacob answered that he would do his mother's pleasure, that no evil would proceed from him or his sons against Esau, and that he would be first in nothing save in love only. The pledge answers Esau's oath with its mirror: supremacy in love alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. They ate and drank together, Rebecca and her sons, that night, and she died, three jubilees and one week and one year old, on that night. The shared meal is the last fellowship of mother and sons, and the death follows it at once, as if the peace were completed just in time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Esau and Jacob buried Rebecca in the double cave near Sarah, their father's mother. Henry notes the same agreement of the brothers at a parent's grave in the case of Isaac: God had wonderfully changed Esau's mind, and the brothers who once stood divided buried their parents together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
