The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 141 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Lord, I cry unto thee: make haste unto me; give ear unto my voice, when I cry unto thee.

2 Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense; and the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice.

3 Set a watch, O LORD, before my mouth; keep the door of my lips.

4 Incline not my heart to any evil thing, to practise wicked works with men that work iniquity: and let me not eat of their dainties.

5 Let the righteous smite me; it shall be a kindness: and let him reprove me; it shall be an excellent oil, which shall not break my head: for yet my prayer also shall be in their calamities.

6 When their judges are overthrown in stony places, they shall hear my words; for they are sweet.

7 Our bones are scattered at the grave’s mouth, as when one cutteth and cleaveth wood upon the earth.

8 But mine eyes are unto thee, O GOD the Lord: in thee is my trust; leave not my soul destitute.

9 Keep me from the snares which they have laid for me, and the gins of the workers of iniquity.

10 Let the wicked fall into their own nets, whilst that I withal escape.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

An evening prayer, in the psalm's own images: let prayer be set forth as incense and the lifting up of hands as the evening sacrifice. The speaker asks God to set a watch before his mouth and keep the door of his lips, not to incline his heart to evil or to practising wicked works with workers of iniquity or eating their dainties. Strikingly, he welcomes correction: let the righteous smite him, it shall be a kindness, and reprove him, an excellent oil that shall not break his head. The psalm's darker middle speaks of judges overthrown in stony places and bones scattered at the grave's mouth like wood cut and cleft on the earth — yet the speaker's eyes are on God, his trust there, praying not to be left destitute, to be kept from snares and gins, and that the wicked fall into their own nets while he escapes. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry cries make haste: David distress pursued most likely Saul violent man — is any distressed let him pray, David did and had comfort; prays favourable acceptance vv1-2, powerful assistance vv3-4, others instrumental good soul as hoped be to souls others vv5-6, he and friends last extremity God appear relief rescue vv7-10; mercy grace necessary as him therefore humbly earnest singing; those know value God's gracious presence more fervent prayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry sets prayer as incense hands evening sacrifice: presented through sacrifice intercession Saviour acceptable God as daily sacrifices burnings incense old; prayer spiritual sacrifice offering soul best affections. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry sets watch mouth keeps door lips: good men know evil tongue sins, enemies provoking danger speaking unadvisedly; live evil world evil hearts need pray neither drawn nor driven do anything sinful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry inclines not heart evil thing practise wicked works eat dainties: mercy accept well grace keep ill two things taught pray; sinners pretend find dainties sin, consider how soon sin turn bitterness dread dainties pray God take out sight grace turn hearts against, good men pray against sweets sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry welcomes righteous smiting as kindness excellent oil not break head: desires told faults — enemies reproached false could not but complain, yet desired friends reprove really amiss particularly anything gave least colour reproaches; righteous God reading welcome rebukes providence tokens love means grace pray instruments trouble; commonly reproofs righteous men best becomes righteous reprove unrighteousness others from them best taken, if reproof just though reprover not so make good use learn obedience; taught receive reproofs — desire reproved whatever amiss done amiss, Lord put heart righteous smite reprove, if own heart not smite as ought let friend, never fall dreadful judgment let alone sin; account friendship not only bear patiently take kindness, reproofs instruction way life (Prov 6:23) means good repentance sins prevent relapses, though cut order cure much more desirable kisses enemy (Prov 27:6) song fools (Eccl 7:5), David blessed God Abigail seasonable admonition (1 Sam 25:32); reckon helped healed excellent oil wound mollify close, shall not break head as some reckon could as well bear heads broken told faults, David not mind sin broken head broken bones (Ps 51:8), reproof excellent oil cure bruises sin given, shall not break head if help break heart; requite kindness at least prayers calamities show take kindly; Hammond another reading reproach bruise righteous rebuke poisonous oil shall not break head destroy mischief intended, yet prayer in mischiefs God preserve prayer not vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry hopes judges overthrown stony places hear sweet words: persecutors some time bear told faults as willing told his — judges Saul officers judged condemned David themselves sole judges overthrown stony places rocks wilderness then hear words sweet; refers relentings Saul breast tears Is this thy voice my son David (1 Sam 24:16; 26:21); generally judges great may come overthrown, greatest figure not always level smooth ways; slighted word God before relish glad affliction opens ear instruction, world bitter word sweet; oppressed innocency cannot gain hearing pomp pleasure, when overthrown themselves more compassionate thoughts afflicted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry bones scattered grave's mouth: complains extremity friends reduced — bones scattered grave's mouth out thrown up so long dead, or into ready thrown near pit, little regarded chips hewers wood thrown neglected heaps; as one cuts cleaves earth ploughman tears earth pieces plough-share (Ps 129:3); can dry bones live? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry eyes unto thee leave not soul destitute: casts upon God depends deliverance — eyes unto thee case ever so deplorable thou canst redress grievances, expect relief bad things trust; those eye towards God hopes him; comfort — leave not soul desolate destitute still see where help is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry keeps from snares laid gins workers iniquity: prevent designs enemies — keep taken snare laid discover evade, gin placed ever subtlety God can will secure people taken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry wicked fall own nets escape altogether: justice turn designs enemies themselves mercy deliver ruined — let wicked fall own net intentionally procured me meritoriously prepared themselves, no law more just architects destruction perish own contrivances, bound over God's justice held cords own iniquity; at same time obtain discharge, entangling ensnaring wicked sometimes prove escape enlargement righteous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
