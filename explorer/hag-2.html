The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Haggai 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the seventh month, in the one and twentieth day of the month, came the word of the LORD by the prophet Haggai, saying,

2 Speak now to Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, and to the residue of the people, saying,

3 Who is left among you that saw this house in her first glory? and how do ye see it now? is it not in your eyes in comparison of it as nothing?

4 Yet now be strong, O Zerubbabel, saith the LORD; and be strong, O Joshua, son of Josedech, the high priest; and be strong, all ye people of the land, saith the LORD, and work: for I am with you, saith the LORD of hosts:

5 According to the word that I covenanted with you when ye came out of Egypt, so my spirit remaineth among you: fear ye not.

6 For thus saith the LORD of hosts; Yet once, it is a little while, and I will shake the heavens, and the earth, and the sea, and the dry land;

7 And I will shake all nations, and the desire of all nations shall come: and I will fill this house with glory, saith the LORD of hosts.

8 The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, saith the LORD of hosts.

9 The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former, saith the LORD of hosts: and in this place will I give peace, saith the LORD of hosts.

10 In the four and twentieth day of the ninth month, in the second year of Darius, came the word of the LORD by Haggai the prophet, saying,

11 Thus saith the LORD of hosts; Ask now the priests concerning the law, saying,

12 If one bear holy flesh in the skirt of his garment, and with his skirt do touch bread, or pottage, or wine, or oil, or any meat, shall it be holy? And the priests answered and said, No.

13 Then said Haggai, If one that is unclean by a dead body touch any of these, shall it be unclean? And the priests answered and said, It shall be unclean.

14 Then answered Haggai, and said, So is this people, and so is this nation before me, saith the LORD; and so is every work of their hands; and that which they offer there is unclean.

15 And now, I pray you, consider from this day and upward, from before a stone was laid upon a stone in the temple of the LORD:

16 Since those days were, when one came to an heap of twenty measures, there were but ten: when one came to the pressfat for to draw out fifty vessels out of the press, there were but twenty.

17 I smote you with blasting and with mildew and with hail in all the labours of your hands; yet ye turned not to me, saith the LORD.

18 Consider now from this day and upward, from the four and twentieth day of the ninth month, even from the day that the foundation of the LORD’s temple was laid, consider it.

19 Is the seed yet in the barn? yea, as yet the vine, and the fig tree, and the pomegranate, and the olive tree, hath not brought forth: from this day will I bless you.

20 And again the word of the LORD came unto Haggai in the four and twentieth day of the month, saying,

21 Speak to Zerubbabel, governor of Judah, saying, I will shake the heavens and the earth;

22 And I will overthrow the throne of kingdoms, and I will destroy the strength of the kingdoms of the heathen; and I will overthrow the chariots, and those that ride in them; and the horses and their riders shall come down, every one by the sword of his brother.

23 In that day, saith the LORD of hosts, will I take thee, O Zerubbabel, my servant, the son of Shealtiel, saith the LORD, and will make thee as a signet: for I have chosen thee, saith the LORD of hosts.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A month after work began, on the twenty-first day of the seventh month, the word comes again to a discouraged people who remember the first house's glory and see this one as nothing: be strong, Zerubbabel, Joshua, and all the people, for I am with you, and the glory of this latter house will be greater than the former, with peace given in this place. On the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, a test question to the priests shows that holiness is not passed on by touch while uncleanness spreads readily, so the people's disobedient works were defiled, but from this day of renewed obedience God will bless them, even before vine, fig, pomegranate, or olive has borne. The book closes on Zerubbabel alone: God will shake heaven and earth, overthrow kingdoms, and make his servant as a signet, for he has chosen him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the seventh month, on the twenty-first day, in the second year of Darius, God's word came again by Haggai. This was not quite a month after the people had begun the work, and shortly before Zechariah began to prophesy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The word is addressed again to Zerubbabel, Joshua, and the residue of the people, the same leaders and remnant as in chapter 1. Henry groups this whole opening section as encouragement to a discouraged people to proceed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Who is left that saw this house in its first glory, and how do you see it now, as nothing in comparison? Elders who remembered Solomon's temple wept at the contrast, and the Jews counted five lacks: sacred fire, Shekinah, ark, Urim and Thummim, and prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Yet now be strong, Zerubbabel, Joshua, and all the people, and work, for I am with you, says the Lord of hosts. The greatest strength is to have the Lord with us as our strength, not man's might but God's Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God is with them according to the word he covenanted when they came out of Egypt, and his Spirit remains among them, so they need not fear. The covenant promise at Sinai and the present inspiration of Haggai are both motives to persevere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Yet once, in a little while, God will shake heavens, earth, sea, and dry land. JFB reads this as a series of shakings of nations and world powers, beginning before Messiah's first coming and finished at his second, clearing the way for his unshakeable kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He will shake all nations, and the Desire of all nations shall come, and fill this house with glory. Henry identifies this Desire as Christ, desirable to all nations, in whom all the earth is blessed; JFB adds that he alone satisfies the yearning all nations felt unconsciously for a Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The silver is mine and the gold is mine, says the Lord of hosts. If he pleased he could adorn the temple with precious metals, but he will adorn it with a far more precious glory, the presence of his Son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The glory of this latter house shall be greater than the former, and in this place God will give peace. That greater glory is Messiah himself present in the temple, and the peace is first peace with God through his blood, then peace in the heart, then peace on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. On the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, in the second year of Darius, God's word came by Haggai a third time. This was a little over two months after the second prophecy, and the people had made considerable progress in the work meanwhile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Haggai is told to ask the priests concerning the law, putting a test question to them. The priests were the authorized expounders of the law, so their ruling would settle the principle Haggai was about to apply. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. If one carries holy flesh in his garment's skirt and the skirt touches bread, stew, wine, oil, or any food, shall it be holy? The priests answered, No: holiness in the skirt cannot be passed onward to something else. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But if one unclean by a dead body touches any of these, shall it be unclean? The priests answered, It shall be unclean. Legal impurity spreads readily, while legal holiness is not so easily communicated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. So is this people and this nation before me, and so is every work of their hands, and what they offer there is unclean. Their sacrifices could not sanctify disobedient offerers; instead their disobedience defiled their very offerings, a warning against falling back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Now consider from this day backward, from before a stone was laid upon a stone in the temple. They are to compare the evils of their disobedient past with the present, when obedience has resumed, and perceive blessing following obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Since those days, one coming to a heap expected to be twenty measures found but ten, and one coming to the press for fifty vessels found but twenty. The shortfall was the visible fruit of the curse on neglected duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. God smote them with blasting, mildew, and hail in all the labours of their hands, yet they did not turn to him. Affliction hardens the heart when it is not referred to God as its author. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Consider now from this day onward, from the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, from the day the foundation of the Lord's temple was laid. The blessing promised in verse 19 is set in deliberate contrast with the curse of verses 16-17. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Is the seed yet in the barn? No: vine, fig, pomegranate, and olive have not yet brought forth, yet from this day God will bless them. They may trust his promise before any visible sign appears, so the blessing will be evidently his, not nature's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Again God's word came to Haggai on the twenty-fourth day of the month, the same date as the third prophecy. This fourth prophecy turns from the people at large to Zerubbabel their civil leader and representative. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Speak to Zerubbabel: I will shake the heavens and the earth. The Jews feared these convulsions would destroy their national existence, so the promise is addressed to their representative to assure them of safety in God's electing love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. God will overthrow the throne of kingdoms, destroy the strength of heathen kingdoms, and overthrow chariots, horses, and riders, each by his brother's sword. All world kingdoms fall to make way for Christ's universal kingdom of peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. In that day God will take Zerubbabel his servant and make him as a signet, for he has chosen him. A signet is the king's seal of delegated authority, constantly in his regard; Zerubbabel is the type, and Messiah his descendant the antitype, in whom God's people are sealed and safe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Signet. the king's seal of delegated authority, constantly in his regard; Haggai 2 makes Zerubbabel as a signet because God has chosen him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
