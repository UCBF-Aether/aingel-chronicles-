The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 53 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God. Corrupt are they, and have done abominable iniquity: there is none that doeth good.

2 God looked down from heaven upon the children of men, to see if there were any that did understand, that did seek God.

3 Every one of them is gone back: they are altogether become filthy; there is none that doeth good, no, not one.

4 Have the workers of iniquity no knowledge? who eat up my people as they eat bread: they have not called upon God.

5 There were they in great fear, where no fear was: for God hath scattered the bones of him that encampeth against thee: thou hast put them to shame, because God hath despised them.

6 Oh that the salvation of Israel were come out of Zion! When God bringeth back the captivity of his people, Jacob shall rejoice, and Israel shall be glad.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Psalm 14 said again, with small changes: the fool's denial, God searching from heaven, all gone back and filthy, workers eating God's people as bread, here fear where no fear was and bones scattered, closing with the same longing, salvation from Zion, Jacob rejoicing. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Psalms 53 and 14: the same psalm twice?

This section investigates a parallel between two texts. It does not conclude how the parallel came to be, and it claims no direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty about either text's origin. Interpreted Study note on verse 1; Standard corpus, ps.json (Psalms 14 and 53)

The study note on verse 1 itself calls this psalm a repetition of Psalm 14 'with small changes,' for the lesson of universal corruption bears repeating.

Parallels. The two printed texts track each other closely: the fool saying in his heart there is no God; God looking down from heaven to see if any understand and seek God; all gone back and altogether become filthy, none doing good, no, not one; workers of iniquity eating God's people as bread and not calling on God; and the same closing longing, that salvation for Israel were come out of Zion, captivity brought back, Jacob rejoicing and Israel glad.

Differences. Psalm 53 is one verse shorter (6 verses against 14's 7): it has no counterpart to Psalm 14 verse 6, shaming the counsel of the poor because the Lord is his refuge. The divine name is mostly 'God' here where Psalm 14 has 'the LORD.' And verse 5 differs most: here, fear where no fear was, God scattering the bones of him that encamps against his people, against Psalm 14's God in the generation of the righteous.

Whether one text was adapted from the other, both were drawn from a shared source, or the likeness arose another way, is not established by the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved No source consulted for this page establishes the relation between the two texts
