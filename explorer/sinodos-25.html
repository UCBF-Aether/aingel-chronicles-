The Text — Sinodos 25 (Ge'ez text of G. Horner, The Statutes of the Apostles; or, Canones Ecclesiastici (London: Williams & Norgate, 1904), public domain)

1 Statute 56. Concerning virgins and widows. And he shall not lay hand upon virgins, for there is no commandment concerning it from …God.

2 …for this work is of the nature of man[?], and it is not that she despiseth marriage[?], but that she may have time for the service of God. And concerning widows: he shall not lay hand upon a widow; but if she be a widow whose husband[?] died many years ago, and if her widowhood be of long time —

3 and she lived diligently[?], and if no accusation be found against her, and if she was able to keep in good order the men of her house as is fitting, like Judith, and if she loved all good things like Judith — then let her be ordained into the order of widows.

4 And if her husband died but a short time ago, and it was not long — let them not trust her, and let her not be ordained into the order of widows; but let her remain a long time in good works, in fasting and prayer, keeping herself apart, that she may subdue herself[?].

5 with patience. For horse and mule are not controlled save by a strong bridle.

6 Concerning male virgins: they shall not ordain male virgins, for this work is from the will of the heart alone.

7 it being his single state[?] by the grace of God and by the gift of Christ Jesus.

8 For when the Holy Spirit abideth upon a man, he obtaineth grace and healing, being known by the grace which is in him, which shall be known unto all men.

9 And if he were called unto this work, that he should be ordained — whether bishop[?], presbyter, or deacon — …[?]

10 And concerning those who have drunk of the blood of Christ —

11 and when he hath finished his prayer, the deacon shall say: Bow your heads before God, that He may bless you.

12 And when he hath finished this, after they are blessed, the deacon shall say: Depart in peace.

13 And concerning the fragments[?], if any remain, let the presbyters and deacons take charge of them, that nothing at all of the oblation remain; and let them watch diligently, that nothing remain, lest there come upon them great condemnation[?] and sin — as upon the sons of Aaron and their children, those whom … destroyed[?].

14 For they have partaken[?] of the sacrifice unto God.

15 And how much more for those who have partaken[?] of the body of Christ and of His honoured blood.

16 And let them account it not a fleshly food[?], but spiritual, for them that receive[?] it in purity.

17 And this we command you — bishops, presbyters, and deacons — concerning the ministry of the holy mystery.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Statute 56 concerns virgins and widows. No hand is to be laid upon virgins, for there is no commandment concerning it from God; virginity is described as a work of man's nature, not contempt of marriage, but time freed for God's service. A widow is not to be laid hands on either, unless she is long proved — diligent, unaccused, keeping her household in good order like Judith, loving all good things like Judith — while a recently bereaved widow must remain a long time in good works, fasting and prayer before being trusted. Male virgins likewise are not ordained, since single state is by the grace of God and the gift of Christ, though a man called to bishop, presbyter or deacon may still be ordained. The chapter ends with fragments on the communion: fragments guarded lest anything of the oblation remain, and the body and blood received not as fleshly food but spiritual, in purity.
