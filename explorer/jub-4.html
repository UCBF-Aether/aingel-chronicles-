How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: In the third week of the second jubilee Eve gave birth to Cain, in the fourth to Abel, and in the fifth to her daughter Awan. In the twenty-fifth jubilee Noah took Emzara, daughter of Rakeel, daughter of his father's brother, in the first year in the fifth week, and in the third year she bare him Shem, in the fifth year Ham, and in the first year in the sixth week Japheth. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the third week of the second jubilee Eve gave birth to Cain, in the fourth to Abel, and in the fifth to her daughter Awan. The book dates the first family in its own jubilee calendar, where Genesis gives no dates for these births. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 2. In the first year of the third jubilee Cain slew Abel because God accepted Abel's sacrifice and did not accept Cain's offering. JFB on Genesis 4:4 explains divine 'respect' as a keen, earnest glance, shown in the offering's consumption by fire, and Abel's offering was made through faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He slew him in the field, and his blood cried from the ground to heaven, complaining because he had slain him. JFB on Genesis 4:10 notes Cain was challenged directly from the Shekinah itself, the blood crying out against a murder done in secret. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The Lord reproved Cain because of Abel, made him a fugitive on the earth because of his brother's blood, and cursed him upon the earth. JFB on Genesis 4:11-12 reads this curse as superadded to the general curse on the ground for Adam's sin, condemning Cain to perpetual exile as an outcast under an accusing conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. On this account it is written on the heavenly tables: 'Cursed is he who smiteth his neighbour treacherously, and let all who have seen and heard say, So be it; and the man who hath seen and not declared it, let him be accursed as the other.' Cain's murder becomes a standing statute: treacherous killing is cursed, and silent witnesses share the curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 6. For this reason the angels announce, when they come before the Lord, all the sin committed in heaven and on earth, in light and darkness, and everywhere. The heavenly tables' witness law is matched by an angelic practice of full disclosure before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. Adam and his wife mourned for Abel four weeks of years, and in the fourth year of the fifth week they became joyful, and Adam knew his wife again. Four weeks of years, twenty-eight years of mourning, mark the depth of the first parents' grief before comfort comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. She bare him a son, and he called his name Seth, saying God had raised up a second seed instead of Abel, for Cain slew him. Seth is named as replacement seed, the line through which the book's history will continue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. This verse opens the next birth notice: in the sixth week he begat his daughter Azura. The sentence is completed by the following verse's date. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. In the years 134-140 AM Seth begat his daughter Azura. The date fragment fixes Azura's birth within the book's anno-mundi reckoning, and she will become Seth's wife in verse 17. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. Cain took Awan his sister to be his wife, and she bare him Enoch at the close of the fourth jubilee. The first city-builder's line begins with a sister-marriage, as the early generations marry within the first family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. The date fragment closes the previous notice: Enoch was born in the years 190-196 AM. Jubilees dates even the Cainite line precisely, keeping its chronology unbroken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. In the first year of the first week of the fifth jubilee, houses were built on the earth. Settled building begins in the Cainite generation, the prelude to the first city in the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. Cain built a city, and called its name after the name of his son Enoch. JFB on Genesis 4:17-22 notes it has ever been in cities that the race made its greatest social progress; here the first city bears the first city-builder's son's name, and settled exile replaces fugitive wandering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Adam knew Eve his wife and she bare yet nine sons. Beyond Seth, the first family grows to nine more sons, filling the world whose population Cain already feared in Genesis 4:14. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. The date fragment, years 225-231 AM, belongs to the births of verse 15. The book attaches its anno-mundi dates even to bare notices of sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. In the fifth week of the fifth jubilee Seth took Azura his sister to be his wife, and in the fourth year of the sixth week she bare him Enos. The Sethite line continues through sister-marriage, as the Cainite line did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. Enos began to call on the name of the Lord on the earth. JFB on Genesis 4:26 renders this 'by the name of the Lord': God's people were called by his name, a name probably applied in contempt by the world, marking the Sethites as the worshipping line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The date fragment, years 309-315 AM, belongs to the Enos generation. The chronology carries the Sethite line forward week by week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. This verse opens the next marriage notice: in the seventh jubilee, in the third week, Enos took a wife. The sentence is completed by the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. Enos took Noam his sister to be his wife, and she bare him a son in the third year of the fifth week. The pattern of sister-marriage and dated birth continues down the Sethite line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. This verse continues the birth notice, giving the year of Kenan's birth, and the next verse supplies his name. The chain of verses 20-23 is a single dated genealogy entry split across units. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. He called his name Kenan. The naming closes the Enos-to-Kenan entry, dated to the third year of the fifth week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. This verse opens the next entry: at the close of the eighth jubilee Kenan took a wife. The sentence is completed by the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. In the years 386-392 AM, at the close of the eighth jubilee, Kenan took Mualeleth his sister to be his wife. The date fragment anchors Kenan's marriage in the anno-mundi count. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. She bare him a son in the ninth jubilee, in the first week, in the third year of this week. The birth is dated to week and year within the jubilee, the book's standard precision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. This verse continues the birth notice, and the next verse gives the son's name and the closing date. The entry spans verses 25-28. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. In the year 395 AM he called his name Mahalalel. The naming closes the Kenan-to-Mahalalel entry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. This verse opens the next entry: in the second week of the tenth jubilee, years 449-455 AM, Mahalalel took a wife. The sentence is completed by the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. Mahalalel took Dinah, daughter of Barakiel, daughter of his father's brother, to wife. The marriage moves outside sister-marriage to a cousin, the father's brother's daughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. This verse continues the marriage notice, identifying Dinah's descent, and the next verses give the birth and naming of Jared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. She bare him a son in the third week, in the sixth year, and he called his name Jared. The dated birth continues the unbroken Sethite chronology. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. He called his name Jared. The naming closes the Mahalalel-to-Jared entry, and the next verse gives the event that defines Jared's days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. For in his days the angels of the Lord descended on the earth, those who are named the Watchers. Jared's generation is the generation of the descent, the turning point from the Sethite genealogy to the Watchers' story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. The Watchers descended that they should instruct the children of men and do judgment and uprightness on the earth. Their mission began as teaching, not corruption: instruction and righteous judgment were their appointed work before they fell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. In the eleventh jubilee Jared took Baraka, daughter of Rasujal, daughter of his father's brother, in the fourth week, and she bare him a son in the fifth week, in the fourth year of the jubilee, and he called his name Enoch. The dated entry introduces the book's great scribe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. Enoch was the first among men born on earth who learnt writing and knowledge and wisdom, and wrote down the signs of heaven according to the order of their months in a book, that men might know the seasons of the years by their separate months. Charles notes this passage as the source for Enoch's astronomical book, and it grounds the book's own calendar in Enoch's writing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. He was the first to write a testimony, testified to the sons of men among the generations of the earth, recounted the weeks of the jubilees, made known the days of the years, set in order the months, and recounted the Sabbaths of the years as they were made known to him. Enoch's testimony is the book's own pattern: jubilees, months and Sabbath years taught to men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. This verse completes Enoch's ordering work: he recounted the Sabbaths of the years as they were made known to him. His calendar teaching comes by revelation, as Moses' will in chapter 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. What was and what will be he saw in a vision of his sleep, as it will happen to the children of men throughout their generations until the day of judgment; he saw and understood everything, wrote his testimony, and placed it on earth for all the children of men and for their generations. Enoch's vision spans past and future to the judgment, and his written testimony preserves it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. This verse completes Enoch's vision notice: the testimony was placed on earth for all the children of men and for their generations. Writing is what lets one man's vision outlast his life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. This verse opens the next entry: in the twelfth jubilee, in the seventh week, years 575-581 AM, Enoch took a wife. The sentence is completed by the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. The date fragment, years 575-581 AM, anchors Enoch's marriage in the seventh week of the twelfth jubilee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. Enoch took Edna, daughter of Danel, daughter of his father's brother, to wife. The marriage follows the cousin pattern of the preceding generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. In the sixth year in this week she bare him a son and he called his name Methuselah. JFB on Genesis 5:21 notes the name signifies 'He dieth, and the sending forth,' given by Enoch as prophetical of the flood, and Methuselah is computed to have died in the year of that catastrophe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. Enoch was moreover with the angels of God these six jubilees of years, and they showed him everything on earth and in the heavens, the rule of the sun, and he wrote down everything. JFB on Genesis 5:24 reads 'walked with God' as constant, familiar intercourse, and his translation as a palpable proof to an unbelieving age that his teaching was true; Jubilees extends the converse to six jubilees of angelic instruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. Enoch testified to the Watchers who had sinned with the daughters of men, for they had begun to unite themselves, so as to be defiled, with the daughters of men, and Enoch testified against them all. The scribe becomes a witness against the fallen teachers, whose original mission was instruction and uprightness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 48. He was taken from among the children of men, and the angels conducted him into the Garden of Eden in majesty and honour, and behold there he writes down the condemnation and judgment of the world and all the wickedness of the children of men. Enoch's translation leads not to rest but to a continuing scribal office in Eden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 49. On account of it God brought the waters of the flood upon all the land of Eden, for there Enoch was set as a sign to testify against all the children of men, recounting all the deeds of the generations until the day of condemnation. Enoch in Eden is a living testimony, and the flood answers the wickedness he records. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 50. This verse completes the testimony notice: Enoch recounts all the deeds of the generations until the day of condemnation. His Eden writings run from the first generations to the final judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 51. He burnt the incense of the sanctuary, even sweet spices acceptable before the Lord on the Mount. Enoch in Eden serves as a priest, offering acceptable incense before the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 52. The Lord has four places on the earth: the Garden of Eden, the Mount of the East, this mountain on which Moses stands, Mount Sinai, and Mount Zion, which will be sanctified in the new creation for a sanctification of the earth, through which the earth will be sanctified from all its guilt and uncleanness throughout the generations of the world. The four holy places link Eden, Sinai and the future Zion in one geography of sanctification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 53. In the fourteenth jubilee Methuselah took Edna, daughter of Azriel, daughter of his father's brother, in the third week, in the first year of this week, and begat a son and called his name Lamech. The genealogy resumes its dated march toward Noah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 54. In the fifteenth jubilee, in the third week, Lamech took Betenos, daughter of Barakiel, daughter of his father's brother, and in this week she bare him a son and he called his name Noah, saying, 'This one will comfort me for my trouble and all my work, and for the ground which the Lord hath cursed.' JFB on Genesis 5:29 notes Noah means 'rest' or 'comfort,' and Lamech's words allude to the penal consequences of the fall and the hope of a Deliverer excited by the promise to Eve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. At the close of the nineteenth jubilee, in the seventh week, in the sixth year, Adam died, and all his sons buried him in the land of his creation, and he was the first to be buried in the earth. The first man is the first buried, in the very land he was formed from. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 56. He lacked seventy years of one thousand years, for one thousand years are as one day in the testimony of the heavens, and therefore was it written concerning the tree of knowledge: 'On the day that ye eat thereof ye will die.' Adam's 930 years fall within the one day of a thousand years, so the sentence of Genesis 2:17 stands fulfilled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 57. This verse completes the thousand-year-day explanation: for this reason Adam did not complete the years of that day, for he died during it. The threatened 'day' of death is the heavenly day, not a single earthly day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 58. This verse is the hinge to the next death: at the close of this jubilee Cain was killed after Adam, in the same year. The first murderer's end follows the first man's burial without delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 59. Cain was killed after him in the same year, for his house fell upon him and he died in the midst of his house, killed by its stones, for with a stone he had killed Abel, and by a stone was he killed in righteous judgment. The manner of death matches the manner of murder, stone for stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 60. For this reason it was ordained on the heavenly tables: 'With the instrument with which a man killeth his neighbour with the same shall he be killed; after the manner that he wounded him, in like manner shall they deal with him.' Cain's end becomes a standing law of retribution, written before Sinai like the witness law of verse 5. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 61. In the twenty-fifth jubilee Noah took Emzara, daughter of Rakeel, daughter of his father's brother, in the first year in the fifth week, and in the third year she bare him Shem, in the fifth year Ham, and in the first year in the sixth week Japheth. JFB on Genesis 5:32 notes Moses records not first-born sons but the succession from Adam through Seth to Abraham; here the succession runs through Noah's three sons, dated to year and week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Matthew Henry and Jamieson-Fausset-Brown wrote on the canonical books: a note credited to them here is on the parallel Genesis passage named in that study note, not commentary on Jubilees itself.

R. H. Charles

  • Verse 1. In the third week of the second jubilee Eve gave birth to Cain, in the fourth to Abel, and in the fifth to her daughter Awan. The book dates the first family in its own jubilee calendar, where Genesis gives no dates for these births. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 5. On this account it is written on the heavenly tables: 'Cursed is he who smiteth his neighbour treacherously, and let all who have seen and heard say, So be it; and the man who hath seen and not declared it, let him be accursed as the other.' Cain's murder becomes a standing statute: treacherous killing is cursed, and silent witnesses share the curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 6. For this reason the angels announce, when they come before the Lord, all the sin committed in heaven and on earth, in light and darkness, and everywhere. The heavenly tables' witness law is matched by an angelic practice of full disclosure before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. Adam and his wife mourned for Abel four weeks of years, and in the fourth year of the fifth week they became joyful, and Adam knew his wife again. Four weeks of years, twenty-eight years of mourning, mark the depth of the first parents' grief before comfort comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. She bare him a son, and he called his name Seth, saying God had raised up a second seed instead of Abel, for Cain slew him. Seth is named as replacement seed, the line through which the book's history will continue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. This verse opens the next birth notice: in the sixth week he begat his daughter Azura. The sentence is completed by the following verse's date. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. In the years 134-140 AM Seth begat his daughter Azura. The date fragment fixes Azura's birth within the book's anno-mundi reckoning, and she will become Seth's wife in verse 17. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. Cain took Awan his sister to be his wife, and she bare him Enoch at the close of the fourth jubilee. The first city-builder's line begins with a sister-marriage, as the early generations marry within the first family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. The date fragment closes the previous notice: Enoch was born in the years 190-196 AM. Jubilees dates even the Cainite line precisely, keeping its chronology unbroken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. In the first year of the first week of the fifth jubilee, houses were built on the earth. Settled building begins in the Cainite generation, the prelude to the first city in the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. Adam knew Eve his wife and she bare yet nine sons. Beyond Seth, the first family grows to nine more sons, filling the world whose population Cain already feared in Genesis 4:14. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. The date fragment, years 225-231 AM, belongs to the births of verse 15. The book attaches its anno-mundi dates even to bare notices of sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. In the fifth week of the fifth jubilee Seth took Azura his sister to be his wife, and in the fourth year of the sixth week she bare him Enos. The Sethite line continues through sister-marriage, as the Cainite line did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. The date fragment, years 309-315 AM, belongs to the Enos generation. The chronology carries the Sethite line forward week by week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. This verse opens the next marriage notice: in the seventh jubilee, in the third week, Enos took a wife. The sentence is completed by the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. Enos took Noam his sister to be his wife, and she bare him a son in the third year of the fifth week. The pattern of sister-marriage and dated birth continues down the Sethite line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. This verse continues the birth notice, giving the year of Kenan's birth, and the next verse supplies his name. The chain of verses 20-23 is a single dated genealogy entry split across units. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. He called his name Kenan. The naming closes the Enos-to-Kenan entry, dated to the third year of the fifth week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. This verse opens the next entry: at the close of the eighth jubilee Kenan took a wife. The sentence is completed by the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. In the years 386-392 AM, at the close of the eighth jubilee, Kenan took Mualeleth his sister to be his wife. The date fragment anchors Kenan's marriage in the anno-mundi count. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. She bare him a son in the ninth jubilee, in the first week, in the third year of this week. The birth is dated to week and year within the jubilee, the book's standard precision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. This verse continues the birth notice, and the next verse gives the son's name and the closing date. The entry spans verses 25-28. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. In the year 395 AM he called his name Mahalalel. The naming closes the Kenan-to-Mahalalel entry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. This verse opens the next entry: in the second week of the tenth jubilee, years 449-455 AM, Mahalalel took a wife. The sentence is completed by the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. Mahalalel took Dinah, daughter of Barakiel, daughter of his father's brother, to wife. The marriage moves outside sister-marriage to a cousin, the father's brother's daughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. This verse continues the marriage notice, identifying Dinah's descent, and the next verses give the birth and naming of Jared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. She bare him a son in the third week, in the sixth year, and he called his name Jared. The dated birth continues the unbroken Sethite chronology. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. He called his name Jared. The naming closes the Mahalalel-to-Jared entry, and the next verse gives the event that defines Jared's days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. For in his days the angels of the Lord descended on the earth, those who are named the Watchers. Jared's generation is the generation of the descent, the turning point from the Sethite genealogy to the Watchers' story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. The Watchers descended that they should instruct the children of men and do judgment and uprightness on the earth. Their mission began as teaching, not corruption: instruction and righteous judgment were their appointed work before they fell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. In the eleventh jubilee Jared took Baraka, daughter of Rasujal, daughter of his father's brother, in the fourth week, and she bare him a son in the fifth week, in the fourth year of the jubilee, and he called his name Enoch. The dated entry introduces the book's great scribe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. Enoch was the first among men born on earth who learnt writing and knowledge and wisdom, and wrote down the signs of heaven according to the order of their months in a book, that men might know the seasons of the years by their separate months. Charles notes this passage as the source for Enoch's astronomical book, and it grounds the book's own calendar in Enoch's writing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. He was the first to write a testimony, testified to the sons of men among the generations of the earth, recounted the weeks of the jubilees, made known the days of the years, set in order the months, and recounted the Sabbaths of the years as they were made known to him. Enoch's testimony is the book's own pattern: jubilees, months and Sabbath years taught to men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. This verse completes Enoch's ordering work: he recounted the Sabbaths of the years as they were made known to him. His calendar teaching comes by revelation, as Moses' will in chapter 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. What was and what will be he saw in a vision of his sleep, as it will happen to the children of men throughout their generations until the day of judgment; he saw and understood everything, wrote his testimony, and placed it on earth for all the children of men and for their generations. Enoch's vision spans past and future to the judgment, and his written testimony preserves it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. This verse completes Enoch's vision notice: the testimony was placed on earth for all the children of men and for their generations. Writing is what lets one man's vision outlast his life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. This verse opens the next entry: in the twelfth jubilee, in the seventh week, years 575-581 AM, Enoch took a wife. The sentence is completed by the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. The date fragment, years 575-581 AM, anchors Enoch's marriage in the seventh week of the twelfth jubilee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. Enoch took Edna, daughter of Danel, daughter of his father's brother, to wife. The marriage follows the cousin pattern of the preceding generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. Enoch testified to the Watchers who had sinned with the daughters of men, for they had begun to unite themselves, so as to be defiled, with the daughters of men, and Enoch testified against them all. The scribe becomes a witness against the fallen teachers, whose original mission was instruction and uprightness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 48. He was taken from among the children of men, and the angels conducted him into the Garden of Eden in majesty and honour, and behold there he writes down the condemnation and judgment of the world and all the wickedness of the children of men. Enoch's translation leads not to rest but to a continuing scribal office in Eden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 49. On account of it God brought the waters of the flood upon all the land of Eden, for there Enoch was set as a sign to testify against all the children of men, recounting all the deeds of the generations until the day of condemnation. Enoch in Eden is a living testimony, and the flood answers the wickedness he records. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 50. This verse completes the testimony notice: Enoch recounts all the deeds of the generations until the day of condemnation. His Eden writings run from the first generations to the final judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 51. He burnt the incense of the sanctuary, even sweet spices acceptable before the Lord on the Mount. Enoch in Eden serves as a priest, offering acceptable incense before the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 52. The Lord has four places on the earth: the Garden of Eden, the Mount of the East, this mountain on which Moses stands, Mount Sinai, and Mount Zion, which will be sanctified in the new creation for a sanctification of the earth, through which the earth will be sanctified from all its guilt and uncleanness throughout the generations of the world. The four holy places link Eden, Sinai and the future Zion in one geography of sanctification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 53. In the fourteenth jubilee Methuselah took Edna, daughter of Azriel, daughter of his father's brother, in the third week, in the first year of this week, and begat a son and called his name Lamech. The genealogy resumes its dated march toward Noah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 55. At the close of the nineteenth jubilee, in the seventh week, in the sixth year, Adam died, and all his sons buried him in the land of his creation, and he was the first to be buried in the earth. The first man is the first buried, in the very land he was formed from. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 56. He lacked seventy years of one thousand years, for one thousand years are as one day in the testimony of the heavens, and therefore was it written concerning the tree of knowledge: 'On the day that ye eat thereof ye will die.' Adam's 930 years fall within the one day of a thousand years, so the sentence of Genesis 2:17 stands fulfilled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 57. This verse completes the thousand-year-day explanation: for this reason Adam did not complete the years of that day, for he died during it. The threatened 'day' of death is the heavenly day, not a single earthly day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 58. This verse is the hinge to the next death: at the close of this jubilee Cain was killed after Adam, in the same year. The first murderer's end follows the first man's burial without delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 59. Cain was killed after him in the same year, for his house fell upon him and he died in the midst of his house, killed by its stones, for with a stone he had killed Abel, and by a stone was he killed in righteous judgment. The manner of death matches the manner of murder, stone for stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 60. For this reason it was ordained on the heavenly tables: 'With the instrument with which a man killeth his neighbour with the same shall he be killed; after the manner that he wounded him, in like manner shall they deal with him.' Cain's end becomes a standing law of retribution, written before Sinai like the witness law of verse 5. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 2. In the first year of the third jubilee Cain slew Abel because God accepted Abel's sacrifice and did not accept Cain's offering. JFB on Genesis 4:4 explains divine 'respect' as a keen, earnest glance, shown in the offering's consumption by fire, and Abel's offering was made through faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He slew him in the field, and his blood cried from the ground to heaven, complaining because he had slain him. JFB on Genesis 4:10 notes Cain was challenged directly from the Shekinah itself, the blood crying out against a murder done in secret. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The Lord reproved Cain because of Abel, made him a fugitive on the earth because of his brother's blood, and cursed him upon the earth. JFB on Genesis 4:11-12 reads this curse as superadded to the general curse on the ground for Adam's sin, condemning Cain to perpetual exile as an outcast under an accusing conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Enos began to call on the name of the Lord on the earth. JFB on Genesis 4:26 renders this 'by the name of the Lord': God's people were called by his name, a name probably applied in contempt by the world, marking the Sethites as the worshipping line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 14. Cain built a city, and called its name after the name of his son Enoch. JFB on Genesis 4:17-22 notes it has ever been in cities that the race made its greatest social progress; here the first city bears the first city-builder's son's name, and settled exile replaces fugitive wandering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. In the sixth year in this week she bare him a son and he called his name Methuselah. JFB on Genesis 5:21 notes the name signifies 'He dieth, and the sending forth,' given by Enoch as prophetical of the flood, and Methuselah is computed to have died in the year of that catastrophe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. Enoch was moreover with the angels of God these six jubilees of years, and they showed him everything on earth and in the heavens, the rule of the sun, and he wrote down everything. JFB on Genesis 5:24 reads 'walked with God' as constant, familiar intercourse, and his translation as a palpable proof to an unbelieving age that his teaching was true; Jubilees extends the converse to six jubilees of angelic instruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. In the fifteenth jubilee, in the third week, Lamech took Betenos, daughter of Barakiel, daughter of his father's brother, and in this week she bare him a son and he called his name Noah, saying, 'This one will comfort me for my trouble and all my work, and for the ground which the Lord hath cursed.' JFB on Genesis 5:29 notes Noah means 'rest' or 'comfort,' and Lamech's words allude to the penal consequences of the fall and the hope of a Deliverer excited by the promise to Eve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 61. In the twenty-fifth jubilee Noah took Emzara, daughter of Rakeel, daughter of his father's brother, in the first year in the fifth week, and in the third year she bare him Shem, in the fifth year Ham, and in the first year in the sixth week Japheth. JFB on Genesis 5:32 notes Moses records not first-born sons but the succession from Adam through Seth to Abraham; here the succession runs through Noah's three sons, dated to year and week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
