The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 After these things the word of the LORD came unto Abram in a vision, saying, Fear not, Abram: I am thy shield, and thy exceeding great reward.

2 And Abram said, LORD God, what wilt thou give me, seeing I go childless, and the steward of my house is this Eliezer of Damascus?

3 And Abram said, Behold, to me thou hast given no seed: and, lo, one born in my house is mine heir.

4 And, behold, the word of the LORD came unto him, saying, This shall not be thine heir; but he that shall come forth out of thine own bowels shall be thine heir.

5 And he brought him forth abroad, and said, Look now toward heaven, and tell the stars, if thou be able to number them: and he said unto him, So shall thy seed be.

6 And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness.

7 And he said unto him, I am the LORD that brought thee out of Ur of the Chaldees, to give thee this land to inherit it.

8 And he said, LORD God, whereby shall I know that I shall inherit it?

9 And he said unto him, Take me an heifer of three years old, and a she goat of three years old, and a ram of three years old, and a turtledove, and a young pigeon.

10 And he took unto him all these, and divided them in the midst, and laid each piece one against another: but the birds divided he not.

11 And when the fowls came down upon the carcases, Abram drove them away.

12 And when the sun was going down, a deep sleep fell upon Abram; and, lo, an horror of great darkness fell upon him.

13 And he said unto Abram, Know of a surety that thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs, and shall serve them; and they shall afflict them four hundred years;

14 And also that nation, whom they shall serve, will I judge: and afterward shall they come out with great substance.

15 And thou shalt go to thy fathers in peace; thou shalt be buried in a good old age.

16 But in the fourth generation they shall come hither again: for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.

17 And it came to pass, that, when the sun went down, and it was dark, behold a smoking furnace, and a burning lamp that passed between those pieces.

18 In the same day the LORD made a covenant with Abram, saying, Unto thy seed have I given this land, from the river of Egypt unto the great river, the river Euphrates:

19 The Kenites, and the Kenizzites, and the Kadmonites,

20 And the Hittites, and the Perizzites, and the Rephaims,

21 And the Amorites, and the Canaanites, and the Girgashites, and the Jebusites.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God promises childless Abram an heir of his own body and offspring as countless as the stars, and Abram's trust is counted as righteousness. In a covenant ceremony, a smoking furnace and burning lamp pass between divided animals while Abram only watches, and God promises the land from the river of Egypt to the Euphrates, foretelling a sojourning and affliction first. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After the battle, God spoke to Abram in a vision, telling him not to fear and promising to be his shield and great reward, since Abram had refused Sodom's riches and might now fear revenge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Abram answered honestly that he was still childless, so what use were God's gifts while his household heir was Eliezer of Damascus, a servant rather than a son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Abram pressed the point: God had given him no offspring, so by the custom of the time a household servant stood to inherit, which seemed to close the promise rather than fulfil it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. God answered plainly that Eliezer would not be the heir, but a son from Abram's own body would, narrowing the promise to a true son and not merely an adopted heir. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God brought Abram outside to count the stars, promising offspring as countless as they were, and giving his faith something vast to look at when his own house looked empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Abram believed God, and God counted that belief as righteousness. Henry and JFB stress this as the heart of the chapter: Abram was accepted by trusting God's word, not by any work of his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God reminded Abram that he had brought him out of Ur to give him this land, grounding the future promise in a past deliverance Abram had already lived through. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Abram asked how he would know he would inherit the land, not in unbelief, JFB notes, but seeking a confirming sign, and God graciously gave him one rather than rebuking the question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. God directed Abram to prepare a heifer, a goat, a ram, a turtledove, and a pigeon, the animals used in solemn covenants, so that the promise would be sealed by a recognized rite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Abram cut the larger animals in half and laid the pieces opposite each other, as parties to a covenant passed between them, but left the birds whole, following the custom of the rite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Birds of prey swooped on the carcasses and Abram drove them away, a small act Henry reads as watchfulness: the sacrifice, and the promise it sealed, had to be guarded from spoiling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. As the sun set, a deep sleep and a great horror of darkness fell on Abram, a solemn dread befitting a man about to hear hard truths about his descendants' future. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. God told Abram his offspring would be strangers and servants in a foreign land, afflicted for four hundred years, so the promise of the land came with a long warning of suffering first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. God added that he would judge the oppressing nation and that Abram's people would leave with great wealth, so their bondage would end in deliverance, not in ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Abram himself, by contrast, would go to his fathers in peace and be buried in a good old age, spared the sight of the bondage that lay ahead for his children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. His descendants would return in the fourth generation, because the iniquity of the Amorites was not yet full, showing God's patience even toward peoples marked for judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. When darkness fell, a smoking furnace and a burning lamp passed between the pieces, and JFB notes that God alone passed through, binding himself to the promise while Abram, who was bound to nothing, only watched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. That same day God made a covenant with Abram, giving his offspring the land from the river of Egypt to the Euphrates, a boundary far wider than Abram then possessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • covenant. A binding promise; in this chapter God alone passes between the divided animals, binding himself to the promise while Abram only watches. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 15:17-18, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
