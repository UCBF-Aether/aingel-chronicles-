The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 19 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork.

2 Day unto day uttereth speech, and night unto night sheweth knowledge.

3 There is no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard.

4 Their line is gone out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world. In them hath he set a tabernacle for the sun,

5 Which is as a bridegroom coming out of his chamber, and rejoiceth as a strong man to run a race.

6 His going forth is from the end of the heaven, and his circuit unto the ends of it: and there is nothing hid from the heat thereof.

7 The law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul: the testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple.

8 The statutes of the LORD are right, rejoicing the heart: the commandment of the LORD is pure, enlightening the eyes.

9 The fear of the LORD is clean, enduring for ever: the judgments of the LORD are true and righteous altogether.

10 More to be desired are they than gold, yea, than much fine gold: sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb.

11 Moreover by them is thy servant warned: and in keeping of them there is great reward.

12 Who can understand his errors? cleanse thou me from secret faults.

13 Keep back thy servant also from presumptuous sins; let them not have dominion over me: then shall I be upright, and I shall be innocent from the great transgression.

14 Let the words of my mouth, and the meditation of my heart, be acceptable in thy sight, O LORD, my strength, and my redeemer.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Two witnesses in one psalm: creation, the heavens, the firmament, and the sun's daily course, preaches God's glory without words to all the earth; then God's law, perfect and pure, converts, enlightens, and warns, more desired than gold. The speaker ends asking cleansing from secret and presumptuous sins, and that speech and meditation be acceptable. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The heavens declare God's glory, and the firmament shows his handiwork: creation is a constant, wordless sermon on its Maker. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Day to day utters speech, and night to night shows knowledge: the succession of days and nights is a continuous teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. There is no speech nor language where their voice is not heard: this universal testimony needs no translation, for it reaches every tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Their line goes out through all the earth, their words to the world's end; in them God sets a tabernacle for the sun: the heavens' witness is worldwide, and the sun's dwelling among them is its centrepiece. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The sun is as a bridegroom from his chamber, rejoicing as a strong man to run a race: brightness, joy, and strength mark its daily course. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Its going forth is from heaven's end, its circuit to the ends of it, and nothing is hidden from its heat: no corner escapes the sun, as none escapes God's witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The law of the Lord is perfect, converting the soul; his testimony is sure, making wise the simple: from creation's witness David turns to Scripture, which does what nature cannot — converts and gives wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The statutes of the Lord are right, rejoicing the heart; his commandment is pure, enlightening the eyes: rightness brings joy, purity brings light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The fear of the Lord is clean, enduring for ever; his judgments are true and righteous altogether: true religion is undefiled and lasting, and God's judgments are wholly just. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They are more to be desired than gold, even much fine gold, and sweeter than honey and the honeycomb: value and delight together — Scripture is treasure and taste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Moreover by them God's servant is warned, and in keeping them there is great reward: warning and reward are the practical fruits of the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Who can understand his errors? Cleanse me from secret faults: even the best reader of the law needs cleansing from sins he does not see. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Keep back your servant also from presumptuous sins; let them not have dominion over me; then shall I be upright and innocent from the great transgression: wilful sin, once it rules, becomes the great transgression, and only God's restraint prevents it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in your sight, O Lord, my strength and my redeemer: speech and inward thought together are offered to God, who is both strength and redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
