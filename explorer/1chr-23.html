The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 So when David was old and full of days, he made Solomon his son king over Israel.

2 And he gathered together all the princes of Israel, with the priests and the Levites.

3 Now the Levites were numbered from the age of thirty years and upward: and their number by their polls, man by man, was thirty and eight thousand.

4 Of which, twenty and four thousand were to set forward the work of the house of the LORD; and six thousand were officers and judges:

5 Moreover four thousand were porters; and four thousand praised the LORD with the instruments which I made, said David, to praise therewith.

6 And David divided them into courses among the sons of Levi, namely, Gershon, Kohath, and Merari.

7 Of the Gershonites were, Laadan, and Shimei.

8 The sons of Laadan; the chief was Jehiel, and Zetham, and Joel, three.

9 The sons of Shimei; Shelomith, and Haziel, and Haran, three. These were the chief of the fathers of Laadan.

10 And the sons of Shimei were, Jahath, Zina, and Jeush, and Beriah. These four were the sons of Shimei.

11 And Jahath was the chief, and Zizah the second: but Jeush and Beriah had not many sons; therefore they were in one reckoning, according to their father’s house.

12 The sons of Kohath; Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel, four.

13 The sons of Amram; Aaron and Moses: and Aaron was separated, that he should sanctify the most holy things, he and his sons for ever, to burn incense before the LORD, to minister unto him, and to bless in his name for ever.

14 Now concerning Moses the man of God, his sons were named of the tribe of Levi.

15 The sons of Moses were, Gershom, and Eliezer.

16 Of the sons of Gershom, Shebuel was the chief.

17 And the sons of Eliezer were, Rehabiah the chief. And Eliezer had none other sons; but the sons of Rehabiah were very many.

18 Of the sons of Izhar; Shelomith the chief.

19 Of the sons of Hebron; Jeriah the first, Amariah the second, Jahaziel the third, and Jekameam the fourth.

20 Of the sons of Uzziel; Micah the first and Jesiah the second.

21 The sons of Merari; Mahli, and Mushi. The sons of Mahli; Eleazar, and Kish.

22 And Eleazar died, and had no sons, but daughters: and their brethren the sons of Kish took them.

23 The sons of Mushi; Mahli, and Eder, and Jeremoth, three.

24 These were the sons of Levi after the house of their fathers; even the chief of the fathers, as they were counted by number of names by their polls, that did the work for the service of the house of the LORD, from the age of twenty years and upward.

25 For David said, The LORD God of Israel hath given rest unto his people, that they may dwell in Jerusalem for ever:

26 And also unto the Levites; they shall no more carry the tabernacle, nor any vessels of it for the service thereof.

27 For by the last words of David the Levites were numbered from twenty years old and above:

28 Because their office was to wait on the sons of Aaron for the service of the house of the LORD, in the courts, and in the chambers, and in the purifying of all holy things, and the work of the service of the house of God;

29 Both for the shewbread, and for the fine flour for meat offering, and for the unleavened cakes, and for that which is baked in the pan, and for that which is fried, and for all manner of measure and size;

30 And to stand every morning to thank and praise the LORD, and likewise at even:

31 And to offer all burnt sacrifices unto the LORD in the sabbaths, in the new moons, and on the set feasts, by number, according to the order commanded unto them, continually before the LORD:

32 And that they should keep the charge of the tabernacle of the congregation, and the charge of the holy place, and the charge of the sons of Aaron their brethren, in the service of the house of the LORD.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells how David, old and full of days, made Solomon king, numbered the Levites, and divided them into their divisions for the service of the house of God. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When David was old and full of days, he made Solomon his son king over Israel. Henry notes David, only seventy yet full of days, settled the crown in solemn assembly, making Adonijah's later attempt impudent, and securing church and state together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. David gathered together all the princes of Israel, with the priests and the Levites. Henry notes the numbering and distribution were done in this solemn assembly for the temple service's order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Now the Levites were numbered from thirty years old and upward, and their number by their polls, man by man, was thirty and eight thousand. Henry notes Moses's Levites by this rule were 8,580, but now serviceable men were 38,000, above fourfold, and David numbered them not in pride but for good purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Of which, twenty and four thousand were to set forward the work of the house of the Lord. Henry notes almost two-thirds were appointed to attend priests in sacrifices, keeping vessels clean and service exact, serving 1,000 a week in twenty-four courses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And six thousand were officers and judges; moreover four thousand were porters, and four thousand praised the Lord with instruments. Henry notes officers and judges administered justice in the country, porters guarded the temple as its life-guards, and singers kept up the new office of praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. David divided them into courses among the sons of Levi, namely, Gershon, Kohath, and Merari. Henry notes families were mustered and disposed by kindred, so duty could be checked by roll call and families employed together would love and assist one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The sons of Merari's line are joined in one reckoning where sons were few. Henry notes two families were joined in one because they had not many sons: those weak separately may appear considerable together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The sons of Amram: Aaron and Moses. Aaron was separated, that he should sanctify the most holy things, he and his sons for ever, to burn incense before the Lord, to minister unto him, and to bless in his name for ever. Henry notes Aaron's family was advanced to the priesthood as recompense for his self-denial in submitting to younger Moses, while Moses' own family stood level with common Levites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Now concerning Moses the man of God, his sons were named of the tribe of Levi. Henry calls Moses' levelling an evidence of self-denial: with his interest with God and man he could have raised his family, but left his children no marks of distinction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And the sons of Eliezer were, Rehabiah the chief. And Eliezer had none other sons, but the sons of Rehabiah were very many. Henry notes Rehabiah's sons were highly multiplied, a recompense for Moses generously refusing a great nation from himself in place of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. These were the sons of Levi after the house of their fathers, even the chief of the fathers, as they were counted by number of names by their polls, that did the work for the service of the house of the Lord, from the age of twenty years and upward. Henry notes David, by God's direction, lowered enlistment from thirty to twenty, confirmed in his last words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. For David said, the Lord God of Israel hath given rest unto his people, that they may dwell in Jerusalem for ever. Henry gives the reason: with rest and Jerusalem as everlasting dwelling, there was no more carrying of tabernacle burdens, so service was easier and twenty-year-olds could bear it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And also unto the Levites, they shall no more carry the tabernacle, nor any vessels of it for the service thereof. Henry notes God considers our frame and lays no more than we can bear: heavy burdens required full strength, but settled service did not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. For by the last words of David the Levites were numbered from twenty years old and above. Henry notes David's last hand to the establishment expressly appointed twenty years forever after, to prevent idleness and pleasure habits in young Levites waiting till twenty-five. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Because their office was to wait on the sons of Aaron for the service of the house of the Lord, in the courts, and in the chambers, and in the purifying of all holy things, and the work of the service of the house of God. Henry notes Levites did the drudgery-work of God's house: keeping courts and chambers clean, setting things in place, ready for use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Both for the shewbread, and for the fine flour for meat offering, and for the unleavened cakes, and for that which is baked in the pan, and for that which is fried, and for all manner of measure and size. Henry notes Levites prepared showbread and offerings so priests had all ready, and kept the sanctuary standards of weights and measures exact. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And to stand every morning to thank and praise the Lord, and likewise at even. Henry notes singers thanked and praised at morning and evening sacrifices, a reasonable, articulate service beyond mere trumpet sound. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And to offer all burnt sacrifices unto the Lord in the sabbaths, in the new moons, and on the set feasts, by number, according to the order commanded unto them, continually before the Lord. Henry notes praise accompanied oblations on sabbaths, new moons, and feasts, as the Jewish church grew more intelligent in devotion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And that they should keep the charge of the tabernacle of the congregation, and the charge of the holy place, and the charge of the sons of Aaron their brethren, in the service of the house of the Lord. Henry notes porters kept charge of tabernacle and holy place, admitting none beyond what was allowed, and served Aaron's sons as brethren, hewn from the same rock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • courses. Rotating divisions of Levites serving in turn: the note on verse 4 describes twenty-four courses, a thousand serving each week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
