The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 43 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And he did as Joseph had told him, and filled all their sacks for them with food and put their money in their sacks, and put the cup in Benjamin's sack.

2 And early in the morning they departed, and it came to pass that, when they had gone from thence, Joseph said unto the steward of his house : " Pursue them, run and seize them, saying, ' For good ye have requited me with evil ; you have stolen from me the silver cup out of which my lord drinks.' And bring back to me their youngest brother, and fetch (him) quickly before I go forth to my seat of judgment." 3.

3 And he ran after them and said unto them according to these words.

4 And they said unto him : " God forbid that thy servants should do this thing, and steal from the house of thy lord any utensil, and the money also which we found in our sacks the first time, we thy servants brought back from the land of Canaan.

5 How then should we steal any utensil ?

6 Behold here are we and our sacks; search, and wherever thou findest the cup in the sack of any man amongst us, let him be slain, and we and our asses will serve thy lord." 6.

7 And he said unto them : Not so, the man with whom I find, him only shall I take as a servant, and ye will return in peace unto your house." 7.

8 And as he was searching in their vessels, beginning with the eldest and ending with the youngest, it was found in Benjamin's sack.

9 And they rent their garments, and laded their asses, and returned to the city and came to the house of Joseph, and they all bowed themselves on their faces to the ground before him.

10 And Joseph said unto them : " Ye have done evil."

11 And they

12 said : " What shall we say and how shall we defend ourselves?

13 Our lord hath discovered the transgression of his servants ; behold we are the servants of our lord, and our asses also." 10.

14 And Joseph said unto them : " I too fear the Lord ; as for you, go ye to your homes and let your brother be my servant, for ye have done evil.

15 Know ye not that a man delighteth in his cup as I with this cup?

16 And yet ye have stolen it from me." 11.

17 And Judah said : " O my lord, let thy servant, I pray thee, speak a word in my lord's ear; two brothers did thy servant's mother bear to our father; one went away and was lost, and hath not been found, and he alone is left of his mother, and thy servant our father loveth him, and his life also is bound up with the life of this (lad).

18 And it will come to pass, when we go to thy servant our father, and the lad is not with us, that he will die, and we shall bring down our father with sorrow unto death.

19 Now rather let me, thy servant, abide instead of the boy as a bondsman unto my lord, and let the lad go with his brethren, for I became surety for him at the hand of thy servant our father, and if I do not bring him back, thy servant will bear the blame to our father for ever." 14.

20 And Joseph saw that they were all accordant in goodness one with another, and he could not refrain himself, and he told them that he was Joseph.

21 And he conversed with them in the Hebrew tongue and fell on their neck and wept.

22 But they knew him not and they began to weep.

23 And he said unto them : " Weep not over me, but hasten and bring my father to me , and ye see that it is my mouth that speaketh and the eyes of my brother Benjamin see.

24 For behold this is the second year of the famine, and there are still five years without harvest or fruit of trees or ploughing.

25 Come down quickly ye and your households, so that ye perish not through the famine,

26 and do not be grieved for your possessions, for the Lord sent me before you to set things in order that many people might live.

27 And tell my father that I am still alive, and ye, behold, ye see that the Lord hath made me as a father to Pharaoh, and ruler over his house and over all the land of Egypt.

28 And tell my father of all my glory, and all the riches and glory that the Lord hath given me." 21.

29 And by the command of the mouth of Pharaoh he gave them chariots and provisions for the way, and he gave them all many-coloured raiment and silver.

30 And to their father he sent raiment and silver and ten asses which carried corn, and he sent them away.

31 And they went up and told their father that Joseph was alive, and was measuring out corn to all the nations of the earth, and that he was ruler over all the land of Egypt.

32 And their father did not believe it, for he was beside himself in his mind ; but when he saw the wagons which Joseph had sent, the life of his spirit revived, and he said : " It is enough for me if Joseph lives ; I will go down and see him before I die.

33 Jacob celebrates the Feast of First-fruits and journeys to Egypt.

34 List of his Descendants.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. The steward did as Joseph had told him, filling all their sacks with food, putting their money in their sacks, and putting the cup in Benjamin's sack. The gifts and the trap travel together: generosity on the surface, testing underneath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Early in the morning the brothers departed, and Joseph told the steward to pursue them, seize them, and charge them with requiting good with evil by stealing the silver cup out of which his lord drank, and to bring back the youngest brother quickly before Joseph went forth to his seat of judgment. Henry notes that Joseph tried how his brethren felt towards Benjamin, to see whether they would leave him to be a slave as they had once left Joseph. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The steward ran after them and spoke to them according to Joseph's words. The pursuit was swift and the charge was grave, the steward acting as Joseph's voice before the brothers knew Joseph's face. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The brothers answered that God forbid they should steal any utensil from their lord's house, and that the money found in their sacks the first time they had brought back from Canaan. Henry notes that they threw themselves on Joseph's mercy while acknowledging the righteousness of God, perhaps thinking of the injury done to Joseph for which God was now reckoning with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They asked how they could steal any utensil, having returned the money before. The argument is from the lesser to the greater: men who returned found money would not steal a lord's cup. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. They offered their sacks to be searched, saying wherever the cup was found, let that man be slain, and they and their asses would serve Joseph's lord. The rash offer, made in innocence of the trap, would have condemned Benjamin to death if Joseph had held them to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The steward answered that it would not be so: only the man with whom the cup was found would be taken as a servant, and the rest would return in peace to their house. The milder term is itself part of the test: Benjamin alone would be kept, and the brothers could walk away free. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. As the steward searched their vessels, beginning with the eldest and ending with the youngest, the cup was found in Benjamin's sack. Henry notes the design: when the cup was found on Benjamin, the brothers would have a pretext for leaving him to be a slave, if their hearts were still what they had been. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The brothers rent their garments, laded their asses, and returned to the city, coming to Joseph's house and bowing on their faces to the ground before him. The rent garments are grief, not for the cup, but for Benjamin and for their father, and the return to the city proves they would not abandon the youngest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Joseph said to them, ye have done evil. The short charge, spoken by the lord of Egypt, is the judgment the brothers feared, and it opens their confession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The brothers spoke, asking what they should say and how they should defend themselves. The question is the voice of men who know they cannot prove a negative, and who throw themselves on mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. They said their lord had discovered the transgression of his servants, and that they and their asses were his servants. Henry notes that even in afflictions where we believe ourselves wronged by men, we must own that God is righteous and finds out our sin, and the brothers' words own a transgression deeper than the cup. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The confession is completed: they and their asses are their lord's servants, surrendering all rather than leaving Benjamin. The surrender is the opposite of their old bargain over Joseph, and it is the evidence Joseph sought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Joseph answered that he too feared the Lord, and that they should go to their homes while their brother remained his servant, for they had done evil. The offer repeats the test's terms: freedom for ten, slavery for one, and the brothers' answer will decide everything. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Joseph asked whether they did not know that a man delights in his cup as he delighted in this cup. Henry notes the steward's charge that the cup was of daily use and would soon be missed, and that diligent search would be made for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Joseph charged them again with stealing the cup from him. The repetition presses the brothers: the evidence is against Benjamin, the terms allow their escape, and only Judah's plea stands between Benjamin and slavery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Judah said, let his servant speak a word in his lord's ear: his mother bore two brothers to their father, one went away and was lost and not found, and Benjamin alone was left of his mother, and their father loved him, and his father's life was bound up with the lad's life. Henry calls Judah's reasoning powerful: even a stranger could not but be wrought upon by it, and Judah's faithful cleaving to Benjamin in distress would later be recompensed when Benjamin's tribe kept with Judah's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Judah warned that when they returned to their father without the lad, he would die, and they would bring down their father with sorrow to death. The father's death is the stake Judah lays before the lord: Benjamin's slavery would kill Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Judah offered himself instead: let him abide as bondsman to his lord, and let the lad go with his brethren, for he became surety for Benjamin to his father, and if he did not bring him back he would bear the blame for ever. Henry notes the parallel Henry draws to Christ: Judah not only interceded for the transgressors but became a surety for them, testifying tender concern both for his father and his brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Joseph saw that they were all accordant in goodness one with another, and he could not refrain himself, and told them he was Joseph. Henry notes that Joseph found his brethren humbled for their sins, mindful of himself, respectful to their father, and tender of Benjamin, and now they were ripe for the comfort he designed by making himself known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Joseph conversed with them in the Hebrew tongue, fell on their neck, and wept. Henry notes that Joseph shed tears of tenderness and strong affection, throwing off the austerity with which he had behaved toward them, a picture of divine compassion toward returning penitents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The brothers knew him not at first and began to weep. Their weeping is the shock of recognition deferred: the lord of Egypt weeps over them in their own tongue, and they do not yet know why. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Joseph told them not to weep over him, but to hasten and bring his father to him, for they saw it was his mouth speaking and Benjamin's eyes saw it. Henry notes the words, I am Joseph, your brother, which humbled them yet more for selling him but encouraged them to hope for kind treatment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Joseph told them this was the second year of the famine, with five years still to come without harvest, fruit of trees, or ploughing. The count of years is the practical ground of the invitation: the famine would outlast any store they could carry home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Joseph urged them to come down quickly with their households, so that they would not perish through the famine. Henry notes that Joseph promised to take care of his father and all the family, and that it is children's duty to support their parents to the utmost of their ability. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Joseph told them not to be grieved for their possessions, for the Lord had sent him before them to set things in order that many people might live. Henry notes the heart of Joseph's comfort: whatever they thought to do against him, God had brought good out of it, and sinners must still grieve for their sins, though God brings good from them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Joseph told them to tell his father he was still alive, and that the Lord had made him as a father to Pharaoh and ruler over his house and all Egypt. The message reverses twenty years of mourning with a single report: the lost son rules the land of their hunger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Joseph told them to tell his father of all his glory and riches that the Lord had given him. The glory is reported not as boast but as proof: the father must believe the messenger, and the riches are the evidence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. By Pharaoh's command Joseph gave them chariots and provisions for the way, and gave them all many-coloured raiment and silver. Henry notes that Pharaoh was kind to Joseph and his relations for his sake, and that Egypt would make up the losses of their removal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Joseph sent to his father raiment and silver and ten asses carrying corn, and sent his brothers away. The gifts to the father are the earnest of the provision to come, corn for the journey and wealth for the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. The brothers went up and told their father that Joseph was alive, measuring out corn to all nations, and ruling over all Egypt. Henry notes that to hear Joseph was alive was too good news to be true, and that Jacob fainted, for he believed it not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Their father did not believe it, for he was beside himself in his mind, but when he saw the wagons Joseph had sent, his spirit revived, and he said it was enough if Joseph lived, and he would go down and see him before he died. Henry notes that Jacob was old and did not expect to live long, but wished his eyes refreshed with this sight before they closed, and then he needed no more to be happy in this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
