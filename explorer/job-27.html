The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 27 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover Job continued his parable, and said,

2 As God liveth, who hath taken away my judgment; and the Almighty, who hath vexed my soul;

3 All the while my breath is in me, and the spirit of God is in my nostrils;

4 My lips shall not speak wickedness, nor my tongue utter deceit.

5 God forbid that I should justify you: till I die I will not remove mine integrity from me.

6 My righteousness I hold fast, and will not let it go: my heart shall not reproach me so long as I live.

7 Let mine enemy be as the wicked, and he that riseth up against me as the unrighteous.

8 For what is the hope of the hypocrite, though he hath gained, when God taketh away his soul?

9 Will God hear his cry when trouble cometh upon him?

10 Will he delight himself in the Almighty? will he always call upon God?

11 I will teach you by the hand of God: that which is with the Almighty will I not conceal.

12 Behold, all ye yourselves have seen it; why then are ye thus altogether vain?

13 This is the portion of a wicked man with God, and the heritage of oppressors, which they shall receive of the Almighty.

14 If his children be multiplied, it is for the sword: and his offspring shall not be satisfied with bread.

15 Those that remain of him shall be buried in death: and his widows shall not weep.

16 Though he heap up silver as the dust, and prepare raiment as the clay;

17 He may prepare it, but the just shall put it on, and the innocent shall divide the silver.

18 He buildeth his house as a moth, and as a booth that the keeper maketh.

19 The rich man shall lie down, but he shall not be gathered: he openeth his eyes, and he is not.

20 Terrors take hold on him as waters, a tempest stealeth him away in the night.

21 The east wind carrieth him away, and he departeth: and as a storm hurleth him out of his place.

22 For God shall cast upon him, and not spare: he would fain flee out of his hand.

23 Men shall clap their hands at him, and shall hiss him out of his place.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Job continues after the friends fall silent: as God lives, who has taken away his judgment, he will not speak wickedness nor utter deceit while his breath is in him, and he will hold fast his righteousness and not let it go. The portion of the wicked man with God, he says, is that his children are multiplied for the sword, his riches pass to the just, and terror takes him as waters in the night — the same portion Zophar had described, now claimed by Job himself. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Job continues his parable, after the friends fall silent. JFB notes it was Zophar's turn, but he and the others were silent, virtually admitting defeat, so after a pause Job proceeds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. As God lives, who has taken away Job's judgment, and the Almighty, who has vexed his soul. JFB notes taken away judgment foreshadows Christ's taken judgment, and that God will not give Job his right by declaring his innocence, with vexed as made bitter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. All the while Job's breath is in him, and the spirit of God is in his nostrils. JFB notes this implies knowledge that the living soul was breathed into man by God, and Maurer's reading: as yet all my breath is in me, the reason he can speak so boldly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Job's lips shall not speak wickedness, nor his tongue utter deceit. JFB notes the deceit would be admitting guilt against his conscience's witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God forbid that Job should justify the friends; till he dies he will not remove his integrity from him. JFB explains justify as approving their views, and integrity as what they deny on account of his misfortunes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Job holds fast his righteousness and will not let it go; his heart shall not reproach him so long as he lives. JFB renders heart as conscience, reproaching not one of his days, not repenting of any day since he came into existence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Let Job's enemy be as the wicked, and he that rises up against him as the unrighteous. JFB explains this not as a curse on enemies, but as accounting an opponent of his innocence as actuated by criminal hostility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For what is the hope of the hypocrite, though he has gained, when God takes away his soul? JFB notes gained is antithetic to taketh away, which literally draws the soul out of the body as from a scabbard, and that Job admits Bildad's and Zophar's point yet proves he is no hypocrite by still calling on God amid trials. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Will God hear the hypocrite's cry when trouble comes upon him? The hypocrite's cry in trouble goes unheard, unlike Job's persistent calling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Will the hypocrite delight himself in the Almighty, and always call upon God? JFB notes he may call in prosperity to seem religious, but will not, as Job does, call in calamities verging on death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Job will teach the friends concerning the hand of God, and what is with the Almighty he will not conceal. JFB notes verses 11-23 state not so much Job's own sentiments as what Zophar would have said, stated more strongly, concerning God's hand in governing men and the counsel regulating his dealings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Behold, you all yourselves have seen it; why then are you thus altogether vain? JFB notes the friends themselves see the wicked often afflicted, though often the reverse, yet vainly make affliction an argument to prove Job wicked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. This is the portion of a wicked man with God, and the heritage of oppressors received from the Almighty. JFB links portion and heritage to Zophar's argument in chapter 20, now stated by Job. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. If the wicked's children are multiplied, it is for the sword, and his offspring shall not be satisfied with bread. JFB notes his family only increases to perish by sword or famine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Those that remain of him shall be buried in death, and his widows shall not weep. JFB explains those escaping war and famine shall be buried by the deadly plague, death personified performing funeral rites with no one else, and widows as their widows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Though the wicked heaps up silver as the dust, and prepares raiment as the clay. JFB explains dust and clay as images of multitudes, and many changes of raiment as a chief constituent of Eastern wealth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. He prepares it, but the just shall put it on, and the innocent shall divide the silver. The wicked's hoard passes to the just by introverted parallelism. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He builds his house as a moth, and as a booth which the keeper makes. JFB explains the moth worm's house, broken whenever raiment is shaken out, and the booth as a bough-formed hut a vineyard guard raises for temporary shelter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The rich man shall lie down, but he shall not be gathered; he opens his eyes, and he is not. JFB notes the readings: honourably buried yet seeing destruction in the unseen world, or lying rich in bed with nothing robbed from him, opening his eyes to find nothing more there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Terrors take hold on him as waters, and a tempest steals him away in the night. JFB compares the terrors to a sudden violent flood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The east wind carries him away, and he departs, and as a storm hurls him out of his place. The wind that carried others' hopes now carries the wicked himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. For God shall cast upon him, and not spare; he would fain flee out of his hand. JFB explains cast as thunderbolts cast upon him, unspared, with flight impossible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Men shall clap their hands at him, and hiss him out of his place. JFB explains clapping as for joy at his downfall, and hissing as derision, alluding to Bildad's words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages
