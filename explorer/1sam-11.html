The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Nahash the Ammonite came up, and encamped against Jabeshgilead: and all the men of Jabesh said unto Nahash, Make a covenant with us, and we will serve thee.

2 And Nahash the Ammonite answered them, On this condition will I make a covenant with you, that I may thrust out all your right eyes, and lay it for a reproach upon all Israel.

3 And the elders of Jabesh said unto him, Give us seven days’ respite, that we may send messengers unto all the coasts of Israel: and then, if there be no man to save us, we will come out to thee.

4 Then came the messengers to Gibeah of Saul, and told the tidings in the ears of the people: and all the people lifted up their voices, and wept.

5 And, behold, Saul came after the herd out of the field; and Saul said, What aileth the people that they weep? And they told him the tidings of the men of Jabesh.

6 And the Spirit of God came upon Saul when he heard those tidings, and his anger was kindled greatly.

7 And he took a yoke of oxen, and hewed them in pieces, and sent them throughout all the coasts of Israel by the hands of messengers, saying, Whosoever cometh not forth after Saul and after Samuel, so shall it be done unto his oxen. And the fear of the LORD fell on the people, and they came out with one consent.

8 And when he numbered them in Bezek, the children of Israel were three hundred thousand, and the men of Judah thirty thousand.

9 And they said unto the messengers that came, Thus shall ye say unto the men of Jabeshgilead, To morrow, by that time the sun be hot, ye shall have help. And the messengers came and shewed it to the men of Jabesh; and they were glad.

10 Therefore the men of Jabesh said, To morrow we will come out unto you, and ye shall do with us all that seemeth good unto you.

11 And it was so on the morrow, that Saul put the people in three companies; and they came into the midst of the host in the morning watch, and slew the Ammonites until the heat of the day: and it came to pass, that they which remained were scattered, so that two of them were not left together.

12 And the people said unto Samuel, Who is he that said, Shall Saul reign over us? bring the men, that we may put them to death.

13 And Saul said, There shall not a man be put to death this day: for to day the LORD hath wrought salvation in Israel.

14 Then said Samuel to the people, Come, and let us go to Gilgal, and renew the kingdom there.

15 And all the people went to Gilgal; and there they made Saul king before the LORD in Gilgal; and there they sacrificed sacrifices of peace offerings before the LORD; and there Saul and all the men of Israel rejoiced greatly.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Nahash the Ammonite encamps against Jabesh-gilead and will covenant only on condition of thrusting out every man's right eye, as a reproach on all Israel; the elders beg seven days to send messengers through Israel. The tidings come to Gibeah while Saul is coming after the herd from the field, the Spirit of God comes on him, his anger is kindled, and he hews a yoke of oxen in pieces and sends them through Israel with the summons, whosoever comes not forth after Saul and after Samuel, so shall it be done to his oxen. Three hundred thousand of Israel and thirty thousand of Judah muster at Bezek; in the morning watch Saul divides them into three companies, slays the Ammonites until the heat of the day, and scatters the remnant so that two are not left together. When the people would kill Saul's former despisers, Saul forbids it, saying today the LORD has wrought salvation in Israel, and at Gilgal the kingdom is renewed before the LORD. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Nahash the Ammonite came up and encamped against Jabesh-gilead, and the men of Jabesh asked for a covenant, offering to serve him. The first fruit of Saul's government, Henry notes, would be the rescue of this besieged city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Nahash answered that he would covenant only on condition of thrusting out all their right eyes, as a reproach upon all Israel. To save their lives, Henry observes, men will part with liberty and even consent to have their eyes put out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The elders of Jabesh asked seven days' respite to send messengers throughout Israel, promising to come out if no man saved them. The week of grace bought time for the very deliverance Nahash scorned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The messengers came to Gibeah of Saul and told the tidings, and all the people lifted up their voices and wept. The city's danger became the nation's grief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Behold, Saul came after the herd out of the field and asked what ailed the people that they wept, and they told him the tidings of Jabesh. The king was still at his farm work when the call came. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The Spirit of God came upon Saul when he heard the tidings, and his anger was kindled greatly. See the faith and confidence of Saul, Henry says, and grounded thereon his courage and resolution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. He took a yoke of oxen, hewed them in pieces, and sent them throughout Israel, saying, Whosoever cometh not forth after Saul and after Samuel, so shall it be done unto his oxen. The fear of the LORD fell on the people, and they came out with one consent. When the Spirit of the Lord comes upon men, Henry notes, it makes them expert even without experience, and when zeal for God's glory and love for the brethren urge earnest efforts, great effects may speedily be produced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. When he numbered them in Bezek, Israel were three hundred thousand and Judah thirty thousand. The muster proved the summons had worked across the whole land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. They told Jabesh's messengers: tomorrow, by the time the sun is hot, ye shall have help. The messengers showed it to Jabesh, and they were glad. Help was promised before the battle was fought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Therefore the men of Jabesh told the Ammonites, Tomorrow we will come out unto you, and ye shall do with us all that seemeth good. Their bold message masked the coming relief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. On the morrow Saul put the people in three companies, came into the midst of the host in the morning watch, and slew the Ammonites until the heat of the day, scattering the remnant so that two were not left together. See Saul's activity in this business, Henry says: with God pleased to help, great effects were speedily produced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The people said to Samuel, Who is he that said, Shall Saul reign over us? Bring the men, that we may put them to death. Victory made the people eager to punish Saul's former despisers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Saul said, There shall not a man be put to death this day, for today the LORD hath wrought salvation in Israel. They now honoured Saul whom they had despised, Henry notes, and if an enemy be made a friend, that is more to our advantage than to have him slain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Then Samuel said to the people, Come, let us go to Gilgal and renew the kingdom there. The kingship, proved in battle and sealed by mercy, would be publicly renewed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. All the people went to Gilgal, made Saul king before the LORD, sacrificed peace offerings, and Saul and all Israel rejoiced greatly. The once-despised Saviour, Henry reflects, will at length be acknowledged by all as the Lord's own anointed king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
