The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 99 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 The LORD reigneth; let the people tremble: he sitteth between the cherubims; let the earth be moved.

2 The LORD is great in Zion; and he is high above all the people.

3 Let them praise thy great and terrible name; for it is holy.

4 The king’s strength also loveth judgment; thou dost establish equity, thou executest judgment and righteousness in Jacob.

5 Exalt ye the LORD our God, and worship at his footstool; for he is holy.

6 Moses and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel among them that call upon his name; they called upon the LORD, and he answered them.

7 He spake unto them in the cloudy pillar: they kept his testimonies, and the ordinance that he gave them.

8 Thou answeredst them, O LORD our God: thou wast a God that forgavest them, though thou tookest vengeance of their inventions.

9 Exalt the LORD our God, and worship at his holy hill; for the LORD our God is holy.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The LORD reigns, seated between the cherubim, and the earth is moved; he is great in Zion, high above all people, and his great and terrible name is holy. His strength loves judgment: he establishes equity and executes judgment and righteousness in Jacob. Moses and Aaron among his priests and Samuel among those who called on his name are held up as examples — they called, God answered, he spoke to them in the cloudy pillar, and he forgave them even while taking vengeance on their doings. The refrain returns twice: exalt the LORD and worship at his footstool, at his holy hill, for he is holy. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Reign, trembling people, cherubim throne, moved earth: Henry splits the trembling, subjects under Old Testament terror and enemies facing golden sceptre or iron rod, while submitters are established and not moved, and opposers are shaken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. The Lord great in Zion and high above all the people means God presided in Israel's religion: he sat between the cherubim as on his throne, to give law by the oracles thence delivered, and as on the mercy-seat, to receive petitions. This was Israel's honour, to have among them the Shechinah, the special presence of God attended by holy angels, the temple as royal palace and the Holy of holies as presence-chamber. In Zion he is known and praised, served as great more than anywhere else, and high there above all people, as what is high is exposed to view and looked up to, so that in Zion the perfections of the divine nature appear more conspicuous and illustrious than anywhere else. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Praising his great and terrible name follows: let those who dwell in Zion and worship there praise it and give him the glory due to it, for it is holy. The holiness of God's name makes it truly great to his friends and terrible to his enemies — this is what those above adore, Holy, holy, holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. King's strength loving judgment, equity established, judgment and righteousness executed in Jacob: power not abused for wrong but delighting in justice, with God giving excellent laws and magistrates judging for God, is the theocracy's happiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Exalting the Lord and worshipping at his footstool is the inference from Israel's happiness above any other people: they had God so nigh unto them, sitting between the cherubim, and statutes and judgments so righteous, by which equity was established and God himself ruled in Jacob, so they are commanded to give him the glory of the good government they are under, as now established in both church and state. The greater the public mercies we share, the more we are obliged to bear a part in public homage — the setting up of Christ's kingdom especially ought to be the matter of our praise. When we draw nigh, our hearts must be filled with high thoughts of him and he must be exalted in our souls, and the more we abase ourselves and lie prostrate before him, the more we exalt him: worship at his footstool, his ark, the footstool to the mercy-seat between the cherubim, or cast down upon the pavement of his courts, for he is holy, and his holiness should strike an awe upon us as it does on the angels themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Moses and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel among those that call on his name, are the prime leaders and most active, useful governors of Israel: in Moses and Aaron the theocracy began, for they formed Israel into a people, and in Samuel that form of government in great measure ended, when the people rejected Samuel and were said to reject God himself as their immediate king. Moses is counted among the priests because he executed the priest's office till Aaron was settled in it and consecrated Aaron and his sons, so the Jews call him the priest of the priests. None of all the nations could produce three such men, with such intercourse with heaven, whom God knew by name: they honoured him by prayer, relying not on their own wisdom or virtue but having recourse to God in every emergency, and by obedience, keeping his testimonies and ordinances as their rule, knowing that unless they did so they could not expect their prayers answered — and God answered them, granting what they asked, working miracles at their special instance, and communing with them as a friend converses with a friend, speaking to them in the cloudy pillar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. He spake unto them in the cloudy pillar: they kept his testimonies, and the ordinance that he gave them, is the intimate communion of Moses, Aaron, and Samuel with God: first, their gracious observance of God. No kingdom had men that honoured God as these three men of the kingdom of Israel did. They honoured him, first, by their prayers. Samuel, though not among his priests, yet was among those that called on his name; and for this they were all famous: they called upon the Lord — they relied not on their own wisdom or virtue, but in every emergency had recourse to God, towards him was their desire, and on him their dependence. Second, by their obedience: they kept his testimonies, and the ordinances that he gave them — they made conscience of their duty, and in everything made God's word and law their rule, as knowing that unless they did so they could not expect their prayers should be answered. Moses did all according to the pattern shown him; it is often repeated, According to all that God commanded Moses, so did he. Aaron and Samuel did likewise. Those were the greatest men and most honourable that were most eminent for keeping God's testimonies and conforming to the rule of his word. Second, God's gracious acceptance of them: he answered them, and granted them the things which they called upon him for. They all wonderfully prevailed with God in prayer; miracles were wrought at their special instance and request; nay, he not only condescended to do that for them which they desired, as a prince for a petitioner, but he communed with them as one friend familiarly converses with another: he spoke unto them in the cloudy pillar. He often spoke to Samuel from his childhood — the word of the Lord came to him, and, probably, sometimes he spoke to him by a bright cloud overshadowing him: however, to Moses and Aaron he often spoke out of the famous cloudy pillar. Israel are now reminded of this, for the confirming of their faith, that though they had not every day such sensible tokens of God's presence as the cloudy pillar was, yet to those that were their first founders, and to him that was their great reformer, God was pleased thus to manifest himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Thou answeredst them and wast a God that forgavest them, though thou tookest vengeance of their inventions, recalls their good offices for Israel: Moses stood in the gap, Aaron between the living and the dead, and Samuel cried unto the Lord when Israel was in distress, and at their prayer God forgave the people they prayed for and, though he took vengeance of their inventions, did not cut them off from being a people as their sin deserved. He was propitious for them, sparing the people at their request even when his wrath was so highly provoked that it was just ready to break in upon them to their utter overthrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Exalting the Lord and worshipping at his holy hill repeats the call on account of what he has done for us formerly as well as of late: worship at Zion, on which he has now set his temple and will shortly set his King, the former a type of the latter, there, as the centre of unity, let all God's Israel meet with their adorations, for the Lord our God is holy, and appears so not only in his holy law but in his holy gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
