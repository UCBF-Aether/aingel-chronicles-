The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Corinthians 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover, brethren, I would not that ye should be ignorant, how that all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea;

2 And were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea;

3 And did all eat the same spiritual meat;

4 And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ.

5 But with many of them God was not well pleased: for they were overthrown in the wilderness.

6 Now these things were our examples, to the intent we should not lust after evil things, as they also lusted.

7 Neither be ye idolaters, as were some of them; as it is written, The people sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play.

8 Neither let us commit fornication, as some of them committed, and fell in one day three and twenty thousand.

9 Neither let us tempt Christ, as some of them also tempted, and were destroyed of serpents.

10 Neither murmur ye, as some of them also murmured, and were destroyed of the destroyer.

11 Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come.

12 Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.

13 There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.

14 Wherefore, my dearly beloved, flee from idolatry.

15 I speak as to wise men; judge ye what I say.

16 The cup of blessing which we bless, is it not the communion of the blood of Christ? The bread which we break, is it not the communion of the body of Christ?

17 For we being many are one bread, and one body: for we are all partakers of that one bread.

18 Behold Israel after the flesh: are not they which eat of the sacrifices partakers of the altar?

19 What say I then? that the idol is any thing, or that which is offered in sacrifice to idols is any thing?

20 But I say, that the things which the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to devils, and not to God: and I would not that ye should have fellowship with devils.

21 Ye cannot drink the cup of the Lord, and the cup of devils: ye cannot be partakers of the Lord’s table, and of the table of devils.

22 Do we provoke the Lord to jealousy? are we stronger than he?

23 All things are lawful for me, but all things are not expedient: all things are lawful for me, but all things edify not.

24 Let no man seek his own, but every man another’s wealth.

25 Whatsoever is sold in the shambles, that eat, asking no question for conscience sake:

26 For the earth is the Lord’s, and the fulness thereof.

27 If any of them that believe not bid you to a feast, and ye be disposed to go; whatsoever is set before you, eat, asking no question for conscience sake.

28 But if any man say unto you, This is offered in sacrifice unto idols, eat not for his sake that shewed it, and for conscience sake: for the earth is the Lord’s, and the fulness thereof:

29 Conscience, I say, not thine own, but of the other: for why is my liberty judged of another man’s conscience?

30 For if I by grace be a partaker, why am I evil spoken of for that for which I give thanks?

31 Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God.

32 Give none offence, neither to the Jews, nor to the Gentiles, nor to the church of God:

33 Even as I please all men in all things, not seeking mine own profit, but the profit of many, that they may be saved.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul warns the Corinthians by Israel's example: all were under the cloud, passed through the sea, and ate and drank spiritual food and drink from Christ the Rock, yet most were overthrown in the wilderness. He forbids sharing in idol feasts, says the cup and bread are communion in Christ's blood and body, and concludes that whether eating or drinking, all should be done to God's glory, seeking not one's own profit but the profit of many. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Corinthians need self-denying watchfulness despite all their privileges, lest they be castaways, for the Israelites with all theirs were mostly castaways for want of it. Our fathers were all under the cloud, continually under the defense of the pillar of cloud, the symbol of God's presence, and all passed through the sea by God's miraculous interposition. Five times 'all' is repeated for five favors God bestowed; five times correspondingly they sinned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. They were baptized unto Moses, God's servant and representative of the old covenant, as Jesus is of the gospel covenant — initiated by the miracle of the cloud and the safe passage through the sea into belief in Moses. Their virtual baptism did not save them from the doom of lust, any more than actual baptism will save Corinthians who walk carelessly. Cloud and sea, both water, took Israel out of sight and restored them to view, as baptismal water does. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They all ate the same spiritual meat, the manna, which corresponded to the Lord's Supper as the water from the rock answered to baptism. It was spiritual food because given by God's Spirit, not human labor, and in its typical sense Christ the true bread of heaven was signified; believing Israelites felt indistinctly that something more was meant, and their implicit faith was counted for justification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They all drank the same spiritual drink from the spiritual Rock that accompanied them — not the literal rock following them, but Christ the Rock, who attended on them to minister to them, going before them yet following behind when occasion required, and satisfying their bodily thirst as a symbol of spiritual drink from himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. But with the majority of them — all except Joshua and Caleb of the first generation — God was not well pleased, for they were overthrown in the wilderness, strewn in heaps far from the land of promise. Privilege did not secure them, and the event showed they had not pleased God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. These things became our examples, samples of what will befall us if we too walk carelessly with all our privileges. First stands lust, the fountain of the other offenses: they lusted after evil things, pining for the fish and leeks of Egypt, discontented with what God provided and longing for what he withheld. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Do not be idolaters, as some of them were, who deliberately sat down, ate, and drank at the idol feast of the calves in Horeb, then rose up to play with lascivious dancing and singing round the calf. The Corinthians were in the same danger by a like act at idol feasts, even without professedly worshipping an idol. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Neither let us commit fornication, as some of them did and fell in one day twenty-three thousand — Moses counts twenty-four thousand including all who died in the plague, while Paul counts those who fell in one day, or both round a number between the two. The seeming difference is reconcilable, and in any case the warning stands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Neither let us tempt Christ, as some of them tempted him — trying his long-suffering so as to wear it out — complaining for water though they had the spiritual Rock, and loathing the light bread though they ate the true manna. They were punished by fiery serpents and saved by the brazen serpent, emblem of Christ. The Corinthians risked provoking God's long-suffering by walking on the verge of idolatry through overweening confidence in their knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Neither murmur, as some of them murmured upon the death of Korah's company, who were themselves murmurers. Their murmurs against Moses and Aaron were virtually murmurs against God, and fourteen thousand seven hundred perished, destroyed by the destroyer, the same destroying angel sent by God. Paul glances here at Corinthian murmurs against himself, Christ's apostle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. All these things happened to them by way of example, and were written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the ages have come. We live in the last dispensation, the winding up of all former ages, so our responsibilities are greater and our guilt greater if we fall short of our privileges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Therefore let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall — from his place in the church of God, temporally and spiritually. Our security, as it relates to God, consists in faith; as it relates to ourselves, it consists in fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. No temptation has overtaken you except such as is common to man, adapted to man's powers of endurance. God is faithful to the covenant he made in calling you, and will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you can bear, but with the temptation will make the way of escape — the appropriate way in each particular temptation, in due time after patience has had its perfect work — that you may be able to bear up under it, not that he will simply take it away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Therefore, my beloved, flee idolatry. Do not tamper with it by doubtful acts such as eating idol meats on the plea of Christian liberty; the only safety is wholly shunning whatever borders on idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Paul appeals to their own judgment, speaking to wise men — with irony toward their boast of wisdom — and urges them to judge what he says. Partaking of the Lord's Supper involves partaking of the Lord himself, and partaking of Jewish sacrificial meats involved partaking of God's altar; so to partake of an idol feast is to have fellowship with devils. Private judgment must be exercised, searching Scripture with every aid and with prayer for the Spirit's teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The cup of blessing, answering to the Jewish cup over which thanks were offered at Passover, which we bless — minister and congregation together, the consecration being the corporate act of the whole church — is the joint participation of the blood of Christ. He who with faith partakes of cup and bread partakes really but spiritually of Christ's blood and body and of the benefits of his sacrifice. It is not said the cup is the blood, but the communion of the blood; the breaking of bread represented the crucifixion of his body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Because there is one loaf, we who are many are one body, for we all partake of that one loaf, which becomes assimilated to all our bodies, so we become one body with Christ and therefore with one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Consider Israel after the flesh, the literal as distinguished from the spiritual Israel: those who eat the sacrifices are partakers of the altar, and so of God whose the altar is, having fellowship in God and his worship, of which the altar is the symbol. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. What then does Paul say — that a thing sacrificed to an idol is anything real in the sense the heathen regard it, or that an idol is anything real? No: the inference from the Supper and sacrifice analogies is guarded against. An idol is nothing as the heathen think it, but behind it there is another reality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. What the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to devils, to demons — subordinate evil spirits under Satan, prince of this world. Heathendom is under Satan's dominion, so he and his demons are in fact the powers worshipped by the heathen, whether consciously or not, and fear rather than love motivates such worship. To partake of idol feasts is therefore to have fellowship with devils. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of devils, nor partake of the Lord's table and the table of devils — really and spiritually, though outwardly you may attempt it. At idol feasts libations were poured to the idol first and then the guests drank, so drinking meant fellowship with the idol. The Lord's Supper is a feast on a table, not a sacrifice on an altar; our only altar is the cross and our only sacrifice Christ once for all, and the communicant has real fellowship in his body once sacrificed and now exalted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Do we provoke the Lord to jealousy by dividing our fellowship between him and idols? Are we stronger than he, that we can risk a contest with him? The question exposes the folly of daring God's jealous power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. All things are lawful, Paul repeats from the Corinthian plea, but all things are not expedient, and all things do not edify — do not build up the spiritual temple of the church in faith and love. Christian freedom is not governed by external things, as though because we can use them we must; use or non-use is regulated by regard to edification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Let no man seek his own, but each another's welfare. The principle of edification is unselfish: the neighbor's good governs the use of liberty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Whatever is sold in the flesh market, eat, asking no question for conscience' sake. If you asked and heard it had been offered to idols, a needless scruple would arise that would never have arisen had you not asked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The ground for eating without questioning is that the earth and its fullness belong to the Lord and are appointed for our use; where conscience suggests no scruple, all meats may be eaten. The creation belongs to God, not to idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. If an unbeliever invites you to a general entertainment — not an idol feast, though idol-offered meat might be served — and you are disposed to go, eat whatever is set before you, asking no question for conscience' sake. Paul tacitly implies they would be as well not to go, but does not forbid it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. But if someone at table, a weak Christian wishing to warn his brother, says the meat has been offered in sacrifice, then do not eat, for the sake of the one who pointed it out and for conscience' sake — not your own conscience, but the weak brother's, lest you cause him a stumbling-block. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Conscience here means the other's, not your own: why should my liberty be judged by another man's conscience? Paul puts himself in their position — in cases where his own conscience has no scruple he is not bound in God's judgment by any conscience but his own; but he is to abstain where his liberty would offend another's conscience or cause his weak brother's destruction. The objector's retort, that he should be judged only by his own conscience, is answered by the limit that all must be done to God's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. If I partake thankfully — by grace meaning thankfully — why should I be evil spoken of for that for which I give thanks? Thanksgiving consecrates all the Christian's acts, and the scrupulous man who eats nothing without questioning should not condemn the thankful eater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Whether you eat or drink or whatever you do, do all to the glory of God — which involves regard for your neighbor's edification. This is the rule that answers the objector and limits liberty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Give no offense in things indifferent — neither to Jews, nor to Gentiles, nor to the church of God. In essential matters of doctrine and practice, even in the smallest detail, believers must not swerve from principle whatever offense results; but offense is unnecessary if caused by our own spirit, necessary only if caused by the truth itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. Even as Paul tries to please all men in all things, not seeking his own profit but the profit of the many, that they may be saved. His example sums up the chapter: liberty surrendered for others' salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • spiritual Rock. Christ, from whom Israel drank in the wilderness, as Paul identifies the rock that followed them. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • communion. Participation or fellowship; Paul says the cup is communion of Christ's blood and the bread of his body. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
