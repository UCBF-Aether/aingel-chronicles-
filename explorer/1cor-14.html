The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Corinthians 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Follow after charity, and desire spiritual gifts, but rather that ye may prophesy.

2 For he that speaketh in an unknown tongue speaketh not unto men, but unto God: for no man understandeth him; howbeit in the spirit he speaketh mysteries.

3 But he that prophesieth speaketh unto men to edification, and exhortation, and comfort.

4 He that speaketh in an unknown tongue edifieth himself; but he that prophesieth edifieth the church.

5 I would that ye all spake with tongues but rather that ye prophesied: for greater is he that prophesieth than he that speaketh with tongues, except he interpret, that the church may receive edifying.

6 Now, brethren, if I come unto you speaking with tongues, what shall I profit you, except I shall speak to you either by revelation, or by knowledge, or by prophesying, or by doctrine?

7 And even things without life giving sound, whether pipe or harp, except they give a distinction in the sounds, how shall it be known what is piped or harped?

8 For if the trumpet give an uncertain sound, who shall prepare himself to the battle?

9 So likewise ye, except ye utter by the tongue words easy to be understood, how shall it be known what is spoken? for ye shall speak into the air.

10 There are, it may be, so many kinds of voices in the world, and none of them is without signification.

11 Therefore if I know not the meaning of the voice, I shall be unto him that speaketh a barbarian, and he that speaketh shall be a barbarian unto me.

12 Even so ye, forasmuch as ye are zealous of spiritual gifts, seek that ye may excel to the edifying of the church.

13 Wherefore let him that speaketh in an unknown tongue pray that he may interpret.

14 For if I pray in an unknown tongue, my spirit prayeth, but my understanding is unfruitful.

15 What is it then? I will pray with the spirit, and I will pray with the understanding also: I will sing with the spirit, and I will sing with the understanding also.

16 Else when thou shalt bless with the spirit, how shall he that occupieth the room of the unlearned say Amen at thy giving of thanks, seeing he understandeth not what thou sayest?

17 For thou verily givest thanks well, but the other is not edified.

18 I thank my God, I speak with tongues more than ye all:

19 Yet in the church I had rather speak five words with my understanding, that by my voice I might teach others also, than ten thousand words in an unknown tongue.

20 Brethren, be not children in understanding: howbeit in malice be ye children, but in understanding be men.

21 In the law it is written, With men of other tongues and other lips will I speak unto this people; and yet for all that will they not hear me, saith the LORD.

22 Wherefore tongues are for a sign, not to them that believe, but to them that believe not: but prophesying serveth not for them that believe not, but for them which believe.

23 If therefore the whole church be come together into one place, and all speak with tongues, and there come in those that are unlearned, or unbelievers, will they not say that ye are mad?

24 But if all prophesy, and there come in one that believeth not, or one unlearned, he is convinced of all, he is judged of all:

25 And thus are the secrets of his heart made manifest; and so falling down on his face he will worship God, and report that God is in you of a truth.

26 How is it then, brethren? when ye come together, every one of you hath a psalm, hath a doctrine, hath a tongue, hath a revelation, hath an interpretation. Let all things be done unto edifying.

27 If any man speak in an unknown tongue, let it be by two, or at the most by three, and that by course; and let one interpret.

28 But if there be no interpreter, let him keep silence in the church; and let him speak to himself, and to God.

29 Let the prophets speak two or three, and let the other judge.

30 If any thing be revealed to another that sitteth by, let the first hold his peace.

31 For ye may all prophesy one by one, that all may learn, and all may be comforted.

32 And the spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets.

33 For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints.

34 Let your women keep silence in the churches: for it is not permitted unto them to speak; but they are commanded to be under obedience as also saith the law.

35 And if they will learn any thing, let them ask their husbands at home: for it is a shame for women to speak in the church.

36 What? came the word of God out from you? or came it unto you only?

37 If any man think himself to be a prophet, or spiritual, let him acknowledge that the things that I write unto you are the commandments of the Lord.

38 But if any man be ignorant, let him be ignorant.

39 Wherefore, brethren, covet to prophesy, and forbid not to speak with tongues.

40 Let all things be done decently and in order.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul urges the Corinthians to pursue love and desire spiritual gifts, above all prophecy, because prophecy edifies the whole church while an unknown tongue, without interpretation, edifies only the speaker. Tongues are a sign to unbelievers, as Isaiah said God would speak to this people with other lips, yet in church Paul would rather speak five intelligible words than ten thousand in a tongue, and all things must be done decently and in order. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Follow after charity as your first and chief aim, since it is the greatest. Yet as a secondary aim desire zealously spiritual gifts, but chiefly that you may prophesy — speak and exhort under inspiration, whether foretelling future events or explaining obscure Scripture and setting forth Christian doctrine and practice. Modern preaching succeeds prophecy without the inspiration, and prophecy is to be desired more than tongues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He who speaks in an unknown tongue speaks not to men but to God, who alone understands all languages; no man generally understands him, since the few with the gift of interpretation are the exception. In the spirit, as opposed to the understanding, he speaks mysteries — unintelligible to hearers, exciting wonder rather than instructing them. Corinth's mixed merchant population gave scope for tongues, but their legitimate use was before an audience understanding the speaker's tongue, not in mere display. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. But he who prophesies speaks to men for edification, exhortation, and comfort. Edification's two principal species are exhortation to remove sluggishness and comfort or consolation to remove sadness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He who speaks in a tongue edifies himself, as he understands what the tongue expresses, but the church, the congregation, does not. The gift benefits the speaker alone when uninterpreted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Paul wishes them all to speak with tongues — so far is he from objecting to tongues — but rather, as his ulterior and higher wish, that they should prophesy. Tongues must therefore mean languages, not ecstatic unintelligible rhapsodies, for Paul could never wish the latter for them. He who prophesies is greater, because more useful, than he who speaks with tongues, unless he interprets, so that the church may receive edifying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. If Paul came speaking with tongues, what would he profit them unless he spoke by revelation or by knowledge or by prophesying or by doctrine? Revelation is the supernatural unveiling of divine truths, prophesying the enunciation of such revelations; knowledge corresponds to doctrine, the teaching of general obvious truths of salvation from the common storehouse of believers. Without interpretation there is no edification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Even lifeless things, pipe or harp, if they give no distinction in the tones, how will what is piped or harped be known? An instrument must sound distinct notes, or its tune cannot be recognized. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. If the trumpet gives an uncertain sound, having no definite meaning, who will prepare for battle? One succession of notes should summon to attack, another to retreat, another to some other evolution; an indistinct call prepares no one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. So likewise you, unless you utter by the tongue words easy to be understood, how will it be known what is spoken? You will be speaking into the air, in vain, like one beating the air — living speakers should be clearer than lifeless instruments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. There are, it may be, so many kinds of voices in the world, kinds of articulate speech, and none of them is without signification — none is without its own distinct voice and meaning. Every language means something to someone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Therefore, if I do not know the meaning of the voice, I shall be a barbarian — a foreigner speaking a foreign language — to him who speaks, and he a barbarian to me. Without shared meaning, speaker and hearer are strangers to each other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Since you are zealous for spiritual gifts — emanations from the one Spirit — seek that you may excel to the edifying of the church. Seek the gifts in abundance precisely for building up the congregation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Let him who speaks in an unknown tongue pray that he may interpret. Though his words are unintelligible to hearers, he should pray to obtain the gift of interpreting, which will make them edifying to the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. If I pray in an unknown tongue, my spirit prays — my higher being, the passive object of the Spirit's operations and instrument of prayer — but my understanding, the active instrument of thought, is unfruitful in edifying others, since the vehicle of expression is unintelligible to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. What then is Paul's determination? He will pray with the spirit, but with the understanding also, and sing with the spirit and with the understanding also. By inference he will keep silence altogether if he cannot pray with understanding so as to be understood by others — a prescient warning against liturgies kept in dead languages unintelligible to the masses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Otherwise, if you bless with the spirit, how shall he who occupies the room of the unlearned — one who, lacking the gift of interpretation, is reduced to the position of a private person — say Amen at your giving of thanks, since he does not understand what you say? Prayer is not a vicarious duty done by others for us; the congregation must join with the leader and say aloud its responsive Amen in assent, as in Jewish and primitive Christian usage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. You indeed give thanks well — the prayers of the church were called blessings and giving of thanks, as synagogue prayers were called eulogies with thanksgiving joined — but the other is not edified. Good thanksgiving that no one understands still fails the congregation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Paul thanks God that he speaks with tongues more than all of them — in a foreign tongue, singular. His correction of tongues comes not from lacking the gift but from possessing it abundantly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Yet in the church Paul would rather speak five words with his understanding, that he might teach others, than ten thousand words in an unknown tongue. He would not wish at all to speak ten thousand words unintelligibly; five intelligible words outweigh them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Brethren — an appellation to conciliate their reception of the exhortation — do not be children in understanding, as preferring gifts abused to non-edification would make you. Be childlike, not childish: in malice be children, but in understanding be men, full-grown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. In the law — the whole Old Testament — it is written from Isaiah that God will speak to this people with other tongues and other lips, yet they will not hear him. Israel heard God not in their familiar language, so he would speak through the tongues of foes sent against them, and even then they would not hearken. Paul applies it: it is a penalty to be associated with men of a strange tongue, yet the Corinthians impose this on the church, and speakers in foreign tongues appear like children just weaned, stammering unintelligibly, ridiculous as babbling drunkards or madmen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Tongues are therefore a sign, not to believers but to unbelievers — often a condemnatory sign, since they will not understand, they shall not understand. Prophesying, by contrast, is not for the obstinately unbelieving but for believers: it makes believers of those not willfully unbelieving and spiritually nourishes those who already believe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. If the whole church comes together and all speak with tongues, and unlearned persons or unbelievers come in from curiosity or better motive, will they not say you are mad? The more assembled and the more speaking in unknown tongues, the more the impression grows that the whole body is a mob of fanatics — like the builders of Babel after the confusion of tongues, or a cause tried between two deaf men before a deaf judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. But if all prophesy — speaking the truth by the Spirit intelligibly — and an unbeliever or unlearned person comes in, he is convinced by all and judged by all. Conviction falls on the unbeliever, and his secret character is searched into, as with the unlearned; the effect on any single entrant would be produced by each speaker in turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Thus the secrets of his heart are made manifest, as his inner character is opened out by the sword of the Spirit, the word of God in the prophet's hand. No argument is stronger for religion than its manifestation of men to themselves in their true character. So convicted, judged, and manifested to himself, he falls down and worships God, announcing openly that God is in you of a truth — and by implication that the God in you is the true God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. What then is the true rule for the use of gifts? When you come together, each one has a psalm — an extemporary inspired song like those of Mary, Zechariah, Simeon, and Anna — a doctrine to impart, a tongue, a revelation, an interpretation. Let all things be done for edifying: that is the general rule under which the particular cases fall, and each is bound to obey church ordinances not adverse to Scripture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. If anyone speaks in an unknown tongue, let it be by two, or at most three, at each meeting, and in turns, and let one interpret — one who has the gift, and not more than one. Order replaces the simultaneous clamor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. If there is no interpreter, let the speaker in tongues keep silence in the church, and speak to himself and to God privately, not in the hearing of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Let the prophets speak two or three at one meeting, one by one in turn, and let the others judge — by their power of discerning spirits whether the speaker really speaks under the Spirit's influence. Paul gives prophets similar rules to tongue-speakers, though he does not add 'at the most,' lest he seem to quench prophesyings, the most edifying of gifts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. If anything is revealed to another sitting by, a hearer, let the first hold his peace. He who came furnished with a previous revelation gives place to him who at the assembly is moved by a sudden revelation from the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. For you can all prophesy one by one, if you will, giving way to one another, that all may learn and all may be comforted. The possibility of orderly succession justifies the command to yield. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets: their own spirits, acted on by the Holy Spirit, are not so hurried away as to cease to be under their own control. They can if they will hear others and not demand to be heard alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. For God is not the author of confusion but of peace, as in all churches of the saints. God is a God of peace, and must not among the Corinthians be supposed a God of confusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Let your women keep silence in the churches, for it is not permitted to them to speak; they are to be in subjection, as the law — the whole Old Testament, here Genesis — says. For women to speak in public would be an act of independence, as if not subject to their husbands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. If they want to learn anything, let them ask their own husbands at home, not other men and not in public. It is a shame — indecorous — for women to speak in the church, and the anticipated objection about asking questions publicly is answered: ask at home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. What! Did the word of God come out from you, or did it come to you only? Do the Corinthians pretend their church is the first from which the gospel came, that they should give law to all others, or the only church to which it has come? Their judgment is not above that of other churches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. If anyone thinks himself a prophet or spiritual — one capable of discerning spirits, as Apollos's followers prided themselves on being spiritual — let him acknowledge that the things Paul writes are the commandments of the Lord, a direct assertion of inspiration: Paul's words as apostle are Christ's words. He appeals to the whole body for facts no body could mistake: their conversion by the Spirit's demonstration and power, the miraculous power then generally exercised, and the gift enabling the spiritual to decide whether his epistle was Scripture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. If anyone is willfully ignorant, not wishing to recognize these ordinances and Paul's apostolic authority in enjoining them, let him be ignorant — left to his ignorance at his own peril. Paul feels it a waste of words to argue further, an argument with weight for Corinthians who admired knowledge so much. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. Therefore, brethren, covet earnestly to prophesy — earnestly desire it, stronger than merely 'forbid not' — and forbid not speaking with tongues. The contrast marks how much higher Paul esteemed prophecy than tongues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. But let all things be done decently and in order. While desiring prophecy and not forbidding tongues, order governs all: church government is the best security for Christian liberty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

Let all things be done for edifying: that is the general rule under which the particular cases fall, and each is bound to obey church ordinances not adverse to Scripture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. If anyone speaks in an unknown tongue, let it be by two, or at most three, at each meeting, and in turns, and let one interpret — one who has the gift, and not more than one. Order replaces the simultaneous clamor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. If there is no interpreter, let the speaker in tongues keep silence in the church, and speak to himself and to God privately, not in the hearing of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Let the prophets speak two or three at one meeting, one by one in turn, and let the others judge — by their power of discerning spirits whether the speaker really speaks under the Spirit's influence. Paul gives prophets similar rules to tongue-speakers, though he does not add 'at the most,' lest he seem to quench prophesyings, the most edifying of gifts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. If anything is revealed to another sitting by, a hearer, let the first hold his peace. He who came furnished with a previous revelation gives place to him who at the assembly is moved by a sudden revelation from the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. For you can all prophesy one by one, if you will, giving way to one another, that all may learn and all may be comforted. The possibility of orderly succession justifies the command to yield. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets: their own spirits, acted on by the Holy Spirit, are not so hurried away as to cease to be under their own control. They can if they will hear others and not demand to be heard alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. For God is not the author of confusion but of peace, as in all churches of the saints. God is a God of peace, and must not among the Corinthians be supposed a God of confusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Let your women keep silence in the churches, for it is not permitted to them to speak; they are to be in subjection, as the law — the whole Old Testament, here Genesis — says. For women to speak in public would be an act of independence, as if not subject to their husbands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. If they want to learn anything, let them ask their own husbands at home, not other men and not in public. It is a shame — indecorous — for women to speak in the church, and the anticipated objection about asking questions publicly is answered: ask at home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. What! Did the word of God come out from you, or did it come to you only? Do the Corinthians pretend their church is the first from which the gospel came, that they should give law to all others, or the only church to which it has come? Their judgment is not above that of other churches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. If anyone thinks himself a prophet or spiritual — one capable of discerning spirits, as Apollos's followers prided themselves on being spiritual — let him acknowledge that the things Paul writes are the commandments of the Lord, a direct assertion of inspiration: Paul's words as apostle are Christ's words. He appeals to the whole body for facts no body could mistake: their conversion by the Spirit's demonstration and power, the miraculous power then generally exercised, and the gift enabling the spiritual to decide whether his epistle was Scripture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. If anyone is willfully ignorant, not wishing to recognize these ordinances and Paul's apostolic authority in enjoining them, let him be ignorant — left to his ignorance at his own peril. Paul feels it a waste of words to argue further, an argument with weight for Corinthians who admired knowledge so much. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. Therefore, brethren, covet earnestly to prophesy — earnestly desire it, stronger than merely 'forbid not' — and forbid not speaking with tongues. The contrast marks how much higher Paul esteemed prophecy than tongues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. But let all things be done decently and in order. While desiring prophecy and not forbidding tongues, order governs all: church government is the best security for Christian liberty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
