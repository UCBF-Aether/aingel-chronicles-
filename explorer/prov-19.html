The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Better is the poor that walketh in his integrity, than he that is perverse in his lips, and is a fool.

2 Also, that the soul be without knowledge, it is not good; and he that hasteth with his feet sinneth.

3 The foolishness of man perverteth his way: and his heart fretteth against the LORD.

4 Wealth maketh many friends; but the poor is separated from his neighbour.

5 A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he that speaketh lies shall not escape.

6 Many will intreat the favour of the prince: and every man is a friend to him that giveth gifts.

7 All the brethren of the poor do hate him: how much more do his friends go far from him? he pursueth them with words, yet they are wanting to him.

8 He that getteth wisdom loveth his own soul: he that keepeth understanding shall find good.

9 A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he that speaketh lies shall perish.

10 Delight is not seemly for a fool; much less for a servant to have rule over princes.

11 The discretion of a man deferreth his anger; and it is his glory to pass over a transgression.

12 The king’s wrath is as the roaring of a lion; but his favour is as dew upon the grass.

13 A foolish son is the calamity of his father: and the contentions of a wife are a continual dropping.

14 House and riches are the inheritance of fathers: and a prudent wife is from the LORD.

15 Slothfulness casteth into a deep sleep; and an idle soul shall suffer hunger.

16 He that keepeth the commandment keepeth his own soul; but he that despiseth his ways shall die.

17 He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the LORD; and that which he hath given will he pay him again.

18 Chasten thy son while there is hope, and let not thy soul spare for his crying.

19 A man of great wrath shall suffer punishment: for if thou deliver him, yet thou must do it again.

20 Hear counsel, and receive instruction, that thou mayest be wise in thy latter end.

21 There are many devices in a man’s heart; nevertheless the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand.

22 The desire of a man is his kindness: and a poor man is better than a liar.

23 The fear of the LORD tendeth to life: and he that hath it shall abide satisfied; he shall not be visited with evil.

24 A slothful man hideth his hand in his bosom, and will not so much as bring it to his mouth again.

25 Smite a scorner, and the simple will beware: and reprove one that hath understanding, and he will understand knowledge.

26 He that wasteth his father, and chaseth away his mother, is a son that causeth shame, and bringeth reproach.

27 Cease, my son, to hear the instruction that causeth to err from the words of knowledge.

28 An ungodly witness scorneth judgment: and the mouth of the wicked devoureth iniquity.

29 Judgments are prepared for scorners, and stripes for the back of fools.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Better the poor walking in integrity than the perverse in lips, and the chapter weighs haste without knowledge, false witness that shall not escape, wealth's many friends against the poor man's distance, and anger deferred as a man's glory. Pity for the poor is lending to the LORD, the counsel of the LORD stands against many devices in a heart, and the fear of the LORD tends to life and satisfied rest. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Better the poor walking in integrity than he perverse in lips and a fool, for a poor man fearing God is more honourable and happy than a man without wisdom and grace, however rich or advanced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. That the soul be without knowledge is not good, and he who hastens with his feet sins, for what good can the soul do without knowledge, and he sins who will not ponder the path of his feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The foolishness of man perverts his way, and his heart frets against the LORD, for men run into troubles by their own folly and then fret at God's appointments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Wealth makes many friends, but the poor is separated from his neighbour, showing how strong is men's love of money. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he who speaks lies shall not escape, for those telling lies in discourse are in a fair way to bear false witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Many will entreat the favour of the prince, and every man is a friend to him who gives gifts, for we are without excuse if we do not love God with all our hearts, since his gifts are past number and men's gifts are fruits of his bounty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. All the brethren of the poor hate him, and his friends go far from him, yet he pursues them with words, for Christ was left by all his disciples, yet the Father was with him, encouraging faith by his large experience of poverty's sorrows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. He who gets wisdom loves his own soul, and he who keeps understanding shall find good, for only those who get true wisdom love their souls aright. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he who speaks lies shall perish, for lying is a damning, destroying sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Delight is not seemly for a fool, much less for a servant to rule over princes, for a man without wisdom and grace has no right to true joy, and it is unseemly for a servant to sin to oppress God's free-men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The discretion of a man defers his anger, and it is his glory to pass over a transgression, for he attains the most true glory who most steadily overcomes evil with good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The king's wrath is as the roaring of a lion, but his favour as dew upon the grass, for Christ is a King whose wrath against enemies is as a lion's roar and whose favour to his people is as refreshing dew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. A foolish son is the calamity of his father, and the contentions of a wife are a continual dropping, showing the vanity of the world, that we are liable to the greatest griefs where we promise ourselves the greatest comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. House and riches are the inheritance of fathers, and a prudent wife is from the LORD, for a discreet and virtuous wife is more valuable than house and riches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Slothfulness casts into a deep sleep, and an idle soul shall suffer hunger, for a sluggish, slothful disposition makes men poor and brings them to want, in this life and the next. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. He who keeps the commandment keeps his own soul, but he who despises his ways shall die, for if we keep God's word, God's word will keep us from everything really hurtful, and free grace does not remove the necessity of obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. He who has pity on the poor lends unto the LORD, and what he has given he will pay again, for God has chosen the poor of this world to be rich in faith and heirs of his kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Chasten thy son while there is hope, and let not thy soul spare for his crying, for when parents keep under foolish tenderness, they do their best to render children a comfort and happy in themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. A man of great wrath shall suffer punishment, for if thou deliver him, yet thou must do it again, for the spared and spoiled child is likely to become a man of great wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Hear counsel and receive instruction, that thou mayest be wise in thy latter end, for those who would be wise in their latter end must be taught and ruled when young. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. There are many devices in a man's heart, nevertheless the counsel of the LORD shall stand, for what should we desire but that all our purposes may agree with God's holy will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. The desire of a man is his kindness, and a poor man is better than a liar, for it is far better to have a heart to do good and want ability, than ability and want a heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. The fear of the LORD tends to life, and he who has it shall abide satisfied and not be visited with evil, for those living in God's fear shall get safety, satisfaction, and true and complete happiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. A slothful man hides his hand in his bosom and will not bring it to his mouth again, for indolence indulged so grows upon people that they have no heart for the most needful things for themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Smite a scorner, and the simple will beware, and reprove one who has understanding, and he will understand knowledge, for a gentle rebuke goes farthest with a man of understanding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. He who wastes his father and chases away his mother is a son causing shame and bringing reproach, for the young man who wastes his father's substance or makes his aged mother destitute is hateful and will come to disgrace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Cease, my son, to hear the instruction that causes to err from the words of knowledge, for it is the wisdom of young men to dread hearing talk that puts loose and evil principles into the mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. An ungodly witness scorns judgment, and the mouth of the wicked devours iniquity, for those are the worst of sinners who are glad of an opportunity to sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Judgments are prepared for scorners, and stripes for the back of fools, for man's unbelief shall not make God's threatenings of no effect, and Christ himself, bearing sins not his own, was not spared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
