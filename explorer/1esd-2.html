The Text — 1 Esdras 2 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 In the first year of Cyrus king of the Persians, that the word of the Lord might be accomplished, that he had promised by the mouth of Jeremy;

2 The Lord raised up the spirit of Cyrus the king of the Persians, and he made proclamation through all his kingdom, and also by writing,

3 Saying, Thus saith Cyrus king of the Persians; The Lord of Israel, the most high Lord, hath made me king of the whole world,

4 And commanded me to build him an house at Jerusalem in Jewry.

5 If therefore there be any of you that are of his people, let the Lord, even his Lord, be with him, and let him go up to Jerusalem that is in Judea, and build the house of the Lord of Israel: for he is the Lord that dwelleth in Jerusalem.

6 Whosoever then dwell in the places about, let them help him, those, I say, that are his neighbours, with gold, and with silver,

7 With gifts, with horses, and with cattle, and other things, which have been set forth by vow, for the temple of the Lord at Jerusalem.

8 Then the chief of the families of Judea and of the tribe of Benjamin stood up; the priests also, and the Levites, and all they whose mind the Lord had moved to go up, and to build an house for the Lord at Jerusalem,

9 And they that dwelt round about them, and helped them in all things with silver and gold, with horses and cattle, and with very many free gifts of a great number whose minds were stirred up thereto.

10 King Cyrus also brought forth the holy vessels, which Nabuchodonosor had carried away from Jerusalem, and had set up in his temple of idols.

11 Now when Cyrus king of the Persians had brought them forth, he delivered them to Mithridates his treasurer:

12 And by him they were delivered to Sanabassar the governor of Judea.

13 And this was the number of them; A thousand golden cups, and a thousand of silver, censers of silver twenty nine, vials of gold thirty, and of silver two thousand four hundred and ten, and a thousand other vessels.

14 So all the vessels of gold and of silver, which were carried away, were five thousand four hundred threescore and nine.

15 These were brought back by Sanabassar, together with them of the captivity, from Babylon to Jerusalem.

16 But in the time of Artexerxes king of the Persians Belemus, and Mithridates, and Tabellius, and Rathumus, and Beeltethmus, and Semellius the secretary, with others that were in commission with them, dwelling in Samaria and other places, wrote unto him against them that dwelt in Judea and Jerusalem these letters following;

17 To king Artexerxes our lord, Thy servants, Rathumus the storywriter, and Semellius the scribe, and the rest of their council, and the judges that are in Celosyria and Phenice.

18 Be it now known to the lord king, that the Jews that are up from you to us, being come into Jerusalem, that rebellious and wicked city, do build the marketplaces, and repair the walls of it and do lay the foundation of the temple.

19 Now if this city and the walls thereof be made up again, they will not only refuse to give tribute, but also rebel against kings.

20 And forasmuch as the things pertaining to the temple are now in hand, we think it meet not to neglect such a matter,

21 But to speak unto our lord the king, to the intent that, if it be thy pleasure it may be sought out in the books of thy fathers:

22 And thou shalt find in the chronicles what is written concerning these things, and shalt understand that that city was rebellious, troubling both kings and cities:

23 And that the Jews were rebellious, and raised always wars therein; for the which cause even this city was made desolate.

24 Wherefore now we do declare unto thee, O lord the king, that if this city be built again, and the walls thereof set up anew, thou shalt from henceforth have no passage into Celosyria and Phenice.

25 Then the king wrote back again to Rathumus the storywriter, to Beeltethmus, to Semellius the scribe, and to the rest that were in commission, and dwellers in Samaria and Syria and Phenice, after this manner;

26 I have read the epistle which ye have sent unto me: therefore I commanded to make diligent search, and it hath been found that that city was from the beginning practising against kings;

27 And the men therein were given to rebellion and war: and that mighty kings and fierce were in Jerusalem, who reigned and exacted tributes in Celosyria and Phenice.

28 Now therefore I have commanded to hinder those men from building the city, and heed to be taken that there be no more done in it;

29 And that those wicked workers proceed no further to the annoyance of kings,

30 Then king Artexerxes his letters being read, Rathumus, and Semellius the scribe, and the rest that were in commission with them, removing in haste toward Jerusalem with a troop of horsemen and a multitude of people in battle array, began to hinder the builders; and the building of the temple in Jerusalem ceased until the second year of the reign of Darius king of the Persians.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

In the first year of Cyrus, king of the Persians, the word promised through Jeremiah is fulfilled: Cyrus proclaims that the Lord has charged him to build the house at Jerusalem, and the temple vessels are delivered to be carried back. The returned exiles begin the work, but adversaries write to King Artaxerxes, whose letters, when read, halt the building in haste, and the work of the house of God ceases. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Jeremy (Jeremiah). the prophet whose word of return Cyrus's proclamation is said to fulfil (verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Samaritans. the adversaries whose letter stops the work, in the study note's account of the religious antipathy that followed (verse 30). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. JFB notes the Persian empire founded by Cyrus is dated to 536 B.C., and that the reference to Jeremiah's word being fulfilled is a parenthetic statement of the historian, not part of the proclamation itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. JFB notes Cyrus's proclamation, though issued in his first year, did not take effect until the year following, and that its phraseology shows he had seen the prophecies foretelling his name and career two hundred years before his birth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. JFB notes Cyrus's claim to all the kingdoms of the earth, though in the Oriental style of hyperbole, was literally true in that the Persian empire was then the greatest ruling power in the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. JFB notes the existence of remarkable predictions led Cyrus to acknowledge that his kingdoms were gifts from the Lord God of heaven, and prompted him to fulfil the duty laid upon him long before his birth, which was the source of the great favor he showed the Jews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. JFB notes the purport of the edict was to grant full permission to those Jewish exiles in every part of his kingdom who chose to return, and to recommend those who remained to aid the poor and feeble on their way and contribute liberally to rebuilding the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. JFB notes the returning exiles were liberally assisted by multitudes of their captive countrymen who chose to remain, and by their neighbors, whether from favorable disposition toward the Jewish faith or imitation of court policy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. JFB notes the emigrants were assisted with gifts and goods by neighbors and by countrymen born in Babylonia or comfortably established there by family connections or property. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. JFB notes the paternal and ecclesiastical chiefs of Judah and Benjamin, with some from other tribes who retained attachment to pure worship, naturally took the lead, and their example was followed by all whose piety and patriotism were strong enough to brave the discouragements of the enterprise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. JFB notes the liberality of neighbors and remaining countrymen toward the emigrants displayed hearty good will and great liberality in aiding and promoting the views of those departing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. JFB notes that though it is said the vessels were cut in pieces, that would not be done to the large and magnificent vases, and the Hebrew word may signify merely cut off, that is, from further use in the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. JFB identifies Shesh-bazzar, the prince of Judah, as Zerubbabel son of Salathiel, born in Babylon, whose court name signified fire-worshipper, and who was recognized among the exiles as hereditary prince of Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. JFB notes Shesh-bazzar, that is Zerubbabel, received the vessels from the treasurer and was recognized as hereditary prince of Judah among the exiles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. JFB notes the vessels here specified amount only to 2,499, so it is probable only the larger vases are mentioned, while the inventory of the whole, including great and small, came to the gross sum stated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. JFB notes the gross sum of the vessels covers great and small together, while the itemized list names only the larger vases. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. JFB notes not all exiles embraced the privilege granted, since the great proportion, born in Babylon, preferred their comfortable homes to a distant and hazardous journey, and the returning exiles did not all go at once, the first band going with Zerubbabel and others later with Ezra and Nehemiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. JFB notes the officers named were supposed to be deputy governors appointed by the Persian king over the provinces west of the Euphrates, and that their letter aimed to press upon the royal notice the inexpediency and danger of rebuilding the walls of Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. JFB notes the three officers were deputy governors over the provinces subject to Persia west of the Euphrates, writing in the Syrian, or Aramaean, language used by the Persians in decrees and communications relative to the Jews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. JFB notes the object of the letter was to prejudice the king's mind against rebuilding the walls, laboring hard to make the measure appear inexpedient and dangerous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. JFB defines the three revenues at stake as a poll tax, a property tax, and excise dues on articles of trade, and notes the letter and the edict that followed, commanding an immediate cessation of the work, form the exclusive subject of this narrative. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. JFB explains the writers' plea in terms of the Oriental phrase of eating a prince's salt, equivalent to receiving maintenance from him, which bound them in loyalty to warn the king of danger to his interests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. JFB notes the writers appealed to their maintenance from the king's palace as the ground of their loyalty, and sought a search of the royal records to prejudice the king against the rebuilding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. JFB notes the letter and the edict that followed commanded an immediate cessation of the work at the city walls, and that the historian then returns from this digression to resume the thread concerning the building of the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. JFB notes the edict commanding cessation of the work was the exclusive subject of the letter and its answer, and that the stoppage first gave rise to the strong religious antipathy between Jews and Samaritans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. JFB notes the work of the house of God then ceased, an occurrence that first gave rise to the strong religious antipathy between the Jews and the Samaritans, later aggravated by the rival temple on Mount Gerizim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
