The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 After these things Paul departed from Athens, and came to Corinth;

2 And found a certain Jew named Aquila, born in Pontus, lately come from Italy, with his wife Priscilla; (because that Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome:) and came unto them.

3 And because he was of the same craft, he abode with them, and wrought: for by their occupation they were tentmakers.

4 And he reasoned in the synagogue every sabbath, and persuaded the Jews and the Greeks.

5 And when Silas and Timotheus were come from Macedonia, Paul was pressed in the spirit, and testified to the Jews that Jesus was Christ.

6 And when they opposed themselves, and blasphemed, he shook his raiment, and said unto them, Your blood be upon your own heads; I am clean; from henceforth I will go unto the Gentiles.

7 And he departed thence, and entered into a certain man’s house, named Justus, one that worshipped God, whose house joined hard to the synagogue.

8 And Crispus, the chief ruler of the synagogue, believed on the Lord with all his house; and many of the Corinthians hearing believed, and were baptized.

9 Then spake the Lord to Paul in the night by a vision, Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace:

10 For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee: for I have much people in this city.

11 And he continued there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.

12 And when Gallio was the deputy of Achaia, the Jews made insurrection with one accord against Paul, and brought him to the judgment seat,

13 Saying, This fellow persuadeth men to worship God contrary to the law.

14 And when Paul was now about to open his mouth, Gallio said unto the Jews, If it were a matter of wrong or wicked lewdness, O ye Jews, reason would that I should bear with you:

15 But if it be a question of words and names, and of your law, look ye to it; for I will be no judge of such matters.

16 And he drave them from the judgment seat.

17 Then all the Greeks took Sosthenes, the chief ruler of the synagogue, and beat him before the judgment seat. And Gallio cared for none of those things.

18 And Paul after this tarried there yet a good while, and then took his leave of the brethren, and sailed thence into Syria, and with him Priscilla and Aquila; having shorn his head in Cenchrea: for he had a vow.

19 And he came to Ephesus, and left them there: but he himself entered into the synagogue, and reasoned with the Jews.

20 When they desired him to tarry longer time with them, he consented not;

21 But bade them farewell, saying, I must by all means keep this feast that cometh in Jerusalem: but I will return again unto you, if God will. And he sailed from Ephesus.

22 And when he had landed at Caesarea, and gone up, and saluted the church, he went down to Antioch.

23 And after he had spent some time there, he departed, and went over all the country of Galatia and Phrygia in order, strengthening all the disciples.

24 And a certain Jew named Apollos, born at Alexandria, an eloquent man, and mighty in the scriptures, came to Ephesus.

25 This man was instructed in the way of the Lord; and being fervent in the spirit, he spake and taught diligently the things of the Lord, knowing only the baptism of John.

26 And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue: whom when Aquila and Priscilla had heard, they took him unto them, and expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly.

27 And when he was disposed to pass into Achaia, the brethren wrote, exhorting the disciples to receive him: who, when he was come, helped them much which had believed through grace:

28 For he mightily convinced the Jews, and that publickly, shewing by the scriptures that Jesus was Christ.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul comes to Corinth, works as a tentmaker with Aquila and Priscilla, and preaches in the synagogue until opposition drives him to the Gentiles next door, where many Corinthians believe. Gallio, the Roman deputy, dismisses the Jews' charge as a question of their own law, Paul sails for Syria, and Apollos, eloquent in the Scriptures but knowing only John's baptism, is instructed more perfectly by Aquila and Priscilla at Ephesus. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. And found a certain Jew named Aquila, born in Pontus, lately come from Italy, with his wife Priscilla, because that Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome, and came unto them. Henry says the love of Christ is the best bond of the saints, and the communings of the saints with each other sweeten labour, contempt, and even persecution, and Paul's coming unto Aquila and Priscilla begins that communion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Because he was of the same craft, he abode with them, and wrought: for by their occupation they were tentmakers. Henry says though Paul was entitled to support from the churches he planted, and from the people to whom he preached, yet he worked at his calling, for an honest trade, by which a man may get his bread, is not to be looked upon with contempt by any, and it was the custom of the Jews to bring up their children to some trade, though they gave them learning or estates, and Paul was careful to prevent prejudices, even the most unreasonable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. He reasoned in the synagogue every sabbath, and persuaded the Jews and the Greeks. Henry's steady work at Corinth begins here: alongside his trade, Paul reasoned in the synagogue every sabbath and persuaded Jews and Greeks, before the opposition of the next verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. When Silas and Timotheus were come from Macedonia, Paul was pressed in the spirit, and testified to the Jews that Jesus was Christ. Henry's pressed testimony is the intensifying of that synagogue work at his companions' coming: pressed in the spirit, Paul testifies that Jesus was Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. When they opposed themselves, and blasphemed, he shook his raiment, and said unto them, Your blood be upon your own heads; I am clean: from henceforth I will go unto the Gentiles. Henry says most of the Jews persisted in contradicting the gospel of Christ, and blasphemed, for they would not believe themselves, and did all they could to keep others from believing, and Paul hereupon left them, though he did not give over his work, for though Israel be not gathered, Christ and his gospel shall be glorious, and the Jews could not complain, for they had the first offer, and when some oppose the gospel, we must turn to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He departed thence, and entered into a certain man's house, named Justus, one that worshipped God, and whose house joined hard to the synagogue. Henry's turning to others is this house: Paul departs from the synagogue opposition and enters Justus's house, joining hard to the synagogue, and does not give over his work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Crispus, the chief ruler of the synagogue, believed on the Lord with all his house, and many of the Corinthians hearing believed, and were baptized. Henry says grief that many persist in unbelief should not prevent gratitude for the conversion of some to Christ, and Crispus with all his house, and many Corinthians, are those some. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Then spake the Lord to Paul in the night by a vision, Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace. Henry's encouragement is that the Lord knows those that are his, yea, and those that shall be his, for it is by his work upon them that they become his, and the night vision bids Paul speak and not hold his peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee: for I have much people in this city. Henry says let us not despair concerning any place, when even in wicked Corinth Christ had much people, and he will gather in his chosen flock from the places where they are scattered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He continued there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them. Henry says thus encouraged, the apostle continued at Corinth, and a numerous and flourishing church grew up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. When Gallio was the deputy of Achaia, the Jews made insurrection with one accord against Paul, and brought him to the judgment seat. Henry's Gallio scene begins with the complaint: Paul was about to show that he did not teach men to worship God contrary to law, but the judge would not allow the Jews to complain to him of what was not within his office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Saying, This fellow persuadeth men to worship God contrary to the law. Henry's complaint is the contrary-to-law charge, which Paul was about to answer by showing he did not teach men to worship God contrary to law, but Gallio would not allow the complaint as within his office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. When Paul was now about to open his mouth, Gallio said unto the Jews, If it were a matter of wrong or wicked lewdness, O ye Jews, reason would that I should bear with you. Henry says it was right in Gallio that he left the Jews to themselves in matters relating to their religion, but yet would not let them, under pretence of that, persecute another, and his distinction between wrong or wicked lewdness and religious questions is that right refusal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. But if it be a question of words and names, and of your law, look ye to it; for I will be no judge of such matters. Henry says it was wrong to speak slightly of a law and religion which he might have known to be of God, and which he ought to have acquainted himself with, for in what way God is to be worshipped, whether Jesus be the Messiah, and whether the gospel be a divine revelation, are not questions of words and names, but questions of vast importance, and Gallio spoke as if he boasted of his ignorance of the Scriptures, as if the law of God was beneath his notice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He drave them from the judgment seat. Henry's refusal is the driving: Gallio left the Jews to themselves in matters of their religion, and would not let them, under pretence of that, persecute another, so he drave them from the judgment seat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Then all the Greeks took Sosthenes, the chief ruler of the synagogue, and beat him before the judgment seat. And Gallio cared for none of these things. Henry says if Gallio cared not for the affronts of bad men, it was commendable, but if he concerned not himself for the abuses done to good men, his indifference was carried too far, and those who see and hear of the sufferings of God's people, and have no feeling with them, or care for them, who do not pity and pray for them, are of the same spirit as Gallio, who cared for none of these things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Paul after this tarried there yet a good while, and then took his leave of the brethren, and sailed thence into Syria, and with him Priscilla and Aquila, having shorn his head in Cenchrea, for he had a vow. Henry's providence rule governs the leave-taking: while Paul found he laboured not in vain, he continued labouring, and our times are in God's hand, we purpose, but he disposes, therefore we must make all promises with submission to the will of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry's continuing labour is the Ephesus reasoning: while Paul found he laboured not in vain, he continued labouring, and at Ephesus he reasons with the Jews in the synagogue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. But bade them farewell, saying, I must by all means keep this feast that cometh in Jerusalem: but I will return again unto you, if God will. And he sailed from Ephesus. Henry's submission is the farewell's condition: we must make all promises with submission to the will of God, not only if providence permits, but if God does not otherwise direct our motions, and Paul's return again, if God will, is that submission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. When he had landed at Caesarea, and gone up, and saluted the church, he went down to Antioch. Henry says a very good refreshment it is to a faithful minister to have for awhile the society of his brethren, and Paul's going up and saluting the church at Caesarea, then to Antioch, is that refreshment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. After he had spent some time there, he departed, and went over all the country of Galatia and Phrygia in order, strengthening all the disciples. Henry says disciples are compassed about with infirmity, and ministers must do what they can to strengthen them, by directing them to Christ, who is their Strength, and Paul's going over Galatia and Phrygia in order, strengthening all the disciples, is that work, with plans formed as most proper but relying on the Lord to bring them to pass if he sees good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. A certain Jew named Apollos, born at Alexandria, an eloquent man, and mighty in the scriptures, came to Ephesus. Henry's complete man of God is Apollos: a lively, affectionate preacher, fervent in spirit, full of zeal for the glory of God and the salvation of precious souls, thoroughly furnished for his work, and his eloquence and might in the Scriptures are that furnishing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. This man was instructed in the way of the Lord; and being fervent in the spirit, he spake and taught diligently the things of the Lord, knowing only the baptism of John. Henry says Apollos taught in the gospel of Christ, as far as John's ministry would carry him, and no further, and though he had not the miraculous gifts of the Spirit, as the apostles, he made use of the gifts he had, for the dispensation of the Spirit, whatever the measure of it may be, is given to every man to profit withal, and he was fervent in spirit.   • Verse 26. He began to speak boldly in the synagogue: whom when Aquila and Priscilla had heard, they took him unto them, and expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly. Henry says Aquila and Priscilla encouraged his ministry by attendance upon it, and did not despise Apollos themselves, or undervalue him to others, but considered the disadvantages he had laboured under, and having themselves got knowledge in the truths of the gospel by their long intercourse with Paul, they told what they knew to him, for young scholars may gain a great deal by converse with old Christians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. When he was disposed to pass into Achaia, the brethren wrote, exhorting the disciples to receive him: who, when he was come, helped them much which had believed through grace. Henry says those who do believe through grace yet still need help, for as long as they are in this world, there are remainders of unbelief, and something lacking in their faith to be perfected, and the work of faith to be fulfilled, and Apollos, received by the brethren's letter, helped much those who had believed through grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. For he mightily convinced the Jews, and that publickly, shewing by the scriptures that Jesus was Christ. Henry says if the Jews were convinced that Jesus is Christ, even their own law would teach them to hear him, and the business of ministers is to preach Christ, not only to preach the truth, but to prove and defend it, with meekness, yet with power, and Apollos mightily convincing the Jews publicly by the Scriptures is that business. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
