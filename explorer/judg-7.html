Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Jerubbaal, who is Gideon, and all the people that were with him, rose up early, and pitched beside the well of Harod: so that the host of the Midianites were on the north side of them, by the hill of Moreh, in the valley.

2 And the LORD said unto Gideon, The people that are with thee are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hands, lest Israel vaunt themselves against me, saying, Mine own hand hath saved me.

3 Now therefore go to, proclaim in the ears of the people, saying, Whosoever is fearful and afraid, let him return and depart early from mount Gilead. And there returned of the people twenty and two thousand; and there remained ten thousand.

4 And the LORD said unto Gideon, The people are yet too many; bring them down unto the water, and I will try them for thee there: and it shall be, that of whom I say unto thee, This shall go with thee, the same shall go with thee; and of whomsoever I say unto thee, This shall not go with thee, the same shall not go.

5 So he brought down the people unto the water: and the LORD said unto Gideon, Every one that lappeth of the water with his tongue, as a dog lappeth, him shalt thou set by himself; likewise every one that boweth down upon his knees to drink.

6 And the number of them that lapped, putting their hand to their mouth, were three hundred men: but all the rest of the people bowed down upon their knees to drink water.

7 And the LORD said unto Gideon, By the three hundred men that lapped will I save you, and deliver the Midianites into thine hand: and let all the other people go every man unto his place.

8 So the people took victuals in their hand, and their trumpets: and he sent all the rest of Israel every man unto his tent, and retained those three hundred men: and the host of Midian was beneath him in the valley.

9 And it came to pass the same night, that the LORD said unto him, Arise, get thee down unto the host; for I have delivered it into thine hand.

10 But if thou fear to go down, go thou with Phurah thy servant down to the host:

11 And thou shalt hear what they say; and afterward shall thine hands be strengthened to go down unto the host. Then went he down with Phurah his servant unto the outside of the armed men that were in the host.

12 And the Midianites and the Amalekites and all the children of the east lay along in the valley like grasshoppers for multitude; and their camels were without number, as the sand by the sea side for multitude.

13 And when Gideon was come, behold, there was a man that told a dream unto his fellow, and said, Behold, I dreamed a dream, and, lo, a cake of barley bread tumbled into the host of Midian, and came unto a tent, and smote it that it fell, and overturned it, that the tent lay along.

14 And his fellow answered and said, This is nothing else save the sword of Gideon the son of Joash, a man of Israel: for into his hand hath God delivered Midian, and all the host.

15 And it was so, when Gideon heard the telling of the dream, and the interpretation thereof, that he worshipped, and returned into the host of Israel, and said, Arise; for the LORD hath delivered into your hand the host of Midian.

16 And he divided the three hundred men into three companies, and he put a trumpet in every man’s hand, with empty pitchers, and lamps within the pitchers.

17 And he said unto them, Look on me, and do likewise: and, behold, when I come to the outside of the camp, it shall be that, as I do, so shall ye do.

18 When I blow with a trumpet, I and all that are with me, then blow ye the trumpets also on every side of all the camp, and say, The sword of the LORD, and of Gideon.

19 So Gideon, and the hundred men that were with him, came unto the outside of the camp in the beginning of the middle watch; and they had but newly set the watch: and they blew the trumpets, and brake the pitchers that were in their hands.

20 And the three companies blew the trumpets, and brake the pitchers, and held the lamps in their left hands, and the trumpets in their right hands to blow withal: and they cried, The sword of the LORD, and of Gideon.

21 And they stood every man in his place round about the camp; and all the host ran, and cried, and fled.

22 And the three hundred blew the trumpets, and the LORD set every man’s sword against his fellow, even throughout all the host: and the host fled to Bethshittah in Zererath, and to the border of Abelmeholah, unto Tabbath.

23 And the men of Israel gathered themselves together out of Naphtali, and out of Asher, and out of all Manasseh, and pursued after the Midianites.

24 And Gideon sent messengers throughout all mount Ephraim, saying, come down against the Midianites, and take before them the waters unto Bethbarah and Jordan. Then all the men of Ephraim gathered themselves together, and took the waters unto Bethbarah and Jordan.

25 And they took two princes of the Midianites, Oreb and Zeeb; and they slew Oreb upon the rock Oreb, and Zeeb they slew at the winepress of Zeeb, and pursued Midian, and brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon on the other side Jordan.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Gideon's army is too many, lest Israel claim its own hand saved it: the fearful go home, twenty-two thousand of them, and a trial at the water leaves only three hundred who lap. That night Gideon overhears a Midianite dream of a barley cake overturning a tent, interpreted at once as Gideon's sword, and attacks with trumpets, empty pitchers and lamps in the dark; Midian turns its swords on itself and flees, and Ephraim, called late, takes the princes Oreb and Zeeb at the fords. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Jerubbaal, that is Gideon, rose early with all his people and pitched beside the well of Harod, while Midian lay north by the hill of Moreh, the armies now face to face. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The Lord said Gideon's people were too many, lest Israel vaunt itself and say its own hand had saved it, and Henry notes that God provides that the praise of victory may be wholly his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The fearful were proclaimed free to return, and twenty-two thousand went home, leaving ten thousand, so that the army was purified by the departure of the faint-hearted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Even ten thousand were too many, and God ordered a second trial at the water, since he chooses instruments not only well affected but zealously affected in a good thing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The test divided those who lapped water with their tongues as a dog laps from those who bowed on their knees to drink, a small outward act made the sign of readiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Three hundred lapped, putting their hands to their mouths, while the rest bowed down, and the few were set apart from the many by that very test. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. By the three hundred God promised to save Israel, and the rest were sent home, and Henry notes that a society made fewer in numbers may gain in purity and expect an increased blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The three hundred took victuals and trumpets, the rest were sent to their tents, and Gideon retained only the tested company with the host below him in the valley. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. That night God told Gideon to go down against the host, for it was delivered into his hand, giving the promise before the battle rather than after it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. If Gideon was afraid, he might go down with his servant Phurah to the host, and God thus met fear with a permitted reconnaissance instead of rebuking it outright. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He was to hear what the Midianites said, and then his hands would be strengthened, so that even the enemy's own talk would become encouragement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Midian, Amalek, and the east lay in the valley like grasshoppers for multitude, with camels beyond number, the same innumerable host Gideon had feared, now seen at close quarters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Gideon overheard a man telling his fellow a dream: a cake of barley bread tumbled into the Midianite camp and overturned a tent, a lowly thing undoing a great one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The fellow interpreted it at once as the sword of Gideon, into whose hand God had delivered Midian, and Henry notes that the name of Gideon had already filled Midian with terror. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Hearing dream and interpretation, Gideon worshipped, returned to his camp, and cried, Arise, for the Lord hath delivered the host into your hand, giving God the praise for what encouraged his faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Gideon divided the three hundred into three companies, giving each man a trumpet, an empty pitcher, and a lamp within the pitcher, weapons of noise and light rather than of iron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. He told them to look on him and do likewise, coming to the outside of the camp and acting exactly as he acted, so that one example would govern three hundred men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. When Gideon blew and his company with him, the rest were to blow and cry, The sword of the Lord, and of Gideon, joining God's name to his servant's in the battle cry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. At the beginning of the middle watch, just as the watch was set, Gideon and his hundred blew and brake their pitchers, timing the alarm for the changing of the guard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. All three companies blew, brake their pitchers, held lamps in their left hands and trumpets in their right, and cried the sword of the Lord and of Gideon, and Henry sees in this the gospel's conquest: light out of earthen vessels, a sword in the mouth rather than the hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Every man stood in his place around the camp while the whole host ran, cried, and fled, so that steadiness on Israel's side met panic on Midian's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The three hundred blew again, and the Lord set every man's sword against his fellow, so that the host fled to Beth-shittah and beyond, and God often makes the church's enemies instruments to destroy one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The men of Naphtali, Asher, and all Manasseh gathered and pursued Midian, the tribes that had been sent home or left uncalled now joining the pursuit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Gideon sent messengers through Ephraim to take the waters as far as Beth-barah and Jordan, cutting off the fugitives' escape across the river. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Ephraim took Oreb and Zeeb, the two princes of Midian, and brought their heads to Gideon beyond Jordan, and Henry wishes all would likewise do readily what help is needed when another calls for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
