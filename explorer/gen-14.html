Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass in the days of Amraphel king of Shinar, Arioch king of Ellasar, Chedorlaomer king of Elam, and Tidal king of nations;

2 That these made war with Bera king of Sodom, and with Birsha king of Gomorrah, Shinab king of Admah, and Shemeber king of Zeboiim, and the king of Bela, which is Zoar.

3 All these were joined together in the vale of Siddim, which is the salt sea.

4 Twelve years they served Chedorlaomer, and in the thirteenth year they rebelled.

5 And in the fourteenth year came Chedorlaomer, and the kings that were with him, and smote the Rephaims in Ashteroth Karnaim, and the Zuzims in Ham, and the Emins in Shaveh Kiriathaim,

6 And the Horites in their mount Seir, unto Elparan, which is by the wilderness.

7 And they returned, and came to Enmishpat, which is Kadesh, and smote all the country of the Amalekites, and also the Amorites, that dwelt in Hazezontamar.

8 And there went out the king of Sodom, and the king of Gomorrah, and the king of Admah, and the king of Zeboiim, and the king of Bela (the same is Zoar;) and they joined battle with them in the vale of Siddim;

9 With Chedorlaomer the king of Elam, and with Tidal king of nations, and Amraphel king of Shinar, and Arioch king of Ellasar; four kings with five.

10 And the vale of Siddim was full of slimepits; and the kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fled, and fell there; and they that remained fled to the mountain.

11 And they took all the goods of Sodom and Gomorrah, and all their victuals, and went their way.

12 And they took Lot, Abram’s brother’s son, who dwelt in Sodom, and his goods, and departed.

13 And there came one that had escaped, and told Abram the Hebrew; for he dwelt in the plain of Mamre the Amorite, brother of Eshcol, and brother of Aner: and these were confederate with Abram.

14 And when Abram heard that his brother was taken captive, he armed his trained servants, born in his own house, three hundred and eighteen, and pursued them unto Dan.

15 And he divided himself against them, he and his servants, by night, and smote them, and pursued them unto Hobah, which is on the left hand of Damascus.

16 And he brought back all the goods, and also brought again his brother Lot, and his goods, and the women also, and the people.

17 And the king of Sodom went out to meet him after his return from the slaughter of Chedorlaomer, and of the kings that were with him, at the valley of Shaveh, which is the king’s dale.

18 And Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine: and he was the priest of the most high God.

19 And he blessed him, and said, Blessed be Abram of the most high God, possessor of heaven and earth:

20 And blessed be the most high God, which hath delivered thine enemies into thy hand. And he gave him tithes of all.

21 And the king of Sodom said unto Abram, Give me the persons, and take the goods to thyself.

22 And Abram said to the king of Sodom, I have lift up mine hand unto the LORD, the most high God, the possessor of heaven and earth,

23 That I will not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet, and that I will not take any thing that is thine, lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich:

24 Save only that which the young men have eaten, and the portion of the men which went with me, Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre; let them take their portion.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Four kings make war on five, take Sodom's spoil and carry off Lot, and Abram pursues with his household, defeats them and recovers Lot and the goods. On his return Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, blesses him and receives a tenth, while Abram refuses any reward from the king of Sodom. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Four eastern kings under Chedorlaomer made war on five kings of the plain, a clash of small princes of the kind common in that age, in which Lot's home would soon be caught. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The war set the five kings of Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, Zeboiim, and Bela against the four eastern kings, a clash of small princes of the kind common in that age. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. All these kings joined forces in the valley of Siddim, the low plain later covered by the salt sea, choosing it as the ground for their battle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The cities of the plain had served Chedorlaomer for twelve years and then rebelled in the thirteenth, so this war was a overlord coming to reclaim rebellious subjects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. In the fourteenth year Chedorlaomer and his allies marched through the region, defeating the Rephaim, Zuzim, Emim, and Horites, clearing all opposition on their way to the plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The campaign reached to the edge of the desert, showing the wide sweep of the eastern kings' power before they turned on Sodom and its allies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Turning back, the invaders struck En-mishpat and the Amalekites and Amorites, so that by the time they reached the plain they had already beaten every people in their path. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The five kings went out to meet them in the valley of Siddim, choosing battle in the open rather than waiting behind their city walls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Four kings fought against five, and the smaller force won, a reminder that numbers alone do not decide battles when discipline and surprise are on the other side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fled and fell into the tar pits, while the rest escaped to the mountains, leaving their cities open to plunder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The victors took all the goods and food of Sodom and Gomorrah and left, so the cities were stripped even before their later destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They also carried off Lot, Abram's nephew, with his goods, which turned a distant war into Abram's own business and called him to act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. An escapee brought the news to Abram, who lived by Mamre's oaks among Amorite allies, and the report named him Abram the Hebrew, marking him out as a man apart from the peoples around him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. On hearing that his kinsman was captive, Abram armed 318 trained men born in his own house and pursued as far as Dan, showing a household strong enough to field its own force. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Abram divided his force and attacked by night, a stratagem JFB notes as typical of such warfare: a sudden rush from several sides in the dark throws an unsuspecting camp into panic. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Abram recovered all the goods and brought back Lot with his goods, the women, and the people, so his rescue was complete and no one was left behind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. On his return the king of Sodom came out to meet Abram in the King's Valley, to honour the deliverer and, as the next verses show, to bargain over the spoils. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, brought out bread and wine, refreshing the weary victors and marking the meeting as sacred, not merely political. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Melchizedek blessed Abram in the name of God Most High, possessor of heaven and earth, ascribing the victory to God rather than to Abram's sword. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He blessed God for delivering Abram's enemies into his hand, and Abram gave him a tenth of all, honouring the priest as God's representative and refusing to keep the whole spoil for himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The king of Sodom then proposed a bargain: the people to him, the goods to Abram, an offer that looked generous but would have left Abram enriched by Sodom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Abram answered with a lifted hand, a solemn oath, to God Most High, showing that his refusal was a matter of conscience before God, not mere bargaining. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Abram would take nothing, not even a thread or a sandal strap, lest Sodom's king ever claim to have made him rich. His reward, Henry notes, must come from God alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Abram made only two exceptions: what his men had already eaten, and the share due to his allies Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre, whose rights he would not sign away on their behalf. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • a tenth. Abram gave Melchizedek a tenth of all, honouring the priest as God's representative. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 14:20, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
