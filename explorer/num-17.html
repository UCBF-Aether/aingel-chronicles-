The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, and take of every one of them a rod according to the house of their fathers, of all their princes according to the house of their fathers twelve rods: write thou every man’s name upon his rod.

3 And thou shalt write Aaron’s name upon the rod of Levi: for one rod shall be for the head of the house of their fathers.

4 And thou shalt lay them up in the tabernacle of the congregation before the testimony, where I will meet with you.

5 And it shall come to pass, that the man’s rod, whom I shall choose, shall blossom: and I will make to cease from me the murmurings of the children of Israel, whereby they murmur against you.

6 And Moses spake unto the children of Israel, and every one of their princes gave him a rod apiece, for each prince one, according to their fathers’ houses, even twelve rods: and the rod of Aaron was among their rods.

7 And Moses laid up the rods before the LORD in the tabernacle of witness.

8 And it came to pass, that on the morrow Moses went into the tabernacle of witness; and, behold, the rod of Aaron for the house of Levi was budded, and brought forth buds, and bloomed blossoms, and yielded almonds.

9 And Moses brought out all the rods from before the LORD unto all the children of Israel: and they looked, and took every man his rod.

10 And the LORD said unto Moses, Bring Aaron’s rod again before the testimony, to be kept for a token against the rebels; and thou shalt quite take away their murmurings from me, that they die not.

11 And Moses did so: as the LORD commanded him, so did he.

12 And the children of Israel spake unto Moses, saying, Behold, we die, we perish, we all perish.

13 Whosoever cometh any thing near unto the tabernacle of the LORD shall die: shall we be consumed with dying?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter settles the priesthood dispute by a sign. Twelve rods, one for each tribe's prince, are laid up before the LORD in the tabernacle of witness, with Aaron's rod for the house of Levi. In the morning Aaron's rod has budded, blossomed, and yielded almonds, while the others are unchanged. The rod is kept before the testimony as a token against the rebels, that their murmurings may cease, and the people cry out in fear that everyone who comes near the tabernacle will die. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. Speak to Israel, and take of every one of them a rod according to the house of their fathers, of all their princes, twelve rods, writing every man's name upon his rod. JFB explains the princes were chosen because, as oldest sons and tribal heads, they could advance the best claims to a shared priesthood, and the name-inscription was a practice borrowed from the Egyptians, giving every tribe a direct interest in the settlement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Write Aaron's name upon the rod of Levi, for one rod shall be for the head of the house of their fathers. JFB notes Aaron's name rather than Levi's was used, since Levi's name would have opened controversy among the Levites, and one rod for Aaron determined him as head of the tribe and of the priestly branch within it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Lay them up in the tabernacle of the congregation before the testimony, where I will meet with you. JFB notes the rods lay close to the ark, where a divine token was promised that would for all time terminate the dispute about the priesthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. It shall come to pass that the man's rod whom I shall choose shall blossom, and I will make to cease from me the murmurings of Israel, whereby they murmur against you. Henry calls this an instance of grace: having worked divers miracles to punish sin, God would work one more to prevent it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Moses spoke to Israel, and every prince gave him a rod apiece, for each prince one, twelve rods, and the rod of Aaron was among their rods. JFB notes the rods were dry sticks, probably old wands transmitted from head to head of each family, and Aaron's was either one of the twelve or, as many suppose, a thirteenth in the midst. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Moses laid up the rods before the Lord in the tabernacle of witness. Moses did not object that the matter was already settled, Henry notes, nor undertake to determine it himself, but left the case before the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. On the morrow Moses went into the tabernacle of witness, and behold, the rod of Aaron for the house of Levi was budded, and brought forth buds, and bloomed blossoms, and yielded almonds. JFB notes, from Josephus, that it was an almond stick bearing fruit in three stages at once, and Henry adds that while all other rods remained as they were, Aaron's became a living branch, with fruitfulness as the best evidence of a divine call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Moses brought out all the rods from before the Lord to all Israel, and they looked upon them, and took every man his rod. The inspection was public: every man saw his own dry rod beside Aaron's living one, and took it back as his own witness to the decision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Bring Aaron's rod again before the testimony, to be kept for a token against the rebels, to quite take away their murmurings from me, that they die not. JFB warns that if, after all admonitions, judgments and miracles, the people still rebelled, they would certainly pay the penalty by death, and Henry adds God's design in all his providences and memorials is to take away sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Moses did so: as the Lord commanded him, so did he. The obedience is recorded without comment, closing the controversy exactly as God ordered it: the rod returned to the testimony, and the case was left there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Israel spoke to Moses, saying, behold, we die, we perish, we all perish! JFB reads this as an exclamation of fear, from remembrance of former judgments and apprehension of future relapses into murmuring, while Henry hears a repining people quarrelling with judgments their own pride and obstinacy had brought on themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Whosoever comes anything near to the tabernacle of the Lord shall die: shall we be consumed with dying? JFB explains nearer than he ought to be, an error into which many may fall, and asks whether stern justice overtakes every slight offence, though some regard the cry as a symptom of new discontent rather than reverential submission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
