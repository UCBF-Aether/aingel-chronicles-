The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The vision of Isaiah the son of Amoz, which he saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah.

2 Hear, O heavens, and give ear, O earth: for the LORD hath spoken, I have nourished and brought up children, and they have rebelled against me.

3 The ox knoweth his owner, and the ass his master’s crib: but Israel doth not know, my people doth not consider.

4 Ah sinful nation, a people laden with iniquity, a seed of evildoers, children that are corrupters: they have forsaken the LORD, they have provoked the Holy One of Israel unto anger, they are gone away backward.

5 Why should ye be stricken any more? ye will revolt more and more: the whole head is sick, and the whole heart faint.

6 From the sole of the foot even unto the head there is no soundness in it; but wounds, and bruises, and putrifying sores: they have not been closed, neither bound up, neither mollified with ointment.

7 Your country is desolate, your cities are burned with fire: your land, strangers devour it in your presence, and it is desolate, as overthrown by strangers.

8 And the daughter of Zion is left as a cottage in a vineyard, as a lodge in a garden of cucumbers, as a besieged city.

9 Except the LORD of hosts had left unto us a very small remnant, we should have been as Sodom, and we should have been like unto Gomorrah.

10 Hear the word of the LORD, ye rulers of Sodom; give ear unto the law of our God, ye people of Gomorrah.

11 To what purpose is the multitude of your sacrifices unto me? saith the LORD: I am full of the burnt offerings of rams, and the fat of fed beasts; and I delight not in the blood of bullocks, or of lambs, or of he goats.

12 When ye come to appear before me, who hath required this at your hand, to tread my courts?

13 Bring no more vain oblations; incense is an abomination unto me; the new moons and sabbaths, the calling of assemblies, I cannot away with; it is iniquity, even the solemn meeting.

14 Your new moons and your appointed feasts my soul hateth: they are a trouble unto me; I am weary to bear them.

15 And when ye spread forth your hands, I will hide mine eyes from you: yea, when ye make many prayers, I will not hear: your hands are full of blood.

16 Wash you, make you clean; put away the evil of your doings from before mine eyes; cease to do evil;

17 Learn to do well; seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow.

18 Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool.

19 If ye be willing and obedient, ye shall eat the good of the land:

20 But if ye refuse and rebel, ye shall be devoured with the sword: for the mouth of the LORD hath spoken it.

21 How is the faithful city become an harlot! it was full of judgment; righteousness lodged in it; but now murderers.

22 Thy silver is become dross, thy wine mixed with water:

23 Thy princes are rebellious, and companions of thieves: every one loveth gifts, and followeth after rewards: they judge not the fatherless, neither doth the cause of the widow come unto them.

24 Therefore saith the LORD, the LORD of hosts, the mighty One of Israel, Ah, I will ease me of mine adversaries, and avenge me of mine enemies:

25 And I will turn my hand upon thee, and purely purge away thy dross, and take away all thy tin:

26 And I will restore thy judges as at the first, and thy counsellors as at the beginning: afterward thou shalt be called, The city of righteousness, the faithful city.

27 Zion shall be redeemed with judgment, and her converts with righteousness.

28 And the destruction of the transgressors and of the sinners shall be together, and they that forsake the LORD shall be consumed.

29 For they shall be ashamed of the oaks which ye have desired, and ye shall be confounded for the gardens that ye have chosen.

30 For ye shall be as an oak whose leaf fadeth, and as a garden that hath no water.

31 And the strong shall be as tow, and the maker of it as a spark, and they shall both burn together, and none shall quench them.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This opening chapter is a courtroom charge against Judah and Jerusalem: a people raised by God have rebelled, their land is ravaged, and their lavish sacrifices mean nothing while their hands are full of blood and the fatherless and widow go undefended. God answers with a call to wash, do justice, and reason together — scarlet sins made white as snow for the obedient — and with a promise to purge the dross from Zion and restore her as the faithful city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The heading names Isaiah, whose name means "The Lord shall save," and sets his vision in Judah and Jerusalem under Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah. JFB treats this title as the program of the whole book, with Jerusalem named as the temple city and centre of the theocracy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Heaven and earth are called as witnesses, as in Moses' song, because prophecy rests on the law as its charter. God's complaint is that children he raised to peculiar privilege have rebelled against him as sons and as subjects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Even an ox knows its owner and an ass its master's crib, yet Israel does not know or consider the Lord who feeds it. JFB reads the crib as God's word and ordinances, which his people ignore despite the provision made for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Sin is described as a load, and the nation as a seed meant to be holy that has become evildoers and corrupters of others. Their offence is sharpened because it is against the Holy One of Israel, and they have gone backward, estranged from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The question is on what part they can still be struck, since the whole body already bears the marks of blows. JFB notes the image covers the universality of their punishment, while sin itself, the disease of head and heart, becomes its own punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. From the lowest to the highest there is no soundness, only wounds that have never been closed, bound up, or softened with ointment. Henry compares the nation to a sick body from sole to head, with no good principle or religion left as the health of the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The land lies desolate and its cities burned, with strangers devouring it before their eyes while they cannot prevent it. This desolation recalls earlier ravages by Syria and Israel, and matches the fire-judgments Amos describes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Jerusalem alone is left, like a cottage in a vineyard or a lodge in a cucumber field — a temporary shelter, not a secure home. JFB reads "left" as a remnant preserved from the desolation all around, rather than a city still besieged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Only the Lord of hosts, God of the angelic and starry hosts, has preserved a very small remnant; without him Jerusalem would have become like Sodom and Gomorrah. Henry stresses that it is of the Lord's mercies alone that they are not consumed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The rulers and people are addressed as rulers of Sodom and people of Gomorrah, and told to hear the word and law of the Lord. The spiritual likeness to Sodom marks how far judgment by fire has already pointed to their condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. God does not disparage sacrifice in itself, but sacrifice without obedience of heart and life; he is full to satiety of burnt offerings brought while sin continues. JFB sees here a foreshadowing of the one real sacrifice that would supersede all the shadowy ones. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Coming to appear before God in his courts is no service to him when the worshipper comes in hypocrisy. God did require attendance at the temple, but not in this spirit, as if the worshipper were doing God a favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Vain oblations, incense, new moons, and sabbaths are an abomination when joined to iniquity; God says he cannot bear the solemn meeting that is yoked to sin. Henry warns that the costliest devotions, without thorough reformation of heart and life, cannot be acceptable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. God is weary of the appointed feasts — sabbath, passover, pentecost, atonement, and tabernacles — because they are kept while the heart stays corrupt. The feasts were fixed by God himself, yet their observance had become a trouble to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. When hands spread in prayer are full of blood, God will hide his eyes and not hear, for all heinous sins and especially persecution of his servants stain the prayer. The prophets' task was to dispel the delusion that outward ritual alone could satisfy God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The command to wash and make clean is given so that the sinner, finding he cannot cleanse himself, cries to God to wash him. The cleansing must be before God's eyes, not mere outward reformation before men who cannot see the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. True religion is active: learn to do well, seek justice, relieve the oppressed, and vindicate the fatherless and the widow. JFB notes that magistrates in particular are called to seek justice instead of seeking bribes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. God deigns to reason the case with his people so that all may see the justice and love of his dealings. Though sins are scarlet, double-dyed and deep-fixed, pardoning mercy can make them white as snow and as wool, restored to original whiteness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Willingness and obedience are the condition of eating the good of the land, with temporal blessings prominent under the old covenant as suited to the childhood of the church. Repentance is presupposed before the promised whitening of verse 18 can be enjoyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Refusal and rebellion bring devouring by the sword, and the sentence stands because the mouth of the Lord has spoken it. Isaiah's prophecies rest on the law, and God does not alter his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The faithful city has become a harlot: righteousness once lodged in her, but now murderers — murderous oppressors — dwell there. Henry notes that neither holy nor royal cities are faithful to their trust if religion does not dwell in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Jerusalem's silver has become dross and its wine mixed with water: solid worth is debased and the living word diluted. The princes and people alike are degenerate, shining like silver while worthless within. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The princes are rebellious companions of thieves, loving gifts and following rewards, judging neither the fatherless nor the widow's cause. JFB observes that a nation's corruption begins with its rulers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The Lord, the mighty One of Israel, declares he will ease himself of his adversaries and avenge himself of his enemies. God's language condescends to human conceptions; his long-tried patience finds relief in at last punishing the guilty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. God will turn his hand upon Zion not in wrath but in grace, purging away dross purely, as alkali purifies silver. The dross is not sins in the abstract but the sinful persons — the degenerate princes — intermingled with the elect remnant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Judges and counsellors will be restored as at the first, and the city will again be called the city of righteousness, the faithful city. The restoration was partly typified after Babylon, but fully and antitypically under Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Zion shall be redeemed with judgment and her converts with righteousness, a redemption that is civil and moral in type and spiritual in its fulfilment by the price of Christ's blood. God's judgment and righteousness come first, and then become man's when converted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. The destruction of transgressors and sinners together will be a breaking into shivers, with those who forsake the Lord consumed. The prophets hasten forward to the final extinction of the ungodly, of which earlier judgments are types. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Those who desired the oaks and chose the gardens of idolatry will be ashamed of them, for groves were dedicated to idols and planted enclosures served idol worship. Henry warns that trusting in idols or in an arm of flesh ends in shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The idolaters will become like an oak whose leaf fades and a garden without water, withering by a law of necessary consequence. People become like the gods they worship and never rise above their level, and their sins become their own scourges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. The strong man will be as tow and his work as a spark, and both will burn together with none to quench them. The wicked ruler is at once the fuel and the cause of the fire, kindling the first spark himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • LORD of hosts. God of the angelic and starry hosts, as the study note on verse 9 puts it — the Lord commanding all the powers of heaven, not a local or limited god. Interpreted Study note on verse v9, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
