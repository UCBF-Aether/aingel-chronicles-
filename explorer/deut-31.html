The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Deuteronomy 31 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Moses went and spake these words unto all Israel.

2 And he said unto them, I am an hundred and twenty years old this day; I can no more go out and come in: also the LORD hath said unto me, Thou shalt not go over this Jordan.

3 The LORD thy God, he will go over before thee, and he will destroy these nations from before thee, and thou shalt possess them: and Joshua, he shall go over before thee, as the LORD hath said.

4 And the LORD shall do unto them as he did to Sihon and to Og, kings of the Amorites, and unto the land of them, whom he destroyed.

5 And the LORD shall give them up before your face, that ye may do unto them according unto all the commandments which I have commanded you.

6 Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them: for the LORD thy God, he it is that doth go with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.

7 And Moses called unto Joshua, and said unto him in the sight of all Israel, Be strong and of a good courage: for thou must go with this people unto the land which the LORD hath sworn unto their fathers to give them; and thou shalt cause them to inherit it.

8 And the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee; he will be with thee, he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee: fear not, neither be dismayed.

9 And Moses wrote this law, and delivered it unto the priests the sons of Levi, which bare the ark of the covenant of the LORD, and unto all the elders of Israel.

10 And Moses commanded them, saying, At the end of every seven years, in the solemnity of the year of release, in the feast of tabernacles,

11 When all Israel is come to appear before the LORD thy God in the place which he shall choose, thou shalt read this law before all Israel in their hearing.

12 Gather the people together, men and women, and children, and thy stranger that is within thy gates, that they may hear, and that they may learn, and fear the LORD your God, and observe to do all the words of this law:

13 And that their children, which have not known any thing, may hear, and learn to fear the LORD your God, as long as ye live in the land whither ye go over Jordan to possess it.

14 And the LORD said unto Moses, Behold, thy days approach that thou must die: call Joshua, and present yourselves in the tabernacle of the congregation, that I may give him a charge. And Moses and Joshua went, and presented themselves in the tabernacle of the congregation.

15 And the LORD appeared in the tabernacle in a pillar of a cloud: and the pillar of the cloud stood over the door of the tabernacle.

16 And the LORD said unto Moses, Behold, thou shalt sleep with thy fathers; and this people will rise up, and go a whoring after the gods of the strangers of the land, whither they go to be among them, and will forsake me, and break my covenant which I have made with them.

17 Then my anger shall be kindled against them in that day, and I will forsake them, and I will hide my face from them, and they shall be devoured, and many evils and troubles shall befall them; so that they will say in that day, Are not these evils come upon us, because our God is not among us?

18 And I will surely hide my face in that day for all the evils which they shall have wrought, in that they are turned unto other gods.

19 Now therefore write ye this song for you, and teach it the children of Israel: put it in their mouths, that this song may be a witness for me against the children of Israel.

20 For when I shall have brought them into the land which I sware unto their fathers, that floweth with milk and honey; and they shall have eaten and filled themselves, and waxen fat; then will they turn unto other gods, and serve them, and provoke me, and break my covenant.

21 And it shall come to pass, when many evils and troubles are befallen them, that this song shall testify against them as a witness; for it shall not be forgotten out of the mouths of their seed: for I know their imagination which they go about, even now, before I have brought them into the land which I sware.

22 Moses therefore wrote this song the same day, and taught it the children of Israel.

23 And he gave Joshua the son of Nun a charge, and said, Be strong and of a good courage: for thou shalt bring the children of Israel into the land which I sware unto them: and I will be with thee.

24 And it came to pass, when Moses had made an end of writing the words of this law in a book, until they were finished,

25 That Moses commanded the Levites, which bare the ark of the covenant of the LORD, saying,

26 Take this book of the law, and put it in the side of the ark of the covenant of the LORD your God, that it may be there for a witness against thee.

27 For I know thy rebellion, and thy stiff neck: behold, while I am yet alive with you this day, ye have been rebellious against the LORD; and how much more after my death?

28 Gather unto me all the elders of your tribes, and your officers, that I may speak these words in their ears, and call heaven and earth to record against them.

29 For I know that after my death ye will utterly corrupt yourselves, and turn aside from the way which I have commanded you; and evil will befall you in the latter days; because ye will do evil in the sight of the LORD, to provoke him to anger through the work of your hands.

30 And Moses spake in the ears of all the congregation of Israel the words of this song, until they were ended.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Moses, now a hundred and twenty and unable to lead further, commissions Joshua in the sight of all Israel — be strong and of good courage, for God goes with you — and writes the law down, to be read aloud to all Israel every seventh year at Tabernacles. He also gives Israel a song to be a witness against them when they turn away, and is told to go up the mountain and die, having seen the land from afar. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And Moses went and spoke these words to all Israel, returning to the assembly on what was probably the last and most important day of his rehearsal of the law, which may have extended over several successive days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And he said to them, I am a hundred and twenty years old this day; I can no more go out and come in; also the Lord has said to me, You shall not go over this Jordan; though neither his physical nor intellectual powers had decayed, he knew by special revelation that his superintendence of Israel was ending. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The leadership changes but the vanguard does not: God himself goes over before Israel to destroy the nations, and Joshua goes over before them only as the Lord has said, for the promise, not the man, leads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Past conquest is the pledge for the next: what God did to Sihon and Og and their land is the measure of what he will do to the nations ahead, so Israel faces Canaan with precedent, not hope alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Victory comes with a condition attached: the nations are given up before Israel so that Israel may deal with them according to all the commandments, for conquest is obedience, not licence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them, for the Lord your God, he it is who goes with you, and he will not fail you, nor forsake you; Moses exhorted them not to be intimidated, but to rest on the same divine power that had discomfited their first assailants east of Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The charge is given in public: Moses calls Joshua in the sight of all Israel, so the succession is witnessed by the whole nation and no rival claim can arise after Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Joshua hears the same promise Moses gave the people, now spoken to him alone: the Lord goes before him, stays with him, and will neither fail nor forsake, so courage rests on presence, not on ability. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And Moses wrote this law, and delivered it to the priests, sons of Levi, who bore the ark of the covenant of the Lord, and to all the elders of Israel; two copies were made, as in public contracts, one deposited with the public representatives, the priests and elders, for reference and preservation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. At the return of the sabbatic year, during the feast of tabernacles, the law was to be read publicly before women and children as well as men; JFB notes this periodical rehearsal both impressed divine truth in the sacred season and guaranteed the faithful transmission of the book to successive ages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Reading is hearing for the whole nation: when all Israel appears at the chosen place, the law is read in their hearing, so no Israelite's obedience rests on secondhand knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Gather the people together, men and women, and children, and your stranger within your gates, that they may hear, and learn, and fear the Lord, and observe to do all the words of this law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. And that their children, who have not known anything, may hear, and learn to fear the Lord your God, as long as you live in the land where you go over Jordan to possess it; the public periodical rehearsal, with women and children present, was a guarantee for the preservation and faithful transmission of the sacred book. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Moses' death is announced as approaching, not sudden: Joshua is called to the tabernacle so that the charge passes in God's presence, in the place of meeting, before Moses is gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And the Lord appeared in the tabernacle in a pillar of a cloud, and the pillar of the cloud stood over the door of the tabernacle; Joshua, already designated commander, was now confirmed by the visible symbols of God's presence and approval. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. And the Lord said to Moses, Behold, you shall sleep with your fathers, and this people will rise up, and go a whoring after the gods of the strangers of the land, where they go to be among them, and will forsake me, and break my covenant which I have made with them; Moses was distinctly apprised of Israel's coming infidelity through intercourse with Canaan's idolaters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Then my anger shall be kindled against them in that day, and I will forsake them, and I will hide my face from them, and they shall be devoured, and many evils and troubles shall befall them, so that they will say in that day, Are not these evils come upon us, because our God is not among us? The hiding of God's face announced the withdrawal of the favour of which the Shekinah was symbol and pledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The hiding is doubled, surely hide, and its cause is named: evils wrought by turning to other gods, so God's absence is presented as the consequence of Israel's turning, not as divine caprice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Now therefore write this song for you, and teach it the children of Israel; put it in their mouths, that this song may be a witness for me against the children of Israel; national songs take deep hold of memory, and this song, embodying the substance of Moses' addresses, was to be learnt in every age. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. For when I shall have brought them into the land which I swore to their fathers, flowing with milk and honey, and they shall have eaten and filled themselves, and waxen fat, then will they turn to other gods, and serve them, and provoke me, and break my covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The song is a witness that outlives memory: even before Israel enters the land, God knows the imagination they go about, and the unforgotten song will testify against them when evils befall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. JFB explains the song's purpose at v 19: national songs take deep hold of memory and stir a people's deepest feelings, so this song was to be learnt by Israel and taught to their children in every age, embodying God's favour to the nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The charge passes from Moses to God himself: he who gives Joshua his charge says, be strong, for you shall bring Israel into the sworn land, and I will be with you, so the same presence promised to Moses now rests on his successor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The writing delivered to the priests at v 9 here reaches its end as a finished book; JFB traces that same written law to its deposit beside the ark at v 26, kept for security and reverence as the nation's covenant record. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The command goes to the Levites who bear the ark: the law's keepers are those who carry the covenant chest itself, so custody and carriage belong together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Take this book of the law, and put it in the side of the ark of the covenant of the Lord your God, that it may be there for a witness against you; this second copy was deposited beside the ark for greater security and reverence, and is thought to be the copy found in Josiah's time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Moses names rebellion while still alive with them: if they rebelled under his eye, he asks how much more after his death, and the book beside the ark is left as witness precisely because he knows their neck. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Elders and officers are gathered as witnesses in their own right: Moses speaks in their ears and calls heaven and earth to record, so the song that follows is heard by representatives and by creation itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Corruption after Moses' death is foreknown, not merely feared: turning aside from the commanded way and provoking God by handmade work brings evil in the latter days, exactly as the song will have warned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The song is spoken whole, in the ears of all the congregation, until ended: nothing is held back from the assembly that must one day answer for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
