The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hosea 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Hear the word of the LORD, ye children of Israel: for the LORD hath a controversy with the inhabitants of the land, because there is no truth, nor mercy, nor knowledge of God in the land.

2 By swearing, and lying, and killing, and stealing, and committing adultery, they break out, and blood toucheth blood.

3 Therefore shall the land mourn, and every one that dwelleth therein shall languish, with the beasts of the field, and with the fowls of heaven; yea, the fishes of the sea also shall be taken away.

4 Yet let no man strive, nor reprove another: for thy people are as they that strive with the priest.

5 Therefore shalt thou fall in the day, and the prophet also shall fall with thee in the night, and I will destroy thy mother.

6 My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge: because thou hast rejected knowledge, I will also reject thee, that thou shalt be no priest to me: seeing thou hast forgotten the law of thy God, I will also forget thy children.

7 As they were increased, so they sinned against me: therefore will I change their glory into shame.

8 They eat up the sin of my people, and they set their heart on their iniquity.

9 And there shall be, like people, like priest: and I will punish them for their ways, and reward them their doings.

10 For they shall eat, and not have enough: they shall commit whoredom, and shall not increase: because they have left off to take heed to the LORD.

11 Whoredom and wine and new wine take away the heart.

12 My people ask counsel at their stocks, and their staff declareth unto them: for the spirit of whoredoms hath caused them to err, and they have gone a whoring from under their God.

13 They sacrifice upon the tops of the mountains, and burn incense upon the hills, under oaks and poplars and elms, because the shadow thereof is good: therefore your daughters shall commit whoredom, and your spouses shall commit adultery.

14 I will not punish your daughters when they commit whoredom, nor your spouses when they commit adultery: for themselves are separated with whores, and they sacrifice with harlots: therefore the people that doth not understand shall fall.

15 Though thou, Israel, play the harlot, yet let not Judah offend; and come not ye unto Gilgal, neither go ye up to Bethaven, nor swear, The LORD liveth.

16 For Israel slideth back as a backsliding heifer: now the LORD will feed them as a lamb in a large place.

17 Ephraim is joined to idols: let him alone.

18 Their drink is sour: they have committed whoredom continually: her rulers with shame do love, Give ye.

19 The wind hath bound her up in her wings, and they shall be ashamed because of their sacrifices.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The LORD brings a controversy against the land: there is no truth, mercy, or knowledge of God, only swearing, lying, killing, stealing, and adultery, until the land itself mourns. Priests and people are judged alike, the people are destroyed for lack of knowledge, and Judah is warned not to follow Israel to the corrupt shrines at Gilgal and Bethaven. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hear the word of the Lord, ye children of Israel, for the Lord has a controversy with the inhabitants of the land, because there is no truth, nor mercy, nor knowledge of God in the land, because the prophet opens a lawsuit, and the charge is the absence of the three things that make a people God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. By swearing, and lying, and killing, and stealing, and committing adultery, they break out, and blood touches blood, because the sins of the second table run together, and violence follows violence without interval. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Therefore shall the land mourn, and every one that dwells therein shall languish, with the beasts of the field, and with the fowls of heaven, yea, the fishes of the sea also shall be taken away, because the curse on man's sin reaches the whole creation under his dominion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Yet let no man strive, nor reprove another, for thy people are as they that strive with the priest, because the people are so hardened that reproof is useless, and they contend even against the priest who should teach them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Therefore shalt thou fall in the day, and the prophet also shall fall with thee in the night, and I will destroy thy mother, because priest and prophet fall together, and the mother, the nation itself, is destroyed with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge, because thou hast rejected knowledge, I will also reject thee, that thou shalt be no priest to me, seeing thou hast forgotten the law of thy God, I will also forget thy children, because the priests rejected the knowledge they were charged to keep, and the people perish with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. As they were increased, so they sinned against me, therefore will I change their glory into shame, because increase of numbers and wealth only increased sin, and the glory they boasted in will be turned to shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They eat up the sin of my people, and they set their heart on their iniquity, because the priests live off the sin offerings and therefore profit from the very sins they should have prevented. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And there shall be, like people, like priest, and I will punish them for their ways, and reward them their doings, because priest and people have become alike, and judgment falls on both without distinction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For they shall eat, and not have enough, and they shall commit whoredom, and shall not increase, because they have left off to take heed to the Lord, because appetite pursued apart from God never satisfies and never multiplies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Whoredom and wine and new wine take away the heart, because sensual indulgence, whether idolatrous or drunken, robs a man of understanding, which is why the nation cannot see its own ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. My people ask counsel at their stocks, and their staff declares unto them, for the spirit of whoredoms has caused them to err, and they have gone a whoring from under their God, because they consult wooden idols and divining rods instead of God, led astray by the very spirit of idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They sacrifice upon the tops of the mountains, and burn incense upon the hills, under oaks and poplars and elms, because the shadow thereof is good, therefore your daughters shall commit whoredom, and your spouses shall commit adultery, because the high places, chosen for their pleasant shade, become schools of both idolatry and immorality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. I will not punish your daughters when they commit whoredom, nor your spouses when they commit adultery, for themselves are separated with whores, and they sacrifice with harlots, therefore the people that does not understand shall fall, because the fathers' example excuses nothing, and a people without understanding falls by its own leaders' sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Though thou, Israel, play the harlot, yet let not Judah offend, and come not ye unto Gilgal, neither go ye up to Bethaven, nor swear, the Lord lives, because Judah is warned not to follow Israel's example nor to join the pilgrimages to the corrupt shrines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. For Israel slides back as a backsliding heifer, now the Lord will feed them as a lamb in a large place, because the nation, stubborn as a heifer that will not be led, will be left to wander in a wide and empty place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Ephraim is joined to idols, let him alone, because the attachment to idols has become so fixed that God gives the nation over to its choice, which is the heaviest judgment of all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Their drink is sour, they have committed whoredom continually, her rulers with shame do love, give ye, because even their drink is corrupted, and their rulers love the shameful exactions that feed their revels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The wind has bound her up in her wings, and they shall be ashamed because of their sacrifices, because idolatry, like wind, carries them away, and the sacrifices they trusted in will end in shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Ephraim. the leading tribe of the northern kingdom, used in the prophets for the northern kingdom of Israel itself, as distinct from Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Bethaven. Hosea's name for the corrupt shrine at Bethel, the calf-shrine whose glory departs when the calf is carried to Assyria. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
