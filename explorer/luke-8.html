The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass afterward, that he went throughout every city and village, preaching and shewing the glad tidings of the kingdom of God: and the twelve were with him,

2 And certain women, which had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities, Mary called Magdalene, out of whom went seven devils,

3 And Joanna the wife of Chuza Herod’s steward, and Susanna, and many others, which ministered unto him of their substance.

4 And when much people were gathered together, and were come to him out of every city, he spake by a parable:

5 A sower went out to sow his seed: and as he sowed, some fell by the way side; and it was trodden down, and the fowls of the air devoured it.

6 And some fell upon a rock; and as soon as it was sprung up, it withered away, because it lacked moisture.

7 And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprang up with it, and choked it.

8 And other fell on good ground, and sprang up, and bare fruit an hundredfold. And when he had said these things, he cried, He that hath ears to hear, let him hear.

9 And his disciples asked him, saying, What might this parable be?

10 And he said, Unto you it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of God: but to others in parables; that seeing they might not see, and hearing they might not understand.

11 Now the parable is this: The seed is the word of God.

12 Those by the way side are they that hear; then cometh the devil, and taketh away the word out of their hearts, lest they should believe and be saved.

13 They on the rock are they, which, when they hear, receive the word with joy; and these have no root, which for a while believe, and in time of temptation fall away.

14 And that which fell among thorns are they, which, when they have heard, go forth, and are choked with cares and riches and pleasures of this life, and bring no fruit to perfection.

15 But that on the good ground are they, which in an honest and good heart, having heard the word, keep it, and bring forth fruit with patience.

16 No man, when he hath lighted a candle, covereth it with a vessel, or putteth it under a bed; but setteth it on a candlestick, that they which enter in may see the light.

17 For nothing is secret, that shall not be made manifest; neither any thing hid, that shall not be known and come abroad.

18 Take heed therefore how ye hear: for whosoever hath, to him shall be given; and whosoever hath not, from him shall be taken even that which he seemeth to have.

19 Then came to him his mother and his brethren, and could not come at him for the press.

20 And it was told him by certain which said, Thy mother and thy brethren stand without, desiring to see thee.

21 And he answered and said unto them, My mother and my brethren are these which hear the word of God, and do it.

22 Now it came to pass on a certain day, that he went into a ship with his disciples: and he said unto them, Let us go over unto the other side of the lake. And they launched forth.

23 But as they sailed he fell asleep: and there came down a storm of wind on the lake; and they were filled with water, and were in jeopardy.

24 And they came to him, and awoke him, saying, Master, master, we perish. Then he arose, and rebuked the wind and the raging of the water: and they ceased, and there was a calm.

25 And he said unto them, Where is your faith? And they being afraid wondered, saying one to another, What manner of man is this! for he commandeth even the winds and water, and they obey him.

26 And they arrived at the country of the Gadarenes, which is over against Galilee.

27 And when he went forth to land, there met him out of the city a certain man, which had devils long time, and ware no clothes, neither abode in any house, but in the tombs.

28 When he saw Jesus, he cried out, and fell down before him, and with a loud voice said, What have I to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of God most high? I beseech thee, torment me not.

29 (For he had commanded the unclean spirit to come out of the man. For oftentimes it had caught him: and he was kept bound with chains and in fetters; and he brake the bands, and was driven of the devil into the wilderness.)

30 And Jesus asked him, saying, What is thy name? And he said, Legion: because many devils were entered into him.

31 And they besought him that he would not command them to go out into the deep.

32 And there was there an herd of many swine feeding on the mountain: and they besought him that he would suffer them to enter into them. And he suffered them.

33 Then went the devils out of the man, and entered into the swine: and the herd ran violently down a steep place into the lake, and were choked.

34 When they that fed them saw what was done, they fled, and went and told it in the city and in the country.

35 Then they went out to see what was done; and came to Jesus, and found the man, out of whom the devils were departed, sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed, and in his right mind: and they were afraid.

36 They also which saw it told them by what means he that was possessed of the devils was healed.

37 Then the whole multitude of the country of the Gadarenes round about besought him to depart from them; for they were taken with great fear: and he went up into the ship, and returned back again.

38 Now the man out of whom the devils were departed besought him that he might be with him: but Jesus sent him away, saying,

39 Return to thine own house, and shew how great things God hath done unto thee. And he went his way, and published throughout the whole city how great things Jesus had done unto him.

40 And it came to pass, that, when Jesus was returned, the people gladly received him: for they were all waiting for him.

41 And, behold, there came a man named Jairus, and he was a ruler of the synagogue: and he fell down at Jesus’ feet, and besought him that he would come into his house:

42 For he had one only daughter, about twelve years of age, and she lay a dying. But as he went the people thronged him.

43 And a woman having an issue of blood twelve years, which had spent all her living upon physicians, neither could be healed of any,

44 Came behind him, and touched the border of his garment: and immediately her issue of blood stanched.

45 And Jesus said, Who touched me? When all denied, Peter and they that were with him said, Master, the multitude throng thee and press thee, and sayest thou, Who touched me?

46 And Jesus said, Somebody hath touched me: for I perceive that virtue is gone out of me.

47 And when the woman saw that she was not hid, she came trembling, and falling down before him, she declared unto him before all the people for what cause she had touched him, and how she was healed immediately.

48 And he said unto her, Daughter, be of good comfort: thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace.

49 While he yet spake, there cometh one from the ruler of the synagogue’s house, saying to him, Thy daughter is dead; trouble not the Master.

50 But when Jesus heard it, he answered him, saying, Fear not: believe only, and she shall be made whole.

51 And when he came into the house, he suffered no man to go in, save Peter, and James, and John, and the father and the mother of the maiden.

52 And all wept, and bewailed her: but he said, Weep not; she is not dead, but sleepeth.

53 And they laughed him to scorn, knowing that she was dead.

54 And he put them all out, and took her by the hand, and called, saying, Maid, arise.

55 And her spirit came again, and she arose straightway: and he commanded to give her meat.

56 And her parents were astonished: but he charged them that they should tell no man what was done.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jesus travels with the twelve and with women who minister to him from their substance, teaching the parable of the sower and the lamp that is not hidden (vv. 1–18), and redefining family as those who hear and do God’s word (vv. 19–21). He stills a storm on the lake (vv. 22–25), frees the Gerasene demoniac whose spirits enter swine (vv. 26–39), and on the way to Jairus’s dying daughter heals a woman who touches his garment in the crowd, then raises the twelve-year-old girl with the words that she is not dead but sleepeth (vv. 40–56). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 8

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Afterward he went through every city and village, preaching and showing the glad tidings of the kingdom of God, and the twelve were with him. Henry notes teaching the gospel was the constant business of his life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Certain women healed of evil spirits and infirmities attended him, Mary called Magdalene, out of whom went seven devils. Healing becomes following: the healed become his company. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Joanna wife of Chuza, Herod's steward, and Susanna, and many others, ministered to him of their substance. Henry notes it showed his mean condition that he needed their kindness, and his great humility that he accepted it, though rich, yet for our sakes becoming poor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. When much people gathered from every city, he spoke by a parable. The parable will teach how hearing itself is tested. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. A sower went out to sow his seed; as he sowed, some fell by the wayside, was trodden down, and fowls devoured it. Seed on a path never enters the soil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Some fell on a rock, and as soon as it sprang up it withered, because it lacked moisture. Quick growth without root is the danger pictured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Some fell among thorns, and thorns sprang up with it and choked it. Growth is possible yet fruitless when rivals grow faster. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Other fell on good ground, sprang up, and bore fruit a hundredfold; and he cried, he that has ears to hear, let him hear. Henry urges heed of things that hinder profiting by the word: careless hearing, prejudice against it, and losing after hearing what was gained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. His disciples asked, what might this parable be? The question itself marks them as hearers who want understanding, not amusement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Unto you it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom, but to others in parables, that seeing they might not see, and hearing they might not understand. Henry counts us happy and indebted to free grace if what is a parable to others, amusing only, is plain truth to us by which we are taught and governed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry notes no relief for souls under guilt and fear of wrath but to go to Christ, call him Master, and say, I am undone if thou dost not help me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry adds that when dangers are over, we should take shame for our fears and give Christ glory for deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. They arrived at the country of the Gadarenes, over against Galilee. The calm crossing lands them in Gentile territory, where the next deliverance waits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 28. Seeing Jesus, he cried out, fell down before him, and said with a loud voice, what have I to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of God most high? I beseech thee, torment me not. Even the possessed man confesses the Son of God most high. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For he had commanded the unclean spirit to come out. Often it had caught him; bound with chains and fetters, he broke the bands and was driven by the devil into the wilderness. Human chains cannot hold what only Christ's word can expel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Jesus asked, what is thy name? And he said, Legion, because many devils were entered into him. Henry notes these malignant spirits are very numerous, and it is a miracle of mercy if those Satan possesses are not brought to destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. They besought him not to command them to go out into the deep. The spirits plead against the abyss, revealing their fear of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. A herd of many swine fed on the mountain, and they besought him to suffer them to enter into them, and he suffered them. Permission is granted, and the consequence will show the spirits' nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. The devils went out of the man into the swine, and the herd ran violently down a steep place into the lake and were choked. Deliverance for the man is destruction for the swine, and the trade is stark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. They that fed them saw what was done, fled, and told it in city and country. The herdsmen become unwilling evangelists of a loss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. People went out to see, and found the man, out of whom devils departed, sitting at Jesus' feet, clothed, and in his right mind, and they were afraid. The transformation is total: sitting, clothed, sane — and it frightens the onlookers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. They also which saw it told them by what means the possessed man was healed. Witnesses confirm the means: not chains, but Christ's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. The whole multitude of the Gadarenes besought him to depart, for they were taken with great fear, and he went up into the ship and returned. Henry warns Christ will not stay with those who slight him, and may no more return to them while others wait glad to receive him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. The man out of whom devils departed besought him that he might be with him, but Jesus sent him away, saying, return to thine own house and show how great things God has done unto thee. Staying with Jesus is refused in favour of witness at home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. He went his way and published throughout the whole city how great things Jesus had done unto him. The sent man becomes the preacher to his own city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. When Jesus returned, the people gladly received him, for they were all waiting for him. On the other shore, waiting replaces the Gadarenes' fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue, fell at Jesus' feet and besought him to come into his house. A ruler publicly humbles himself for his child's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. He had one only daughter, about twelve years old, and she lay dying; but as he went, the people thronged him. Henry notes we should not complain of crowd and hurry while in the way of duty and doing good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. A woman having an issue of blood twelve years, who had spent all her living on physicians and could not be healed of any, was hidden in that crowd. Twelve years of dying daughter and twelve years of bleeding woman meet in one throng. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. She came behind him, touched the border of his garment, and immediately her issue stanched. Henry notes many a poor soul is healed and saved by Christ hidden in a crowd, and nobody notices it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Jesus said, who touched me? Peter answered, Master, the multitude throng and press thee, and sayest thou, who touched me? The crowd presses, but one touch differs from all pressing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Somebody has touched me, for I perceive virtue is gone out of me. Power given is power felt, even in a throng. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Seeing she was not hid, the woman came trembling, fell down before him, and declared before all for what cause she touched him and how she was healed immediately. Henry notes this woman came trembling, yet her faith saved her; there may be trembling where yet there is saving faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Daughter, be of good comfort: thy faith has made thee whole; go in peace. Trembling faith is named, comforted, and sent in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. While he yet spoke, one came from the ruler's house, saying, thy daughter is dead; trouble not the Master. The interruption seems to end Jairus's hope at the worst moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. When Jesus heard it, he answered, fear not, believe only, and she shall be made whole. Henry quotes his comfortable words to Jairus, and notes in perfect faith there is no fear; the more we fear, the less we believe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. Coming into the house, he suffered none to go in save Peter, James, John, and the father and mother of the maiden. The inner circle is small for the work of raising. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. All wept and bewailed her, but he said, weep not, she is not dead, but sleeps. Death is renamed sleep by the one about to wake her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. They laughed him to scorn, knowing she was dead. Knowledge of death mocks the claim, and will be answered by the event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. He put them all out, took her by the hand, and called, Maid, arise. Henry notes the hand of Christ's grace goes with the calls of his word, to make them effectual. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. Her spirit came again, she arose straightway, and he commanded to give her meat. Henry notes, as babes newborn, so those newly raised from sin desire spiritual food, that they may grow thereby. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Her parents were astonished, but he charged them to tell no man what was done. Astonishment is left without a publicity campaign; the family keeps the wonder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 34. They that fed them saw what was done, fled, and told it in city and country. The herdsmen become unwilling evangelists of a loss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. People went out to see, and found the man, out of whom devils departed, sitting at Jesus' feet, clothed, and in his right mind, and they were afraid. The transformation is total: sitting, clothed, sane — and it frightens the onlookers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. They also which saw it told them by what means the possessed man was healed. Witnesses confirm the means: not chains, but Christ's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. The whole multitude of the Gadarenes besought him to depart, for they were taken with great fear, and he went up into the ship and returned. Henry warns Christ will not stay with those who slight him, and may no more return to them while others wait glad to receive him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. The man out of whom devils departed besought him that he might be with him, but Jesus sent him away, saying, return to thine own house and show how great things God has done unto thee. Staying with Jesus is refused in favour of witness at home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. He went his way and published throughout the whole city how great things Jesus had done unto him. The sent man becomes the preacher to his own city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. When Jesus returned, the people gladly received him, for they were all waiting for him. On the other shore, waiting replaces the Gadarenes' fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue, fell at Jesus' feet and besought him to come into his house. A ruler publicly humbles himself for his child's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. He had one only daughter, about twelve years old, and she lay dying; but as he went, the people thronged him. Henry notes we should not complain of crowd and hurry while in the way of duty and doing good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. A woman having an issue of blood twelve years, who had spent all her living on physicians and could not be healed of any, was hidden in that crowd. Twelve years of dying daughter and twelve years of bleeding woman meet in one throng. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. She came behind him, touched the border of his garment, and immediately her issue stanched. Henry notes many a poor soul is healed and saved by Christ hidden in a crowd, and nobody notices it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Jesus said, who touched me? Peter answered, Master, the multitude throng and press thee, and sayest thou, who touched me? The crowd presses, but one touch differs from all pressing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Somebody has touched me, for I perceive virtue is gone out of me. Power given is power felt, even in a throng. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Seeing she was not hid, the woman came trembling, fell down before him, and declared before all for what cause she touched him and how she was healed immediately. Henry notes this woman came trembling, yet her faith saved her; there may be trembling where yet there is saving faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Daughter, be of good comfort: thy faith has made thee whole; go in peace. Trembling faith is named, comforted, and sent in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. While he yet spoke, one came from the ruler's house, saying, thy daughter is dead; trouble not the Master. The interruption seems to end Jairus's hope at the worst moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. When Jesus heard it, he answered, fear not, believe only, and she shall be made whole. Henry quotes his comfortable words to Jairus, and notes in perfect faith there is no fear; the more we fear, the less we believe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. Coming into the house, he suffered none to go in save Peter, James, John, and the father and mother of the maiden. The inner circle is small for the work of raising. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. All wept and bewailed her, but he said, weep not, she is not dead, but sleeps. Death is renamed sleep by the one about to wake her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. They laughed him to scorn, knowing she was dead. Knowledge of death mocks the claim, and will be answered by the event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. He put them all out, took her by the hand, and called, Maid, arise. Henry notes the hand of Christ's grace goes with the calls of his word, to make them effectual. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. Her spirit came again, she arose straightway, and he commanded to give her meat. Henry notes, as babes newborn, so those newly raised from sin desire spiritual food, that they may grow thereby. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Her parents were astonished, but he charged them to tell no man what was done. Astonishment is left without a publicity campaign; the family keeps the wonder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
