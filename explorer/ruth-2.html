The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ruth 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Naomi had a kinsman of her husband’s, a mighty man of wealth, of the family of Elimelech; and his name was Boaz.

2 And Ruth the Moabitess said unto Naomi, Let me now go to the field, and glean ears of corn after him in whose sight I shall find grace. And she said unto her, Go, my daughter.

3 And she went, and came, and gleaned in the field after the reapers: and her hap was to light on a part of the field belonging unto Boaz, who was of the kindred of Elimelech.

4 And, behold, Boaz came from Bethlehem, and said unto the reapers, The LORD be with you. And they answered him, The LORD bless thee.

5 Then said Boaz unto his servant that was set over the reapers, Whose damsel is this?

6 And the servant that was set over the reapers answered and said, It is the Moabitish damsel that came back with Naomi out of the country of Moab:

7 And she said, I pray you, let me glean and gather after the reapers among the sheaves: so she came, and hath continued even from the morning until now, that she tarried a little in the house.

8 Then said Boaz unto Ruth, Hearest thou not, my daughter? Go not to glean in another field, neither go from hence, but abide here fast by my maidens:

9 Let thine eyes be on the field that they do reap, and go thou after them: have I not charged the young men that they shall not touch thee? and when thou art athirst, go unto the vessels, and drink of that which the young men have drawn.

10 Then she fell on her face, and bowed herself to the ground, and said unto him, Why have I found grace in thine eyes, that thou shouldest take knowledge of me, seeing I am a stranger?

11 And Boaz answered and said unto her, It hath fully been shewed me, all that thou hast done unto thy mother in law since the death of thine husband: and how thou hast left thy father and thy mother, and the land of thy nativity, and art come unto a people which thou knewest not heretofore.

12 The LORD recompense thy work, and a full reward be given thee of the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings thou art come to trust.

13 Then she said, Let me find favour in thy sight, my lord; for that thou hast comforted me, and for that thou hast spoken friendly unto thine handmaid, though I be not like unto one of thine handmaidens.

14 And Boaz said unto her, At mealtime come thou hither, and eat of the bread, and dip thy morsel in the vinegar. And she sat beside the reapers: and he reached her parched corn, and she did eat, and was sufficed, and left.

15 And when she was risen up to glean, Boaz commanded his young men, saying, Let her glean even among the sheaves, and reproach her not:

16 And let fall also some of the handfuls of purpose for her, and leave them, that she may glean them, and rebuke her not.

17 So she gleaned in the field until even, and beat out that she had gleaned: and it was about an ephah of barley.

18 And she took it up, and went into the city: and her mother in law saw what she had gleaned: and she brought forth, and gave to her that she had reserved after she was sufficed.

19 And her mother in law said unto her, Where hast thou gleaned to day? and where wroughtest thou? blessed be he that did take knowledge of thee. And she shewed her mother in law with whom she had wrought, and said, The man’s name with whom I wrought to day is Boaz.

20 And Naomi said unto her daughter in law, Blessed be he of the LORD, who hath not left off his kindness to the living and to the dead. And Naomi said unto her, The man is near of kin unto us, one of our next kinsmen.

21 And Ruth the Moabitess said, He said unto me also, Thou shalt keep fast by my young men, until they have ended all my harvest.

22 And Naomi said unto Ruth her daughter in law, It is good, my daughter, that thou go out with his maidens, that they meet thee not in any other field.

23 So she kept fast by the maidens of Boaz to glean unto the end of barley harvest and of wheat harvest; and dwelt with her mother in law.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ruth proposes to glean after him in whose sight she shall find grace, and her hap is to light on a field belonging to Boaz, a mighty man of wealth and a kinsman of Elimelech. Boaz greets his reapers with the LORD be with you, learns who the Moabitish damsel is, bids her abide in his field and keep close to his maidens, lets her drink from what the young men draw, invites her to his table, and orders handfuls of purpose to be left for her. She gleans about an ephah of barley, carries it to Naomi with what she reserved, and learns that Boaz is one of their near kinsmen; she keeps fast by his maidens until barley and wheat harvest are ended. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Naomi had a kinsman of her husband's, Boaz, a mighty man of wealth from Elimelech's family. Henry's chapter outline makes him the providential figure whose field Ruth will glean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Ruth herself proposed to go and glean after him in whose sight she should find grace. Henry praises her humility and industry: she cheerfully stooped to her poor lot and would not eat the bread of idleness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Ruth's hap was to light on a part of the field belonging to Boaz. Henry notes that God wisely orders what seem to us small, uncertain events to serve his glory and the good of his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Boaz came from Bethlehem greeting his reapers, "The LORD be with you," and they answered, "The LORD bless thee." Henry holds this pious, kind language up as proof that godly persons were still in Israel, and that true religion makes kind masters and faithful servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Boaz asked his servant set over the reapers whose damsel this was. Henry notes that a good master inquires concerning strangers in his field and orders them to be well treated, taking care his servants do no wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The servant answered that she was the Moabitish damsel who came back with Naomi from Moab. Henry's theme here is the godly household: Boaz's men answered courteously and truthfully rather than cursing their master behind his back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Ruth had asked leave to glean and gather after the reapers, and had worked from morning until now, resting only a little in the house. Henry presents this as diligence that promises well for this world and the other: no honest labour is a reproach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Boaz told Ruth to abide in his field, gleaning only there and keeping close to his maidens. Henry reads this as the kindness of a master who protects the stranger under his care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Boaz assured Ruth she might drink from what the young men drew, having charged them not to touch her. Henry notes that masters must see not only that they do no hurt themselves, but that those under them do no wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Ruth fell on her face and asked why she, a stranger, had found grace in his sight. Henry observes that she humbly owned herself unworthy of favours, having been born and brought up a heathen, and that we should likewise think humbly of ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Boaz answered that all Ruth had done for her mother-in-law since her husband's death had been fully shown to him. Henry sees in Boaz's kindness to Ruth a picture of the Lord Jesus Christ's kindness to poor sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Boaz prayed that the LORD would recompense Ruth's work and give her a full reward, since she had come to trust under his wings. Henry's passage stresses mutual kindness expressed in praying for one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Ruth thanked Boaz for comforting her and speaking friendly to her handmaid, though she was not like one of his handmaidens. Henry notes her humble, grateful spirit in receiving kindness as a favour, not demanding it as a debt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. At mealtime Boaz invited Ruth to eat of the bread and dip her morsel in the vinegar, and she ate and was sufficed, leaving some over. Henry treats this generous table as part of Boaz's ordered kindness to the stranger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. When Ruth rose to glean, Boaz commanded his young men to let her glean even among the sheaves and not reproach her. Henry's lesson is that true religion teaches a man to behave aright in every state and condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Boaz further ordered them to let fall handfuls of purpose for her and leave them for her to glean, rebuking none. Henry presents this deliberate generosity as the pattern of a kind master toward the poor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Ruth gleaned until evening, beat out what she had gathered, and it was about an ephah of barley. Henry remarks that it encourages industry that in all labour, even gleaning, there is profit, and Ruth was careful to secure what she had gained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Ruth carried her gleanings into the city, showed Naomi what she had gleaned, and gave her what she had reserved after being sufficed. Henry praises her regard for her mother and her care not to lose what she had wrought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Naomi asked where Ruth had gleaned and blessed him who had taken knowledge of her. Henry likens Naomi's questioning to a parent examining a child — to commend if well done, and with mildness to caution otherwise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Ruth told Naomi she had wrought with Boaz, and Naomi blessed the LORD, who had not left off his kindness to the living and the dead. Henry's nightly question applies here: "Where have I gleaned to-day?" and what improvement has been made in grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Naomi told Ruth that Boaz was near of kin to them, one of their next kinsmen. Henry notes this kinship as the providential opening toward the rest and preferment that would follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Boaz had also told Ruth to keep fast by his young men until the harvest was ended. Henry stresses Ruth's dutiful observance of direction: when the LORD deals bountifully, we should not be found in any other field. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. So Ruth kept fast by Boaz's maidens to glean until barley and wheat harvest ended, and dwelt with her mother-in-law. Henry contrasts Ruth, who kept at home helping her mother, with Dinah, whose going out to see the daughters of the land ended in disgrace; Ruth's humility and industry ended in preferment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words in this chapter that a modern reader may stumble on. They explain the chapter text; they are not Scripture.

  • glean. to gather the grain left behind by the reapers, the poor's lawful share of the harvest Interpreted Editorial gloss, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes
  • ephah. a measure of grain, the amount Ruth beats out from her day's gleaning Interpreted Editorial gloss, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Connections to other passages

  • Ruth contrasted with Dinah: Matthew Henry contrasts Ruth, who kept fast by Boaz's maidens to glean until harvest ended and dwelt with her mother-in-law, with Dinah, whose going out to see the daughters of the land ended in disgrace. See Dinah's story in Genesis 34. Interpreted Study note on Ruth 2:23, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
