The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Thessalonians 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Furthermore then we beseech you, brethren, and exhort you by the Lord Jesus, that as ye have received of us how ye ought to walk and to please God, so ye would abound more and more.

2 For ye know what commandments we gave you by the Lord Jesus.

3 For this is the will of God, even your sanctification, that ye should abstain from fornication:

4 That every one of you should know how to possess his vessel in sanctification and honour;

5 Not in the lust of concupiscence, even as the Gentiles which know not God:

6 That no man go beyond and defraud his brother in any matter: because that the Lord is the avenger of all such, as we also have forewarned you and testified.

7 For God hath not called us unto uncleanness, but unto holiness.

8 He therefore that despiseth, despiseth not man, but God, who hath also given unto us his holy Spirit.

9 But as touching brotherly love ye need not that I write unto you: for ye yourselves are taught of God to love one another.

10 And indeed ye do it toward all the brethren which are in all Macedonia: but we beseech you, brethren, that ye increase more and more;

11 And that ye study to be quiet, and to do your own business, and to work with your own hands, as we commanded you;

12 That ye may walk honestly toward them that are without, and that ye may have lack of nothing.

13 But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope.

14 For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.

15 For this we say unto you by the word of the Lord, that we which are alive and remain unto the coming of the Lord shall not prevent them which are asleep.

16 For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first:

17 Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord.

18 Wherefore comfort one another with these words.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter calls readers to holiness in body and conduct, to brotherly love already practised, and to quiet, honest work. Then it comforts them about believers who have died: they are not lost, for the Lord himself will descend, the dead in Christ will rise first, and the living will be caught up together with them, so they should comfort one another with these words. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Furthermore we beseech and exhort you by the Lord Jesus, that as you received of us how you ought to walk and please God, so you would abound more and more. The exhortation is affectionate and earnest, Henry notes, and it is not enough to abide in faith; we must abound in the work of faith and grow better, walking more evenly and closely with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. For you know what commandments we gave you by the Lord Jesus. They had been informed of their duty and could not plead ignorance, Henry says, and the apostles taught only what Christ commanded, not commandments of their own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. For this is the will of God, even your sanctification, that you should abstain from fornication. Sanctification is the will of God in general, Henry explains, because he who called us is holy, and chastity is one branch of it, requiring purity in our bodies as well as holiness in heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. That every one of you should know how to possess his vessel in sanctification and honour. The body is the vessel of the soul, Henry says, and must be kept pure from defiling lusts; it is greatly for our honour to keep appetites from tyrannising over reason and conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Not in the lust of concupiscence, even as the Gentiles which know not God. To indulge lust is to live as heathens, Henry observes, for the Gentiles did not know God or his will as Christians do, and those in Christ have crucified the flesh with its affections and lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. That no man go beyond and defraud his brother in any matter, because the Lord is the avenger of all such, as we also forewarned and testified. Uncleanness is often injustice, Henry notes, defrauding those joined in marriage and their posterity, and though it may lie undiscovered among men, God will render recompense, for whoremongers and adulterers God will judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. For God has not called us to uncleanness, but to holiness. The gospel calls us from uncleanness to holiness, Henry says, forbidding all impurity and requiring the greatest purity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He therefore that despises, despises not man, but God, who has also given us his Holy Spirit. Some make light of purity because they hear it from men, Henry warns, but these are God's commands, and uncleanness grieves the Holy Spirit given to arm us against these sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. But as touching brotherly love, you need not that I write to you, for you yourselves are taught of God to love one another. It is God's grace, not their own virtue, that Henry takes notice of: all savingly taught of God are taught this lesson, the livery of Christ's family, and the Spirit's teaching exceeds men's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And indeed you do it toward all the brethren in all Macedonia, but we beseech you, brethren, that you increase more and more. Their love was extensive, not confined to their own city or sentiments, Henry notes, yet even the eminent must pray and labour for more, for none on this side of heaven loves in perfection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And that you study to be quiet, and do your own business, and work with your own hands, as we commanded you. Quietness of temper and behaviour tends to our own and others' happiness, Henry says, and busy-bodies meddling in others' matters lose quiet in their own minds; Christianity does not discharge us from our callings but teaches diligence in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. That you may walk honestly toward them that are without, and that you may have lack of nothing. Quiet diligence gains a good report from strangers and even enemies, Henry observes, and the diligent live comfortably, earning their own bread, not burdensome to friends nor scandalous to strangers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. But I would not have you ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that you sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. All grief for friends' deaths is not unlawful, Henry says, but it must not be immoderate, for the dead in the Lord are not as those without hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. For if we believe Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him. They sleep in Jesus, Henry explains: death does not annihilate them but is rest, and being still in union with him they sleep in his arms under his care, great gainers by death; and Christ, risen as firstfruits, is the pledge of their rising. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. For this we say to you by the word of the Lord, that we which are alive and remain to the coming of the Lord shall not prevent them which are asleep. By divine revelation, Henry notes, we know the living shall not go before the dead, for the Redeemer's first care in that day will be about his dead saints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God, and the dead in Christ shall rise first. The Lord will come down in pomp and power, Henry says, with the shout of a king, an innumerable company of angels, and the trump of God awakening those who sleep in the dust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air, and so shall we ever be with the Lord. Those found alive will then be changed, Henry notes, and will have no greater privilege or joy at that day than those who fell asleep in Jesus, for all shall be ever with the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Wherefore comfort one another with these words. The doctrine of the resurrection and second coming, Henry says, is a great antidote against fear of death and inordinate sorrow, and these words are given to be spoken to one another as comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • them which are asleep. believers who have died, the chapter’s image for the dead in Christ who will rise first (verses 13–14) Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • prevent. in this older wording, go before or precede: the living will not precede those who have died at the Lord’s coming (verse 15) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
