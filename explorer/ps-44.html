The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 44 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 We have heard with our ears, O God, our fathers have told us, what work thou didst in their days, in the times of old.

2 How thou didst drive out the heathen with thy hand, and plantedst them; how thou didst afflict the people, and cast them out.

3 For they got not the land in possession by their own sword, neither did their own arm save them: but thy right hand, and thine arm, and the light of thy countenance, because thou hadst a favour unto them.

4 Thou art my King, O God: command deliverances for Jacob.

5 Through thee will we push down our enemies: through thy name will we tread them under that rise up against us.

6 For I will not trust in my bow, neither shall my sword save me.

7 But thou hast saved us from our enemies, and hast put them to shame that hated us.

8 In God we boast all the day long, and praise thy name for ever. Selah.

9 But thou hast cast off, and put us to shame; and goest not forth with our armies.

10 Thou makest us to turn back from the enemy: and they which hate us spoil for themselves.

11 Thou hast given us like sheep appointed for meat; and hast scattered us among the heathen.

12 Thou sellest thy people for nought, and dost not increase thy wealth by their price.

13 Thou makest us a reproach to our neighbours, a scorn and a derision to them that are round about us.

14 Thou makest us a byword among the heathen, a shaking of the head among the people.

15 My confusion is continually before me, and the shame of my face hath covered me,

16 For the voice of him that reproacheth and blasphemeth; by reason of the enemy and avenger.

17 All this is come upon us; yet have we not forgotten thee, neither have we dealt falsely in thy covenant.

18 Our heart is not turned back, neither have our steps declined from thy way;

19 Though thou hast sore broken us in the place of dragons, and covered us with the shadow of death.

20 If we have forgotten the name of our God, or stretched out our hands to a strange god;

21 Shall not God search this out? for he knoweth the secrets of the heart.

22 Yea, for thy sake are we killed all the day long; we are counted as sheep for the slaughter.

23 Awake, why sleepest thou, O Lord? arise, cast us not off for ever.

24 Wherefore hidest thou thy face, and forgettest our affliction and our oppression?

25 For our soul is bowed down to the dust: our belly cleaveth unto the earth.

26 Arise for our help, and redeem us for thy mercies’ sake.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A communal lament of innocent suffering: fathers told of God's old works, land won not by sword but by his right hand, yet now God has cast them off, turned them back, given them like sheep for slaughter, scattered and sold for nothing, a byword among nations. Though heart unturned and covenant unbroken, they are killed all day for his sake, and the psalm ends: arise for our help, redeem us for mercies' sake. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. We have heard with our ears, O God, our fathers have told us, what work you did in their days, in the times of old: heard history, told by fathers, is the psalm's foundation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. How you drove out the heathen with your hand, and planted them; how you afflicted the people and cast them out: God's hand drove out, planted, afflicted, and cast out — the conquest was his act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For they got not the land by their own sword, neither did their own arm save them, but your right hand, and your arm, and the light of your countenance, because you had favour to them: sword and arm are denied, and right hand, arm, light of countenance, and favour are credited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. You are my King, O God; command deliverances for Jacob: the King of the past is asked to command deliverances for Jacob now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Through you we will push down our enemies; through your name we will tread under those who rise against us: pushing and treading are done through God and his name, not through weapons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For I will not trust in my bow, neither shall my sword save me: bow and sword are explicitly distrusted as saviours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But you have saved us from our enemies, and put to shame those who hated us: past salvation and shaming of haters are the remembered pattern. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. In God we boast all the day long, and praise your name for ever: boasting in God all day, and praise for ever, are the response to remembered salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. But you have cast off, and put us to shame, and go not forth with our armies: the turn is stark — casting off, shame, and God's absence from the armies replace the boasting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. You make us turn back from the enemy, and those who hate us spoil for themselves: retreat and spoiling by haters follow God's casting off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. You have given us like sheep appointed for meat, and scattered us among the heathen: sheep for slaughter and scattering among nations picture defeat as sacrifice and exile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. You sell your people for nought, and do not increase your wealth by their price: selling for nothing — no profit even to the seller — stresses the worthlessness to which they have fallen in men's eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. You make us a reproach to neighbours, a scorn and derision to those round about us: neighbours' reproach and the surrounding scorn complete the public shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. You make us a byword among the heathen, a shaking of the head among the people: a byword and a shaken head — they have become a proverb of ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. My confusion is continually before me, and the shame of my face has covered me: confusion continually before him, shame covering his face — the shame is personal now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. For the voice of him who reproaches and blasphemes, by reason of the enemy and avenger: reproach and blasphemy, from enemy and avenger, are the voices behind the shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. All this is come upon us, yet we have not forgotten you, neither dealt falsely in your covenant: despite all this, no forgetting and no false dealing with the covenant — innocence is claimed amid suffering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Our heart is not turned back, neither have our steps declined from your way: heart unturned and steps undeclined — inward and outward fidelity both stand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Though you have sore broken us in the place of dragons, and covered us with the shadow of death: broken in a dragons' place, covered with death's shadow — yet fidelity held. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. If we have forgotten the name of our God, or stretched out our hands to a strange god: the hypothetical is stated — forgetting God's name or reaching to a strange god would be the apostasy charged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Shall not God search this out? For he knows the secrets of the heart: God's searching and his knowledge of heart-secrets would expose any such apostasy — and none is found. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Yea, for your sake we are killed all the day long; we are counted as sheep for the slaughter: suffering for God's sake, all day, as slaughter-sheep — Paul cites this of the church's sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Awake, why do you sleep, O Lord? Arise, cast us not off for ever: God is called to awake from seeming sleep, to arise, and not to cast off for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Wherefore do you hide your face, and forget our affliction and oppression? The hiding of God's face and forgetting of affliction are the questions of a people who have kept covenant and still suffer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. For our soul is bowed down to the dust; our belly cleaves to the earth: soul to dust, belly to earth — prostration is total. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Arise for our help, and redeem us for your mercies' sake: the final plea is for arising, help, and redemption — grounded, as always, in mercies' sake, not in merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
