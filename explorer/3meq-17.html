Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter reasons from the grain of wheat: unless it dies it bears no fruit, but if it dies it takes root, shoots, becomes an ear, and bears fruit — and likewise, it says, the grain profits from wind, water, earth, and sun, without which it cannot bear. The grain is set, the chapter says, in the likeness of Adam, upon whom God’s spirit was breathed, and the vine is treated the same way: watered, rooted, ascending heavenward by God’s dew of mercy, it brings forth fruit that makes a sweet savour, bread that satisfies hunger and thirst, and wine that, as the chapter quotes from the Psalm, “maketh glad the heart of man.” But the chapter turns sharply on excess: the drunkenness of wine causes a man to err exceedingly, not knowing what is in his hands and feet. God made the vine and its fruit, it closes, that His name may be glorified [?] for those who do His will and believe the resurrection of the dead, who shall rejoice in the dwelling of life. Several verses break off mid-thought (verses 2–4, 6–9), so the chain of the argument is fragmentary in places. Interpreted The chapter text itself; no study note exists for this chapter
