The Text — Judith 5 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Then was it declared to Holofernes, the chief captain of the army of Assur, that the children of Israel had prepared for war, and had shut up the passages of the hill country, and had fortified all the tops of the high hills and had laid impediments in the champaign countries:

2 Wherewith he was very angry, and called all the princes of Moab, and the captains of Ammon, and all the governors of the sea coast,

3 And he said unto them, Tell me now, ye sons of Chanaan, who this people is, that dwelleth in the hill country, and what are the cities that they inhabit, and what is the multitude of their army, and wherein is their power and strength, and what king is set over them, or captain of their army;

4 And why have they determined not to come and meet me, more than all the inhabitants of the west.

5 Then said Achior, the captain of all the sons of Ammon, Let my lord now hear a word from the mouth of thy servant, and I will declare unto thee the truth concerning this people, which dwelleth near thee, and inhabiteth the hill countries: and there shall no lie come out of the mouth of thy servant.

6 This people are descended of the Chaldeans:

7 And they sojourned heretofore in Mesopotamia, because they would not follow the gods of their fathers, which were in the land of Chaldea.

8 For they left the way of their ancestors, and worshipped the God of heaven, the God whom they knew: so they cast them out from the face of their gods, and they fled into Mesopotamia, and sojourned there many days.

9 Then their God commanded them to depart from the place where they sojourned, and to go into the land of Chanaan: where they dwelt, and were increased with gold and silver, and with very much cattle.

10 But when a famine covered all the land of Chanaan, they went down into Egypt, and sojourned there, while they were nourished, and became there a great multitude, so that one could not number their nation.

11 Therefore the king of Egypt rose up against them, and dealt subtilly with them, and brought them low with labouring in brick, and made them slaves.

12 Then they cried unto their God, and he smote all the land of Egypt with incurable plagues: so the Egyptians cast them out of their sight.

13 And God dried the Red sea before them,

14 And brought them to mount Sina, and Cades-Barne, and cast forth all that dwelt in the wilderness.

15 So they dwelt in the land of the Amorites, and they destroyed by their strength all them of Esebon, and passing over Jordan they possessed all the hill country.

16 And they cast forth before them the Chanaanite, the Pherezite, the Jebusite, and the Sychemite, and all the Gergesites, and they dwelt in that country many days.

17 And whilst they sinned not before their God, they prospered, because the God that hateth iniquity was with them.

18 But when they departed from the way which he appointed them, they were destroyed in many battles very sore, and were led captives into a land that was not their's, and the temple of their God was cast to the ground, and their cities were taken by the enemies.

19 But now are they returned to their God, and are come up from the places where they were scattered, and have possessed Jerusalem, where their sanctuary is, and are seated in the hill country; for it was desolate.

20 Now therefore, my lord and governor, if there be any error against this people, and they sin against their God, let us consider that this shall be their ruin, and let us go up, and we shall overcome them.

21 But if there be no iniquity in their nation, let my lord now pass by, lest their Lord defend them, and their God be for them, and we become a reproach before all the world.

22 And when Achior had finished these sayings, all the people standing round about the tent murmured, and the chief men of Holofernes, and all that dwelt by the sea side, and in Moab, spake that he should kill him.

23 For, say they, we will not be afraid of the face of the children of Israel: for, lo, it is a people that have no strength nor power for a strong battle

24 Now therefore, lord Holofernes, we will go up, and they shall be a prey to be devoured of all thine army.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Holofernes is told Israel has prepared for war and shut up the hill passes, and he asks the princes of Moab and Ammon who these people are. Achior, leader of the Ammonites, answers with Israel's whole history: their departure from Chaldea, sojourn in Egypt, deliverance, conquest of Canaan, captivity, and return, and the rule he draws from it: while they sin not before their God they prosper, because the God who hates iniquity is with them, but if they sin, they will be a prey to his army. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Achior. the leader of the children of Ammon, who recounts Israel's history to Holofernes (verse 5). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Chanaan. Canaan, the land Israel entered and dwelt in with gold, silver, and cattle (verse 9). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. It was declared to Holofernes that Israel had prepared for war and shut up the passages of the hill country; the Greek adds that they "had fortified every summit of a high mountain, and had placed scandals in the plains," obstructing the passage with ditches, trees, and snares of every description. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. He was very angry and called all the princes of Moab, captains of Ammon, and governors of the sea coast; the Greek adds "and the satraps of the maritime country," the Philistines, who had submitted like the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. He said, "Tell me now, ye sons of Chanaan, who this people is"; the Greek adds, "Ye sons of Chanaan tell" — he was not acquainted with their origin, for the title belonged only to the Phoenicians, who might also be present. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. He asked why they had determined not to come and meet him more than all the inhabitants of the west; the Greek reads "west," which seems more accurate unless Holofernes was on the sea-coast, and he was not absolutely unacquainted with the Jews but spoke in contempt, wishing to know if they had formed a league with the Egyptians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. Then said Achior, captain of all the sons of Ammon, "Let my lord now hear a word from the mouth of thy servant"; the Greek reads "the mouth of thy slave." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. "This people are descended of the Chaldeans": Abraham was a native of Ur (Genesis 12), and this tended to conciliate the favour of the general, who was of the same country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. They sojourned heretofore in Mesopotamia because they would not follow the gods of their fathers in Chaldea; the Greek adds that they knew the God of heaven, "and they (the Chaldean idolaters) cast them out from the face of the gods, and they fled into Mesopotamia, and dwelt there many days," about two years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. For they left the way of their ancestors and worshipped the God of heaven, the God whom they knew, so they cast them out from the face of their gods — the same Greek addition explains the expulsion from Chaldea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. Then their God commanded them to depart from where they sojourned and go into the land of Chanaan, where they dwelt and were enriched; the Greek adds, "and their God commanded them to leave their abode, and to go into the land of Chanaan; and they dwelt there, and were enriched," and "there they became innumerable." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. Therefore the king of Egypt rose up against them and dealt subtilly with them, bringing them low with labouring in brick; "oppressed" is in the Greek "craftily inveigled them" (Exodus 1:10). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. Then they cried unto their God, and he smote all the land of Egypt with incurable plagues, so the Egyptians cast them out of their sight; the Greek adds, "for which there was no remedy. And the Egyptians cast them out from among them." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. And God dried the Red Sea before them; the Greek continues, "And God dried up the Red Sea before them, and conducted them to Mount Sinai and Cades-Barne." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. And brought them to Mount Sina and Cades-Barne, and cast forth all that dwelt in the wilderness; the Greek adds that God "cast out all the inhabitants of the desert" — attested in Deuteronomy 32:10 and Jeremiah 2:6. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. So they dwelt in the land of the Amorites, and destroyed by their strength all them of Esebon, and passing over Jordan possessed all the hill country; the Greek adds that they "dwelt in the land of the Amorrhites, and exterminated all the Hesebon, by their power. Then crossing the Jordan, they took as their inheritance all the mountainous parts." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. And they cast forth before them the Chanaanite, Pherezite, Jebusite, Sychemite, and all the Gergesites, and dwelt in those lands; the Greek adds that they "ejected the Chanaanite, Pherezite, Jebusite, Sichem, and all the Gergesites, and dwelt therein many days," and the environs of Sichem belonged to the Hevites; they overcame in the days of Josue, at Jericho and elsewhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. But when they departed from the way which he appointed them, they were destroyed in many battles very sore and led captives into a land that was not theirs; he seems to speak of the captivity of Manasses, of the ten tribes, and of the loss sustained by Achaz (2 Chronicles 28:5), and the Greek reads, "exceedingly; and they were led captive into a land which was not their own, and the temple of their God became as a pavement, and their cities were taken by the enemies." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. But now are they returned to their God and are come up from the places where they were scattered, and have possessed Jerusalem, where their sanctuary is, and are seated in the hill country; the Greek continues, "But now, returning to their God, they are come up from the places to which they had been scattered, and have possession of Jerusalem, where is their sanctuary; and they have inhabited the mountainous country, for it was a desert." Adversaries infer from this that the siege of Bethulia could not have taken place till after the Babylonian captivity, and many Catholics allow it, but the proof is not conclusive, as all this might be verified under Manasses, when the temple was trampled on and profaned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. But if there be no iniquity in their nation, let my lord now pass by, lest their Lord defend them, and their God be for them, and we become a reproach; the Greek reads, "let my lord indeed pass by, lest their Lord should cover them with a shield, for their God is for them." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. And when Achior had finished these sayings, all the people standing round about the tent murmured, and the chief men of Holofernes and all that dwelt by the sea side and in Moab spake; the Greek reads, "all the people round the tent murmured; and the great men of Holofernes, and all who dwelt in the maritime country, and in Moab, threatened to cut him to pieces." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 23. For, say they, we will not be afraid of the face of the children of Israel, for lo, it is a people that have no strength nor power for a strong battle; the Greek continues, "For we shall not fear the children of Israel. Lo! a people without power, strength, or army in battle array!" Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 24. Now therefore, lord Holofernes, we will go up, and they shall be a prey to be devoured of all thine army; the Greek continues, "We shall then go up, and they shall be for food to all thy army, lord Holofernes." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
