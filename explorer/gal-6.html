The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Galatians 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.

2 Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.

3 For if a man think himself to be something, when he is nothing, he deceiveth himself.

4 But let every man prove his own work, and then shall he have rejoicing in himself alone, and not in another.

5 For every man shall bear his own burden.

6 Let him that is taught in the word communicate unto him that teacheth in all good things.

7 Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.

8 For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting.

9 And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.

10 As we have therefore opportunity, let us do good unto all men, especially unto them who are of the household of faith.

11 Ye see how large a letter I have written unto you with mine own hand.

12 As many as desire to make a fair shew in the flesh, they constrain you to be circumcised; only lest they should suffer persecution for the cross of Christ.

13 For neither they themselves who are circumcised keep the law; but desire to have you circumcised, that they may glory in your flesh.

14 But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom the world is crucified unto me, and I unto the world.

15 For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision, but a new creature.

16 And as many as walk according to this rule, peace be on them, and mercy, and upon the Israel of God.

17 From henceforth let no man trouble me: for I bear in my body the marks of the Lord Jesus.

18 Brethren, the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Restore a fallen brother gently, bear one another's burdens, and do not grow weary in doing good, sowing to the Spirit rather than to the flesh. Paul closes in his own hand: those pressing circumcision seek boasting, but he glories only in the cross of Christ, by which the world is crucified to him. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Brethren, if a man is overtaken in a fault, the spiritual are to restore him in meekness, considering themselves, lest they also be tempted, since the better we know our own hearts the less we despise others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ, mutual forbearance and compassion being obligatory after Christ's example, as fellow travelers help one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. For if a man thinks himself something when he is nothing, he deceives himself, pretending to what he has not and putting a cheat upon himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. But let every man prove his own work, and then he shall have rejoicing in himself alone and not in another, self-knowledge disposing him to help others under infirmities rather than to dictate to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. For every man shall bear his own burden, since no man can pay a ransom for his brother and each must reckon with God about his own sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Let him taught in the word communicate to him who teaches in all good things, the taught supporting the teacher, a duty many excuse themselves from while making a show of religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Be not deceived: God is not mocked, for whatever a man sows, that shall he also reap, since God knows hearts as well as actions and cannot be imposed upon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He who sows to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption, but he who sows to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting, present time being seed time and the other world harvest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Let them not be weary in well doing, for in due season they shall reap if they faint not, only perseverance in well doing having the reward promised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. As they have opportunity, let them do good to all men, especially to those of the household of faith, making doing good the business of their lives while occasions offer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. See how large a letter Paul has written with his own hand, a mark of his personal earnestness in this closing warning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. As many as desire to make a fair show in the flesh constrain the Galatians to be circumcised, only lest they suffer persecution for the cross of Christ, proud and carnal hearts content with just enough religion to keep up a fair show. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. For neither do the circumcised themselves keep the law, but they desire the Galatians circumcised that they may glory in their flesh, seeking credit from others' conformity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. But God forbid Paul should glory save in the cross of the Lord Jesus Christ, by whom the world is crucified to him and he to the world, his principal glory being a crucified Redeemer and salvation by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision avails anything, but a new creature, old things passed away and new views and dispositions brought in by the Holy Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. As many as walk according to this rule, peace be on them, and mercy, and upon the Israel of God, the new creation to Christ's image being the greatest distinction between men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. From henceforth let no man trouble Paul, for he bears in his body the marks of the Lord Jesus, scars from persecuting enemies for cleaving to Christ and the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Brethren, the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with their spirit, Paul closing not with the law or works' righteousness but with Christ's grace, quickening, comforting, and strengthening them, to which he sets his Amen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
