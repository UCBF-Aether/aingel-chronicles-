The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God;

2 As it is written in the prophets, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face, which shall prepare thy way before thee.

3 The voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make his paths straight.

4 John did baptize in the wilderness, and preach the baptism of repentance for the remission of sins.

5 And there went out unto him all the land of Judaea, and they of Jerusalem, and were all baptized of him in the river of Jordan, confessing their sins.

6 And John was clothed with camel’s hair, and with a girdle of a skin about his loins; and he did eat locusts and wild honey;

7 And preached, saying, There cometh one mightier than I after me, the latchet of whose shoes I am not worthy to stoop down and unloose.

8 I indeed have baptized you with water: but he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost.

9 And it came to pass in those days, that Jesus came from Nazareth of Galilee, and was baptized of John in Jordan.

10 And straightway coming up out of the water, he saw the heavens opened, and the Spirit like a dove descending upon him:

11 And there came a voice from heaven, saying, Thou art my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.

12 And immediately the spirit driveth him into the wilderness.

13 And he was there in the wilderness forty days, tempted of Satan; and was with the wild beasts; and the angels ministered unto him.

14 Now after that John was put in prison, Jesus came into Galilee, preaching the gospel of the kingdom of God,

15 And saying, The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel.

16 Now as he walked by the sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and Andrew his brother casting a net into the sea: for they were fishers.

17 And Jesus said unto them, Come ye after me, and I will make you to become fishers of men.

18 And straightway they forsook their nets, and followed him.

19 And when he had gone a little farther thence, he saw James the son of Zebedee, and John his brother, who also were in the ship mending their nets.

20 And straightway he called them: and they left their father Zebedee in the ship with the hired servants, and went after him.

21 And they went into Capernaum; and straightway on the sabbath day he entered into the synagogue, and taught.

22 And they were astonished at his doctrine: for he taught them as one that had authority, and not as the scribes.

23 And there was in their synagogue a man with an unclean spirit; and he cried out,

24 Saying, Let us alone; what have we to do with thee, thou Jesus of Nazareth? art thou come to destroy us? I know thee who thou art, the Holy One of God.

25 And Jesus rebuked him, saying, Hold thy peace, and come out of him.

26 And when the unclean spirit had torn him, and cried with a loud voice, he came out of him.

27 And they were all amazed, insomuch that they questioned among themselves, saying, What thing is this? what new doctrine is this? for with authority commandeth he even the unclean spirits, and they do obey him.

28 And immediately his fame spread abroad throughout all the region round about Galilee.

29 And forthwith, when they were come out of the synagogue, they entered into the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John.

30 But Simon’s wife’s mother lay sick of a fever, and anon they tell him of her.

31 And he came and took her by the hand, and lifted her up; and immediately the fever left her, and she ministered unto them.

32 And at even, when the sun did set, they brought unto him all that were diseased, and them that were possessed with devils.

33 And all the city was gathered together at the door.

34 And he healed many that were sick of divers diseases, and cast out many devils; and suffered not the devils to speak, because they knew him.

35 And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed.

36 And Simon and they that were with him followed after him.

37 And when they had found him, they said unto him, All men seek for thee.

38 And he said unto them, Let us go into the next towns, that I may preach there also: for therefore came I forth.

39 And he preached in their synagogues throughout all Galilee, and cast out devils.

40 And there came a leper to him, beseeching him, and kneeling down to him, and saying unto him, If thou wilt, thou canst make me clean.

41 And Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him, and saith unto him, I will; be thou clean.

42 And as soon as he had spoken, immediately the leprosy departed from him, and he was cleansed.

43 And he straitly charged him, and forthwith sent him away;

44 And saith unto him, See thou say nothing to any man: but go thy way, shew thyself to the priest, and offer for thy cleansing those things which Moses commanded, for a testimony unto them.

45 But he went out, and began to publish it much, and to blaze abroad the matter, insomuch that Jesus could no more openly enter into the city, but was without in desert places: and they came to him from every quarter.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter opens at speed: John the Baptist prepares the way in the wilderness (vv. 1–8), Jesus is baptized and the Spirit descends on him, and he is tempted forty days in the wilderness (vv. 9–13). He then preaches the kingdom of God in Galilee and calls Simon, Andrew, James, and John (vv. 14–20). A single Sabbath day in Capernaum follows — teaching with authority, casting out an unclean spirit, healing Simon’s mother-in-law, and healing many at evening — before a dawn prayer, a preaching tour of Galilee, and the cleansing of a leper who spreads the news despite being told to stay silent (vv. 21–45). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 1

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The gospel is good news about Jesus Christ, and its foundation is that he is the Son of God. Henry stresses that Christ is both the author and the subject of this gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Malachi's messenger is John the Baptist, sent to prepare Christ's way before him. The gospel begins exactly as the prophets said it would, showing one divine original for both testaments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Isaiah's voice crying in the wilderness calls for straight paths: crooked judgments rectified and crooked affections set right, so Christ may enter the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. John baptized in the wilderness and preached repentance as the way to remission of sins. Repentance and reformation were the duties required; forgiveness and sanctification were the blessings promised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. People from all Judea and Jerusalem went out to John, confessed their sins, and were baptized in the Jordan. In this willing crowd Henry sees the first beginnings of a gospel church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. John's camel's-hair clothing and food of locusts and wild honey bespoke self-denial and contempt of the world. The more we sit loose to the body and the world, the better we are prepared for Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. John preached one mightier than himself, whose shoe-latchet he was unworthy to loose. The greatest saints are the most humble, and John labours to give all honour to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. John baptized with water, but Christ would baptize with the Holy Ghost. That baptism purifies by grace and refreshes by comfort, which water alone could only signify. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Jesus came from Nazareth and was baptized by John in the Jordan. Henry reads this humility as Christ fulfilling all righteousness and sanctifying himself for our sakes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Coming up out of the water, Jesus saw the heavens opened and the Spirit descending like a dove upon him. Heaven's opening and the Spirit's descent together owned him as the Lord from heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The voice from heaven named Jesus the beloved Son, in whom God is well pleased. God loved him no less for his low estate, and delights in him as the referee between God and man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The Spirit immediately drove Jesus into the wilderness. Retirement for converse with God often follows great ordinations, and temptation follows baptism. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. For forty days he was tempted by Satan, was with the wild beasts, and was ministered to by angels. His preservation among beasts was an earnest of seasonable supply, and angel ministry comforts us against evil designs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. After John was put in prison, Jesus came into Galilee preaching the gospel of the kingdom of God. The silencing of one minister does not suppress the gospel; God raises up others to carry it on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Jesus announced that the time was fulfilled and the kingdom of God at hand, and called hearers to repent and believe. Repentance and faith must go together: reform without trust in Christ cannot save, nor trust without reformation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus saw Simon and Andrew casting a net, for they were fishers. Christ chose weak and lowly instruments, that the power might appear to be wholly God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Jesus called them to follow him and promised to make them fishers of men. Ministers cast the gospel net into the sea of the world to win lost souls to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Straightway they forsook their nets and followed him. Those Christ calls must sit loose to the world and forsake whatever is inconsistent with duty to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Going a little farther, Jesus saw James and John in the ship mending their nets. Christ puts honour on those who are diligent in their business and loving to one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He called them at once, and they left their father Zebedee in the ship with the hired servants and went after him. Even relations and companions must be left for Christ, though they left their father not without help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. They went into Capernaum, and straightway on the sabbath Jesus entered the synagogue and taught. He lost no time in his work and honoured the sabbath by sabbath-work rather than by niceties of rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The hearers were astonished at his doctrine, for he taught with authority and not as the scribes. The scribes repeated the law by rote, but Christ knew the mind of God and was commissioned to declare it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. In the synagogue there was a man with an unclean spirit, who cried out. The devil is called unclean because he has lost purity, opposes the Holy Spirit, and pollutes the spirits of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The spirit cried, let us alone, and confessed Jesus as the Holy One of God, come to destroy them. Devils believe and tremble, but have no hope in him; a bare notion of Christ goes no further than the devil does. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Jesus rebuked him, saying, hold thy peace, and come out of him. Christ disdains testimony from an unclean spirit and commands with authority; he will not let a confession cloak malicious designs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The unclean spirit tore the man and cried loudly, then came out of him. When Christ delivers souls from Satan, it is often not without a grievous toss and tumult in the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. All were amazed and questioned what new doctrine this was, since with authority he commanded even unclean spirits and they obeyed. Miracles confirmed doctrine and showed the design of his teaching: to conquer Satan and cure sick souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Immediately his fame spread throughout all the region round about Galilee. The report prepared his way, now that John his forerunner was imprisoned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Forthwith they left the synagogue and entered the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John. Christ's work moved from public teaching to private household mercy without pause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Simon's wife's mother lay sick of a fever, and at once they told Jesus of her. Bringing the sick to Christ by prayer and intercession is the proper use of acquaintance with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. He came, took her by the hand, lifted her up, and immediately the fever left her and she ministered to them. A complete cure restores to service, and gratitude shows itself in ministry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. At evening, when the sun had set, they brought to him all the diseased and those possessed with devils. The sabbath's end freed them to carry their sick, and Christ's day of teaching closed in healing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. All the city was gathered together at the door. The whole town's crowding shows both the misery that sought him and the fame his miracles had won. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. He healed many sick of divers diseases, cast out many devils, and suffered not the devils to speak because they knew him. Christ would not be witnessed to by devils, whose praise would dishonour him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. In the morning, rising a great while before day, he went into a solitary place and prayed. Before the press of work, Christ sought free converse with God in retirement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. Simon and those with him followed after him, pursuing him to his retreat. The disciples' eagerness shows how the crowds already pressed upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. When they found him they said, all men seek for thee. Popular demand urged him to stay, but it did not govern his movements. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. He answered, let us go into the next towns, that I may preach there also, for therefore came I forth. Preaching was his chief business; miracles confirmed it but did not replace it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. He preached in their synagogues throughout all Galilee and cast out devils. His doctrine and his war against Satan went together wherever he went. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. A leper came beseeching him, kneeling down, saying, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean. His prayer joins full faith in Christ's power with full submission to his will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, touched him, and said, I will; be thou clean. Compassion moved him to touch the untouchable, and his word effected what his touch signified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. As soon as he had spoken, immediately the leprosy departed from him and he was cleansed. The cure was instant and complete, proving the authority of the word that commanded it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. Jesus straitly charged him and forthwith sent him away, forbidding delay or display. Christ sought no applause for his works and ordered the man's conduct as well as his cure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. He was to say nothing to any man, but show himself to the priest and offer what Moses commanded, for a testimony to them. Obedience to the law gave the priests legal proof of the cleansing and of Christ's respect for Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. But the man went out and began to publish it much, so that Jesus could no more openly enter the city, but stayed in desert places, and people came to him from every quarter. Zeal without obedience hindered Christ's public work, yet it could not stop the crowds from seeking him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a parallel examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a possible parallel. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

A Sabbath day in Capernaum — Mark 1 and Luke 4

Mark 1:21–34 and Luke 4:31–41 tell the same Sabbath sequence: teaching in the synagogue, an unclean spirit crying out and cast out with authority, the healing of Simon’s wife’s mother of a fever, and healings at sunset when the Sabbath ends. The differences are in detail, not in outline: Mark names Simon and Andrew’s house and four disciples present, and says the fever left her and she ministered to them; Luke says Jesus stood over her and rebuked the fever, and that he laid his hands on every one of the sick brought at sunset. Whether the two accounts record one day remembered with different details, or the same pattern on separate occasions, is not settled by the sources consulted for this page.
