The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 65 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 I am sought of them that asked not for me; I am found of them that sought me not: I said, Behold me, behold me, unto a nation that was not called by my name.

2 I have spread out my hands all the day unto a rebellious people, which walketh in a way that was not good, after their own thoughts;

3 A people that provoketh me to anger continually to my face; that sacrificeth in gardens, and burneth incense upon altars of brick;

4 Which remain among the graves, and lodge in the monuments, which eat swine’s flesh, and broth of abominable things is in their vessels;

5 Which say, Stand by thyself, come not near to me; for I am holier than thou. These are a smoke in my nose, a fire that burneth all the day.

6 Behold, it is written before me: I will not keep silence, but will recompense, even recompense into their bosom,

7 Your iniquities, and the iniquities of your fathers together, saith the LORD, which have burned incense upon the mountains, and blasphemed me upon the hills: therefore will I measure their former work into their bosom.

8 Thus saith the LORD, As the new wine is found in the cluster, and one saith, Destroy it not; for a blessing is in it: so will I do for my servants’ sakes, that I may not destroy them all.

9 And I will bring forth a seed out of Jacob, and out of Judah an inheritor of my mountains: and mine elect shall inherit it, and my servants shall dwell there.

10 And Sharon shall be a fold of flocks, and the valley of Achor a place for the herds to lie down in, for my people that have sought me.

11 But ye are they that forsake the LORD, that forget my holy mountain, that prepare a table for that troop, and that furnish the drink offering unto that number.

12 Therefore will I number you to the sword, and ye shall all bow down to the slaughter: because when I called, ye did not answer; when I spake, ye did not hear; but did evil before mine eyes, and did choose that wherein I delighted not.

13 Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD, Behold, my servants shall eat, but ye shall be hungry: behold, my servants shall drink, but ye shall be thirsty: behold, my servants shall rejoice, but ye shall be ashamed:

14 Behold, my servants shall sing for joy of heart, but ye shall cry for sorrow of heart, and shall howl for vexation of spirit.

15 And ye shall leave your name for a curse unto my chosen: for the Lord GOD shall slay thee, and call his servants by another name:

16 That he who blesseth himself in the earth shall bless himself in the God of truth; and he that sweareth in the earth shall swear by the God of truth; because the former troubles are forgotten, and because they are hid from mine eyes.

17 For, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth: and the former shall not be remembered, nor come into mind.

18 But be ye glad and rejoice for ever in that which I create: for, behold, I create Jerusalem a rejoicing, and her people a joy.

19 And I will rejoice in Jerusalem, and joy in my people: and the voice of weeping shall be no more heard in her, nor the voice of crying.

20 There shall be no more thence an infant of days, nor an old man that hath not filled his days: for the child shall die an hundred years old; but the sinner being an hundred years old shall be accursed.

21 And they shall build houses, and inhabit them; and they shall plant vineyards, and eat the fruit of them.

22 They shall not build, and another inhabit; they shall not plant, and another eat: for as the days of a tree are the days of my people, and mine elect shall long enjoy the work of their hands.

23 They shall not labour in vain, nor bring forth for trouble; for they are the seed of the blessed of the LORD, and their offspring with them.

24 And it shall come to pass, that before they call, I will answer; and while they are yet speaking, I will hear.

25 The wolf and the lamb shall feed together, and the lion shall eat straw like the bullock: and dust shall be the serpent’s meat. They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain, saith the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God answers the prayer of chapter 64: he was found by those who did not seek him, while his own people rebelled, provoking him with idol feasts in gardens. The rebellious are judged, but a seed is preserved, and the chapter ends with new heavens and a new earth, where the wolf and the lamb feed together and nothing hurts or destroys in God's holy mountain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. I am sought of them that asked not for me; I am found of them that sought me not; I said, Behold me, behold me, unto a nation that was not called by my name. This chapter treats God's reply in justification of His dealings with Israel. In chapter 64 verse 9, their plea was, we are all Thy people. In answer, God declares that others, Gentiles, would be taken into covenant with Him, while His ancient people would be rejected. The Jews were slow to believe this; hence Paul says, as in Romans, that Isaiah was very bold in advancing so unpopular a sentiment; he implies what Paul states, as in Romans, that they are not all, in opposition to the Jews' plea, chapter 64 verse 9, Israel which are of Israel. God's reason for so severely dealing with Israel is not changeableness in Him, but sin in them, verses 2 to 7. Yet the whole nation shall not be destroyed, but only the wicked; a remnant shall be saved, verses 8 to 10 and 11 to 16. There shall be, finally, universal blessedness to Israel, such as they had prayed for, verses 17 to 25. I am sought is Hebrew, I have granted access unto Me to them, etc., as in Ezekiel and Ephesians. Found means Romans renders this, I was made manifest. As an instance of the sentiment in the clause, I am sought, etc., see John; of the sentiment in this clause, Acts. Compare as to the Gentile converts, Ephesians. Behold me recalls chapter 45. Nation not called by my name means that is, the Gentiles. God retorts in their own words, chapter 63 verse 19, that their plea as being exclusively called by His name will not avail, for God's gospel invitation is not so exclusive, as in Romans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. I have spread out my hands all the day unto a rebellious people, which walks in a way that was not good, after their own thoughts. Hands means inviting them earnestly, as in Proverbs. All day means continually, late and early, as in Jeremiah. Rebellious people means Israel, whose rebellion was the occasion of God's turning to the Gentiles, as in Romans. Way not good means that is, the very reverse of good, very bad, as in Ezekiel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. A people that provokes me to anger continually to my face; that sacrifices in gardens, and burns incense upon altars of brick. Continually means answering to all the day, verse 2. God was continually inviting them, and they continually offending Him, as in Deuteronomy. To my face means they made no attempt to hide their sin, as in chapter 3. Compare before Me, as in Exodus. In gardens recalls chapters 1 and 66 and Leviticus. Altars of brick is Hebrew, bricks. God had commanded His altars to be of unhewn stone, as in Exodus. This was in order to separate them, even in external respects, from idolaters; also, as all chiselling was forbidden, they could not inscribe superstitious symbols on them as the heathen did. Bricks were more easily so inscribed than stone; hence their use for the cuneiform inscriptions at Babylon, and also for idolatrous altars. Some, not so well, have supposed that the bricks here mean the flat brick-paved roofs of houses on which they sacrificed to the sun, etc., as in 2 Kings and Jeremiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Which remain among the graves, and lodge in the monuments, which eat swine's flesh, and broth of abominable things is in their vessels. Graves means namely, for purposes of necromancy, as if to hold converse with the dead, as in chapters 8 and Mark; or, for the sake of purifications, usually performed at night among sepulchres, to appease the manes. Monuments is Hebrew, pass the night in hidden recesses, either the idol's inmost shrines, consecrated precincts, where they used to sleep, in order to have divine communications in dreams, as Jerome; or better, on account of the parallel graves, sepulchral caves. Eat swine's flesh means to eat it at all was contrary to God's law, as in Leviticus, but it much increased their guilt that they ate it in idolatrous sacrifices, as in chapter 66. Varro says that swine were first used in sacrifices; the Latins sacrificed a pig to Ceres; it was also offered on occasion of treaties and marriages. Broth means so called from the pieces, margin, or fragments of bread over which the broth was poured; such broth, made of swine's flesh, offered in sacrifice, was thought to be especially acceptable to the idol and was used in magic rites. Or, fragments, pieces, of abominable foods, etc. This fourth clause explains more fully the third, as the second does the first. Is in is rather, literally, is their vessels, that is, constitute their vessels' contents. The Jews, in our Lord's days, and ever since the return from Babylon, have been free from idolatry; still the imagery from idolatrous abominations, as being the sin most loathsome in God's eyes and that most prevalent in Isaiah's time, is employed to describe the foul sin of Israel in all ages, culminating in their killing Messiah, and still rejecting Him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Which say, Stand by yourself, come not near to me; for I am holier than you. These are a smoke in my nose, a fire that burns all the day. This recalls Matthew, Luke, and Judges. Applicable to the hypocritical self-justifiers of our Lord's time. Smoke means alluding to the smoke of their self-righteous sacrifices; the fire of God's wrath was kindled at the sight, and exhibited itself in the smoke that breathed forth from His nostrils; in Hebrew the nose is the seat of anger; and the nostrils distended in wrath, as it were, breathe forth smoke, as in Psalm 18. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Behold, it is written before me; I will not keep silence, but will recompense, even recompense into their bosom. Written before me means it is decreed by Me, namely, what follows, as in Job; or, their guilt is recorded before Me, as in Daniel, Revelation, and Malachi. Into bosom recalls Psalm 79, Jeremiah, and Luke. The Orientals used the loose fold of the garment falling on the bosom or lap, as a receptacle for carrying things. The sense thus is: I will repay their sin so abundantly that the hand will not be able to receive it; it will need the spacious fold on the bosom to contain it. Rather it is, I will repay it to the very person from whom it has emanated. Compare God did render the evil of the men of Shechem upon their heads, as in Judges and Psalm 7. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Your iniquities, and the iniquities of your fathers together, says the Lord, which have burned incense upon the mountains, and blasphemed me upon the hills, therefore will I measure their former work into their bosom. Their sin had been accumulating from age to age until God at last repaid it in full. Mountains recalls chapters 57 and Ezekiel and Hosea. Their means Your had preceded. From speaking to, He speaks of them; this implies growing alienation from them and greater distance. Work means the full recompense of their work, as in chapter 49. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Thus says the Lord, As the new wine is found in the cluster, and one says, Destroy it not; for a blessing is in it; so will I do for my servants' sake, that I may not destroy them all. New wine means as if some grapes having good wine-producing juice in them, be found in a cluster which the vinedresser was about to throw away as bad, and one saith, etc. Blessing means that is, good wine-producing juice, as in Judges and Joel. So means God will spare the godly remnant, while the ungodly mass of the nation shall be destroyed, as in chapters 1, 6, 10, and 11. My servants means the godly remnant. But some read, for the sake of my servant, Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. I will bring forth a seed out of Jacob, and out of Judah an inheritor of my mountains, and my elect will inherit it, and my servants will dwell there. Seed means the holy seed, chapter 6 verse 13, a posterity from Jacob, designed to repossess the Holy Land, forfeited by the sin of the former Jews. My mountains means Jerusalem and the rest of Judea, peculiarly God's, as in chapters 2, 11, and 14. It means the Holy Land. Elect recalls verses 15 and 22. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Sharon will be a fold of flocks, and the valley of Achor a place for the herds to lie down in, for my people that have sought me. Sharon recalls chapters 33 and 35. Achor means meaning trouble; a valley near Jericho, so called from the trouble caused to Israel by Achan's sin, as in Joshua. The valley of Achor, proverbial for whatever caused calamity, shall become proverbial for joy and prosperity, as in Hosea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. But you are they that forsake the Lord, that forget my holy mountain, that prepare a table for that troop, and that furnish the drink offering unto that number. Holy mountain means Moriah, on which the temple was. Troop is rather Gad, the Babylonian god of fortune, the planet Jupiter, answering to Baal or Bel; the Arabs called it the Greater Good Fortune; and the planet Venus answering to Meni, the Lesser Good Fortune. Tables were laid out for their idols with all kinds of viands, and a cup containing a mixture of wine and honey, in Egypt especially, on the last day of the year, as Jerome. Drink offering is rather, mixed drink. Number is rather, Meni; as goddess of fortune she was thought to number the fates of men. Some understand Gad to be the sun; Meni the moon, or Ashtaroth or Astarte, as in 1 Kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Therefore will I number you to the sword, and you will all bow down to the slaughter, because when I called, you did not answer; when I spoke, you did not hear, but did evil before my eyes, and chose that wherein I delighted not. Number means doom you. Alluding to the number, as Meni, verse 11, means. Retribution in kind, the punishment answering to the sin, as in 2 Chronicles. I called, ye not answer means I called, though none had called upon Me, chapter 64 verse 7; yet even then none answered, as in Proverbs. Contrast with this God and His people's mutual fellowship in prayer, verse 24. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Therefore thus says the Lord God, Behold, my servants will eat, but you will be hungry; behold, my servants will drink, but you will be thirsty; behold, my servants will rejoice, but you will be ashamed. Eat means enjoy all blessings from me, as in Song of Solomon. Hungry recalls Amos. This may refer to the siege of Jerusalem under Titus, when 1,100,000 are said to have perished by famine; thus verse 15 will refer to God's people without distinction of Jew and Gentile receiving another name, namely, that of Christians. A further fulfilment may still remain, just before the creation of the new heavens and earth, as the context, verse 17, implies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Behold, my servants will sing for joy of heart, but you will cry for sorrow of heart, and will howl for vexation of spirit. Howl recalls chapters 15 and Matthew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. You will leave your name for a curse unto my chosen, for the Lord God will slay you, and call his servants by another name. Curse means the name of Jew has been for long a formula of execration, as in Jeremiah; if one wishes to curse another, he can utter nothing worse than this, God make thee what the Jew is! Contrast the formula, as in Genesis. My chosen means the elect church, gathered from Jews and Gentiles, called by another name, Christians, as in Acts. However, as My chosen, or elect, in verse 9, refers to the seed of Jacob, the believing Jews, hereafter about to possess their land, as in verses 19 and 22, are ultimately meant by My chosen, as contrasted with the unbelieving Jews, ye. These elect Jews shall be called by another, or a new name, that is, shall no longer be forsaken of God for unbelief, but shall be His delight and married to Him, as in chapter 62. Thee means unbelieving Israel. Isaiah here speaks of God, whereas in the preceding sentences God Himself spake. This change of persons marks without design how completely the prophet realised God with him and in him, so that he passes, without formally announcing it, from God's words to his own, and vice versa, both alike being from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. That he who blesses himself in the earth will bless himself in the God of truth, and he that swears in the earth will swear by the God of truth, because the former troubles are forgotten, and because they are hid from my eyes. That he is rather, he who, etc. Blesseth, etc. recalls Psalm 72 and Jeremiah. God of truth means very God, as opposed to false gods; Hebrew, Amen: the very name of Messiah, as in 2 Corinthians and Revelation, faithful to His promises, as in John. Real, substantial, spiritual, eternal, as opposed to the shadowy types of the law. Sweareth, etc. means God alone shall be appealed to as God, as in chapters 19 and Deuteronomy and Psalm 63. Troubles means that is, sins, provocations. Rather, calamities caused by your sins; so far from these visiting you again, the very remembrance of them is hid from Mine eyes by the magnitude of the blessings I will confer on you, as in verse 17, etc. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth, and the former will not be remembered, nor come into mind. As Caleb inherited the same land which his feet trod on, as in Deuteronomy and Joshua, so Messiah and His saints shall inherit the renovated earth which once they trod while defiled by the enemy, as in chapters 34, 51, and 66, Ezekiel, Psalms, 2 Peter, Hebrews, and Revelation. Not be remembered means see verse 16, note on troubles; the words here answer to the former forgotten, etc. The former sorrows of the earth, under the fall, shall be so far from recurring, that their very remembrance shall be obliterated by the many mercies I will bestow on the new earth, as in Revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. But be glad and rejoice for ever in that which I create, for, behold, I create Jerusalem a rejoicing, and her people a joy. Jerusalem recalls chapter 51. Everlasting joy, Zion. Spiritually, as in 1 Thessalonians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. I will rejoice in Jerusalem, and joy in my people, and the voice of weeping will be no more heard in her, nor the voice of crying. This recalls chapter 62. Weeping no more recalls chapters 25 and 35 and Revelation, primarily, foretold of Jerusalem; secondarily, of all the redeemed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. There will be no more thence an infant of days, nor an old man that has not filled his days, for the child will die a hundred years old, but the sinner being a hundred years old will be accursed. The longevity of men in the first age of the world shall be enjoyed again. Thence means from that time forward. Infant of days means that is, an infant who shall only complete a few days; short-lived. Filled days means none shall die without attaining a full old age. Child die hundred years means that is, he that dieth an hundred years old shall die a mere child. Sinner hundred be accursed means the sinner that dieth at an hundred years shall be deemed accursed, that is, his death at so early an age, which in those days the hundredth year will be regarded, just as if it were mere childhood, shall be deemed the effect of God's special visitation in wrath. This passage proves that the better age to come on earth, though much superior to the present will not be a perfect state; sin and death shall have place in it, as in Revelation, but much less frequently than now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. They will build houses, and inhabit them, and they will plant vineyards, and eat the fruit of them. This recalls chapter 62 and Amos. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. They will not build, and another inhabit; they will not plant, and another eat, for as the days of a tree are the days of my people, and my elect will long enjoy the work of their hands. They shall not experience the curse pronounced, as in Leviticus and Deuteronomy. Tree means among the most long-lived of objects in nature. They shall live as long as the trees they plant, as in chapter 61 and Psalm 92. Enjoy is Hebrew, consume, wear out; they shall live to enjoy the last of it, as in chapter 62. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. They will not labour in vain, nor bring forth for trouble, for they are the seed of the blessed of the Lord, and their offspring with them. Bring forth for trouble is literally, for terror, that is, They shall not bring forth children for a sudden death, as in Leviticus and Jeremiah. Seed blessed recalls chapter 61. Offspring with them recalls Hosea: Their offspring shall be with themselves; not brought forth only to be cut off by sudden death, as in the parallel clause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. It will come to pass, that before they call, I will answer, and while they are yet speaking, I will hear. Calletb, etc.; and see verse 12, I called, ye did not answer. Some translate, They shall hardly, literally, not yet, call, when, literally, and, I will answer; they shall be still speaking, when I will hear, as in Psalm 32 and Daniel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The wolf and the lamb will feed together, and the lion will eat straw like the bullock, and dust will be the serpent's meat. They will not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain, says the Lord. This recalls chapter 11. And the lion shall eat straw like the bullock recalls chapter 11. And dust is rather, but dust, etc. The curse shall remain on the serpent, as in Genesis and Micah. To lick the dust is figurative of the utter and perpetual degradation of Satan and his emissaries, as in chapters 49 and Psalm 72. Satan fell self-tempted; therefore no atonement was contrived for him, as there was for man, who fell by his temptation, as in Jude and John. From his peculiar connection with the earth and man, it has been conjectured that the exciting cause of his rebellion was God's declaration that human nature was to be raised into union with the Godhead; this was the truth concerning the person of the Son of God which he abode not in; it galled his pride that a lower race was to be raised to that which he had aspired to, as in 1 Timothy. How exultingly he might say, when man fell through him, God would raise manhood into union with Himself; I have brought it down below the beasts by sin! At that very moment and spot he was told that the seed of the abhorred race, man, should bruise his head, as in 1 John. He was raised up for this, to show forth God's glory, as in Exodus and Romans. In his unfallen state he may have been God's vicegerent over the earth and the animal kingdom before man: this will account for his assuming the form of a serpent, as in Genesis. Man succeeded to that office, as in Genesis, but forfeited it by sin, whence Satan became prince of this world; Jesus Christ supplants the usurper, and as Son of man regains the lost inheritance, as in Psalm 8. The steps in Satan's overthrow are these: he is cast out, first, from heaven, as in Revelation, on earth; next, he is bound a thousand years, as in Revelation; finally, he is cast into the lake of fire for ever, as in Revelation. The serpent's meat recalls chapter 11. They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain recalls chapter 11. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 5. Which say, Stand by yourself, come not near to me; for I am holier than you. These are a smoke in my nose, a fire that burns all the day. This recalls Matthew, Luke, and Judges. Applicable to the hypocritical self-justifiers of our Lord's time. Smoke means alluding to the smoke of their self-righteous sacrifices; the fire of God's wrath was kindled at the sight, and exhibited itself in the smoke that breathed forth from His nostrils; in Hebrew the nose is the seat of anger; and the nostrils distended in wrath, as it were, breathe forth smoke, as in Psalm 18. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Behold, it is written before me; I will not keep silence, but will recompense, even recompense into their bosom. Written before me means it is decreed by Me, namely, what follows, as in Job; or, their guilt is recorded before Me, as in Daniel, Revelation, and Malachi. Into bosom recalls Psalm 79, Jeremiah, and Luke. The Orientals used the loose fold of the garment falling on the bosom or lap, as a receptacle for carrying things. The sense thus is: I will repay their sin so abundantly that the hand will not be able to receive it; it will need the spacious fold on the bosom to contain it. Rather it is, I will repay it to the very person from whom it has emanated. Compare God did render the evil of the men of Shechem upon their heads, as in Judges and Psalm 7. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Your iniquities, and the iniquities of your fathers together, says the Lord, which have burned incense upon the mountains, and blasphemed me upon the hills, therefore will I measure their former work into their bosom. Their sin had been accumulating from age to age until God at last repaid it in full. Mountains recalls chapters 57 and Ezekiel and Hosea. Their means Your had preceded. From speaking to, He speaks of them; this implies growing alienation from them and greater distance. Work means the full recompense of their work, as in chapter 49. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Thus says the Lord, As the new wine is found in the cluster, and one says, Destroy it not; for a blessing is in it; so will I do for my servants' sake, that I may not destroy them all. New wine means as if some grapes having good wine-producing juice in them, be found in a cluster which the vinedresser was about to throw away as bad, and one saith, etc. Blessing means that is, good wine-producing juice, as in Judges and Joel. So means God will spare the godly remnant, while the ungodly mass of the nation shall be destroyed, as in chapters 1, 6, 10, and 11. My servants means the godly remnant. But some read, for the sake of my servant, Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. I will bring forth a seed out of Jacob, and out of Judah an inheritor of my mountains, and my elect will inherit it, and my servants will dwell there. Seed means the holy seed, chapter 6 verse 13, a posterity from Jacob, designed to repossess the Holy Land, forfeited by the sin of the former Jews. My mountains means Jerusalem and the rest of Judea, peculiarly God's, as in chapters 2, 11, and 14. It means the Holy Land. Elect recalls verses 15 and 22. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Sharon will be a fold of flocks, and the valley of Achor a place for the herds to lie down in, for my people that have sought me. Sharon recalls chapters 33 and 35. Achor means meaning trouble; a valley near Jericho, so called from the trouble caused to Israel by Achan's sin, as in Joshua. The valley of Achor, proverbial for whatever caused calamity, shall become proverbial for joy and prosperity, as in Hosea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. But you are they that forsake the Lord, that forget my holy mountain, that prepare a table for that troop, and that furnish the drink offering unto that number. Holy mountain means Moriah, on which the temple was. Troop is rather Gad, the Babylonian god of fortune, the planet Jupiter, answering to Baal or Bel; the Arabs called it the Greater Good Fortune; and the planet Venus answering to Meni, the Lesser Good Fortune. Tables were laid out for their idols with all kinds of viands, and a cup containing a mixture of wine and honey, in Egypt especially, on the last day of the year, as Jerome. Drink offering is rather, mixed drink. Number is rather, Meni; as goddess of fortune she was thought to number the fates of men. Some understand Gad to be the sun; Meni the moon, or Ashtaroth or Astarte, as in 1 Kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Therefore will I number you to the sword, and you will all bow down to the slaughter, because when I called, you did not answer; when I spoke, you did not hear, but did evil before my eyes, and chose that wherein I delighted not. Number means doom you. Alluding to the number, as Meni, verse 11, means. Retribution in kind, the punishment answering to the sin, as in 2 Chronicles. I called, ye not answer means I called, though none had called upon Me, chapter 64 verse 7; yet even then none answered, as in Proverbs. Contrast with this God and His people's mutual fellowship in prayer, verse 24. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Therefore thus says the Lord God, Behold, my servants will eat, but you will be hungry; behold, my servants will drink, but you will be thirsty; behold, my servants will rejoice, but you will be ashamed. Eat means enjoy all blessings from me, as in Song of Solomon. Hungry recalls Amos. This may refer to the siege of Jerusalem under Titus, when 1,100,000 are said to have perished by famine; thus verse 15 will refer to God's people without distinction of Jew and Gentile receiving another name, namely, that of Christians. A further fulfilment may still remain, just before the creation of the new heavens and earth, as the context, verse 17, implies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Behold, my servants will sing for joy of heart, but you will cry for sorrow of heart, and will howl for vexation of spirit. Howl recalls chapters 15 and Matthew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. You will leave your name for a curse unto my chosen, for the Lord God will slay you, and call his servants by another name. Curse means the name of Jew has been for long a formula of execration, as in Jeremiah; if one wishes to curse another, he can utter nothing worse than this, God make thee what the Jew is! Contrast the formula, as in Genesis. My chosen means the elect church, gathered from Jews and Gentiles, called by another name, Christians, as in Acts. However, as My chosen, or elect, in verse 9, refers to the seed of Jacob, the believing Jews, hereafter about to possess their land, as in verses 19 and 22, are ultimately meant by My chosen, as contrasted with the unbelieving Jews, ye. These elect Jews shall be called by another, or a new name, that is, shall no longer be forsaken of God for unbelief, but shall be His delight and married to Him, as in chapter 62. Thee means unbelieving Israel. Isaiah here speaks of God, whereas in the preceding sentences God Himself spake. This change of persons marks without design how completely the prophet realised God with him and in him, so that he passes, without formally announcing it, from God's words to his own, and vice versa, both alike being from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. That he who blesses himself in the earth will bless himself in the God of truth, and he that swears in the earth will swear by the God of truth, because the former troubles are forgotten, and because they are hid from my eyes. That he is rather, he who, etc. Blesseth, etc. recalls Psalm 72 and Jeremiah. God of truth means very God, as opposed to false gods; Hebrew, Amen: the very name of Messiah, as in 2 Corinthians and Revelation, faithful to His promises, as in John. Real, substantial, spiritual, eternal, as opposed to the shadowy types of the law. Sweareth, etc. means God alone shall be appealed to as God, as in chapters 19 and Deuteronomy and Psalm 63. Troubles means that is, sins, provocations. Rather, calamities caused by your sins; so far from these visiting you again, the very remembrance of them is hid from Mine eyes by the magnitude of the blessings I will confer on you, as in verse 17, etc. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth, and the former will not be remembered, nor come into mind. As Caleb inherited the same land which his feet trod on, as in Deuteronomy and Joshua, so Messiah and His saints shall inherit the renovated earth which once they trod while defiled by the enemy, as in chapters 34, 51, and 66, Ezekiel, Psalms, 2 Peter, Hebrews, and Revelation. Not be remembered means see verse 16, note on troubles; the words here answer to the former forgotten, etc. The former sorrows of the earth, under the fall, shall be so far from recurring, that their very remembrance shall be obliterated by the many mercies I will bestow on the new earth, as in Revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. But be glad and rejoice for ever in that which I create, for, behold, I create Jerusalem a rejoicing, and her people a joy. Jerusalem recalls chapter 51. Everlasting joy, Zion. Spiritually, as in 1 Thessalonians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. I will rejoice in Jerusalem, and joy in my people, and the voice of weeping will be no more heard in her, nor the voice of crying. This recalls chapter 62. Weeping no more recalls chapters 25 and 35 and Revelation, primarily, foretold of Jerusalem; secondarily, of all the redeemed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. There will be no more thence an infant of days, nor an old man that has not filled his days, for the child will die a hundred years old, but the sinner being a hundred years old will be accursed. The longevity of men in the first age of the world shall be enjoyed again. Thence means from that time forward. Infant of days means that is, an infant who shall only complete a few days; short-lived. Filled days means none shall die without attaining a full old age. Child die hundred years means that is, he that dieth an hundred years old shall die a mere child. Sinner hundred be accursed means the sinner that dieth at an hundred years shall be deemed accursed, that is, his death at so early an age, which in those days the hundredth year will be regarded, just as if it were mere childhood, shall be deemed the effect of God's special visitation in wrath. This passage proves that the better age to come on earth, though much superior to the present will not be a perfect state; sin and death shall have place in it, as in Revelation, but much less frequently than now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. They will build houses, and inhabit them, and they will plant vineyards, and eat the fruit of them. This recalls chapter 62 and Amos. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. They will not build, and another inhabit; they will not plant, and another eat, for as the days of a tree are the days of my people, and my elect will long enjoy the work of their hands. They shall not experience the curse pronounced, as in Leviticus and Deuteronomy. Tree means among the most long-lived of objects in nature. They shall live as long as the trees they plant, as in chapter 61 and Psalm 92. Enjoy is Hebrew, consume, wear out; they shall live to enjoy the last of it, as in chapter 62. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. They will not labour in vain, nor bring forth for trouble, for they are the seed of the blessed of the Lord, and their offspring with them. Bring forth for trouble is literally, for terror, that is, They shall not bring forth children for a sudden death, as in Leviticus and Jeremiah. Seed blessed recalls chapter 61. Offspring with them recalls Hosea: Their offspring shall be with themselves; not brought forth only to be cut off by sudden death, as in the parallel clause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. It will come to pass, that before they call, I will answer, and while they are yet speaking, I will hear. Calletb, etc.; and see verse 12, I called, ye did not answer. Some translate, They shall hardly, literally, not yet, call, when, literally, and, I will answer; they shall be still speaking, when I will hear, as in Psalm 32 and Daniel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The wolf and the lamb will feed together, and the lion will eat straw like the bullock, and dust will be the serpent's meat. They will not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain, says the Lord. This recalls chapter 11. And the lion shall eat straw like the bullock recalls chapter 11. And dust is rather, but dust, etc. The curse shall remain on the serpent, as in Genesis and Micah. To lick the dust is figurative of the utter and perpetual degradation of Satan and his emissaries, as in chapters 49 and Psalm 72. Satan fell self-tempted; therefore no atonement was contrived for him, as there was for man, who fell by his temptation, as in Jude and John. From his peculiar connection with the earth and man, it has been conjectured that the exciting cause of his rebellion was God's declaration that human nature was to be raised into union with the Godhead; this was the truth concerning the person of the Son of God which he abode not in; it galled his pride that a lower race was to be raised to that which he had aspired to, as in 1 Timothy. How exultingly he might say, when man fell through him, God would raise manhood into union with Himself; I have brought it down below the beasts by sin! At that very moment and spot he was told that the seed of the abhorred race, man, should bruise his head, as in 1 John. He was raised up for this, to show forth God's glory, as in Exodus and Romans. In his unfallen state he may have been God's vicegerent over the earth and the animal kingdom before man: this will account for his assuming the form of a serpent, as in Genesis. Man succeeded to that office, as in Genesis, but forfeited it by sin, whence Satan became prince of this world; Jesus Christ supplants the usurper, and as Son of man regains the lost inheritance, as in Psalm 8. The steps in Satan's overthrow are these: he is cast out, first, from heaven, as in Revelation, on earth; next, he is bound a thousand years, as in Revelation; finally, he is cast into the lake of fire for ever, as in Revelation. The serpent's meat recalls chapter 11. They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain recalls chapter 11. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
