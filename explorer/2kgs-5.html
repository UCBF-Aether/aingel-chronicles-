The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now Naaman, captain of the host of the king of Syria, was a great man with his master, and honourable, because by him the LORD had given deliverance unto Syria: he was also a mighty man in valour, but he was a leper.

2 And the Syrians had gone out by companies, and had brought away captive out of the land of Israel a little maid; and she waited on Naaman’s wife.

3 And she said unto her mistress, Would God my lord were with the prophet that is in Samaria! for he would recover him of his leprosy.

4 And one went in, and told his lord, saying, Thus and thus said the maid that is of the land of Israel.

5 And the king of Syria said, Go to, go, and I will send a letter unto the king of Israel. And he departed, and took with him ten talents of silver, and six thousand pieces of gold, and ten changes of raiment.

6 And he brought the letter to the king of Israel, saying, Now when this letter is come unto thee, behold, I have therewith sent Naaman my servant to thee, that thou mayest recover him of his leprosy.

7 And it came to pass, when the king of Israel had read the letter, that he rent his clothes, and said, Am I God, to kill and to make alive, that this man doth send unto me to recover a man of his leprosy? wherefore consider, I pray you, and see how he seeketh a quarrel against me.

8 And it was so, when Elisha the man of God had heard that the king of Israel had rent his clothes, that he sent to the king, saying, Wherefore hast thou rent thy clothes? let him come now to me, and he shall know that there is a prophet in Israel.

9 So Naaman came with his horses and with his chariot, and stood at the door of the house of Elisha.

10 And Elisha sent a messenger unto him, saying, Go and wash in Jordan seven times, and thy flesh shall come again to thee, and thou shalt be clean.

11 But Naaman was wroth, and went away, and said, Behold, I thought, He will surely come out to me, and stand, and call on the name of the LORD his God, and strike his hand over the place, and recover the leper.

12 Are not Abana and Pharpar, rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? may I not wash in them, and be clean? So he turned and went away in a rage.

13 And his servants came near, and spake unto him, and said, My father, if the prophet had bid thee do some great thing, wouldest thou not have done it? how much rather then, when he saith to thee, Wash, and be clean?

14 Then went he down, and dipped himself seven times in Jordan, according to the saying of the man of God: and his flesh came again like unto the flesh of a little child, and he was clean.

15 And he returned to the man of God, he and all his company, and came, and stood before him: and he said, Behold, now I know that there is no God in all the earth, but in Israel: now therefore, I pray thee, take a blessing of thy servant.

16 But he said, As the LORD liveth, before whom I stand, I will receive none. And he urged him to take it; but he refused.

17 And Naaman said, Shall there not then, I pray thee, be given to thy servant two mules’ burden of earth? for thy servant will henceforth offer neither burnt offering nor sacrifice unto other gods, but unto the LORD.

18 In this thing the LORD pardon thy servant, that when my master goeth into the house of Rimmon to worship there, and he leaneth on my hand, and I bow myself in the house of Rimmon: when I bow down myself in the house of Rimmon, the LORD pardon thy servant in this thing.

19 And he said unto him, Go in peace. So he departed from him a little way.

20 But Gehazi, the servant of Elisha the man of God, said, Behold, my master hath spared Naaman this Syrian, in not receiving at his hands that which he brought: but, as the LORD liveth, I will run after him, and take somewhat of him.

21 So Gehazi followed after Naaman. And when Naaman saw him running after him, he lighted down from the chariot to meet him, and said, Is all well?

22 And he said, All is well. My master hath sent me, saying, Behold, even now there be come to me from mount Ephraim two young men of the sons of the prophets: give them, I pray thee, a talent of silver, and two changes of garments.

23 And Naaman said, Be content, take two talents. And he urged him, and bound two talents of silver in two bags, with two changes of garments, and laid them upon two of his servants; and they bare them before him.

24 And when he came to the tower, he took them from their hand, and bestowed them in the house: and he let the men go, and they departed.

25 But he went in, and stood before his master. And Elisha said unto him, Whence comest thou, Gehazi? And he said, Thy servant went no whither.

26 And he said unto him, Went not mine heart with thee, when the man turned again from his chariot to meet thee? Is it a time to receive money, and to receive garments, and oliveyards, and vineyards, and sheep, and oxen, and menservants, and maidservants?

27 The leprosy therefore of Naaman shall cleave unto thee, and unto thy seed for ever. And he went out from his presence a leper as white as snow.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Naaman, Syria's great commander, comes to Elisha for his leprosy and is told to wash seven times in the Jordan; he rages at the simplicity of it, is persuaded by his servants, washes, and is clean. He offers gifts Elisha refuses, and Gehazi, chasing the gifts, takes Naaman's leprosy for himself. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Naaman, captain of Syria's host, was great and honourable with his master because the LORD had given deliverance to Syria by him, yet he was a leper. Henry notes no man's greatness can place him out of reach of life's sorest calamities: every man has some blemish, some damp to his joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The Syrians had brought away captive from Israel a little maid, who waited on Naaman's wife. Henry observes that though only a girl, she could give an account of the famous prophet among the Israelites, and children should early be told of God's wondrous works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The maid said to her mistress, "Would God my lord were with the prophet that is in Samaria! for he would recover him of his leprosy." Henry praises her as a good servant who desired her master's welfare though a captive, and notes servants may bless families by telling what they know of God's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. One went in and told Naaman what the maid from Israel had said. Henry notes Naaman did not despise her words because of her meanness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The king of Syria sent Naaman with a letter to the king of Israel, bearing ten talents of silver, six thousand pieces of gold, and ten changes of raiment. Henry's lesson is that those who seek the blessings the LORD sends must come as beggars for a free gift, not as lords to demand or purchase. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The letter said the king had sent Naaman so the king of Israel might recover him of his leprosy. Henry frames this as a demand made in worldly terms, expecting a cure to be commanded like a court favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. When the king of Israel read the letter he rent his clothes, asking, "Am I God, to kill and to make alive, that this man doth send unto me to recover a man of his leprosy?" Henry notes the king saw only the impossible demand, not the prophet in his land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. When Elisha heard the king had rent his clothes, he sent to ask why, and bade the king let Naaman come to him so he would know there was a prophet in Israel. Henry presents Elisha as the true channel of the healing the king despaired of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Naaman came with horses and chariot and stood at Elisha's door. Henry notes Elisha knew Naaman to be a proud man, and would let him know that before the great God all men stand on the same level. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Elisha sent a messenger telling Naaman to wash in Jordan seven times, and his flesh would come again and he would be clean. Henry observes God's commands make trial of men's spirits, especially those directing a sinner how to apply for salvation's blessings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Naaman was wroth and went away, saying he had thought Elisha would come out, stand, call on his God, and strike his hand over the place. Henry sees in him the folly of pride: a cure will not content him unless he is cured with pomp and parade, and he scorns to be healed unless humoured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Naaman asked whether Abana and Pharpar, rivers of Damascus, were not better than all Israel's waters, and whether he might not wash in them and be clean. Henry notes human wisdom thinks it can supply wiser methods of cleansing than God's plain way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. His servants reasoned that if the prophet had bid him do some great thing he would have done it, so how much rather should he do it when told simply to wash and be clean. Henry advises masters to hear reason, and to have ears open to good advice even from those below them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Naaman went down and dipped himself seven times in Jordan according to the man of God's word, and his flesh came again like a little child's, and he was clean. Henry's summary is plain: "Believe, and be saved; Repent, and be pardoned; Wash, and be clean" — the believer applies for salvation without neglecting, altering, or adding to the Saviour's directions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Naaman returned to Elisha with all his company, confessing that now he knew there was no God in all the earth but in Israel, and begging Elisha to take a blessing. Henry notes the mercy of the cure affected Naaman more than the miracle, and those who experience divine grace are best able to speak of its power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Elisha swore by the LORD that he would receive none, and though Naaman urged him, he refused. Henry explains Elisha refused not because receiving was unlawful, but to show the new convert that God's servants looked on worldly wealth with holy contempt, and the whole work was from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Naaman asked for two mules' burden of earth, for he would henceforth offer sacrifice to no god but the LORD. Henry notes it is not well violently to oppose the lesser mistakes that unite with men's first convictions, for we cannot bring men forward faster than the LORD prepares them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Naaman asked the LORD's pardon for bowing in the house of Rimmon when his master leaned on his hand there. Henry warns that if, in covenanting with God, we desire to reserve any known sin and continue indulging it, that is a breach of his covenant, and those who truly hate evil will abstain from all appearances of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Elisha said, "Go in peace," and Naaman departed a little way. Henry treats this dismissal as patient allowance for a new convert's weakness, not approval of reserved sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Gehazi, Elisha's servant, said his master had spared Naaman the Syrian, taking nothing, and swore he would run after him and take somewhat of him. Henry contrasts him with Naaman's wise and good servants: Elisha, a holy prophet, has but one servant, and he proves a base liar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Gehazi followed Naaman, who lighted down from his chariot to meet him and asked if all was well. Henry notes the love of money, the root of all evil, was at the bottom of Gehazi's sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Gehazi lied that his master had sent him for a talent of silver and two changes of garments for two young prophets newly come from Mount Ephraim. Henry observes he thought to impose on the prophet, but soon found the Spirit of prophecy could not be deceived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Naaman urged Gehazi to take two talents, binding them in two bags with two changes of garments, and sending two servants to bear them. Henry warns it is folly to presume upon sin in hopes of secrecy, for conscience and the eye of God go with us into every by-path. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. When Gehazi came to the tower he took the gifts from the servants' hands, hid them in the house, and sent the men away. Henry notes he that covers his sin shall not prosper, and a lying tongue is but for a moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Gehazi went in and stood before his master, and when Elisha asked whence he came, he said he had gone nowhere. Henry stresses it was in vain to lie to the Holy Ghost, and all carnal contrivances are open before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Elisha asked whether his heart had not gone with Gehazi when Naaman turned from his chariot to meet him, and whether it was a time to receive money, garments, oliveyards, vineyards, sheep, oxen, and servants. Henry warns it is not a time to increase wealth by ways dishonourable to God and religion or injurious to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Elisha declared Naaman's leprosy would cleave to Gehazi and his seed for ever, and Gehazi went out from his presence a leper as white as snow. Henry asks what Gehazi was profited, gaining two talents but losing his health, honour, peace, service, and, without repentance, his soul for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms
