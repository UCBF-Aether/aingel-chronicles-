The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 But a certain man named Ananias, with Sapphira his wife, sold a possession,

2 And kept back part of the price, his wife also being privy to it, and brought a certain part, and laid it at the apostles’ feet.

3 But Peter said, Ananias, why hath Satan filled thine heart to lie to the Holy Ghost, and to keep back part of the price of the land?

4 Whiles it remained, was it not thine own? and after it was sold, was it not in thine own power? why hast thou conceived this thing in thine heart? thou hast not lied unto men, but unto God.

5 And Ananias hearing these words fell down, and gave up the ghost: and great fear came on all them that heard these things.

6 And the young men arose, wound him up, and carried him out, and buried him.

7 And it was about the space of three hours after, when his wife, not knowing what was done, came in.

8 And Peter answered unto her, Tell me whether ye sold the land for so much? And she said, Yea, for so much.

9 Then Peter said unto her, How is it that ye have agreed together to tempt the Spirit of the Lord? behold, the feet of them which have buried thy husband are at the door, and shall carry thee out.

10 Then fell she down straightway at his feet, and yielded up the ghost: and the young men came in, and found her dead, and, carrying her forth, buried her by her husband.

11 And great fear came upon all the church, and upon as many as heard these things.

12 And by the hands of the apostles were many signs and wonders wrought among the people; (and they were all with one accord in Solomon’s porch.

13 And of the rest durst no man join himself to them: but the people magnified them.

14 And believers were the more added to the Lord, multitudes both of men and women.)

15 Insomuch that they brought forth the sick into the streets, and laid them on beds and couches, that at the least the shadow of Peter passing by might overshadow some of them.

16 There came also a multitude out of the cities round about unto Jerusalem, bringing sick folks, and them which were vexed with unclean spirits: and they were healed every one.

17 Then the high priest rose up, and all they that were with him, (which is the sect of the Sadducees,) and were filled with indignation,

18 And laid their hands on the apostles, and put them in the common prison.

19 But the angel of the Lord by night opened the prison doors, and brought them forth, and said,

20 Go, stand and speak in the temple to the people all the words of this life.

21 And when they heard that, they entered into the temple early in the morning, and taught. But the high priest came, and they that were with him, and called the council together, and all the senate of the children of Israel, and sent to the prison to have them brought.

22 But when the officers came, and found them not in the prison, they returned and told,

23 Saying, The prison truly found we shut with all safety, and the keepers standing without before the doors: but when we had opened, we found no man within.

24 Now when the high priest and the captain of the temple and the chief priests heard these things, they doubted of them whereunto this would grow.

25 Then came one and told them, saying, Behold, the men whom ye put in prison are standing in the temple, and teaching the people.

26 Then went the captain with the officers, and brought them without violence: for they feared the people, lest they should have been stoned.

27 And when they had brought them, they set them before the council: and the high priest asked them,

28 Saying, Did not we straitly command you that ye should not teach in this name? and, behold, ye have filled Jerusalem with your doctrine, and intend to bring this man’s blood upon us.

29 Then Peter and the other apostles answered and said, We ought to obey God rather than men.

30 The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom ye slew and hanged on a tree.

31 Him hath God exalted with his right hand to be a Prince and a Saviour, for to give repentance to Israel, and forgiveness of sins.

32 And we are his witnesses of these things; and so is also the Holy Ghost, whom God hath given to them that obey him.

33 When they heard that, they were cut to the heart, and took counsel to slay them.

34 Then stood there up one in the council, a Pharisee, named Gamaliel, a doctor of the law, had in reputation among all the people, and commanded to put the apostles forth a little space;

35 And said unto them, Ye men of Israel, take heed to yourselves what ye intend to do as touching these men.

36 For before these days rose up Theudas, boasting himself to be somebody; to whom a number of men, about four hundred, joined themselves: who was slain; and all, as many as obeyed him, were scattered, and brought to nought.

37 After this man rose up Judas of Galilee in the days of the taxing, and drew away much people after him: he also perished; and all, even as many as obeyed him, were dispersed.

38 And now I say unto you, Refrain from these men, and let them alone: for if this counsel or this work be of men, it will come to nought:

39 But if it be of God, ye cannot overthrow it; lest haply ye be found even to fight against God.

40 And to him they agreed: and when they had called the apostles, and beaten them, they commanded that they should not speak in the name of Jesus, and let them go.

41 And they departed from the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer shame for his name.

42 And daily in the temple, and in every house, they ceased not to teach and preach Jesus Christ.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ananias and Sapphira pretend to give the whole price of their land and fall dead after Peter confronts their lie to the Holy Spirit, and great fear falls on the church. Signs multiply, the apostles are imprisoned and freed by an angel, and before the council they answer that they must obey God rather than men; Gamaliel counsels restraint, and they leave rejoicing to suffer shame for Jesus' name. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. A certain man named Ananias, with Sapphira his wife, sold a possession. Henry diagnoses the root sin at once: they were ambitious of being thought eminent disciples when they were not true disciples, hypocrites who deny themselves in one instance with a prospect of finding their account in something else. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Ananias kept back part of the price, his wife also being privy to it, and brought a certain part to the apostles' feet. Henry adds the motives: they were covetous of the wealth of the world and distrustful of God and his providence, thinking they might serve both God and mammon and deceive the apostles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Peter said, Ananias, why hath Satan filled thine heart to lie to the Holy Ghost? Henry notes the Spirit of God in Peter discerned the principle of unbelief reigning in Ananias's heart, yet Satan could not have filled his heart with this wickedness had he not been consenting, and the falsehood was an attempt to deceive the Spirit of truth who so manifestly spoke and acted by the apostles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Whiles it remained, was it not thine own, and after it was sold, was it not in thine own power? Henry corrects a common misreading: the crime was not retaining part of the price, for Ananias might have kept it all had he pleased, but endeavouring to impose upon the apostles with an awful lie, from a desire to make a vain show, joined with covetousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Ananias hearing these words fell down and gave up the ghost, and great fear came on all them that heard. Henry says this punishment was in reality mercy to vast numbers: it would cause strict self-examination, prayer, and dread of hypocrisy, covetousness and vain-glory, and prevent the increase of false professors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Peter answered Sapphira, Tell me whether ye sold the land for so much, and she said, Yea, for so much. Henry's sad reflection fits her confirmation of the lie: relations who should quicken one another to that which is good were instead hardening one another in that which is evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Peter said, How is it that ye have agreed together to tempt the Spirit of the Lord? Henry's warning is that if we think to put a cheat upon God, we shall put a fatal cheat upon our own souls, and the agreed lie of husband and wife shows relations hardening one another in evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Then fell she down straightway at his feet and yielded up the ghost. Henry draws the standing lesson: learn how hateful falsehood is to the God of truth, and shun not only a direct lie but all advantages from doubtful expressions and double meaning in speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Great fear came upon all the church and upon as many as heard these things. Henry repeats the merciful purpose of the judgment: it would cause strict self-examination and dread of hypocrisy, and it should still do so in the church today. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. By the hands of the apostles were many signs and wonders wrought among the people, all with one accord in Solomon's porch. Henry says the separation of hypocrites by distinguishing judgments should make the sincere cleave closer to each other and to the gospel ministry, and whatever tends to the purity and reputation of the church promotes its enlargement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Of the rest durst no man join himself to them, but the people magnified them. Henry reads this reserve as the wholesome effect of the judgment: hypocrites held back, while the sincere cleaved closer, and the church's purity advanced its reputation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Believers were the more added to the Lord, multitudes both of men and women. Henry insists that only the power which wrought such miracles by the apostles can rescue sinners from the power of sin and Satan and add believers to his worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. They brought forth the sick into the streets on beds and couches, that at least the shadow of Peter passing by might overshadow some of them. Henry's general word for these healings is that Christ will work by all his faithful servants, and every one who applies to him shall be healed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. There came also a multitude out of the cities round about unto Jerusalem, bringing sick folks and them vexed with unclean spirits, and they were healed every one. Henry's point stands: every one who applies to Christ shall be healed, and the same power that wrought the miracles adds believers to his worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Then the high priest rose up, and all they that were with him, being of the sect of the Sadducees, were filled with indignation. Henry calls them wretched in being vexed at the success of the gospel: they cannot but see that the word and power of the Lord are against them, and they tremble for the consequences, yet they will go on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. They laid their hands on the apostles and put them in the common prison. Henry answers the prison with confidence: there is no prison so dark or so strong but God can visit his people in it, and, if he pleases, fetch them out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. But the angel of the Lord by night opened the prison doors and brought them forth. Henry draws the purpose of such deliverances: recoveries from sickness and releases out of trouble are granted, not that we may enjoy the comforts of life, but that God may be honoured with the services of our life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Go, stand and speak in the temple to the people all the words of this life. Henry presses the command: it is not for preachers of Christ's gospel to retire into corners while they can preach in the great congregation, and they must speak to the lowest, whose souls are as precious to Christ as the souls of the greatest, all the words of this heavenly, divine life, whereby we may be saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. When they heard that, they entered into the temple early in the morning and taught. Henry's description of such preaching fits their obedience: speak as those who resolve to stand to it, to live and die by it, speaking the words of life which the Holy Ghost puts into their mouths. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. When the high priest and the captain of the temple and the chief priests heard these things, they doubted of them whereunto this would grow. Henry sees the rulers' vexation at gospel success: they cannot but see the word and power of the Lord are against them, and they tremble for the consequences, yet they will go on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Then came one and told them, Behold, the men whom ye put in prison are standing in the temple and teaching the people. Henry's point is the rulers' helpless vexation: the word and power of the Lord are against them, yet, trembling for the consequences, they still go on opposing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Did not we straitly command you that ye should not teach in this name, and behold ye have filled Jerusalem with your doctrine and intend to bring this man's blood upon us? Henry observes that many will do an evil thing with daring, yet cannot bear to hear of it afterward or to have it charged upon them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Then Peter and the other apostles answered, We ought to obey God rather than men. Henry's principle behind the answer is that we cannot expect to be redeemed and healed by Christ unless we give up ourselves to be ruled by him, taking the Saviour in all his offices, who came not to save us in our sins but to save us from our sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom ye slew and hanged on a tree. Henry's sermon summary begins here: the apostles testify that the Jesus the rulers slew is the one God raised, and that repentance and remission of sins are the blessings at issue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Him hath God exalted with his right hand to be a Prince and a Saviour, for to give repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins. Henry stresses what the chief priests neither valued nor saw their need of: wherever repentance is wrought, remission is granted without fail, and none are freed from the guilt of sin but those freed from its power and dominion, turned from it and against it, by Christ's Spirit working with the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And we are his witnesses of these things, and so is also the Holy Ghost, whom God hath given to them that obey him. Henry calls the giving of the Holy Ghost plain evidence that it is the will of God that Christ should be obeyed, and warns that he will surely destroy those who will not have him to reign over them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. When they heard that, they were cut to the heart and took counsel to slay them. Henry's warning closes the scene: he will surely destroy those who will not have him to reign over them, and the rulers' murderous counsel shows that refusal hardening into action. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Then stood there up one in the council, a Pharisee named Gamaliel, a doctor of the law, had in reputation among all the people. Henry sees the Lord's hand in this interruption: the Lord still has all hearts in his hands, and sometimes directs the prudence of the worldly wise so as to restrain the persecutors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Ye men of Israel, take heed to yourselves what ye intend to do as touching these men. Henry's gloss is common sense: be cautious, while experience and observation show that the success of frauds in matters of religion has been very short. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. For before these days rose up Theudas, boasting himself to be somebody, to whom a number of men joined, who was slain, and all were scattered and brought to nought. Henry uses the example as Gamaliel intended: the success of frauds in religion has been very short, and Theudas came to nought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. After this man rose up Judas of Galilee in the days of the taxing, and drew away much people after him, and he also perished, and all were dispersed. Henry's point repeats: experience shows religious frauds rise and fall quickly, and Judas of Galilee likewise perished with his following dispersed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. And now I say unto you, Refrain from these men and let them alone, for if this counsel or this work be of men, it will come to nought. Henry commends the caution: common sense tells us to be cautious, and the short success of frauds is the prudence Gamaliel urges on the council. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. But if it be of God, ye cannot overthrow it, lest haply ye be found even to fight against God. Henry's frame for the counsel is sovereignty: the Lord has all hearts in his hands, and a work of God cannot be overthrown by the council's prudence or violence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. They departed from the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer shame for his name. Henry calls reproach for Christ true preferment, as it makes us conformable to his pattern and serviceable to his interest, and says if we suffer ill for doing well, provided we suffer it well, we ought to rejoice in the grace which enabled us so to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Daily in the temple and in every house they ceased not to teach and preach Jesus Christ. Henry closes the chapter on the apostles' business: they did not preach themselves but Christ, and it ought to be the constant business of gospel ministers to preach Christ, and him crucified, Christ, and him glorified, nothing beside this but what has reference to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 41. They departed from the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer shame for his name. Henry calls reproach for Christ true preferment, as it makes us conformable to his pattern and serviceable to his interest, and says if we suffer ill for doing well, provided we suffer it well, we ought to rejoice in the grace which enabled us so to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Daily in the temple and in every house they ceased not to teach and preach Jesus Christ. Henry closes the chapter on the apostles' business: they did not preach themselves but Christ, and it ought to be the constant business of gospel ministers to preach Christ, and him crucified, Christ, and him glorified, nothing beside this but what has reference to it.
