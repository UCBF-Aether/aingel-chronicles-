The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the word of Samuel came to all Israel. Now Israel went out against the Philistines to battle, and pitched beside Ebenezer: and the Philistines pitched in Aphek.

2 And the Philistines put themselves in array against Israel: and when they joined battle, Israel was smitten before the Philistines: and they slew of the army in the field about four thousand men.

3 And when the people were come into the camp, the elders of Israel said, Wherefore hath the LORD smitten us to day before the Philistines? Let us fetch the ark of the covenant of the LORD out of Shiloh unto us, that, when it cometh among us, it may save us out of the hand of our enemies.

4 So the people sent to Shiloh, that they might bring from thence the ark of the covenant of the LORD of hosts, which dwelleth between the cherubims: and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were there with the ark of the covenant of God.

5 And when the ark of the covenant of the LORD came into the camp, all Israel shouted with a great shout, so that the earth rang again.

6 And when the Philistines heard the noise of the shout, they said, What meaneth the noise of this great shout in the camp of the Hebrews? And they understood that the ark of the LORD was come into the camp.

7 And the Philistines were afraid, for they said, God is come into the camp. And they said, Woe unto us! for there hath not been such a thing heretofore.

8 Woe unto us! who shall deliver us out of the hand of these mighty Gods? these are the Gods that smote the Egyptians with all the plagues in the wilderness.

9 Be strong and quit yourselves like men, O ye Philistines, that ye be not servants unto the Hebrews, as they have been to you: quit yourselves like men, and fight.

10 And the Philistines fought, and Israel was smitten, and they fled every man into his tent: and there was a very great slaughter; for there fell of Israel thirty thousand footmen.

11 And the ark of God was taken; and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain.

12 And there ran a man of Benjamin out of the army, and came to Shiloh the same day with his clothes rent, and with earth upon his head.

13 And when he came, lo, Eli sat upon a seat by the wayside watching: for his heart trembled for the ark of God. And when the man came into the city, and told it, all the city cried out.

14 And when Eli heard the noise of the crying, he said, What meaneth the noise of this tumult? And the man came in hastily, and told Eli.

15 Now Eli was ninety and eight years old; and his eyes were dim, that he could not see.

16 And the man said unto Eli, I am he that came out of the army, and I fled to day out of the army. And he said, What is there done, my son?

17 And the messenger answered and said, Israel is fled before the Philistines, and there hath been also a great slaughter among the people, and thy two sons also, Hophni and Phinehas, are dead, and the ark of God is taken.

18 And it came to pass, when he made mention of the ark of God, that he fell from off the seat backward by the side of the gate, and his neck brake, and he died: for he was an old man, and heavy. And he had judged Israel forty years.

19 And his daughter in law, Phinehas’ wife, was with child, near to be delivered: and when she heard the tidings that the ark of God was taken, and that her father in law and her husband were dead, she bowed herself and travailed; for her pains came upon her.

20 And about the time of her death the women that stood by her said unto her, Fear not; for thou hast born a son. But she answered not, neither did she regard it.

21 And she named the child Ichabod, saying, The glory is departed from Israel: because the ark of God was taken, and because of her father in law and her husband.

22 And she said, The glory is departed from Israel: for the ark of God is taken.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Israel goes out against the Philistines and is smitten, losing about four thousand men, and the elders fetch the ark from Shiloh to save them, supposing they can oblige God to appear for them by bringing it into the camp. The shout when the ark arrives frightens the Philistines, who stiffen themselves and fight: Israel flees, thirty thousand footmen fall, the ark is taken, and Hophni and Phinehas are slain. A Benjaminite runner brings the news to Shiloh, where ninety-eight-year-old Eli, blind, sits watching by the wayside; at the mention of the ark he falls backward from his seat, breaks his neck and dies after judging Israel forty years, and Phinehas's wife, dying in childbirth, names the child Ichabod, the glory is departed from Israel, for the ark of God is taken. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The word of Samuel came to all Israel, and Israel went out against the Philistines, pitching beside Ebenezer while the Philistines pitched at Aphek. Samuel's word now guided the nation, even into a battle it would lose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The Philistines put themselves in array, and Israel was smitten, losing about four thousand men in the field. Sin, the accursed thing, was in the camp, Henry observes, and gave their enemies all the advantage they could wish for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The elders asked why the LORD had smitten them, and proposed fetching the ark from Shiloh to save them. They owned God's hand in their trouble, Henry notes, but instead of submitting they spoke angrily, unaware of any just provocation they had given him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The people sent to Shiloh for the ark of the covenant of the LORD of hosts, who dwells between the cherubim, and Eli's two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, came with it. They supposed they could oblige God to appear for them by bringing the ark into the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. When the ark came into the camp, all Israel shouted so that the earth rang. Outward observance was clung to as if the ark itself, God's throne in the camp, would save them while the world and flesh sat on the throne of the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The Philistines heard the shout and asked what the great shout in the Hebrew camp meant, and learned that the ark of the LORD had come into the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The Philistines were afraid, saying God had come into the camp, and woe to them, for nothing like this had happened before. Even Israel's enemies recognised the ark's dread significance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Woe to us, they cried; who shall deliver us from these mighty Gods, the Gods who smote Egypt with all the plagues in the wilderness? Their fear recalled God's ancient judgments, though they did not know the God of Israel himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Be strong and quit yourselves like men, the Philistines urged one another, lest they become servants to the Hebrews as the Hebrews had been to them. Fear stiffened them into desperate courage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The Philistines fought, Israel was smitten and fled every man to his tent, and the slaughter was very great — thirty thousand footmen fell. The ark in the camp did not save an unrepentant people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The ark of God was taken, and Eli's two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain. The taking of the ark was a great judgment and a certain token of God's displeasure; let none think to shelter from God's wrath under the cloak of outward profession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. A man of Benjamin ran from the army and came to Shiloh the same day with clothes rent and earth on his head, the signs of calamity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Eli sat by the wayside watching, for his heart trembled for the ark of God, and when the man came into the city the whole city cried out. The old judge's chief concern was not the army but the ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Eli heard the noise of the crying and asked what the tumult meant, and the man came hastily to tell him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Eli was ninety-eight years old, his eyes dim so he could not see. Age and blindness frame his final hour, dependent on others' tidings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The man said he had fled that day from the army, and Eli asked, What is there done, my son? The question waited for the worst. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The messenger reported that Israel had fled, there had been a great slaughter, Eli's two sons were dead, and the ark of God was taken. Each blow fell harder than the last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. When mention was made of the ark, Eli fell backward from his seat by the gate, broke his neck, and died, for he was old and heavy, after judging Israel forty years. The defeat grieved him as judge, his sons' deaths touched him as father, but the ark struck him to the heart; Henry adds that a man may die miserably yet not die eternally. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Phinehas' wife, near delivery, heard that the ark was taken and that her father-in-law and husband were dead, and she bowed herself and travailed, for her pains came upon her. The tidings brought on her labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. About the time of her death the women standing by said, Fear not, for thou hast born a son, but she answered not, neither did she regard it. Earthly joy could not reach a heart fixed on the lost ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. She named the child Ichabod, saying, The glory is departed from Israel, because the ark was taken and because of her father-in-law and husband. Her dying regret was for the loss of the ark and the departure of the glory from Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. She repeated, The glory is departed from Israel, for the ark of God is taken. What pleasure can we take in creature comforts, Henry asks, if we want God's gracious presence? If God goes, the glory goes; yet though glory is withdrawn from one sinful nation after another, it shall never depart altogether. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
