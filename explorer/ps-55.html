Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 55 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Give ear to my prayer, O God; and hide not thyself from my supplication.

2 Attend unto me, and hear me: I mourn in my complaint, and make a noise;

3 Because of the voice of the enemy, because of the oppression of the wicked: for they cast iniquity upon me, and in wrath they hate me.

4 My heart is sore pained within me: and the terrors of death are fallen upon me.

5 Fearfulness and trembling are come upon me, and horror hath overwhelmed me.

6 And I said, Oh that I had wings like a dove! for then would I fly away, and be at rest.

7 Lo, then would I wander far off, and remain in the wilderness. Selah.

8 I would hasten my escape from the windy storm and tempest.

9 Destroy, O Lord, and divide their tongues: for I have seen violence and strife in the city.

10 Day and night they go about it upon the walls thereof: mischief also and sorrow are in the midst of it.

11 Wickedness is in the midst thereof: deceit and guile depart not from her streets.

12 For it was not an enemy that reproached me; then I could have borne it: neither was it he that hated me that did magnify himself against me; then I would have hid myself from him:

13 But it was thou, a man mine equal, my guide, and mine acquaintance.

14 We took sweet counsel together, and walked unto the house of God in company.

15 Let death seize upon them, and let them go down quick into hell: for wickedness is in their dwellings, and among them.

16 As for me, I will call upon God; and the LORD shall save me.

17 Evening, and morning, and at noon, will I pray, and cry aloud: and he shall hear my voice.

18 He hath delivered my soul in peace from the battle that was against me: for there were many with me.

19 God shall hear, and afflict them, even he that abideth of old. Selah. Because they have no changes, therefore they fear not God.

20 He hath put forth his hands against such as be at peace with him: he hath broken his covenant.

21 The words of his mouth were smoother than butter, but war was in his heart: his words were softer than oil, yet were they drawn swords.

22 Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee: he shall never suffer the righteous to be moved.

23 But thou, O God, shalt bring them down into the pit of destruction: bloody and deceitful men shall not live out half their days; but I will trust in thee.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Betrayal's psalm: noisy mourning under death's terrors, wishing for doves' wings to fly to wilderness rest, violence and strife patrolling the city day and night, and the wound is not an enemy's but an equal's, guide and acquaintance, with whom he took sweet counsel to God's house. Against butter-smooth words hiding war stand thrice-daily prayer and the counsel: cast your burden on the Lord, who sustains and never lets the righteous be moved. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Heading note: the text panel prints the verses only, without a heading. The betrayer identified in verses 13 and 20 as Ahithophel in David's case comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. Authorship and date remain Unresolved (see Trace). Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Give ear to my prayer, O God, and hide not yourself from my supplication: the psalm opens begging an open ear and an unhidden God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Attend to me and hear me; I mourn in my complaint and make a noise: mourning complaint made aloud — grief is noisy, not stoic. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Because of the voice of the enemy, because of the oppression of the wicked, for they cast iniquity upon me, and in wrath they hate me: the enemy's voice, the wicked's oppression, iniquity cast on him, and wrathful hatred are the causes of the noise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. My heart is sore pained within me, and the terrors of death are fallen upon me: inward pain and death's terrors falling — the heart itself is the battlefield. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Fearfulness and trembling are come upon me, and horror has overwhelmed me: fear, trembling, and overwhelming horror complete the portrait of a man undone by dread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And I said, Oh that I had wings like a dove! For then would I fly away and be at rest: the dove's wings are wished for — flight and rest, not victory, seem the only hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Lo, then would I wander far off, and remain in the wilderness: far wandering and wilderness dwelling are the imagined refuge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. I would hasten my escape from the windy storm and tempest: hastened escape from storm and tempest — the psalmist's troubles are weather, and he would outrun them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Destroy, O Lord, and divide their tongues, for I have seen violence and strife in the city: dividing tongues — as at Babel — answers the violence and strife seen in the city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Day and night they go about it upon the walls thereof; mischief also and sorrow are in the midst of it: day-and-night patrols of mischief and sorrow within the city walls — evil keeps watch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Wickedness is in the midst thereof; deceit and guile depart not from her streets: wickedness at the centre, deceit and guile never leaving the streets — the city is corrupt at heart and in public. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. For it was not an enemy that reproached me, then I could have borne it; neither was it he who hated me that magnified himself against me, then I would have hidden myself: an enemy's reproach could be borne, a hater's magnifying could be fled — but this is worse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But it was you, a man my equal, my guide, and my acquaintance: the betrayer was his equal, guide, and acquaintance — Ahithophel, in David's case, and the pattern of Judas. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. We took sweet counsel together, and walked to the house of God in company: sweet counsel and company to God's house make the betrayal bitter — shared worship, broken trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Let death seize upon them, and let them go down quick into hell, for wickedness is in their dwellings and among them: sudden death and a quick descent to hell are prayed on those whose dwellings and company are wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. As for me, I will call upon God, and the Lord shall save me: against their fate stands his resolve — calling on God, and the Lord saving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Evening, and morning, and at noon, will I pray and cry aloud, and he shall hear my voice: thrice-daily prayer, cried aloud, with heard voice as its promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He has delivered my soul in peace from the battle against me, for there were many with me: deliverance in peace from battle, with many on his side — God's host, or friends, outnumbering the foe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. God shall hear and afflict them, even he who abides of old; because they have no changes, therefore they fear not God: the abiding God hears and afflicts the changeless, who fear not God precisely because nothing changes for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He has put forth his hands against such as are at peace with him; he has broken his covenant: hands against the peaceable and a broken covenant describe the betrayer again — Ahithophel against David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The words of his mouth were smoother than butter, but war was in his heart; his words were softer than oil, yet they were drawn swords: butter-smooth, oil-soft words hide war in the heart and drawn swords in the mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Cast your burden upon the Lord, and he shall sustain you; he shall never suffer the righteous to be moved: the famous counsel — cast the burden, be sustained, never moved — is the psalm's practical centre. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. But you, O God, shall bring them down into the pit of destruction; bloody and deceitful men shall not live out half their days; but I will trust in you: the pit of destruction and half-lived days await the bloody and deceitful, while the psalmist's last word is trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
