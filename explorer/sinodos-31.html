The Text — Sinodos 31 (Ge'ez text of G. Horner, The Statutes of the Apostles; or, Canones Ecclesiastici (London: Williams & Norgate, 1904), public domain)

1 Statute 63. Concerning trades[?] and occupations: let them examine them concerning their works, and their manner of life[?], with firmness — whether they be slaves or free. And if he be a slave, let them examine his master, that testimony may be borne[?] for him concerning his work.

2 And if there be good testimony for him, let him be received[?].

3 But if there be no testimony for him, let him be sent away, that he may make himself worthy[?], that his master may bear testimony for him.

4 And if there be testimony for him, let him be received[?]. And if there be a slave and a handmaid, let them be taught[?] to live according to the will of their masters, that the word of God be not blasphemed[?].

5 And if there be one who hath a wife, or a wife who hath a husband, let them be taught not to commit adultery, and to abstain; and if there be such as are unmarried, let them be taught not to commit adultery, but to marry according to the law.

6 And if his master be a believer, and knoweth that his slave or his handmaid committeth adultery, let him give them in marriage according to the law; and if he give them not in marriage according to the law …[?], let that master of slaves be rejected[?] from the church, for he made them unclean[?].

7 And if there be a man who hath a demon upon him, let him be taught[?] that he be healed, and let him not enter the church to partake of the mystery until he be purified; and if he drew near unto death, let them bring him to receive …[?] — Statute 59. And afterward: the ordained presbyters shall not do the work of the deacons, nor anything which is not theirs.

8 The deaconesses, women, shall not bless, and shall do nothing of what the presbyters and deacons do.

9 But they shall keep the gates of the church; apart from this there is nothing at all else which they do.

10 except the ministry unto the presbyters, and the baptizing of women — for this is fitting for them. And the deacon shall reprove[?] the subdeacon[?], and the reader, and the singers, and the deaconess.

11 Thus indeed it is fitting …[?], if the presbyter be not present. But for the subdeacon[?] it is not fitting to reprove[?], neither the readers, nor the ministers of the Scripture, nor the deaconess, women.

12 neither those in orders nor the people — for they are the servants of the deacons.

13 Statute 60. Concerning firstfruits and tithes.

14 And all the firstfruits and the tithes they shall bring unto the bishops, and unto the presbyters, and unto the deacons, for their sustenance; but all the tithes they shall bring for the sustenance of the rest of the ordained that remain, and for the virgins, and for the widows, and for them that are afflicted by poverty[?], for the firstfruits are for the priests and for their ministers.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Statute 63 concerns trades and occupations: candidates are examined concerning their works and manner of life, slave or free, with a slave's master examined and his testimony required before reception, slaves and handmaids taught to live according to their masters' will so that God's word is not blasphemed, married and unmarried taught against adultery and toward lawful marriage, and a man with a demon taught toward healing and kept from partaking until purified, though brought in if near death. The chapter then repeats, word for word in substance, the statute 59 and 60 material of chapter 28 — deaconesses keeping the gates, the reproof hierarchy, and firstfruits and tithes for the ordained.
