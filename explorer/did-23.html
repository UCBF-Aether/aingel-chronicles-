The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Didascalia 23 (J. M. Harden, The Ethiopic Didascalia (London: SPCK, 1920), public domain (Cornell University Library scan, archive.org cu31924096083336))

*THAT SERVANTS OUGHT TO BE SUBJECT IN ALL PURITY TO THEIR MASTERS WHETHER THEY BE UNGODLY OR FAITHFUL.*

1 What say ye then concerning servants? Let them be prudent and serve their masters in the fear of God. And if a servant be a Christian and his master an unbeliever, yet ought he to serve him in everything; but let him have no fellowship with him in faith. And in like manner let masters love their servants.

2 And if a man’s master be a believer, let him serve him, and fear him as God, and count him as a father and a good master. And for this he shall receive a good reward for the service wherewith he hath served his master.

3 And if there be a master who hath a servant, a believer, and he doeth his will, let him love him as his son, and count him as his brother for the love of the faith.

4 Humble yourselves and obey kings and” princes who fear God, for they are God’s ministers. Fear them then who are set in authority, as is fitting, and offer to them from that which ye have with honour and obedience, for so hath God commanded. Make for yourselves no excuses (), but rather love one another, even as the Lord God hath commanded by His Son, our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In plain language: this chapter tells servants to serve their masters prudently and in the fear of God, whether the master is unbelieving or believing — in the latter case serving him as a father and good master, while keeping no fellowship in faith with an unbelieving master. Masters, in turn, are to love believing servants as sons and brothers. It closes by urging obedience and honour toward God-fearing kings and princes, as God's ministers.
