The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hosea 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 When I would have healed Israel, then the iniquity of Ephraim was discovered, and the wickedness of Samaria: for they commit falsehood; and the thief cometh in, and the troop of robbers spoileth without.

2 And they consider not in their hearts that I remember all their wickedness: now their own doings have beset them about; they are before my face.

3 They make the king glad with their wickedness, and the princes with their lies.

4 They are all adulterers, as an oven heated by the baker, who ceaseth from raising after he hath kneaded the dough, until it be leavened.

5 In the day of our king the princes have made him sick with bottles of wine; he stretched out his hand with scorners.

6 For they have made ready their heart like an oven, whiles they lie in wait: their baker sleepeth all the night; in the morning it burneth as a flaming fire.

7 They are all hot as an oven, and have devoured their judges; all their kings are fallen: there is none among them that calleth unto me.

8 Ephraim, he hath mixed himself among the people; Ephraim is a cake not turned.

9 Strangers have devoured his strength, and he knoweth it not: yea, gray hairs are here and there upon him, yet he knoweth not.

10 And the pride of Israel testifieth to his face: and they do not return to the LORD their God, nor seek him for all this.

11 Ephraim also is like a silly dove without heart: they call to Egypt, they go to Assyria.

12 When they shall go, I will spread my net upon them; I will bring them down as the fowls of the heaven; I will chastise them, as their congregation hath heard.

13 Woe unto them! for they have fled from me: destruction unto them! because they have transgressed against me: though I have redeemed them, yet they have spoken lies against me.

14 And they have not cried unto me with their heart, when they howled upon their beds: they assemble themselves for corn and wine, and they rebel against me.

15 Though I have bound and strengthened their arms, yet do they imagine mischief against me.

16 They return, but not to the most High: they are like a deceitful bow: their princes shall fall by the sword for the rage of their tongue: this shall be their derision in the land of Egypt.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

When God would have healed Israel, Ephraim's iniquity and Samaria's wickedness were exposed instead: adultery like a heated oven, princes made sick with wine, kings fallen one after another, and none calling on God. Ephraim is a cake not turned, a silly dove fluttering to Egypt and Assyria, and the chapter closes with princes falling by the sword for the rage of their tongue. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When I would have healed Israel, then the iniquity of Ephraim was discovered, and the wickedness of Samaria, for they commit falsehood, and the thief comes in, and the troop of robbers spoils without, because the very attempt to heal exposes the disease, with fraud within the city and robbery without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And they consider not in their hearts that I remember all their wickedness, now their own doings have beset them about, they are before my face, because they forget that God remembers, while their own deeds surround them and stand before his face. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They make the king glad with their wickedness, and the princes with their lies, because court favour is won by flattering wickedness, and the king is pleased with the very sins that ruin him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They are all adulterers, as an oven heated by the baker, who ceases from raising after he has kneaded the dough, until it be leavened, because their lust, like a baker's oven, is heated and kept ready, only pausing until the dough has risen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. In the day of our king the princes have made him sick with bottles of wine, he stretched out his hand with scorners, because the court keeps festival with wine until the king himself is sickened, and joins hands with mockers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For they have made ready their heart like an oven, while they lie in wait, their baker sleeps all the night, in the morning it burns as a flaming fire, because conspiracy smoulders overnight like a banked oven and bursts into flame by morning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They are all hot as an oven, and have devoured their judges, all their kings are fallen, there is none among them that calls unto me, because the heat of faction consumes judge after judge and king after king, and in all the revolutions no one turns to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Ephraim, he has mixed himself among the people, Ephraim is a cake not turned, because meddling with foreign alliances has half-baked the nation, burnt on one side and raw on the other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Strangers have devoured his strength, and he knows it not, yea, grey hairs are here and there upon him, yet he knows it not, because foreign powers drain his strength and age creeps upon him, while he remains insensible to both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. And the pride of Israel testifies to his face, and they do not return to the Lord their God, nor seek him for all this, because pride, which witnesses against them, still will not turn them back, even after all that has befallen them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Ephraim also is like a silly dove without heart, they call to Egypt, they go to Assyria, because the nation, witless as a dove, flutters between Egypt and Assyria, seeking help from both and finding it from neither. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. When they shall go, I will spread my net upon them, I will bring them down as the fowls of the heaven, I will chastise them, as their congregation has heard, because the dove that flies to foreign help will be caught in God's net, and chastised as the congregation was warned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Woe unto them, for they have fled from me, destruction unto them, because they have transgressed against me, though I have redeemed them, yet they have spoken lies against me, because flight from God, after redemption by God, deserves both woe and destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. And they have not cried unto me with their heart, when they howled upon their beds, they assemble themselves for corn and wine, and they rebel against me, because their crying is howling for provisions, not prayer from the heart, and they gather for corn and wine while rebelling against the Giver. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Though I have bound and strengthened their arms, yet do they imagine mischief against me, because God strengthened the very arms that are now lifted to plot against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. They return, but not to the most High, they are like a deceitful bow, their princes shall fall by the sword for the rage of their tongue, this shall be their derision in the land of Egypt, because a return that is not to God is no return, and a bow that fails at the shot is the image of their princes, derided even in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Ephraim. the leading tribe of the northern kingdom, used in the prophets for the northern kingdom of Israel itself, as distinct from Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Samaria. the capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, standing in the prophets for the ten tribes as Jerusalem stands for Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
