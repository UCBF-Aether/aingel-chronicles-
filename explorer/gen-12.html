The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the LORD had said unto Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father’s house, unto a land that I will shew thee:

2 And I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing:

3 And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.

4 So Abram departed, as the LORD had spoken unto him; and Lot went with him: and Abram was seventy and five years old when he departed out of Haran.

5 And Abram took Sarai his wife, and Lot his brother’s son, and all their substance that they had gathered, and the souls that they had gotten in Haran; and they went forth to go into the land of Canaan; and into the land of Canaan they came.

6 And Abram passed through the land unto the place of Sichem, unto the plain of Moreh. And the Canaanite was then in the land.

7 And the LORD appeared unto Abram, and said, Unto thy seed will I give this land: and there builded he an altar unto the LORD, who appeared unto him.

8 And he removed from thence unto a mountain on the east of Bethel, and pitched his tent, having Bethel on the west, and Hai on the east: and there he builded an altar unto the LORD, and called upon the name of the LORD.

9 And Abram journeyed, going on still toward the south.

10 And there was a famine in the land: and Abram went down into Egypt to sojourn there; for the famine was grievous in the land.

11 And it came to pass, when he was come near to enter into Egypt, that he said unto Sarai his wife, Behold now, I know that thou art a fair woman to look upon:

12 Therefore it shall come to pass, when the Egyptians shall see thee, that they shall say, This is his wife: and they will kill me, but they will save thee alive.

13 Say, I pray thee, thou art my sister: that it may be well with me for thy sake; and my soul shall live because of thee.

14 And it came to pass, that, when Abram was come into Egypt, the Egyptians beheld the woman that she was very fair.

15 The princes also of Pharaoh saw her, and commended her before Pharaoh: and the woman was taken into Pharaoh’s house.

16 And he entreated Abram well for her sake: and he had sheep, and oxen, and he asses, and menservants, and maidservants, and she asses, and camels.

17 And the LORD plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai Abram’s wife.

18 And Pharaoh called Abram and said, What is this that thou hast done unto me? why didst thou not tell me that she was thy wife?

19 Why saidst thou, She is my sister? so I might have taken her to me to wife: now therefore behold thy wife, take her, and go thy way.

20 And Pharaoh commanded his men concerning him: and they sent him away, and his wife, and all that he had.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God calls Abram to leave his country and kindred for a land he will be shown, promising descendants, blessing and a blessing to all families of the earth through him. Abram journeys to Canaan, is driven by famine into Egypt, where Pharaoh takes Sarai and is plagued until Abram is sent away with his household intact. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God's call tested whether Abram loved God more than his native soil, his kindred, and his father's house, and whether he would trust God to show him a land he had not yet seen. Henry notes the call came first in Ur, before Abram lived in Haran. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. God promised to make Abram a great nation, to bless him, and to make his name great, which was a particular comfort to a childless man, since his greatness would come through God's gift, not his own family strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God bound himself to Abram's cause, blessing those who blessed him and opposing those who cursed him, and crowned the promise with blessing for all families of the earth through him, which Henry reads as pointing to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Abram obeyed at once, without dispute, leaving Haran at seventy-five, an age when most would seek rest. Lot went with him, drawn by his uncle's example. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Abram took Sarai, Lot, all their goods, and the people they had gathered in Haran, and pressed on to Canaan itself, not stopping short as the earlier journey from Ur had done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Abram passed through the land to Shechem, but found Canaanites already in possession, so even in the promised land he lived as a guest by permission, learning to live by faith rather than by present possession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God appeared to Abram in Canaan and renewed the promise of the land to his offspring, and Abram answered with worship, building an altar where God had met him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Abram moved on to the hill country between Bethel and Ai, pitched his tent there, and built another altar and called on God's name, marking each stage of his journey with open worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Abram kept journeying southward, still unsettled, which Henry reads as the lot of God's people in this world: strangers and pilgrims, moving on from strength to strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. A severe famine in Canaan drove Abram to Egypt, where food was plentiful. The commentators note the trial: the land of promise itself could not yet feed him, and faith had to survive want. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. As they neared Egypt, Abram spoke to Sarai about her beauty, showing that his fear of danger there was already working on his mind before any threat had appeared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Abram feared the Egyptians would kill him to take Sarai, a fear that reveals how his trust, so firm in leaving home, wavered when his own life seemed at stake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Abram asked Sarai to say she was his sister, which was partly true of their kinship but was meant to hide their marriage. Henry calls this his fault: a device of fear rather than honest trust in God's protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. On entering Egypt, Sarai's beauty was noticed at once, just as Abram had feared, and the danger he had tried to manage by his own plan now fell on her instead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Pharaoh's princes praised Sarai to the king, and she was taken into his house, which put her in real peril and showed how Abram's scheme had endangered the very promise of an heir. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Pharaoh treated Abram well for Sarai's sake, giving him livestock and servants, so Abram grew rich by the very deceit that had put his wife at risk, an uneasy prosperity Henry notes without excusing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. God struck Pharaoh's house with plagues, protecting Sarai when Abram's plan could not, and thereby exposing the truth and guarding the promised line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Pharaoh confronted Abram about the deception, showing that even a pagan king could see the wrong of it, and Abram had no defence to offer for what he had done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Pharaoh's question pressed the point home: by calling Sarai his sister, Abram had led the king to take another man's wife, and only God's intervention had kept the wrong from going further. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Pharaoh sent Abram away under guard with his wife and all he had, a merciful dismissal rather than punishment, and a humbling end to Abram's first venture outside the promised land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
