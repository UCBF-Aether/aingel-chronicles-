The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now when they had passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where was a synagogue of the Jews:

2 And Paul, as his manner was, went in unto them, and three sabbath days reasoned with them out of the scriptures,

3 Opening and alleging, that Christ must needs have suffered, and risen again from the dead; and that this Jesus, whom I preach unto you, is Christ.

4 And some of them believed, and consorted with Paul and Silas; and of the devout Greeks a great multitude, and of the chief women not a few.

5 But the Jews which believed not, moved with envy, took unto them certain lewd fellows of the baser sort, and gathered a company, and set all the city on an uproar, and assaulted the house of Jason, and sought to bring them out to the people.

6 And when they found them not, they drew Jason and certain brethren unto the rulers of the city, crying, These that have turned the world upside down are come hither also;

7 Whom Jason hath received: and these all do contrary to the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, one Jesus.

8 And they troubled the people and the rulers of the city, when they heard these things.

9 And when they had taken security of Jason, and of the other, they let them go.

10 And the brethren immediately sent away Paul and Silas by night unto Berea: who coming thither went into the synagogue of the Jews.

11 These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.

12 Therefore many of them believed; also of honourable women which were Greeks, and of men, not a few.

13 But when the Jews of Thessalonica had knowledge that the word of God was preached of Paul at Berea, they came thither also, and stirred up the people.

14 And then immediately the brethren sent away Paul to go as it were to the sea: but Silas and Timotheus abode there still.

15 And they that conducted Paul brought him unto Athens: and receiving a commandment unto Silas and Timotheus for to come to him with all speed, they departed.

16 Now while Paul waited for them at Athens, his spirit was stirred in him, when he saw the city wholly given to idolatry.

17 Therefore disputed he in the synagogue with the Jews, and with the devout persons, and in the market daily with them that met with him.

18 Then certain philosophers of the Epicureans, and of the Stoicks, encountered him. And some said, What will this babbler say? other some, He seemeth to be a setter forth of strange gods: because he preached unto them Jesus, and the resurrection.

19 And they took him, and brought him unto Areopagus, saying, May we know what this new doctrine, whereof thou speakest, is?

20 For thou bringest certain strange things to our ears: we would know therefore what these things mean.

21 (For all the Athenians and strangers which were there spent their time in nothing else, but either to tell, or to hear some new thing.)

22 Then Paul stood in the midst of Mars’ hill, and said, Ye men of Athens, I perceive that in all things ye are too superstitious.

23 For as I passed by, and beheld your devotions, I found an altar with this inscription, TO THE UNKNOWN GOD. Whom therefore ye ignorantly worship, him declare I unto you.

24 God that made the world and all things therein, seeing that he is Lord of heaven and earth, dwelleth not in temples made with hands;

25 Neither is worshipped with men’s hands, as though he needed any thing, seeing he giveth to all life, and breath, and all things;

26 And hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth, and hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation;

27 That they should seek the Lord, if haply they might feel after him, and find him, though he be not far from every one of us:

28 For in him we live, and move, and have our being; as certain also of your own poets have said, For we are also his offspring.

29 Forasmuch then as we are the offspring of God, we ought not to think that the Godhead is like unto gold, or silver, or stone, graven by art and man’s device.

30 And the times of this ignorance God winked at; but now commandeth all men every where to repent:

31 Because he hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man whom he hath ordained; whereof he hath given assurance unto all men, in that he hath raised him from the dead.

32 And when they heard of the resurrection of the dead, some mocked: and others said, We will hear thee again of this matter.

33 So Paul departed from among them.

34 Howbeit certain men clave unto him, and believed: among the which was Dionysius the Areopagite, and a woman named Damaris, and others with them.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul reasons in the synagogues at Thessalonica and Berea, where the Bereans search the Scriptures daily to test his message, then speaks at Athens, first in the synagogue and marketplace and then on the Areopagus. There he takes the Athenians' altar to the unknown god as his starting point, proclaims the Creator who commands repentance, and points to the risen Jesus as judge; some mock, some believe. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. Paul, as his manner was, went in unto them, and three sabbath days reasoned with them out of the scriptures. Henry states the drift and scope of Paul's preaching and arguing: it was to prove that Jesus is the Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Opening and alleging, that Christ must needs have suffered, and risen again from the dead; and that this Jesus, whom I preach unto you, is Christ. Henry explains the must: he must needs suffer for us, because he could not otherwise purchase our redemption for us, and he must needs have risen again, because he could not otherwise apply the redemption to us, and we are to preach concerning Jesus that he is Christ, therefore we may hope to be saved by him, and are bound to be ruled by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Some of them believed, and consorted with Paul and Silas; and of the devout Greeks a great multitude, and of the chief women not a few. Henry's preaching drift bears this fruit: the argument that Jesus is the Christ, who suffered and rose, draws believers who consort with Paul and Silas. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. But the Jews which believed not, moved with envy, took unto them certain lewd fellows of the baser sort, and gathered a company, and set all the city on an uproar, and assaulted the house of Jason, and sought to bring them out to the people. Henry says the unbelieving Jews were angry because the apostles preached to the Gentiles, that they might be saved, and asks how strange it is that men should grudge others the privileges they will not themselves accept, while turbulent spirits make religion the pretext for evil designs, from whom we should withdraw, showing a desire to act aright in society while claiming our right to worship God according to our consciences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. When they found them not, they drew Jason and certain brethren unto the rulers of the city, crying, These that have turned the world upside down are come hither also. Henry says neither rulers nor people need be troubled at the increase of real Christians, even though turbulent spirits should make religion the pretext for evil designs, and Jason drawn before the rulers is that pretext at work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Whom Jason hath received: and these all do contrary to the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, one Jesus. Henry's pretext charge is the another-king cry: turbulent spirits make religion the pretext for evil designs, turning the preaching of Jesus as Christ into a charge of contrary decrees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. When they had taken security of Jason, and of the other, they let them go. Henry's society rule fits the release: let us show a desire to act aright in society, while we claim our right to worship God according to our consciences, and the brethren, security taken, are let go. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The brethren immediately sent away Paul and Silas by night unto Berea: who coming thither went into the synagogue of the Jews. Henry's Berean study begins at the synagogue: the Jews in Berea applied seriously to the study of the word preached unto them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so. Henry says they not only heard Paul preach on the sabbath, but daily searched the Scriptures, and compared what they read with the facts related to them, for the doctrine of Christ does not fear inquiry, and advocates for his cause desire no more than that people will fully and fairly examine whether things are so or not, and those are truly noble, and likely to be more and more so, who make the Scriptures their rule, and consult them accordingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Therefore many of them believed; also of honourable women which were Greeks, and of men, not a few. Henry's therefore is the fruit of searching: receiving the word with readiness and searching the Scriptures daily, whether those things were so, many believed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Now while Paul waited for them at Athens, his spirit was stirred in him, when he saw the city wholly given to idolatry. Henry says Athens was then famed for polite learning, philosophy, and the fine arts, but none are more childish and superstitious, more impious, or more credulous, than some persons deemed eminent for learning and ability, and the city was wholly given to idolatry, which stirred Paul's spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Therefore disputed he in the synagogue with the Jews, and with the devout persons, and in the market daily with them that met with him. Henry says the zealous advocate for the cause of Christ will be ready to plead for it in all companies, as occasion offers, in synagogue, with devout persons, and in the market daily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Then certain philosophers of the Epicureans, and of the Stoicks, encountered him. And some said, What will this babbler say? other some, He seemeth to be a setter forth of strange gods: because he preached unto them Jesus, and the resurrection. Henry says the apostle ever dwelt upon two points, which are indeed the principal doctrines of Christianity, Christ and a future state, Christ our way, and heaven our end, and most of these learned men took no notice of Paul, but some, whose principles were most directly contrary to Christianity, made remarks upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. They took him, and brought him unto Areopagus, saying, May we know what this new doctrine, whereof thou speakest, is? Henry says they led him to the place where judges sat who inquired into such matters, and they asked about Paul's doctrine, not because it was good, but because it was new. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. For thou bringest certain strange things to our ears: we would know therefore what these things mean. Henry says they desire to know more of it, but only because it was new and strange, and they asked about the doctrine because it was new, not because it was good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. For all the Athenians and strangers which were there spent their time in nothing else, but either to tell, or to hear some new thing. Henry says great talkers are always busy-bodies, spending their time in nothing else, and a very uncomfortable account they have to give of their time who thus spend it, for time is precious, and we are concerned to employ it well, because eternity depends upon it, but much is wasted in unprofitable conversation.   • Verse 22. Then Paul stood in the midst of Mars' hill, and said, Ye men of Athens, I perceive that in all things ye are too superstitious.
