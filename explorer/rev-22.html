The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he shewed me a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb.

2 In the midst of the street of it, and on either side of the river, was there the tree of life, which bare twelve manner of fruits, and yielded her fruit every month: and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations.

3 And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him:

4 And they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads.

5 And there shall be no night there; and they need no candle, neither light of the sun; for the Lord God giveth them light: and they shall reign for ever and ever.

6 And he said unto me, These sayings are faithful and true: and the Lord God of the holy prophets sent his angel to shew unto his servants the things which must shortly be done.

7 Behold, I come quickly: blessed is he that keepeth the sayings of the prophecy of this book.

8 And I John saw these things, and heard them. And when I had heard and seen, I fell down to worship before the feet of the angel which shewed me these things.

9 Then saith he unto me, See thou do it not: for I am thy fellowservant, and of thy brethren the prophets, and of them which keep the sayings of this book: worship God.

10 And he saith unto me, Seal not the sayings of the prophecy of this book: for the time is at hand.

11 He that is unjust, let him be unjust still: and he which is filthy, let him be filthy still: and he that is righteous, let him be righteous still: and he that is holy, let him be holy still.

12 And, behold, I come quickly; and my reward is with me, to give every man according as his work shall be.

13 I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end, the first and the last.

14 Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city.

15 For without are dogs, and sorcerers, and whoremongers, and murderers, and idolaters, and whosoever loveth and maketh a lie.

16 I Jesus have sent mine angel to testify unto you these things in the churches. I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star.

17 And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.

18 For I testify unto every man that heareth the words of the prophecy of this book, If any man shall add unto these things, God shall add unto him the plagues that are written in this book:

19 And if any man shall take away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his part out of the book of life, and out of the holy city, and from the things which are written in this book.

20 He which testifieth these things saith, Surely I come quickly. Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus.

21 The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The book closes as it opened, with the city described as a paradise: a pure river of water of life proceeding from the throne of God and of the Lamb, and on either side the tree of life bearing twelve manner of fruits, its leaves for the healing of the nations, with no more curse and no night, for the Lord God gives them light. The angel attests that these sayings are faithful and true, Christ says, Surely I come quickly, and the Spirit and the bride answer, Come. John, falling again to worship the angel, is again refused — worship God — and the book seals itself with a warning neither to add to nor take away from its words, ending in grace: the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The angel showed John a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb. Henry notes the heavenly state, before described as a city, is here described as a paradise restored by the second Adam, its river springing from the throne of God through the Lamb's mediation, clear, salutary, and refreshing, giving and preserving life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. In the midst of the street of it, and on either side of the river, was there the tree of life, which bare twelve manner of fruits, and yielded her fruit every month: and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations. Henry notes this tree far excels Eden's, fed by the river from God's throne, never empty or barren, its fruit always fresh, pleasant, and wholesome, a remedy for all former maladies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. There shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him. Henry notes the paradise's great excellency is its perfect freedom from evil: no accursed one, no serpent as in the earthly paradise, and the devil cannot draw the saints from serving God or disturb them in his service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads. Henry notes the supreme felicity: the saints shall enjoy the beatific vision, and God will own them as having his seal and name on their foreheads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. There shall be no night there; and they need no candle, neither light of the sun; for the Lord God giveth them light: and they shall reign for ever and ever. Henry notes their service shall be not only freedom but honour and dominion, with perfect knowledge and joy, continually walking in the light of the Lord, not for a time but for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The angel said to John, "These sayings are faithful and true: and the Lord God of the holy prophets sent his angel to shew unto his servants the things which must shortly be done." Henry notes the ratification rests on God's name and nature, faithful and true, on his holy messengers, who would not deceive the world, and on speedy accomplishment, since Christ will come quickly and put all things out of doubt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Behold, I come quickly: blessed is he that keepeth the sayings of the prophecy of this book. Henry notes those will prove wise and happy who have believed and kept his words, since Christ will make haste and come quickly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. John saw these things, and heard them, and when he had heard and seen, he fell down to worship before the feet of the angel which shewed him these things. Henry notes the apostle's integrity: he confesses his own sin and folly, into which he had again relapsed, leaving his failing on perpetual record, showing he was a faithful and impartial writer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The angel said, "See thou do it not: for I am thy fellowservant, and of thy brethren the prophets, and of them which keep the sayings of this book: worship God." Henry notes the angel's integrity: he not only refused religious adoration but reproved John for it, and one so tender of God's honour would never lead God's people into mere dreams and delusions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. He said to John, "Seal not the sayings of the prophecy of this book: for the time is at hand." Henry notes God deals freely and openly, leaving the book open to be perused by all, that they might labour to understand it, make objections against it, and compare the prophecy with the events. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He that is unjust, let him be unjust still: and he which is filthy, let him be filthy still: and he that is righteous, let him be righteous still: and he that is holy, let him be holy still. Henry notes the book kept open will have opposite effects: the filthy and unjust take occasion to be more so, while it confirms, strengthens, and further sanctifies the upright, a savour of life to some and of death to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Behold, I come quickly; and my reward is with me, to give every man according as his work shall be. Henry notes Christ's word will be his rule of judgment at the great day, dispensing rewards and punishments according as men's works agree or disagree with it, and therefore the word itself must be faithful and true. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end, the first and the last. Henry notes Christ is the author, finisher, and rewarder of his people's faith and holiness, the same from first to last, and so is his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city. Henry notes Christ will by this word give his people, who conform themselves to it, a right to the tree of life and entrance into heaven, confirming the truth and authority of his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. For without are dogs, and sorcerers, and whoremongers, and murderers, and idolaters, and whosoever loveth and maketh a lie. Henry notes the book condemns and excludes from heaven all wicked, unrighteous persons, particularly those that love and make lies, and therefore can never be itself a lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. I Jesus have sent mine angel to testify unto you these things in the churches. I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star. Henry notes Jesus, as God, is the root of David, though as man his offspring, a person in whom all uncreated and created excellencies meet, too great and too good to deceive his churches, and the fountain of all light giving this morning light of prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The Spirit and the bride say, "Come." And let him that heareth say, "Come." And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely. Henry notes the open and general invitation to all to partake of the gospel's promises, tendered to all who feel a thirst nothing in this world can quench, confirmed by the joint testimony of the Spirit of God and the Spirit in all true members of the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. If any man shall add unto these things, God shall add unto him the plagues that are written in this book. Henry notes this most solemn sanction, like a flaming sword, guards the canon of scripture from profane hands, as God set a fence about the law and the whole Old Testament. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. If any man shall take away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his part out of the book of life, and out of the holy city, and from the things which are written in this book. Henry notes he who takes anything away cuts himself off from all the promises and privileges of the book, which is of the most sacred nature, divine authority, and last importance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. He which testifieth these things saith, "Surely I come quickly. Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus." Henry notes Christ parts with a promise of speedy return, and the church echoes it in firm belief and earnest desire: what comes from heaven in a promise should be sent back to heaven in a prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen. Henry notes the Bible ends with a clear proof of the Godhead of Christ, since the apostle blesses his people in Christ's name and begs a blessing from Christ, a proper act of adoration, and nothing should be more desired than that his grace may prepare us for his glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms
