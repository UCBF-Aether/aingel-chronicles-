The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Haggai 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the second year of Darius the king, in the sixth month, in the first day of the month, came the word of the LORD by Haggai the prophet unto Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, saying,

2 Thus speaketh the LORD of hosts, saying, This people say, The time is not come, the time that the LORD’s house should be built.

3 Then came the word of the LORD by Haggai the prophet, saying,

4 Is it time for you, O ye, to dwell in your cieled houses, and this house lie waste?

5 Now therefore thus saith the LORD of hosts; Consider your ways.

6 Ye have sown much, and bring in little; ye eat, but ye have not enough; ye drink, but ye are not filled with drink; ye clothe you, but there is none warm; and he that earneth wages earneth wages to put it into a bag with holes.

7 Thus saith the LORD of hosts; Consider your ways.

8 Go up to the mountain, and bring wood, and build the house; and I will take pleasure in it, and I will be glorified, saith the LORD.

9 Ye looked for much, and, lo it came to little; and when ye brought it home, I did blow upon it. Why? saith the LORD of hosts. Because of mine house that is waste, and ye run every man unto his own house.

10 Therefore the heaven over you is stayed from dew, and the earth is stayed from her fruit.

11 And I called for a drought upon the land, and upon the mountains, and upon the corn, and upon the new wine, and upon the oil, and upon that which the ground bringeth forth, and upon men, and upon cattle, and upon all the labour of the hands.

12 Then Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, with all the remnant of the people, obeyed the voice of the LORD their God, and the words of Haggai the prophet, as the LORD their God had sent him, and the people did fear before the LORD.

13 Then spake Haggai the LORD’s messenger in the LORD’s message unto the people, saying, I am with you, saith the LORD.

14 And the LORD stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and the spirit of Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, and the spirit of all the remnant of the people; and they came and did work in the house of the LORD of hosts, their God,

15 In the four and twentieth day of the sixth month, in the second year of Darius the king.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the second year of Darius, in the sixth month, the word of the LORD comes by Haggai to Zerubbabel the governor and Joshua the high priest against the people's excuse that the time has not come to build the LORD's house. Is it time, the prophet asks, for you to dwell in cieled houses while this house lies waste? Twice comes the command to consider your ways: they sow much and bring in little, eat and are not satisfied, earn wages to put into a bag with holes, because God's house lies waste while every man runs to his own house, and heaven is stayed from dew and earth from fruit. Then Zerubbabel, Joshua, and all the remnant obey and fear before the LORD, Haggai answers 'I am with you,' and work begins on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth month. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the second year of Darius the Persian king, God's word came by Haggai to Zerubbabel the governor and Joshua the high priest. JFB notes the Jews, having no king of their own, dated by the reigning world king, and Haggai addresses civil and religious leaders together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The people said the time had not come to build the Lord's house, pleading delay as if past interruption proved God's timing. JFB calls this ingenuity in excuses: their real motive was selfish dislike of trouble, expense, and danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God's word came again by Haggai to answer that excuse directly. Henry groups this verse with the whole reproof of verses 1-11: the people did not refuse outright, they only said, Not yet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Is it time for you to dwell in panelled, luxurious houses while God's house lies waste? The shameful contrast, JFB notes, is between their comfort and a temple whose walls were not even raised above the foundations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Therefore the Lord of hosts says, Consider your ways: set your heart on what you have done and what you have suffered. Pondering earnestly whether seeking self at God's expense has actually gained anything is the first step back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They sowed much and brought in little, ate without being satisfied, drank without being filled, clothed themselves without warmth, and wages vanished like money put into a bag with holes. The punishment answered the sin: the poverty they hoped to avoid by neglecting the temple came upon them for neglecting it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Again the Lord says, Consider your ways, repeating the call to self-examination. Henry urges every reader to examine his own spiritual state rather than prying into other people's ways while careless of his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Go up to the mountain, bring wood, and build the house, and God will take pleasure in it and be glorified. Wood is named as the first necessity from the wooded hills around, not to the exclusion of stone, and building honours God, whose presence the temple mirrors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They looked for much and it came to little, and even the little brought home God blew upon and scattered. The reason is given plainly: his house lay waste while every man ran eagerly to his own house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Therefore heaven was stayed from dew and earth from her fruit, as inanimate nature obeys God's will. JFB pictures the sky withholding its goods, shocked, as it were, at the people's disobedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. God himself called for drought on land, mountains, corn, wine, oil, all the ground's produce, men, cattle, and all the labour of their hands. The wordplay is pointed: they left his house waste, so a wasting drought came on all that was theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Then Zerubbabel, Joshua, and all the remnant obeyed the voice of the Lord and Haggai's words, and the people feared before the Lord. Their purpose of obedience counted as obedience in God's eyes even before the work itself began. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Haggai, the Lord's messenger speaking in the Lord's message, answered them: I am with you, says the Lord. On the mere disposition to obey, God at once passes from reproof to tenderness, and his presence is the best of blessings, guaranteeing success. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The Lord stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel, of Joshua, and of all the remnant, and they came and did work in the house of the Lord. Every good impulse and revival, JFB notes, is God's direct work by his Spirit, giving slothful people alacrity and perseverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. This began on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth month, in the second year of Darius, twenty-three days after Haggai's first message. Those who have lost time, Henry urges, need to redeem it, and the longer they loitered, the more haste they should make. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Cieled houses. panelled, finished houses, the comfortable homes Haggai contrasts with the LORD's house lying waste. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
