The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Kings 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 So king Solomon was king over all Israel.

2 And these were the princes which he had; Azariah the son of Zadok the priest,

3 Elihoreph and Ahiah, the sons of Shisha, scribes; Jehoshaphat the son of Ahilud, the recorder.

4 And Benaiah the son of Jehoiada was over the host: and Zadok and Abiathar were the priests:

5 And Azariah the son of Nathan was over the officers: and Zabud the son of Nathan was principal officer, and the king’s friend:

6 And Ahishar was over the household: and Adoniram the son of Abda was over the tribute.

7 And Solomon had twelve officers over all Israel, which provided victuals for the king and his household: each man his month in a year made provision.

8 And these are their names: The son of Hur, in mount Ephraim:

9 The son of Dekar, in Makaz, and in Shaalbim, and Bethshemesh, and Elonbethhanan:

10 The son of Hesed, in Aruboth; to him pertained Sochoh, and all the land of Hepher:

11 The son of Abinadab, in all the region of Dor; which had Taphath the daughter of Solomon to wife:

12 Baana the son of Ahilud; to him pertained Taanach and Megiddo, and all Bethshean, which is by Zartanah beneath Jezreel, from Bethshean to Abelmeholah, even unto the place that is beyond Jokneam:

13 The son of Geber, in Ramothgilead; to him pertained the towns of Jair the son of Manasseh, which are in Gilead; to him also pertained the region of Argob, which is in Bashan, threescore great cities with walls and brasen bars:

14 Ahinadab the son of Iddo had Mahanaim:

15 Ahimaaz was in Naphtali; he also took Basmath the daughter of Solomon to wife:

16 Baanah the son of Hushai was in Asher and in Aloth:

17 Jehoshaphat the son of Paruah, in Issachar:

18 Shimei the son of Elah, in Benjamin:

19 Geber the son of Uri was in the country of Gilead, in the country of Sihon king of the Amorites, and of Og king of Bashan; and he was the only officer which was in the land.

20 Judah and Israel were many, as the sand which is by the sea in multitude, eating and drinking, and making merry.

21 And Solomon reigned over all kingdoms from the river unto the land of the Philistines, and unto the border of Egypt: they brought presents, and served Solomon all the days of his life.

22 And Solomon’s provision for one day was thirty measures of fine flour, and threescore measures of meal,

23 Ten fat oxen, and twenty oxen out of the pastures, and an hundred sheep, beside harts, and roebucks, and fallowdeer, and fatted fowl.

24 For he had dominion over all the region on this side the river, from Tiphsah even to Azzah, over all the kings on this side the river: and he had peace on all sides round about him.

25 And Judah and Israel dwelt safely, every man under his vine and under his fig tree, from Dan even to Beersheba, all the days of Solomon.

26 And Solomon had forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen.

27 And those officers provided victual for king Solomon, and for all that came unto king Solomon’s table, every man in his month: they lacked nothing.

28 Barley also and straw for the horses and dromedaries brought they unto the place where the officers were, every man according to his charge.

29 And God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding exceeding much, and largeness of heart, even as the sand that is on the sea shore.

30 And Solomon’s wisdom excelled the wisdom of all the children of the east country, and all the wisdom of Egypt.

31 For he was wiser than all men; than Ethan the Ezrahite, and Heman, and Chalcol, and Darda, the sons of Mahol: and his fame was in all nations round about.

32 And he spake three thousand proverbs: and his songs were a thousand and five.

33 And he spake of trees, from the cedar tree that is in Lebanon even unto the hyssop that springeth out of the wall: he spake also of beasts, and of fowl, and of creeping things, and of fishes.

34 And there came of all people to hear the wisdom of Solomon, from all kings of the earth, which had heard of his wisdom.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Solomon's court is listed, officials, twelve providers for the royal household month by month, and the sheer scale of daily provisions, and his wisdom is described as wider than all the East, spoken in thousands of proverbs and songs. The chapter is a portrait of a kingdom at peace and plenty. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Solomon was king over all Israel, the kingdom undivided and at peace under one sceptre at the height of its power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Azariah son of Zadok served as priest, heading a court whose officers Henry lists as the instruments of a well-ordered government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Solomon kept his father's officers, the scribes and the keeper of the great seal, honouring his father's memory rather than affecting to be wiser than him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Benaiah commanded the host, and Zadok and Abiathar were priests, though Abiathar's name lingered after his removal, as a former dignity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Solomon raised priests' and prophet's sons to first rank, honouring their fathers: Azariah as chief minister, and Nathan's sons beside him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Ahishar was over the household and Adoniram over the tribute, the steward of the palace and the overseer of the forced labour and revenues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Twelve officers over all Israel provided victuals for the king's household, each for one month in the year, an orderly rotation that fed a great court without burdening one district. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Judah and Israel were many as the sand by the sea, eating, drinking, and making merry, the promised multitude now enjoying peace and plenty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Neighbouring princes held their crowns from Solomon and brought presents, a subjection David won by war and Solomon made easy by wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. His daily provision, thirty measures of fine flour and sixty of meal, shows a court of vast scale, yet fed by the abundance of a peaceful land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Ten fat oxen, twenty pasture oxen, a hundred sheep, plus deer and fowl, made up the daily table, a plenty Henry reads as the fruit of God's blessing on the kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. No subject nation offered to shake off his yoke or give him disturbance; they thought themselves happy in their dependence, and he had peace on every side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Judah and Israel dwelt safely, every man under his vine and fig tree, from Dan to Beersheba, the classic picture of a people at rest under a just king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Solomon had forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, a military strength that later became a snare, though here it marks his power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The purveyors' trust was divided into many hands, so no man was continually burdened with it nor grew exorbitantly rich by it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The same officers who provided for the household provided also for the stable, every man knowing his place, work, and time, so the great court was kept without confusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. God gave Solomon largeness of heart: a vast compass of knowledge, able to take things entire and lay them together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. His wisdom excelled the wisdom of the east and of Egypt, the two famed schools of ancient learning, so that Solomon stood above them all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Solomon excelled even the reputed wise men of his day and confounded them, his counsel proving much more valuable than theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. He spoke three thousand proverbs and his songs were a thousand and five, a written wisdom of which only a part survives, yet enough to show its range. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. He spoke of trees, from the cedar of Lebanon to the hyssop on the wall, and of beasts, birds, creeping things, and fishes, a natural philosophy joined to moral wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. People came from all nations, sent by kings who had heard of his wisdom, to hear Solomon, so that wisdom itself became an embassy for Israel's God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
