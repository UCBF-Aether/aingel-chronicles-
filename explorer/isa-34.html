The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 34 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Come near, ye nations, to hear; and hearken, ye people: let the earth hear, and all that is therein; the world, and all things that come forth of it.

2 For the indignation of the LORD is upon all nations, and his fury upon all their armies: he hath utterly destroyed them, he hath delivered them to the slaughter.

3 Their slain also shall be cast out, and their stink shall come up out of their carcases, and the mountains shall be melted with their blood.

4 And all the host of heaven shall be dissolved, and the heavens shall be rolled together as a scroll: and all their host shall fall down, as the leaf falleth off from the vine, and as a falling fig from the fig tree.

5 For my sword shall be bathed in heaven: behold, it shall come down upon Idumea, and upon the people of my curse, to judgment.

6 The sword of the LORD is filled with blood, it is made fat with fatness, and with the blood of lambs and goats, with the fat of the kidneys of rams: for the LORD hath a sacrifice in Bozrah, and a great slaughter in the land of Idumea.

7 And the unicorns shall come down with them, and the bullocks with the bulls; and their land shall be soaked with blood, and their dust made fat with fatness.

8 For it is the day of the LORD’s vengeance, and the year of recompences for the controversy of Zion.

9 And the streams thereof shall be turned into pitch, and the dust thereof into brimstone, and the land thereof shall become burning pitch.

10 It shall not be quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof shall go up for ever: from generation to generation it shall lie waste; none shall pass through it for ever and ever.

11 But the cormorant and the bittern shall possess it; the owl also and the raven shall dwell in it: and he shall stretch out upon it the line of confusion, and the stones of emptiness.

12 They shall call the nobles thereof to the kingdom, but none shall be there, and all her princes shall be nothing.

13 And thorns shall come up in her palaces, nettles and brambles in the fortresses thereof: and it shall be an habitation of dragons, and a court for owls.

14 The wild beasts of the desert shall also meet with the wild beasts of the island, and the satyr shall cry to his fellow; the screech owl also shall rest there, and find for herself a place of rest.

15 There shall the great owl make her nest, and lay, and hatch, and gather under her shadow: there shall the vultures also be gathered, every one with her mate.

16 Seek ye out of the book of the LORD, and read: no one of these shall fail, none shall want her mate: for my mouth it hath commanded, and his spirit it hath gathered them.

17 And he hath cast the lot for them, and his hand hath divided it unto them by line: they shall possess it for ever, from generation to generation shall they dwell therein.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paired with chapter 35, this is judgment on the nations, with Edom standing as representative of all God's people's enemies: the heavens are rolled up, the land is turned to burning pitch, and the ruined country is apportioned by lot to wild creatures, generation after generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Come near, you nations, to hear, and hearken, you people: let the earth hear, and all that is therein, the world, and all things that come forth of it. Chapters 34 and 35 form one prophecy, the former denouncing God's judgment against His people's enemies, of whom Edom is representative, the latter the flourishing state of the church consequent on those judgments; this forms the termination of the prophecies of Isaiah's first part, the historical chapters 36 to 39 excepted, and is a kind of summary of what went before, setting forth the one main truth that Israel will be delivered from all its foes and happier times will succeed under Messiah. All creation is summoned to hear God's judgments, for they set forth His glory, which is the end of creation; that come forth of it answers to all that is therein, or in Hebrew, all whatever fills it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. For the indignation of the Lord is upon all nations, and his fury upon all their armies: he has utterly destroyed them, he has delivered them to the slaughter. Utterly destroyed is rather doomed them to an utter curse, and delivered is rather appointed, that is, appointed to the slaughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Their slain also will be cast out, and their stink will come up out of their carcases, and the mountains will be melted with their blood. Cast out means unburied, as in chapter 14, and melted means washed away as with a descending torrent of blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. All the host of heaven will be dissolved, and the heavens will be rolled together as a scroll, and all their host will fall down, as the leaf falls off from the vine, and as a falling fig from the fig tree. This recalls Psalm 102, Joel, and Matthew 24: dissolved, as in 2 Peter, for violent convulsions of nature are in Scripture the images of great changes in the human world, and will literally accompany them at the winding up of the present dispensation. The scroll recalls Revelation: books in those days were sheets of parchment rolled together, the stars will fall when the heavens in which they are fixed pass away, and the falling leaf recalls the fig tree of Revelation 6. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For my sword will be bathed in heaven: behold, it will come down upon Idumea, and upon the people of my curse, to judgment. The sword is Jeremiah's, or else the knife for sacrifice, for God does not here appear as a warrior with His sword but as one about to sacrifice victims doomed to slaughter; bathed is rather intoxicated, namely with anger, and in heaven implies the place where God's purpose of wrath is formed, in antithesis to its coming down in the next clause. Idumea originally extended from the Dead Sea to the Red Sea, and afterwards obtained the country east of Moab, of which Bozrah was capital; Petra or Selah, called Joktheel, was capital of South Edom. David subjugated Edom, under Jehoram they regained independence, under Amaziah they were again subdued and Selah taken; when Judah was captive in Babylon, Edom insulted over her fallen mistress and killed many Jews whom the Chaldeans had left, and hence was held guilty of fratricide by God, Esau their ancestor having been brother to Jacob — this was the cause of the prophets' denunciations against Edom, and Nebuchadnezzar humbled Idumea accordingly. The people of my curse are those doomed to it, and to judgment means to execute it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The sword of the Lord is filled with blood, it is made fat with fatness, and with the blood of lambs and goats, with the fat of the kidneys of rams: for the Lord has a sacrifice in Bozrah, and a great slaughter in the land of Idumea. Filled is glutted, continuing the sacrifice image; blood and fat are the parts especially devoted to God in a sacrifice, and the lambs and goats are sacrificial animals, the Idumeans of all classes doomed to slaughter. Bozrah, called Bostra by the Romans and assigned in Jeremiah to Moab, seems to have been at one time in Edom's dominion and at another in Moab's; it was strictly not in Edom but the capital of Auranitis, the Houran, though Edom extended its dominion to include it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The unicorns will come down with them, and the bullocks with the bulls, and their land will be soaked with blood, and their dust made fat with fatness. Unicorns in Hebrew, reem, convey loftiness, power, and pre-eminence; at one time the image answers to a reality in nature, at another it symbolizes an abstraction, with the rhinoceros as original type and the Arab rim, a two-horned oryx antelope, bold and pugnacious, from which, when one horn was lost by accident or artifice, the notion of the unicorn arose. Here is meant the portion of the Edomites strong and warlike; come down is rather fall down, slain, with them means with the lambs and goats, the less powerful Edomites, and bullocks with bulls are the young and old Edomites, all classes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For it is the day of the Lord's vengeance, and the year of recompences for the controversy of Zion. The recompences for the controversy of Zion are the year when God will retaliate on those who have contended with Zion, for her controversy is His. Edom had thought to extend its borders by laying hold of its neighbour's lands and instigated Babylon to cruelty towards fallen Judah, therefore Edom will suffer the same herself; and the final winding up of the controversy between God and all enemies of Him and His people is also foreshadowed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Its streams will be turned into pitch, and its dust into brimstone, and its land will become burning pitch. These are images from the overthrow of Sodom and Gomorrah, as in Deuteronomy and Jeremiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. It will not be quenched night nor day; the smoke thereof will go up for ever: from generation to generation it will lie waste; none will pass through it for ever and ever. It is the burning pitch of verse 9, and the smoke forever recalls Revelation; generation to generation recalls Malachi. None passing through is recompense in kind: Edom's original offence was that they would not let Israel pass through their land in peace to Canaan, so no traveller will pass through Edom — Volney the infidel was forced to confirm this prophecy, reporting from the Arabs southeast of the Dead Sea upwards of thirty ruined towns absolutely deserted within three days' journey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. But the cormorant and the bittern will possess it, the owl also and the raven will dwell in it, and he will stretch out upon it the line of confusion, and the stones of emptiness. The cormorant in Hebrew, rendered pelican in Psalm 102, is a seafowl and cannot be meant here; some waterfowl that tenants desert places is intended. The bittern is rather the hedgehog or porcupine, and the owl, enumerated among water birds in Leviticus and Deuteronomy, may rather be the heron or crane, from a Hebrew root to blow, as it utters a sound like blowing a horn. Confusion is devastation, and the line and stones are a metaphor from an architect with line and plummet-stone: God will render the exact measure of justice without mercy, and emptiness is desolation, for Edom is now a waste of stones. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They will call its nobles unto the kingdom, but none will be there, and all its princes will be nothing. Rather, as to her nobles, there will be none there who will declare a kingdom, that is, a king, or else there will be no one there whom they will call to the kingdom; Idumea was at first governed by dukes, out of whom the king was chosen when the constitution became a monarchy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Thorns will come up in its palaces, nettles and brambles in its fortresses, and it will be a habitation of dragons, and a court for owls. The dragons are as in chapter 13, and the court for owls is rather a dwelling for ostriches, in the desolate palaces and fortresses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The wild beasts of the desert will also meet with the wild beasts of the island, and the satyr will cry to his fellow; the screech owl also will rest there, and find for herself a place of rest. Island is rather wild cats and jackals, as in chapter 13, and the screech owl is rather the night spectre, in Jewish superstition a female elegantly dressed who carried off children by night; the text does not assert the existence of such objects of superstition, but describes the place as one superstition would people with such beings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. There the great owl will make her nest, and lay, and hatch, and gather under her shadow; there the vultures also will be gathered, every one with her mate. The great owl is rather the arrow snake, so called from darting on its prey; lay means eggs, and gather under her shadow is rather cherish her young under it, while the vultures gather each with her mate in desolate Idumea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Seek out of the book of the Lord, and read: no one of these will fail, none will want her mate: for my mouth it has commanded, and his spirit it has gathered them. The book of the Lord is the volume in which the various prophecies and other parts of Scripture began henceforward to be collected together; seek is as in chapters 8 and 30 and John's gospel, and no one failing recalls Matthew 5. None wanting her mate is an image from the pairing of animals in verse 15: no prediction will want a fulfilment as its companion, or rather none of these wild animals will be wanting, none without its mate to pair and breed with in desolate Idumea; such changes of person are frequent in Hebrew poetry, and them are the wild beasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. He has cast the lot for them, and his hand has divided it unto them by line: they will possess it for ever, from generation to generation they will dwell therein. The lot is as conquerors apportion lands by lot, so Jehovah has appointed and marked out, divided, Edom for the wild beasts, to possess it forever from generation to generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
