The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Amos 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Thus hath the Lord GOD shewed unto me; and, behold, he formed grasshoppers in the beginning of the shooting up of the latter growth; and, lo, it was the latter growth after the king’s mowings.

2 And it came to pass, that when they had made an end of eating the grass of the land, then I said, O Lord GOD, forgive, I beseech thee: by whom shall Jacob arise? for he is small.

3 The LORD repented for this: It shall not be, saith the LORD.

4 Thus hath the Lord GOD shewed unto me: and, behold, the Lord GOD called to contend by fire, and it devoured the great deep, and did eat up a part.

5 Then said I, O Lord GOD, cease, I beseech thee: by whom shall Jacob arise? for he is small.

6 The LORD repented for this: This also shall not be, saith the Lord GOD.

7 Thus he shewed me: and, behold, the LORD stood upon a wall made by a plumbline, with a plumbline in his hand.

8 And the LORD said unto me, Amos, what seest thou? And I said, A plumbline. Then said the LORD, Behold, I will set a plumbline in the midst of my people Israel: I will not again pass by them any more:

9 And the high places of Isaac shall be desolate, and the sanctuaries of Israel shall be laid waste; and I will rise against the house of Jeroboam with the sword.

10 Then Amaziah the priest of Bethel sent to Jeroboam king of Israel, saying, Amos hath conspired against thee in the midst of the house of Israel: the land is not able to bear all his words.

11 For thus Amos saith, Jeroboam shall die by the sword, and Israel shall surely be led away captive out of their own land.

12 Also Amaziah said unto Amos, O thou seer, go, flee thee away into the land of Judah, and there eat bread, and prophesy there:

13 But prophesy not again any more at Bethel: for it is the king’s chapel, and it is the king’s court.

14 Then answered Amos, and said to Amaziah, I was no prophet, neither was I a prophet’s son; but I was an herdman, and a gatherer of sycomore fruit:

15 And the LORD took me as I followed the flock, and the LORD said unto me, Go, prophesy unto my people Israel.

16 Now therefore hear thou the word of the LORD: Thou sayest, Prophesy not against Israel, and drop not thy word against the house of Isaac.

17 Therefore thus saith the LORD; Thy wife shall be an harlot in the city, and thy sons and thy daughters shall fall by the sword, and thy land shall be divided by line; and thou shalt die in a polluted land: and Israel shall surely go into captivity forth of his land.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Three visions come in turn: locusts devouring the latter growth, fire devouring the deep, and the LORD standing on a wall with a plumbline in his hand. Amos intercedes against the first two and judgment is turned back, but at the plumbline God says he will not pass by Israel any more. Then Amaziah, priest of Bethel, accuses Amos of conspiracy and tells him to flee to Judah, and Amos answers with judgment on Amaziah's own house. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Thus the Lord God showed Amos: behold, he formed grasshoppers at the beginning of the latter growth's shooting up, after the king's mowings, the second crop, the people's sustenance, threatened at its most vulnerable moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And when they had made an end of eating the land's grass, Amos said, O Lord God, forgive, I beseech thee, by whom shall Jacob arise, for he is small, the prophet most earnest for forgiveness of sin, since sin will soon make a great people small. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The Lord repented for this: it shall not be, says the Lord, showing how ready and swift God is to show mercy and how he waits to be gracious, and what a blessing praying people are to a land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Thus the Lord God showed Amos: behold, the Lord God called to contend by fire, and it devoured the great deep and ate up a part, judgment by fire consuming even the sea's depths. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Then Amos said, O Lord God, cease, I beseech thee, by whom shall Jacob arise, for he is small, repeated prayer meeting repeated mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The Lord repented for this also: this also shall not be, says the Lord God, God bearing long, though he will not bear always with a provoking people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Thus he showed Amos: behold, the Lord stood upon a wall made by a plumbline, with a plumbline in his hand, Israel being a wall God himself reared as defense to his sanctuary, now measured and found bowing and bulging. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And the Lord said, Amos, what seest thou, and he said, a plumbline, then the Lord said, behold, I will set a plumbline in the midst of my people Israel, I will not again pass by them any more, the time coming when those spared often shall be spared no longer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And Isaac's high places shall be desolate, Israel's sanctuaries laid waste, and God will rise against Jeroboam's house with the sword, the idolatrous sanctuaries and the royal house both falling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Then Amaziah, priest of Bethel, sent to Jeroboam king of Israel, saying, Amos has conspired against thee in Israel's midst, the land not able to bear all his words, accusers misrepresenting God's friends as traitors and troublers of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. For thus Amos says, Jeroboam shall die by the sword, and Israel shall surely be led captive out of their own land, Amaziah's report distorting the prophecy into a personal conspiracy charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Also Amaziah said to Amos, O seer, flee away to Judah, eat bread and prophesy there, those who make gain their godliness thinking wealth and preferment the most powerful motives with others too. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. But prophesy not again any more at Bethel, for it is the king's chapel and the king's court, the priest defending a royal sanctuary against God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Then Amos answered Amaziah: I was no prophet, neither a prophet's son, but a herdman and gatherer of sycamore fruit, the Lord often choosing weak and foolish things to confound the wise and mighty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And the Lord took him as he followed the flock, and said, go, prophesy to my people Israel, a warrant from God that ought not to fear the face of man, and which, if God had not strengthened him, could not have set his face as flint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Now therefore hear the word of the Lord: thou sayest, prophesy not against Israel, and drop not thy word against Isaac's house, Amaziah's prohibition itself becoming the text of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Therefore thus says the Lord: thy wife shall be a harlot in the city, thy sons and daughters shall fall by the sword, thy land shall be divided by line, thou shalt die in a polluted land, and Israel shall surely go into captivity forth of his land, since all who oppose or despise God's word must expect fatal effects unless they repent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Plumbline. a builder's weighted line for testing whether a wall stands true; in Amos 7 God sets it in the midst of Israel, a wall he himself reared, now measured and found bowing. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
