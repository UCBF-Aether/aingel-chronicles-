The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 28 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Unto thee will I cry, O LORD my rock; be not silent to me: lest, if thou be silent to me, I become like them that go down into the pit.

2 Hear the voice of my supplications, when I cry unto thee, when I lift up my hands toward thy holy oracle.

3 Draw me not away with the wicked, and with the workers of iniquity, which speak peace to their neighbours, but mischief is in their hearts.

4 Give them according to their deeds, and according to the wickedness of their endeavours: give them after the work of their hands; render to them their desert.

5 Because they regard not the works of the LORD, nor the operation of his hands, he shall destroy them, and not build them up.

6 Blessed be the LORD, because he hath heard the voice of my supplications.

7 The LORD is my strength and my shield; my heart trusted in him, and I am helped: therefore my heart greatly rejoiceth; and with my song will I praise him.

8 The LORD is their strength, and he is the saving strength of his anointed.

9 Save thy people, and bless thine inheritance: feed them also, and lift them up for ever.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Cry to the rock against God's silence: hands lifted toward the holy oracle, prayer not to share the fate of peace-speaking workers of iniquity, justice matched to deeds, then the turn, the Lord has heard, strength and shield, joy and song, widening to save, bless, feed, and lift his people for ever. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Heading note: the text panel prints the verses only, without a heading. The reference to David in the verse notes (verse 8) comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Unto you I will cry, O Lord my rock; be not silent to me, lest if you are silent I become like those who go down to the pit: God's silence would leave him as good as dead, so urgent is the need for an answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Hear the voice of my supplications when I cry to you, lifting my hands toward your holy oracle: hands lifted toward the mercy-seat accompany the cry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Draw me not away with the wicked and workers of iniquity, who speak peace to neighbours but have mischief in their hearts: he prays not to share the fate of the peace-speaking, mischief-plotting wicked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Give them according to their deeds and the wickedness of their endeavours, after the work of their hands; render to them their desert: justice is asked to match deed for deed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Because they regard not the works of the Lord nor the operation of his hands, he shall destroy them and not build them up: contempt for God's works ends in being unbuilt — destroyed, not restored. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Blessed be the Lord, because he has heard the voice of my supplications: the psalm turns, as often, from petition to blessing on the strength of heard prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The Lord is my strength and my shield; my heart trusted in him and I am helped; therefore my heart greatly rejoices, and with my song I will praise him: trust, help, joy, and song follow in order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The Lord is their strength, and the saving strength of his anointed: the help given to David is the people's help, and the anointed king's strength is theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Save your people, bless your inheritance, feed them also, and lift them up for ever: the prayer widens to shepherding — saving, blessing, feeding, and lifting the people for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
