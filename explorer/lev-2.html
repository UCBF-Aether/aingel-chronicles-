The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when any will offer a meat offering unto the LORD, his offering shall be of fine flour; and he shall pour oil upon it, and put frankincense thereon:

2 And he shall bring it to Aaron’s sons the priests: and he shall take thereout his handful of the flour thereof, and of the oil thereof, with all the frankincense thereof; and the priest shall burn the memorial of it upon the altar, to be an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD:

3 And the remnant of the meat offering shall be Aaron’s and his sons’: it is a thing most holy of the offerings of the LORD made by fire.

4 And if thou bring an oblation of a meat offering baken in the oven, it shall be unleavened cakes of fine flour mingled with oil, or unleavened wafers anointed with oil.

5 And if thy oblation be a meat offering baken in a pan, it shall be of fine flour unleavened, mingled with oil.

6 Thou shalt part it in pieces, and pour oil thereon: it is a meat offering.

7 And if thy oblation be a meat offering baken in the fryingpan, it shall be made of fine flour with oil.

8 And thou shalt bring the meat offering that is made of these things unto the LORD: and when it is presented unto the priest, he shall bring it unto the altar.

9 And the priest shall take from the meat offering a memorial thereof, and shall burn it upon the altar: it is an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.

10 And that which is left of the meat offering shall be Aaron’s and his sons’: it is a thing most holy of the offerings of the LORD made by fire.

11 No meat offering, which ye shall bring unto the LORD, shall be made with leaven: for ye shall burn no leaven, nor any honey, in any offering of the LORD made by fire.

12 As for the oblation of the firstfruits, ye shall offer them unto the LORD: but they shall not be burnt on the altar for a sweet savour.

13 And every oblation of thy meat offering shalt thou season with salt; neither shalt thou suffer the salt of the covenant of thy God to be lacking from thy meat offering: with all thine offerings thou shalt offer salt.

14 And if thou offer a meat offering of thy firstfruits unto the LORD, thou shalt offer for the meat offering of thy firstfruits green ears of corn dried by the fire, even corn beaten out of full ears.

15 And thou shalt put oil upon it, and lay frankincense thereon: it is a meat offering.

16 And the priest shall burn the memorial of it, part of the beaten corn thereof, and part of the oil thereof, with all the frankincense thereof: it is an offering made by fire unto the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the law of the meat offering, that is, a bloodless gift offering of food, usually fine flour with oil and frankincense. Only a memorial portion is burnt on the altar; the rest belongs to Aaron and his sons. It covers unbaked flour, oven-baked cakes, pan-baked and frying-pan offerings, and firstfruits of green ears, with two standing rules: no leaven or honey may be burnt, and every offering must be seasoned with salt. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Meat offering. Here 'meat' means food generally: a bloodless gift offering, usually of fine flour with oil and frankincense. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Memorial. The handful of flour, oil, and all the frankincense that the priest burns on the altar, standing for the whole offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The “meat offering” is a bloodless gift offering, “meat” here meaning food generally, here the flour of wheat. These voluntary oblations, offered alone, are a thankful acknowledgment of the bounty of Providence. Oil symbolises the influences of the Spirit, as frankincense symbolises prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The priest burns only a memorial portion, a part of the offering, with all the frankincense, as an offering made by fire of a sweet savour unto the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The remnant goes to Aaron and his sons, and this appropriation to the priests distinguishes the meat offering from a burnt offering. They alone partake of it within the sacred precincts, as among the most holy things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. An oven-baked offering is unleavened cakes of fine flour mingled with oil, or wafers anointed with oil. The Eastern oven is generally a circular hole in the floor, plastered inside, on which cakes are baked like pancakes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. A pan-baked offering is fine flour unleavened, mingled with oil, baked on a thin plate of copper or iron over a slow fire, like a girdle for baking oatmeal cakes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The pan offering is parted in pieces and oil poured on it, as Eastern people commonly eat broken bread dipped in oil. The offering was prepared beforehand by the offerer, and the priest took it from his hands and burnt a portion on the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. A fryingpan offering follows the same pattern: fine flour with oil, prepared by the offerer and presented for the priest to burn a memorial portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Whatever its form, the meat offering is brought to the LORD, presented to the priest, and brought by him to the altar, following the same order as the offerings already described. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The priest takes a memorial from the offering and burns it on the altar, an offering made by fire of a sweet savour unto the LORD, as in the unbaked offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. What is left of the offering is Aaron's and his sons', a thing most holy of the offerings made by fire, just as with the offering of fine flour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. No meat offering may be made with leaven, and no leaven or honey may be burnt in a fire offering. Nothing sweet or sour was to be offered: leavened bread soon spoils in warm climates, and leaven was regarded as an emblem of hypocrisy or corruption, while some think the ban reflects leaven and honey's use in heathen rites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Firstfruits oblations are voluntary offerings from a person's increase, in which leaven and honey might be used. Though presented at the altar, they are not burnt for a sweet savour but assigned by God for the priests' use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Every meat offering must be seasoned with salt, the salt of the covenant never lacking. Salt, unlike leaven, preserves and does not putrefy, so it became an emblem of incorruption and purity and of a perpetual covenant, a perfect reconciliation and lasting friendship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. A firstfruits meat offering of green ears of corn, dried by fire and beaten out of full ears, seems to be a voluntary offering before the harvest, the ears parched at the fire in the favourite Eastern way. It is an early tribute of pious thankfulness for the earth's increase, offered by the usual directions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Oil is put on the firstfruits offering and frankincense laid on it, as with the offering of fine flour, the oil symbolising the Spirit and the frankincense prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The priest burns the memorial of it, part of the beaten corn and part of the oil, with all the frankincense, an offering made by fire unto the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
