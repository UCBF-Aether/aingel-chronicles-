The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 135 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Praise ye the LORD. Praise ye the name of the LORD; praise him, O ye servants of the LORD.

2 Ye that stand in the house of the LORD, in the courts of the house of our God.

3 Praise the LORD; for the LORD is good: sing praises unto his name; for it is pleasant.

4 For the LORD hath chosen Jacob unto himself, and Israel for his peculiar treasure.

5 For I know that the LORD is great, and that our Lord is above all gods.

6 Whatsoever the LORD pleased, that did he in heaven, and in earth, in the seas, and all deep places.

7 He causeth the vapours to ascend from the ends of the earth; he maketh lightnings for the rain; he bringeth the wind out of his treasuries.

8 Who smote the firstborn of Egypt, both of man and beast.

9 Who sent tokens and wonders into the midst of thee, O Egypt, upon Pharaoh, and upon all his servants.

10 Who smote great nations, and slew mighty kings;

11 Sihon king of the Amorites, and Og king of Bashan, and all the kingdoms of Canaan:

12 And gave their land for an heritage, an heritage unto Israel his people.

13 Thy name, O LORD, endureth for ever; and thy memorial, O LORD, throughout all generations.

14 For the LORD will judge his people, and he will repent himself concerning his servants.

15 The idols of the heathen are silver and gold, the work of men’s hands.

16 They have mouths, but they speak not; eyes have they, but they see not;

17 They have ears, but they hear not; neither is there any breath in their mouths.

18 They that make them are like unto them: so is every one that trusteth in them.

19 Bless the LORD, O house of Israel: bless the LORD, O house of Aaron:

20 Bless the LORD, O house of Levi: ye that fear the LORD, bless the LORD.

21 Blessed be the LORD out of Zion, which dwelleth at Jerusalem. Praise ye the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A praise psalm that gathers creation, Exodus and the folly of idols into one song. Servants standing in the house of the LORD are called to praise his name, for the LORD is good, has chosen Jacob for himself and Israel for his peculiar treasure, and is great above all gods, doing whatever he pleases in heaven, earth, seas and deep places — causing vapours to ascend, making lightnings for the rain, bringing wind out of his treasuries. The song then recalls Egypt's firstborn smitten, tokens and wonders sent on Pharaoh, great nations and mighty kings smitten — Sihon of the Amorites, Og of Bashan, all the kingdoms of Canaan — and their land given as heritage to Israel. God's name endures for ever, he will judge his people and repent himself concerning his servants. Against him stand the idols of the heathen, silver and gold, the work of men's hands, with mouths that speak not and no breath in them, and those who make them are like them. The psalm closes by calling house of Israel, house of Aaron, house of Levi and all who fear the LORD to bless the LORD out of Zion, who dwells at Jerusalem. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry opens a Hallelujah-psalm — title and Amen both, Alpha and Omega — call to praise Lord, praise his name, praise him and again praise him; not only thank for what done for us but praise for what he is in himself and done for others, take all occasions speak well of God give truths and ways good word; persons called servants of Lord, priests Levites that stand in his house, all devout pious Israelites that stand in courts to worship; those most reason who admitted privileges of house, there behold beauty taste bounty, from them expected for to that end enjoy places — who should praise if they do not? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry stands in house and courts: servants standing in house of Lord, in courts of house of our God — the place of privilege and worship where beauty beheld and bounty tasted, whence praise is expected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry praises because good and pleasant: he whom we praise is good, goodness that which everybody will speak well of, good to all, praise of that, goodness his glory mention to his glory; work its own wages — sing praises for it is pleasant, best done cheerful spirit, pleasure of having done duty, heaven upon earth praising God, pleasure should quite put mouths out of taste for pleasures of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry chooses Jacob: Lord hath chosen Jacob to himself, therefore Jacob bound praise, God chose people to himself that they might be unto him for name and praise (Jer 13:11), therefore Jacob abundant matter being dignified distinguished; Israel God's peculiar treasure above all people (Exod 19:5), his Segullah people appropriated delight in precious honourable; for distinguishing surprising favour if seed of Jacob do not praise most unworthy ungrateful people under sun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry asserts greatness with assurance: Lord great, great indeed, knows no limits time or place; know not only by observation proofs but belief revelation, sure by own experience divine greatness working on soul; holy defiance of all pretenders though join confederacy, not only above any god but above all gods infinitely, between him and them no comparison; goodness matter of cheerful praises, greatness matter of awful praises, most copious because less forward to consider. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry proves greatness by power: absolute power may do what he will — whatsoever Lord pleased that did he, none control or say What doest thou, does what pleases because pleases gives not account of matters; almighty power can do what he will, if he will work none hinder; absolute almighty power universal extent — does what he will in heaven earth seas all deep places bottom of sea bowels of earth; gods of heathen can do nothing, our God can do anything and does everything. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry instances power in nature: all powers of nature prove greatness of God of nature derived depended; chain natural causes framed at first still preserved; exhalations drawn up from terraqueous globe — heat of sun raises them but power from God, nothing hidden from heat (Ps 19:6), causes vapours ascend unseen from earth ends of earth seas surrounding; out of vapours forms rain earth no loser returned with advantage fruitful showers; out of same vapours makes lightnings for rain, opens bottles heaven shakes clouds water earth, fire water reconciled omnipotence together yet water not quench fire nor fire lick water as fire from heaven when God pleased (1 Kings 18:38); same exhalations converted winds blow where list what compass will, far from directing cannot tell whence whither, God brings out treasuries exactness design as prudent prince orders money exchequer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry smites Egypt's firstborn: sovereign dominion irresistible power bringing Israel out Egypt humbling Pharaoh many plagues forcing let go; plagues tokens wonders not common providence miraculous each, sent upon Pharaoh all servants subjects, Israelites God claimed servants son first-born free-born exempted no plague nigh dwelling; death first-born men and cattle heaviest of all plagues and that which gained point. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry sends tokens and wonders into Egypt: plagues upon Pharaoh and servants as v8 — miraculous tokens not common course, sent against oppressors while God's claimed people exempt, proving greatness as well as goodness and confirming scriptures Moses employed in working miracles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry smites great nations: destroying kingdoms of Canaan before them — possessors all advantages keeping possession, people numerous warlike confederate, great nations, yet if great nation meek mean-spirited prince lies exposed, but these great nations had mighty kings yet all smitten slain; no power hell or earth prevent accomplishment of promise when time set time come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry names Sihon king of Amorites and Og king of Bashan and all kingdoms of Canaan — the mighty kings of v10 smitten and slain, their kingdoms given away, as instances of irresistible power fulfilling promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry gives land heritage: he that gives kingdoms whomsoever pleases gave Canaan heritage to Israel his people; came by inheritance ancestors promise though not possession, descended inheritance to seed; done long before yet God now praised, children now enjoying benefit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry triumphs perpetuity of glory: Thy name O God endures for ever — manifestations to people everlasting fruits consequences, what God doeth shall be for ever (Eccl 3:14), name endures constant everlasting praises, memorial endured hitherto shall still endure throughout generations church; refers Exod 3:15 God Abraham Isaac Jacob adds This is my name for ever memorial unto all generations; God is and will be always same to church gracious faithful wonder-working, church same to him thankful praising, thus name endures for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry judges and repents concerning servants: kind to people — plead cause against contenders, judge his people judge for them not suffer run down; not himself contend for ever but repent himself concerning servants not proceed controversy, be entreated or comforted concerning them, return ways of mercy delight do good; verse taken song of Moses (Deut 32:36). Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry arms against idolatry showing heathen gods as before (Ps 115:4-8): gods of own making, could have no power but makers gave, then what power could makers receive from them? Images work of men's hands, deities supposed inform them creatures of men's fancy imagination; idols silver and gold — matter at best dust metals of earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry mocks senseless shapes: had shape of animals but could not perform least act no not animal life — neither see nor hear nor speak nor so much as breathe; making them eyes ears mouths nostrils such jest wonder reasonable creatures suffer imposed upon expect good from mock-deities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry continues idol impotence: mouths that speak not, eyes see not, ears hear not, no breath in mouths — the senseless image cannot act, completing the catalogue of v16. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry makes worshippers like idols: therefore as stupid senseless as they — both makers to be worshipped and trusters when made; worshipping gods objects of sense and senseless made worshippers sensual senseless; let worshipping God Spirit make us spiritual wise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry calls house of Israel to bless: stir up true devotion, more deplorable Gentile idol condition more bound thank God know better; set about acts devotion employ in them bless Lord again again; parallel place (Ps 115:9-11) inference from impotency idols duty trust Lord, here bless him, putting trust gives glory, dependents shall not want matter thanksgiving; all persons knew God called — house of Israel nation general. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry calls house of Aaron to bless: Lord's ministers attended sanctuary — Aaron's house with Israel called to bless Lord, their ministry itself a call to praise the true God against idols' impotence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry blesses Lord out of Zion: tribute of praise arises out of Zion, all God's works praise him but saints bless him, need not go far pay tribute for he dwells in Jerusalem in his church members of, always nigh receive homage; condescensions grace dwelling with men upon earth call grateful thankful returns repeated Hallelujahs; house of Levi and all fearing Lord though not house of Israel likewise called in this closing exhortation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
