The Text — Jubilees 33 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And Jacob went and dwelt to the south of Magdaladra'ef.

2 And he went to his father Isaac, he and Leah his wife, on the new moon of the tenth month.

3 And Reuben saw Bilhah, Rachel's maid, the concubine of his father, bathing in water in a

4 secret place, and he loved her.

5 And he hid himself at night, and he entered the house of Bilhah [at night], and he found her sleeping alone on a bed in her house.

6 And he lay with her, and she awoke and saw, and behold Reuben was lying with her in the bed, and she uncovered the border of her covering and seized him, and cried out, and discovered that it was Reuben.

7 And she was ashamed because of him, and released her hand from him, and lie fled.

8 And she lamented because of this thing exceedingly, and did not tell it to anyone.

9 And when Jacob returned and sought her, she said unto him : "I am not clean for thee, for I have been defiled as regards thee ; for Reuben hath defiled me, and hath lain with me in the night, and I was asleep, and did not discover until he uncovered my skirt and slept with me." 8.

10 And Jacob was exceedingly wroth with Reuben because he had lain with Bilhah, because he had uncovered his father's skirt.

11 And Jacob did not approach her again because Reuben had defiled her.

12 And as for any man who uncovereth his father's skirt his deed is wicked exceedingly, for he is abominable before the Lord.

13 For this reason it is written and ordained on the heavenly tables that a man should not lie with his father's wife, and should not uncover his father's skirt, for this is unclean : they shall surely die together the man who lieth with his father's wife and the woman also, for they have wrought uncleanness on the earth.

14 And there shall be nothing unclean before our God in the nation which He hath chosen for Himself as a possession.

15 And again, it is written a second time : " Cursed he be who lieth with the wife of his father, for he hath uncovered his father's shame"; and all the holy ones of the Lord said " So be it ; so be it." 13.

16 And do thou, Moses, command the children of Israel that they observe this word ; for it (entaileth) a punishment of death; and it is unclean, and there is no atonement

17 for ever to atone for the man who hath committed this, but he is to be put to death and slain, and stoned with stones, and rooted out from the midst of the people of our God.

18 For to no man who doeth so in Israel is it permitted to remain alive a single day on the earth, for he is abominable and unclean.

19 And let them not say : to Reuben was granted life and forgiveness after he had lain with his father's concubine, and to her also though she had a husband, and her husband Jacob, his father, was still alive.

20 For until that time there had not been revealed the ordinance and judgment and law in its completeness for all, but in thy days (it hath been revealed) as a law of seasons and of days, and an everlasting law for the everlasting generations.

21 And for this law there is no consummation of days, and no atonement for it, but they must both be rooted out in the midst of the nation : on the day whereon they committed it they shall slay them.

22 And clo thou, Moses, write (it) down for Israel that they may observe it, and do according to these words, and not commit a sin unto death ; for the Lord our God is judge, who respecteth not persons and accepteth not gifts.

23 And tell them these words of the covenant, that they may hear and observe, and be on their guard with respect to them, and not be destroyed and rooted out of the land ; for an uncleanncss, and an abomination, and a contamination, and a pollution are all they who commit it on the earth before our God.

24 And there is no greater sin than the fornication which they commit on earth; for Israel is a holy nation unto the Lord its God, and a nation of inheritance, and a priestly and royal nation and for (His own) possession; and there shall no such uncleanncss appear in the midst of the holy nation.

25 And in the third year of this sixth week Jacob and all his sons went and dwelt in the house of Abra-

26 ham, near Isaac his father and Rebecca his mother. 22.

27 And these were the names of the sons of Jacob : the first-born Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulon, the sons of Leah ; and the sons of Rachel, Joseph and Benjamin; and the sons of Bilhah, Dan and Naphtali ; and the sons of Zilpali, Gad and Asher ; and Dinah, the daughter of Leah, the only daughter of Jacob.

28 And they came and bowed themselves to Isaac and Rebecca, and when they saw them thev blessed Jacob and all his sons, and Isaac rejoiced exceedingly, for he saw the sons of Jacob, his younger son, and he blessed them.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: Jacob went and dwelt to the south of Magdaladra'ef, moving his household after the Bethel days. The sons came and bowed themselves to Isaac and Rebecca, who blessed Jacob and all his sons, and Isaac rejoiced exceedingly to see the sons of Jacob his younger son. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. Jacob went and dwelt to the south of Magdaladra'ef, moving his household after the Bethel days. The move places the family near Isaac again, where the next sorrow of the house will fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jacob went to his father Isaac, with Leah his wife, on the new moon of the tenth month. The visit keeps the family together around the aged patriarch, the pattern Henry finds in Jacob's return to Isaac at Hebron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Reuben saw Bilhah, Rachel's maid and his father's concubine, bathing in water in a secret place, and he loved her. Henry's warning about Reuben's sin is that those who promise themselves secrecy in sin are generally disappointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The bathing scene is completed in the next verse's secret entry: Reuben's lust fastened on what he had seen in secret, and secrecy became the cover for the sin itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Reuben hid himself at night, entered Bilhah's house, and found her sleeping alone on a bed in her house. The stealth of the act matches Henry's point that Reuben thought his father would never hear of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Reuben lay with Bilhah, and she awoke to find him lying with her, uncovered the border of her covering, seized him, cried out, and discovered it was Reuben. The cry and discovery end the secrecy at once: the sin was known to its victim the same night. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Bilhah was ashamed because of Reuben, released her hand from him, and he fled. Her shame and his flight divide the guilty from the wronged, though Jubilees' law will later judge them together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Bilhah lamented exceedingly over what had happened and told it to no one. Her silence was grief, not consent, and the lament marks the wrong done to her as well as to her husband's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. When Jacob returned and sought Bilhah, she told him she was not clean for him, for Reuben had defiled her and lain with her in the night while she slept and did not discover it until he uncovered her skirt. Her own account clears her of willingness and lays the deed on Reuben. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jacob was exceedingly wroth with Reuben because he had lain with Bilhah and uncovered his father's skirt. Henry calls Reuben's sin a sore affliction, shown by the words, and Israel heard it, adding that no more is said, but that is enough. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Jacob did not approach Bilhah again because Reuben had defiled her. The broken household relation could not be restored by ignoring the deed, and the separation stands as the lasting mark of Reuben's sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Any man who uncovers his father's skirt commits a deed wicked exceedingly, abominable before the Lord. Henry's verdict on Reuben agrees: by gross sin he forfeited the birthright, and his character was unstable as water, leaving a lasting infamy on his family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. In the third year of this sixth week Jacob and all his sons went and dwelt in the house of Abraham, near Isaac and Rebecca. The household gathered again around the patriarchs after the Reuben sorrow, as it had gathered after each earlier crisis. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The fragment completes the move: Jacob's household dwelt near Isaac his father and Rebecca his mother, the whole family under the patriarchs' roof-tree. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The names of Jacob's sons are listed: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulon, sons of Leah; Joseph and Benjamin, sons of Rachel; Dan and Naphtali, sons of Bilhah; Gad and Asher, sons of Zilpah; and Dinah, daughter of Leah, Jacob's only daughter. Henry notes that all Jacob's sons were living, and that his calling them together was a precept to unite in love and to make one people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The sons came and bowed themselves to Isaac and Rebecca, who blessed Jacob and all his sons, and Isaac rejoiced exceedingly to see the sons of Jacob his younger son. The blessing of the gathered household closes the chapter as the family stands whole before its patriarchs, despite Reuben's sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
