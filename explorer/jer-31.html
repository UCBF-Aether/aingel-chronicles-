The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 31 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 At the same time, saith the LORD, will I be the God of all the families of Israel, and they shall be my people.

2 Thus saith the LORD, The people which were left of the sword found grace in the wilderness; even Israel, when I went to cause him to rest.

3 The LORD hath appeared of old unto me, saying, Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee.

4 Again I will build thee, and thou shalt be built, O virgin of Israel: thou shalt again be adorned with thy tabrets, and shalt go forth in the dances of them that make merry.

5 Thou shalt yet plant vines upon the mountains of Samaria: the planters shall plant, and shall eat them as common things.

6 For there shall be a day, that the watchmen upon the mount Ephraim shall cry, Arise ye, and let us go up to Zion unto the LORD our God.

7 For thus saith the LORD; Sing with gladness for Jacob, and shout among the chief of the nations: publish ye, praise ye, and say, O LORD, save thy people, the remnant of Israel.

8 Behold, I will bring them from the north country, and gather them from the coasts of the earth, and with them the blind and the lame, the woman with child and her that travaileth with child together: a great company shall return thither.

9 They shall come with weeping, and with supplications will I lead them: I will cause them to walk by the rivers of waters in a straight way, wherein they shall not stumble: for I am a father to Israel, and Ephraim is my firstborn.

10 Hear the word of the LORD, O ye nations, and declare it in the isles afar off, and say, He that scattered Israel will gather him, and keep him, as a shepherd doth his flock.

11 For the LORD hath redeemed Jacob, and ransomed him from the hand of him that was stronger than he.

12 Therefore they shall come and sing in the height of Zion, and shall flow together to the goodness of the LORD, for wheat, and for wine, and for oil, and for the young of the flock and of the herd: and their soul shall be as a watered garden; and they shall not sorrow any more at all.

13 Then shall the virgin rejoice in the dance, both young men and old together: for I will turn their mourning into joy, and will comfort them, and make them rejoice from their sorrow.

14 And I will satiate the soul of the priests with fatness, and my people shall be satisfied with my goodness, saith the LORD.

15 Thus saith the LORD; A voice was heard in Ramah, lamentation, and bitter weeping; Rahel weeping for her children refused to be comforted for her children, because they were not.

16 Thus saith the LORD; Refrain thy voice from weeping, and thine eyes from tears: for thy work shall be rewarded, saith the LORD; and they shall come again from the land of the enemy.

17 And there is hope in thine end, saith the LORD, that thy children shall come again to their own border.

18 I have surely heard Ephraim bemoaning himself thus; Thou hast chastised me, and I was chastised, as a bullock unaccustomed to the yoke: turn thou me, and I shall be turned; for thou art the LORD my God.

19 Surely after that I was turned, I repented; and after that I was instructed, I smote upon my thigh: I was ashamed, yea, even confounded, because I did bear the reproach of my youth.

20 Is Ephraim my dear son? is he a pleasant child? for since I spake against him, I do earnestly remember him still: therefore my bowels are troubled for him; I will surely have mercy upon him, saith the LORD.

21 Set thee up waymarks, make thee high heaps: set thine heart toward the highway, even the way which thou wentest: turn again, O virgin of Israel, turn again to these thy cities.

22 How long wilt thou go about, O thou backsliding daughter? for the LORD hath created a new thing in the earth, A woman shall compass a man.

23 Thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; As yet they shall use this speech in the land of Judah and in the cities thereof, when I shall bring again their captivity; The LORD bless thee, O habitation of justice, and mountain of holiness.

24 And there shall dwell in Judah itself, and in all the cities thereof together, husbandmen, and they that go forth with flocks.

25 For I have satiated the weary soul, and I have replenished every sorrowful soul.

26 Upon this I awaked, and beheld; and my sleep was sweet unto me.

27 Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will sow the house of Israel and the house of Judah with the seed of man, and with the seed of beast.

28 And it shall come to pass, that like as I have watched over them, to pluck up, and to break down, and to throw down, and to destroy, and to afflict; so will I watch over them, to build, and to plant, saith the LORD.

29 In those days they shall say no more, The fathers have eaten a sour grape, and the children’s teeth are set on edge.

30 But every one shall die for his own iniquity: every man that eateth the sour grape, his teeth shall be set on edge.

31 Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah:

32 Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day that I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt; which my covenant they brake, although I was an husband unto them, saith the LORD:

33 But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith the LORD, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people.

34 And they shall teach no more every man his neighbour, and every man his brother, saying, Know the LORD: for they shall all know me, from the least of them unto the greatest of them, saith the LORD: for I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more.

35 Thus saith the LORD, which giveth the sun for a light by day, and the ordinances of the moon and of the stars for a light by night, which divideth the sea when the waves thereof roar; The LORD of hosts is his name:

36 If those ordinances depart from before me, saith the LORD, then the seed of Israel also shall cease from being a nation before me for ever.

37 Thus saith the LORD; If heaven above can be measured, and the foundations of the earth searched out beneath, I will also cast off all the seed of Israel for all that they have done, saith the LORD.

38 Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that the city shall be built to the LORD from the tower of Hananeel unto the gate of the corner.

39 And the measuring line shall yet go forth over against it upon the hill Gareb, and shall compass about to Goath.

40 And the whole valley of the dead bodies, and of the ashes, and all the fields unto the brook of Kidron, unto the corner of the horse gate toward the east, shall be holy unto the LORD; it shall not be plucked up, nor thrown down any more for ever.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

At the same time, the Lord says, he will be the God of all the families of Israel, and the people left of the sword will find grace in the wilderness. Rachel weeping for her children is answered with return, Ephraim is pitied, and the chapter's centre is the new covenant, written not on stone but on hearts, with sins remembered no more. It closes with Jerusalem rebuilt to the brook Kidron. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. At the same time, says the Lord, will I be the God of all the families of Israel, and they shall be my people. Henry notes God assures his people that he will again take them into covenant relation to himself: at the same time, I will be the God of all the families of Israel, and they shall be my people, is the covenant renewed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Thus says the Lord, The people which were left of the sword found grace in the wilderness; even Israel, when I went to cause him to rest. Henry's people left of the sword found grace in the wilderness, even Israel, when I went to cause him to rest, is the former church's pattern: when brought very low, and difficulties appear, it is good to remember that it has been so with the church formerly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The Lord has appeared of old unto me, saying, Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee. Henry notes it is the happiness of those who, through grace, are interested in the love of God, that it is an everlasting love, from everlasting in the counsels, to everlasting in the continuance: those whom God loves with this love, he will draw to himself, by the influences of his Spirit upon their souls, and the Lord appeared of old, I have loved thee with an everlasting love, therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Again I will build thee, and thou shalt be built, O virgin of Israel: thou shalt again be adorned with thy tabrets, and shalt go forth in the dances of them that make merry. Henry's again I will build thee, and thou shalt be built, O virgin of Israel, adorned with tabrets, going forth in merry dances, is the rebuilding: when praising God for what he has done, we must call upon him for the favours his church needs and expects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Thou shalt yet plant vines upon the mountains of Samaria: the planters shall plant, and shall eat them as common things. Henry's thou shalt yet plant vines upon Samaria's mountains, planters planting and eating as common things, is the settlement: when the Lord calls, we must not plead that we cannot come, for he that calls us will help us, will strengthen us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. For there shall be a day, that the watchmen upon the mount Ephraim shall cry, Arise ye, and let us go up to Zion unto the Lord our God. Henry's day when Ephraim's watchmen cry, Arise, let us go up to Zion unto the Lord our God, is the call answered: the goodness of God shall lead them to repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. For thus says the Lord; Sing with gladness for Jacob, and shout among the chief of the nations: publish ye, praise ye, and say, O Lord, save thy people, the remnant of Israel. Henry's sing with gladness for Jacob, shout among chief nations, publish, praise, O Lord, save thy people, the remnant of Israel, is praise calling for favour: if we take God for our Father, and join the church of the first-born, we shall want nothing that is good for us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Behold, I will bring them from the north country, and gather them from the coasts of the earth, and with them the blind and the lame, the woman with child and her that travails with child together: a great company shall return thither. Henry notes these predictions doubtless refer also to a future gathering of the Israelites from all quarters of the globe: behold, I will bring them from the north country, gather from earth's coasts, blind, lame, woman with child, and travailing together, a great company returning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. They shall come with weeping, and with supplications will I lead them: I will cause them to walk by the rivers of waters in a straight way, wherein they shall not stumble: for I am a father to Israel, and Ephraim is my firstborn. Henry notes they shall weep for sin with more bitterness, and more tenderness, when delivered out of their captivity, than when groaning under it, and they figuratively describe the conversion of sinners to Christ, and the plain and safe way in which they are led: they shall come with weeping, supplications leading them, rivers of waters in a straight way wherein they stumble not, for I am a father to Israel, and Ephraim is my firstborn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Hear the word of the Lord, O ye nations, and declare it in the isles afar off, and say, He that scattered Israel will gather him, and keep him, as a shepherd does his flock. Henry notes he that scattered Israel knows where to find them: hear the word, O nations, declare in isles afar off, he that scattered Israel will gather him, and keep him as a shepherd his flock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. For the Lord has redeemed Jacob, and ransomed him from the hand of him that was stronger than he. Henry's Lord redeemed Jacob, ransomed him from the stronger hand, is the gathering's ground: he that scattered knows where to find, and redemption ransoms from strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Therefore they shall come and sing in the height of Zion, and shall flow together to the goodness of the Lord, for wheat, and for wine, and for oil, and for the young of the flock and of the herd: and their soul shall be as a watered garden; and they shall not sorrow any more at all. Henry notes it is comfortable to observe the goodness of the Lord in the gifts of providence, but our souls are never valuable as gardens, unless watered with the dews of God's Spirit and grace: they shall come and sing in Zion's height, flow together to the Lord's goodness for wheat, wine, oil, flock, and herd, soul as a watered garden, sorrowing no more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Then shall the virgin rejoice in the dance, both young men and old together: for I will turn their mourning into joy, and will comfort them, and make them rejoice from their sorrow. Henry's virgin rejoicing in dance, young and old together, mourning turned to joy, comforted, rejoicing from sorrow, is the garden watered: let them be satisfied of God's loving-kindness, and they will be satisfied with it, and desire no more to make them happy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. And I will satiate the soul of the priests with fatness, and my people shall be satisfied with my goodness, says the Lord. Henry's priests satiated with fatness, people satisfied with goodness, is the satisfaction: a precious promise follows, which will not have full accomplishment except in the heavenly Zion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Thus says the Lord; A voice was heard in Ramah, lamentation, and bitter weeping; Rahel weeping for her children refused to be comforted for her children, because they were not. Henry notes Rachel is represented as rising from her grave, and refusing to be comforted, supposing her offspring rooted out: a voice in Ramah, lamentation and bitter weeping, Rachel weeping for children, refusing comfort, because they were not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Thus says the Lord; Refrain thy voice from weeping, and thine eyes from tears: for thy work shall be rewarded, says the Lord; and they shall come again from the land of the enemy. Henry notes the murder of the children at Bethlehem, by Herod, in some degree fulfilled this prediction, but could not be its full meaning: refrain voice from weeping, eyes from tears, for thy work shall be rewarded, and they shall come again from the enemy's land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. And there is hope in thine end, says the Lord, that thy children shall come again to their own border. Henry notes if we have hope in the end, concerning an eternal inheritance, for ourselves and those belonging to us, all temporal afflictions may be borne, and will be for our good: there is hope in thine end, children coming again to their own border. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. I have surely heard Ephraim bemoaning himself thus; Thou hast chastised me, and I was chastised, as a bullock unaccustomed to the yoke: turn thou me, and I shall be turned; for thou art the Lord my God. Henry notes Ephraim, the ten tribes, is weeping for sin: he is angry at himself for his sin, and folly, and frowardness, he finds he cannot, by his own power, keep himself close with God, much less bring himself back when he is revolted, therefore he prays, Turn thou me, and I shall be turned, and I have surely heard Ephraim bemoaning, chastised as an unaccustomed bullock, turn thou me, for thou art the Lord my God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Surely after that I was turned, I repented; and after that I was instructed, I smote upon my thigh: I was ashamed, yea, even confounded, because I did bear the reproach of my youth. Henry notes his will was bowed to the will of God: when the teaching of God's Spirit went with the corrections of his providence, then the work was done, and after turned, repented, after instructed, smote upon thigh, ashamed and confounded, bearing youth's reproach, is the Spirit-taught correction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Is Ephraim my dear son? Is he a pleasant child? For since I spoke against him, I do earnestly remember him still: therefore my bowels are troubled for him; I will surely have mercy upon him, says the Lord. Henry notes this is our comfort in affliction, that the Lord thinks upon us: God has mercy in store, rich mercy, sure mercy, suitable mercy, for all who seek him in sincerity, and is Ephraim my dear son, a pleasant child, since I spoke against him I earnestly remember him still, bowels troubled, surely mercy, is that mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Set thee up waymarks, make thee high heaps: set thine heart toward the highway, even the way which thou wentest: turn again, O virgin of Israel, turn again to these thy cities. Henry notes the way from the bondage of sin to the liberty of God's children is a high-way: it is plain, it is safe, yet none are likely to walk in it, unless they set their hearts towards it, and set up waymarks, make high heaps, set heart toward the highway, the way thou wentest, turn again, O virgin of Israel, to these thy cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. How long wilt thou go about, O thou backsliding daughter? For the Lord has created a new thing in the earth, A woman shall compass a man. Henry notes they are encouraged by the promise of a new, unheard-of, extraordinary thing; a creation, a work of Almighty power; the human nature of Christ, formed and prepared by the power of the Holy Ghost, and this is here mentioned as an encouragement to the Jews to return to their own land: how long go about, backsliding daughter, for the Lord created a new thing, a woman shall compass a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; As yet they shall use this speech in the land of Judah and in the cities thereof, when I shall bring again their captivity; The Lord bless thee, O habitation of justice, and mountain of holiness. Henry notes a comfortable prospect is given them of a happy settlement there: godliness and honesty God has joined, let no man think to put them asunder, or to make the one atone for the want of the other, and as yet they shall use this speech in Judah's land and cities, when captivity is brought again, The Lord bless thee, O habitation of justice, and mountain of holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. And there shall dwell in Judah itself, and in all the cities thereof together, husbandmen, and they that go forth with flocks. Henry's Judah itself and all cities together, husbandmen and flock-goers dwelling, is the happy settlement: godliness and honesty joined in dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. For I have satiated the weary soul, and I have replenished every sorrowful soul. Henry notes in the love and favour of God the weary soul shall find rest, and the sorrowful shall find joy: I have satiated the weary soul, replenished every sorrowful soul, is rest and joy in favour. Henry notes the people of God shall become numerous and prosperous: behold, the days come, I will sow Israel and Judah with seed of man and seed of beast, is numerous prosperity sown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. And it shall come to pass, that like as I have watched over them, to pluck up, and to break down, and to throw down, and to destroy, and to afflict; so will I watch over them, to build, and to plant, says the Lord. Henry's like as I watched to pluck up, break down, throw down, destroy, and afflict, so will I watch to build and plant, is God's care over the church: watching for building replaces watching for breaking. Henry notes no man shall finally perish, but for his own sins: in those days they shall say no more, fathers ate sour grapes, children's teeth set on edge, for national reckoning for fathers' sins ends. Henry's every one shall die for his own iniquity, every man eating sour grapes, his teeth set on edge, is personal reckoning: none, who is willing to accept of Christ's salvation, finally perishes for another's sins. Henry notes not, I will give them a new law, for Christ came not to destroy the law, but to fulfil it: not according to the Egypt-out covenant they broke, although I was an husband unto them, is the new covenant's not-according. Henry notes the law shall be written in their hearts by the finger of the Spirit, as formerly written in the tables of stone: the Lord will, by his grace, make his people willing people in the day of his power, and this shall be the covenant, law in inward parts, written in hearts, I their God, they my people. Henry notes all shall know the Lord; all shall be welcome to the knowledge of God, and shall have the means of that knowledge, and there shall be an outpouring of the Holy Spirit, at the time the gospel is published: they shall teach no more, Know the Lord, for all shall know me, least to greatest, iniquity forgiven, sin remembered no more. Henry notes as surely as the heavenly bodies will continue their settled course, according to the will of their Creator, to the end of time, and as the raging sea obeys him, so surely will the Jews be continued a separate people: thus says the Lord, sun for day light, moon and stars ordinances for night, dividing the sea when waves roar, Lord of hosts his name. Henry's if heaven can be measured, earth's foundations searched out, I will also cast off all Israel's seed for all they have done, is the immeasurable surety: the personal happiness of every true believer, as well as the future restoration of Israel, is secured by promise, covenant, and oath. And the whole valley of the dead bodies, and of the ashes, and all the fields unto the brook of Kidron, unto the corner of the horse gate toward the east, shall be holy unto the Lord; it shall not be plucked up, nor thrown down any more for ever. Henry's whole valley of dead bodies and ashes, all fields to Kidron's brook, to the east horse gate's corner, holy unto the Lord, never plucked up nor thrown down for ever, is the establishment: the rebuilt city, enlarged and established, is earnest of great things for the gospel church. Henry's sing with gladness for Jacob, shout among chief nations, publish, praise, O Lord, save thy people, the remnant of Israel, is praise calling for favour: if we take God for our Father, and join the church of the first-born, we shall want nothing that is good for us. Behold, I will bring them from the north country, and gather them from the coasts of the earth, and with them the blind and the lame, the woman with child and her that travails with child together: a great company shall return thither. Henry notes these predictions doubtless refer also to a future gathering of the Israelites from all quarters of the globe: behold, I will bring them from the north country, gather from earth's coasts, blind, lame, woman with child, and travailing together, a great company returning.   • Verse 9. They shall come with weeping, and with supplications will I lead them: I will cause them to walk by the rivers of waters in a straight way, wherein they shall not stumble: for I am a father to Israel, and Ephraim is my firstborn. Henry notes they shall weep for sin with more bitterness, and more tenderness, when delivered out of their captivity, than when groaning under it, and they figuratively describe the conversion of sinners to Christ, and the plain and safe way in which they are led: they shall come with weeping, supplications leading them, rivers of waters in a straight way wherein they stumble not, for I am a father to Israel, and Ephraim is my firstborn.   • Verse 10. Hear the word of the Lord, O ye nations, and declare it in the isles afar off, and say, He that scattered Israel will gather him, and keep him, as a shepherd does his flock. Henry notes he that scattered Israel knows where to find them: hear the word, O nations, declare in isles afar off, he that scattered Israel will gather him, and keep him as a shepherd his flock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. For the Lord has redeemed Jacob, and ransomed him from the hand of him that was stronger than he. Henry's Lord redeemed Jacob, ransomed him from the stronger hand, is the gathering's ground: he that scattered knows where to find, and redemption ransoms from strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Therefore they shall come and sing in the height of Zion, and shall flow together to the goodness of the Lord, for wheat, and for wine, and for oil, and for the young of the flock and of the herd: and their soul shall be as a watered garden; and they shall not sorrow any more at all. Henry notes it is comfortable to observe the goodness of the Lord in the gifts of providence, but our souls are never valuable as gardens, unless watered with the dews of God's Spirit and grace: they shall come and sing in Zion's height, flow together to the Lord's goodness for wheat, wine, oil, flock, and herd, soul as a watered garden, sorrowing no more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Then shall the virgin rejoice in the dance, both young men and old together: for I will turn their mourning into joy, and will comfort them, and make them rejoice from their sorrow. Henry's virgin rejoicing in dance, young and old together, mourning turned to joy, comforted, rejoicing from sorrow, is the garden watered: let them be satisfied of God's loving-kindness, and they will be satisfied with it, and desire no more to make them happy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. And I will satiate the soul of the priests with fatness, and my people shall be satisfied with my goodness, says the Lord. Henry's priests satiated with fatness, people satisfied with goodness, is the satisfaction: a precious promise follows, which will not have full accomplishment except in the heavenly Zion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Thus says the Lord; A voice was heard in Ramah, lamentation, and bitter weeping; Rahel weeping for her children refused to be comforted for her children, because they were not. Henry notes Rachel is represented as rising from her grave, and refusing to be comforted, supposing her offspring rooted out: a voice in Ramah, lamentation and bitter weeping, Rachel weeping for children, refusing comfort, because they were not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Thus says the Lord; Refrain thy voice from weeping, and thine eyes from tears: for thy work shall be rewarded, says the Lord; and they shall come again from the land of the enemy. Henry notes the murder of the children at Bethlehem, by Herod, in some degree fulfilled this prediction, but could not be its full meaning: refrain voice from weeping, eyes from tears, for thy work shall be rewarded, and they shall come again from the enemy's land.   • Verse 17. And there is hope in thine end, says the Lord, that thy children shall come again to their own border. Henry notes if we have hope in the end, concerning an eternal inheritance, for ourselves and those belonging to us, all temporal afflictions may be borne, and will be for our good: there is hope in thine end, children coming again to their own border. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. I have surely heard Ephraim bemoaning himself thus; Thou hast chastised me, and I was chastised, as a bullock unaccustomed to the yoke: turn thou me, and I shall be turned; for thou art the Lord my God. Henry notes Ephraim, the ten tribes, is weeping for sin: he is angry at himself for his sin, and folly, and frowardness, he finds he cannot, by his own power, keep himself close with God, much less bring himself back when he is revolted, therefore he prays, Turn thou me, and I shall be turned, and I have surely heard Ephraim bemoaning, chastised as an unaccustomed bullock, turn thou me, for thou art the Lord my God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Surely after that I was turned, I repented; and after that I was instructed, I smote upon my thigh: I was ashamed, yea, even confounded, because I did bear the reproach of my youth. Henry notes his will was bowed to the will of God: when the teaching of God's Spirit went with the corrections of his providence, then the work was done, and after turned, repented, after instructed, smote upon thigh, ashamed and confounded, bearing youth's reproach, is the Spirit-taught correction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Is Ephraim my dear son? Is he a pleasant child? For since I spoke against him, I do earnestly remember him still: therefore my bowels are troubled for him; I will surely have mercy upon him, says the Lord. Henry notes this is our comfort in affliction, that the Lord thinks upon us: God has mercy in store, rich mercy, sure mercy, suitable mercy, for all who seek him in sincerity, and is Ephraim my dear son, a pleasant child, since I spoke against him I earnestly remember him still, bowels troubled, surely mercy, is that mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Set thee up waymarks, make thee high heaps: set thine heart toward the highway, even the way which thou wentest: turn again, O virgin of Israel, turn again to these thy cities. Henry notes the way from the bondage of sin to the liberty of God's children is a high-way: it is plain, it is safe, yet none are likely to walk in it, unless they set their hearts towards it, and set up waymarks, make high heaps, set heart toward the highway, the way thou wentest, turn again, O virgin of Israel, to these thy cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. How long wilt thou go about, O thou backsliding daughter? For the Lord has created a new thing in the earth, A woman shall compass a man. Henry notes they are encouraged by the promise of a new, unheard-of, extraordinary thing; a creation, a work of Almighty power; the human nature of Christ, formed and prepared by the power of the Holy Ghost, and this is here mentioned as an encouragement to the Jews to return to their own land: how long go about, backsliding daughter, for the Lord created a new thing, a woman shall compass a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; As yet they shall use this speech in the land of Judah and in the cities thereof, when I shall bring again their captivity; The Lord bless thee, O habitation of justice, and mountain of holiness. Henry notes a comfortable prospect is given them of a happy settlement there: godliness and honesty God has joined, let no man think to put them asunder, or to make the one atone for the want of the other, and as yet they shall use this speech in Judah's land and cities, when captivity is brought again, The Lord bless thee, O habitation of justice, and mountain of holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. And there shall dwell in Judah itself, and in all the cities thereof together, husbandmen, and they that go forth with flocks. Henry's Judah itself and all cities together, husbandmen and flock-goers dwelling, is the happy settlement: godliness and honesty joined in dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Henry notes in the love and favour of God the weary soul shall find rest, and the sorrowful shall find joy: I have satiated the weary soul, replenished every sorrowful soul, is rest and joy in favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Henry notes the people of God shall become numerous and prosperous: behold, the days come, I will sow Israel and Judah with seed of man and seed of beast, is numerous prosperity sown. And it shall come to pass, that like as I have watched over them, to pluck up, and to break down, and to throw down, and to destroy, and to afflict; so will I watch over them, to build, and to plant, says the Lord. Henry's like as I watched to pluck up, break down, throw down, destroy, and afflict, so will I watch to build and plant, is God's care over the church: watching for building replaces watching for breaking. Henry's every one shall die for his own iniquity, every man eating sour grapes, his teeth set on edge, is personal reckoning: none, who is willing to accept of Christ's salvation, finally perishes for another's sins. Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day that I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt; which my covenant they broke, although I was an husband unto them, says the Lord. Henry notes not, I will give them a new law, for Christ came not to destroy the law, but to fulfil it: not according to the Egypt-out covenant they broke, although I was an husband unto them, is the new covenant's not-according. Henry notes the law shall be written in their hearts by the finger of the Spirit, as formerly written in the tables of stone: the Lord will, by his grace, make his people willing people in the day of his power, and this shall be the covenant, law in inward parts, written in hearts, I their God, they my people.   • Verse 34. And they shall teach no more every man his neighbour, and every man his brother, saying, Know the Lord: for they shall all know me, from the least of them unto the greatest of them, says the Lord: for I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more. Henry notes all shall know the Lord; all shall be welcome to the knowledge of God, and shall have the means of that knowledge, and there shall be an outpouring of the Holy Spirit, at the time the gospel is published: they shall teach no more, Know the Lord, for all shall know me, least to greatest, iniquity forgiven, sin remembered no more.
