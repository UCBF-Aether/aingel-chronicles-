The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 33 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Wherefore, Job, I pray thee, hear my speeches, and hearken to all my words.

2 Behold, now I have opened my mouth, my tongue hath spoken in my mouth.

3 My words shall be of the uprightness of my heart: and my lips shall utter knowledge clearly.

4 The spirit of God hath made me, and the breath of the Almighty hath given me life.

5 If thou canst answer me, set thy words in order before me, stand up.

6 Behold, I am according to thy wish in God’s stead: I also am formed out of the clay.

7 Behold, my terror shall not make thee afraid, neither shall my hand be heavy upon thee.

8 Surely thou hast spoken in mine hearing, and I have heard the voice of thy words, saying,

9 I am clean without transgression, I am innocent; neither is there iniquity in me.

10 Behold, he findeth occasions against me, he counteth me for his enemy,

11 He putteth my feet in the stocks, he marketh all my paths.

12 Behold, in this thou art not just: I will answer thee, that God is greater than man.

13 Why dost thou strive against him? for he giveth not account of any of his matters.

14 For God speaketh once, yea twice, yet man perceiveth it not.

15 In a dream, in a vision of the night, when deep sleep falleth upon men, in slumberings upon the bed;

16 Then he openeth the ears of men, and sealeth their instruction,

17 That he may withdraw man from his purpose, and hide pride from man.

18 He keepeth back his soul from the pit, and his life from perishing by the sword.

19 He is chastened also with pain upon his bed, and the multitude of his bones with strong pain:

20 So that his life abhorreth bread, and his soul dainty meat.

21 His flesh is consumed away, that it cannot be seen; and his bones that were not seen stick out.

22 Yea, his soul draweth near unto the grave, and his life to the destroyers.

23 If there be a messenger with him, an interpreter, one among a thousand, to shew unto man his uprightness:

24 Then he is gracious unto him, and saith, Deliver him from going down to the pit: I have found a ransom.

25 His flesh shall be fresher than a child’s: he shall return to the days of his youth:

26 He shall pray unto God, and he will be favourable unto him: and he shall see his face with joy: for he will render unto man his righteousness.

27 He looketh upon men, and if any say, I have sinned, and perverted that which was right, and it profited me not;

28 He will deliver his soul from going into the pit, and his life shall see the light.

29 Lo, all these things worketh God oftentimes with man,

30 To bring back his soul from the pit, to be enlightened with the light of the living.

31 Mark well, O Job, hearken unto me: hold thy peace, and I will speak.

32 If thou hast anything to say, answer me: speak, for I desire to justify thee.

33 If not, hearken unto me: hold thy peace, and I shall teach thee wisdom.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Elihu turns from the friends to Job alone: hear my speeches, for my words are upright and my tongue speaks what it knows. God, he says, speaks in dreams and visions of the night to open men’s ears and seal their instruction, and chastens a man with pain on his bed so that his soul draws near to the grave — not to destroy him, but to bring back his soul from the pit. If a messenger, an interpreter, shows him what is right, God is gracious, restores his flesh like a child’s, and he prays and sees God’s face with joy. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Wherefore, Job, Elihu prays him to hear his speeches and hearken to all his words. The address turns from the friends to Job alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Behold, now Elihu has opened his mouth, and his tongue has spoken in his mouth. JFB renders mouth as palate, whereby the taste discerns: few, as Elihu, try their words with discrimination first and only say what is really good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Elihu's words shall be of the uprightness of his heart, and his lips shall utter knowledge clearly. JFB renders clearly as purely: sincerely, not distorting the truth through passion, as the friends did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The Spirit of God has made Elihu, and the breath of the Almighty has given him life. JFB notes the breath of the Almighty has inspired him, so he stands according to Job's wish in God's stead, a daysman or mediator between God and Job, designed as a type of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. If Job can answer Elihu, let him set his words in order before him and stand up. JFB notes images from a court of justice, alluding to Job's words about standing before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Behold, Elihu is according to Job's wish in God's stead, also formed out of the clay. JFB explains formed as pressed together as a mass of clay by the potter, so that though acting as God's representative, he is but a creature like Job. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Behold, Elihu's terror shall not make Job afraid, neither shall his hand be heavy upon him. JFB notes this alludes to Job's words asking that God's hand not lie heavy upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Surely Job has spoken in Elihu's hearing, and he has heard the voice of his words. JFB explains that Job had acknowledged sin in general, but the general spirit of his words was to maintain himself clean and charge God with injustice, going too far against the friends' false charge of hypocrisy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Job said, I am clean without transgression, innocent, with no iniquity in me. JFB renders clean as spotless, the claim Elihu takes up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Behold, God finds occasions against Job and counts him for his enemy. JFB renders occasions as enmities, the hostility Job charged upon God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. God puts Job's feet in the stocks and marks all his paths. JFB notes marketh as narrowly watches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Behold, in this Job is not just, for God is greater than man. JFB explains it cannot be that God should jealously watch man, though spotless, as an enemy, for there must be sin in man, even though no hypocrite, which needs correction by suffering for the sufferer's good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Why does Job strive against God, for he gives not account of any of his matters? JFB renders his matters as ways: our part is not to strive with God but to submit, believing it right because he does it, not because we see all the reasons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. For God speaks once, yea twice, yet man perceives it not. JFB renders it as man regards it not, or twice he repeats the warning if man gives no heed to the first: God tries what affliction will effect when man in prosperity has not heeded his will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. In a dream, in a vision of the night, when deep sleep falls upon men, in slumberings upon the bed. JFB notes slumberings as light sleep opposed to deep sleep, and dreams and visions as among the ways God then spoke to man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Then God opens the ears of men and seals their instruction. JFB renders it literally as sealing their ears to himself by warnings, with the sureness and secrecy of a seal he reveals his warnings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. That God may withdraw man from his purpose and hide pride from man. JFB explains purpose as work or business in a bad sense, and pride as an open pit which God hides or covers up, lest man fall into it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. God keeps back man's soul from the pit, and his life from perishing by the sword. JFB explains soul as life, the pit as the grave and symbol of hell, and perishing by the sword as a violent death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Man is chastened also with pain upon his bed, and the multitude of his bones with strong pain. JFB notes the new thought: affliction is disciplinary for the good of the godly, with the perpetual contest of his bones as the never-resting fever in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. So that man's life abhors bread, and his soul dainty meat. JFB explains life as appetite, which ordinarily sustains life: the taking away of desire for food by sickness symbolizes the removal by affliction of lust for things which foster the spiritual fever of pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. His flesh is consumed away, that it cannot be seen, and his bones that were not seen stick out. JFB notes flesh once prominent can no more be seen, while bones once not seen now appear prominent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Yea, man's soul draws near unto the grave, and his life to the destroyers. JFB explains destroyers as angels of death commissioned by God to end man's life, or the death pains personified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. If there be a messenger with him, an interpreter, one among a thousand, to show unto man his uprightness. JFB explains Elihu refers to himself as the divinely-sent messenger and interpreter to explain and vindicate God's righteousness, one rarely to be found, a type of Jesus Christ the Mediator. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Then God is gracious unto him, and says, deliver him from going down to the pit, for I have found a ransom. JFB explains deliver literally as redeem, with ransom as a price paid for deliverance, receiving its full realization only in the eternal redemption found in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. His flesh shall be fresher than a child's, and he shall return to the days of his youth. JFB calls these the effects of restoration to God's favour: literally a temporal revival for Job, spiritually an eternal regeneration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. He shall pray unto God, and he will be favourable unto him, and he shall see his face with joy, for God will render unto man his righteousness. JFB notes Job shall no longer pray in vain, and God shall no longer hide his face, restoring Job to justify God in his dealings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The restored man looks upon men and says, I have sinned and perverted that which was right, and it profited me not. JFB renders looketh as singeth joyfully before men, with perverted as making the straight crooked, as Job had misrepresented God's character, and profited as punishment not commensurate with sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. God will deliver the penitent's soul from going into the pit, and his life shall see the light. JFB renders it as he has delivered my soul and my life, a continuation of the penitent's testimony to the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Lo, all these things God works oftentimes with man. JFB explains twice and thrice as alluding to the three ways: once by visions, secondly by afflictions, now thirdly by the messenger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. To bring back man's soul from the pit, to be enlightened with the light of the living. God's repeated dealings aim at rescue into light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Mark well, O Job, hearken unto Elihu, hold thy peace, and he will speak. The appeal returns to direct address after the teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. If Job has anything to say, let him answer Elihu, for Elihu desires to justify him. JFB explains justify as doing Job justice, and if he can, consistently with it, declaring him innocent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. If not, let Job hearken unto Elihu, hold his peace, and Elihu shall teach him wisdom. JFB notes Elihu pauses here for a reply, then proceeds in the following chapter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
