The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when the sabbath was past, Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James, and Salome, had bought sweet spices, that they might come and anoint him.

2 And very early in the morning the first day of the week, they came unto the sepulchre at the rising of the sun.

3 And they said among themselves, Who shall roll us away the stone from the door of the sepulchre?

4 And when they looked, they saw that the stone was rolled away: for it was very great.

5 And entering into the sepulchre, they saw a young man sitting on the right side, clothed in a long white garment; and they were affrighted.

6 And he saith unto them, Be not affrighted: Ye seek Jesus of Nazareth, which was crucified: he is risen; he is not here: behold the place where they laid him.

7 But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.

8 And they went out quickly, and fled from the sepulchre; for they trembled and were amazed: neither said they any thing to any man; for they were afraid.

9 Now when Jesus was risen early the first day of the week, he appeared first to Mary Magdalene, out of whom he had cast seven devils.

10 And she went and told them that had been with him, as they mourned and wept.

11 And they, when they had heard that he was alive, and had been seen of her, believed not.

12 After that he appeared in another form unto two of them, as they walked, and went into the country.

13 And they went and told it unto the residue: neither believed they them.

14 Afterward he appeared unto the eleven as they sat at meat, and upbraided them with their unbelief and hardness of heart, because they believed not them which had seen him after he was risen.

15 And he said unto them, Go ye into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature.

16 He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved; but he that believeth not shall be damned.

17 And these signs shall follow them that believe; In my name shall they cast out devils; they shall speak with new tongues;

18 They shall take up serpents; and if they drink any deadly thing, it shall not hurt them; they shall lay hands on the sick, and they shall recover.

19 So then after the Lord had spoken unto them, he was received up into heaven, and sat on the right hand of God.

20 And they went forth, and preached every where, the Lord working with them, and confirming the word with signs following. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

When the Sabbath is past, Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James, and Salome come at sunrise to anoint Jesus and find the great stone rolled away (vv. 1–4). A young man in white tells them he is risen and to tell his disciples and Peter that he goes before them into Galilee; they flee, trembling, and say nothing to anyone (vv. 5–8). The chapter then records appearances — first to Mary Magdalene, then to two travellers, then to the eleven — the commission to preach the gospel to every creature, and the Lord’s ascension to the right hand of God (vv. 9–20). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 16

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When the sabbath was past, Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James, and Salome had bought sweet spices, that they might come and anoint him. After the sabbath, three women bought spices to anoint him, for love's last service was prepared as soon as the holy day allowed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Very early in the morning, the first day of the week, they came to the sepulchre at the rising of the sun. At sunrise on the first day, the week's first morning, they came to the tomb, and the rising sun matched the rising they would find. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They said among themselves, Who shall roll us away the stone from the door of the sepulchre? Their question about the stone shows they expected a closed tomb, for love went without a plan for the obstacle, trusting to find help at the place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. When they looked, they saw that the stone was rolled away, for it was very great. The very great stone was already rolled away, for the obstacle they feared had been removed before they arrived, and looking up they saw what they had not dared hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Entering into the sepulchre, they saw a young man sitting on the right side, clothed in a long white garment, and they were affrighted. A young man in white, sitting on the right, affrighted them, for an angel in the tomb is a more startling sight than an empty one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He said to them, Be not affrighted: You seek Jesus of Nazareth, who was crucified: he is risen; he is not here: behold the place where they laid him. 'He is risen, he is not here' answers their seeking, and the empty place is shown as proof, for the tomb they came to anoint held no body to anoint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goes before you into Galilee: there shall you see him, as he said to you. Tell his disciples, and Peter by name, for the denier is named to assure him he is not cast off, and Galilee is the appointed meeting, as he had said. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They went out quickly, fled from the sepulchre, for they trembled and were amazed, neither said they anything to any man, for they were afraid. Trembling, amazement, and fear fled from the tomb in silence, for the news was too great for immediate speech, and fear held their tongues on the road. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Now when Jesus was risen early the first day of the week, he appeared first to Mary Magdalene, out of whom he had cast seven devils. Risen early, he appeared first to Mary Magdalene, once possessed by seven devils, for grace honours a forgiven sinner with the first sight of the risen Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. She went and told them that had been with him, as they mourned and wept. She told the mourning company, for the first witness preached to weepers, and her tidings met grief, not expectation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They, when they heard that he was alive and had been seen by her, believed not. Hearing he was alive and seen, they believed not, for grief and the strangeness of resurrection overcame a woman's testimony, though it was true. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. After that he appeared in another form to two of them, as they walked and went into the country. In another form he appeared to two walking into the country, for the risen Lord was not always recognised at once, and the road became a place of revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They went and told it to the residue, neither believed they them. The two told the rest, and again belief failed, for the disciples' unbelief persisted through a second witness, and their hardness is recorded without excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Afterward he appeared to the eleven as they sat at meat, and upbraided them with their unbelief and hardness of heart, because they believed not those who had seen him after he was risen. Appearing to the eleven at meat, he upbraided unbelief and hardness, for refusing witnesses was itself a fault, and rebuke preceded commission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He said to them, Go into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature. Go into all the world and preach to every creature is the commission after rebuke, for the gospel's scope is the world, and its hearers are every creature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He that believes and is baptized shall be saved, but he that believes not shall be damned. Believing and baptized shall be saved, and not believing shall be damned, for faith and its sign belong together, and unbelief alone condemns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. These signs shall follow them that believe: In my name shall they cast out devils; they shall speak with new tongues. Signs follow believers, not as their seeking, but as Christ's confirmation: devils cast out and new tongues spoken in his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They shall take up serpents, and if they drink any deadly thing, it shall not hurt them; they shall lay hands on the sick, and they shall recover. Serpents taken up, deadly drink harmless, and hands healing the sick are the apostolic signs, given to confirm the word in its first publishing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. So then after the Lord had spoken to them, he was received up into heaven, and sat on the right hand of God. After speaking, he was received up and sat at God's right hand, for the ascension crowns the commission, and the seated Lord rules the work he sends them to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. They went forth and preached everywhere, the Lord working with them, and confirming the word with signs following. Amen. They went forth everywhere, the Lord working with them, and signs confirmed the word, for the gospel's spread was the Lord's work through his preachers, and Amen seals the book. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a parallel examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a possible parallel. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

The empty tomb — Mark 16, Luke 24, and John 20

All three chapters open on the first day of the week, very early, with women coming to the tomb and finding the stone moved, and a message that Jesus is risen and not there. The differences are sharp: Mark’s women find one young man in white and flee saying nothing to anyone for fear; Luke’s women find two men in shining garments, return, and tell the eleven, who dismiss it as idle tales until Peter runs to the tomb; John’s Mary Magdalene comes alone while it is yet dark, runs to Peter and the beloved disciple, and only later meets the risen Jesus in the garden. Whether these are different witnesses to one morning or reports shaped differently is not settled by the sources consulted for this page.
