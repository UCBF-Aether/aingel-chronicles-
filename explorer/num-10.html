The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Make thee two trumpets of silver; of a whole piece shalt thou make them: that thou mayest use them for the calling of the assembly, and for the journeying of the camps.

3 And when they shall blow with them, all the assembly shall assemble themselves to thee at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

4 And if they blow but with one trumpet, then the princes, which are heads of the thousands of Israel, shall gather themselves unto thee.

5 When ye blow an alarm, then the camps that lie on the east parts shall go forward.

6 When ye blow an alarm the second time, then the camps that lie on the south side shall take their journey: they shall blow an alarm for their journeys.

7 But when the congregation is to be gathered together, ye shall blow, but ye shall not sound an alarm.

8 And the sons of Aaron, the priests, shall blow with the trumpets; and they shall be to you for an ordinance for ever throughout your generations.

9 And if ye go to war in your land against the enemy that oppresseth you, then ye shall blow an alarm with the trumpets; and ye shall be remembered before the LORD your God, and ye shall be saved from your enemies.

10 Also in the day of your gladness, and in your solemn days, and in the beginnings of your months, ye shall blow with the trumpets over your burnt offerings, and over the sacrifices of your peace offerings; that they may be to you for a memorial before your God: I am the LORD your God.

11 And it came to pass on the twentieth day of the second month, in the second year, that the cloud was taken up from off the tabernacle of the testimony.

12 And the children of Israel took their journeys out of the wilderness of Sinai; and the cloud rested in the wilderness of Paran.

13 And they first took their journey according to the commandment of the LORD by the hand of Moses.

14 In the first place went the standard of the camp of the children of Judah according to their armies: and over his host was Nahshon the son of Amminadab.

15 And over the host of the tribe of the children of Issachar was Nethaneel the son of Zuar.

16 And over the host of the tribe of the children of Zebulun was Eliab the son of Helon.

17 And the tabernacle was taken down; and the sons of Gershon and the sons of Merari set forward, bearing the tabernacle.

18 And the standard of the camp of Reuben set forward according to their armies: and over his host was Elizur the son of Shedeur.

19 And over the host of the tribe of the children of Simeon was Shelumiel the son of Zurishaddai.

20 And over the host of the tribe of the children of Gad was Eliasaph the son of Deuel.

21 And the Kohathites set forward, bearing the sanctuary: and the other did set up the tabernacle against they came.

22 And the standard of the camp of the children of Ephraim set forward according to their armies: and over his host was Elishama the son of Ammihud.

23 And over the host of the tribe of the children of Manasseh was Gamaliel the son of Pedahzur.

24 And over the host of the tribe of the children of Benjamin was Abidan the son of Gideoni.

25 And the standard of the camp of the children of Dan set forward, which was the rereward of all the camps throughout their hosts: and over his host was Ahiezer the son of Ammishaddai.

26 And over the host of the tribe of the children of Asher was Pagiel the son of Ocran.

27 And over the host of the tribe of the children of Naphtali was Ahira the son of Enan.

28 Thus were the journeyings of the children of Israel according to their armies, when they set forward.

29 And Moses said unto Hobab, the son of Raguel the Midianite, Moses’ father in law, We are journeying unto the place of which the LORD said, I will give it you: come thou with us, and we will do thee good: for the LORD hath spoken good concerning Israel.

30 And he said unto him, I will not go; but I will depart to mine own land, and to my kindred.

31 And he said, Leave us not, I pray thee; forasmuch as thou knowest how we are to encamp in the wilderness, and thou mayest be to us instead of eyes.

32 And it shall be, if thou go with us, yea, it shall be, that what goodness the LORD shall do unto us, the same will we do unto thee.

33 And they departed from the mount of the LORD three days’ journey: and the ark of the covenant of the LORD went before them in the three days’ journey, to search out a resting place for them.

34 And the cloud of the LORD was upon them by day, when they went out of the camp.

35 And it came to pass, when the ark set forward, that Moses said, Rise up, LORD, and let thine enemies be scattered; and let them that hate thee flee before thee.

36 And when it rested, he said, Return, O LORD, unto the many thousands of Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the silver trumpets and the departure from Sinai. Moses makes two trumpets of beaten silver: blown singly or together they call the assembly or the princes, blown with an alarm they order the camps to march, and they are sounded over burnt and peace offerings, in war, and on feast days. On the twentieth day of the second month of the second year the cloud lifts and Israel marches in order, Judah first and Dan last, with the ark going before them. Moses invites Hobab to travel with them, and the chapter closes with his sayings when the ark set forward and when it rested. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. Moses was to make two trumpets of silver, of a whole piece. JFB describes them as straight, a cubit or more long, of solid silver for a shrill, distinct sound, unlike the curved Egyptian trumpets, and two in number probably because Aaron had two sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. When both trumpets were blown, all the assembly gathered at the tabernacle door. JFB explains the signals were distinguished by loudness and variety of notes: a uniform sound by both trumpets summoned the general assembly, a single trumpet the princes, and other notes sounded alarms for journeying or war. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. If but one trumpet was blown, the princes, heads of the thousands of Israel, gathered to Moses. The distinct sound matched the persons addressed, as Henry stresses: the gospel trumpet likewise must give a distinct sound according to the persons addressed or the end proposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. When an alarm was blown, the camps on the east went forward. One alarm was the recognised signal for Judah's eastern division, the van of the host, to march. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. When an alarm was blown the second time, the camps on the south took their journey. JFB adds that the Septuagint supplies the rest of the sequence: three alarms for the west, four for the north, so that no military march could be better regulated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. When the congregation was to be gathered, they blew, but did not sound an alarm. Gathering and marching had different sounds, so that the people were never left to guess whether they were summoned to assemble or to move. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The sons of Aaron, the priests, blew the trumpets, an ordinance forever. JFB stresses no Levite or common Israelite could hold this office: it was reserved to the priests as the Lord's ministers, and blown from the tabernacle, the visible residence of Israel's King, as alarm trumpets in Persia sounded from the sovereign's tent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. If Israel went to war against an oppressor, the priests blew an alarm and Israel would be remembered before the Lord and saved. JFB calls this an act of devout confidence, later acted on in battle, whose deeper meaning is that God would, as it were, be aroused by the trumpet to bless with his presence and aid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Also in the day of gladness, solemn days and beginnings of months, the trumpets sounded over burnt and peace offerings, as a memorial before God. JFB notes festive occasions were ushered in with trumpets to intimate the joyous feelings with which Israel engaged in God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. On the twentieth day of the second month of the second year, the cloud was taken up from the tabernacle of testimony. JFB reckons Israel had lain at Sinai eleven months and twenty-nine days, an interval of repose after Egyptian oppression and of moral education, fitting them for the warlike peoples of Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Israel took their journeys out of the wilderness of Sinai, and the cloud rested in the wilderness of Paran. JFB describes Paran as stretching from the Sinaitic group over a plateau to the southwestern borders of Palestine, and Henry warns that all our removes are but from one wilderness to another, until heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They first took their journey according to the Lord's commandment by the hand of Moses. JFB pictures Moses stationed on an eminence watching the ranks defile in order, with extraordinary care for the safe conveyance of the holy things in the actual migration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. In the first place went the standard of Judah's camp according to its armies. Judah, with Issachar and Zebulun, led the van, and behind it followed Gershon and Merari with the heavy materials of the tabernacle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Then the tabernacle was taken down, and the Gershonites and Merarites bore it, setting forward after Judah. Their place behind the van meant the framework could be reared at the new encampment before the sacred vessels arrived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Then the standard of Reuben's camp set forward according to its armies. Reuben's division formed the second great body on the march, following the tabernacle's framework bearers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Then the Kohathites set forward, bearing the sanctuary, while the others set up the tabernacle before they came. The Kohathites occupied the centre of the moving mass, so far behind Gershon and Merari that the framework stood ready to receive the holy things on arrival. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Then the standard of Ephraim's camp set forward according to its armies. Ephraim's division followed the sanctuary itself, guarding the sacred centre from behind as Judah had guarded it from before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Then the standard of Dan's camp set forward, the rereward of all the camps throughout their hosts. Dan, with Asher and Naphtali, brought up the rear of the immense caravan, closing the host as Judah had opened it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Thus were the journeyings of Israel according to their armies, when they set forward. The summary seals the chapter's order: every tribe marched under its prince and rallied around its own standard, in the order appointed at Sinai. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Moses said to Hobab, son of Raguel the Midianite, Moses' father in law, that they were journeying to the place God promised, and asked him to come and do them good. JFB identifies Raguel with Reuel, that is Jethro, making Hobab Moses' brother-in-law, who had sojourned with Israel throughout the Sinai encampment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Hobab answered, I will not go, but will depart to my own land and kindred. His first answer was refusal: the ties of home and clan pulled against the invitation, and Moses' urging had to overcome a settled purpose to return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Moses urged, leave us not, for you know how we are to encamp in the wilderness, and you may be to us instead of eyes. JFB answers the surprise at Moses' importunity despite the cloud: the cloud showed the general route, not the minute places of pasture, shade and water hidden by shifting sands, and Hobab, a prince of a powerful clan, could serve detachments sent from the main body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. If Hobab went with them, whatever goodness the Lord showed to Israel, they would do the same to him. The inducement was shared blessing, yet JFB notes it seems not to have changed the young man's purpose, for he departed and settled in his own district. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. They departed from the mount of the Lord three days' journey, and the ark of the covenant went before them to search out a resting place. JFB notes the first day's progress was small, about eighteen or twenty miles, and the cloudy pillar, which rested on the ark while stationary, preceded them in the march. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. The cloud of the Lord was upon them by day when they went out of the camp. The visible sign did not stay behind at Sinai: it travelled with the host, covering the march as it had covered the encampment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. When the ark set forward, Moses said, Rise up, Lord, and let thine enemies be scattered, and those that hate thee flee before thee. JFB notes Moses, as the people's organ, uttered a prayer at the commencement and end of each journey, so that all the journeys were sanctified by devotion, and Henry adds that God's arising alone suffices to scatter his enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. When the ark rested, Moses said, Return, O Lord, unto the many thousands of Israel. Henry notes the welfare of Israel consisted in God's continual presence among them: their safety lay not in numbers but in God's favour and gracious return to rest with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
