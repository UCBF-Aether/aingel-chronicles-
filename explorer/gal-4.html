The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Galatians 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now I say, That the heir, as long as he is a child, differeth nothing from a servant, though he be lord of all;

2 But is under tutors and governors until the time appointed of the father.

3 Even so we, when we were children, were in bondage under the elements of the world:

4 But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,

5 To redeem them that were under the law, that we might receive the adoption of sons.

6 And because ye are sons, God hath sent forth the Spirit of his Son into your hearts, crying, Abba, Father.

7 Wherefore thou art no more a servant, but a son; and if a son, then an heir of God through Christ.

8 Howbeit then, when ye knew not God, ye did service unto them which by nature are no gods.

9 But now, after that ye have known God, or rather are known of God, how turn ye again to the weak and beggarly elements, whereunto ye desire again to be in bondage?

10 Ye observe days, and months, and times, and years.

11 I am afraid of you, lest I have bestowed upon you labour in vain.

12 Brethren, I beseech you, be as I am; for I am as ye are: ye have not injured me at all.

13 Ye know how through infirmity of the flesh I preached the gospel unto you at the first.

14 And my temptation which was in my flesh ye despised not, nor rejected; but received me as an angel of God, even as Christ Jesus.

15 Where is then the blessedness ye spake of? for I bear you record, that, if it had been possible, ye would have plucked out your own eyes, and have given them to me.

16 Am I therefore become your enemy, because I tell you the truth?

17 They zealously affect you, but not well; yea, they would exclude you, that ye might affect them.

18 But it is good to be zealously affected always in a good thing, and not only when I am present with you.

19 My little children, of whom I travail in birth again until Christ be formed in you,

20 I desire to be present with you now, and to change my voice; for I stand in doubt of you.

21 Tell me, ye that desire to be under the law, do ye not hear the law?

22 For it is written, that Abraham had two sons, the one by a bondmaid, the other by a freewoman.

23 But he who was of the bondwoman was born after the flesh; but he of the freewoman was by promise.

24 Which things are an allegory: for these are the two covenants; the one from the mount Sinai, which gendereth to bondage, which is Agar.

25 For this Agar is mount Sinai in Arabia, and answereth to Jerusalem which now is, and is in bondage with her children.

26 But Jerusalem which is above is free, which is the mother of us all.

27 For it is written, Rejoice, thou barren that bearest not; break forth and cry, thou that travailest not: for the desolate hath many more children than she which hath an husband.

28 Now we, brethren, as Isaac was, are the children of promise.

29 But as then he that was born after the flesh persecuted him that was born after the Spirit, even so it is now.

30 Nevertheless what saith the scripture? Cast out the bondwoman and her son: for the son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the freewoman.

31 So then, brethren, we are not children of the bondwoman, but of the free.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter moves from childhood under guardians to adoption as sons, with God's Spirit crying in believers' hearts. Paul pleads with readers slipping back into observances of days and seasons, and retells Abraham's two sons, Hagar and Sarah, as an allegory: believers are children of the free woman, children of promise, not of bondage. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The heir, as long as he is a child, differs nothing from a servant, though he is lord of all, a picture of believers under the law's tutelage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. But he is under tutors and governors until the time appointed by the father, as the law was a dispensation of darkness and bondage with burdensome rites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Even so, when they were children, they were in bondage under the elements of the world, tied to observances by which they were taught and kept subject. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. But when the fullness of time came, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law, the Father's sending, the Son's low submission, and the Spirit's dwelling showing the wonders of divine love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He came to redeem those under the law, that they might receive the adoption of sons, by nature children of wrath becoming by grace children of love, all heirs as eldest sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Because they are sons, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into their hearts, crying, Abba, Father, the Spirit witnessing with their spirits that they are children and heirs of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Wherefore a believer is no more a servant but a son, and if a son, then an heir of God through Christ, and the temper and conduct of sons should ever show their adoption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Then, when they knew not God, they served those which by nature are no gods, idols being no gods at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. But now, after knowing God, or rather being known by God, how do they turn again to the weak and beggarly elements, to which they desire again to be in bondage, since all knowledge of God begins on his part. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They observe days, months, times, and years, returning to legal observances for justification, a folly greater because of the mercy already shown them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Paul is afraid for them, lest he has bestowed labor on them in vain, yet he still labors, which, whatever follows, is true wisdom and the fear of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Brethren, Paul beseeches them to be as he is, for he is as they are, and they have not injured him at all, desiring one mind with him about the law and union in love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. They know how through infirmity of the flesh he preached the gospel to them at first, laboring under difficulty when he first came among them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. His temptation in his flesh they despised not nor rejected, but received him as an angel of God, even as Christ Jesus, welcoming the messenger despite his infirmity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Where then is the blessedness they spoke of, for Paul bears record that if possible they would have plucked out their own eyes and given them to him, so uncertain are the favor and respect of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Am I therefore become your enemy, because I tell you the truth? Christians must not forbear speaking truth for fear of offending, and Paul was their friend, not their enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The false teachers zealously affect them, but not well, seeking to exclude them that they might affect the teachers in return, pretending affection without sincerity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. But it is good to be zealously affected always in a good thing, and not only when Paul is present, zeal being a rule to maintain always, not for a time only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. My little children, of whom Paul travails in birth again until Christ be formed in them, shows his parental feeling toward them, anxious over their present delusions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. He desires to be present with them now and to change his voice, for he stands in doubt of them, and nothing so surely proves justification as Christ formed in a sinner by the Spirit's renewal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Tell me, you who desire to be under the law, do you not hear the law itself, which Paul will now use against legal confidence? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by a bondmaid, the other by a freewoman, the histories of Isaac and Ishmael explaining the two covenants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. He who was of the bondwoman was born after the flesh, but he of the freewoman was by promise, works of man's own strength being legal, while fruit arising from faith in Christ is evangelical. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. These things are an allegory, for these are the two covenants, Hagar and Sarah being apt emblems of the two dispensations, beyond the literal sense the Spirit pointing further. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. For Hagar is Mount Sinai in Arabia, answering to Jerusalem which now is, in bondage with her children, the first covenant spirit being bondage unto sin and death, and a spirit of persecution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. But Jerusalem which is above is free, which is the mother of us all, the heavenly Jerusalem, the true church from above, free and mother of all believers born of the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. For it is written, rejoice, barren that bearest not, break forth and cry, thou that travailest not, for the desolate has many more children than she who has a husband, the free covenant bearing children by promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Now they, brethren, as Isaac was, are children of promise, by regeneration and true faith made part of Abraham's true seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. But as then he born after the flesh persecuted him born after the Spirit, even so it is now, the first covenant spirit persecuting while the second is a spirit of love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Nevertheless what says Scripture? Cast out the bondwoman and her son, for the son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the freewoman, a text Paul applies directly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. So then, brethren, they are not children of the bondwoman but of the free, and it is absurd for Gentile converts to be under a law which could not deliver unbelieving Jews from bondage or condemnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • tutors and governors. guardians and stewards placed over a child heir until the appointed time (verses 1–2) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
  • elements of the world. the elementary principles and observances under which readers lived as children, before adoption as sons (verse 3) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
