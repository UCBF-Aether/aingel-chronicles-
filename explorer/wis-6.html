The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Wisdom of Solomon 6 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Hear therefore, O ye kings, and understand; learn, ye that be judges of the ends of the earth.

2 Give ear, ye that rule the people, and glory in the multitude of nations.

3 For power is given you of the Lord, and sovereignty from the Highest, who shall try your works, and search out your counsels.

4 Because, being ministers of his kingdom, ye have not judged aright, nor kept the law, nor walked after the counsel of God;

5 Horribly and speedily shall he come upon you: for a sharp judgment shall be to them that be in high places.

6 For mercy will soon pardon the meanest: but mighty men shall be mightily tormented.

7 For he which is Lord over all shall fear no man's person, neither shall he stand in awe of any man's greatness: for he hath made the small and great, and careth for all alike.

8 But a sore trial shall come upon the mighty.

9 Unto you therefore, O kings, do I speak, that ye may learn wisdom, and not fall away.

10 For they that keep holiness holily shall be judged holy: and they that have learned such things shall find what to answer.

11 Wherefore set your affection upon my words; desire them, and ye shall be instructed.

12 Wisdom is glorious, and never fadeth away: yea, she is easily seen of them that love her, and found of such as seek her.

13 She preventeth them that desire her, in making herself first known unto them.

14 Whoso seeketh her early shall have no great travail: for he shall find her sitting at his doors.

15 To think therefore upon her is perfection of wisdom: and whoso watcheth for her shall quickly be without care.

16 For she goeth about seeking such as are worthy of her, sheweth herself favourably unto them in the ways, and meeteth them in every thought.

17 For the very true beginning of her is the desire of discipline; and the care of discipline is love;

18 And love is the keeping of her laws; and the giving heed unto her laws is the assurance of incorruption;

19 And incorruption maketh us near unto God:

20 Therefore the desire of wisdom bringeth to a kingdom.

21 If your delight be then in thrones and sceptres, O ye kings of the people, honour wisdom, that ye may reign for evermore.

22 As for wisdom, what she is, and how she came up, I will tell you, and will not hide mysteries from you: but will seek her out from the beginning of her nativity, and bring the knowledge of her into light, and will not pass over the truth.

23 Neither will I go with consuming envy; for such a man shall have no fellowship with wisdom.

24 But the multitude of the wise is the welfare of the world: and a wise king is the upholding of the people.

25 Receive therefore instruction through my words, and it shall do you good.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Kings and judges of the ends of the earth are warned that power comes from the Lord, who tries their works, and that mighty men face mighty torment while the lowly may find mercy. They are urged to learn wisdom, who is glorious, never fades, is easily found by seekers, and whose true beginning is the desire of discipline, whose keeping leads to a kingdom. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hear therefore, O kings, and understand; learn, you judges of the ends of the earth. Haydock notes the address turns to rulers: wisdom is first a lesson for those who judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. Give ear, you who rule the people and glory in the multitude of nations. Haydock observes that glorying in multitudes is no substitute for understanding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. Power is given you of the Lord, and sovereignty from the Highest, who shall try your works and search out your counsels. Haydock stresses that royal power is derived, and will itself be tried. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. Because, being ministers of his kingdom, you have not judged aright, nor kept the law, nor walked after God's counsel, therefore he shall come upon you terribly and swiftly. Haydock's warning is that ministers of a kingdom are judged by the kingdom's law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. A sharp judgment shall be to those in high places, for mercy will soon pardon the meanest, but the mighty shall be mightily tormented. Haydock notes the proportion: high place brings sharper judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. God, Lord over all, shall not fear any man's greatness, nor respect any person's greatness, for he made small and great alike and cares for all alike. Haydock stresses God's impartiality: he made both, and regards both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. The mighty are mightily examined. Haydock's point is that examination matches station. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. To you therefore, O kings, are my words, that you may learn wisdom and not fall away. Haydock reads the address as instruction, not flattery: learn, lest you fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. They who keep holy things holily shall be judged holy, and they who learn them shall find a defence. Haydock observes that keeping and learning holy things is itself a defence at judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. Desire therefore my words, long for them, and you shall be instructed. Haydock notes desire is the beginning of instruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. Wisdom is glorious and never fades away, and is easily seen by those who love her, and found by those who seek her. Haydock's point is that wisdom, unlike earthly glory, does not fade. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. She prevents those who desire her, making herself first known to them. Haydock observes that wisdom meets the seeker halfway, coming before he finds her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. Whoever seeks her early shall have no great travail, for he shall find her sitting at his doors. Haydock notes the nearness: wisdom waits at the door, not at the ends of the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. To think upon her is perfection of wisdom, and whoever watches for her shall quickly be without care. Haydock stresses that meditation on wisdom is itself wisdom's perfection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. She goes about seeking those worthy of her, shows herself favourably to them in the ways, and meets them in every thought. Haydock pictures wisdom as the seeker, finding the worthy in their daily ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. The very true beginning of her is the desire of discipline, and care of discipline is love. Haydock notes the chain: desire leads to discipline, discipline to love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. Love is the keeping of her laws, and giving heed to her laws is the assurance of incorruption. Haydock observes that love proves itself by keeping laws, and keeping them assures incorruption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. Incorruption makes us near to God. Haydock's point is that the incorruptible life draws near to the incorruptible God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. Therefore the desire of wisdom brings to a kingdom. Haydock notes the end of the chain: desire of wisdom ends in a kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. If your delight is in thrones and sceptres, O kings of the people, honour wisdom, that you may reign for evermore. Haydock's counsel to kings is that lasting reign comes by honouring wisdom, not by holding sceptres. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. I will tell you what wisdom is, and from whence she came; I will not hide mysteries from you, but will seek her out from the beginning of her birth. Haydock observes the author's promise: wisdom's origin will be told openly, not hidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. I will not go with consuming envy, for such a man shall have no fellowship with wisdom. Haydock notes envy and wisdom cannot dwell together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 23. A multitude of wise men is the welfare of the world, and a wise king is the upholding of the people. Haydock's political maxim is that wise counsel is a people's welfare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 24. Receive therefore instruction through my words, and it shall do you good. Haydock closes the address with the practical end: instruction received does good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 25. Receive therefore instruction through my words, and it shall do you good. Haydock closes the address to kings with the practical end: instruction received does good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
