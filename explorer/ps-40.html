The Text — Psalms 40 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry.

2 He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings.

3 And he hath put a new song in my mouth, even praise unto our God: many shall see it, and fear, and shall trust in the LORD.

4 Blessed is that man that maketh the LORD his trust, and respecteth not the proud, nor such as turn aside to lies.

5 Many, O LORD my God, are thy wonderful works which thou hast done, and thy thoughts which are to us-ward: they cannot be reckoned up in order unto thee: if I would declare and speak of them, they are more than can be numbered.

6 Sacrifice and offering thou didst not desire; mine ears hast thou opened: burnt offering and sin offering hast thou not required.

7 Then said I, Lo, I come: in the volume of the book it is written of me,

8 I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart.

9 I have preached righteousness in the great congregation: lo, I have not refrained my lips, O LORD, thou knowest.

10 I have not hid thy righteousness within my heart; I have declared thy faithfulness and thy salvation: I have not concealed thy lovingkindness and thy truth from the great congregation.

11 Withhold not thou thy tender mercies from me, O LORD: let thy lovingkindness and thy truth continually preserve me.

12 For innumerable evils have compassed me about: mine iniquities have taken hold upon me, so that I am not able to look up; they are more than the hairs of mine head: therefore my heart faileth me.

13 Be pleased, O LORD, to deliver me: O LORD, make haste to help me.

14 Let them be ashamed and confounded together that seek after my soul to destroy it; let them be driven backward and put to shame that wish me evil.

15 Let them be desolate for a reward of their shame that say unto me, Aha, aha.

16 Let all those that seek thee rejoice and be glad in thee: let such as love thy salvation say continually, The LORD be magnified.

17 But I am poor and needy; yet the Lord thinketh upon me: thou art my help and my deliverer; make no tarrying, O my God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Patient waiting answered: lifted from pit and miry clay to rock, a new song in the mouth, wonderful works beyond reckoning. Sacrifice gives way to opened ears and the coming One who delights to do God's will and preaches righteousness openly, then evils compass him again, and the psalm closes in the urgent cry that Psalm 70 repeats: poor and needy, yet the Lord thinks on him, make no tarrying. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Psalm 40 and Psalm 70: a closing cry standing alone?

This section investigates a parallel between two texts. It does not conclude how the parallel came to be, and it claims no direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty about either text's origin. Interpreted Standard corpus, ps.json (Psalms 40 and 70)

Parallels. Psalm 70, all five verses of it, reproduces the closing cry of Psalm 40 (verses 13-17) nearly verse for verse: haste asked for deliverance and help; soul-seekers ashamed, confounded, and turned backward; the mocking 'Aha, aha' turned back as shame's reward; all who seek God rejoicing and glad, lovers of his salvation saying continually that God be magnified; and the close, poor and needy, God as help and deliverer, make no tarrying.

Differences. Psalm 70 opens 'Make haste, O God, to deliver me' where Psalm 40 verse 13 opens 'Be pleased, O LORD, to deliver me.' Psalm 70 verse 4 says 'Let God be magnified' where Psalm 40 verse 16 says 'The LORD be magnified.' And Psalm 70 stands without Psalm 40's first twelve verses: the pit and miry clay, the new song, the wonderful works beyond reckoning, opened ears, and righteousness preached in the great congregation.

Whether one text was adapted from the other, both were drawn from a shared source, or the likeness arose another way, is not established by the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved No source consulted for this page establishes the relation between the two texts
