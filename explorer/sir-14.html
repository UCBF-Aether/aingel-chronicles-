The Text — Sirach 14 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Blessed is the man that hath not slipped with his mouth, and is not pricked with the multitude of sins.

2 Blessed is he whose conscience hath not condemned him, and who is not fallen from his hope in the Lord.

3 Riches are not comely for a niggard: and what should an envious man do with money?

4 He that gathereth by defrauding his own soul gathereth for others, that shall spend his goods riotously.

5 He that is evil to himself, to whom will he be good? he shall not take pleasure in his goods.

6 There is none worse than he that envieth himself; and this is a recompence of his wickedness.

7 And if he doeth good, he doeth it unwillingly; and at the last he will declare his wickedness.

8 The envious man hath a wicked eye; he turneth away his face, and despiseth men.

9 A covetous man's eye is not satisfied with his portion; and the iniquity of the wicked drieth up his soul.

10 A wicked eye envieth [his] bread, and he is a niggard at his table.

11 My son, according to thy ability do good to thyself, and give the Lord his due offering.

12 Remember that death will not be long in coming, and that the covenant of the grave is not shewed unto thee.

13 Do good unto thy friend before thou die, and according to thy ability stretch out thy hand and give to him.

14 Defraud not thyself of the good day, and let not the part of a good desire overpass thee.

15 Shalt thou not leave thy travails unto another? and thy labours to be divided by lot?

16 Give, and take, and sanctify thy soul; for there is no seeking of dainties in the grave.

17 All flesh waxeth old as a garment: for the covenant from the beginning is, Thou shalt die the death.

18 As of the green leaves on a thick tree, some fall, and some grow; so is the generation of flesh and blood, one cometh to an end, and another is born.

19 Every work rotteth and consumeth away, and the worker thereof shall go withal.

20 Blessed is the man that doth meditate good things in wisdom, and that reasoneth of holy things by his understanding. ing.

21 He that considereth her ways in his heart shall also have understanding in her secrets.

22 Go after her as one that traceth, and lie in wait in her ways.

23 He that prieth in at her windows shall also hearken at her doors.

24 He that doth lodge near her house shall also fasten a pin in her walls.

25 He shall pitch his tent nigh unto her, and shall lodge in a lodging where good things are.

26 He shall set his children under her shelter, and shall lodge under her branches.

27 By her he shall be covered from heat, and in her glory shall he dwell.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Blessed is the man guarded in speech and not pricked by a multitude of sins. The chapter counsels enjoying the good day rather than defrauding oneself of it, and closes by picturing wisdom as shelter: the wise man sets his children under her branches and dwells in her glory, covered from heat. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Blessed is the man who has not slipped with his mouth and is not pricked with the multitude of sins. Guarded speech is blessedness, because the slip of the mouth pricks a man with many sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Blessed is he whose conscience has not condemned him, and who has not fallen from his hope in the Lord. A clear conscience and unfallen hope in the Lord are blessed together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. Riches are not comely for a niggard, and what should an envious man do with money? Wealth sits ill on a miser, and money in an envious man's hands serves no good purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. He who gathers by defrauding his own soul gathers for others, who will spend his goods riotously. Wealth gathered at the soul's cost is gathered for others, who will waste it in riot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. He who is evil to himself, to whom will he be good? He will not take pleasure in his goods. A man who is evil even to himself cannot be good to anyone, and he takes no pleasure in his own goods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. There is none worse than he who envies himself, and this is a recompense of his wickedness. Self-envy is the worst condition, and it is itself the repayment of wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. If he does good, he does it unwillingly, and at last he will declare his wickedness. Even the miser's good deeds are unwilling, and in the end his wickedness shows itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. The envious man has a wicked eye; he turns away his face and despises men. Envy shows as a wicked eye, a turned face, and contempt for men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. A covetous man's eye is not satisfied with his portion, and the iniquity of the wicked dries up his soul. Covetousness is never satisfied with its portion, and wicked iniquity withers the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. A wicked eye envies his bread, and he is a niggard at his table. The wicked eye grudges even bread, and the covetous man is stingy at his own table. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. According to your ability do good to yourself, and give the Lord his due offering. Ability sets the measure: do good to yourself within it, and give God his due offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. Remember that death will not be long in coming, and that the covenant of the grave is not shown to you. Death comes soon and its terms are not shown in advance, so delay is folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. Do good to your friend before you die, and according to your ability stretch out your hand and give to him. Good to a friend must be done before death, in proportion to ability, while the hand can still give. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. Do not defraud yourself of the good day, and do not let the part of a good desire pass you by. The good day is not to be denied to oneself, nor a good desire allowed to slip past unused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. Will you not leave your travails to another, and your labours to be divided by lot? All your toils will be left to another and divided by lot, so hoarding them for yourself is vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. Give and take, and sanctify your soul, for there is no seeking of dainties in the grave. Giving and receiving in this life sanctify the soul, because the grave offers no dainties to seek. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. All flesh waxes old as a garment, for the covenant from the beginning is, you shall die the death. All flesh ages like a garment, under the original covenant of death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. As of the green leaves on a thick tree, some fall and some grow, so is the generation of flesh and blood: one comes to an end and another is born. Generations replace each other like leaves on a thick tree, some falling while others grow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. Every work rots and consumes away, and the worker thereof will go with it. Works decay, and the worker goes with them; nothing made by hands endures apart from its maker. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 20. Blessed is the man who meditates good things in wisdom and reasons of holy things by his understanding. Meditation on good things in wisdom and reasoning on holy things bring blessedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 21. He who considers her ways in his heart will also have understanding in her secrets. Considering wisdom's ways inwardly leads to understanding her secrets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 22. Go after her as one who traces, and lie in wait in her ways. Wisdom must be pursued like a tracker and waited for on her paths. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 23. He who pries in at her windows will also hearken at her doors. Prying at wisdom's windows and listening at her doors picture eager, close attention to her house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 24. He who lodges near her house will also fasten a pin in her walls. Lodging near wisdom's house and fastening a pin in its walls picture settling down beside her for good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 25. He will pitch his tent near to her, and will lodge in a lodging where good things are. Camping beside wisdom means lodging where good things are found. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 26. He will set his children under her shelter, and will lodge under her branches. Wisdom's shelter covers children too, and the wise man lives under her branches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 27. By her he will be covered from heat, and in her glory he will dwell. Wisdom shades from heat and her glory becomes the wise man's dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
