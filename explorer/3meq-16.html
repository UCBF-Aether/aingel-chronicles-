Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter argues from seed to resurrection: what is planted is not changed into something else — vine stays vine, fig stays fig, root stays root, and sägäm stays sägäm — each bringing forth fruit in its own seed, tree, and kind by the dew of mercy from God. It opens by insisting the resurrection is in one’s own flesh, not in another flesh, and that each shall be judged according to his deeds at the resurrection. Likewise, it says, the earth shall bring forth what God has sown, and doers of good are not changed into doers of evil nor the reverse. When the horn blows, the dead shall rise by that dew of mercy: those who did good, in the resurrection of life, to Paradise of joy, a dwelling for the pure where there is no disease or sickness and they shall not die again; those who did evil, in the resurrection of judgment, with the devil who led them and his evil angels, beneath in darkness where there is weeping, no forgiveness, no mercy, and no escape for ever — because they did not good in their flesh upon earth. The chapter ends by insisting the judgment is of soul and flesh together, though its first and last verses are fragments. Interpreted The chapter text itself; no study note exists for this chapter
