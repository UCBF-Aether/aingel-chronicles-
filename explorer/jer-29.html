The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 29 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now these are the words of the letter that Jeremiah the prophet sent from Jerusalem unto the residue of the elders which were carried away captives, and to the priests, and to the prophets, and to all the people whom Nebuchadnezzar had carried away captive from Jerusalem to Babylon;

2 (After that Jeconiah the king, and the queen, and the eunuchs, the princes of Judah and Jerusalem, and the carpenters, and the smiths, were departed from Jerusalem;)

3 By the hand of Elasah the son of Shaphan, and Gemariah the son of Hilkiah, (whom Zedekiah king of Judah sent unto Babylon to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon) saying,

4 Thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, unto all that are carried away captives, whom I have caused to be carried away from Jerusalem unto Babylon;

5 Build ye houses, and dwell in them; and plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them;

6 Take ye wives, and beget sons and daughters; and take wives for your sons, and give your daughters to husbands, that they may bear sons and daughters; that ye may be increased there, and not diminished.

7 And seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto the LORD for it: for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace.

8 For thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Let not your prophets and your diviners, that be in the midst of you, deceive you, neither hearken to your dreams which ye cause to be dreamed.

9 For they prophesy falsely unto you in my name: I have not sent them, saith the LORD.

10 For thus saith the LORD, That after seventy years be accomplished at Babylon I will visit you, and perform my good word toward you, in causing you to return to this place.

11 For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end.

12 Then shall ye call upon me, and ye shall go and pray unto me, and I will hearken unto you.

13 And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart.

14 And I will be found of you, saith the LORD: and I will turn away your captivity, and I will gather you from all the nations, and from all the places whither I have driven you, saith the LORD; and I will bring you again into the place whence I caused you to be carried away captive.

15 Because ye have said, The LORD hath raised us up prophets in Babylon;

16 Know that thus saith the LORD of the king that sitteth upon the throne of David, and of all the people that dwelleth in this city, and of your brethren that are not gone forth with you into captivity;

17 Thus saith the LORD of hosts; Behold, I will send upon them the sword, the famine, and the pestilence, and will make them like vile figs, that cannot be eaten, they are so evil.

18 And I will persecute them with the sword, with the famine, and with the pestilence, and will deliver them to be removed to all the kingdoms of the earth, to be a curse, and an astonishment, and an hissing, and a reproach, among all the nations whither I have driven them:

19 Because they have not hearkened to my words, saith the LORD, which I sent unto them by my servants the prophets, rising up early and sending them; but ye would not hear, saith the LORD.

20 Hear ye therefore the word of the LORD, all ye of the captivity, whom I have sent from Jerusalem to Babylon:

21 Thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, of Ahab the son of Kolaiah, and of Zedekiah the son of Maaseiah, which prophesy a lie unto you in my name; Behold, I will deliver them into the hand of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon; and he shall slay them before your eyes;

22 And of them shall be taken up a curse by all the captivity of Judah which are in Babylon, saying, The LORD make thee like Zedekiah and like Ahab, whom the king of Babylon roasted in the fire;

23 Because they have committed villany in Israel, and have committed adultery with their neighbours’ wives, and have spoken lying words in my name, which I have not commanded them; even I know, and am a witness, saith the LORD.

24 Thus shalt thou also speak to Shemaiah the Nehelamite, saying,

25 Thus speaketh the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, saying, Because thou hast sent letters in thy name unto all the people that are at Jerusalem, and to Zephaniah the son of Maaseiah the priest, and to all the priests, saying,

26 The LORD hath made thee priest in the stead of Jehoiada the priest, that ye should be officers in the house of the LORD, for every man that is mad, and maketh himself a prophet, that thou shouldest put him in prison, and in the stocks.

27 Now therefore why hast thou not reproved Jeremiah of Anathoth, which maketh himself a prophet to you?

28 For therefore he sent unto us in Babylon, saying, This captivity is long: build ye houses, and dwell in them; and plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them.

29 And Zephaniah the priest read this letter in the ears of Jeremiah the prophet.

30 Then came the word of the LORD unto Jeremiah, saying,

31 Send to all them of the captivity, saying, Thus saith the LORD concerning Shemaiah the Nehelamite; Because that Shemaiah hath prophesied unto you, and I sent him not, and he caused you to trust in a lie:

32 Therefore thus saith the LORD; Behold, I will punish Shemaiah the Nehelamite, and his seed: he shall not have a man to dwell among this people; neither shall he behold the good that I will do for my people, saith the LORD; because he hath taught rebellion against the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This is Jeremiah's letter from Jerusalem to the exiles in Babylon: build houses, plant gardens, marry, and seek the peace of the city where you are captive, for the exile will last seventy years before the promised return. The letter also exposes false prophets among the exiles, and ends with punishment pronounced on Shemaiah the Nehelamite, who opposed it. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Now these are the words of the letter that Jeremiah the prophet sent from Jerusalem unto the residue of the elders which were carried away captives, and to the priests, and to the prophets, and to all the people whom Nebuchadnezzar had carried away captive from Jerusalem to Babylon. Henry notes the written word of God is as truly given by inspiration of God as his spoken word: the letter to the residue of elders, priests, prophets, and all the people carried from Jerusalem to Babylon is inspired writing sent to the far off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. After that Jeconiah the king, and the queen, and the eunuchs, the princes of Judah and Jerusalem, and the carpenters, and the smiths, were departed from Jerusalem. Henry's letter follows Jeconiah, queen, eunuchs, princes, carpenters, and smiths departed from Jerusalem: the zealous servant uses every means to profit those far off, and writing reaches the departed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. By the hand of Elasah the son of Shaphan, and Gemariah the son of Hilkiah, whom Zedekiah king of Judah sent unto Babylon to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, saying. Henry notes the art of writing is very profitable for this end, and by the art of printing it is rendered most beneficial for circulating the knowledge of the word of God: by Elasah and Gemariah, sent by Zedekiah to Nebuchadnezzar, the letter travels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, unto all that are carried away captives, whom I have caused to be carried away from Jerusalem unto Babylon. Henry notes God's sending to the captives by this letter would show that he had not forsaken them, though he was displeased, and corrected them: thus says the Lord of hosts to all carried from Jerusalem to Babylon is displeasure with presence, not forsaking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Build ye houses, and dwell in them; and plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them. Henry notes if they live in the fear of God, they may live comfortably in Babylon: build houses and dwell, plant gardens and eat fruit, for in all conditions of life it is our wisdom and duty not to throw away the comfort of what we may have, because we have not all we would have. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Take ye wives, and beget sons and daughters; and take wives for your sons, and give your daughters to husbands, that they may bear sons and daughters; that ye may be increased there, and not diminished. Henry's increase there, not diminished, is living comfortably in Babylon under God's fear: take wives, beget sons and daughters, marry sons and daughters, and be increased in the place of captivity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. And seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto the Lord for it: for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace. Henry notes they are directed to seek the good of the country where they were captives: while the king of Babylon protected them, they must live quiet and peaceable lives under him, in all godliness and honesty, patiently leaving it to God to work deliverance for them in due time, and in the city's peace they have peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. For thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Let not your prophets and your diviners, that be in the midst of you, deceive you, neither hearken to your dreams which ye cause to be dreamed. Henry warns let men beware how they call those prophets whom they choose after their own fancies, and how they consider their fancies and dreams to be revelations from God: let not prophets and diviners deceive, nor dreams caused to be dreamed be hearkened to. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. For they prophesy falsely unto you in my name: I have not sent them, says the Lord. Henry notes false prophets flatter people in their sins, because they love to be flattered, and they speak smoothly to their prophets, that their prophets may speak smoothly to them: they prophesy falsely in God's name, unsent, and flattery is the falsehood's currency. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. For thus says the Lord, That after seventy years be accomplished at Babylon I will visit you, and perform my good word toward you, in causing you to return to this place. Henry notes God promises that they should return after seventy years were accomplished: by this it appears that the seventy years of the captivity are not to be reckoned from the last captivity, but the first, and it will be the bringing to pass of God's good word to them, visiting and causing return to this place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the Lord, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end. Henry notes this shall form God's purposes: we often do not know our own minds, but the Lord is never at an uncertainty, and thoughts of peace, not evil, to give an expected end are God's formed purposes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Then shall ye call upon me, and ye shall go and pray unto me, and I will hearken unto you. Henry notes when the Lord pours out an especial spirit of prayer, it is a good sign that he is coming toward us in mercy: promises are given to quicken and encourage prayer, and then shall ye call, go and pray, and he will hearken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart. Henry notes he never said, Seek ye me in vain: ye shall seek and find when ye search with all your heart, and the seeking is not vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. And I will be found of you, says the Lord: and I will turn away your captivity, and I will gather you from all the nations, and from all the places whither I have driven you, says the Lord; and I will bring you again into the place whence I caused you to be carried away captive. Henry notes we are sometimes ready to fear that God's designs are all against us, but as to his own people, even that which seems evil is for good: he will give them, not the expectations of their fears, or the expectations of their fancies, but the expectations of their faith, the end he has promised, which will be the best for them, and being found, turning captivity, gathering from all nations and places, and bringing again to the place of departure is that promised end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Because ye have said, The Lord has raised us up prophets in Babylon. Henry's because ye have said, The Lord has raised us up prophets in Babylon, is the fancy-prophet claim warned against: calling chosen prophets after one's own fancies, and dreams revelations, is the delusion the letter answers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Know that thus says the Lord of the king that sits upon the throne of David, and of all the people that dwell in this city, and of your brethren that are not gone forth with you into captivity. Henry's know that thus says the Lord of the David-throne king, all this city's people, and brethren not gone into captivity, turns the letter to those who remained: their fate, not the Babylon prophets' promise, is the next word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Thus says the Lord of hosts; Behold, I will send upon them the sword, the famine, and the pestilence, and will make them like vile figs, that cannot be eaten, they are so evil. Henry notes those who remained at Jerusalem would be utterly destroyed, notwithstanding what the false prophets said to the contrary: sword, famine, and pestilence, like vile uneatable figs, are the remaining people's fate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. And I will persecute them with the sword, with the famine, and with the pestilence, and will deliver them to be removed to all the kingdoms of the earth, to be a curse, and an astonishment, and an hissing, and a reproach, among all the nations whither I have driven them. Henry's persecution with sword, famine, and pestilence, removal to all kingdoms, curse, astonishment, hissing, and reproach among all nations, is the utter destruction promised despite false prophets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Because they have not hearkened to my words, says the Lord, which I sent unto them by my servants the prophets, rising up early and sending them; but ye have not hearkened, says the Lord. Henry notes the reason has often been given, and it justifies the eternal ruin of impenitent sinners: because they have not hearkened to my words, sent by servants the prophets, rising early and sending, but ye have not hearkened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Hear ye therefore the word of the Lord, all ye of the captivity, whom I have sent from Jerusalem to Babylon. Henry notes Jeremiah foretells judgments upon the false prophets, who deceived the Jews in Babylon: hear ye therefore the word of the Lord, all ye of the captivity sent from Jerusalem to Babylon, for judgment on deceivers follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, of Ahab the son of Kolaiah, and of Zedekiah the son of Maaseiah, which prophesy a lie unto you in my name; Behold, I will deliver them into the hand of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon; and he shall slay them before your eyes. Henry notes lying was bad; lying to the people of the Lord, to delude them into a false hope, was worse; but pretending to rest their own lies upon the God of truth, was worst of all: Ahab and Zedekiah, prophesying lies in God's name, are delivered to Nebuchadnezzar to be slain before their eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. And of them shall be taken up a curse by all the captivity of Judah which are in Babylon, saying, The Lord make thee like Zedekiah and like Ahab, whom the king of Babylon roasted in the fire. Henry's curse taken up by all Babylon's Judah captivity, the Lord make thee like Zedekiah and Ahab, whom Babylon's king roasted in fire, is the false prophets' name become a curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Because they have committed villany in Israel, and have committed adultery with their neighbours' wives, and have spoken lying words in my name, which I have not commanded them; even I know, and am a witness, says the Lord. Henry notes they flattered others in their sins, because they could not reprove them without condemning themselves: the most secret sins are known to God, and there is a day coming when he will bring to light all the hidden works of darkness, and villany, adultery, and lying words uncommanded are known, with God witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Thus shalt thou also speak to Shemaiah the Nehelamite, saying. Henry's thus shalt thou also speak to Shemaiah the Nehelamite begins the second letter: judgment on the false prophet in Babylon who wrote to Jerusalem follows the judgment on Ahab and Zedekiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Thus speaks the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, saying, Because thou hast sent letters in thy name unto all the people that are at Jerusalem, and to Zephaniah the son of Maaseiah the priest, and to all the priests, saying. Henry notes Shemaiah urges the priests to persecute Jeremiah: because thou hast sent letters in thy name to all Jerusalem's people, to Zephaniah and all priests, the letter-against-letter contest is named. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. The Lord has made thee priest in the stead of Jehoiada the priest, that ye should be officers in the house of the Lord, for every man that is mad, and makes himself a prophet, that thou shouldest put him in prison, and in the stocks. Henry notes their hearts are wretchedly hardened who justify doing mischief by having power to do it: the Lord made thee priest instead of Jehoiada, to be officers for every mad self-made prophet, to put him in prison and stocks, is Shemaiah's pretence of office for persecution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Now therefore why hast thou not reproved Jeremiah of Anathoth, which makes himself a prophet to you? Henry notes the accusations against many active Christians in all ages amount to no more than this, that they earnestly counsel men to attend to their true interest and duties, and to wait for the performance of God's promises in his appointed way: why hast thou not reproved Jeremiah of Anathoth, which makes himself a prophet to you, is the accusation against the true prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. For therefore he sent unto us in Babylon, saying, This captivity is long: build ye houses, and dwell in them; and plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them. Henry's therefore he sent unto us in Babylon, saying, This captivity is long, build houses and dwell, plant gardens and eat fruit, is the true counsel quoted as crime: the letter's patience is Shemaiah's charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. And Zephaniah the priest read this letter in the ears of Jeremiah the prophet. Henry notes they were in a miserable thraldom for mocking the messengers of the Lord, and misusing his prophets, yet in their distress they trespass still more against the Lord: Zephaniah reads Shemaiah's letter in Jeremiah's ears, and the mocking of messengers continues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. Then came the word of the Lord unto Jeremiah, saying. Henry's then came the word of the Lord unto Jeremiah is the answer to Shemaiah's letter: afflictions will not of themselves cure men of their sins, unless the grace of God works with them, and the word comes to answer the persecuting letter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. Send to all them of the captivity, saying, Thus says the Lord concerning Shemaiah the Nehelamite; Because that Shemaiah has prophesied unto you, and I sent him not, and he caused you to trust in a lie. Henry notes those who slight the blessings deserve to lose the benefit of God's word, like Shemaiah: send to all the captivity, thus says the Lord concerning Shemaiah, because he prophesied unsent and caused trust in a lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. Therefore thus says the Lord; Behold, I will punish Shemaiah the Nehelamite, and his seed: he shall not have a man to dwell among this people; neither shall he behold the good that I will do for my people, says the Lord; because he has taught rebellion against the Lord. Henry's punishment is he shall not have a man to dwell among this people, neither behold the good God will do for his people, because he taught rebellion: slighting blessings loses the benefit of God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Because they have not hearkened to my words, says the Lord, which I sent unto them by my servants the prophets, rising up early and sending them; but ye have not hearkened, says the Lord. Henry notes the reason has often been given, and it justifies the eternal ruin of impenitent sinners: because they have not hearkened to my words, sent by servants the prophets, rising early and sending, but ye have not hearkened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Hear ye therefore the word of the Lord, all ye of the captivity, whom I have sent from Jerusalem to Babylon. Henry notes Jeremiah foretells judgments upon the false prophets, who deceived the Jews in Babylon: hear ye therefore the word of the Lord, all ye of the captivity sent from Jerusalem to Babylon, for judgment on deceivers follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, of Ahab the son of Kolaiah, and of Zedekiah the son of Maaseiah, which prophesy a lie unto you in my name; Behold, I will deliver them into the hand of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon; and he shall slay them before your eyes. Henry notes lying was bad; lying to the people of the Lord, to delude them into a false hope, was worse; but pretending to rest their own lies upon the God of truth, was worst of all: Ahab and Zedekiah, prophesying lies in God's name, are delivered to Nebuchadnezzar to be slain before their eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. And of them shall be taken up a curse by all the captivity of Judah which are in Babylon, saying, The Lord make thee like Zedekiah and like Ahab, whom the king of Babylon roasted in the fire. Henry's curse taken up by all Babylon's Judah captivity, the Lord make thee like Zedekiah and Ahab, whom Babylon's king roasted in fire, is the false prophets' name become a curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Because they have committed villany in Israel, and have committed adultery with their neighbours' wives, and have spoken lying words in my name, which I have not commanded them; even I know, and am a witness, says the Lord. Henry notes they flattered others in their sins, because they could not reprove them without condemning themselves: the most secret sins are known to God, and there is a day coming when he will bring to light all the hidden works of darkness, and villany, adultery, and lying words uncommanded are known, with God witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Thus shalt thou also speak to Shemaiah the Nehelamite, saying. Henry's thus shalt thou also speak to Shemaiah the Nehelamite begins the second letter: judgment on the false prophet in Babylon who wrote to Jerusalem follows the judgment on Ahab and Zedekiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Thus speaks the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, saying, Because thou hast sent letters in thy name unto all the people that are at Jerusalem, and to Zephaniah the son of Maaseiah the priest, and to all the priests, saying. Henry notes Shemaiah urges the priests to persecute Jeremiah: because thou hast sent letters in thy name to all Jerusalem's people, to Zephaniah and all priests, the letter-against-letter contest is named. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. The Lord has made thee priest in the stead of Jehoiada the priest, that ye should be officers in the house of the Lord, for every man that is mad, and makes himself a prophet, that thou shouldest put him in prison, and in the stocks. Henry notes their hearts are wretchedly hardened who justify doing mischief by having power to do it: the Lord made thee priest instead of Jehoiada, to be officers for every mad self-made prophet, to put him in prison and stocks, is Shemaiah's pretence of office for persecution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Now therefore why hast thou not reproved Jeremiah of Anathoth, which makes himself a prophet to you? Henry notes the accusations against many active Christians in all ages amount to no more than this, that they earnestly counsel men to attend to their true interest and duties, and to wait for the performance of God's promises in his appointed way: why hast thou not reproved Jeremiah of Anathoth, which makes himself a prophet to you, is the accusation against the true prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. For therefore he sent unto us in Babylon, saying, This captivity is long: build ye houses, and dwell in them; and plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them. Henry's therefore he sent unto us in Babylon, saying, This captivity is long, build houses and dwell, plant gardens and eat fruit, is the true counsel quoted as crime: the letter's patience is Shemaiah's charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. And Zephaniah the priest read this letter in the ears of Jeremiah the prophet. Henry notes they were in a miserable thraldom for mocking the messengers of the Lord, and misusing his prophets, yet in their distress they trespass still more against the Lord: Zephaniah reads Shemaiah's letter in Jeremiah's ears, and the mocking of messengers continues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. Then came the word of the Lord unto Jeremiah, saying. Henry's then came the word of the Lord unto Jeremiah is the answer to Shemaiah's letter: afflictions will not of themselves cure men of their sins, unless the grace of God works with them, and the word comes to answer the persecuting letter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. Send to all them of the captivity, saying, Thus says the Lord concerning Shemaiah the Nehelamite; Because that Shemaiah has prophesied unto you, and I sent him not, and he caused you to trust in a lie. Henry notes those who slight the blessings deserve to lose the benefit of God's word, like Shemaiah: send to all the captivity, thus says the Lord concerning Shemaiah, because he prophesied unsent and caused trust in a lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. Therefore thus says the Lord; Behold, I will punish Shemaiah the Nehelamite, and his seed: he shall not have a man to dwell among this people; neither shall he behold the good that I will do for my people, says the Lord; because he has taught rebellion against the Lord. Henry's punishment is he shall not have a man to dwell among this people, neither behold the good God will do for his people, because he taught rebellion: slighting blessings loses the benefit of God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
