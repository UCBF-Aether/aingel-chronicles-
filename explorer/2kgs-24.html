The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 24 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up, and Jehoiakim became his servant three years: then he turned and rebelled against him.

2 And the LORD sent against him bands of the Chaldees, and bands of the Syrians, and bands of the Moabites, and bands of the children of Ammon, and sent them against Judah to destroy it, according to the word of the LORD, which he spake by his servants the prophets.

3 Surely at the commandment of the LORD came this upon Judah, to remove them out of his sight, for the sins of Manasseh, according to all that he did;

4 And also for the innocent blood that he shed: for he filled Jerusalem with innocent blood; which the LORD would not pardon.

5 Now the rest of the acts of Jehoiakim, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

6 So Jehoiakim slept with his fathers: and Jehoiachin his son reigned in his stead.

7 And the king of Egypt came not again any more out of his land: for the king of Babylon had taken from the river of Egypt unto the river Euphrates all that pertained to the king of Egypt.

8 Jehoiachin was eighteen years old when he began to reign, and he reigned in Jerusalem three months. And his mother’s name was Nehushta, the daughter of Elnathan of Jerusalem.

9 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, according to all that his father had done.

10 At that time the servants of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up against Jerusalem, and the city was besieged.

11 And Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came against the city, and his servants did besiege it.

12 And Jehoiachin the king of Judah went out to the king of Babylon, he, and his mother, and his servants, and his princes, and his officers: and the king of Babylon took him in the eighth year of his reign.

13 And he carried out thence all the treasures of the house of the LORD, and the treasures of the king’s house, and cut in pieces all the vessels of gold which Solomon king of Israel had made in the temple of the LORD, as the LORD had said.

14 And he carried away all Jerusalem, and all the princes, and all the mighty men of valour, even ten thousand captives, and all the craftsmen and smiths: none remained, save the poorest sort of the people of the land.

15 And he carried away Jehoiachin to Babylon, and the king’s mother, and the king’s wives, and his officers, and the mighty of the land, those carried he into captivity from Jerusalem to Babylon.

16 And all the men of might, even seven thousand, and craftsmen and smiths a thousand, all that were strong and apt for war, even them the king of Babylon brought captive to Babylon.

17 And the king of Babylon made Mattaniah his father’s brother king in his stead, and changed his name to Zedekiah.

18 Zedekiah was twenty and one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Hamutal, the daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.

19 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, according to all that Jehoiakim had done.

20 For through the anger of the LORD it came to pass in Jerusalem and Judah, until he had cast them out from his presence, that Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Judah's end begins: Nebuchadnezzar comes, Jehoiakim rebels and dies, and Jehoiachin surrenders after three months and is carried to Babylon with the treasures and the craftsmen. Zedekiah is set on the throne and rebels in turn, and the chapter closes with Jerusalem under siege. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In Jehoiakim's days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up, and Jehoiakim became his servant three years, then turned and rebelled. JFB identifies Nebuchadnezzar as the son of Nabopolassar, founder of the Chaldee monarchy, whose invasion fell in Jehoiakim's fourth year and Nebuchadnezzar's first, the young king, detained at home by his father's death, dispatching tributary nations' armies to chastise the revolt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The LORD sent against Jehoiakim bands of Chaldees, Syrians, Moabites, and Ammonites, sending them against Judah to destroy it according to the word spoken by his servants the prophets. JFB notes this hostile band was only an instrument executing the divine judgment denounced against Judah, and though marching by Assyria's monarch's orders, they are described as sent by the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Surely at the LORD's commandment this came upon Judah, to remove them out of his sight for Manasseh's sins, according to all he did. JFB presents this as the divine judgment for the people's sins, executed through the tributary armies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. This was also for the innocent blood Manasseh shed, filling Jerusalem with innocent blood, which the LORD would not pardon. JFB cross-refers this unpardonable guilt to the earlier declaration of God's unturned wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Jehoiakim slept with his fathers, and Jehoiachin his son reigned in his stead. JFB explains "slept with his fathers" means only that he died, for he was not buried with his royal ancestors, and whether he fell in battle or suffered posthumous insults, he was not honoured with sepulture rites as predicted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Egypt's king came no more out of his land, for Babylon's king had taken from Egypt's river to the Euphrates all that had pertained to Egypt's king. JFB identifies Egypt's king as Pharaohnechoh, now confined by Babylon's conquests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Jehoiachin was eighteen when he began to reign, reigning three months in Jerusalem, his mother Nehushta daughter of Elnathan of Jerusalem. JFB explains Jehoiachin means "God-appointed," contracted into Jeconiah and Coniah, and at eight his father had taken him into partnership, so he began to reign alone at eighteen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Jehoiachin did evil in the LORD's sight, according to all his father had done. JFB notes that, untaught by experience and deaf to prophetic warnings, he pursued the evil courses that had brought disasters on Judah's royal family and people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. At that time Nebuchadnezzar's servants came up against Jerusalem, and the city was besieged. JFB notes this was within three months of Jehoiachin's accession, in the spring, when he showed hostility to his Assyrian liege lord by forming a league with Egypt, so Nebuchadnezzar sent generals to besiege Jerusalem as Jeremiah had foretold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Nebuchadnezzar himself came against the city while his servants besieged it. JFB notes Nebuchadnezzar soon followed his generals in person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Jehoiachin went out to Babylon's king with his mother, servants, princes, and officers, and Babylon's king took him in his eighth year. JFB explains that, convinced resistance was hopeless, Jehoiachin surrendered expecting to retain his throne as vassal, but Nebuchadnezzar's clemency toward Judah's kings was exhausted, so Jehoiachin was sent captive to Babylon with the queen mother, generals, and officers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Nebuchadnezzar carried out all the treasures of the LORD's house and the king's house, cutting in pieces all the gold vessels Solomon had made, as the LORD had said. JFB notes the palace and temple were ransacked, smaller golden vessels having been taken at the first capture and placed in his god's temple, while now the gold plating was torn off all the larger temple furniture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He carried away all Jerusalem, all the princes, all mighty men of valour, ten thousand captives, and all craftsmen and smiths, leaving none but the poorest sort of the land's people. JFB explains the elite for rank, usefulness, and moral worth, all who might be useful in Babylon or dangerous in Palestine, were carried off, ten thousand in all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He carried away Jehoiachin to Babylon, with the king's mother, wives, officers, and Judah's mighty men. JFB notes those left consisted chiefly of the poorer sort and unskilled workmen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. All the men of might, seven thousand, and craftsmen and smiths, a thousand, all strong and apt for war, did Babylon's king bring captive to Babylon. JFB breaks down the ten thousand captives as seven thousand warriors, one thousand craftsmen and smiths, and two thousand kings' wives, officers, princes, priests, and prophets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Babylon's king made Mattaniah, Jehoiachin's father's brother, king in his stead, changing his name to Zedekiah. JFB explains Nebuchadnezzar, maintaining a show of monarchy, appointed Josiah's third and youngest son, full brother of Jehoahaz and uncle of captive Jehoiachin, and, as conquerors changed captives' names in token of supremacy, gave him the new name Zedekiah, "The righteous of God," apparently allowing the puppet king to choose his own Hebrew name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Zedekiah was twenty-one when he began to reign and reigned eleven years in Jerusalem, his mother Hamutal daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah. JFB's section on Zedekiah's evil reign opens with this accession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Zedekiah did evil in the LORD's sight, according to all Jehoiakim had done. JFB notes his heart toward God was the same as Jehoiakim's: impenitent and heedless of God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. For through the LORD's anger it came to pass in Jerusalem and Judah, until he had cast them out from his presence, that Zedekiah rebelled against Babylon's king. JFB explains that in God's righteous providence Zedekiah's policy as king would prove ruinous, instigated by neighbouring ambassadors urging a common league to throw off the yoke, though Jeremiah warned against it, and the infatuated, perjured Zedekiah persisted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
