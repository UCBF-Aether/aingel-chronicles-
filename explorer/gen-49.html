The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 49 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Jacob called unto his sons, and said, Gather yourselves together, that I may tell you that which shall befall you in the last days.

2 Gather yourselves together, and hear, ye sons of Jacob; and hearken unto Israel your father.

3 Reuben, thou art my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my strength, the excellency of dignity, and the excellency of power:

4 Unstable as water, thou shalt not excel; because thou wentest up to thy father’s bed; then defiledst thou it: he went up to my couch.

5 Simeon and Levi are brethren; instruments of cruelty are in their habitations.

6 O my soul, come not thou into their secret; unto their assembly, mine honour, be not thou united: for in their anger they slew a man, and in their selfwill they digged down a wall.

7 Cursed be their anger, for it was fierce; and their wrath, for it was cruel: I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel.

8 Judah, thou art he whom thy brethren shall praise: thy hand shall be in the neck of thine enemies; thy father’s children shall bow down before thee.

9 Judah is a lion’s whelp: from the prey, my son, thou art gone up: he stooped down, he couched as a lion, and as an old lion; who shall rouse him up?

10 The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, nor a lawgiver from between his feet, until Shiloh come; and unto him shall the gathering of the people be.

11 Binding his foal unto the vine, and his ass’s colt unto the choice vine; he washed his garments in wine, and his clothes in the blood of grapes:

12 His eyes shall be red with wine, and his teeth white with milk.

13 Zebulun shall dwell at the haven of the sea; and he shall be for an haven of ships; and his border shall be unto Zidon.

14 Issachar is a strong ass couching down between two burdens:

15 And he saw that rest was good, and the land that it was pleasant; and bowed his shoulder to bear, and became a servant unto tribute.

16 Dan shall judge his people, as one of the tribes of Israel.

17 Dan shall be a serpent by the way, an adder in the path, that biteth the horse heels, so that his rider shall fall backward.

18 I have waited for thy salvation, O LORD.

19 Gad, a troop shall overcome him: but he shall overcome at the last.

20 Out of Asher his bread shall be fat, and he shall yield royal dainties.

21 Naphtali is a hind let loose: he giveth goodly words.

22 Joseph is a fruitful bough, even a fruitful bough by a well; whose branches run over the wall:

23 The archers have sorely grieved him, and shot at him, and hated him:

24 But his bow abode in strength, and the arms of his hands were made strong by the hands of the mighty God of Jacob; (from thence is the shepherd, the stone of Israel:)

25 Even by the God of thy father, who shall help thee; and by the Almighty, who shall bless thee with blessings of heaven above, blessings of the deep that lieth under, blessings of the breasts, and of the womb:

26 The blessings of thy father have prevailed above the blessings of my progenitors unto the utmost bound of the everlasting hills: they shall be on the head of Joseph, and on the crown of the head of him that was separate from his brethren.

27 Benjamin shall ravin as a wolf: in the morning he shall devour the prey, and at night he shall divide the spoil.

28 All these are the twelve tribes of Israel: and this is it that their father spake unto them, and blessed them; every one according to his blessing he blessed them.

29 And he charged them, and said unto them, I am to be gathered unto my people: bury me with my fathers in the cave that is in the field of Ephron the Hittite,

30 In the cave that is in the field of Machpelah, which is before Mamre, in the land of Canaan, which Abraham bought with the field of Ephron the Hittite for a possession of a buryingplace.

31 There they buried Abraham and Sarah his wife; there they buried Isaac and Rebekah his wife; and there I buried Leah.

32 The purchase of the field and of the cave that is therein was from the children of Heth.

33 And when Jacob had made an end of commanding his sons, he gathered up his feet into the bed, and yielded up the ghost, and was gathered unto his people.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Gathering his sons, Jacob speaks over each in turn: blessing, warning and prophecy mixed, from Reuben's lost pre-eminence through Judah's sceptre and Joseph's fruitful bough to Benjamin's ravening wolf. He then charges them to bury him in Machpelah with Abraham, Sarah, Isaac and Rebekah, and dies. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Jacob calls his sons together to tell them what shall befall them in the last days, speaking not as a dying saint only but as an inspired prophet of the tribes to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He bids them gather and listen to Israel their father, a summons to unity, since they are to remain one people and not be separated as Abraham's and Isaac's sons were. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Reuben is addressed as firstborn, might, and beginning of strength, with the excellency of dignity and power that primogeniture should have carried. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Reuben is unstable as water, boiling up with passion in his incest, and shall not excel, so his tribe never produces a judge, prophet, or ruler and is first carried captive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Simeon and Levi are brethren in disposition as in blood, united by similar tempers, and their instruments of cruelty recall the slaughter at Shechem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Jacob refuses to join their secret council or assembly, dissociating his honour from their anger, in which they slew a man and hamstrung oxen in self-will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Their anger is cursed for its fierceness and cruelty, not their persons, and they shall be divided in Jacob and scattered in Israel, a sentence later turned to blessing for Levi as priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Judah is he whom his brothers shall praise, a play on his name, and his hand shall be on his enemies' neck, with his father's children bowing before him, marking his pre-eminence among the tribes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Judah is a lion's whelp, going up from the prey and crouching as a lion, so none dare rouse him, picturing a tribe growing from dependence to victorious strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The sceptre shall not depart from Judah nor a lawgiver from between his feet until Shiloh comes, and to him shall the gathering of the peoples be, a promise of enduring rule ending in the promised one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Binding his foal to the vine and washing his garments in wine pictures Shiloh's kingdom as rural peace and plenty, with vines so abundant that even a donkey is tied to the choicest vine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. His eyes dark with wine and teeth white with milk picture the richness of Judah's hill country, famed for vineyards and pastures, where wine and milk are plentiful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Zebulun shall dwell at the haven of the sea and be a haven of ships, with his border toward Zidon, a seafaring tribe mentioned before his older brother Issachar for his coming superiority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Issachar is a strong ass couching between two burdens, stout and strong-bodied, a tribe built for labour and endurance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Seeing rest is good and the land pleasant, Issachar bows his shoulder to bear and becomes a servant to tribute, preferring to buy peace on the fertile plain of Esdraelon rather than live in constant jeopardy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Dan shall judge his people as one of the tribes of Israel, put on exact equality with Leah's and Rachel's sons despite being a secondary wife's son, with a ruler of his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Dan shall be a serpent by the way, an adder biting the horse's heels so its rider falls backward, picturing a small border tribe striking with stealth and suddenness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Jacob breaks off to say he has waited for the Lord's salvation, a prayer for relief when he foresees the troubles and backslidings that will come on his posterity, especially Dan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Gad, a troop shall overcome him, but he shall overcome at the last, or, by the heel, a border tribe often raided yet fighting back, whose name itself sounds like a troop. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Out of Asher his bread shall be fat, and he shall yield royal dainties, since the rich soil of his settlement will supply plenty of food fit for kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Naphtali is a hind let loose, giving goodly words, picturing swiftness and freedom, with speech that is graceful and persuasive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Joseph is a fruitful bough by a well, whose branches run over the wall, an image of progressive growth and luxuriant fruitfulness for the two tribes sprung from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The archers have sorely grieved him, shot at him, and hated him, and JFB reads the archers as his brothers, Potiphar, and his wife, whose envy, revenge, and temptation were the arrows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. His bow abides in strength and his arms are made strong by the mighty God of Jacob, since his steady virtue, innocence, patience, and faith resisted every trial, and from God comes Israel's shepherd and stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The God of his father shall help him and the Almighty bless him with blessings of heaven above, rain and dew, of the deep beneath, springs and rivers, and of breast and womb, numerous progeny and cattle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. His father's blessings prevail above the blessings of his progenitors, even to the everlasting hills, and rest on Joseph, the one separate from his brothers, as greater in extent, definiteness, and nearness of fulfilment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Benjamin shall ravin as a wolf, devouring prey in the morning and dividing spoil at night, a border tribe marked by impetuosity, fierceness, and warlike appetite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. All these are the twelve tribes of Israel, and Jacob blesses each according to his blessing, so each tribe's portion and character become its heraldic motto under God's foreordaining love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Jacob charges them to bury him with his fathers in the cave in Ephron's field, declaring with his latest breath, before all his family, that he dies in the same faith as Abraham. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. He names the cave of Machpelah, bought by Abraham for a burial possession, grounding his burial wish in a purchase already made, not a new claim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. There Abraham and Sarah, Isaac and Rebekah, and Leah were buried, so Jacob's burial joins a family grave already full of the patriarchs and their wives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The field and cave were bought from the Hittites, a legal possession in Canaan, so Jacob's claim to a grave there rests on purchase, not conquest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. Having finished commanding his sons, Jacob gathers up his feet into the bed, yields up the ghost, and is gathered to his people, a phrase distinct from mere burial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Shiloh. In Jacob's blessing, the figure to whom the gathering of the peoples shall be, when the sceptre departs from Judah; Henry and Jamieson-Fausset-Brown read this as the promised one. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 49:10, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
