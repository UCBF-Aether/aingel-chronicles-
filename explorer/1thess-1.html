The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Thessalonians 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Paul, and Silvanus, and Timotheus, unto the church of the Thessalonians which is in God the Father and in the Lord Jesus Christ: Grace be unto you, and peace, from God our Father, and the Lord Jesus Christ.

2 We give thanks to God always for you all, making mention of you in our prayers;

3 Remembering without ceasing your work of faith, and labour of love, and patience of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ, in the sight of God and our Father;

4 Knowing, brethren beloved, your election of God.

5 For our gospel came not unto you in word only, but also in power, and in the Holy Ghost, and in much assurance; as ye know what manner of men we were among you for your sake.

6 And ye became followers of us, and of the Lord, having received the word in much affliction, with joy of the Holy Ghost.

7 So that ye were ensamples to all that believe in Macedonia and Achaia.

8 For from you sounded out the word of the Lord not only in Macedonia and Achaia, but also in every place your faith to God-ward is spread abroad; so that we need not to speak any thing.

9 For they themselves shew of us what manner of entering in we had unto you, and how ye turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God;

10 And to wait for his Son from heaven, whom he raised from the dead, even Jesus, which delivered us from the wrath to come.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul, with Silvanus and Timothy, thanks God constantly for the Thessalonians' work of faith, labour of love and endurance of hope. The gospel came to them not in word only but in power, they turned from idols to serve the living God, and their faith has sounded out everywhere, as they wait for God's Son from heaven, who delivers from the wrath to come. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy write to the church of the Thessalonians in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, with grace and peace from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. As all good comes from God, Henry notes, no good can be hoped for by sinners but from God in Christ, and the best good may be expected from God as our Father for Christ's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. We give thanks to God always for you all, making mention of you in our prayers. Thanksgiving for others is constant, not occasional, and prayer remembers the whole church by name before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Remembering without ceasing your work of faith, labour of love, and patience of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ, in the sight of God and our Father. Wherever there is true faith it will work, Henry says, affecting heart and life; faith works by love to God and neighbour, and well-grounded hope appears in patience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Knowing, brethren beloved, your election of God. Election is known, Henry observes, not by mere talk of the things of God, but by feeling their power in the heart, mortifying lusts, weaning from the world, and raising us to heavenly things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Our gospel came not to you in word only, but also in power, and in the Holy Ghost, and in much assurance, as you know what manner of men we were among you for your sake. Unless the Spirit comes with the word, Henry warns, it will be a dead letter; the Thessalonians entertained it by the Holy Ghost's power, fully convinced and willing to venture their souls on the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. You became followers of us and of the Lord, having received the word in much affliction, with joy of the Holy Ghost. When careless and immoral persons turn to believe and obey the Lord Jesus and live soberly and godly, Henry says, the matter speaks for itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. So you were ensamples to all who believe in Macedonia and Achaia. A young church in affliction becomes the pattern for two whole regions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. From you the word of the Lord sounded out not only in Macedonia and Achaia, but in every place your faith toward God is spread abroad, so that we need not speak anything. The converts' witness outruns the apostles' preaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. They themselves show what manner of entering in we had to you, and how you turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God. Turning from idols is the visible proof of the gospel's power, and strangers report it unasked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. And to wait for his Son from heaven, whom he raised from the dead, even Jesus, who delivered us from the wrath to come. Believers under the Old Testament waited for the Messiah's coming, Henry notes, and believers now wait for his second coming; God raised him, which assures all men he will come to judgment, bringing full and final deliverance from wrath to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
