The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover the word of the LORD came to me, saying,

2 Go and cry in the ears of Jerusalem, saying, Thus saith the LORD; I remember thee, the kindness of thy youth, the love of thine espousals, when thou wentest after me in the wilderness, in a land that was not sown.

3 Israel was holiness unto the LORD, and the firstfruits of his increase: all that devour him shall offend; evil shall come upon them, saith the LORD.

4 Hear ye the word of the LORD, O house of Jacob, and all the families of the house of Israel:

5 Thus saith the LORD, What iniquity have your fathers found in me, that they are gone far from me, and have walked after vanity, and are become vain?

6 Neither said they, Where is the LORD that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, that led us through the wilderness, through a land of deserts and of pits, through a land of drought, and of the shadow of death, through a land that no man passed through, and where no man dwelt?

7 And I brought you into a plentiful country, to eat the fruit thereof and the goodness thereof; but when ye entered, ye defiled my land, and made mine heritage an abomination.

8 The priests said not, Where is the LORD? and they that handle the law knew me not: the pastors also transgressed against me, and the prophets prophesied by Baal, and walked after things that do not profit.

9 Wherefore I will yet plead with you, saith the LORD, and with your children’s children will I plead.

10 For pass over the isles of Chittim, and see; and send unto Kedar, and consider diligently, and see if there be such a thing.

11 Hath a nation changed their gods, which are yet no gods? but my people have changed their glory for that which doth not profit.

12 Be astonished, O ye heavens, at this, and be horribly afraid, be ye very desolate, saith the LORD.

13 For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water.

14 Is Israel a servant? is he a homeborn slave? why is he spoiled?

15 The young lions roared upon him, and yelled, and they made his land waste: his cities are burned without inhabitant.

16 Also the children of Noph and Tahapanes have broken the crown of thy head.

17 Hast thou not procured this unto thyself, in that thou hast forsaken the LORD thy God, when he led thee by the way?

18 And now what hast thou to do in the way of Egypt, to drink the waters of Sihor? or what hast thou to do in the way of Assyria, to drink the waters of the river?

19 Thine own wickedness shall correct thee, and thy backslidings shall reprove thee: know therefore and see that it is an evil thing and bitter, that thou hast forsaken the LORD thy God, and that my fear is not in thee, saith the Lord GOD of hosts.

20 For of old time I have broken thy yoke, and burst thy bands; and thou saidst, I will not transgress; when upon every high hill and under every green tree thou wanderest, playing the harlot.

21 Yet I had planted thee a noble vine, wholly a right seed: how then art thou turned into the degenerate plant of a strange vine unto me?

22 For though thou wash thee with nitre, and take thee much soap, yet thine iniquity is marked before me, saith the Lord GOD.

23 How canst thou say, I am not polluted, I have not gone after Baalim? see thy way in the valley, know what thou hast done: thou art a swift dromedary traversing her ways;

24 A wild ass used to the wilderness, that snuffeth up the wind at her pleasure; in her occasion who can turn her away? all they that seek her will not weary themselves; in her month they shall find her.

25 Withhold thy foot from being unshod, and thy throat from thirst: but thou saidst, There is no hope: no; for I have loved strangers, and after them will I go.

26 As the thief is ashamed when he is found, so is the house of Israel ashamed; they, their kings, their princes, and their priests, and their prophets.

27 Saying to a stock, Thou art my father; and to a stone, Thou hast brought me forth: for they have turned their back unto me, and not their face: but in the time of their trouble they will say, Arise, and save us.

28 But where are thy gods that thou hast made thee? let them arise, if they can save thee in the time of thy trouble: for according to the number of thy cities are thy gods, O Judah.

29 Wherefore will ye plead with me? ye all have transgressed against me, saith the LORD.

30 In vain have I smitten your children; they received no correction: your own sword hath devoured your prophets, like a destroying lion.

31 O generation, see ye the word of the LORD. Have I been a wilderness unto Israel? a land of darkness? wherefore say my people, We are lords; we will come no more unto thee?

32 Can a maid forget her ornaments, or a bride her attire? yet my people have forgotten me days without number.

33 Why trimmest thou thy way to seek love? therefore hast thou also taught the wicked ones thy ways.

34 Also in thy skirts is found the blood of the souls of the poor innocents: I have not found it by secret search, but upon all these.

35 Yet thou sayest, Because I am innocent, surely his anger shall turn from me. Behold, I will plead with thee, because thou sayest, I have not sinned.

36 Why gaddest thou about so much to change thy way? thou also shalt be ashamed of Egypt, as thou wast ashamed of Assyria.

37 Yea, thou shalt go forth from him, and thine hands upon thine head: for the LORD hath rejected thy confidences, and thou shalt not prosper in them.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This is a long reproof of Jerusalem for unfaithfulness. God remembers the devotion of Israel's youth, when the people followed him through the wilderness, and contrasts it with their present course: they have forsaken him, described as the fountain of living waters, for broken cisterns that hold no water, and they have turned to idols and to alliances with Egypt and Assyria that cannot save them. The chapter ends with the shame of those failed confidences. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The word of the Lord comes to Jeremiah with a message to cry in Jerusalem's ears. Henry introduces the chapter as likely Jeremiah's first sermon after his ordination, a lively and pathetic reproof meant to show God's people their transgressions and bring them to repentance before ruin comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. God remembers the kindness of Israel's youth and the love of their espousals, when they followed him in a wilderness land not sown. Henry notes that those who begin well but do not persevere are justly upbraided with their hopeful beginnings, and God's spiritual Israel must own their obligations for safe conduct through this world's dangerous wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Israel was holiness to the Lord and the firstfruits of his increase, so that all who devoured him would offend and evil would come upon them. Henry's point is the aggravation: professions once so devoted now aggravate the crime of desertion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. All the house of Jacob and all the families of Israel are summoned to hear the word of the Lord. Henry frames the whole chapter as reproof and conviction, its charge high and its arguments close, so that the people might repent and prevent the coming ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. God asks what iniquity their fathers found in him, that they went far from him, walked after vanity, and became vain. Henry stresses there could be no excuse: those who desert religion commonly oppose it more than those who never knew it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. They never asked where the Lord was who brought them up out of Egypt and led them through deserts, pits, drought, and the shadow of death, a land no man passed through or dwelt in. Henry notes the obligation they forgot: safe conduct through a wilderness so dangerous to the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. God brought them into a plentiful country to eat its fruit and goodness, but when they entered they defiled his land and made his heritage an abomination. Henry's charge is ingratitude: kindness so great makes the defilement inexcusable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The priests did not ask where the Lord was, those who handled the law did not know him, the pastors transgressed, and the prophets prophesied by Baal and walked after things that do not profit. Henry's section shows the failure reaching every office: priest, teacher, pastor, and prophet alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. God will yet plead with them, and with their children's children he will plead. Henry notes that before God punishes sinners he pleads with them to bring them to repentance, urging what we should plead with ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They are sent to pass over to the isles of Chittim and to Kedar, to consider diligently whether any such thing has happened. Henry's point is the search for a precedent: God pleads by asking the nations to witness an unheard-of exchange. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Has a nation changed their gods, which are yet no gods? But God's people have changed their glory for what does not profit. Henry calls this unparalleled fickleness and folly: even idolatrous nations kept their gods, while Israel traded the true God for nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Heaven itself is called to be astonished, horribly afraid, and very desolate at this. Henry notes the horror is proportioned to the sin: be afraid to think of the wrath and curse that will be the portion of those who throw themselves out of God's grace and favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. God's people have committed two evils: forsaking him, the fountain of living waters, and hewing out broken cisterns that can hold no water. Henry compares grace in Christ to fountain water, cooling, refreshing, cleansing, and fruitful, living water that quickens the dead and issues in eternal life; the cisterns are the world and men's inventions, followed and depended on in vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Is Israel a servant, or a homeborn slave, that he should be spoiled? Henry answers no: they are the seed of Abraham, and the soul of man is likewise no slave by nature, but has sold its own liberty and enslaved itself to divers lusts and passions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The young lions roared upon him and made his land waste, his cities burned without inhabitant. Henry identifies the lions as the Assyrian princes who prevailed against Israel once it departed from the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The children of Noph and Tahapanes have broken the crown of his head. Henry notes the people of Egypt destroyed Israel's glory and strength, a calamity Israel brought on itself by departing from the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Hast thou not procured this unto thyself, in forsaking the Lord thy God when he led thee by the way? Henry's application is repentance: repent of the sin, so that correction may not become ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. What has Israel to do in the way of Egypt, drinking the waters of Sihor, or in the way of Assyria, drinking the waters of the river? Henry turns the question on the reader: what has a Christian to do in the ways of forbidden pleasure, vain mirth, covetousness, or ambition? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Their own wickedness shall correct them and their backslidings reprove them, until they know and see how evil and bitter it is to forsake the Lord and to have no fear of him. Henry urges the use of the correction: let it reprove, not ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. God broke their yoke and burst their bands long ago, and they said they would not transgress, yet upon every high hill and under every green tree they wander, playing the harlot. Henry notes that despite all their advantages, Israel became like the wild vine bearing poisonous fruit, breaking covenant and degenerating from good beginnings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. God planted a noble vine, wholly a right seed, yet it turned into the degenerate plant of a strange vine. Henry observes how men fall under unbridled desires and sinful lusts as much as brute beasts fall under instinct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Though they wash with nitre and take much soap, their iniquity is marked before God. Henry's charge is that their wickedness is too plain to be concealed and too bad to be excused: no washing covers what God has marked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. How can they say they are not polluted and have not gone after Baalim? Let them see their way in the valley and know what they have done, like a swift dromedary traversing her ways. Henry points to the evidence itself: the valley ways expose the denial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Like a wild ass used to the wilderness, snuffing up the wind at her pleasure, whom none can turn away in her occasion, Israel pursues idolatry indefatigably. Henry notes they persist wilfully and obstinately, irreclaimable in their idolatries, and weary themselves only in pursuits bringing distress and misery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. They are told to withhold their foot from being unshod and their throat from thirst, but they answer there is no hope, for they have loved strangers and after them they will go. Henry warns neither to despair of God's mercy, sufficient to pardon, nor of his grace, able to subdue corruptions however strong, yet Israel chooses hopeless pursuit instead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. As the thief is ashamed when found, so the house of Israel is ashamed, with their kings, princes, priests, and prophets. Henry notes they shamed themselves by idolatry, and would shortly be made ashamed of it when their idols proved unable to help them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. They say to a stock, 'Thou art my father,' and to a stone, 'Thou hast brought me forth,' having turned their back to God and not their face, yet in trouble they will say, 'Arise, and save us.' Henry calls this a downright disowning of their obligations to the Father who made them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Where are the gods they made for themselves? Let them arise, if they can save in the time of trouble, for according to the number of Judah's cities are her gods. Henry's point is the coming shame: idols multiplied by the city will fail by the city in the day of trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Why will they plead with God? They have all transgressed against him. Henry notes the nation, unwrought upon by God's judgments, sought instead to justify themselves, but the plea collapses against universal transgression. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. In vain God smote their children, for they received no correction, and their own sword devoured their prophets like a destroying lion. Henry observes they had not been convinced or reformed by the rebukes of providence they had been under. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. O generation, see the word of the Lord: has he been a wilderness to Israel, or a land of darkness, that his people say, 'We are lords, we will come no more unto thee'? Henry answers that the world is a wilderness and land of darkness only to those who make it their home and portion, while those who dwell in God have pleasant places; the presumption lies in calling freedom from God lordship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Can a maid forget her ornaments, or a bride her attire? Yet God's people have forgotten him days without number.   • Verse 34. In her skirts is found the blood of the souls of the poor innocents, not found by secret search but upon all these. Henry charges that with their idolatries they mixed the most unnatural murders, shedding innocent blood openly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Yet she says, 'Because I am innocent, surely his anger shall turn from me,' so God will plead with her because she says she has not sinned. Henry calls this the language of presumptuous sinners, who cannot find excuse for sin yet insist on innocence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Why does she gad about so much to change her way? She shall be ashamed of Egypt as she was ashamed of Assyria. Henry notes the Lord was displeased with their confidences and would not prosper them therein; they may shift from one sin to another, but none ever hardened himself against God and prospered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. She shall go forth from Egypt with her hands upon her head, for the Lord has rejected her confidences, and she shall not prosper in them. Henry closes that men employ all their ingenuity but cannot find happiness in the way of sin, or an excuse for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

Glosses below rest on the chapter's own study notes, paraphrased above; they explain words, not Scripture itself.

  • Espousals (v. 2). Betrothal love: the devotion of Israel's youth, when the people followed God through the wilderness as a bride follows her husband. Interpreted Study note for this chapter, as paraphrased in the verse-by-verse list above
  • Fountain of living waters / broken cisterns (v. 13). The chapter's own contrast: God as a spring that never fails, set against cisterns the people hewed for themselves that can hold no water. Interpreted Study note for this chapter, as paraphrased in the verse-by-verse list above
  • Baalim (v. 23). The plural of Baal — the local gods whose worship the chapter charges Jerusalem with pursuing. Interpreted Study note for this chapter, as paraphrased in the verse-by-verse list above
