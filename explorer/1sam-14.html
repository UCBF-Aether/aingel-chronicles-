The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now it came to pass upon a day, that Jonathan the son of Saul said unto the young man that bare his armour, Come, and let us go over to the Philistines’ garrison, that is on the other side. But he told not his father.

2 And Saul tarried in the uttermost part of Gibeah under a pomegranate tree which is in Migron: and the people that were with him were about six hundred men;

3 And Ahiah, the son of Ahitub, Ichabod’s brother, the son of Phinehas, the son of Eli, the LORD’s priest in Shiloh, wearing an ephod. And the people knew not that Jonathan was gone.

4 And between the passages, by which Jonathan sought to go over unto the Philistines’ garrison, there was a sharp rock on the one side, and a sharp rock on the other side: and the name of the one was Bozez, and the name of the other Seneh.

5 The forefront of the one was situate northward over against Michmash, and the other southward over against Gibeah.

6 And Jonathan said to the young man that bare his armour, Come, and let us go over unto the garrison of these uncircumcised: it may be that the LORD will work for us: for there is no restraint to the LORD to save by many or by few.

7 And his armourbearer said unto him, Do all that is in thine heart: turn thee; behold, I am with thee according to thy heart.

8 Then said Jonathan, Behold, we will pass over unto these men, and we will discover ourselves unto them.

9 If they say thus unto us, Tarry until we come to you; then we will stand still in our place, and will not go up unto them.

10 But if they say thus, Come up unto us; then we will go up: for the LORD hath delivered them into our hand: and this shall be a sign unto us.

11 And both of them discovered themselves unto the garrison of the Philistines: and the Philistines said, Behold, the Hebrews come forth out of the holes where they had hid themselves.

12 And the men of the garrison answered Jonathan and his armourbearer, and said, Come up to us, and we will shew you a thing. And Jonathan said unto his armourbearer, Come up after me: for the LORD hath delivered them into the hand of Israel.

13 And Jonathan climbed up upon his hands and upon his feet, and his armourbearer after him: and they fell before Jonathan; and his armourbearer slew after him.

14 And that first slaughter, which Jonathan and his armourbearer made, was about twenty men, within as it were an half acre of land, which a yoke of oxen might plow.

15 And there was trembling in the host, in the field, and among all the people: the garrison, and the spoilers, they also trembled, and the earth quaked: so it was a very great trembling.

16 And the watchmen of Saul in Gibeah of Benjamin looked; and, behold, the multitude melted away, and they went on beating down one another.

17 Then said Saul unto the people that were with him, Number now, and see who is gone from us. And when they had numbered, behold, Jonathan and his armourbearer were not there.

18 And Saul said unto Ahiah, Bring hither the ark of God. For the ark of God was at that time with the children of Israel.

19 And it came to pass, while Saul talked unto the priest, that the noise that was in the host of the Philistines went on and increased: and Saul said unto the priest, Withdraw thine hand.

20 And Saul and all the people that were with him assembled themselves, and they came to the battle: and, behold, every man’s sword was against his fellow, and there was a very great discomfiture.

21 Moreover the Hebrews that were with the Philistines before that time, which went up with them into the camp from the country round about, even they also turned to be with the Israelites that were with Saul and Jonathan.

22 Likewise all the men of Israel which had hid themselves in mount Ephraim, when they heard that the Philistines fled, even they also followed hard after them in the battle.

23 So the LORD saved Israel that day: and the battle passed over unto Bethaven.

24 And the men of Israel were distressed that day: for Saul had adjured the people, saying, Cursed be the man that eateth any food until evening, that I may be avenged on mine enemies. So none of the people tasted any food.

25 And all they of the land came to a wood; and there was honey upon the ground.

26 And when the people were come into the wood, behold, the honey dropped; but no man put his hand to his mouth: for the people feared the oath.

27 But Jonathan heard not when his father charged the people with the oath: wherefore he put forth the end of the rod that was in his hand, and dipped it in an honeycomb, and put his hand to his mouth; and his eyes were enlightened.

28 Then answered one of the people, and said, Thy father straitly charged the people with an oath, saying, Cursed be the man that eateth any food this day. And the people were faint.

29 Then said Jonathan, My father hath troubled the land: see, I pray you, how mine eyes have been enlightened, because I tasted a little of this honey.

30 How much more, if haply the people had eaten freely to day of the spoil of their enemies which they found? for had there not been now a much greater slaughter among the Philistines?

31 And they smote the Philistines that day from Michmash to Aijalon: and the people were very faint.

32 And the people flew upon the spoil, and took sheep, and oxen, and calves, and slew them on the ground: and the people did eat them with the blood.

33 Then they told Saul, saying, Behold, the people sin against the LORD, in that they eat with the blood. And he said, Ye have transgressed: roll a great stone unto me this day.

34 And Saul said, Disperse yourselves among the people, and say unto them, Bring me hither every man his ox, and every man his sheep, and slay them here, and eat; and sin not against the LORD in eating with the blood. And all the people brought every man his ox with him that night, and slew them there.

35 And Saul built an altar unto the LORD: the same was the first altar that he built unto the LORD.

36 And Saul said, Let us go down after the Philistines by night, and spoil them until the morning light, and let us not leave a man of them. And they said, Do whatsoever seemeth good unto thee. Then said the priest, Let us draw near hither unto God.

37 And Saul asked counsel of God, Shall I go down after the Philistines? wilt thou deliver them into the hand of Israel? But he answered him not that day.

38 And Saul said, Draw ye near hither, all the chief of the people: and know and see wherein this sin hath been this day.

39 For, as the LORD liveth, which saveth Israel, though it be in Jonathan my son, he shall surely die. But there was not a man among all the people that answered him.

40 Then said he unto all Israel, Be ye on one side, and I and Jonathan my son will be on the other side. And the people said unto Saul, Do what seemeth good unto thee.

41 Therefore Saul said unto the LORD God of Israel, Give a perfect lot. And Saul and Jonathan were taken: but the people escaped.

42 And Saul said, Cast lots between me and Jonathan my son. And Jonathan was taken.

43 Then Saul said to Jonathan, Tell me what thou hast done. And Jonathan told him, and said, I did but taste a little honey with the end of the rod that was in mine hand, and, lo, I must die.

44 And Saul answered, God do so and more also: for thou shalt surely die, Jonathan.

45 And the people said unto Saul, Shall Jonathan die, who hath wrought this great salvation in Israel? God forbid: as the LORD liveth, there shall not one hair of his head fall to the ground; for he hath wrought with God this day. So the people rescued Jonathan, that he died not.

46 Then Saul went up from following the Philistines: and the Philistines went to their own place.

47 So Saul took the kingdom over Israel, and fought against all his enemies on every side, against Moab, and against the children of Ammon, and against Edom, and against the kings of Zobah, and against the Philistines: and whithersoever he turned himself, he vexed them.

48 And he gathered an host, and smote the Amalekites, and delivered Israel out of the hands of them that spoiled them.

49 Now the sons of Saul were Jonathan, and Ishui, and Melchishua: and the names of his two daughters were these; the name of the firstborn Merab, and the name of the younger Michal:

50 And the name of Saul’s wife was Ahinoam, the daughter of Ahimaaz: and the name of the captain of his host was Abner, the son of Ner, Saul’s uncle.

51 And Kish was the father of Saul; and Ner the father of Abner was the son of Abiel.

52 And there was sore war against the Philistines all the days of Saul: and when Saul saw any strong man, or any valiant man, he took him unto him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jonathan, without telling his father, proposes to his armourbearer that they cross to the Philistine garrison between the sharp rocks Bozez and Seneh, trusting the sign he frames: if the garrison says, Come up unto us, the LORD has delivered them into Israel's hand. The sign is given, the two climb up on hands and feet and slay about twenty men, and a trembling of God falls on host, field and spoilers, so that the Philistines turn their swords on one another and the hidden Hebrews come out to pursue. Saul, who has tarried under a pomegranate tree at Migron, spoils the pursuit with a rash oath, cursed be the man that eats any food until evening; Jonathan, who heard not the oath, tastes honey and his eyes are enlightened, the faint people eat spoil with the blood, and when the lot points to Jonathan for God's silence, Saul would kill his own son, but the people rescue him, swearing not one hair of his head shall fall, since he has wrought with God that day. The chapter ends with a general account of Saul's wars on every side and his household. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

But he was in such haste to fight a fallen enemy, Henry notes, that he would not stay to end his devotions nor hear what answer God would give; he that believeth will not make such haste, nor reckon any business so urgent as not to allow time to take God with him. Saul's severe order was very unwise, Henry says: if it gained time, it lost strength for the pursuit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 27. But Jonathan heard not when his father charged the people with the oath, so he dipped the end of his rod in a honeycomb, put it to his mouth, and his eyes were enlightened. Innocent refreshment, taken in ignorance of the oath, revived the very man who had won the day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. One of the people told him his father had straitly charged the people with an oath, Cursed be the man that eateth any food this day, and the people were faint. The warning came too late for Jonathan, but it showed the army's condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Jonathan said, My father hath troubled the land; see how mine eyes have been enlightened because I tasted a little of this honey. Such is the nature of our bodies, Henry notes, that daily work cannot be done without daily bread, which our Father in heaven graciously gives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 31. They smote the Philistines that day from Michmash to Aijalon, and the people were very faint. A long pursuit on empty stomachs ended in exhaustion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. The people flew upon the spoil, took sheep, oxen, and calves, slew them on the ground, and ate them with the blood. Famished men broke the law in their haste to eat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. They told Saul the people sinned against the LORD in eating with the blood, and he said, Ye have transgressed; roll a great stone unto me this day. The king who had caused the faintness now condemned its consequence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. Saul told the people to bring every man his ox and sheep, slay them there, and eat, and sin not against the LORD in eating with the blood, and all the people brought their oxen that night and slew them there. The great stone gave them a lawful place to slaughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. Saul built an altar unto the LORD, the same being the first altar he built unto the LORD. Saul was turning aside from God, Henry observes, and now began to build altars, being most zealous for the form of godliness when denying its power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. Saul said, Let us go down after the Philistines by night and spoil them until morning, leaving not a man, and the people agreed, but the priest said, Let us draw near hither unto God. The priest's counsel checked the king's haste with a call to enquire of God first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. Saul asked counsel of God whether to go down after the Philistines and whether God would deliver them into Israel's hand, but God answered him not that day. If God turns away our prayer, Henry says, we have reason to suspect some sin harboured in our hearts, which we should find out and put away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. Saul said, Draw near, all chiefs of the people, and know and see wherein this sin hath been this day. The silence was treated as proof of hidden sin in the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. As the LORD liveth, which saveth Israel, though it be in Jonathan my son, he shall surely die, Saul swore, but no man among the people answered him. We should always first suspect and examine ourselves, Henry warns, but an unhumbled heart suspects every other person and looks everywhere but at home for the cause of calamity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. He said to all Israel, Be ye on one side, and I and Jonathan my son on the other, and the people said, Do what seemeth good unto thee. The lot was arranged to isolate the king's house from the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 41. Saul said to the LORD God of Israel, Give a perfect lot, and Saul and Jonathan were taken, but the people escaped. The lot pointed at the royal house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 42. Saul said, Cast lots between me and Jonathan my son, and Jonathan was taken. The lot narrowed from the nation to the household to the son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 43. Saul said to Jonathan, Tell me what thou hast done, and Jonathan told him, I did but taste a little honey with the end of my rod, and lo, I must die. Jonathan was discovered to be the offender, Henry notes, though his offence was unwitting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 44. Saul answered, God do so and more also, for thou shalt surely die, Jonathan. Those most indulgent to their own sins are most severe upon others, Henry observes, and those who most disregard God's authority are most impatient when their own commands are slighted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 45. The people protested that Jonathan, who had wrought this great salvation in Israel, should not die, swearing that not one hair of his head should fall since he had wrought with God that day, and so they rescued Jonathan. The people overruled the king's rash oath to save the day's hero. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 46. Then Saul went up from following the Philistines, and the Philistines went to their own place. The pursuit ended in an unfinished victory and a rescued son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 47. So Saul took the kingdom over Israel and fought against all his enemies on every side — Moab, Ammon, Edom, Zobah, and the Philistines — and whithersoever he turned himself, he vexed them. Here begins a general account of Saul's court and camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 48. He gathered a host and smote the Amalekites and delivered Israel out of the hands of them that spoiled them. War on every border marked his reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 49. The sons of Saul were Jonathan, Ishui, and Melchishua, and his daughters were Merab the firstborn and Michal the younger. The family list sets the persons who will fill the rest of the book. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 50. Saul's wife was Ahinoam daughter of Ahimaaz, and the captain of his host was Abner son of Ner, Saul's uncle. Abner, the king's kinsman and general, enters the story here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 51. Kish was Saul's father, and Ner, Abner's father, was the son of Abiel. The genealogy ties king and captain as cousins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 52. There was sore war against the Philistines all the days of Saul, and when Saul saw any strong or valiant man, he took him unto him. He had little reason to be proud of his royal dignity, Henry notes, nor had his neighbours cause to envy him, for he had little enjoyment after he took the kingdom; often men's earthly glory makes a blaze just before the dark night of disgrace and woe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

It is called a trembling of God, Henry notes, signifying not only a great trembling they could not resist, but that it came at once from the hand of God, who made the heart and knows how to make it tremble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Saul's watchmen in Gibeah looked, and behold, the multitude melted away, and they went on beating down one another. The Philistines were, by God's power, set against one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Saul told his people to number and see who was gone, and when they numbered, Jonathan and his armourbearer were not there. The missing two explained the whole rout. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Saul said to Ahiah, Bring hither the ark of God, for the ark was at that time with Israel. The more evident it was that God did all, Henry observes, the more reason Saul had to enquire whether God would give him leave to do anything. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. While Saul talked to the priest, the noise in the Philistine host went on and increased, and Saul said to the priest, Withdraw thine hand. But he was in such haste to fight a fallen enemy, Henry notes, that he would not stay to end his devotions nor hear what answer God would give; he that believeth will not make such haste, nor reckon any business so urgent as not to allow time to take God with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Saul and all his people assembled and came to the battle, and behold, every man's sword was against his fellow, and there was a very great discomfiture. The victory was already God's before Israel struck a blow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Moreover the Hebrews who were with the Philistines turned to be with the Israelites with Saul and Jonathan. The turncoats turned back when the tide turned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Likewise all the men of Israel who had hid in Mount Ephraim, when they heard the Philistines fled, followed hard after them in battle. The hidden came out of their holes to join the pursuit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. So the LORD saved Israel that day, and the battle passed over unto Bethaven. The day's salvation is credited to the LORD, not to Jonathan's daring or Saul's army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. The men of Israel were distressed that day, for Saul had adjured the people, saying, Cursed be the man that eateth any food until evening, that I may be avenged on mine enemies, so none tasted food. Saul's severe order was very unwise, Henry says: if it gained time, it lost strength for the pursuit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. All they of the land came to a wood, and there was honey upon the ground. The land itself offered the famished army food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. When the people came into the wood, behold, the honey dropped, but no man put his hand to his mouth, for the people feared the oath. Hunger yielded to fear of the curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. But Jonathan heard not when his father charged the people with the oath, so he dipped the end of his rod in a honeycomb, put it to his mouth, and his eyes were enlightened. Innocent refreshment, taken in ignorance of the oath, revived the very man who had won the day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. One of the people told him his father had straitly charged the people with an oath, Cursed be the man that eateth any food this day, and the people were faint. The warning came too late for Jonathan, but it showed the army's condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Jonathan said, My father hath troubled the land; see how mine eyes have been enlightened because I tasted a little of this honey. Such is the nature of our bodies, Henry notes, that daily work cannot be done without daily bread, which our Father in heaven graciously gives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. How much more, if the people had eaten freely today of the spoil of their enemies, would there not now have been a much greater slaughter among the Philistines? The rash oath had cost Israel a fuller victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. They smote the Philistines that day from Michmash to Aijalon, and the people were very faint. A long pursuit on empty stomachs ended in exhaustion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. The people flew upon the spoil, took sheep, oxen, and calves, slew them on the ground, and ate them with the blood. Famished men broke the law in their haste to eat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. They told Saul the people sinned against the LORD in eating with the blood, and he said, Ye have transgressed; roll a great stone unto me this day. The king who had caused the faintness now condemned its consequence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. Saul told the people to bring every man his ox and sheep, slay them there, and eat, and sin not against the LORD in eating with the blood, and all the people brought their oxen that night and slew them there. The great stone gave them a lawful place to slaughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. Saul built an altar unto the LORD, the same being the first altar he built unto the LORD. Saul was turning aside from God, Henry observes, and now began to build altars, being most zealous for the form of godliness when denying its power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. Saul said, Let us go down after the Philistines by night and spoil them until morning, leaving not a man, and the people agreed, but the priest said, Let us draw near hither unto God. The priest's counsel checked the king's haste with a call to enquire of God first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. Saul asked counsel of God whether to go down after the Philistines and whether God would deliver them into Israel's hand, but God answered him not that day. If God turns away our prayer, Henry says, we have reason to suspect some sin harboured in our hearts, which we should find out and put away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. Saul said, Draw near, all chiefs of the people, and know and see wherein this sin hath been this day. The silence was treated as proof of hidden sin in the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. As the LORD liveth, which saveth Israel, though it be in Jonathan my son, he shall surely die, Saul swore, but no man among the people answered him. We should always first suspect and examine ourselves, Henry warns, but an unhumbled heart suspects every other person and looks everywhere but at home for the cause of calamity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. He said to all Israel, Be ye on one side, and I and Jonathan my son on the other, and the people said, Do what seemeth good unto thee. The lot was arranged to isolate the king's house from the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 41. Saul said to the LORD God of Israel, Give a perfect lot, and Saul and Jonathan were taken, but the people escaped. The lot pointed at the royal house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 42. Saul said, Cast lots between me and Jonathan my son, and Jonathan was taken. The lot narrowed from the nation to the household to the son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 43. Saul said to Jonathan, Tell me what thou hast done, and Jonathan told him, I did but taste a little honey with the end of my rod, and lo, I must die. Jonathan was discovered to be the offender, Henry notes, though his offence was unwitting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 44. Saul answered, God do so and more also, for thou shalt surely die, Jonathan. Those most indulgent to their own sins are most severe upon others, Henry observes, and those who most disregard God's authority are most impatient when their own commands are slighted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 45. The people protested that Jonathan, who had wrought this great salvation in Israel, should not die, swearing that not one hair of his head should fall since he had wrought with God that day, and so they rescued Jonathan. The people overruled the king's rash oath to save the day's hero. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 46. Then Saul went up from following the Philistines, and the Philistines went to their own place. The pursuit ended in an unfinished victory and a rescued son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 47. So Saul took the kingdom over Israel and fought against all his enemies on every side — Moab, Ammon, Edom, Zobah, and the Philistines — and whithersoever he turned himself, he vexed them. Here begins a general account of Saul's court and camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 48. He gathered a host and smote the Amalekites and delivered Israel out of the hands of them that spoiled them. War on every border marked his reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 49. The sons of Saul were Jonathan, Ishui, and Melchishua, and his daughters were Merab the firstborn and Michal the younger. The family list sets the persons who will fill the rest of the book. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 50. Saul's wife was Ahinoam daughter of Ahimaaz, and the captain of his host was Abner son of Ner, Saul's uncle. Abner, the king's kinsman and general, enters the story here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 51. Kish was Saul's father, and Ner, Abner's father, was the son of Abiel. The genealogy ties king and captain as cousins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 52. There was sore war against the Philistines all the days of Saul, and when Saul saw any strong or valiant man, he took him unto him. He had little reason to be proud of his royal dignity, Henry notes, nor had his neighbours cause to envy him, for he had little enjoyment after he took the kingdom; often men's earthly glory makes a blaze just before the dark night of disgrace and woe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
