The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word that Isaiah the son of Amoz saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem.

2 And it shall come to pass in the last days, that the mountain of the LORD’s house shall be established in the top of the mountains, and shall be exalted above the hills; and all nations shall flow unto it.

3 And many people shall go and say, Come ye, and let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, to the house of the God of Jacob; and he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths: for out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.

4 And he shall judge among the nations, and shall rebuke many people: and they shall beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruninghooks: nation shall not lift up sword against nation, neither shall they learn war any more.

5 O house of Jacob, come ye, and let us walk in the light of the LORD.

6 Therefore thou hast forsaken thy people the house of Jacob, because they be replenished from the east, and are soothsayers like the Philistines, and they please themselves in the children of strangers.

7 Their land also is full of silver and gold, neither is there any end of their treasures; their land is also full of horses, neither is there any end of their chariots:

8 Their land also is full of idols; they worship the work of their own hands, that which their own fingers have made:

9 And the mean man boweth down, and the great man humbleth himself: therefore forgive them not.

10 Enter into the rock, and hide thee in the dust, for fear of the LORD, and for the glory of his majesty.

11 The lofty looks of man shall be humbled, and the haughtiness of men shall be bowed down, and the LORD alone shall be exalted in that day.

12 For the day of the LORD of hosts shall be upon every one that is proud and lofty, and upon every one that is lifted up; and he shall be brought low:

13 And upon all the cedars of Lebanon, that are high and lifted up, and upon all the oaks of Bashan,

14 And upon all the high mountains, and upon all the hills that are lifted up,

15 And upon every high tower, and upon every fenced wall,

16 And upon all the ships of Tarshish, and upon all pleasant pictures.

17 And the loftiness of man shall be bowed down, and the haughtiness of men shall be made low: and the LORD alone shall be exalted in that day.

18 And the idols he shall utterly abolish.

19 And they shall go into the holes of the rocks, and into the caves of the earth, for fear of the LORD, and for the glory of his majesty, when he ariseth to shake terribly the earth.

20 In that day a man shall cast his idols of silver, and his idols of gold, which they made each one for himself to worship, to the moles and to the bats;

21 To go into the clefts of the rocks, and into the tops of the ragged rocks, for fear of the LORD, and for the glory of his majesty, when he ariseth to shake terribly the earth.

22 Cease ye from man, whose breath is in his nostrils: for wherein is he to be accounted of?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter opens with a vision of the last days, when the nations stream up to the mountain of the Lord to learn his ways, and then turns to judgment on everything proud and idolatrous in Judah: silver, horses, and idols are exposed as worthless, and the chapter ends by telling the people to stop trusting in man, whose breath is in his nostrils. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. This is the inscription of a new section: the word, the revelation, that Isaiah saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem. JFB treats the whole chapter, verses 1–22, as one prophecy under this heading. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. In the last days, Messiah's days, the mountain of the Lord's house will be established on the highest hill, and all nations will flow to it as a broad stream. The temple on Mount Moriah is a type of the gospel beginning at Jerusalem, made so conspicuous that the nations are attracted to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Many peoples will come up to the mountain of the Lord to learn his ways, and the law will go forth from Jerusalem. JFB reads this as a literal promised blessing matching the literal fulfilment of the curse, with the Holy Spirit poured out for a general conversion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The Lord will judge as sovereign umpire, settling controversies between nations, and swords will be beaten into plowshares and spears into pruninghooks. Eastern plowshares resembled short swords, so the image is of weapons turned to farming tools when war is learned no more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. In view of Israel's high destiny to be a blessing to all nations, the house of Jacob is urged to walk in the light of the Lord. The exhortation follows from the glorious future just described: Israel should live worthy of it now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The Lord has forsaken his people because they are filled with the superstitions of the East, with soothsayers forbidden by the law, and with alliances with strangers. Joining hands with the Philistines and others, in marriage and in national league, was forbidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The land is full of silver and gold, horses and chariots, heaped together against the law's forbidding. God meant his people to be wholly dependent on him rather than on the ordinary means of warfare, and horses were also connected with idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The land is also full of idols, the work of men's hands, worshipped in private even when public idolatry was not sanctioned under Uzziah and Jotham. The turn from military trust to idolatry is natural, since horses themselves were tied to idol worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Both the mean man and the great man bow down to idols, so all ranks are idolaters together. The words "forgive them not" are a threat expressed as an imperative: Isaiah so identifies with God's will that he prays for what he knows God purposes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Sinners will be driven to hide in the rock and in the dust from the terror of the Lord and the glory of his majesty. This is a poetical way of saying that God's judgments will force them to seek a hiding-place from his wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The lofty looks of man will be humbled and his haughtiness bowed down, and the Lord alone will be exalted in that day. God will so vindicate his honour in the day of judgments that no one else shall be exalted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The day of the Lord will come at last upon every proud and lofty thing on which the nation prided itself, not merely upon proud persons. Man has had many days; this is God's day, beginning with judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The day of the Lord falls on the cedars of Lebanon and the oaks of Bashan, images for haughty nobles and princes. Bashan, east of Jordan, was famous for fine oaks, and the oaks may also recall the idolatry of chapter 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The day falls on all high mountains and hills, the high places where sacrifices were unlawfully offered, and also the strong fastnesses in which men trusted rather than in God. Both idolatrous worship and military confidence are struck together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Every high tower and fenced wall will be brought low in that day, the fortifications on which cities relied for safety. Towers were often built upon city walls, and "fenced" means strongly fortified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Judgment will fall on the ships of Tarshish, richly laden far-voyaging vessels, and on all pleasant pictures and luxurious ornaments. The judgment reaches all that ministers to man's luxury, and the Vulgate's wider reading covers everything beautiful to the sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The loftiness of man will be bowed down and the haughtiness of men made low, and the Lord alone will be exalted in that day. The verse repeats verse 11 for emphatic confirmation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The idols, literally "vain things" and "nothings," will utterly pass away. JFB notes this was fulfilled to the letter: before the Babylonian captivity the Jews were most prone to idolatry, and in no instance ever since. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Men will go into the holes of the rocks and caves of the earth, abounding in hilly Judea, to hide from the terror of the Lord when he arises to shake the earth. The fulfilment answers exactly to the threat of verse 10, as a figure for severe and universal judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. In that day men will cast their idols of silver and gold to the moles and to the bats, creatures of darkness and of tenantless ruins. Idols made for worship will be thrown away to unclean, underground dwellers when their worshippers flee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Therefore cease from man, whose breath is in his nostrils, for the high ones on whom the people trusted will themselves be brought low. Dependence belongs on the Lord, not on princes who must themselves fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
