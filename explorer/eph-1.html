The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ephesians 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, to the saints which are at Ephesus, and to the faithful in Christ Jesus:

2 Grace be to you, and peace, from God our Father, and from the Lord Jesus Christ.

3 Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ:

4 According as he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before him in love:

5 Having predestinated us unto the adoption of children by Jesus Christ to himself, according to the good pleasure of his will,

6 To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved.

7 In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace;

8 Wherein he hath abounded toward us in all wisdom and prudence;

9 Having made known unto us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure which he hath purposed in himself:

10 That in the dispensation of the fulness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven, and which are on earth; even in him:

11 In whom also we have obtained an inheritance, being predestinated according to the purpose of him who worketh all things after the counsel of his own will:

12 That we should be to the praise of his glory, who first trusted in Christ.

13 In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,

14 Which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, unto the praise of his glory.

15 Wherefore I also, after I heard of your faith in the Lord Jesus, and love unto all the saints,

16 Cease not to give thanks for you, making mention of you in my prayers;

17 That the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give unto you the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him:

18 The eyes of your understanding being enlightened; that ye may know what is the hope of his calling, and what the riches of the glory of his inheritance in the saints,

19 And what is the exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward who believe, according to the working of his mighty power,

20 Which he wrought in Christ, when he raised him from the dead, and set him at his own right hand in the heavenly places,

21 Far above all principality, and power, and might, and dominion, and every name that is named, not only in this world, but also in that which is to come:

22 And hath put all things under his feet, and gave him to be the head over all things to the church,

23 Which is his body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all.

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The letter opens in praise: believers were chosen, adopted, redeemed and given an inheritance in Christ, sealed with the Holy Spirit as a pledge. Paul prays their eyes will be enlightened to know the hope, riches and power at work in them, the same power that raised Christ above every power and made him head of the church, his body. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul presents himself as an apostle by God's will alone, writing to believers at Ephesus who are both set apart as saints and faithful in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Grace and peace come only from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, and Henry notes there is no peace without grace first received from them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. God is praised for blessing believers with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly realm, all of it given in Christ rather than earned by them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Believers were chosen in Christ before the world began, so that holiness and blamelessness before God, not mere happiness, is the end of their election. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. That choice reaches its goal in adoption: by Jesus Christ believers are made God's children, according to the good pleasure of his will alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The whole design aims at the praise of the glory of God's grace, by which believers are made accepted in the Beloved, not in themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. In Christ believers already possess redemption through his blood, which Henry explains as forgiveness of sins given according to the riches of God's grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. That grace has abounded toward believers with all wisdom and prudence, God's wise ordering of the whole plan of redemption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. God has now made known the mystery of his will, the once-hidden purpose of salvation through Christ, according to his own good pleasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. In the fullness of times God purposes to gather all things together in Christ, things in heaven and things on earth, under one head. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. In Christ believers have obtained an inheritance, predestined according to the purpose of him who works all things after the counsel of his own will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Jewish believers who first trusted in Christ exist to the praise of his glory, showing that God's promises came first to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Gentile believers too, hearing the word of truth and believing, were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise, marking them as God's own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The Spirit is the earnest, the first installment and pledge, of the inheritance until the final redemption of God's purchased possession, all to the praise of his glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Paul had heard of their faith in the Lord Jesus and their love toward all the saints, and that report moves him to thanksgiving rather than to boasting about them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He never stops giving thanks for them and mentioning them in his prayers, since even the best Christians need to be prayed for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. He prays that the Father of glory would give them the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him, since true knowledge of God is itself a gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. With enlightened understanding they would know the hope of their calling and the riches of the glory of God's inheritance in the saints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. They would also know the exceeding greatness of God's power toward believers, the same mighty power that works in every conversion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. That power was displayed when God raised Christ from the dead and seated him at his right hand in the heavenly places. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Christ sits far above every principality, power, might, dominion, and every name named, in this age and in the age to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. God has put all things under Christ's feet and given him to be head over all things to the church, so no rival power stands outside his rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The church is Christ's body, the fullness of him who fills all in all, supplied with every blessing from its head. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
