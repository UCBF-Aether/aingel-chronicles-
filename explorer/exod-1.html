The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now these are the names of the children of Israel, which came into Egypt; every man and his household came with Jacob.

2 Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah,

3 Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin,

4 Dan, and Naphtali, Gad, and Asher.

5 And all the souls that came out of the loins of Jacob were seventy souls: for Joseph was in Egypt already.

6 And Joseph died, and all his brethren, and all that generation.

7 And the children of Israel were fruitful, and increased abundantly, and multiplied, and waxed exceeding mighty; and the land was filled with them.

8 Now there arose up a new king over Egypt, which knew not Joseph.

9 And he said unto his people, Behold, the people of the children of Israel are more and mightier than we:

10 Come on, let us deal wisely with them; lest they multiply, and it come to pass, that, when there falleth out any war, they join also unto our enemies, and fight against us, and so get them up out of the land.

11 Therefore they did set over them taskmasters to afflict them with their burdens. And they built for Pharaoh treasure cities, Pithom and Raamses.

12 But the more they afflicted them, the more they multiplied and grew. And they were grieved because of the children of Israel.

13 And the Egyptians made the children of Israel to serve with rigour:

14 And they made their lives bitter with hard bondage, in morter, and in brick, and in all manner of service in the field: all their service, wherein they made them serve, was with rigour.

15 And the king of Egypt spake to the Hebrew midwives, of which the name of the one was Shiphrah, and the name of the other Puah:

16 And he said, When ye do the office of a midwife to the Hebrew women, and see them upon the stools; if it be a son, then ye shall kill him: but if it be a daughter, then she shall live.

17 But the midwives feared God, and did not as the king of Egypt commanded them, but saved the men children alive.

18 And the king of Egypt called for the midwives, and said unto them, Why have ye done this thing, and have saved the men children alive?

19 And the midwives said unto Pharaoh, Because the Hebrew women are not as the Egyptian women; for they are lively, and are delivered ere the midwives come in unto them.

20 Therefore God dealt well with the midwives: and the people multiplied, and waxed very mighty.

21 And it came to pass, because the midwives feared God, that he made them houses.

22 And Pharaoh charged all his people, saying, Every son that is born ye shall cast into the river, and every daughter ye shall save alive.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After Joseph's generation dies, Israel grows numerous in Egypt. A new king who knew not Joseph fears their numbers, sets taskmasters over them, and makes their lives bitter with brick and field labour. When oppression only makes them multiply, Pharaoh orders the Hebrew midwives to kill newborn sons, and then orders all his people to cast every Hebrew son into the river. The midwives fear God and save the children alive. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Exodus opens by naming those who went down into Egypt with Jacob, continuing the Genesis story into the forming of Israel as a church and a nation. JFB sends the reader back to the fuller list in Genesis 46. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. After Joseph's generation died, Israel grew fruitful and exceedingly mighty in Egypt. JFB adds the natural circumstance of Egypt's famed fruitfulness to the fulfilment of the promise made to Abraham. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. A new king arose who knew not Joseph, after a revolution some sixty years after Joseph's death united upper and lower Egypt. JFB notes the new dynasty would have known nothing of the Hebrews and regarded them, as foreigners and shepherds, with dislike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The new king told his people Israel had become more and mightier than they, fearing they would join Egypt's enemies. Henry observes that the offence of Israel was simply that he prospered, a sight hateful to a wicked man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Pharaoh proposed dealing wisely with Israel lest they multiply further and escape in war. Henry calls this human wisdom foolish and sinful, since counsel against the Lord only creates greater vexation to those who take it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Taskmasters were set over Israel to afflict them, and they built the treasure cities Pithom and Raamses. JFB describes serf labour driven in bands on public works, and places both fortified store-cities in Goshen near the invasion route from Asia. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The more Israel was afflicted, the more they multiplied and grew. Henry draws the church's comfort from it: Christianity spread most when persecuted, and the blood of the martyrs was the seed of the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The Egyptians made Israel serve with rigour, embittering their lives with mortar and brick. JFB notes crude sun-baked brick was universal in Egypt, and monuments still depict brickmakers watched by taskmasters with uplifted sticks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Every kind of field service was added to the brickwork, all of it imposed with rigour. Henry stresses the burden was meant not only for Pharaoh's profit but to afflict, so that their very lives became bitter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The king spoke to the Hebrew midwives Shiphrah and Puah, evidently the heads of their company. JFB suggests that by tampering with these two, Pharaoh designed to terrify the rest into secret compliance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The midwives were ordered to kill every Hebrew son at birth and spare the daughters. JFB records divided opinion on the method, whether the birth-stools were low seats or stone troughs by the riverside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The midwives feared God and saved the male children alive, disobeying a cruel tyrant at the risk of their lives. JFB adds that their faith was blended with weakness, shrinking from speaking the whole truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Pharaoh demanded why the midwives had saved the children alive. Their answer, that Hebrew women were lively and delivered before the midwives arrived, was the plea by which they covered their disobedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The midwives told Pharaoh Hebrew women were not like Egyptian women but vigorous, giving birth before help arrived. Henry reads the whole episode as the serpent's seed showing enmity against the Seed of the woman, forgetting all pity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. God dealt well with the midwives and the people multiplied and waxed very mighty. JFB explains the Hebrew idiom: to build a house means to have a numerous progeny, so God protected the midwives and Israel prospered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Because the midwives feared God, he made them houses, that is, gave them families of their own. Henry notes services done for God's Israel are often repaid in kind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Pharaoh then charged all his people to cast every Hebrew son into the river and save every daughter alive. Henry sees the same enemy who tried to destroy the church in its infancy still busy stifling godly reflection in the heart of man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • taskmasters. Overseers set over forced labourers to drive the work. Interpreted Study note on verse 11, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • treasure cities. Fortified store-cities built by serf labour; JFB places Pithom and Raamses in Goshen near the invasion route from Asia. Interpreted Study note on verse 11, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • the stools (verse 16). The exact sense is uncertain: JFB records divided opinion, whether low birth-seats or stone troughs by the riverside. Unresolved Study note on verse 16, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • he made them houses (verse 21). A Hebrew idiom: to build a house means to have a numerous family, so God gave the midwives families of their own. Interpreted Study note on verses 20-21, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown and Matthew Henry

Connections to other passages

  • Genesis 46. The opening list of those who came into Egypt with Jacob continues the fuller list in Genesis 46, to which JFB sends the reader. Interpreted Study note on verse 1, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
