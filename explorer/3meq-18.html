Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Addressed to those who believe not the resurrection, the chapter warns that they shall be requited according to their deeds, good or evil, and that their teaching misleads their neighbours, who err because their teachers knew not whence they came nor whither they go. Its ground is Adam: “we are all the fruit of Adam, and through Adam we die” — through the sin of the father, the judgment of death found all, flesh consumed in the grave and bones broken. The chapter then turns to a “where is…” lament over everything death takes — beauty, strength, honour for rulers, horses, gold and silver, goods of the field, the taste of sweet wine — but from verse 10 on the text grows heavily fragmentary, sentences breaking off amid untranslated words, so the lament’s particulars cannot be paraphrased with confidence beyond that sweep: all that seemed great is dust and ashes.
