The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Joel 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 For, behold, in those days, and in that time, when I shall bring again the captivity of Judah and Jerusalem,

2 I will also gather all nations, and will bring them down into the valley of Jehoshaphat, and will plead with them there for my people and for my heritage Israel, whom they have scattered among the nations, and parted my land.

3 And they have cast lots for my people; and have given a boy for an harlot, and sold a girl for wine, that they might drink.

4 Yea, and what have ye to do with me, O Tyre, and Zidon, and all the coasts of Palestine? will ye render me a recompence? and if ye recompense me, swiftly and speedily will I return your recompence upon your own head;

5 Because ye have taken my silver and my gold, and have carried into your temples my goodly pleasant things:

6 The children also of Judah and the children of Jerusalem have ye sold unto the Grecians, that ye might remove them far from their border.

7 Behold, I will raise them out of the place whither ye have sold them, and will return your recompence upon your own head:

8 And I will sell your sons and your daughters into the hand of the children of Judah, and they shall sell them to the Sabeans, to a people far off: for the LORD hath spoken it.

9 Proclaim ye this among the Gentiles; Prepare war, wake up the mighty men, let all the men of war draw near; let them come up:

10 Beat your plowshares into swords and your pruninghooks into spears: let the weak say, I am strong.

11 Assemble yourselves, and come, all ye heathen, and gather yourselves together round about: thither cause thy mighty ones to come down, O LORD.

12 Let the heathen be wakened, and come up to the valley of Jehoshaphat: for there will I sit to judge all the heathen round about.

13 Put ye in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe: come, get you down; for the press is full, the fats overflow; for their wickedness is great.

14 Multitudes, multitudes in the valley of decision: for the day of the LORD is near in the valley of decision.

15 The sun and the moon shall be darkened, and the stars shall withdraw their shining.

16 The LORD also shall roar out of Zion, and utter his voice from Jerusalem; and the heavens and the earth shall shake: but the LORD will be the hope of his people, and the strength of the children of Israel.

17 So shall ye know that I am the LORD your God dwelling in Zion, my holy mountain: then shall Jerusalem be holy, and there shall no strangers pass through her any more.

18 And it shall come to pass in that day, that the mountains shall drop down new wine, and the hills shall flow with milk, and all the rivers of Judah shall flow with waters, and a fountain shall come forth out of the house of the LORD, and shall water the valley of Shittim.

19 Egypt shall be a desolation, and Edom shall be a desolate wilderness, for the violence against the children of Judah, because they have shed innocent blood in their land.

20 But Judah shall dwell for ever, and Jerusalem from generation to generation.

21 For I will cleanse their blood that I have not cleansed: for the LORD dwelleth in Zion.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

When the captivity of Judah and Jerusalem is turned back, all nations will be gathered into the valley of Jehoshaphat and judged there for scattering God's people, casting lots for them, and selling children for wine. Tyre, Zidon, and the coasts of Palestine are called to account, the nations are summoned to beat plowshares into swords, and the harvest is reaped in the valley of decision, but the LORD will be the hope of his people, Jerusalem will be holy, and Judah will dwell for ever with the LORD dwelling in Zion. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the days when God brings again the captivity of Judah and Jerusalem, the deliverance promised at the end of chapter 2 is given its historical ground, and Henry notes this chapter is a comment on that promise, showing how the deliverance is wrought and perfected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. God will gather all nations into the valley of Jehoshaphat and plead with them for his people and heritage Israel, whom they scattered and whose land they parted, and Henry notes that God's people are his heritage, his peculiar portion and treasure, so he has a good action against those who trespass upon them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They cast lots for God's people, giving a boy for a harlot and selling a girl for wine, treating human lives as small change for their pleasures, and Henry notes that even little persecutors will be called to account according to the wickedness of their endeavours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Tyre, Zidon, and Philistia are asked what they have to do with God, and whether they will recompense him, with swift recompense returning on their own heads, since vengeance is his and he will repay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They took God's silver and gold and carried his goodly pleasant things into their temples, robbing his house to enrich their idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. They sold the children of Judah and Jerusalem to the Grecians to remove them far from their border, turning captivity into exile at the far edge of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. God will raise his people from the place where they were sold and return the recompense upon the sellers' own heads, reversing the exile deed for deed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Their sons and daughters will be sold to Judah, who will sell them to the Sabeans, a people far off, and Henry notes this was in part accomplished by the Maccabees' victories and looks forward to the last day, when the upright shall have dominion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The challenge is proclaimed among the Gentiles: prepare war, wake up the mighty men, let all men of war draw near, and Henry reads it ironically, as God bidding defiance to all opposition, since he who sits in heaven laughs at confederacies against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They are told to beat plowshares into swords and pruning-hooks into spears, and let the weak say, I am strong, so that the enemy's total mobilisation becomes the measure of his coming defeat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The heathen must assemble and gather round about, while the prophet prays, Cause thy mighty ones to come down, O Lord, setting God's army against the gathered nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The heathen must be wakened and come up to the valley of Jehoshaphat, where God will sit to judge them, and Henry notes Jehoshaphat signifies the judgment of the Lord, and that the challenge has now become a summons they cannot escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The sickle must be put in because the harvest is ripe, the press is full and the fats overflow because their wickedness is great, so that judgment is pictured as a harvest whose ripeness is sin itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Multitudes, multitudes stand in the valley of decision, for the day of the Lord is near in that valley, and the gathered nations become the crowd awaiting sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The sun and moon are darkened and the stars withdraw their shining, the heavens themselves dimming at the judgment of the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The Lord roars out of Zion and utters his voice from Jerusalem, so that heaven and earth shake, yet the Lord will be the hope of his people and the strength of Israel, and Henry notes that as blessings out of Zion are the sweetest blessings, so terrors out of Zion are the sorest terrors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Then they shall know that the Lord their God dwells in Zion, his holy mountain, and Jerusalem shall be holy with no strangers passing through her any more, and Henry calls this an appropriating and experimental knowledge, found in the worst of times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The mountains will drop down new wine, the hills flow with milk, all Judah's rivers run with waters, and a fountain from the house of the Lord will water the valley of Shittim, and Henry notes the fountain is Christ himself, whose sufferings, merit, and grace cleanse, refresh, and make fruitful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Egypt will be a desolation and Edom a desolate wilderness for their violence against Judah and innocent blood shed in their land, while the enemies' lands wither as God's house overflows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Judah shall dwell for ever and Jerusalem from generation to generation, the permanence of God's people set against the desolation of their enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. God will cleanse their blood that he had not cleansed, for the Lord dwells in Zion, and the book closes where it began, with the Lord's own dwelling as the ground of cleansing and of rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • The day of the LORD. the day of God's visitation and judgment, pictured in the prophets as darkness, trumpet alarm, and distress, and also as deliverance for those who call on his name. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Valley of Jehoshaphat. the valley where Joel 3 gathers all nations for judgment, called in the same chapter the valley of decision. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
