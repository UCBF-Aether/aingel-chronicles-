Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The book’s last chapter opens with a question — where are those who gathered others’ goods by violence? — and answers with images of vanishing: like the śaḥəw [?] of a cloud, smoke lifted by wind, shaken grass-fruit, wax melting before fire, so shall perish the glory of sinners. Words the chapter attributes to David — the wicked seen great like the cedar of Lebanon, then sought and his place not found — point the same way. Servants are warned that they and their wealth pass away: multiplied children mean many graves, multiplied houses mean something the text leaves untranslated (mäzəbär), and multiplied cattle become spoil to enemies, because they did not the Lord’s will and their produce is unblessed. The other side of the contrast is blessing for keepers of law, commandment, and statute: fruit of land and womb, cattle multiplied, deliverance from tribulation and disease, exaltation above the nations, and standing in judgment. The chapter then sets out provision for the Levite who keeps the tabernacle service — tithe and firstborn by the former law — and a city of refuge, as Moses commanded Joshua, where the unwitting slayer (an axe, stone, or tree falling by accident) is saved after inquiry, while the witting killer is judged without pity. Moses’ final commands close the book: keep the commandment, walk in His judgments, put far evil and death, follow the pattern of the tabernacle, and rest with the fathers who did good — for believers are the seed of Adam and Seth, made in God’s likeness and image, who shall inherit the land of the living, rejoice in the kingdom for ever, and offer the sacrifice of praise. Amen. Verse 16’s Levite passage is convoluted and heavily marked [?], and several terms in the closing verses are left untranslated.
