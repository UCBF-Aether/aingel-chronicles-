Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when David was a little past the top of the hill, behold, Ziba the servant of Mephibosheth met him, with a couple of asses saddled, and upon them two hundred loaves of bread, and an hundred bunches of raisins, and an hundred of summer fruits, and a bottle of wine.

2 And the king said unto Ziba, What meanest thou by these? And Ziba said, The asses be for the king’s household to ride on; and the bread and summer fruit for the young men to eat; and the wine, that such as be faint in the wilderness may drink.

3 And the king said, And where is thy master’s son? And Ziba said unto the king, Behold, he abideth at Jerusalem: for he said, To day shall the house of Israel restore me the kingdom of my father.

4 Then said the king to Ziba, Behold, thine are all that pertained unto Mephibosheth. And Ziba said, I humbly beseech thee that I may find grace in thy sight, my lord, O king.

5 And when king David came to Bahurim, behold, thence came out a man of the family of the house of Saul, whose name was Shimei, the son of Gera: he came forth, and cursed still as he came.

6 And he cast stones at David, and at all the servants of king David: and all the people and all the mighty men were on his right hand and on his left.

7 And thus said Shimei when he cursed, Come out, come out, thou bloody man, and thou man of Belial:

8 The LORD hath returned upon thee all the blood of the house of Saul, in whose stead thou hast reigned; and the LORD hath delivered the kingdom into the hand of Absalom thy son: and, behold, thou art taken in thy mischief, because thou art a bloody man.

9 Then said Abishai the son of Zeruiah unto the king, Why should this dead dog curse my lord the king? let me go over, I pray thee, and take off his head.

10 And the king said, What have I to do with you, ye sons of Zeruiah? so let him curse, because the LORD hath said unto him, Curse David. Who shall then say, Wherefore hast thou done so?

11 And David said to Abishai, and to all his servants, Behold, my son, which came forth of my bowels, seeketh my life: how much more now may this Benjamite do it? let him alone, and let him curse; for the LORD hath bidden him.

12 It may be that the LORD will look on mine affliction, and that the LORD will requite me good for his cursing this day.

13 And as David and his men went by the way, Shimei went along on the hill’s side over against him, and cursed as he went, and threw stones at him, and cast dust.

14 And the king, and all the people that were with him, came weary, and refreshed themselves there.

15 And Absalom, and all the people the men of Israel, came to Jerusalem, and Ahithophel with him.

16 And it came to pass, when Hushai the Archite, David’s friend, was come unto Absalom, that Hushai said unto Absalom, God save the king, God save the king.

17 And Absalom said to Hushai, Is this thy kindness to thy friend? why wentest thou not with thy friend?

18 And Hushai said unto Absalom, Nay; but whom the LORD, and this people, and all the men of Israel, choose, his will I be, and with him will I abide.

19 And again, whom should I serve? should I not serve in the presence of his son? as I have served in thy father’s presence, so will I be in thy presence.

20 Then said Absalom to Ahithophel, Give counsel among you what we shall do.

21 And Ahithophel said unto Absalom, Go in unto thy father’s concubines, which he hath left to keep the house; and all Israel shall hear that thou art abhorred of thy father: then shall the hands of all that are with thee be strong.

22 So they spread Absalom a tent upon the top of the house; and Absalom went in unto his father’s concubines in the sight of all Israel.

23 And the counsel of Ahithophel, which he counselled in those days, was as if a man had enquired at the oracle of God: so was all the counsel of Ahithophel both with David and with Absalom.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

On the flight from Jerusalem, Ziba meets David with supplies and a story against Mephibosheth, and Shimei curses and stones David, who refuses to retaliate. In Jerusalem Absalom takes counsel: Ahithophel's advice is taken, and Absalom publicly takes his father's concubines on the top of the house, the revolt now open and irreversible. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Just past the top of the hill, Ziba, Mephibosheth's servant, meets David with saddled asses loaded with bread, raisins, summer fruits, and wine. Provision arrives for a fugitive king at the moment he needs it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. David asks what Ziba means by these, and Ziba explains the asses are for the household to ride, the bread and fruit for the young men, and the wine for the faint in the wilderness. The gift is practical and well-judged, which is what makes it persuasive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. David asks where Mephibosheth is, and Ziba answers that he abides at Jerusalem, claiming the house of Israel will restore him his father's kingdom that day. Ziba belied Mephibosheth, Henry says plainly, and great men ought to be jealous of flatterers and careful to hear both sides. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. David at once gives Ziba all that pertained to Mephibosheth, and Ziba humbly begs grace in the king's sight. Flattery works instantly: David bears Shimei's curses better than Ziba's flatteries, Henry observes, for by flattery he was brought to pass a wrong judgment on another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. At Bahurim a man of Saul's family, Shimei son of Gera, comes forth cursing still as he comes. The king's flight is met by the open hatred of the house he displaced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Shimei casts stones at David and all his servants, while all the people and mighty men are on David's right and left. The assault is public and brazen, done before the whole army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Shimei cries, Come out, come out, thou bloody man, thou man of Belial. The charge is false in its particulars, and Henry notes innocence is no defence against malice and falsehood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Shimei claims the Lord returned on David all the blood of Saul's house, in whose stead he reigned, and delivered the kingdom to Absalom. David, who once and again spared Saul's life while Saul sought his, is charged with the very blood he refused to shed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Abishai asks why this dead dog should curse the king, and begs leave to take off his head. The soldier's instinct is to silence the reviler with the sword. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. David answers, What have I to do with you, ye sons of Zeruiah? Let him curse, because the Lord has said to him, Curse David; who shall say, Wherefore hast thou done so? David sees the hand of God in the abuse, Henry says, and comforts himself that God will bring good out of his affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. David tells Abishai and all his servants that his own son seeks his life, so how much more may this Benjamite, and bids them let him alone and curse, for the Lord has bidden him. A humble spirit, Henry observes, turns reproaches into reproofs and gets good from them instead of being provoked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. It may be the Lord will look on David's affliction and requite him good for his cursing this day. Patience under abuse rests on hope that God repays not only services but sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. As David's men go by, Shimei goes along the hillside over against him, cursing, throwing stones, and casting dust. The abuse continues mile after mile, and David continues to bear it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The king and all the people with him come weary and refresh themselves there. The halt after the long abuse is the first rest of the flight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Absalom and all Israel come to Jerusalem, with Ahithophel. The usurper enters the capital David left, with the counsellor who betrayed David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. When Hushai the Archite, David's friend, comes to Absalom, he cries, God save the king, God save the king. The loyal friend greets the usurper as king, beginning his double game. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Absalom asks Hushai whether this is his kindness to his friend, and why he did not go with his friend. Suspicion is natural, and Hushai must answer it convincingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Hushai answers that whom the Lord, this people, and all Israel choose, his will he be, and with him will he abide. The appeal to the nation's choice covers a loyalty that has not changed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Whom should he serve, Hushai asks, if not in the son's presence as he served in the father's? The argument is plausible enough to pass, and Absalom accepts it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Absalom asks Ahithophel to give counsel what they shall do. The wisest counsellors of the age, Henry notes, are now both in Absalom's camp, and he thinks himself sure of success with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Ahithophel counsels Absalom to go in to his father's concubines left to keep the house, so all Israel shall hear he is abhorred of his father and the hands of his followers be strong. The counsel is wicked, Henry says, and they that advise men to sin certainly advise them to their hurt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. A tent is spread on the housetop, and Absalom goes in to his father's concubines in the sight of all Israel. The private sin of the father is answered by a public sin of the son, fulfilling Nathan's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Ahithophel's counsel in those days was as if a man enquired at the oracle of God, so it was both with David and Absalom. Yet miserable counsellors they both were, Henry observes: Hushai would never counsel Absalom wisely, and Ahithophel counselled wickedly, betraying him as effectually as a designed traitor, for honesty is the best policy in the long run. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
