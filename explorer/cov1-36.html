The Text — 1 Book of the Covenant 36 (J. Cooper & A. J. Maclean, The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1902), public domain (archive.org cu31924029296170))

1 Let the deacon be such as this, so that he may appear with fear and modesty and reverence. With regard to fervour of spirit, let him have a perfect manner of life. Let him observe and look at those who come into the house of the sanctuary. Let him investigate who they are, so that he may know if they are lambs or wolves.

2 And when he asketh, let him bring in him that is worthy, lest, if a spy enter, the liberty of the Church be searched out, and his sin be on his head.

3 If any one come late to the [service of] praise, either when that of the dawn is being said or when the Offering is being offered, whoever he be, let him remain outside, and let not the deacon bring him in,—for it’ is a type of the day of judgment which is to come,—lest by the noise of the entrance there be distraction to those who are praying.

4 But when he cometh and findeth that the door is shut,’ let him not knock, because of what hath been said already.

5 But after the hymn of praise which is placed first is finished, let the faithful man or faithful woman enter. Let the deacon say, either, Over the offering: or, For! the hymn of praise, let us beseech that the Lord may write our supplication! in the book of life, and [that] God who [is] for ever may remember us in His holy habitations of light. For?

6 [this] brother who is late, let us beseech that the Lord may give him earnestness and labour, and turn away from him every bond of this world, and give him the will of affection and love and hope.

7 Similarly also for a sister or for a deaconess, for those who are’ late or remain outside, let him admonish that all the people may beseech for them. For thus when a deacon mentioneth and admonisheth about them, earnestness is strengthened and the bond of love ‘" is fulfilled, and the despiser and the slothful is disciplined.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The deacon is to be such that he may appear with fear, modesty and reverence, with a perfect manner of life and fervour of spirit. He is to observe and look at those who come into the house of the sanctuary and investigate who they are, so that he may know if they are lambs or wolves; when he asks, he is to bring in him that is worthy, lest if a spy enter, the liberty of the Church be searched out and his sin be on his head. If anyone comes late to the service of praise — either when that of the dawn is being said or when the Offering is being offered — whoever he be, he is to remain outside and the deacon is not to bring him in, for it is a type of the day of judgment to come, lest the noise of entrance distract those praying. But when he comes and finds the door shut, he is not to knock, because of what has been said already; after the hymn of praise placed first is finished, a faithful man or faithful woman may enter. The deacon is to say, either, Over the offering, or, For the hymn of praise, let us beseech that the Lord may write our supplication in the book of life and that God who is for ever may remember us in His holy habitations of light. For a brother who is late, the people are to beseech that the Lord may give him earnestness and labour, turn away from him every bond of this world, and give him the will of affection, love and hope; and similarly for a sister or a deaconess, for those late or remaining outside, the deacon is to admonish that all the people may beseech for them — for thus, when a deacon mentions and admonishes about them, earnestness is strengthened, the bond of love is fulfilled, and the despiser and the slothful are disciplined.
