The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Daniel 10 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–12; King James Version, Apocrypha for the additions)

1 In the third year of Cyrus king of Persia a thing was revealed unto Daniel, whose name was called Belteshazzar; and the thing was true, but the time appointed was long: and he understood the thing, and had understanding of the vision.

2 In those days I Daniel was mourning three full weeks.

3 I ate no pleasant bread, neither came flesh nor wine in my mouth, neither did I anoint myself at all, till three whole weeks were fulfilled.

4 And in the four and twentieth day of the first month, as I was by the side of the great river, which is Hiddekel;

5 Then I lifted up mine eyes, and looked, and behold a certain man clothed in linen, whose loins were girded with fine gold of Uphaz:

6 His body also was like the beryl, and his face as the appearance of lightning, and his eyes as lamps of fire, and his arms and his feet like in colour to polished brass, and the voice of his words like the voice of a multitude.

7 And I Daniel alone saw the vision: for the men that were with me saw not the vision; but a great quaking fell upon them, so that they fled to hide themselves.

8 Therefore I was left alone, and saw this great vision, and there remained no strength in me: for my comeliness was turned in me into corruption, and I retained no strength.

9 Yet heard I the voice of his words: and when I heard the voice of his words, then was I in a deep sleep on my face, and my face toward the ground.

10 And, behold, an hand touched me, which set me upon my knees and upon the palms of my hands.

11 And he said unto me, O Daniel, a man greatly beloved, understand the words that I speak unto thee, and stand upright: for unto thee am I now sent. And when he had spoken this word unto me, I stood trembling.

12 Then said he unto me, Fear not, Daniel: for from the first day that thou didst set thine heart to understand, and to chasten thyself before thy God, thy words were heard, and I am come for thy words.

13 But the prince of the kingdom of Persia withstood me one and twenty days: but, lo, Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help me; and I remained there with the kings of Persia.

14 Now I am come to make thee understand what shall befall thy people in the latter days: for yet the vision is for many days.

15 And when he had spoken such words unto me, I set my face toward the ground, and I became dumb.

16 And, behold, one like the similitude of the sons of men touched my lips: then I opened my mouth, and spake, and said unto him that stood before me, O my lord, by the vision my sorrows are turned upon me, and I have retained no strength.

17 For how can the servant of this my lord talk with this my lord? for as for me, straightway there remained no strength in me, neither is there breath left in me.

18 Then there came again and touched me one like the appearance of a man, and he strengthened me,

19 And said, O man greatly beloved, fear not: peace be unto thee, be strong, yea, be strong. And when he had spoken unto me, I was strengthened, and said, Let my lord speak; for thou hast strengthened me.

20 Then said he, Knowest thou wherefore I come unto thee? and now will I return to fight with the prince of Persia: and when I am gone forth, lo, the prince of Grecia shall come.

21 But I will shew thee that which is noted in the scripture of truth: and there is none that holdeth with me in these things, but Michael your prince.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the third year of Cyrus, after three weeks of mourning and fasting, Daniel by the river Hiddekel sees a man clothed in linen, his face like lightning and his voice like a multitude, and falls into a deep sleep until a hand touches and strengthens him. The messenger explains that he was delayed twenty-one days by the prince of Persia, and that Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help him. He has come to show Daniel what is noted in the scripture of truth. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the third year of Cyrus king of Persia a thing was revealed unto Daniel, whose name was called Belteshazzar; and the thing was true, but the time appointed was long, and he understood the thing, and had understanding of the vision. This chapter relates the beginning of Daniel's last vision, Henry says, which is continued to the end of the book, and the time would be long before all would be accomplished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. In those days I Daniel was mourning three full weeks. Daniel's mourning, Henry's account shows, preceded the glorious vision, as earnest seeking before revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. I ate no pleasant bread, neither came flesh nor wine in my mouth, neither did I anoint myself at all, till three whole weeks were fulfilled. His three weeks of abstinence, Henry implies, were the chastening of himself before God that the angel later names. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. And in the four and twentieth day of the first month, as I was by the side of the great river, which is Hiddekel. The vision came by the great river, Henry's account shows, after the weeks of mourning were fulfilled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Then I lifted up mine eyes, and looked, and behold a certain man clothed in linen, whose loins were girded with fine gold of Uphaz. Christ appeared to Daniel in a glorious form, Henry says, and it should engage us to think highly and honourably of him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. His body also was like the beryl, and his face as the appearance of lightning, and his eyes as lamps of fire, and his arms and his feet like in colour to polished brass, and the voice of his words like the voice of a multitude. Let us admire his condescension for us and our salvation, Henry says, in appearing thus to Daniel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. And I Daniel alone saw the vision, for the men that were with me saw not the vision, but a great quaking fell upon them, so that they fled to hide themselves. The companions felt the terror without seeing the vision, Henry's account shows, and fled to hide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Therefore I was left alone, and saw this great vision, and there remained no strength in me, for my comeliness was turned in me into corruption, and I retained no strength. The greatest and best of men cannot bear the full discoveries of the divine glory, Henry says, for no man can see it and live. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Yet heard I the voice of his words, and when I heard the voice of his words, then was I in a deep sleep on my face, and my face toward the ground. How dreadful soever Christ may appear to those under convictions of sin, Henry says, there is enough in his word to quiet their spirits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. And behold, a hand touched me, which set me upon my knees and upon the palms of my hands. Whenever we enter into communion with God, Henry says, it becomes us to have a due sense of the infinite distance between us and the holy God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. And he said unto me: O Daniel, a man greatly beloved, understand the words that I speak unto thee, and stand upright, for unto thee am I now sent. And when he had spoken this word unto me, I stood trembling. Nothing is more likely to revive the drooping spirits of the saints, Henry says, than to be assured of God's love to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Then said he unto me: Fear not, Daniel, for from the first day that thou didst set thine heart to understand, and to chasten thyself before thy God, thy words were heard, and I am come for thy words. From the very first day we begin to look toward God in a way of duty, Henry says, he is ready to meet us in the way of mercy; thus ready is God to hear prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. But the prince of the kingdom of Persia withstood me one and twenty days, but lo, Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help me, and I remained there with the kings of Persia. The angels are employed as God's ministering servants, Henry says, and though much was done against the Jews by the kings of Persia, God permitting it, much more mischief would have been done if God had not prevented it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Now I am come to make thee understand what shall befall thy people in the latter days, for yet the vision is for many days. He would now more fully show what were God's purposes, Henry says, of which the prophecies form an outline, and we are concerned to study what is written in these Scriptures of truth, for they belong to our everlasting peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. And when he had spoken such words unto me, I set my face toward the ground, and I became dumb. Daniel's dumbness, Henry's account shows, was the weight of the coming sorrows upon his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. And behold, one like the similitude of the sons of men touched my lips; then I opened my mouth, and spoke, and said unto him that stood before me: O my lord, by the vision my sorrows are turned upon me, and I have retained no strength. How shall we, that are dust and ashes, speak to the Lord of glory, Henry asks, yet the touch opened Daniel's mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. For how can the servant of this my lord talk with this my lord? For as for me, straightway there remained no strength in me, neither is there breath left in me. Daniel's confession of no strength, Henry implies, is the fitting posture before the Lord of glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Then there came again and touched me one like the appearance of a man, and he strengthened me. The repeated touch, Henry's account shows, strengthened Daniel anew for the revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. And said: O man greatly beloved, fear not; peace be unto thee, be strong, yea, be strong. And when he had spoken unto me, I was strengthened, and said: Let my lord speak, for thou hast strengthened me. To be assured of God's love, Henry says, revives drooping spirits, and Daniel, strengthened, was ready to hear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Then said he: Knowest thou wherefore I come unto thee? And now will I return to fight with the prince of Persia, and when I am gone forth, lo, the prince of Grecia shall come. When the angel had told the prophet of things to come, Henry notes, he was to return and oppose the decrees of the Persian kings against the Jews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. But I will show thee that which is noted in the scripture of truth, and there is none that holds with me in these things, but Michael your prince. While Satan and his angels and evil counsellors excite princes to mischief against the church, Henry says, we may rejoice that Christ our Prince, and all his mighty angels, act against our enemies; yet the whole counsel of God shall be established. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
