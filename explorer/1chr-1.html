The Text — 1 Chronicles 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Adam, Sheth, Enosh,

2 Kenan, Mahalaleel, Jered,

3 Henoch, Methuselah, Lamech,

4 Noah, Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

5 The sons of Japheth; Gomer, and Magog, and Madai, and Javan, and Tubal, and Meshech, and Tiras.

6 And the sons of Gomer; Ashchenaz, and Riphath, and Togarmah.

7 And the sons of Javan; Elishah, and Tarshish, Kittim, and Dodanim.

8 The sons of Ham; Cush, and Mizraim, Put, and Canaan.

9 And the sons of Cush; Seba, and Havilah, and Sabta, and Raamah, and Sabtecha. And the sons of Raamah; Sheba, and Dedan.

10 And Cush begat Nimrod: he began to be mighty upon the earth.

11 And Mizraim begat Ludim, and Anamim, and Lehabim, and Naphtuhim,

12 And Pathrusim, and Casluhim, (of whom came the Philistines,) and Caphthorim.

13 And Canaan begat Zidon his firstborn, and Heth,

14 The Jebusite also, and the Amorite, and the Girgashite,

15 And the Hivite, and the Arkite, and the Sinite,

16 And the Arvadite, and the Zemarite, and the Hamathite.

17 The sons of Shem; Elam, and Asshur, and Arphaxad, and Lud, and Aram, and Uz, and Hul, and Gether, and Meshech.

18 And Arphaxad begat Shelah, and Shelah begat Eber.

19 And unto Eber were born two sons: the name of the one was Peleg; because in his days the earth was divided: and his brother’s name was Joktan.

20 And Joktan begat Almodad, and Sheleph, and Hazarmaveth, and Jerah,

21 Hadoram also, and Uzal, and Diklah,

22 And Ebal, and Abimael, and Sheba,

23 And Ophir, and Havilah, and Jobab. All these were the sons of Joktan.

24 Shem, Arphaxad, Shelah,

25 Eber, Peleg, Reu,

26 Serug, Nahor, Terah,

27 Abram; the same is Abraham.

28 The sons of Abraham; Isaac, and Ishmael.

29 These are their generations: The firstborn of Ishmael, Nebaioth; then Kedar, and Adbeel, and Mibsam,

30 Mishma, and Dumah, Massa, Hadad, and Tema,

31 Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah. These are the sons of Ishmael.

32 Now the sons of Keturah, Abraham’s concubine: she bare Zimran, and Jokshan, and Medan, and Midian, and Ishbak, and Shuah. And the sons of Jokshan; Sheba, and Dedan.

33 And the sons of Midian; Ephah, and Epher, and Henoch, and Abida, and Eldaah. All these are the sons of Keturah.

34 And Abraham begat Isaac. The sons of Isaac; Esau and Israel.

35 The sons of Esau; Eliphaz, Reuel, and Jeush, and Jaalam, and Korah.

36 The sons of Eliphaz; Teman, and Omar, Zephi, and Gatam, Kenaz, and Timna, and Amalek.

37 The sons of Reuel; Nahath, Zerah, Shammah, and Mizzah.

38 And the sons of Seir; Lotan, and Shobal, and Zibeon, and Anah, and Dishon, and Ezar, and Dishan.

39 And the sons of Lotan; Hori, and Homam: and Timna was Lotan’s sister.

40 The sons of Shobal; Alian, and Manahath, and Ebal, Shephi, and Onam. and the sons of Zibeon; Aiah, and Anah.

41 The sons of Anah; Dishon. And the sons of Dishon; Amram, and Eshban, and Ithran, and Cheran.

42 The sons of Ezer; Bilhan, and Zavan, and Jakan. The sons of Dishan; Uz, and Aran.

43 Now these are the kings that reigned in the land of Edom before any king reigned over the children of Israel; Bela the son of Beor: and the name of his city was Dinhabah.

44 And when Bela was dead, Jobab the son of Zerah of Bozrah reigned in his stead.

45 And when Jobab was dead, Husham of the land of the Temanites reigned in his stead.

46 And when Husham was dead, Hadad the son of Bedad, which smote Midian in the field of Moab, reigned in his stead: and the name of his city was Avith.

47 And when Hadad was dead, Samlah of Masrekah reigned in his stead.

48 And when Samlah was dead, Shaul of Rehoboth by the river reigned in his stead.

49 And when Shaul was dead, Baalhanan the son of Achbor reigned in his stead.

50 And when Baalhanan was dead, Hadad reigned in his stead: and the name of his city was Pai; and his wife’s name was Mehetabel, the daughter of Matred, the daughter of Mezahab.

51 Hadad died also. And the dukes of Edom were; duke Timnah, duke Aliah, duke Jetheth,

52 Duke Aholibamah, duke Elah, duke Pinon,

53 Duke Kenaz, duke Teman, duke Mibzar,

54 Duke Magdiel, duke Iram. These are the dukes of Edom.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter is a genealogy, not a story: it traces the line from Adam through Noah's sons to Abraham, then through Isaac and Ishmael and Esau's Edomite kings, laying out the family lines from which Israel's own history will come. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The chapter opens with Adam and runs to Abraham, the sacred line of Christ in descending order, as Luke gives it ascending. Henry notes Adam is the common father of our flesh and Abraham the common father of the faithful, and JFB adds that only the member in the direct order of succession is named, with begat understood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Noah, Shem, Ham, and Japheth are enumerated because the three sons were founders of the new world after the flood. JFB adds that listing them also shows Noah's prophecy over his sons being verified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The sons of Japheth are strangers to the church, planted in the isles of the Gentiles, the western parts of the world. Henry notes the account is short because the Jews had hitherto had little dealing with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The sons of Ham moved southward toward Africa and the parts of Asia that way, and many of them became enemies of the church. Henry notes Mizraim, father of the Egyptians, and Canaan, father of the Canaanites, are names of great note in Israel's story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Cush begat Nimrod, who began to be mighty on the earth. Henry reads Nimrod as an oppressor, probably to the people of God in his time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Casluhim is the source of both the Philistines and the Caphtorim, who were brethren and at first dwelt together, so their names are used interchangeably. JFB places the Caphtorim at Azzah, or Gaza, the seat of the Philistines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. From the Jebusite onward the names are not individuals but peoples sprung from Canaan. JFB explains that as several peoples became extinct or were amalgamated, their national names are given instead of personal ancestors' names. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Uz, Hul, Gether, and Meshech, or Mash, were children of Aram and grandsons of Shem. Henry notes Shem's posterity peopled Asia and spread eastward, becoming Assyrians, Syrians, Chaldeans, Persians, and Arabians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Arphaxad begat Shelah, and Shelah begat Eber, with Cainan's name omitted here. JFB notes the omission against the fuller pedigree in Luke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Unto Eber were born Peleg, because in his days the earth was divided, and his brother Joktan. JFB refers the reader to the Genesis account of Peleg for the division of the earth in his days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Ebal in this list is Obal in Genesis, a small spelling difference of the kind Henry notes between this genealogy and Genesis, where some names are written with a different letter as pronunciation changed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Shem's line from Shem through Arphaxad, Shelah, Eber, Peleg, Reu, Serug, Nahor, and Terah to Abram is a list of ten inclusive of Abraham. JFB marks this as the sacred line closing the chapter's first movement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. The sons of Abraham are narrowed to Isaac and Ishmael, and Henry observes that all other nations are now shaken off from the genealogy: the Lord's portion is his people, whom he knows by name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Ishmael's twelve sons are the heads of his twelve tribes, named to show God's promise to Abraham that Ishmael should become a great nation with twelve princes. JFB traces Nebaioth to the Nabataeans and Kedar to an Arab tribe, as evidence of the names surviving among Arab clans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Dumah and Tema are traced by JFB to great Arab tribes, the Beni Teman, continuing the identification of Ishmael's twelve heads with Arab clans of later geography. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The sons of Keturah became founders of nomadic tribes in the north of Arabia and Syria, Midian giving the Midianites. JFB notes Shuah as the ancestor of Bildad, Job's friend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. The sons of Eliphaz are traced to Arab tribes, Teman giving the land of Teman near the head of the Red Sea and Amalek the Beni Malak. JFB treats these identifications as the historian's way of grounding Edom's families in known peoples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. The sons of Reuel are likewise traced to Arab tribes, Reuel to a powerful branch of the Aeneze and Shammah to the great Beni Shammar tribe. JFB adds that Jobab, one of Edom's kings, is considered to be Job, seated at Dinahab. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. These are the kings who reigned in Edom before any king reigned over Israel, beginning with Bela son of Beor of Dinhabah. Henry notes the whole Edomite account is taken from Genesis, with slight name differences from changed pronunciation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. When Bela died, Jobab son of Zerah of Bozrah reigned in his stead. JFB identifies this Jobab with Job, whose royal city Dinahab is placed in the northern desert toward Chaldea and the Euphrates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages

Investigation (editorial — a question, not a conclusion)

This section investigates a question; it does not conclude it. The study note for verse 18 observes that Cainan's name is omitted here, against the fuller pedigree in Luke.

Parallels. Both lines run Arphaxad to Shelah to Eber. Differences. Luke's pedigree includes Cainan between Arphaxad and Shelah; this chapter passes directly from Arphaxad to Shelah.

Why the name is absent here is not settled by the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
