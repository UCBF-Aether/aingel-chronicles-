The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 18 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And God said to him, " Abraham, Abraham " ; and he said, "Behold, (here) am I." 2.

2 And He said, " Take thy beloved son whom thou lovest, (even) Isaac, and go unto the high country and offer him on one of the mountains which I will point out unto thee." 3.

3 And he rose early in the morning and saddled his ass, and took his two young men with him, and Isaac his son, and clave the wood of the burnt-offering, and he went to the place on

4 the third day, and he saw the place afar off.

5 And he came to a well of water, and he said to his young men, " Abide ye here with the ass, and I and the lad shall go (yonder), and when we have worshipped we shall come again to you." 5.

6 And he took the wood of the burnt-offering and laid it on Isaac his son, and he took in his hand the fire and the knife, and they went both of them together to that place.

7 And Isaac said to his father, " Father " ; and he said, " Here am I, my son."

8 And he said unto him, " Behold the fire, and the knife, and the wood ; but where is the sheep for the burnt-offering, father?

9 And he said, " God will provide for himself a sheep for a burnt-offering, my son."

10 And he drew near to the place of the mount of God.

11 And he built an altar, and he placed the wood on the altar, and bound Isaac his son, and placed him on the wood which was upon the altar, and stretched forth his hand to take the knife to slay Isaac his son.

12 And I stood before him, and before the prince Mastema, and the Lord said, " Bid him not to lay his hand on the lad, nor to do anything to him, for I have shown that he feareth the Lord." 10.

13 And I called to him from heaven, and said unto him : "Abraham, Abraham"; and he was terrified and said : " Behold, (here) am I."

14 And I said unto him : " Lay not thy hand upon the lad, neither do thou anything to him ; for now I have shown that thou fearest the Lord, and hast not withheld thy son, thy first-born son, from me." 12.

15 And the prince Mastema was put to shame ; and Abraham lifted up his eyes and looked, and, behold, a single ram caught ... by his horns, and Abraham went and took the ram and offered it for a burnt-offering in the stead of his son.

16 And Abraham called

17 that place " The Lord hath seen," so that it is said " (in the mount) the Lord hath seen " : that is Mount Sion.

18 And the Lord called Abraham by his name a second time from heaven, as he caused us to appear to speak to him in the name of the Lord.

19 And He said : " By Myself have I sworn, saith

20 the Lord, Because thou hast done this thing, And hast not withheld thy son, thy beloved

21 son, from Me,

22 That in blessing I shall bless thee,

23 And in multiplying I shall multiply thy seed

24 As the stars of heaven,

25 And as the sand which is on the seashore.

26 And thy seed will inherit the cities of its enemies,

27 And in thy seed will all nations of the earth be

28 blessed ;

29 Because thou hast obeyed My voice,

30 And I have shown to all that thou art faithful

31 unto Me in all that I have said unto thee : Go in peace."

32 And Abraham went to his young men, and they arose and went together to Beersheba, and Abraham dwelt by the Well of the Oath.

33 And he celebrated this festival every year, seven days with joy, and he called it the festival of the Lord according to the seven days during which he went and returned in peace.

34 And accordingly hath it been ordained and written on the heavenly tables regarding Israel and its seed that they should observe this festival seven days with the joy of festival.

35 The Death and Burial of Sarah.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

In plain language: God said to him, 'Abraham, Abraham,' and he said, 'Behold, here am I.' Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. God said to him, 'Abraham, Abraham,' and he said, 'Behold, here am I.' JFB on Genesis 22:1 notes God 'did tempt Abraham,' not incite to sin, but try and prove, giving occasion for the development of faith, and 'Here am I' is readiness at a moment's warning for God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He said, 'Take thy beloved son whom thou lovest, even Isaac, and go unto the high country and offer him on one of the mountains which I will point out unto thee.' JFB on Genesis 22:2 notes every circumstance mentioned was calculated to give a deeper stab to the parental bosom: to lose his only son, and by an act of his own hand, yet he heard and obeyed without a murmur. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He rose early in the morning and saddled his ass, and took his two young men with him, and Isaac his son, and clave the wood of the burnt-offering, and went to the place. JFB on Genesis 22:3 notes there was no appearance of delay or reluctance: he made every preparation before setting out, the materials, the knife, and the servants to convey them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. This verse completes the journey: on the third day he saw the place afar off. JFB on Genesis 22:3 notes from Beer-sheba to Moriah was a journey of two days, the painful secret pent up in his bosom, and the distant place chosen for some important reason, generally thought one of the hills of Jerusalem, on which the Great Sacrifice was afterwards offered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He came to a well of water, and said to his young men, 'Abide ye here with the ass, and I and the lad shall go yonder, and when we have worshipped we shall come again to you.' The servants are left at the well, while father and lad go to worship, with return promised: 'we shall come again to you.' Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 6. He took the wood of the burnt-offering and laid it on Isaac his son, and took in his hand the fire and the knife, and they went both of them together to that place. JFB on Genesis 22:4 notes the father and son ascended the hill, the one bearing the knife and the other the wood for consuming the sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Isaac said to his father, 'Father,' and he said, 'Here am I, my son.' The father-son exchange opens Isaac's question about the victim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. He said unto him, 'Behold the fire, and the knife, and the wood; but where is the sheep for the burnt-offering, father?' JFB on Genesis 22:4 notes there was no victim, and the question was so naturally put by Isaac. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. He said, 'God will provide for himself a sheep for a burnt-offering, my son.' JFB on Genesis 22:4 notes the design of this extraordinary transaction was to show him, by action instead of words, the way in which all the families of the earth should be blessed, and that in his answer Abraham anticipated some substitution, or spoke in unbounded confidence that his son, though sacrificed, would in some miraculous way be restored. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. He drew near to the place of the mount of God. The ascent ends at the mount of God, the pointed-out place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. He built an altar, and placed the wood on the altar, and bound Isaac his son, and placed him on the wood upon the altar, and stretched forth his hand to take the knife to slay Isaac his son. JFB on Genesis 22:9 notes had not the patriarch been sustained by the full consciousness of acting in obedience to God's will, the effort would have been too great for human endurance, and had not Isaac, then upwards of twenty years of age, displayed equal faith in submitting, this great trial could not have gone through. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And I stood before him, and before the prince Mastema, and the Lord said, 'Bid him not to lay his hand on the lad, nor to do anything to him, for I have shown that he feareth the Lord.' The angel narrator stands before Abraham and before Mastema alike, and the Lord's word halts the hand: fear of the Lord is shown, with Mastema as witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. And I called to him from heaven, and said unto him: 'Abraham, Abraham'; and he was terrified and said: 'Behold, here am I.' The heavenly call terrifies, and Abraham answers with the same readiness as at the first call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. And I said unto him: 'Lay not thy hand upon the lad, neither do thou anything to him; for now I have shown that thou fearest the Lord, and hast not withheld thy son, thy first-born son, from me.' JFB on Genesis 22:11-12 notes the sacrifice was virtually offered, the intention and purpose shown in all sincerity and fulness, and the Omniscient witness declared his acceptance in the highest terms of approval, the apostle speaking of it as actually made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The prince Mastema was put to shame, and Abraham lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, a single ram caught by his horns, and Abraham went and took the ram and offered it for a burnt-offering in the stead of his son. JFB on Genesis 22:13-19 notes no method was more admirably calculated to give the patriarch a distinct idea of the purpose of grace than this scenic representation, hence the Lord's allusion to it; here the ram's substitution shames the accuser who proposed the test. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. This verse opens the naming: Abraham called that place 'The Lord hath seen.' The name is completed by the saying of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. So that it is said '(in the mount) the Lord hath seen': that is Mount Sion. The saying fixes the place-name, and Jubilees identifies it as Mount Sion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. The Lord called Abraham by his name a second time from heaven, as he caused us to appear to speak to him in the name of the Lord. The second heavenly call comes through the angels speaking in the Lord's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. This verse opens the oath: 'By Myself have I sworn, saith the Lord.' The oath is sworn by God's own self, the highest form of swearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. The reason of the oath: 'Because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thy beloved.' The deed and the non-withholding of the beloved son ground everything that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. This verse completes the phrase: 'son, from me.' The beloved son was not withheld from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. 'That in blessing I shall bless thee.' The first promise of the oath is blessing upon Abraham himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. 'And in multiplying I shall multiply thy seed.' The second promise is multiplication of the seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. 'As the stars of heaven.' The stars give the measure of the multiplied seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. 'And as the sand which is on the seashore.' The seashore sand gives the second measure of the multiplied seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. 'And thy seed will inherit the cities of its enemies.' The seed's inheritance extends to its enemies' cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. 'And in thy seed will all nations of the earth be blessed.' The promise opens outward: in Abraham's seed all nations of the earth are blessed, completed by the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. This verse completes the blessing of the nations: 'blessed.' All nations of the earth are blessed in Abraham's seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. 'Because thou hast obeyed my voice.' Obedience to God's voice is the stated ground of the oath's promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. 'And I have shown to all that thou art faithful.' Faithfulness is shown to all, not to God alone: the test's verdict is public. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. This verse completes the verdict and dismisses him: 'unto me in all that I have said unto thee: Go in peace.' Faithful in all God said, Abraham goes in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. Abraham went to his young men, and they arose and went together to Beersheba, and Abraham dwelt by the Well of the Oath. The return is to Beersheba, dwelling by the Well of the Oath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. He celebrated this festival every year, seven days with joy, and called it the festival of the Lord according to the seven days during which he went and returned in peace. The annual seven-day festival of the Lord marks the going and the returning in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. Accordingly it has been ordained and written on the heavenly tables regarding Israel and its seed that they should observe this festival seven days with the joy of festival. The tables ordain Israel's seven-day festival observance with festival joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the death and burial of Sarah. It divides the Akedah festival from Sarah's death in chapter 19. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
