The Text — Psalms 35 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Plead my cause, O LORD, with them that strive with me: fight against them that fight against me.

2 Take hold of shield and buckler, and stand up for mine help.

3 Draw out also the spear, and stop the way against them that persecute me: say unto my soul, I am thy salvation.

4 Let them be confounded and put to shame that seek after my soul: let them be turned back and brought to confusion that devise my hurt.

5 Let them be as chaff before the wind: and let the angel of the LORD chase them.

6 Let their way be dark and slippery: and let the angel of the LORD persecute them.

7 For without cause have they hid for me their net in a pit, which without cause they have digged for my soul.

8 Let destruction come upon him at unawares; and let his net that he hath hid catch himself: into that very destruction let him fall.

9 And my soul shall be joyful in the LORD: it shall rejoice in his salvation.

10 All my bones shall say, LORD, who is like unto thee, which deliverest the poor from him that is too strong for him, yea, the poor and the needy from him that spoileth him?

11 False witnesses did rise up; they laid to my charge things that I knew not.

12 They rewarded me evil for good to the spoiling of my soul.

13 But as for me, when they were sick, my clothing was sackcloth: I humbled my soul with fasting; and my prayer returned into mine own bosom.

14 I behaved myself as though he had been my friend or brother: I bowed down heavily, as one that mourneth for his mother.

15 But in mine adversity they rejoiced, and gathered themselves together: yea, the abjects gathered themselves together against me, and I knew it not; they did tear me, and ceased not:

16 With hypocritical mockers in feasts, they gnashed upon me with their teeth.

17 Lord, how long wilt thou look on? rescue my soul from their destructions, my darling from the lions.

18 I will give thee thanks in the great congregation: I will praise thee among much people.

19 Let not them that are mine enemies wrongfully rejoice over me: neither let them wink with the eye that hate me without a cause.

20 For they speak not peace: but they devise deceitful matters against them that are quiet in the land.

21 Yea, they opened their mouth wide against me, and said, Aha, aha, our eye hath seen it.

22 This thou hast seen, O LORD: keep not silence: O Lord, be not far from me.

23 Stir up thyself, and awake to my judgment, even unto my cause, my God and my Lord.

24 Judge me, O LORD my God, according to thy righteousness; and let them not rejoice over me.

25 Let them not say in their hearts, Ah, so would we have it: let them not say, We have swallowed him up.

26 Let them be ashamed and brought to confusion together that rejoice at mine hurt: let them be clothed with shame and dishonour that magnify themselves against me.

27 Let them shout for joy, and be glad, that favour my righteous cause: yea, let them say continually, Let the LORD be magnified, which hath pleasure in the prosperity of his servant.

28 And my tongue shall speak of thy righteousness and of thy praise all the day long.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God as advocate and champion: arming for defence, the speaker pleads causeless nets and pits, false witnesses, and evil repaid for good after he had fasted and mourned for his enemies. He prays their own traps catch them and their road be dark and slippery, and ends promising praise in the great congregation all day long. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Plead my cause, O Lord, with those who strive with me; fight against those who fight against me: God is asked to act as advocate and champion in a lawsuit become warfare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Take hold of shield and buckler, and stand up for my help: God is pictured arming himself for his servant's defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Draw out the spear, and stop the way against my persecutors; say to my soul, I am your salvation: the spear blocks the pursuers' road, and God's own word to the soul is salvation itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Let them be confounded and put to shame who seek after my soul; let them be turned back and brought to confusion who devise my hurt: shame and confusion are prayed on the soul-seekers, reversing their intent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Let them be as chaff before the wind, and let the angel of the Lord chase them: chaff driven by wind, with God's angel driving, pictures helpless, pursued wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Let their way be dark and slippery, and let the angel of the Lord persecute them: darkness and slipperiness make the pursuers' own road their ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For without cause they hid for me their net in a pit, which without cause they dug for my soul: causelessness — twice stressed — is the injustice pleaded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Let destruction come upon him at unawares, and let his own hidden net catch himself; into that very destruction let him fall: the trapper caught in his own trap is the psalm's justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And my soul shall be joyful in the Lord, and rejoice in his salvation: joy in the Lord, not in revenge, is the stated fruit of deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. All my bones shall say, Lord, who is like you, who delivers the poor from him too strong for him, the poor and needy from him who spoils him? The whole body joins the praise, for deliverance of the weak from the strong has no parallel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. False witnesses rose up; they laid to my charge things I knew not: the charge is falsehood itself — accusations about things unknown to the accused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They rewarded me evil for good, to the spoiling of my soul: good repaid with evil, to the soul's spoiling, is the deepest ingratitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But as for me, when they were sick, my clothing was sackcloth; I humbled my soul with fasting, and my prayer returned into my own bosom: he had mourned and fasted for these very enemies in their sickness, and his prayer, unanswered by them, returned to bless himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. I behaved as though he had been my friend or brother; I bowed down heavily, as one mourning for his mother: friendship, brotherhood, and a son's mourning for his mother measure his past sympathy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. But in my adversity they rejoiced and gathered together; the abjects gathered against me, and I knew it not; they tore me and ceased not: his fall became their feast, and even base men joined the tearing without ceasing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. With hypocritical mockers in feasts, they gnashed on me with their teeth: feast mockery and gnashing teeth picture contempt turned savage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Lord, how long will you look on? Rescue my soul from their destructions, my darling from the lions: the 'how long' of a watcher God, and the precious life threatened by lions, plead for rescue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. I will give you thanks in the great congregation, and praise you among much people: deliverance will be thanked publicly, not privately alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Let not my enemies wrongfully rejoice over me, nor let those who hate me without cause wink with the eye: wrongful joy and the secret wink of conspiracy are both prayed against. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. For they speak not peace, but devise deceitful matters against those quiet in the land: their speech is not peace but plots against the quiet — the peaceable are their target. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Yea, they opened their mouth wide against me, and said, Aha, aha, our eye has seen it: the wide mouth and the exultant 'Aha' claim a witnessed triumph. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. This you have seen, O Lord; keep not silence; O Lord, be not far from me: against their claimed seeing stands God's true seeing — and his silence must end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Stir yourself, and awake to my judgment, even to my cause, my God and my Lord: God is called to wake, as it were, to the judgment of his servant's cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Judge me, O Lord my God, according to your righteousness, and let them not rejoice over me: judgment by God's righteousness, not by the enemies' slander, is the plea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Let them not say in their hearts, Ah, so would we have it; let them not say, We have swallowed him up: the enemies' inward boast and their claim to have swallowed him are both forbidden by the prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Let them be ashamed and brought to confusion together who rejoice at my hurt; let them be clothed with shame and dishonour who magnify themselves against me: joint shame and clothing in dishonour answer joint rejoicing and self-magnifying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Let them shout for joy and be glad who favour my righteous cause; let them say continually, Let the Lord be magnified, who has pleasure in the prosperity of his servant: the friends' joy is to magnify God, whose pleasure is his servant's prosperity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. And my tongue shall speak of your righteousness and of your praise all the day long: the psalm ends where praise must — in a tongue speaking God's righteousness and praise all day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
