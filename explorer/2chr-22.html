The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the inhabitants of Jerusalem made Ahaziah his youngest son king in his stead: for the band of men that came with the Arabians to the camp had slain all the eldest. So Ahaziah the son of Jehoram king of Judah reigned.

2 Forty and two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother’s name also was Athaliah the daughter of Omri.

3 He also walked in the ways of the house of Ahab: for his mother was his counsellor to do wickedly.

4 Wherefore he did evil in the sight of the LORD like the house of Ahab: for they were his counsellors after the death of his father to his destruction.

5 He walked also after their counsel, and went with Jehoram the son of Ahab king of Israel to war against Hazael king of Syria at Ramothgilead: and the Syrians smote Joram.

6 And he returned to be healed in Jezreel because of the wounds which were given him at Ramah, when he fought with Hazael king of Syria. And Azariah the son of Jehoram king of Judah went down to see Jehoram the son of Ahab at Jezreel, because he was sick.

7 And the destruction of Ahaziah was of God by coming to Joram: for when he was come, he went out with Jehoram against Jehu the son of Nimshi, whom the LORD had anointed to cut off the house of Ahab.

8 And it came to pass, that, when Jehu was executing judgment upon the house of Ahab, and found the princes of Judah, and the sons of the brethren of Ahaziah, that ministered to Ahaziah, he slew them.

9 And he sought Ahaziah: and they caught him, (for he was hid in Samaria,) and brought him to Jehu: and when they had slain him, they buried him: Because, said they, he is the son of Jehoshaphat, who sought the LORD with all his heart. So the house of Ahaziah had no power to keep still the kingdom.

10 But when Athaliah the mother of Ahaziah saw that her son was dead, she arose and destroyed all the seed royal of the house of Judah.

11 But Jehoshabeath, the daughter of the king, took Joash the son of Ahaziah, and stole him from among the king’s sons that were slain, and put him and his nurse in a bedchamber. So Jehoshabeath, the daughter of king Jehoram, the wife of Jehoiada the priest, (for she was the sister of Ahaziah,) hid him from Athaliah, so that she slew him not.

12 And he was with them hid in the house of God six years: and Athaliah reigned over the land.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Ahaziah's brief reign under his mother Athaliah's counsel, his death at Jehu's hands, Athaliah's seizure of the throne, and the infant Joash hidden in the house of God for six years. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And the inhabitants of Jerusalem made Ahaziah his youngest son king in his stead, for the band with the Arabians had slain all the eldest. So Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah reigned. Henry notes one son and one wife of Jehoram were left, Ahaziah and Athaliah, both reserved to be the shame and plague of his family, Ahaziah as partaker in Ahab's sin and destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Forty and two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother's name also was Athaliah daughter of Omri. Henry notes the forty-two cannot be his age, since his father died at forty, and Kings says twenty-two; some take it as his mother Athaliah's age, put for his in reproach because she managed him and in effect reigned, while many expositors allow a transcriber's mistake of forty-two for twenty-two. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He also walked in the ways of the house of Ahab, for his mother was his counsellor to do wickedly. Henry notes his reign, summed up in two clauses, was that his mother and her relations counselled him to do wickedly, and it was to his destruction, for he walked in Ahab's way and worshipped Baalim and Ashtaroth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Wherefore he did evil in the sight of the Lord like the house of Ahab, for they were his counsellors after his father's death to his destruction. Henry notes after his father's death Ahab's house was industrious to keep him to idolatry, lest his father's miserable end deter him, making him seven times more a child of hell, and he gave himself up to be led by them, forsaking divine guidance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He walked also after their counsel, and went with Jehoram son of Ahab king of Israel to war against Hazael king of Syria at Ramothgilead, and the Syrians smote Joram. Henry notes those who counsel wickedly counsel to destruction: they exposed him to Syria's sword, drawing him into an expedition to Ramoth-Gilead where Joram was wounded, not for his honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And he returned to be healed in Jezreel because of the wounds given him at Ramah, when he fought with Hazael king of Syria. And Azariah son of Jehoram king of Judah went down to see Jehoram son of Ahab at Jezreel, because he was sick. Henry notes Ahaziah came on a visit to Joram just at the time Jehu was executing God's judgment on Ahab's family, and so was cut off with them, for bad company brings not only infection but destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And the destruction of Ahaziah was of God by coming to Joram, for when he was come, he went out with Jehoram against Jehu son of Nimshi, whom the Lord had anointed to cut off the house of Ahab. Henry notes God's providence brought Ahaziah to Joram at this fatal juncture, that he might fall with him and be taken as in a snare, acknowledging God's justice in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And it came to pass, when Jehu was executing judgment upon the house of Ahab, and found the princes of Judah, and the sons of Ahaziah's brethren, that ministered to Ahaziah, he slew them. Henry notes Jehu, executing judgment on Ahab's house, slew Judah's princes and Ahaziah's brethren's sons who ministered to him, part of the common ruin of Ahab's intimacy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And he sought Ahaziah, and they caught him, for he was hid in Samaria, and brought him to Jehu, and when they had slain him, they buried him, because, said they, he is the son of Jehoshaphat, who sought the Lord with all his heart. So Ahaziah's house had no power to keep still the kingdom. Henry notes he was decently buried, unlike Jehoram, because he was Jehoshaphat's grandson who sought the Lord with his heart, so the just are remembered with honour and respect shown even to degenerate seed for their sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. But when Athaliah mother of Ahaziah saw that her son was dead, she arose and destroyed all the seed royal of the house of Judah. Henry's chapter outline names Athaliah as the plague of Jehoram's family: she destroyed all the seed royal and usurped the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. But Jehoshabeath, daughter of the king, took Joash son of Ahaziah, and stole him from among the king's sons that were slain, and put him and his nurse in a bedchamber. So Jehoshabeath, daughter of Jehoram, wife of Jehoiada the priest, sister of Ahaziah, hid him from Athaliah, so that she slew him not. Henry's Athaliah account applies: amid the destruction of the seed royal, Joash was stolen away and hidden by Jehoshabeath, preserving David's line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And he was with them hid in the house of God six years, and Athaliah reigned over the land. Henry notes Athaliah usurped the throne and reigned over the land, while Joash remained hidden in God's house for six years, the seed royal preserved in the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Investigation (editorial — a question, not a conclusion)

This section investigates a question; it does not conclude it. The study note for verse 2 observes that forty-two cannot be Ahaziah's age, since his father died at forty, while Kings gives twenty-two.

Parallels. Both accounts give Ahaziah a one-year reign at Jerusalem. Differences. The age at accession differs by twenty years: some read forty-two as his mother Athaliah's age, put for his in reproach, while many expositors allow a transcriber's mistake of forty-two for twenty-two.

Which explanation is correct is not established by the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
