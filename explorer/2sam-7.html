Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, when the king sat in his house, and the LORD had given him rest round about from all his enemies;

2 That the king said unto Nathan the prophet, See now, I dwell in an house of cedar, but the ark of God dwelleth within curtains.

3 And Nathan said to the king, Go, do all that is in thine heart; for the LORD is with thee.

4 And it came to pass that night, that the word of the LORD came unto Nathan, saying,

5 Go and tell my servant David, Thus saith the LORD, Shalt thou build me an house for me to dwell in?

6 Whereas I have not dwelt in any house since the time that I brought up the children of Israel out of Egypt, even to this day, but have walked in a tent and in a tabernacle.

7 In all the places wherein I have walked with all the children of Israel spake I a word with any of the tribes of Israel, whom I commanded to feed my people Israel, saying, Why build ye not me an house of cedar?

8 Now therefore so shalt thou say unto my servant David, Thus saith the LORD of hosts, I took thee from the sheepcote, from following the sheep, to be ruler over my people, over Israel:

9 And I was with thee whithersoever thou wentest, and have cut off all thine enemies out of thy sight, and have made thee a great name, like unto the name of the great men that are in the earth.

10 Moreover I will appoint a place for my people Israel, and will plant them, that they may dwell in a place of their own, and move no more; neither shall the children of wickedness afflict them any more, as beforetime,

11 And as since the time that I commanded judges to be over my people Israel, and have caused thee to rest from all thine enemies. Also the LORD telleth thee that he will make thee an house.

12 And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels, and I will establish his kingdom.

13 He shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever.

14 I will be his father, and he shall be my son. If he commit iniquity, I will chasten him with the rod of men, and with the stripes of the children of men:

15 But my mercy shall not depart away from him, as I took it from Saul, whom I put away before thee.

16 And thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever before thee: thy throne shall be established for ever.

17 According to all these words, and according to all this vision, so did Nathan speak unto David.

18 Then went king David in, and sat before the LORD, and he said, Who am I, O Lord GOD? and what is my house, that thou hast brought me hitherto?

19 And this was yet a small thing in thy sight, O Lord GOD; but thou hast spoken also of thy servant’s house for a great while to come. And is this the manner of man, O Lord GOD?

20 And what can David say more unto thee? for thou, Lord GOD, knowest thy servant.

21 For thy word’s sake, and according to thine own heart, hast thou done all these great things, to make thy servant know them.

22 Wherefore thou art great, O LORD God: for there is none like thee, neither is there any God beside thee, according to all that we have heard with our ears.

23 And what one nation in the earth is like thy people, even like Israel, whom God went to redeem for a people to himself, and to make him a name, and to do for you great things and terrible, for thy land, before thy people, which thou redeemedst to thee from Egypt, from the nations and their gods?

24 For thou hast confirmed to thyself thy people Israel to be a people unto thee for ever: and thou, LORD, art become their God.

25 And now, O LORD God, the word that thou hast spoken concerning thy servant, and concerning his house, establish it for ever, and do as thou hast said.

26 And let thy name be magnified for ever, saying, The LORD of hosts is the God over Israel: and let the house of thy servant David be established before thee.

27 For thou, O LORD of hosts, God of Israel, hast revealed to thy servant, saying, I will build thee an house: therefore hath thy servant found in his heart to pray this prayer unto thee.

28 And now, O Lord GOD, thou art that God, and thy words be true, and thou hast promised this goodness unto thy servant:

29 Therefore now let it please thee to bless the house of thy servant, that it may continue for ever before thee: for thou, O Lord GOD, hast spoken it: and with thy blessing let the house of thy servant be blessed for ever.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Settled in his palace, David proposes to build God a house of cedar, but through Nathan God turns the offer around: instead God will build David a house, a lasting dynasty, and his offspring will sit on the throne and build the temple. David answers with a long prayer of humility and thanks. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The king sits in his house, and the Lord has given him rest round about from all his enemies. David, at rest in his palace, considers how he might best employ his leisure and prosperity in the service of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. David tells Nathan he dwells in a house of cedar while the ark dwells within curtains. Having built a palace for himself and a city for his servants, he now thinks of building a habitation for the ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Nathan answers, Go, do all that is in thine heart, for the Lord is with thee. Nathan here speaks not as a prophet but as a godly man encouraging David by his private judgment, and Henry urges us likewise to forward the good designs of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. That night the word of the Lord comes to Nathan. Private encouragement gives way to a prophetic message that will both accept David's purpose and countermand its performance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The Lord asks, Shalt thou build me a house for me to dwell in? The question reverses David's plan: God will be the builder, not David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. God has not dwelt in any house since bringing Israel out of Egypt, but walked in a tent and a tabernacle. His presence travelled with his people rather than settling in a fixed house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. In all his walkings with Israel, God never asked any tribe he commanded to feed his people why they had not built him a house of cedar. The absence of such a command shows David's plan, though devout, was not required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. God recounts taking David from the sheepcote, from following sheep, to be ruler over Israel. Every honour David holds is traced to God's choosing, not to his own rising. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. God was with David wherever he went, cut off his enemies, and made him a great name like the great men of the earth. The past record of God's presence is the ground for the promises that follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. God will appoint a place for Israel and plant them, that they may dwell in their own place and move no more, no longer afflicted by the children of wickedness. The promise secures the people, not only the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. As since the judges, God has given David rest from his enemies, and now the Lord tells him that he will make David a house. The house David wanted to build for God becomes the house God builds for David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. When David sleeps with his fathers, God will set up his seed after him, proceeding out of his bowels, and establish his kingdom. These blessings relate to Solomon, David's immediate successor, and to the royal line of Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The seed shall build a house for God's name, and God will establish the throne of his kingdom for ever. Henry notes the everlasting throne can be applied to no other than Christ and his kingdom, for David's house and kingdom long since came to an end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. God will be his father and he his son; if he commits iniquity, he will be chastened with the rod of men. The committing of iniquity cannot be applied to the Messiah himself, Henry observes, but to his spiritual seed, who have infirmities yet are not cast off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. God's mercy shall not depart from him as it was taken from Saul, whom God put away before David. Unlike Saul's house, David's line is held in mercy even under chastening. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. David's house and kingdom shall be established for ever before him, and his throne for ever. The promise reaches beyond Solomon to Christ, who is often called David and the Son of David, and who builds the gospel temple, the spiritual temple of true believers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. According to all these words and this vision, Nathan speaks to David. Nathan faithfully delivers a message that contradicts his own earlier words, once he is better informed of God's mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. David goes in, sits before the Lord, and asks, Who am I, O Lord God, and what is my house, that thou hast brought me hitherto? His prayer is full of devout affection, with low thoughts of his own merits and all he has counted as divine gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. This was yet a small thing in God's sight, but he has spoken also of David's house for a great while to come. David is amazed that God should deal with man as he does, considering what man is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. David asks what more he can say, for the Lord God knows his servant. He knows us better than we know ourselves, Henry notes, so we may be satisfied with what he has done for us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. For his word's sake and according to his own heart, God has done all these great things to make his servant know them. David ascribes all to the free grace of God, both the great things done for him and the great things made known to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Therefore thou art great, O Lord God, for there is none like thee, neither any God beside thee. David speaks very highly and honourably of the Lord's favours, as one who has received far beyond desert. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. What one nation is like Israel, whom God redeemed to be a people to himself, to make him a name, doing great and terrible things for their land? The promise to David's house rests on the same redeeming grace shown to the whole nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. God has confirmed Israel to himself as a people for ever, and has become their God. The covenant with the house stands inside the covenant with the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. David prays that the word spoken concerning his servant and his house be established for ever, and that God do as he has said. He builds his faith and hopes to speed upon the sureness of God's promise, for with God saying and doing are not two things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Let God's name be magnified for ever, saying, The Lord of hosts is the God over Israel, and let David's house be established before him. The prayer seeks God's glory first, and the house's continuance as its witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Because God revealed that he will build David a house, David has found in his heart to pray this prayer. His heart was found, Henry says, fixed and gathered from its wanderings, not a prayer of the tongue only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Thou art that God, and thy words are true, and thou hast promised this goodness to thy servant. The promise of Christ includes all, Henry observes: if the Lord God be ours, what more can we ask or think of? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Let it please thee to bless the house of thy servant, that it may continue for ever before thee, for thou hast spoken it. The promises of God are not made to us by name as to David, Henry notes, but they belong to all who believe in Jesus Christ and plead them in his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
