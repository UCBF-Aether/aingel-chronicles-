The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Matthew 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham.

2 Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob; and Jacob begat Judas and his brethren;

3 And Judas begat Phares and Zara of Thamar; and Phares begat Esrom; and Esrom begat Aram;

4 And Aram begat Aminadab; and Aminadab begat Naasson; and Naasson begat Salmon;

5 And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse;

6 And Jesse begat David the king; and David the king begat Solomon of her that had been the wife of Urias;

7 And Solomon begat Roboam; and Roboam begat Abia; and Abia begat Asa;

8 And Asa begat Josaphat; and Josaphat begat Joram; and Joram begat Ozias;

9 And Ozias begat Joatham; and Joatham begat Achaz; and Achaz begat Ezekias;

10 And Ezekias begat Manasses; and Manasses begat Amon; and Amon begat Josias;

11 And Josias begat Jechonias and his brethren, about the time they were carried away to Babylon:

12 And after they were brought to Babylon, Jechonias begat Salathiel; and Salathiel begat Zorobabel;

13 And Zorobabel begat Abiud; and Abiud begat Eliakim; and Eliakim begat Azor;

14 And Azor begat Sadoc; and Sadoc begat Achim; and Achim begat Eliud;

15 And Eliud begat Eleazar; and Eleazar begat Matthan; and Matthan begat Jacob;

16 And Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ.

17 So all the generations from Abraham to David are fourteen generations; and from David until the carrying away into Babylon are fourteen generations; and from the carrying away into Babylon unto Christ are fourteen generations.

18 Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost.

19 Then Joseph her husband, being a just man, and not willing to make her a publick example, was minded to put her away privily.

20 But while he thought on these things, behold, the angel of the LORD appeared unto him in a dream, saying, Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost.

21 And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins.

22 Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying,

23 Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us.

24 Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife:

25 And knew her not till she had brought forth her firstborn son: and he called his name JESUS.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Matthew opens with the genealogy of Jesus Christ, son of David and son of Abraham, arranged in three groups of fourteen generations, from Abraham to David, from David to the carrying away to Babylon, and from Babylon to Christ, with four women named along the way. The chapter then turns to Joseph: Mary is found with child of the Holy Ghost while espoused to him, an angel tells him in a dream not to fear to take her as wife, the child is to be called Jesus because he shall save his people from their sins, and the prophecy of Emmanuel, God with us, is quoted as fulfilled. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The opening title covers only the first seventeen verses: “the book of the generation” is a Jewish table of genealogy. Henry and JFB stress the two names that matter most to a Jewish reader, son of David and son of Abraham, the two promised starting-points of the Messiah's line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Of Jacob's sons only Judah is named here, because it was from Judah's line that the Messiah was to spring, as promised in Genesis 49:10. The genealogy is selective by design, tracing the promised line rather than every branch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Tamar is the first of four women named in the genealogy, three of whom carry a blot in the Old Testament record. JFB reads this as grace on display from the outset, reaching even to those with stained histories. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Salmon's marriage to Rahab, and Boaz's to Ruth, bring two Gentiles by birth into the Messiah's line. JFB notes the fitness of this in a Gospel written by Matthew the publican: grace that fetches in “them that are afar off.” Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. David is twice emphatically called “David the king,” the first of the royal line from which the Messiah descends and the king whose name the promised throne bears. Solomon follows by Bathsheba, named only as her who had been the wife of Uriah, another stained name in the line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The royal succession runs through Solomon, Rehoboam, Abijah and Asa, kings who matter here chiefly as links joining the first king of the line to its last. JFB notes the intermediate kings are otherwise sunk, their importance being the connection they preserve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Between Joram and Uzziah three kings are omitted — Ahaziah, Joash and Amaziah — so the whole may be compressed into three fourteens. JFB traces the omission to religious considerations, likely their connection with the house of Ahab. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Jeconiah was Josiah's grandson, Jehoiakim being sunk as a mere puppet of Egypt, and his “brethren” are his uncles, chiefly Zedekiah. The “carrying away to Babylon” is softened to a migration, respecting the national feeling about a bitter memory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. After the migration, Jeconiah begets Salathiel and Salathiel Zerubbabel, though Zerubbabel was strictly Salathiel's grandson through the omitted Pedaiah. Jeremiah's “childless” for Coniah means no reigning child on David's throne, not no seed at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. None of the post-exilic names from Zerubbabel onward appear in the Old Testament, but JFB notes they were doubtless taken from the public or family registers the Jews carefully kept, whose accuracy was never challenged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The chain ends with Jacob begetting Joseph, the husband of Mary, of whom Jesus was born — making plain this is Joseph's genealogy, not Mary's. Joseph is proclaimed as legal father only, never natural father, so that Jesus' Davidic descent stands unquestioned while his virgin birth is preserved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The whole is arranged in three fourteens, each embracing a marked era and ending with a notable event — aids to memory familiar to Jewish readers. Counting David twice, as first fourteen's end and second's beginning, gives the last division its full fourteen as well. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Mary, betrothed to Joseph — betrothal being valid marriage under Jewish law — was found with child of the Holy Ghost before they came together. JFB notes only the fact was discovered; the explanation is the Evangelist's own, implying the Spirit as a living, conscious Person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Joseph, being a just man, would not expose Mary publicly, and planned a private divorcement before two or three witnesses with no cause assigned, rather than dragging her before a magistrate. Henry adds that it is the thoughtful, not the unthinking, whom God guides. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. While Joseph brooded alone over the painful step, the angel came in a dream at the last extremity, addressing him as “son of David” to remind him of the Davidic hope. The message clears the cloud: what is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost, and the relationship is unsullied still. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The child is to be called Jesus, from the Hebrew meaning “Jehovah the Saviour,” the same name as Joshua. He himself, personally and by his own acts, shall save his people from their sins — from guilt by his death and from power by the Spirit's grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. All this was done that the word spoken by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled, Isaiah 7:14. Matthew's Gospel dwells on fulfilled prophecy because, to Jewish readers, fulfilment was strong evidence for the Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The prophecy speaks of “the virgin,” that particular virgin destined to this unparalleled distinction. Emmanuel is not given as a proper name like Jesus, but as his character: God manifested in the flesh, the living bond of fellowship between God and men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Joseph, raised from sleep with his difficulties removed, did as the angel bade him, speedily and without dispute, and took Mary as his wife. Henry notes the pattern for all of life's great turns: take direction from God's written word, and the way proves safe and comfortable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Joseph knew her not till she brought forth her first-born son, and called his name Jesus as directed. JFB cautions that “till” does not necessarily imply a different footing afterwards, nor does “first-born” decide whether Mary had later children, since the law regarded only that none were born before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Espoused / betrothed: betrothal was valid marriage under Jewish law, so Mary was already Joseph's wife in law when she was found with child Interpreted Study note on Matthew 1:18, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Emmanuel: not given as a proper name like Jesus, but as his character: God manifested in the flesh Interpreted Study note on Matthew 1:23, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
