The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 37 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Bezaleel made the ark of shittim wood: two cubits and a half was the length of it, and a cubit and a half the breadth of it, and a cubit and a half the height of it:

2 And he overlaid it with pure gold within and without, and made a crown of gold to it round about.

3 And he cast for it four rings of gold, to be set by the four corners of it; even two rings upon the one side of it, and two rings upon the other side of it.

4 And he made staves of shittim wood, and overlaid them with gold.

5 And he put the staves into the rings by the sides of the ark, to bear the ark.

6 And he made the mercy seat of pure gold: two cubits and a half was the length thereof, and one cubit and a half the breadth thereof.

7 And he made two cherubims of gold, beaten out of one piece made he them, on the two ends of the mercy seat;

8 One cherub on the end on this side, and another cherub on the other end on that side: out of the mercy seat made he the cherubims on the two ends thereof.

9 And the cherubims spread out their wings on high, and covered with their wings over the mercy seat, with their faces one to another; even to the mercy seatward were the faces of the cherubims.

10 And he made the table of shittim wood: two cubits was the length thereof, and a cubit the breadth thereof, and a cubit and a half the height thereof:

11 And he overlaid it with pure gold, and made thereunto a crown of gold round about.

12 Also he made thereunto a border of an handbreadth round about; and made a crown of gold for the border thereof round about.

13 And he cast for it four rings of gold, and put the rings upon the four corners that were in the four feet thereof.

14 Over against the border were the rings, the places for the staves to bear the table.

15 And he made the staves of shittim wood, and overlaid them with gold, to bear the table.

16 And he made the vessels which were upon the table, his dishes, and his spoons, and his bowls, and his covers to cover withal, of pure gold.

17 And he made the candlestick of pure gold: of beaten work made he the candlestick; his shaft, and his branch, his bowls, his knops, and his flowers, were of the same:

18 And six branches going out of the sides thereof; three branches of the candlestick out of the one side thereof, and three branches of the candlestick out of the other side thereof:

19 Three bowls made after the fashion of almonds in one branch, a knop and a flower; and three bowls made like almonds in another branch, a knop and a flower: so throughout the six branches going out of the candlestick.

20 And in the candlestick were four bowls made like almonds, his knops, and his flowers:

21 And a knop under two branches of the same, and a knop under two branches of the same, and a knop under two branches of the same, according to the six branches going out of it.

22 Their knops and their branches were of the same: all of it was one beaten work of pure gold.

23 And he made his seven lamps, and his snuffers, and his snuffdishes, of pure gold.

24 Of a talent of pure gold made he it, and all the vessels thereof.

25 And he made the incense altar of shittim wood: the length of it was a cubit, and the breadth of it a cubit; it was foursquare; and two cubits was the height of it; the horns thereof were of the same.

26 And he overlaid it with pure gold, both the top of it, and the sides thereof round about, and the horns of it: also he made unto it a crown of gold round about.

27 And he made two rings of gold for it under the crown thereof, by the two corners of it, upon the two sides thereof, to be places for the staves to bear it withal.

28 And he made the staves of shittim wood, and overlaid them with gold.

29 And he made the holy anointing oil, and the pure incense of sweet spices, according to the work of the apothecary.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Bezaleel makes the furniture of the sanctuary after the pattern: the ark of shittim wood overlaid with gold, its mercy seat and cherubim, the table and its vessels, the pure golden lampstand with its branches and flowers, the altar of incense, and the holy anointing oil and pure incense. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And Bezaleel made the ark of shittim wood, two cubits and a half long, a cubit and a half broad, and a cubit and a half high. Henry notes in the furniture of the tabernacle were emblems of a spiritual and acceptable service, and the ark was first, the chest of the testimony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And he overlaid it with pure gold within and without, and made a crown of gold to it round about. Henry observes the gold within and without figured the sinless nature in union with the Divine, for the ark was a type of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And he cast for it four rings of gold, to be set by the four corners of it, even two rings upon the one side of it, and two rings upon the other side of it. Henry notes the rings were the ark's provision for journeying, that the throne might travel with the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And he made staves of shittim wood, and overlaid them with gold. Henry observes the staves were wood overlaid with gold like the ark itself, common material made glorious by service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And he put the staves into the rings by the sides of the ark, to bear the ark. Henry notes the ark was borne, not dragged, for God's presence moved by appointed means. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And he made the mercy seat of pure gold, two cubits and a half long, and one cubit and a half broad. Henry observes the mercy seat was the covering of gold where God dwelt between the cherubim, as a prince on his throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And he made two cherubims of gold, beaten out of one piece made he them, on the two ends of the mercy seat. Henry notes the cherubim were beaten out of one piece with the mercy seat, denoting the angels' attendance on the Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. One cherub on the end on this side, and another cherub on the other end on that side, out of the mercy seat made he the cherubims on the two ends thereof. Henry observes one cherub stood at each end, both of the mercy seat itself, for the angels' ministry belongs to the throne of mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And the cherubims spread out their wings on high, and covered with their wings over the mercy seat, with their faces one to another, even to the mercy seatward were the faces of the cherubims. Henry notes the cherubim looked toward each other and downward toward the ark, attending on the Redeemer and desiring to look into the mysteries of the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And he made the table of shittim wood, two cubits long, a cubit broad, and a cubit and a half high. Henry notes the table stood in the outer tabernacle, always furnished with shew-bread, and the shew-bread represented that provision for those who hunger and thirst after righteousness which the gospel and ordinances abundantly bestow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And he overlaid it with pure gold, and made thereunto a crown of gold round about. Henry observes the gold overlay and crown marked the table as royal furniture, for the provisions of God's house are a king's feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Also he made thereunto a border of a handbreadth round about, and made a crown of gold for the border thereof round about. Henry notes the border kept the vessels in place, a small detail of order in the house where God dwelt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And he cast for it four rings of gold, and put the rings upon the four corners that were in the four feet thereof. Henry observes the table, like the ark, was made to travel, with rings for bearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Over against the border were the rings, the places for the staves to bear the table. Henry notes the rings stood by the border, fitted to the frame, that the table might be borne steadily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And he made the staves of shittim wood, and overlaid them with gold, to bear the table. Henry observes the table's staves matched the ark's, wood overlaid with gold, for the table of communion was borne with the same honour as the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And he made the vessels which were upon the table, his dishes, and his spoons, and his bowls, and his covers to cover withal, of pure gold. Henry notes the vessels were all pure gold, for the food for souls is served in vessels worthy of the feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And he made the candlestick of pure gold, of beaten work made he the candlestick, his shaft, and his branch, his bowls, his knops, and his flowers, were of the same. Henry notes the candlestick, with its lights, represented the teaching and enlightening of the Holy Spirit, afforded in this dark world to believers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And six branches going out of the sides thereof, three branches of the candlestick out of the one side thereof, and three branches out of the other side thereof. Henry observes the six branches spread the light in every direction, for one lamp was not enough for the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Three bowls made after the fashion of almonds in one branch, a knop and a flower, and three bowls made like almonds in another branch, a knop and a flower, so throughout the six branches going out of the candlestick. Henry notes the almond bowls figured life and fruitfulness, for the light of God's house is a living light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And in the candlestick were four bowls made like almonds, his knops, and his flowers. Henry observes the central shaft bore four bowls, more than any branch, for the shaft carried the whole. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And a knop under two branches of the same, and a knop under two branches of the same, and a knop under two branches of the same, according to the six branches going out of it. Henry notes the knops joined each pair of branches to the shaft, for all the lights were one candlestick. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Their knops and their branches were of the same, all of it was one beaten work of pure gold. Henry observes the candlestick was one beaten work from a single talent, for the light of God's word is one, though it shines through many branches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And he made his seven lamps, and his snuffers, and his snuffdishes, of pure gold. Henry notes the seven lamps and their tending tools were pure gold, for nothing that touched the light was to be common. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Of a talent of pure gold made he it, and all the vessels thereof. Henry observes the whole candlestick with all its vessels came from a single talent, a great weight of gold for a single light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And he made the incense altar of shittim wood, the length of it was a cubit, and the breadth of it a cubit, foursquare, and two cubits was the height of it, and the horns thereof were of the same. Henry notes the incense represented the prayers of the saints, and the altar was small and square, a cubit each way, for the place of intercession was not large. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And he overlaid it with pure gold, both the top of it, and the sides thereof round about, and the horns thereof, and he made unto it a crown of gold round about. Henry observes the pure gold overlay marked it as the golden altar, distinct from the brazen altar, for intercession stands nearer the throne than sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And he made two rings of gold for it under the crown thereof, by the two corners of it, upon the two sides thereof, to be places for the staves to bear it withal. Henry notes the rings under the crown bore the altar, that the place of prayer might travel with the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And he made the staves of shittim wood, and overlaid them with gold. Henry observes the staves were wood overlaid with gold like the altar itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And he made the holy anointing oil, and the pure incense of sweet spices, according to the work of the apothecary. Henry closes on the exactness of the workmen to their rule, which should be followed by us, seeking the influences of the Holy Spirit, that we may rejoice in and glorify God while in this world, and at length be with him forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
