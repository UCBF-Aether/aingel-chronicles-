The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 24 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 The earth is the LORD’s, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein.

2 For he hath founded it upon the seas, and established it upon the floods.

3 Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD? or who shall stand in his holy place?

4 He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart; who hath not lifted up his soul unto vanity, nor sworn deceitfully.

5 He shall receive the blessing from the LORD, and righteousness from the God of his salvation.

6 This is the generation of them that seek him, that seek thy face, O Jacob. Selah.

7 Lift up your heads, O ye gates; and be ye lift up, ye everlasting doors; and the King of glory shall come in.

8 Who is this King of glory? The LORD strong and mighty, the LORD mighty in battle.

9 Lift up your heads, O ye gates; even lift them up, ye everlasting doors; and the King of glory shall come in.

10 Who is this King of glory? The LORD of hosts, he is the King of glory. Selah.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The earth's Lord, and the question of access: the world is his, founded on the seas, and only clean hands and a pure heart may ascend his hill and receive blessing. Then the gates are bidden twice to lift for the King of glory, the Lord strong in battle and Lord of hosts. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The earth is the Lord's, and its fullness, the world and its dwellers: ownership is universal because God made all and fills all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. For he founded it upon the seas and established it upon the floods: the world stands firm on waters that would swallow it, by God's establishing power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Who shall ascend into the hill of the Lord, or stand in his holy place? The question of access follows ownership: the earth's Lord must be approached on his terms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He who has clean hands and a pure heart, who has not lifted his soul to vanity nor sworn deceitfully: outward deeds and inward purity, with truth in oaths and freedom from idols, mark the one who may ascend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He shall receive blessing from the Lord, and righteousness from the God of his salvation: blessing and the righteousness that fits him for God's presence are both God's gifts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. This is the generation of those who seek him, who seek your face, O Jacob: true seekers, not mere descendants, are Jacob's true generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Lift up your heads, O gates, and be lifted up, you everlasting doors, and the King of glory shall come in: the gates of Zion — and, in the ascension reading of Henry and JFB, the gates of heaven — are bidden to open wide for the returning King. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Who is this King of glory? The Lord strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in battle: the answer names the warrior King, fresh from victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Lift up your heads, O gates, even lift them up, you everlasting doors, and the King of glory shall come in: the summons is repeated because the entry deserves a double welcome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Who is this King of glory? The Lord of hosts, he is the King of glory: the final answer names him commander of heaven's armies, and the psalm closes in acclamation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
