The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 They say, If a man put away his wife, and she go from him, and become another man’s, shall he return unto her again? shall not that land be greatly polluted? but thou hast played the harlot with many lovers; yet return again to me, saith the LORD.

2 Lift up thine eyes unto the high places, and see where thou hast not been lien with. In the ways hast thou sat for them, as the Arabian in the wilderness; and thou hast polluted the land with thy whoredoms and with thy wickedness.

3 Therefore the showers have been withholden, and there hath been no latter rain; and thou hadst a whore’s forehead, thou refusedst to be ashamed.

4 Wilt thou not from this time cry unto me, My father, thou art the guide of my youth?

5 Will he reserve his anger for ever? will he keep it to the end? Behold, thou hast spoken and done evil things as thou couldest.

6 The LORD said also unto me in the days of Josiah the king, Hast thou seen that which backsliding Israel hath done? she is gone up upon every high mountain and under every green tree, and there hath played the harlot.

7 And I said after she had done all these things, Turn thou unto me. But she returned not. And her treacherous sister Judah saw it.

8 And I saw, when for all the causes whereby backsliding Israel committed adultery I had put her away, and given her a bill of divorce; yet her treacherous sister Judah feared not, but went and played the harlot also.

9 And it came to pass through the lightness of her whoredom, that she defiled the land, and committed adultery with stones and with stocks.

10 And yet for all this her treacherous sister Judah hath not turned unto me with her whole heart, but feignedly, saith the LORD.

11 And the LORD said unto me, The backsliding Israel hath justified herself more than treacherous Judah.

12 Go and proclaim these words toward the north, and say, Return, thou backsliding Israel, saith the LORD; and I will not cause mine anger to fall upon you: for I am merciful, saith the LORD, and I will not keep anger for ever.

13 Only acknowledge thine iniquity, that thou hast transgressed against the LORD thy God, and hast scattered thy ways to the strangers under every green tree, and ye have not obeyed my voice, saith the LORD.

14 Turn, O backsliding children, saith the LORD; for I am married unto you: and I will take you one of a city, and two of a family, and I will bring you to Zion:

15 And I will give you pastors according to mine heart, which shall feed you with knowledge and understanding.

16 And it shall come to pass, when ye be multiplied and increased in the land, in those days, saith the LORD, they shall say no more, The ark of the covenant of the LORD: neither shall it come to mind: neither shall they remember it; neither shall they visit it; neither shall that be done any more.

17 At that time they shall call Jerusalem the throne of the LORD; and all the nations shall be gathered unto it, to the name of the LORD, to Jerusalem: neither shall they walk any more after the imagination of their evil heart.

18 In those days the house of Judah shall walk with the house of Israel, and they shall come together out of the land of the north to the land that I have given for an inheritance unto your fathers.

19 But I said, How shall I put thee among the children, and give thee a pleasant land, a goodly heritage of the hosts of nations? and I said, Thou shalt call me, My father; and shalt not turn away from me.

20 Surely as a wife treacherously departeth from her husband, so have ye dealt treacherously with me, O house of Israel, saith the LORD.

21 A voice was heard upon the high places, weeping and supplications of the children of Israel: for they have perverted their way, and they have forgotten the LORD their God.

22 Return, ye backsliding children, and I will heal your backslidings. Behold, we come unto thee; for thou art the LORD our God.

23 Truly in vain is salvation hoped for from the hills, and from the multitude of mountains: truly in the LORD our God is the salvation of Israel.

24 For shame hath devoured the labour of our fathers from our youth; their flocks and their herds, their sons and their daughters.

25 We lie down in our shame, and our confusion covereth us: for we have sinned against the LORD our God, we and our fathers, from our youth even unto this day, and have not obeyed the voice of the LORD our God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Using the image of marriage and divorce, this chapter charges both Israel and Judah with unfaithfulness, yet keeps calling them back. Israel, already put away, is invited to return; Judah, who watched her sister and did the same, is warned that her repentance has been feigned rather than wholehearted. It ends in the people's own confession of shame for sins committed from their youth to that day. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The law's rule of divorce is cited: if a man puts away his wife and she joins another, her first husband shall never take her again, lest the land be greatly polluted. Henry notes Israel deserved to be abandoned utterly by that rule, yet God graciously says, 'yet return again to me,' for he has not tied himself by the laws he made for us and has none of man's peevish resentment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Lift up her eyes to the high places and see where she has not been lien with: in the ways she sat for idols, like the Arabian in the wilderness, polluting the land with whoredoms and wickedness. Henry notes they were insatiable in lusting after false worships, seeking opportunity and courting new gods, not only sinners themselves but tempters to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The showers have been withheld and there is no latter rain, yet she has a whore's forehead and refuses to be ashamed. Henry observes God corrected gently, withholding only the latter rain as an intimation of continual dependence, but it had no effect, and those past shame are past hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Wilt thou not from this time cry, 'My father, thou art the guide of my youth?' Henry explains the expected return: coming to God as a father to beg pardon like a returning prodigal, and as the guide and husband of their youth, covenanting to follow his guidance henceforward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Will he reserve his anger for ever, or keep it to the end? Behold, she has spoken and done evil things as she could. Henry notes repenting sinners may encourage themselves that though God chide, he will not always chide, yet he also records the hypocritical reading: crying 'My Father' while still doing evil as they can, and flattering themselves his anger will end while treasuring up wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. In the days of Josiah, God asks whether Jeremiah has seen what backsliding Israel has done, going up on every high mountain and under every green tree to play the harlot. Henry dates the sermon to Josiah's reformation, in which the king was hearty but the people were not sincere, and notes Israel's idolatry ran downhill from the golden calves at Dan and Bethel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. God said after all these things, 'Turn thou unto me,' but she returned not, and her treacherous sister Judah saw it. Henry notes God by his prophets invited Israel to repent, pressing serious piety and return to the true God's worship, yet they persisted, and God keeps account of how often he has called and been refused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When backsliding Israel committed adultery, God put her away and gave her a bill of divorce, yet treacherous sister Judah feared not but played the harlot also. Henry explains the divorce as God throwing Israel out of his protection, leaving them an easy prey, scattering their synagogues and schools of the prophets, and excluding further claim to the covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Through the lightness of her whoredom she defiled the land and committed adultery with stones and stocks. Henry charges Judah with heartily concurring under wicked kings like Manasseh and Amon, so that notorious whoredom made the whole country an abomination to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Yet for all this, treacherous Judah has not turned to God with her whole heart, but feignedly. Henry notes the present case under Josiah: the people were forced to external compliance, keeping a solemn passover and renewing covenants, but their hearts were not right with God, and feigned return deceives no one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Backsliding Israel has justified herself more than treacherous Judah. Henry warns this comparative justification stands Israel in little stead, but aggravates Judah doubly: more was expected from her sacred profession, and she refused the warning of Israel's ruin, so that God's judgments on others, unheeded, aggravate her destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Proclaim toward the north: 'Return, thou backsliding Israel, and I will not cause mine anger to fall upon you, for I am merciful and will not keep anger for ever.' Henry notes the call looks north to the captive ten tribes to show God had not forgotten them, and to upbraid Judah, since backsliding Israel will sooner accept mercy than treacherous Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Only acknowledge thine iniquity: thou hast transgressed against the Lord, scattered thy ways to strangers under every green tree, and not obeyed his voice. Henry calls the terms of pardon easy and reasonable: confess the corruption of nature, the actual transgressions, their multitude, and above all the disobedience in them, for the sinfulness of sin is the worst thing in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Turn, O backsliding children, for God is married unto them, and will take one of a city and two of a family and bring them to Zion. Henry notes that of the many backsliders few return, like gleanings of the vintage, yet not one of the few shall be lost: though there be but one in a city, God will find that one and bring him safely to Zion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. God will give pastors according to his heart, who shall feed them with knowledge and understanding. Henry observes a gathered church must be governed, not left to range like wild beasts, and pastors after God's heart feed the flock with the word of God rather than feeding themselves and fleecing the flock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. When they are multiplied in the land, they shall say no more, 'The ark of the covenant of the Lord,' nor shall it come to mind or be visited any more. Henry reads this as the ceremonial law set aside in the Messiah's kingdom: Christ, the truth of all those types, is the ark and mercy-seat, and true worshippers worship in spirit and in truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. At that time Jerusalem shall be called the throne of the Lord, and all nations shall be gathered to it, no more walking after the imagination of their evil heart. Henry notes two things make the gospel church famous: God's special residence and government there as the throne of his glory, government, and grace, and the accession of the Gentiles gathered to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The house of Judah shall walk with the house of Israel, coming together out of the land of the north to the land given to their fathers. Henry sees the return under Cyrus as a type of Jews and Gentiles united in the gospel church, one sheepfold under one shepherd, with all enmities slain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. God asks how he shall put them among the children and give them a pleasant land, a goodly heritage of the hosts of nations, and answers that they shall call him 'My father' and not turn away. Henry notes the difficulty is our utter unworthiness, and God's own answer supplies the Spirit of adoption crying 'Abba, Father' and the fear in their hearts that they never turn from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Surely as a wife treacherously departs from her husband, so Israel has dealt treacherously with God. Henry calls this treason, not mere unfaithfulness: joined to God by a marriage covenant, they broke it against one who had always dealt kindly and faithfully with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. A voice is heard upon the high places, weeping and supplications, for Israel has perverted its way and forgotten the Lord its God. Henry notes sin is the perverting of the way, forgetting God lies at the bottom of all sin, and prayers and tears well become consciences that know they have perverted their way, rather than hearts fretting against the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Return, ye backsliding children, and God will heal your backslidings; the people answer, 'Behold, we come unto thee, for thou art the Lord our God.' Henry notes God calls them children in tenderness, foolish and froward yet his sons whom he corrects but will not disinherit, and promises healing by pardoning mercy, quieting peace, and renewing grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Truly in vain is salvation hoped for from the hills and the multitude of mountains; truly in the Lord our God is the salvation of Israel. Henry notes the returning people now disclaim all creature confidences: they worshipped idols on hills and mountains, but from God alone comes every good thing, even salvation itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Shame has devoured the labour of their fathers from their youth, their flocks, herds, sons, and daughters. Henry observes true penitents learn to call their beloved sin 'shame' and to charge all their mischiefs upon it: their idolatry both provoked desolating judgments and consumed their substance in sacrifices, even human sacrifices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. We lie down in our shame and our confusion covers us, for we have sinned against the Lord our God, we and our fathers, from our youth even to this day, and have not obeyed his voice. Henry notes both penal and penitential shame surround them, and the worst thing in sin is the affront put upon God by disobeying his voice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
