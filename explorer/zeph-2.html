The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Zephaniah 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Gather yourselves together, yea, gather together, O nation not desired;

2 Before the decree bring forth, before the day pass as the chaff, before the fierce anger of the LORD come upon you, before the day of the LORD’s anger come upon you.

3 Seek ye the LORD, all ye meek of the earth, which have wrought his judgment; seek righteousness, seek meekness: it may be ye shall be hid in the day of the LORD’s anger.

4 For Gaza shall be forsaken, and Ashkelon a desolation: they shall drive out Ashdod at the noon day, and Ekron shall be rooted up.

5 Woe unto the inhabitants of the sea coast, the nation of the Cherethites! the word of the LORD is against you; O Canaan, the land of the Philistines, I will even destroy thee, that there shall be no inhabitant.

6 And the sea coast shall be dwellings and cottages for shepherds, and folds for flocks.

7 And the coast shall be for the remnant of the house of Judah; they shall feed thereupon: in the houses of Ashkelon shall they lie down in the evening: for the LORD their God shall visit them, and turn away their captivity.

8 I have heard the reproach of Moab, and the revilings of the children of Ammon, whereby they have reproached my people, and magnified themselves against their border.

9 Therefore as I live, saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, Surely Moab shall be as Sodom, and the children of Ammon as Gomorrah, even the breeding of nettles, and saltpits, and a perpetual desolation: the residue of my people shall spoil them, and the remnant of my people shall possess them.

10 This shall they have for their pride, because they have reproached and magnified themselves against the people of the LORD of hosts.

11 The LORD will be terrible unto them: for he will famish all the gods of the earth; and men shall worship him, every one from his place, even all the isles of the heathen.

12 Ye Ethiopians also, ye shall be slain by my sword.

13 And he will stretch out his hand against the north, and destroy Assyria; and will make Nineveh a desolation, and dry like a wilderness.

14 And flocks shall lie down in the midst of her, all the beasts of the nations: both the cormorant and the bittern shall lodge in the upper lintels of it; their voice shall sing in the windows; desolation shall be in the thresholds; for he shall uncover the cedar work.

15 This is the rejoicing city that dwelt carelessly, that said in her heart, I am, and there is none beside me: how is she become a desolation, a place for beasts to lie down in! every one that passeth by her shall hiss, and wag his hand.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Gather yourselves together, O nation not desired, before the decree brings forth and the day passes as chaff, before the fierce anger of the LORD comes upon you. The meek of the earth are told to seek the LORD, seek righteousness, seek meekness, that they may be hid in the day of anger. Then judgment moves round the neighbours in turn: the Philistine coast emptied to shepherds' cottages and given to Judah's remnant, Moab as Sodom and Ammon as Gomorrah for reproaching God's people, the Ethiopians slain by the sword, and Assyria's Nineveh, the rejoicing city that said 'I am, and there is none beside me,' made a desolation where beasts lie down. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The prophet's design in the terrible description of the foregoing chapter was not to drive the people to despair, but to drive them to God and to their duty, and so he summons them to a national assembly. Gather yourselves together, that in a body you may humble yourselves before God, fast, pray, and seek his face, and enquire into yourselves to find the sin that provoked his displeasure; the nation is called not desired, either not desiring God or not desirable in itself, yet God says, gather together to me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. They must act in earnest and with all speed, before the decree bring forth and before the day pass as the chaff. As the embryo lies hidden in the womb and then emerges in its due time, so God for a time hides his vengeance, yet brings it forth at the proper season, and then it will hurry the impenitent away into captivity as chaff before the wind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Seek the Lord, all you meek of the earth who have wrought his judgment, that is, who have obeyed his laws and made conscience of duty to him. The meek are modest, humble, and quiet in the land, yet even they must seek righteousness and seek meekness still, for those who excel in a grace must seek to excel yet more in it; it may be you shall be hid in the day of the Lord's anger, taken under special protection, whether by deliverance in this life or by being taken to heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Here begins the denunciation of the judgments of God against the neighbouring nations, first the Philistines, in verses 4 to 7. Gaza shall be forsaken, Ashkelon a desolation, Ashdod driven out at noonday, and Ekron rooted up, as the Chaldean inundation sweeps over those parts of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Woe unto the inhabitants of the sea coast, the nation of the Cherethites: the word of the Lord is against you, O Canaan, the land of the Philistines, and he will destroy it so that there shall be no inhabitant. The neighbouring nations, which assisted or rejoiced in Israel's calamity, shall drink of the same cup of trembling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The sea coast, emptied of its people, shall become dwellings and cottages for shepherds, and folds for flocks. What was once a populous coast of cities is reduced to pasture land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The coast shall be for the remnant of the house of Judah, who shall feed thereupon and lie down in the evening in the houses of Ashkelon. The Lord their God shall visit them and turn away their captivity, so that the judgment on their neighbours comforts the Jews with the thought that their case, though sad, is not singular. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Next come the Moabites and Ammonites, in verses 8 to 11: God has heard the reproach of Moab and the revilings of the children of Ammon, whereby they reproached his people and magnified themselves against their border. The neighbouring nations are judged for the part they took against God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Therefore, as the Lord of hosts lives, Moab shall be as Sodom and the children of Ammon as Gomorrah, a place overrun with nettles and saltpits, a perpetual desolation. The residue and remnant of God's people shall spoil them and possess their lands, in retribution for their having occupied Judah's land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. This shall they have for their pride, because they have reproached and magnified themselves against the people of the Lord of hosts. Their sin was not only against Judah, but against the Lord whose people Judah were. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The Lord will be terrible unto them, for he will famish all the gods of the earth, and men shall worship him, every one from his place, even all the isles of the heathen. The judgment on the nations thus issues in the worship of the Lord far beyond Judah's borders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. You Ethiopians also, you shall be slain by my sword: the third group in this denunciation, the Ethiopians and Assyrians in verses 12 to 15, is judged in turn. No distant nation is outside the reach of the Lord's sword. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. He will stretch out his hand against the north and destroy Assyria, and make Nineveh a desolation, as dry as the wilderness. The great imperial city that once threatened Judah is itself to be laid waste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Herds shall lie down in the midst of Nineveh, and every beast of the nation, with the cormorant and bittern lodging on the capitals of its pillars and singing in its windows. Such a heap of ruins shall this once pompous city be that wild beasts take up their abode in its houses, its fine cedar-work lies exposed, and songs of mirth are turned into hideous noises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. This is the rejoicing city that dwelt carelessly, that said in her heart, I am, and there is none beside me: now she is become a desolation, a place for beasts to lie down in. Every one that passes by shall hiss and wag his hand in derision, for the adversities of those who were insolent and haughty in prosperity are unpitied and unlamented. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • The day of the LORD. the day of God's visitation and judgment, pictured in the prophets as darkness, trumpet alarm, and distress, and also as deliverance for those who call on his name. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Nineveh. the great Assyrian city, three days' journey across, to which Jonah is sent and whose repentance spares it in Jonah 3, and whose fall Nahum announces. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Cherethites. the inhabitants of the Philistine sea coast in Zephaniah 2, the land of Canaan against which the word of the LORD is spoken. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
