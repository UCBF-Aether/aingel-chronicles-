The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 48 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Against Moab thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Woe unto Nebo! for it is spoiled: Kiriathaim is confounded and taken: Misgab is confounded and dismayed.

2 There shall be no more praise of Moab: in Heshbon they have devised evil against it; come, and let us cut it off from being a nation. Also thou shalt be cut down, O Madmen; the sword shall pursue thee.

3 A voice of crying shall be from Horonaim, spoiling and great destruction.

4 Moab is destroyed; her little ones have caused a cry to be heard.

5 For in the going up of Luhith continual weeping shall go up; for in the going down of Horonaim the enemies have heard a cry of destruction.

6 Flee, save your lives, and be like the heath in the wilderness.

7 For because thou hast trusted in thy works and in thy treasures, thou shalt also be taken: and Chemosh shall go forth into captivity with his priests and his princes together.

8 And the spoiler shall come upon every city, and no city shall escape: the valley also shall perish, and the plain shall be destroyed, as the LORD hath spoken.

9 Give wings unto Moab, that it may flee and get away: for the cities thereof shall be desolate, without any to dwell therein.

10 Cursed be he that doeth the work of the LORD deceitfully, and cursed be he that keepeth back his sword from blood.

11 Moab hath been at ease from his youth, and he hath settled on his lees, and hath not been emptied from vessel to vessel, neither hath he gone into captivity: therefore his taste remained in him, and his scent is not changed.

12 Therefore, behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will send unto him wanderers, that shall cause him to wander, and shall empty his vessels, and break their bottles.

13 And Moab shall be ashamed of Chemosh, as the house of Israel was ashamed of Bethel their confidence.

14 How say ye, We are mighty and strong men for the war?

15 Moab is spoiled, and gone up out of her cities, and his chosen young men are gone down to the slaughter, saith the King, whose name is the LORD of hosts.

16 The calamity of Moab is near to come, and his affliction hasteth fast.

17 All ye that are about him, bemoan him; and all ye that know his name, say, How is the strong staff broken, and the beautiful rod!

18 Thou daughter that dost inhabit Dibon, come down from thy glory, and sit in thirst; for the spoiler of Moab shall come upon thee, and he shall destroy thy strong holds.

19 O inhabitant of Aroer, stand by the way, and espy; ask him that fleeth, and her that escapeth, and say, What is done?

20 Moab is confounded; for it is broken down: howl and cry; tell ye it in Arnon, that Moab is spoiled,

21 And judgment is come upon the plain country; upon Holon, and upon Jahazah, and upon Mephaath,

22 And upon Dibon, and upon Nebo, and upon Bethdiblathaim,

23 And upon Kiriathaim, and upon Bethgamul, and upon Bethmeon,

24 And upon Kerioth, and upon Bozrah, and upon all the cities of the land of Moab, far or near.

25 The horn of Moab is cut off, and his arm is broken, saith the LORD.

26 Make ye him drunken: for he magnified himself against the LORD: Moab also shall wallow in his vomit, and he also shall be in derision.

27 For was not Israel a derision unto thee? was he found among thieves? for since thou spakest of him, thou skippedst for joy.

28 O ye that dwell in Moab, leave the cities, and dwell in the rock, and be like the dove that maketh her nest in the sides of the hole’s mouth.

29 We have heard the pride of Moab, (he is exceeding proud) his loftiness, and his arrogancy, and his pride, and the haughtiness of his heart.

30 I know his wrath, saith the LORD; but it shall not be so; his lies shall not so effect it.

31 Therefore will I howl for Moab, and I will cry out for all Moab; mine heart shall mourn for the men of Kirheres.

32 O vine of Sibmah, I will weep for thee with the weeping of Jazer: thy plants are gone over the sea, they reach even to the sea of Jazer: the spoiler is fallen upon thy summer fruits and upon thy vintage.

33 And joy and gladness is taken from the plentiful field, and from the land of Moab, and I have caused wine to fail from the winepresses: none shall tread with shouting; their shouting shall be no shouting.

34 From the cry of Heshbon even unto Elealeh, and even unto Jahaz, have they uttered their voice, from Zoar even unto Horonaim, as an heifer of three years old: for the waters also of Nimrim shall be desolate.

35 Moreover I will cause to cease in Moab, saith the LORD, him that offereth in the high places, and him that burneth incense to his gods.

36 Therefore mine heart shall sound for Moab like pipes, and mine heart shall sound like pipes for the men of Kirheres: because the riches that he hath gotten are perished.

37 For every head shall be bald, and every beard clipped: upon all the hands shall be cuttings, and upon the loins sackcloth.

38 There shall be lamentation generally upon all the housetops of Moab, and in the streets thereof: for I have broken Moab like a vessel wherein is no pleasure, saith the LORD.

39 They shall howl, saying, How is it broken down! how hath Moab turned the back with shame! so shall Moab be a derision and a dismaying to all them about him.

40 For thus saith the LORD; Behold, he shall fly as an eagle, and shall spread his wings over Moab.

41 Kerioth is taken, and the strong holds are surprised, and the mighty men’s hearts in Moab at that day shall be as the heart of a woman in her pangs.

42 And Moab shall be destroyed from being a people, because he hath magnified himself against the LORD.

43 Fear, and the pit, and the snare, shall be upon thee, O inhabitant of Moab, saith the LORD.

44 He that fleeth from the fear shall fall into the pit; and he that getteth up out of the pit shall be taken in the snare: for I will bring upon it, even upon Moab, the year of their visitation, saith the LORD.

45 They that fled stood under the shadow of Heshbon because of the force: but a fire shall come forth out of Heshbon, and a flame from the midst of Sihon, and shall devour the corner of Moab, and the crown of the head of the tumultuous ones.

46 Woe be unto thee, O Moab! the people of Chemosh perisheth: for thy sons are taken captives, and thy daughters captives.

47 Yet will I bring again the captivity of Moab in the latter days, saith the LORD. Thus far is the judgment of Moab.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Against Moab the chapter moves city by city — Nebo, Kiriathaim, Heshbon, Dibon, and many more — charging Moab with pride, with trust in its works and treasures, and with wine left too long on its lees. Yet it ends, as several of these oracles do, with a latter-days promise: the captivity of Moab will be brought again. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry notes Moab is set to the bar before Jeremiah, whom God constituted judge over nations, and the author of its destruction is the LORD of hosts, who has all armies at his command, and the God of Israel, pleading Israel's cause against a people always vexatious to them, though Israel had been forbidden to meddle with Moab, so the destruction is called the work of the LORD and will exactly agree with his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry explains there shall be no more praise or vaunting in Moab concerning Heshbon, for the invaders have devised evil against that principal city and aim at nothing less than cutting Moab off from being a nation, not merely plundering it, while Madmen too shall be cut down with the sword pursuing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry notes the voice of crying from Horonaim is nothing but spoiling and great destruction, a cry so great it cannot be hid, so that even the enemies hear it and are animated by it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry notes that when the great ones have quitted the cities to shift for their own safety, even the little ones have caused a cry to be heard, the meaner sort or the little children, whose cries at such a time are the most piteous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry pictures continual weeping with weeping on the going up of Luhith and the going down of Horonaim, where all are in tears and none have dry eyes, as sorrow meets the traveller on hill and in valley alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry notes the great hurry of flight: flee, save your lives, shifting for safety with all imaginable speed, though escaping as bare and naked as the heath or dry shrub in the wilderness, thinking not of carrying away anything, for attempting it may cost life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry traces the ruin to carnal confidence: Moab trusted in its works, the fortifications it took pains to build, and in its treasures, the exchequer and private coffers it filled, thinking itself in as good a posture for war as any people and that none durst invade it, so God will send an enemy to master its works and rifle its treasures, and Chemosh himself, with his priests and princes together, shall go into captivity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry stresses the universality: the spoiler shall come upon every city and no city shall escape, the strongest unable to secure itself against the enemy's power nor the finest to recommend itself to his pity, and the valley shall perish and the plain be destroyed, corn and flocks eaten up, trodden down, or carried off, exactly as the LORD has spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry notes that giving wings to Moab would be the greatest kindness, since unless they can fly there will be no escaping, and the cities shall be desolate without any to dwell therein, with no houses, no people, or no safety and ease for those who would dwell in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry applies the curse generally: cursed is he who does the work of the LORD deceitfully or negligently, pretending to do it but not to purpose, serving his own ends, or slothful in God's business, and here the Chaldeans, charged by a secret instinct to destroy the Moabites, must not out of foolish pity keep back their sword from blood, lest like Saul sparing Amalek or Ahab letting Benhadad go they bring a sword and curse upon themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry explains Moab's long ease by the wine image: Moab has been at ease from his youth, an ancient kingdom enjoying tranquillity though small and surrounded by potent neighbours, never emptied from vessel to vessel, but settled on his lees like wine never racked off, retaining its strength and body, and because it had no changes of peace and prosperity, its heart and life remained unchanged and it feared not God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry notes the reversal of the wine image: God will send wanderers who seem as vagrants themselves but shall cause the Moabites really to wander, emptying his vessels and breaking their bottles, so the long undisturbed nation shall at last know unsettling change. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry notes Moab shall be ashamed of Chemosh, ashamed of all prayers made to and confidence put in that dunghill deity, as Israel was ashamed of Bethel, the golden calf confided in as protector but unable to save them from the Assyrians, and Henry draws the rule that those not convinced of idolatry's folly by God's word shall be convinced by God's judgments when they find by woeful experience their gods can do them no service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry notes the surprised boast, how say ye, we are mighty and strong men for the war, is answered by the suddenness of the calamity: Moab thought itself strong and able to deal with the most powerful enemy, yet the affliction hastens fast and will soon come to a crisis, and the enemy cannot be kept long in parley. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry stresses the utter ruin: Moab is spoiled, quite spoiled, its cities laid in ashes or seized so the people are forced to quit them, and his chosen young men, the strength and beauty of the kingdom who went down to battle promising themselves victory, have gone down to the slaughter, so sure are those to fall against whom God fights, spoken by the King whose name is the LORD of hosts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry notes the calamity is near to come and the affliction hastens fast, so that Moab has not time to recollect considerations that might animate it, and it requires more than ordinary courage not to be afraid of sudden fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry calls neighbours to mourn: all about him and all who know his name should say, how is the strong staff broken, whose strength was the terror of enemies, and the beautiful rod, whose beauty was the pride of friends, so nations may learn not to be puffed up with or put confidence in strength or beauty, since neither secures against God's judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry pictures Dibon's daughter, who sat in glory amid wealth, mirth, and pleasure, coming down to sit in thirst in a dry land where no water and no comfort is, inuring herself to hardship once the spoiler has stripped her and destroyed her strong holds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry notes the inquisitive fear of remote corners: the inhabitant of Aroer stands by the way and asks him that flees and her that escapes, what is done, and when told all is gone and the invader is conqueror, they will howl and cry in bitterness of spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry notes Moab is confounded because broken down, and the command to tell it in Arnon, that Moab is spoiled, spreads the lamentation along the border river where the news of ruin travels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry notes judgment has come upon the plain country, upon Holon, Jahazah, and Mephaath, and the list of cities that follows concludes with an et cetera, since when iniquity is universal there is reason to expect calamity should be universal too, coming upon all the cities of Moab, far and near. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry applies the same universal judgment to Dibon, named again among the plain-country cities upon which judgment has come, showing no city, however often named or formerly secure, is exempt from the general spoiling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry applies the same universal judgment to Kiriathaim, Beth-gamul, and Beth-meon, whose naming shows the roll of threatened cities deliberately covering the breadth of Moab, far and near. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry applies the same universal judgment to Kerioth and Bozrah and to all the cities of the land of Moab, far or near, completing the plain-country list with the general clause that leaves no city outside the sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry notes the horn of Moab is cut off, the horn of its strength and power both offensive and defensive, and his arm is broken, so he can neither give a blow nor prevent a blow, as the kingdom is deprived of dignity and authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry explains the drunkenness image: Moab is made drunk with the cup of God's wrath until he staggers, wallows in his vomit, and becomes an odious spectacle in derision, intoxicated till brought to his wits' end and made ridiculous by the wildness of his passions and counsels, because he magnified himself against the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry notes Moab's scorn toward Israel: when the Jews fell into calamity, the generality of Moabites, instead of lamenting, rejoiced and skipped for joy, industriously proclaiming their joy and triumphing over every distressed Israelite, which Henry calls as inhuman as impious, and an affront both to man, whose nature they shared, and to God, whose name Israel was called by. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry pictures the mourning-bird retreat: the dwellers in Moab leave the cities once full of mirth and dwell in the rock, like the dove making her nest in the sides of the hole's mouth, no longer singing birds but mourning birds, since God can soon change the note of those given up to mirth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry notes pride is mentioned six times in this verse, with multiplied words, loftiness, arrogancy, pride, and haughtiness of heart, intimating how many instances discovered it and how offensive it was to God and man, and that Moab, charged with it already in Isaiah, had grown more arrogant under humbling providences, marking it for the utter destruction of which pride is the forerunner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 30. Henry explains God's knowledge of Moab's wrath: Moab made a jest of Judah's desolations, pretending it was but sport, but God knows it comes from old enmity to Abraham's seed and the worshippers of the true God, and that Moab irritates the Chaldeans against the Jews hoping for their extirpation, yet it shall not be so, and his lies shall not effect it, since the nation whose fall they triumph in shall recover itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 31. Henry notes the prophet himself laments at the very foresight: he will howl for Moab and his heart shall mourn for the men of Kir-heres, though the destruction would prove him a true prophet, because the ruin of sinners is no pleasure to God and should be a pain to us, and these passages echo Isaiah's Moab prophecies, dictated by the same Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 32. Henry notes the vine of Sibmah wept with the weeping of Jazer, its plants gone over the sea, carried into other countries to be planted there, while the spoiler has fallen upon its summer fruits and upon its vintage, which is what makes the cry of Heshbon reach even to Elealeh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 33. Henry explains that Moab's joy was the delights of sense, pleasant fruits and rich wines, so when joy and gladness are taken from the plentiful field and wine fails from the winepresses once trodden with shouting, their shouting shall be no shouting, and those who make sense-delights their chief joy subject themselves to the greatest grief when easily deprived of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 34. Henry notes the cry stretches from Heshbon to Elealeh and Jahaz, and from Zoar to Horonaim, loud like the lowing of a heifer of three years old, borrowed from Isaiah, and even the waters of Nimrim shall be desolate, so Moab loses water as well as wine and its grief grows extravagantly loud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 35. Henry notes the ending of false worship itself: God will cause to cease him who offers in the high places and burns incense to his gods, for the high places shall be destroyed, the fields of offerings laid waste, and the priests slain or carried into captivity, and Henry adds that only true religion and the worship of the true God will stand in stead in a day of trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 36. Henry notes the prophet's heart sounds like pipes for Moab and for the men of Kir-heres, because the riches he had gotten are perished, though he thought he had laid them up safely for long enjoyment, and Henry observes hoarded money is as liable to perishing as summer fruits in the open field, riches slipping through fingers even when gripped hard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 37. Henry notes the desperate grief expressed in bald heads, clipped beards, cuttings upon the hands, and sackcloth upon the loins, and cautions that while Job rent his mantle and shaved his head he did not cut himself, so wisdom and grace must set bounds to the flood of passion and restrain it from such barbarities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 38. Henry notes the lamentation is universal, upon all the housetops of Moab, where they worshipped idols to whom they shall in vain bemoan themselves, and in all the streets where they conversed, because God has broken Moab like a vessel wherein is no pleasure, which shall not be regarded and cannot be pieced again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 39. Henry notes Moab shall howl, how is it broken down, how has Moab turned the back with shame, and so shall be a derision and a dismaying to all about him, who shall laugh at the fall of the pomp and power he was so proud of, since the haughty prepare reproach for themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 40. Henry stresses the swiftness and encompassing power of the enemy: he shall fly as an eagle upon his prey and spread the wings of his army over Moab, surrounding it so none may escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 41. Henry notes Kerioth is taken and the strong holds surprised, so all Moab's strength stands it in no stead, and the hearts of even its mighty men shall be as the heart of a woman in her pangs, failing for want of time to recollect animating considerations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 42. Henry gives the stated reason for destruction from being a people: Moab magnified himself against the LORD, preferring Chemosh before Jehovah and thinking himself a match for the God of Israel, whom he set at defiance, so those who are enemies to God's people will soon be made no people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 43. Henry explains the complicated destruction: fear, pit, and snare shall be upon Moab, fear driving them into the pit and a snare holding them fast in it, so they neither escape from destruction nor out of it, as Isaiah's general word about sinners is particularly foretold of Moab. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 44. Henry notes the sequence in the year of visitation: he who flees from the fear shall fall into the pit, and he who gets up out of the pit shall be taken in the snare, for God comes to reckon with them and will be known by the judgments he executes as the King whose name is the LORD of hosts, able to do what he has determined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 45. Henry explains the figure by the Heshbon instance: those who fled put themselves under the shadow of Heshbon, supposing they stood safely as armies retire under a fortified city's cannon, but Heshbon devours them, as Moses foretold, with fire out of Heshbon and flame from Sihon devouring the corner of Moab and the crown of the head of the tumultuous ones, meaning the blustering great men on whom God's judgments shall light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 46. Henry sums the chapter's conclusion: woe be to thee, O Moab, thou art undone, the people of Chemosh perish and are gone, and thy sons and daughters, the hopes of the next generation, have gone into captivity after the Jews whose calamities they rejoiced in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 47. Henry closes on comfort: yet the destruction is not perpetual, for God who brings Moab into captivity will bring again its captivity in the latter days, dealing tenderly even with Moabites and not contending for ever, and Henry adds it looks further to gospel times, when the captivity of the Gentiles under sin and Satan shall be brought back by divine grace, so this long prophecy ends comfortably with, thus far is the judgment of Moab. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

Henry stresses the utter ruin: Moab is spoiled, quite spoiled, its cities laid in ashes or seized so the people are forced to quit them, and his chosen young men, the strength and beauty of the kingdom who went down to battle promising themselves victory, have gone down to the slaughter, so sure are those to fall against whom God fights, spoken by the King whose name is the LORD of hosts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry notes the calamity is near to come and the affliction hastens fast, so that Moab has not time to recollect considerations that might animate it, and it requires more than ordinary courage not to be afraid of sudden fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry calls neighbours to mourn: all about him and all who know his name should say, how is the strong staff broken, whose strength was the terror of enemies, and the beautiful rod, whose beauty was the pride of friends, so nations may learn not to be puffed up with or put confidence in strength or beauty, since neither secures against God's judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry pictures Dibon's daughter, who sat in glory amid wealth, mirth, and pleasure, coming down to sit in thirst in a dry land where no water and no comfort is, inuring herself to hardship once the spoiler has stripped her and destroyed her strong holds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry notes the inquisitive fear of remote corners: the inhabitant of Aroer stands by the way and asks him that flees and her that escapes, what is done, and when told all is gone and the invader is conqueror, they will howl and cry in bitterness of spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry notes Moab is confounded because broken down, and the command to tell it in Arnon, that Moab is spoiled, spreads the lamentation along the border river where the news of ruin travels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry notes judgment has come upon the plain country, upon Holon, Jahazah, and Mephaath, and the list of cities that follows concludes with an et cetera, since when iniquity is universal there is reason to expect calamity should be universal too, coming upon all the cities of Moab, far and near. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry applies the same universal judgment to Dibon, named again among the plain-country cities upon which judgment has come, showing no city, however often named or formerly secure, is exempt from the general spoiling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry applies the same universal judgment to Kiriathaim, Beth-gamul, and Beth-meon, whose naming shows the roll of threatened cities deliberately covering the breadth of Moab, far and near. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry applies the same universal judgment to Kerioth and Bozrah and to all the cities of the land of Moab, far or near, completing the plain-country list with the general clause that leaves no city outside the sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry notes the horn of Moab is cut off, the horn of its strength and power both offensive and defensive, and his arm is broken, so he can neither give a blow nor prevent a blow, as the kingdom is deprived of dignity and authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry explains the drunkenness image: Moab is made drunk with the cup of God's wrath until he staggers, wallows in his vomit, and becomes an odious spectacle in derision, intoxicated till brought to his wits' end and made ridiculous by the wildness of his passions and counsels, because he magnified himself against the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry notes Moab's scorn toward Israel: when the Jews fell into calamity, the generality of Moabites, instead of lamenting, rejoiced and skipped for joy, industriously proclaiming their joy and triumphing over every distressed Israelite, which Henry calls as inhuman as impious, and an affront both to man, whose nature they shared, and to God, whose name Israel was called by. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry pictures the mourning-bird retreat: the dwellers in Moab leave the cities once full of mirth and dwell in the rock, like the dove making her nest in the sides of the hole's mouth, no longer singing birds but mourning birds, since God can soon change the note of those given up to mirth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry notes pride is mentioned six times in this verse, with multiplied words, loftiness, arrogancy, pride, and haughtiness of heart, intimating how many instances discovered it and how offensive it was to God and man, and that Moab, charged with it already in Isaiah, had grown more arrogant under humbling providences, marking it for the utter destruction of which pride is the forerunner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 30. Henry explains God's knowledge of Moab's wrath: Moab made a jest of Judah's desolations, pretending it was but sport, but God knows it comes from old enmity to Abraham's seed and the worshippers of the true God, and that Moab irritates the Chaldeans against the Jews hoping for their extirpation, yet it shall not be so, and his lies shall not effect it, since the nation whose fall they triumph in shall recover itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 31. Henry notes the prophet himself laments at the very foresight: he will howl for Moab and his heart shall mourn for the men of Kir-heres, though the destruction would prove him a true prophet, because the ruin of sinners is no pleasure to God and should be a pain to us, and these passages echo Isaiah's Moab prophecies, dictated by the same Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 32. Henry notes the vine of Sibmah wept with the weeping of Jazer, its plants gone over the sea, carried into other countries to be planted there, while the spoiler has fallen upon its summer fruits and upon its vintage, which is what makes the cry of Heshbon reach even to Elealeh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 33. Henry explains that Moab's joy was the delights of sense, pleasant fruits and rich wines, so when joy and gladness are taken from the plentiful field and wine fails from the winepresses once trodden with shouting, their shouting shall be no shouting, and those who make sense-delights their chief joy subject themselves to the greatest grief when easily deprived of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 34. Henry notes the cry stretches from Heshbon to Elealeh and Jahaz, and from Zoar to Horonaim, loud like the lowing of a heifer of three years old, borrowed from Isaiah, and even the waters of Nimrim shall be desolate, so Moab loses water as well as wine and its grief grows extravagantly loud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 35. Henry notes the ending of false worship itself: God will cause to cease him who offers in the high places and burns incense to his gods, for the high places shall be destroyed, the fields of offerings laid waste, and the priests slain or carried into captivity, and Henry adds that only true religion and the worship of the true God will stand in stead in a day of trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 36. Henry notes the prophet's heart sounds like pipes for Moab and for the men of Kir-heres, because the riches he had gotten are perished, though he thought he had laid them up safely for long enjoyment, and Henry observes hoarded money is as liable to perishing as summer fruits in the open field, riches slipping through fingers even when gripped hard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 37. Henry notes the desperate grief expressed in bald heads, clipped beards, cuttings upon the hands, and sackcloth upon the loins, and cautions that while Job rent his mantle and shaved his head he did not cut himself, so wisdom and grace must set bounds to the flood of passion and restrain it from such barbarities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 38. Henry notes the lamentation is universal, upon all the housetops of Moab, where they worshipped idols to whom they shall in vain bemoan themselves, and in all the streets where they conversed, because God has broken Moab like a vessel wherein is no pleasure, which shall not be regarded and cannot be pieced again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 39. Henry notes Moab shall howl, how is it broken down, how has Moab turned the back with shame, and so shall be a derision and a dismaying to all about him, who shall laugh at the fall of the pomp and power he was so proud of, since the haughty prepare reproach for themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 40. Henry stresses the swiftness and encompassing power of the enemy: he shall fly as an eagle upon his prey and spread the wings of his army over Moab, surrounding it so none may escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 41. Henry notes Kerioth is taken and the strong holds surprised, so all Moab's strength stands it in no stead, and the hearts of even its mighty men shall be as the heart of a woman in her pangs, failing for want of time to recollect animating considerations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 42. Henry gives the stated reason for destruction from being a people: Moab magnified himself against the LORD, preferring Chemosh before Jehovah and thinking himself a match for the God of Israel, whom he set at defiance, so those who are enemies to God's people will soon be made no people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 43. Henry explains the complicated destruction: fear, pit, and snare shall be upon Moab, fear driving them into the pit and a snare holding them fast in it, so they neither escape from destruction nor out of it, as Isaiah's general word about sinners is particularly foretold of Moab. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 44. Henry notes the sequence in the year of visitation: he who flees from the fear shall fall into the pit, and he who gets up out of the pit shall be taken in the snare, for God comes to reckon with them and will be known by the judgments he executes as the King whose name is the LORD of hosts, able to do what he has determined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 45. Henry explains the figure by the Heshbon instance: those who fled put themselves under the shadow of Heshbon, supposing they stood safely as armies retire under a fortified city's cannon, but Heshbon devours them, as Moses foretold, with fire out of Heshbon and flame from Sihon devouring the corner of Moab and the crown of the head of the tumultuous ones, meaning the blustering great men on whom God's judgments shall light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 46. Henry sums the chapter's conclusion: woe be to thee, O Moab, thou art undone, the people of Chemosh perish and are gone, and thy sons and daughters, the hopes of the next generation, have gone into captivity after the Jews whose calamities they rejoiced in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 47. Henry closes on comfort: yet the destruction is not perpetual, for God who brings Moab into captivity will bring again its captivity in the latter days, dealing tenderly even with Moabites and not contending for ever, and Henry adds it looks further to gospel times, when the captivity of the Gentiles under sin and Satan shall be brought back by divine grace, so this long prophecy ends comfortably with, thus far is the judgment of Moab. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
