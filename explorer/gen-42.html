The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 42 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now when Jacob saw that there was corn in Egypt, Jacob said unto his sons, Why do ye look one upon another?

2 And he said, Behold, I have heard that there is corn in Egypt: get you down thither, and buy for us from thence; that we may live, and not die.

3 And Joseph’s ten brethren went down to buy corn in Egypt.

4 But Benjamin, Joseph’s brother, Jacob sent not with his brethren; for he said, Lest peradventure mischief befall him.

5 And the sons of Israel came to buy corn among those that came: for the famine was in the land of Canaan.

6 And Joseph was the governor over the land, and he it was that sold to all the people of the land: and Joseph’s brethren came, and bowed down themselves before him with their faces to the earth.

7 And Joseph saw his brethren, and he knew them, but made himself strange unto them, and spake roughly unto them; and he said unto them, Whence come ye? And they said, From the land of Canaan to buy food.

8 And Joseph knew his brethren, but they knew not him.

9 And Joseph remembered the dreams which he dreamed of them, and said unto them, Ye are spies; to see the nakedness of the land ye are come.

10 And they said unto him, Nay, my lord, but to buy food are thy servants come.

11 We are all one man’s sons; we are true men, thy servants are no spies.

12 And he said unto them, Nay, but to see the nakedness of the land ye are come.

13 And they said, Thy servants are twelve brethren, the sons of one man in the land of Canaan; and, behold, the youngest is this day with our father, and one is not.

14 And Joseph said unto them, That is it that I spake unto you, saying, Ye are spies:

15 Hereby ye shall be proved: By the life of Pharaoh ye shall not go forth hence, except your youngest brother come hither.

16 Send one of you, and let him fetch your brother, and ye shall be kept in prison, that your words may be proved, whether there be any truth in you: or else by the life of Pharaoh surely ye are spies.

17 And he put them all together into ward three days.

18 And Joseph said unto them the third day, This do, and live; for I fear God:

19 If ye be true men, let one of your brethren be bound in the house of your prison: go ye, carry corn for the famine of your houses:

20 But bring your youngest brother unto me; so shall your words be verified, and ye shall not die. And they did so.

21 And they said one to another, We are verily guilty concerning our brother, in that we saw the anguish of his soul, when he besought us, and we would not hear; therefore is this distress come upon us.

22 And Reuben answered them, saying, Spake I not unto you, saying, Do not sin against the child; and ye would not hear? therefore, behold, also his blood is required.

23 And they knew not that Joseph understood them; for he spake unto them by an interpreter.

24 And he turned himself about from them, and wept; and returned to them again, and communed with them, and took from them Simeon, and bound him before their eyes.

25 Then Joseph commanded to fill their sacks with corn, and to restore every man’s money into his sack, and to give them provision for the way: and thus did he unto them.

26 And they laded their asses with the corn, and departed thence.

27 And as one of them opened his sack to give his ass provender in the inn, he espied his money; for, behold, it was in his sack’s mouth.

28 And he said unto his brethren, My money is restored; and, lo, it is even in my sack: and their heart failed them, and they were afraid, saying one to another, What is this that God hath done unto us?

29 And they came unto Jacob their father unto the land of Canaan, and told him all that befell unto them; saying,

30 The man, who is the lord of the land, spake roughly to us, and took us for spies of the country.

31 And we said unto him, We are true men; we are no spies:

32 We be twelve brethren, sons of our father; one is not, and the youngest is this day with our father in the land of Canaan.

33 And the man, the lord of the country, said unto us, Hereby shall I know that ye are true men; leave one of your brethren here with me, and take food for the famine of your households, and be gone:

34 And bring your youngest brother unto me: then shall I know that ye are no spies, but that ye are true men: so will I deliver you your brother, and ye shall traffick in the land.

35 And it came to pass as they emptied their sacks, that, behold, every man’s bundle of money was in his sack: and when both they and their father saw the bundles of money, they were afraid.

36 And Jacob their father said unto them, Me have ye bereaved of my children: Joseph is not, and Simeon is not, and ye will take Benjamin away: all these things are against me.

37 And Reuben spake unto his father, saying, Slay my two sons, if I bring him not to thee: deliver him into my hand, and I will bring him to thee again.

38 And he said, My son shall not go down with you; for his brother is dead, and he is left alone: if mischief befall him by the way in the which ye go, then shall ye bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to the grave.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Driven by famine, Jacob's sons go to Egypt for grain and bow before the governor they do not recognise: Joseph. He tests them as spies, holds Simeon, and demands Benjamin, sending them home with their money returned in their sacks and Jacob refusing to let Benjamin go. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Jacob learns from common rumour that there is corn in Egypt, and his household, already worn down by scarcity, must use the natural means within reach rather than wait for a miracle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Jacob sends his sons down to buy corn so the family may live and not die, turning the famine into a journey that will unexpectedly bring them face to face with Joseph. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Ten brothers go down to buy corn, a full company of the family acting together, which makes their later bowing before Joseph a fulfilment involving them all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Benjamin is kept at home because Jacob fears harm may befall him, showing that the loss of Joseph still shapes the father's care for Rachel's remaining son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The same failed rains that dried Egypt also struck Canaan, so caravans pour across the desert for grain, and Israel's sons must join a journey full of painful associations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Joseph, at the height of his power, directs the sales himself, likely overseeing the border storehouses where he may have expected messengers from his father's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Joseph knows his brothers at once while they do not know him, since he was a lad at parting and now wears official robes, and he speaks roughly to draw out news of his family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. That they do not recognise him is no wonder: they see a governor, not the boy they sold, while he has ten faces to judge by and has long expected them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Joseph remembers his dreams and charges them as spies come to see the land's naked, unfortified places, a charge that tests them and begins to humble them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The brothers protest they came only to buy food, insisting they are honest men, which sets up Joseph's demand for proof of their story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They claim to be true men, not spies, using honesty as their defence, though Joseph's test will probe whether their conduct now matches their words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Joseph repeats the spy charge, holding his stern ruler's part, not from vindictiveness but to press them toward honesty about themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The brothers explain that one brother is not, a phrase for one cut off from the living, and that the youngest remains with their father, unknowingly telling Joseph his own story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Joseph answers that they are still spies, keeping the pressure on so that the test he is about to impose cannot be evaded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Joseph swears by Pharaoh's life, in Egyptian court style, and makes their release depend on bringing Benjamin, the proof that will test their truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. One brother is to be sent for Benjamin while the rest wait, so their words can be proved and their honesty put to a real test. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. All are put in ward for three days, a confinement designed to bring them to reflection, and it works, as conscience begins to speak. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. On the third day Joseph softens the terms, saying they shall live if they act honestly, since he fears God, and the governor's mercy begins to show through his severity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. One brother is to stay bound while the rest carry corn home, so the family's hunger is met at the same time as the test is held in place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. They must bring Benjamin down, and only then will their words be verified and they escape death, making Benjamin the key to the whole trial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The brothers confess to one another that they are guilty concerning their brother, reading their present distress as the just measure of what they once did to Joseph. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Reuben reminds them he warned them not to sin against the lad, and now his blood is required, so even the brother who tried to save Joseph shares the guilt of the company. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. They speak freely before Joseph because an interpreter stands between them, not knowing he understands every word of their remorse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Joseph turns away to weep, then takes Simeon and binds him before their eyes, choosing the brother who may have been the most violent against him as the hostage for their return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Joseph fills their sacks with corn and secretly returns each man's money, a private generosity he could afford from his own purse, meant as kindness, not fraud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. They load their asses and set out by the desert route toward Hebron, carrying the corn home while the hostage and the test remain behind them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. At a lodging place one brother finds his money in his sack, and the discovery throws them into greater perplexity, since it gives the governor a new handle against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Their hearts fail and they tremble, asking what God has done to them, so that even an apparent kindness is read as a judgment from heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Back in Canaan they tell Jacob all that befell them, and Henry notes how the report, money and all, troubled a father who already distrusted sons he had not seen with Joseph since. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. They report the lord of the land speaking roughly and taking them for spies, repeating the charge exactly as Joseph had pressed it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. They repeat their protest that they are true men, not spies, carrying their defence home to their father as they had made it in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. They repeat the account of twelve brothers, one not, and the youngest with their father, so Jacob hears again the painful tally of his family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. They report the governor's terms, one brother left behind and Benjamin to be brought, which is the condition Jacob must now weigh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. They report the promise that if Benjamin comes they may trade and the bound brother will be restored, so the way home for Simeon runs through Benjamin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Emptying their sacks, every man's money is found, and father and sons alike are afraid, since innocence does not remove the danger of how it will look. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. Jacob cries that he is bereaved, Joseph not, Simeon not, and Benjamin now threatened, concluding all things are against him, though Henry notes they were in fact working for his good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Reuben offers his two sons as pledge for Benjamin's return, a thoughtless and unwarrantable bargain meant only to assure his father of the utmost care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. Jacob refuses to let Benjamin go down, fearing that calamity on the way would bring his grey hairs down with sorrow to the grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
