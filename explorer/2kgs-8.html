The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then spake Elisha unto the woman, whose son he had restored to life, saying, Arise, and go thou and thine household, and sojourn wheresoever thou canst sojourn: for the LORD hath called for a famine; and it shall also come upon the land seven years.

2 And the woman arose, and did after the saying of the man of God: and she went with her household, and sojourned in the land of the Philistines seven years.

3 And it came to pass at the seven years’ end, that the woman returned out of the land of the Philistines: and she went forth to cry unto the king for her house and for her land.

4 And the king talked with Gehazi the servant of the man of God, saying, Tell me, I pray thee, all the great things that Elisha hath done.

5 And it came to pass, as he was telling the king how he had restored a dead body to life, that, behold, the woman, whose son he had restored to life, cried to the king for her house and for her land. And Gehazi said, My lord, O king, this is the woman, and this is her son, whom Elisha restored to life.

6 And when the king asked the woman, she told him. So the king appointed unto her a certain officer, saying, Restore all that was hers, and all the fruits of the field since the day that she left the land, even until now.

7 And Elisha came to Damascus; and Benhadad the king of Syria was sick; and it was told him, saying, The man of God is come hither.

8 And the king said unto Hazael, Take a present in thine hand, and go, meet the man of God, and enquire of the LORD by him, saying, Shall I recover of this disease?

9 So Hazael went to meet him, and took a present with him, even of every good thing of Damascus, forty camels’ burden, and came and stood before him, and said, Thy son Benhadad king of Syria hath sent me to thee, saying, Shall I recover of this disease?

10 And Elisha said unto him, Go, say unto him, Thou mayest certainly recover: howbeit the LORD hath shewed me that he shall surely die.

11 And he settled his countenance stedfastly, until he was ashamed: and the man of God wept.

12 And Hazael said, Why weepeth my lord? And he answered, Because I know the evil that thou wilt do unto the children of Israel: their strong holds wilt thou set on fire, and their young men wilt thou slay with the sword, and wilt dash their children, and rip up their women with child.

13 And Hazael said, But what, is thy servant a dog, that he should do this great thing? And Elisha answered, The LORD hath shewed me that thou shalt be king over Syria.

14 So he departed from Elisha, and came to his master; who said to him, What said Elisha to thee? And he answered, He told me that thou shouldest surely recover.

15 And it came to pass on the morrow, that he took a thick cloth, and dipped it in water, and spread it on his face, so that he died: and Hazael reigned in his stead.

16 And in the fifth year of Joram the son of Ahab king of Israel, Jehoshaphat being then king of Judah, Jehoram the son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah began to reign.

17 Thirty and two years old was he when he began to reign; and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.

18 And he walked in the way of the kings of Israel, as did the house of Ahab: for the daughter of Ahab was his wife: and he did evil in the sight of the LORD.

19 Yet the LORD would not destroy Judah for David his servant’s sake, as he promised him to give him alway a light, and to his children.

20 In his days Edom revolted from under the hand of Judah, and made a king over themselves.

21 So Joram went over to Zair, and all the chariots with him: and he rose by night, and smote the Edomites which compassed him about, and the captains of the chariots: and the people fled into their tents.

22 Yet Edom revolted from under the hand of Judah unto this day. Then Libnah revolted at the same time.

23 And the rest of the acts of Joram, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

24 And Joram slept with his fathers, and was buried with his fathers in the city of David: and Ahaziah his son reigned in his stead.

25 In the twelfth year of Joram the son of Ahab king of Israel did Ahaziah the son of Jehoram king of Judah begin to reign.

26 Two and twenty years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign; and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Athaliah, the daughter of Omri king of Israel.

27 And he walked in the way of the house of Ahab, and did evil in the sight of the LORD, as did the house of Ahab: for he was the son in law of the house of Ahab.

28 And he went with Joram the son of Ahab to the war against Hazael king of Syria in Ramothgilead; and the Syrians wounded Joram.

29 And king Joram went back to be healed in Jezreel of the wounds which the Syrians had given him at Ramah, when he fought against Hazael king of Syria. And Ahaziah the son of Jehoram king of Judah went down to see Joram the son of Ahab in Jezreel, because he was sick.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Elisha warns the Shunammite of seven years' famine and she sojourns in the land of the Philistines, returning to recover her house and land through Gehazi's timely tale to the king. In Damascus Elisha tells Hazael he will be king and do great evil to Israel, and Hazael murders Ben-hadad; the chapter closes with Judah's kings Jehoram and Ahaziah walking in Ahab's ways. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Elisha had spoken to the Shunammite, telling her to arise and sojourn wherever she could, for the LORD had called for a famine of seven years. JFB notes this direction probably preceded the events of chapters 5 and 6, and that such calamities are chastisements from God's hand, this famine being double Elijah's in length because Israel remained obdurate under Elisha's ministry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The woman sojourned in the land of the Philistines seven years. JFB notes that territory was recommended by its closeness to her home, its reduced state lessening the risk of idolatry's seductions, and many Jews and Israelites residing there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. At the seven years' end she returned and cried to the king for her house and land. JFB explains that during her long absence her possessions had been occupied by kindred or confiscated by the crown, an innovation with no statute in Moses' law ordaining such alienation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The king was talking with Gehazi, asking him to tell all Elisha's great things. JFB notes ceremonial pollution came by contact alone, so a conference with the leper at a distance, perhaps at the gate or in a royal garden, was possible, and providence had led Jehoram to inquire with great interest into Elisha's deeds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Just as Gehazi was telling the king how Elisha had restored a dead body to life, the very woman whose son he had restored appeared to cry for her house. JFB stresses providence ordained this timing, so the king was pleased to grant her request. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. When the king asked the woman, she told him, and he appointed an officer to restore all that was hers with all the fruits of the field since she left. JFB notes a state officer was charged to afford her every facility in recovering her family possession from its occupier. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Elisha came to Damascus while Benhadad king of Syria was sick, and the king was told the man of God had come. JFB explains Elisha was directed there by the Spirit in pursuance of the mission given to Elijah at Horeb, to anoint Hazael king of Syria. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The king told Hazael to take a present and meet the man of God, inquiring of the LORD whether he would recover from his disease. JFB notes Benhadad, following heathen practice in consulting soothsayers, ordered a liberal present in remuneration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Hazael went with a present of every good thing of Damascus, forty camels' burden, calling Benhadad Elisha's son and asking whether he would recover. JFB notes the present was liberal and magnificent, though Orientals fond of display would lay on forty beasts what four could bear, and "son" was the established usage of calling the prophet "father." Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Elisha told Hazael to say to Benhadad, "Thou mayest certainly recover," yet added that the LORD had showed him Benhadad would surely die. JFB explains there was no contradiction: the first part answered Benhadad's inquiry, while the second was intended for Hazael, who artfully reported only what suited his views. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Elisha settled his countenance stedfastly until Hazael was ashamed, and the man of God wept. JFB explains the prophet's penetrating look convinced Hazael his secret designs were known, and Elisha's deep emotion was justified by the horrible atrocities the usurper would commit in Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. When Hazael asked why his lord wept, Elisha answered because he knew the evil Hazael would do to Israel's children: dashing their children, ripping up their women with child, and burning their strong holds. JFB links this grief to the atrocities, too common in ancient warfare, that Hazael as usurper would commit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Hazael protested, "What, is thy servant a dog, that he should do this great thing?" and Elisha answered the LORD had showed him Hazael would be king over Syria. JFB notes the prophet's steadfast look had already convinced Hazael that his secret designs were known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Hazael returned to his master, who asked what Elisha had said, and Hazael answered that Elisha had said he would certainly recover. JFB notes Hazael, like an artful and ambitious courtier, reported only as much of the prophet's statement as suited his own views. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. On the morrow Hazael took a thick cloth, dipped it in water, and spread it on Benhadad's face, so that he died, and Hazael reigned in his stead. JFB explains the coverlet was a thick quilt whose weight when steeped in water could accomplish murder without marks of violence, though some suppose Hazael may have spread it as a refreshing remedy, and the king's rapid death and burial favoured Hazael's instant elevation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. In the fifth year of Joram son of Ahab king of Israel, Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah began to reign. JFB notes his father had resigned the throne to him two years before his own death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Jehoram walked in the way of the kings of Israel, as did the house of Ahab, for Ahab's daughter was his wife, and he did evil in the LORD's sight. JFB identifies the daughter as Athaliah, through whose influence Jehoram introduced Baal worship and many other evils into Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Yet the LORD would not destroy Judah for David his servant's sake, as he had promised him a light always for his children. JFB notes this apostasy would have led to the total extinction of Judah's royal family had it not been for the divine promise to David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. In Jehoram's days Edom revolted from under Judah's hand and made a king over themselves. JFB explains Edom, hitherto governed by a tributary ruler, now erected the standard of independence, a national chastisement on Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Joram went over to Zair with all his chariots, rose by night, and smote the Edomites who compassed him and the captains of the chariots, though the people fled to their tents. JFB's account of the Edomite revolt frames this night action as Judah's failed attempt to recover its tributary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Yet Edom revolted from under Judah's hand to this day, and Libnah revolted at the same time. JFB presents this lasting independence as the chastisement inflicted on Judah for Jehoram's apostasy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Joram slept with his fathers and was buried with them in the city of David, and Ahaziah his son reigned in his stead. JFB heads this section "Ahaziah succeeds him," marking the succession that closes Jehoram's wicked reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
