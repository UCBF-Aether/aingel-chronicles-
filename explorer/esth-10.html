The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Esther 10 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–10; King James Version, Apocrypha (the Rest of Esther) for the Greek additions)

1 And the king Ahasuerus laid a tribute upon the land, and upon the isles of the sea.

2 And all the acts of his power and of his might, and the declaration of the greatness of Mordecai, whereunto the king advanced him, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia?

3 For Mordecai the Jew was next unto king Ahasuerus, and great among the Jews, and accepted of the multitude of his brethren, seeking the wealth of his people, and speaking peace to all his seed.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The book closes briefly: Ahasuerus lays a tribute upon the land and upon the isles of the sea, the acts of his power and the greatness of Mordecai are recorded in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia, and Mordecai stands next unto the king, great among the Jews and accepted of the multitude of his brethren, seeking the wealth of his people and speaking peace to all his seed. Interpreted Chapter text and study-note summaries for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And the king Ahasuerus laid a tribute upon the land, and upon the isles of the sea. Henry notes the book ends with the king's power, not the Jews' triumph: Ahasuerus still taxes land and isles, and the story's deliverance happened inside his empire, not by overthrowing it. Clarke adds the tribute fell on all one hundred and twenty-seven provinces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke
  • Verse 2. And all the acts of his power and might, and the declaration of the greatness of Mordecai, whereunto the king advanced him, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia? JFB notes the appeal to the Persian chronicles, now lost, while the sacred story lives: human chronicles perish, but this record remains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For Mordecai the Jew was next unto king Ahasuerus, and great among the Jews, and accepted of the multitude of his brethren, seeking the wealth of his people, and speaking peace to all his seed. Henry notes the final portrait: second to the king, great among his own, accepted because he sought their wealth and spoke their peace. Clarke adds he was prime minister under Ahasuerus, and studied to settle his people's prosperity on a permanent basis. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke

Unfamiliar terms

  • Tribute. The levy the king lays upon the land and upon the isles of the sea, with which the book's closing chapter opens. Interpreted Chapter text, verse 1
  • Book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia. The record the chapter points to for the acts of the king's power and the greatness of Mordecai. Documented Chapter text, verse 2
