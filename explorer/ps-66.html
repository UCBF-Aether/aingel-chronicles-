The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 66 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Make a joyful noise unto God, all ye lands:

2 Sing forth the honour of his name: make his praise glorious.

3 Say unto God, How terrible art thou in thy works! through the greatness of thy power shall thine enemies submit themselves unto thee.

4 All the earth shall worship thee, and shall sing unto thee; they shall sing to thy name. Selah.

5 Come and see the works of God: he is terrible in his doing toward the children of men.

6 He turned the sea into dry land: they went through the flood on foot: there did we rejoice in him.

7 He ruleth by his power for ever; his eyes behold the nations: let not the rebellious exalt themselves. Selah.

8 O bless our God, ye people, and make the voice of his praise to be heard:

9 Which holdeth our soul in life, and suffereth not our feet to be moved.

10 For thou, O God, hast proved us: thou hast tried us, as silver is tried.

11 Thou broughtest us into the net; thou laidst affliction upon our loins.

12 Thou hast caused men to ride over our heads; we went through fire and through water: but thou broughtest us out into a wealthy place.

13 I will go into thy house with burnt offerings: I will pay thee my vows,

14 Which my lips have uttered, and my mouth hath spoken, when I was in trouble.

15 I will offer unto thee burnt sacrifices of fatlings, with the incense of rams; I will offer bullocks with goats. Selah.

16 Come and hear, all ye that fear God, and I will declare what he hath done for my soul.

17 I cried unto him with my mouth, and he was extolled with my tongue.

18 If I regard iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear me:

19 But verily God hath heard me; he hath attended to the voice of my prayer.

20 Blessed be God, which hath not turned away my prayer, nor his mercy from me.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Corporate thanksgiving: joyful noise from all lands, terrible works, sea turned to dry land, flood crossed on foot, rejoicing there. Ruling for ever, eyes on nations, proved as silver, brought into nets, through fire and water into a wealthy place. Vows are paid with burnt offerings, and personal testimony follows: cried with mouth, extolled with tongue, iniquity regarded would block hearing, but God has heard and not turned away prayer or mercy. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Make a joyful noise unto God, all you lands: joyful noise from all lands opens the psalm of corporate thanksgiving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Sing forth the honour of his name; make his praise glorious: singing the honour of his name, making praise glorious — praise itself is to be glorious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Say unto God, How terrible are you in your works! Through the greatness of your power shall your enemies submit themselves unto you: terrible works and great power make enemies submit — the confession is addressed to God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. All the earth shall worship you, and shall sing unto you; they shall sing to your name: all-earth worship and song to God's name — the psalm's horizon is worldwide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Come and see the works of God; he is terrible in his doing toward the children of men: come and see — the invitation is to inspect God's terrible doings toward men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He turned the sea into dry land; they went through the flood on foot; there did we rejoice in him: sea to dry land, flood crossed on foot, rejoicing there in him — the Exodus is the exhibit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He rules by his power for ever; his eyes behold the nations; let not the rebellious exalt themselves: everlasting rule, eyes on the nations, no exalting for the rebellious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. O bless our God, you people, and make the voice of his praise to be heard: blessing God, with praise's voice heard — the peoples are bidden to be audible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Which holds our soul in life, and suffers not our feet to be moved: soul held in life, feet unmoved — preservation is both inward and outward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For you, O God, have proved us; you have tried us, as silver is tried: proving and trying as silver — affliction is assay, not abandonment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. You brought us into the net; you laid affliction upon our loins: net and loins' affliction are ascribed to God — he, not chance, brought the trial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. You have caused men to ride over our heads; we went through fire and through water; but you brought us out into a wealthy place: men riding over heads, fire and water passed through, then a wealthy place — the sequence is trial, then abundance, all under God's bringing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. I will go into your house with burnt offerings; I will pay you my vows: house-entry with burnt offerings, vows paid — worship follows deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Which my lips have uttered, and my mouth has spoken, when I was in trouble: lips' utterances in trouble are now honoured in the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. I will offer unto you burnt sacrifices of fatlings, with the incense of rams; I will offer bullocks with goats: fatlings, rams' incense, bullocks with goats — the offering is costly and complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Come and hear, all you that fear God, and I will declare what he has done for my soul: come and hear, you God-fearers — the testimony turns personal, what God did for my soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. I cried unto him with my mouth, and he was extolled with my tongue: crying with the mouth, extolling with the tongue — prayer and praise are one act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. If I regard iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear me: regarded iniquity in the heart blocks hearing — the condition of answered prayer is stated plainly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. But verily God has heard me; he has attended to the voice of my prayer: verily heard, attended to the voice of prayer — the condition met, the answer given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Blessed be God, which has not turned away my prayer, nor his mercy from me: prayer unturned, mercy unremoved — the psalm ends in blessing for both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
