The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 29 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And this is the thing that thou shalt do unto them to hallow them, to minister unto me in the priest’s office: Take one young bullock, and two rams without blemish,

2 And unleavened bread, and cakes unleavened tempered with oil, and wafers unleavened anointed with oil: of wheaten flour shalt thou make them.

3 And thou shalt put them into one basket, and bring them in the basket, with the bullock and the two rams.

4 And Aaron and his sons thou shalt bring unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and shalt wash them with water.

5 And thou shalt take the garments, and put upon Aaron the coat, and the robe of the ephod, and the ephod, and the breastplate, and gird him with the curious girdle of the ephod:

6 And thou shalt put the mitre upon his head, and put the holy crown upon the mitre.

7 Then shalt thou take the anointing oil, and pour it upon his head, and anoint him.

8 And thou shalt bring his sons, and put coats upon them.

9 And thou shalt gird them with girdles, Aaron and his sons, and put the bonnets on them: and the priest’s office shall be theirs for a perpetual statute: and thou shalt consecrate Aaron and his sons.

10 And thou shalt cause a bullock to be brought before the tabernacle of the congregation: and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the bullock.

11 And thou shalt kill the bullock before the LORD, by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

12 And thou shalt take of the blood of the bullock, and put it upon the horns of the altar with thy finger, and pour all the blood beside the bottom of the altar.

13 And thou shalt take all the fat that covereth the inwards, and the caul that is above the liver, and the two kidneys, and the fat that is upon them, and burn them upon the altar.

14 But the flesh of the bullock, and his skin, and his dung, shalt thou burn with fire without the camp: it is a sin offering.

15 Thou shalt also take one ram; and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the ram.

16 And thou shalt slay the ram, and thou shalt take his blood, and sprinkle it round about upon the altar.

17 And thou shalt cut the ram in pieces, and wash the inwards of him, and his legs, and put them unto his pieces, and unto his head.

18 And thou shalt burn the whole ram upon the altar: it is a burnt offering unto the LORD: it is a sweet savour, an offering made by fire unto the LORD.

19 And thou shalt take the other ram; and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the ram.

20 Then shalt thou kill the ram, and take of his blood, and put it upon the tip of the right ear of Aaron, and upon the tip of the right ear of his sons, and upon the thumb of their right hand, and upon the great toe of their right foot, and sprinkle the blood upon the altar round about.

21 And thou shalt take of the blood that is upon the altar, and of the anointing oil, and sprinkle it upon Aaron, and upon his garments, and upon his sons, and upon the garments of his sons with him: and he shall be hallowed, and his garments, and his sons, and his sons’ garments with him.

22 Also thou shalt take of the ram the fat and the rump, and the fat that covereth the inwards, and the caul above the liver, and the two kidneys, and the fat that is upon them, and the right shoulder; for it is a ram of consecration:

23 And one loaf of bread, and one cake of oiled bread, and one wafer out of the basket of the unleavened bread that is before the LORD:

24 And thou shalt put all in the hands of Aaron, and in the hands of his sons; and shalt wave them for a wave offering before the LORD.

25 And thou shalt receive them of their hands, and burn them upon the altar for a burnt offering, for a sweet savour before the LORD: it is an offering made by fire unto the LORD.

26 And thou shalt take the breast of the ram of Aaron’s consecration, and wave it for a wave offering before the LORD: and it shall be thy part.

27 And thou shalt sanctify the breast of the wave offering, and the shoulder of the heave offering, which is waved, and which is heaved up, of the ram of the consecration, even of that which is for Aaron, and of that which is for his sons:

28 And it shall be Aaron’s and his sons’ by a statute for ever from the children of Israel: for it is an heave offering: and it shall be an heave offering from the children of Israel of the sacrifice of their peace offerings, even their heave offering unto the LORD.

29 And the holy garments of Aaron shall be his sons’ after him, to be anointed therein, and to be consecrated in them.

30 And that son that is priest in his stead shall put them on seven days, when he cometh into the tabernacle of the congregation to minister in the holy place.

31 And thou shalt take the ram of the consecration, and seethe his flesh in the holy place.

32 And Aaron and his sons shall eat the flesh of the ram, and the bread that is in the basket by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

33 And they shall eat those things wherewith the atonement was made, to consecrate and to sanctify them: but a stranger shall not eat thereof, because they are holy.

34 And if ought of the flesh of the consecrations, or of the bread, remain unto the morning, then thou shalt burn the remainder with fire: it shall not be eaten, because it is holy.

35 And thus shalt thou do unto Aaron, and to his sons, according to all things which I have commanded thee: seven days shalt thou consecrate them.

36 And thou shalt offer every day a bullock for a sin offering for atonement: and thou shalt cleanse the altar, when thou hast made an atonement for it, and thou shalt anoint it, to sanctify it.

37 Seven days thou shalt make an atonement for the altar, and sanctify it; and it shall be an altar most holy: whatsoever toucheth the altar shall be holy.

38 Now this is that which thou shalt offer upon the altar; two lambs of the first year day by day continually.

39 The one lamb thou shalt offer in the morning; and the other lamb thou shalt offer at even:

40 And with the one lamb a tenth deal of flour mingled with the fourth part of an hin of beaten oil; and the fourth part of an hin of wine for a drink offering.

41 And the other lamb thou shalt offer at even, and shalt do thereto according to the meat offering of the morning, and according to the drink offering thereof, for a sweet savour, an offering made by fire unto the LORD.

42 This shall be a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the LORD: where I will meet you, to speak there unto thee.

43 And there I will meet with the children of Israel, and the tabernacle shall be sanctified by my glory.

44 And I will sanctify the tabernacle of the congregation, and the altar: I will sanctify also both Aaron and his sons, to minister to me in the priest’s office.

45 And I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God.

46 And they shall know that I am the LORD their God, that brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them: I am the LORD their God.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Aaron and his sons are washed, clothed, anointed, and consecrated over seven days, with a bullock for a sin offering and two rams, one for a burnt offering and one for consecration. A lamb morning and evening is appointed as the continual burnt offering at the door of the tabernacle, where God promises to meet Israel and dwell among them. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And this is the thing that thou shalt do unto them to hallow them, to minister unto me in the priest's office: take one young bullock, and two rams without blemish. Henry notes the consecration began with unblemished victims, for the priests who would offer for sin must themselves be set apart by sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And unleavened bread, and cakes unleavened tempered with oil, and wafers unleavened anointed with oil, of wheaten flour shalt thou make them. Henry observes the unleavened bread of consecration matched the bread of the Exodus: no leaven of malice or hypocrisy in those set apart to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And thou shalt put them into one basket, and bring them in the basket, with the bullock and the two rams. Henry notes the bread and the beasts were brought together, for the priests' consecration joined sacrifice and sustenance in one basket. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And Aaron and his sons thou shalt bring unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and shalt wash them with water. Henry observes the washing came first: the priests were washed before they were clothed, for cleansing precedes clothing in consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And thou shalt take the garments, and put upon Aaron the coat, and the robe of the ephod, and the ephod, and the breastplate, and gird him with the curious girdle of the ephod. Henry notes the clothing followed the washing: the priest was arrayed in the garments of glory and beauty before he was anointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And thou shalt put the mitre upon his head, and put the holy crown upon the mitre. Henry observes the mitre and the holy crown completed the array, with Holiness to the LORD upon his forehead, that all might see to whom he belonged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Then shalt thou take the anointing oil, and pour it upon his head, and anoint him. Henry notes the anointing poured upon the head signified the Spirit's gifts for office: the priest was not merely dressed but anointed for his work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And thou shalt bring his sons, and put coats upon them. Henry observes the sons were clothed in coats, simpler than Aaron's, yet still holy garments, for every priest shared the consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And thou shalt gird them with girdles, Aaron and his sons, and put the bonnets on them, and the priest's office shall be theirs for a perpetual statute, and thou shalt consecrate Aaron and his sons. Henry notes the girdles and bonnets completed the sons' array, and the office was perpetual: the priesthood would pass from father to son by statute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And thou shalt cause a bullock to be brought before the tabernacle of the congregation, and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the bullock. Henry observes the laying on of hands transferred the priests' sins to the victim, for even consecrated priests needed atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And thou shalt kill the bullock before the LORD, by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation. Henry notes the killing was before the LORD at the door, in the sight of all, for the priests' sin offering was public, not private. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And thou shalt take of the blood of the bullock, and put it upon the horns of the altar with thy finger, and pour all the blood beside the bottom of the altar. Henry observes the blood on the horns and at the base consecrated the altar itself: the place of sacrifice was sanctified by the sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And thou shalt take all the fat that covereth the inwards, and the caul that is above the liver, and the two kidneys, and the fat that is upon them, and burn them upon the altar. Henry notes the fat, the best part, was burned to God, for the inward parts belonged wholly to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. But the flesh of the bullock, and his skin, and his dung, shalt thou burn with fire without the camp, for it is a sin offering. Henry observes the sin offering was burned outside the camp, for sin was to be carried away from the holy place, a figure of Christ suffering outside the gate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Thou shalt also take one ram, and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the ram. Henry notes the first ram was the burnt offering of consecration, and the hands laid on it again confessed the priests' need of atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And thou shalt slay the ram, and thou shalt take his blood, and sprinkle it round about upon the altar. Henry observes the blood sprinkled round about the altar covered every side: the consecration was total, leaving no side unsprinkled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And thou shalt cut the ram in pieces, and wash the inwards of him, and his legs, and put them unto his pieces, and unto his head. Henry notes the washing of inwards and legs showed the inward and outward cleansing required: the sacrifice must be clean within and without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And thou shalt burn the whole ram upon the altar, for it is a burnt offering unto the LORD, a sweet savour, an offering made by fire unto the LORD. Henry observes the whole ram was burned, nothing kept back, for the burnt offering was whole surrender, a sweet savour to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And thou shalt take the other ram, and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the ram. Henry notes the second ram was the ram of consecration itself, distinct from the burnt offering, for the priests' own portion was yet to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Then shalt thou kill the ram, and take of his blood, and put it upon the tip of the right ear of Aaron, and upon the tip of the right ear of his sons, and upon the thumb of their right hand, and upon the great toe of their right foot, and sprinkle the blood upon the altar round about. Henry observes the blood marked ear, hand, and foot: the priest must hear God's word, do God's work, and walk God's way, all under blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And thou shalt take of the blood that is upon the altar, and of the anointing oil, and sprinkle it upon Aaron, and upon his garments, and upon his sons, and upon their garments with him, and he shall be hallowed, and his garments, and his sons, and his sons' garments with him. Henry notes blood and oil together hallowed priest and garments: atonement and anointing joined, for no service is holy without both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Also thou shalt take of the ram the fat and the rump, and the fat that covereth the inwards, and the caul above the liver, and the two kidneys, and the fat that is upon them, and the right shoulder, for it is a ram of consecration. Henry observes the choice parts, with the right shoulder, were taken for the wave offering, for the best belonged to God and the priest together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And one loaf of bread, and one cake of oiled bread, and one wafer out of the basket of the unleavened bread that is before the LORD. Henry notes the three kinds of bread joined the ram: loaf, cake, and wafer, the priests' bread of consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And thou shalt put all in the hands of Aaron, and in the hands of his sons, and shalt wave them for a wave offering before the LORD. Henry observes the wave offering was put into the priests' own hands and waved before God, for they offered what they had received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And thou shalt receive them of their hands, and burn them upon the altar for a burnt offering, for a sweet savour before the LORD, for it is an offering made by fire unto the LORD. Henry notes the waved portions were then burned, a sweet savour, for the priests' consecration ended in fire, as all service must. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And thou shalt take the breast of the ram of Aaron's consecration, and wave it for a wave offering before the LORD, and it shall be thy part. Henry observes the breast was Moses's part as the consecrator, for the wave breast belonged to the one who waved it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And thou shalt sanctify the breast of the wave offering, and the shoulder of the heave offering, which is waved, and which is heaved up, of the ram of the consecration, even of that which is for Aaron, and of that which is for his sons. Henry notes the breast and shoulder were sanctified as the priests' perpetual portion: the breast of love and the shoulder of strength, waved and heaved before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And it shall be Aaron's and his sons' by a statute forever from the children of Israel, for it is a heave offering, and it shall be a heave offering from the children of Israel of the sacrifice of their peace offerings, even their heave offering unto the LORD. Henry observes the statute was forever: the priests' portion was not a gift of the moment but a law for generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And the holy garments of Aaron shall be his sons' after him, to be anointed therein, and to be consecrated therein. Henry notes the garments passed down: the sons were anointed in their father's robes, for the office outlived the man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And that son that is priest in his stead shall put them on seven days, when he cometh into the tabernacle of the congregation to minister in the holy place. Henry observes the successor wore the garments seven days, a full week of consecration before he ministered, for the new priest must be fully set apart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And thou shalt take the ram of the consecration, and seethe his flesh in the holy place. Henry notes the ram's flesh was boiled in the holy place, for the priests' meal was itself holy, eaten where God dwelt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And Aaron and his sons shall eat the flesh of the ram, and the bread that is in the basket by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation. Henry observes the priests ate at the door, between the camp and the holy place, for their consecration meal stood at the threshold of service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And they shall eat those things wherewith the atonement was made, to consecrate and to sanctify them, but a stranger shall not eat thereof, because they are holy. Henry notes the meal was atonement eaten: the priests fed on the very things that had made atonement, and no stranger might share, for the food was holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. And if ought of the flesh of the consecrations, or of the bread, remain unto the morning, then thou shalt burn the remainder with fire, and it shall not be eaten, because it is holy. Henry observes the remainder was burned, not kept, for holy food must not grow common by delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And thus shalt thou do unto Aaron, and to his sons, according to all things which I have commanded thee, and seven days shalt thou consecrate them. Henry notes the consecration lasted seven days, a complete week, for the priests were set apart fully, not hastily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And thou shalt offer every day a bullock for a sin offering for atonement, and thou shalt cleanse the altar, when thou hast made an atonement for it, and thou shalt anoint it, to sanctify it. Henry observes the daily bullock atoned for the altar itself during the seven days, for even the place of sacrifice needed cleansing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Seven days thou shalt make an atonement for the altar, and sanctify it, and it shall be an altar most holy, and whatsoever toucheth the altar shall be holy. Henry notes the altar, after seven days of atonement, became most holy, so that whatever touched it was holy, for holiness spread from the altar to all it touched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Now this is that which thou shalt offer upon the altar: two lambs of the first year day by day continually. Henry observes the daily offering began here: two lambs a day, continually, the perpetual worship of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. The one lamb thou shalt offer in the morning, and the other lamb thou shalt offer at even. Henry notes the morning and evening lambs framed the day: worship opened and closed the day, that Israel might live between two sacrifices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. And with the one lamb a tenth deal of flour mingled with the fourth part of a hin of beaten oil, and the fourth part of a hin of wine for a drink offering. Henry observes the meat and drink offerings joined the lamb: flour and oil and wine, the fruits of the field, offered with the flock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. And the other lamb thou shalt offer at even, and shalt do thereto according to the meat offering of the morning, and according to the drink offering thereof, for a sweet savour, an offering made by fire unto the LORD. Henry notes the evening lamb matched the morning exactly, for the perpetual offering admitted no variation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. This shall be a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the LORD, where I will meet you, to speak there unto thee. Henry observes the continual offering stood at the door, where God would meet his people, for the place of sacrifice was the place of meeting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. And there I will meet with the children of Israel, and the tabernacle shall be sanctified by my glory. Henry notes the tabernacle was sanctified not by its gold but by God's glory dwelling in it, for his presence made it holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. And I will sanctify the tabernacle of the congregation, and the altar, and I will sanctify also both Aaron and his sons, to minister unto me in the priest's office. Henry observes God himself sanctified house, altar, and priests together, for consecration is God's work, not man's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. And I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God. Henry notes the promise that crowns the chapter: God would dwell among them and be their God, the end of all the tabernacle's gold and all the priests' array. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. And they shall know that I am the LORD their God, that brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them, and I am the LORD their God. Henry closes on the reason: the Exodus itself was for this dwelling, that God might live among the people he had redeemed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Also thou shalt take of the ram the fat and the rump, and the fat that covereth the inwards, and the caul above the liver, and the two kidneys, and the fat that is upon them, and the right shoulder, for it is a ram of consecration. Henry observes the choice parts, with the right shoulder, were taken for the wave offering, for the best belonged to God and the priest together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And one loaf of bread, and one cake of oiled bread, and one wafer out of the basket of the unleavened bread that is before the LORD. Henry notes the three kinds of bread joined the ram: loaf, cake, and wafer, the priests' bread of consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And thou shalt put all in the hands of Aaron, and in the hands of his sons, and shalt wave them for a wave offering before the LORD. Henry observes the wave offering was put into the priests' own hands and waved before God, for they offered what they had received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And thou shalt receive them of their hands, and burn them upon the altar for a burnt offering, for a sweet savour before the LORD, for it is an offering made by fire unto the LORD. Henry notes the waved portions were then burned, a sweet savour, for the priests' consecration ended in fire, as all service must. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And thou shalt take the breast of the ram of Aaron's consecration, and wave it for a wave offering before the LORD, and it shall be thy part. Henry observes the breast was Moses's part as the consecrator, for the wave breast belonged to the one who waved it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And thou shalt sanctify the breast of the wave offering, and the shoulder of the heave offering, which is waved, and which is heaved up, of the ram of the consecration, even of that which is for Aaron, and of that which is for his sons. Henry notes the breast and shoulder were sanctified as the priests' perpetual portion: the breast of love and the shoulder of strength, waved and heaved before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And it shall be Aaron's and his sons' by a statute forever from the children of Israel, for it is a heave offering, and it shall be a heave offering from the children of Israel of the sacrifice of their peace offerings, even their heave offering unto the LORD. Henry observes the statute was forever: the priests' portion was not a gift of the moment but a law for generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And the holy garments of Aaron shall be his sons' after him, to be anointed therein, and to be consecrated therein. Henry notes the garments passed down: the sons were anointed in their father's robes, for the office outlived the man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And that son that is priest in his stead shall put them on seven days, when he cometh into the tabernacle of the congregation to minister in the holy place. Henry observes the successor wore the garments seven days, a full week of consecration before he ministered, for the new priest must be fully set apart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And thou shalt take the ram of the consecration, and seethe his flesh in the holy place. Henry notes the ram's flesh was boiled in the holy place, for the priests' meal was itself holy, eaten where God dwelt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And Aaron and his sons shall eat the flesh of the ram, and the bread that is in the basket by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation. Henry observes the priests ate at the door, between the camp and the holy place, for their consecration meal stood at the threshold of service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And they shall eat those things wherewith the atonement was made, to consecrate and to sanctify them, but a stranger shall not eat thereof, because they are holy. Henry notes the meal was atonement eaten: the priests fed on the very things that had made atonement, and no stranger might share, for the food was holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. And if ought of the flesh of the consecrations, or of the bread, remain unto the morning, then thou shalt burn the remainder with fire, and it shall not be eaten, because it is holy. Henry observes the remainder was burned, not kept, for holy food must not grow common by delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And thus shalt thou do unto Aaron, and to his sons, according to all things which I have commanded thee, and seven days shalt thou consecrate them. Henry notes the consecration lasted seven days, a complete week, for the priests were set apart fully, not hastily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And thou shalt offer every day a bullock for a sin offering for atonement, and thou shalt cleanse the altar, when thou hast made an atonement for it, and thou shalt anoint it, to sanctify it. Henry observes the daily bullock atoned for the altar itself during the seven days, for even the place of sacrifice needed cleansing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Seven days thou shalt make an atonement for the altar, and sanctify it, and it shall be an altar most holy, and whatsoever toucheth the altar shall be holy. Henry notes the altar, after seven days of atonement, became most holy, so that whatever touched it was holy, for holiness spread from the altar to all it touched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Now this is that which thou shalt offer upon the altar: two lambs of the first year day by day continually. Henry observes the daily offering began here: two lambs a day, continually, the perpetual worship of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. The one lamb thou shalt offer in the morning, and the other lamb thou shalt offer at even. Henry notes the morning and evening lambs framed the day: worship opened and closed the day, that Israel might live between two sacrifices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. And with the one lamb a tenth deal of flour mingled with the fourth part of a hin of beaten oil, and the fourth part of a hin of wine for a drink offering. Henry observes the meat and drink offerings joined the lamb: flour and oil and wine, the fruits of the field, offered with the flock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. And the other lamb thou shalt offer at even, and shalt do thereto according to the meat offering of the morning, and according to the drink offering thereof, for a sweet savour, an offering made by fire unto the LORD. Henry notes the evening lamb matched the morning exactly, for the perpetual offering admitted no variation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. This shall be a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the LORD, where I will meet you, to speak there unto thee. Henry observes the continual offering stood at the door, where God would meet his people, for the place of sacrifice was the place of meeting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. And there I will meet with the children of Israel, and the tabernacle shall be sanctified by my glory. Henry notes the tabernacle was sanctified not by its gold but by God's glory dwelling in it, for his presence made it holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. And I will sanctify the tabernacle of the congregation, and the altar, and I will sanctify also both Aaron and his sons, to minister unto me in the priest's office. Henry observes God himself sanctified house, altar, and priests together, for consecration is God's work, not man's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. And I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God. Henry notes the promise that crowns the chapter: God would dwell among them and be their God, the end of all the tabernacle's gold and all the priests' array. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. And they shall know that I am the LORD their God, that brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them, and I am the LORD their God. Henry closes on the reason: the Exodus itself was for this dwelling, that God might live among the people he had redeemed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
