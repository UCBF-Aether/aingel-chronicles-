The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 21 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then came David to Nob to Ahimelech the priest: and Ahimelech was afraid at the meeting of David, and said unto him, Why art thou alone, and no man with thee?

2 And David said unto Ahimelech the priest, The king hath commanded me a business, and hath said unto me, Let no man know any thing of the business whereabout I send thee, and what I have commanded thee: and I have appointed my servants to such and such a place.

3 Now therefore what is under thine hand? give me five loaves of bread in mine hand, or what there is present.

4 And the priest answered David, and said, There is no common bread under mine hand, but there is hallowed bread; if the young men have kept themselves at least from women.

5 And David answered the priest, and said unto him, Of a truth women have been kept from us about these three days, since I came out, and the vessels of the young men are holy, and the bread is in a manner common, yea, though it were sanctified this day in the vessel.

6 So the priest gave him hallowed bread: for there was no bread there but the shewbread, that was taken from before the LORD, to put hot bread in the day when it was taken away.

7 Now a certain man of the servants of Saul was there that day, detained before the LORD; and his name was Doeg, an Edomite, the chiefest of the herdmen that belonged to Saul.

8 And David said unto Ahimelech, And is there not here under thine hand spear or sword? for I have neither brought my sword nor my weapons with me, because the king’s business required haste.

9 And the priest said, The sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom thou slewest in the valley of Elah, behold, it is here wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod: if thou wilt take that, take it: for there is no other save that here. And David said, There is none like that; give it me.

10 And David arose and fled that day for fear of Saul, and went to Achish the king of Gath.

11 And the servants of Achish said unto him, Is not this David the king of the land? did they not sing one to another of him in dances, saying, Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands?

12 And David laid up these words in his heart, and was sore afraid of Achish the king of Gath.

13 And he changed his behaviour before them, and feigned himself mad in their hands, and scrabbled on the doors of the gate, and let his spittle fall down upon his beard.

14 Then said Achish unto his servants, Lo, ye see the man is mad: wherefore then have ye brought him to me?

15 Have I need of mad men, that ye have brought this fellow to play the mad man in my presence? shall this fellow come into my house?

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

David comes to Nob to Ahimelech the priest, alone, and the priest is afraid at meeting him; David answers with a story of the king's secret business, which the notes call plainly a gross untruth, and Scripture does not conceal it. He asks for bread and receives the hallowed shewbread, there being no common bread, and for a weapon receives the sword of Goliath, wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod, saying, There is none like that, give it me. Doeg the Edomite, chiefest of Saul's herdmen, is there that day detained before the LORD and sees it all. David then flees to Achish king of Gath, where the servants quote the song, Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands, and David, sore afraid, changes his behaviour, feigns madness, scrabbles on the doors and lets spittle fall on his beard, until Achish answers, Lo, ye see the man is mad, have I need of mad men, shall this fellow come into my house. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David came to Nob to Ahimelech the priest, and Ahimelech was afraid at meeting David, asking, Why art thou alone, and no man with thee? David in distress fled to the tabernacle of God, Henry notes; it is great comfort in a day of trouble to have a God to go to, to whom we may open our cases and from whom we may ask direction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. David said to Ahimelech, The king hath commanded me a business, and said, Let no man know anything of the business, and I have appointed my servants to such and such a place. David told Ahimelech a gross untruth, Henry says plainly: Scripture does not conceal it, and we dare not justify it; it was ill done and proved of bad consequence, occasioning the death of the priests of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Now therefore what is under thine hand? Give me five loaves of bread, or what there is present. David asked the priest for bread and, next, for a sword — the fugitive's two needs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The priest answered, There is no common bread under mine hand, but there is hallowed bread, if the young men have kept themselves at least from women. Only the shewbread was available, and it came with a condition of cleanness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. David answered that women had been kept from them about three days, and the vessels of the young men were holy, and the bread was in a manner common, though sanctified that day in the vessel. The priest's scruple was met with an assurance of cleanness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. So the priest gave him hallowed bread, for there was no bread there but the shewbread taken from before the LORD to put hot bread in its place. Ahimelech supposed they might eat the shewbread, Henry notes, and the Son of David taught from it that mercy is to be preferred to sacrifice, and ritual observances must give way to moral duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Now a certain man of Saul's servants was there that day, detained before the LORD: Doeg, an Edomite, chiefest of Saul's herdmen. Doeg set his foot as far within the tabernacle as David did, Henry observes; we little know with what hearts people come to God's house, nor what use they will make of pretended devotion — only God and the event can distinguish a David from a Doeg. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. David said to Ahimelech, Is there not here spear or sword? For I have neither brought my sword nor weapons, because the king's business required haste. The lie was extended to explain his weaponless state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The priest said, The sword of Goliath, whom thou slewest in the valley of Elah, is here wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod; if thou wilt take that, take it, for there is no other. And David said, There is none like that; give it me. The giant's sword, kept as a trophy behind the ephod, became the fugitive's weapon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. David arose and fled that day for fear of Saul, and went to Achish king of Gath. God's persecuted people, Henry notes, have often found better usage from Philistines than from Israelites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Achish's servants said, Is not this David the king of the land? Did they not sing of him in dances, Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands? The song that had angered Saul now endangered David in Gath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. David laid up these words in his heart, and was sore afraid of Achish king of Gath. David had reason to put confidence in Achish, Henry says, yet he began to be afraid, and his conduct discovered wavering in his faith and courage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. He changed his behaviour before them, feigned himself mad in their hands, scrabbled on the doors of the gate, and let his spittle fall down upon his beard. His conduct was degrading, Henry observes; David had great faith and courage, yet both failed him through fear and the weakness of his faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Then said Achish to his servants, Lo, ye see the man is mad; wherefore then have ye brought him to me? The feigned madness worked on the king's contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Have I need of mad men, that ye have brought this fellow to play the mad man in my presence? Shall this fellow come into my house? The more simply we depend on God and obey him, Henry concludes, the more comfortably and surely we shall walk through this troublesome world; David's sorry shift is written for our warning, not for imitation, even in the greatest straits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
