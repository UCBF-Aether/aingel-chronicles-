The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 54 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Save me, O God, by thy name, and judge me by thy strength.

2 Hear my prayer, O God; give ear to the words of my mouth.

3 For strangers are risen up against me, and oppressors seek after my soul: they have not set God before them. Selah.

4 Behold, God is mine helper: the Lord is with them that uphold my soul.

5 He shall reward evil unto mine enemies: cut them off in thy truth.

6 I will freely sacrifice unto thee: I will praise thy name, O LORD; for it is good.

7 For he hath delivered me out of all trouble: and mine eye hath seen his desire upon mine enemies.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Save by God's name, judge by his strength: strangers and oppressors who have not set God before them seek his soul. God is helper, with those who uphold him, enemies are cut off in God's truth, and the psalm ends in free sacrifice and praise, delivered out of all trouble. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Heading note: the text panel prints the verses only, without a heading. The title recalled in verse 1, the Ziphites betraying David to Saul, comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Save me, O God, by your name, and judge me by your strength: salvation by God's name and judgment by his strength are the two pleas — the title recalls the Ziphites betraying David to Saul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Hear my prayer, O God; give ear to the words of my mouth: hearing and giving ear are asked for the prayer's very words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For strangers are risen up against me, and oppressors seek after my soul; they have not set God before them: strangers and oppressors, who have not set God before them, are the enemies — godlessness explains their hostility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Behold, God is my helper; the Lord is with those who uphold my soul: against risen strangers stands God as helper, with those who uphold his soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He shall reward evil to my enemies; cut them off in your truth: evil rewarded to enemies and cutting off in God's truth — truth keeps the promise of justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. I will freely sacrifice to you; I will praise your name, O Lord, for it is good: free sacrifice and praise of God's good name answer deliverance before it is fully seen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For he has delivered me out of all trouble, and my eye has seen his desire upon my enemies: deliverance out of all trouble, and the eye seeing God's desire on enemies, close the psalm in accomplished fact. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
