The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 37 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 At this also my heart trembleth, and is moved out of his place.

2 Hear attentively the noise of his voice, and the sound that goeth out of his mouth.

3 He directeth it under the whole heaven, and his lightning unto the ends of the earth.

4 After it a voice roareth: he thundereth with the voice of his excellency; and he will not stay them when his voice is heard.

5 God thundereth marvellously with his voice; great things doeth he, which we cannot comprehend.

6 For he saith to the snow, Be thou on the earth; likewise to the small rain, and to the great rain of his strength.

7 He sealeth up the hand of every man; that all men may know his work.

8 Then the beasts go into dens, and remain in their places.

9 Out of the south cometh the whirlwind: and cold out of the north.

10 By the breath of God frost is given: and the breadth of the waters is straitened.

11 Also by watering he wearieth the thick cloud: he scattereth his bright cloud:

12 And it is turned round about by his counsels: that they may do whatsoever he commandeth them upon the face of the world in the earth.

13 He causeth it to come, whether for correction, or for his land, or for mercy.

14 Hearken unto this, O Job: stand still, and consider the wondrous works of God.

15 Dost thou know when God disposed them, and caused the light of his cloud to shine?

16 Dost thou know the balancings of the clouds, the wondrous works of him which is perfect in knowledge?

17 How thy garments are warm, when he quieteth the earth by the south wind?

18 Hast thou with him spread out the sky, which is strong, and as a molten looking glass?

19 Teach us what we shall say unto him; for we cannot order our speech by reason of darkness.

20 Shall it be told him that I speak? if a man speak, surely he shall be swallowed up.

21 And now men see not the bright light which is in the clouds: but the wind passeth, and cleanseth them.

22 Fair weather cometh out of the north: with God is terrible majesty.

23 Touching the Almighty, we cannot find him out: he is excellent in power, and in judgment, and in plenty of justice: he will not afflict.

24 Men do therefore fear him: he respecteth not any that are wise of heart.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Elihu closes by pointing to the storm gathering overhead: at this his heart trembles, for the noise of God’s voice is thunder, and the sound from his mouth rolls under the whole heaven. God sends snow, rain, and ice at his command, makes the clouds turn by his counsels, and spreads the sky as a molten looking glass. Touching the Almighty, we cannot find him out — excellent in power, in judgment, and in plenty of justice, he will not oppress — and therefore men fear him, though he respects not any that are wise in their own conceit. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. At this also Elihu's heart trembles, and is moved out of his place. JFB explains at this as when he hears the thundering of the Divine Majesty, perhaps the storm already begun out of which God was to address Job. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Hear attentively the noise of God's voice, and the sound that goes out of his mouth. JFB renders hear attentively as the thunder, and sound as the muttering of the thunder: hearing it, one feels good reason to tremble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God directs it under the whole heaven, and his lightning unto the ends of the earth. JFB explains directeth as applying to the pealing roll of the thunder, showing God's all-embracing power, and ends literally as wings or skirts, the habitable earth compared to an extended garment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. After it a voice roars: God thunders with the voice of his excellency, and he will not stay them when his voice is heard. JFB notes the thunderclap follows at an interval after the flash, and stay them as not holding back the lightnings, or the rain and hail that accompany thunder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God thunders marvellously with his voice; great things does he, which we cannot comprehend. JFB notes the sublimity lies in God being everywhere in the storm, directing it whither he will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For God says to the snow, be thou on the earth; likewise to the small rain, and to the great rain of his strength. JFB renders be as more forcible than fall, and notes the shower increasing from small to great is expressed by the plural showers following the singular shower, as winter rain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God seals up the hand of every man, that all men may know his work. JFB explains that in winter God stops man's out-of-doors activity, sealing or closing up hands, so that all men whom he has made may be brought to acknowledgment of his work, in antithesis to man's own work which at other times makes men forget their dependence on God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Then the beasts go into dens, and remain in their places. JFB notes it is beautifully ordered that during the cold, when they could not obtain food, many lie torpid, a state wherein they need no food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Out of the south comes the whirlwind, and cold out of the north. JFB renders south literally as chambers, the whirlwinds poetically pent up by God in his southern chambers whence he sends them forth, and north literally as scattering, the north wind scattering the clouds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. By the breath of God frost is given, and the breadth of the waters is straitened. JFB explains breath of God poetically as the ice-producing north wind, frost as ice, and straitened as physically accurate: frost compresses the expanded liquid into a congealed mass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Also by watering God wearies the thick cloud: he scatters his bright cloud. JFB explains how the thunderclouds are dispersed or employed by God either for correction or mercy: by loading the cloud with water he burdens it so that it falls in rain, and bright cloud literally as cloud of his light, that is, of his lightning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And it is turned round about by God's counsels, that they may do whatsoever he commands them upon the face of the world in the earth. JFB explains it as the cloud of lightning, counsels as guidance or steering: the clouds obey God's guidance as the ship does the helmsman, and neither is haphazard in its movements. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. God causes it to come, whether for correction, or for his land, or for mercy. JFB renders it literally as he makes the rain-cloud find place, whether for correction if destined for his land, the part inhabited by man, or for mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Hearken unto this, O Job: stand still, and consider the wondrous works of God. Elihu pauses the storm description to bid Job stop and consider. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Dost thou know when God disposed them, and caused the light of his cloud to shine? JFB renders when as how, and disposed as laying his charge on these wonders to arise, asking how light arises from the dark thundercloud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Dost thou know the balancings of the clouds, the wondrous works of him which is perfect in knowledge? JFB renders it as hast thou understanding of the balancings, how the clouds are poised in the air so that their watery gravity does not bring them to earth, the descending moisture meeting warmer temperature which dissipates it into vapour and counteracts the descending force. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. How thy garments are warm, when God quiets the earth by the south wind? JFB explains it as dost thou know how thy body grows warm so as to affect thy garments with heat, when God makes still and sultry the earth by the south wind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Hast thou with God spread out the sky, which is strong, and as a molten looking glass? JFB explains with him as like as he does, spread out as given expanse to, strong pieces as firm, whence the term firmament, and molten looking glass as the bright smiling sky, mirrors then being formed of molten polished metal, not glass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Teach us what we shall say unto God, for we cannot order our speech by reason of darkness. JFB notes men cannot explain God's wonders, so we ought to be dumb and not contend with God, and order as frame, with darkness as ignorance of mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Shall it be told God that Elihu speaks? If a man speak, surely he shall be swallowed up. JFB explains that what a mortal says against God's dealings is not worthy of being told him, in opposition to Job's wish to speak before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. And now men see not the bright light which is in the clouds, but the wind passes, and cleanses them. JFB renders cleanseth as clearing the air of clouds: when the bright light of the sun, previously not seen through clouds, suddenly shines out owing to the wind clearing them away, the effect is dazzling, so if God's majesty, now hidden, were suddenly revealed it would spread darkness over Job's eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Fair weather comes out of the north: with God is terrible majesty. JFB renders it as golden splendour, with brightness chiefly associated with the north because the north wind clears the air, and majesty as splendour upon God as a garment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Touching the Almighty, we cannot find him out: he is excellent in power, and in judgment, and in plenty of justice: he will not afflict. JFB explains afflict as oppressively, so as to pervert judgment as Job implied, and notes the reading he answers not, that is, gives no account of his dealings, is like a transcriber's correction from chapter 33. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Men do therefore fear him: he respects not any that are wise of heart. JFB renders do as ought, and wise as in their own conceits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
