The Text — Sinodos 1 (Ge'ez text of G. Horner, The Statutes of the Apostles; or, Canones Ecclesiastici (London: Williams & Norgate, 1904), public domain)

1 The fourth commandment, the second: said Peter: Thou shalt not kill a soul; and thou shalt not commit fornication; and thou shalt not corrupt a young child.

2 Thou shalt not steal; and be not a soothsayer; and be not a star-gazer; and be not a sorcerer.

3 And thou shalt not kill the tiny babe in the womb[?]; … after it is born, thou shalt not kill it.

4 And thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's goods, nor anything[?] …; and thou shalt not withhold mercy from man; and be not a false witness.

5 And be not an evil speaker against any man.

6 And be not hasty[?] to do evil; and be not double of heart, and double of tongue; for the double-tongued is a snare of death.

7 And let not thy word be vain[?]; and be not a liar.

8 And be not contentious; and be not a lover of much property; and be not proud[?]; and …[?]; and be not …[?], and be not evil of heart.

9 And be not proud; and take not evil counsel against thy neighbour; and withhold not mercy from man; but rebuke: and hate some, and deliver[?] some, and love some from thy soul exceedingly.

10 The fifth commandment, the fifth: said Andrew: O my son, flee[?] from all evil, and hate all evil; and swear no oath[?]; for an oath leadeth into murder.

13 And be not envious, and be not …[?], and be not a lover of murder; for this corrupteth and bringeth forth[?] evil.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Spoken in the names of Peter and then Andrew, this chapter gives moral commandments in the negative: do not kill, commit fornication, corrupt a child, steal, practise soothsaying, star-gazing or sorcery, kill the unborn or the born child, covet, bear false witness, speak evil, or be double of heart and tongue. Against these it sets mercy withheld from no man, rebuke, and love from the soul exceedingly — though several verses are marked in the text itself as uncertain or broken, with words lost and shown as [?] and … .
