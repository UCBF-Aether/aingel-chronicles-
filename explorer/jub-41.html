The Text — Jubilees 41 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the forty-fifth jubilee, in the second week, (and) in the second year, Judah took for his first-born Er, a wife from the daughters of Aram named Tamar.

2 But he hated, and did not lie with her, because his mother was of the daughters of Canaan, and he wished to take him a wife of the kinsfolk of his mother, but Judah, his father, would not permit him.

3 And this Er, the first-born of

4 Judah, was wicked, and the Lord slew him.

5 And

6 Judah said unto Onan, his brother : " Go in unto

7 thy brother's wife and perform the duty of a husband's

8 brother unto her and raise up seed unto thy brother."

9 And Onan knew that the seed would not be his,

10 (but) his brother's only, and he went into the house

11 of his brother's wife, and spilt the seed on the ground.

12 and he was wicked in the eyes of the Lord, and He

13 slew him.

14 And Judah said unto Tamar, his

15 daughter-in-law : " Remain in thy father's house

16 as a widow till Shelah my son be grown up, and I

17 shall give thee to him to wife." 7.

18 And he grew up;

19 but Bedsu'el the wife of Judah, did not permit her

20 son Shelah to marry.

21 And Bedsu'el, the wife of

22 Judah, died in the fifth year of this week.

24 And in the sixth year Judah went up to shear his sheep at

25 Timnah.

26 And they told Tamar: "Behold thy

27 father-in-law goeth up to Timnah to shear his sheep."

28 And she put off her widow's clothes, and put on a

29 veil, and adorned herself, and sat in the gate adjoining

30 the way to Timnah.

31 And as Judah was going

32 along he found her, and thought her to be an harlot,

33 and he said unto her : " Let me come in unto thee " ;

34 and she said unto him : " Come in," and he went in.

35 And she said unto him : " Give me my hire " ;

36 and he said unto her : " I have nothing in my hand

37 save my ring that is on my finger, and my necklace,

38 and my staff which is in my hand." 12.

39 And she

40 said unto him : " Give them to me until thou dost

41 send me my hire " ; and he said unto her : " I will

42 send unto thee a kid of the goats " ; and he gave them

43 to her, (and he went in unto her,) and she conceived

44 by him.

45 And Judah went unto his sheep, and

46 she went to her father's house.

47 And Judah sent

48 a kid of the goats by the hand of his shepherd, an

49 Adullamite, and he found her not ; and he asked the

50 people of the place, saying : " Where is the harlot

51 who was here? ' And they said unto him : " There

52 is no harlot here with us." 15.

53 And he returned and informed him; and said unto him that he had not found her ; " I asked the people of the place, and they said unto me : ' There is no harlot here.' " And he said : " Let her keep (them) lest we become a cause of derision." 16.

54 And when she had completed three months, it was manifest that she was with child, and they told Judah, saying : " Behold Tamar, thy daughter-in-law, is with child by whoredom." 17.

55 And Judah went to the house of her father, and said unto her father and her brothers : " Bring her forth, and let them burn her for she hath wrought uncleanness in Israel." 18.

56 And it came to pass when they brought her forth to burn her that she sent to her father-in-law the ring and the necklace, and the staff, saying : " Discern whose are these, for by him am I with child."

57 And Judah acknowledged, and said : " Tamar is more righteous than I am.

58 And therefore let them burn her not." 20.

59 And for that reason she was not given to Shelah, and he did not again approach her.

60 And after that she bare two sons, Perez and Zerah, in the seventh year of this second week.

61 And thereupon the seven years of fruitfulness were accomplished, of which Joseph spake to Pharaoh.

62 And Judah acknowledged that the deed which he had done was evil, for he had lain with his daughter-in-law, and he esteemed it hateful in his eyes, and he acknowledged that he had transgressed and gone astray; for he had uncovered the skirt of his son, and he began to lament and to supplicate before the Lord because of his transgression.

63 And we told him in a dream that it was forgiven him because he supplicated earnestly, and lamented, and did not again commit it.

64 And he received forgiveness because he turned from his

65 sin and from his ignorance, for he transgressed greatly before our God; and every one that acteth thus, every one who lies with his mother-in-law, let them burn him with fire that he may burn therein for there is uncleanness and pollution upon them ; with fire let them burn them.

66 And do thou command the children of Israel that there be no uncleanness amongst them, for every one who lieth with his daughter-in-law or with his mother-in-law hath wrought uncleanness ; with fire let them burn the man who hath lain with her, and likewise the woman, and He will turn away wrath and punishment from Israel.

67 And unto Judah we said that his two sons had not lain with her, and for this reason his seed was established for a second generation, and would not be rooted out.

68 For in singleness of eye he had gone and sought for punishment, namely, according to the judgment of Abraham which he had commanded his sons, Judah had sought to burn her with fire.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the forty-fifth jubilee, in the second week, in the second year, Judah took for his first-born Er a wife from the daughters of Aram named Tamar. Henry introduces the whole family account with wonder: of all Jacob's sons, the Lord should spring out of Judah, showing God's choice is of grace and not of merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Er hated Tamar and did not lie with her, because her mother was of the daughters of Canaan, and he wished to take a wife from his mother's kin, but Judah his father would not permit him. The hatred began in the same Canaanite question that runs through Jubilees: the house must not be mixed with the daughters of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. This Er, the first-born of Judah, was wicked, and the Lord slew him. Henry's warning over the chapter stands: what awful examples the Lord proclaims in his punishments, of his utter displeasure at sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The sentence on Er is completed: the Lord slew him for his wickedness. The first-born's death opens the levirate duty that drives the rest of the chapter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Judah said to Onan his brother that he should go in to his brother's wife, perform the duty of a husband's brother, and raise up seed to his brother. The duty was to preserve the dead brother's name and line, the obligation that Tamar would later claim for herself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Judah's charge to Onan continued: he must go in to his brother's wife and perform the husband's brother's duty. The command is plain, and Onan's evasion of it is the next sin of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The duty was defined: to raise up seed to his brother, not to himself. The seed would bear Er's name, and that self-denial is exactly what Onan refused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Onan knew the seed would not be his but his brother's only. Knowledge makes the sin deliberate: he understood the duty and chose to frustrate it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Onan went into his brother's wife's house but spilt the seed on the ground, defeating the duty while seeming to perform it. Henry's warning fits: seek grace to avoid every appearance of sin, for God sees the deed behind the show. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The spilling of the seed is named for what it was: a refusal of the brother's duty, not an accident. The act is the centre of Onan's guilt, a deliberate frustration of the command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Onan was wicked in the eyes of the Lord, and the Lord slew him. The second son dies as the first did, and Henry's point stands: the Lord proclaims his utter displeasure at sin in awful examples, even within the chosen house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The sentence is completed: the Lord slew Onan as he had slain Er. Two sons dead, and the line of Judah now hangs on the youngest son and the widowed Tamar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Judah said to Tamar his daughter-in-law that she should remain in her father's house as a widow till Shelah his son was grown up, and then he would give her to him as wife. The promise kept Tamar waiting for the levirate duty, and her later action answers Judah's failure to keep it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Tamar was to remain as a widow in her father's house, the waiting place of a woman whose husband's line was not yet settled. The widowhood was meant to be temporary, until Shelah grew up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The promise was repeated in full: when Shelah was grown, Judah would give him to Tamar as wife. The repetition binds Judah to the promise his later conduct would break. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The promise's condition is stated once more: till Shelah his son was grown up, and then he would give Tamar to him. The condition was fulfilled in time, but the promise was not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Shelah grew up, and the time for Judah's promise arrived. The growth of the boy is the quiet turning point: the excuse of youth was gone, and Tamar's claim stood due. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Bedsu'el, Judah's wife, did not permit her son Shelah to marry Tamar. The mother's refusal blocked the promise, and the house's failure to do right passed from father to mother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The refusal is completed: Shelah was not permitted to marry, and Tamar remained a widow beyond the promised time. The broken promise is what drives Tamar to the road to Timnah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Bedsu'el, Judah's wife, died in the fifth year of this week. The death removes the mother who blocked the marriage, but Judah still does not give Shelah to Tamar, and the widow's wait continues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The death is dated to the fifth year of the week, fixing it in Jubilees' chronology between Shelah's growing up and Judah's journey to Timnah. The dates mark how long Tamar waited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. In the sixth year Judah went up to shear his sheep at Timnah. The shearing journey is the occasion for the encounter: Judah travels, and Tamar, hearing of it, takes her place on his road. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The destination is named: Timnah, where Judah went to shear his sheep. The place-name matters, for Tamar will sit in the gate adjoining the way to Timnah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Tamar was told that her father-in-law was going up to Timnah to shear his sheep. The news reached the widow who had waited for a husband, and she acted on it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The message is completed: Judah goes up to Timnah to shear his sheep, and Tamar knows his road and his time. Knowledge becomes her opportunity to claim the right denied her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Tamar put off her widow's clothes, put on a veil, adorned herself, and sat in the gate adjoining the way to Timnah. Henry's warning about avoiding every appearance of sin cuts both ways here: Tamar's disguise wore the appearance of harlotry, though her aim was the levirate seed that was her due. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. The veil and adornment completed the disguise, and Tamar sat where Judah must pass. The gate by the way to Timnah was her chosen place, public enough to be found, veiled enough to be mistaken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The gate adjoining the way to Timnah is named as her seat, the threshold between the town and the road. There she waited for the father-in-law who owed her a husband. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. As Judah was going along he found her and thought her to be a harlot. Henry calls the chapter's account profligate conduct, and Judah's mistaking of his daughter-in-law for a harlot is its darkest turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Judah found Tamar on his way and took her for a harlot, the disguise working exactly as she intended. The mistake is Judah's, but the design is Tamar's, and both will be judged in the chapter's end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Judah said to her, let me come in to thee, and she said come in, and he went in. The encounter is stated plainly, without excuse, as Henry states the whole chapter: an account of profligate conduct in the house from which Christ would come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The meeting is completed: he went in to her, and the act that would produce Perez was done in ignorance on his side and in design on hers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Tamar asked for her hire, and Judah said he had nothing in his hand save his ring, his necklace, and his staff. The pledges she demanded would become the proof of paternity, and Henry's theme of grace runs through it: God would bring the promised Seed from this very union. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Judah named his pledges one by one: the ring on his finger, the necklace, and the staff in his hand. Each token was personal and unmistakable, the seal of a man who could later be known by them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. The staff in Judah's hand completes the list of pledges, with the ring and necklace. Three tokens, each bearing its owner's mark, would speak for Tamar when Judah spoke against her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. The staff is named again as the pledge is handed over, the last of the three tokens passing into Tamar's keeping. The exchange is businesslike, and its business would later become judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Tamar said to give the tokens to her until he sent her hire, and Judah said he would send a kid of the goats, and gave them to her, and went in to her, and she conceived by him. The conception is the point of the whole design: the seed denied through Shelah came through Judah himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. The agreement is sealed: the tokens stay with Tamar until the kid arrives, and the union that produced the promised seed is accomplished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Judah promised to send a kid of the goats as her hire, the payment that would redeem his pledges. The kid would never reach her, because the messenger would not find her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. The kid is named again as the promised hire, and the tokens were given in its place. The pledge-and-payment bargain is the legal frame Tamar will later use to prove her case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Judah gave the tokens to Tamar, went in to her, and she conceived by him. The conception closes the encounter: the seed of Judah was secured, though by a road of sin and disguise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. The conception by Judah is stated plainly, the fact on which the rest of the chapter turns. From this union would come Perez, ancestor of the Messiah, the grace Henry finds wonderful in this chapter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Judah went to his sheep, and Tamar went to her father's house. Each returned to their place, Judah to his shearing and Tamar to her widowhood, with the tokens hidden and the child growing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. The parting is complete: he to his flocks, she to her father's house, and the secret between them carried in her keeping and in her womb. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Judah sent a kid of the goats by his shepherd, an Adullamite, to redeem his pledges, and the shepherd found her not. The honest attempt to pay the hire failed, because Tamar had vanished from the roadside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. The shepherd asked the people of the place where the harlot who was there had gone, and they said there was no harlot with them. The place denied what the roadside had shown, and the tokens stayed with Tamar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. The shepherd's question is repeated: where is the harlot who was here? The question itself shows Judah's view of the encounter, a hired woman, not the daughter-in-law claiming her right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. The people answered that there was no harlot there with them, and the search ended. The denial protected Tamar's secret until the pregnancy revealed it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. The people of the place said there was no harlot there, closing the search. Judah's pledges remained unredeemed, waiting for the day they would be produced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. The shepherd returned and told Judah he had not found her, and that the people said there was no harlot there. The report left Judah without his tokens and without knowledge of the child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. Judah answered that she might keep the tokens, lest they become a cause of derision. The fear of derision, not justice, settled the matter for Judah, and the tokens stayed where they would later condemn him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. When three months had completed, it was manifest that Tamar was with child, and they told Judah that his daughter-in-law was with child by whoredom. The pregnancy exposed what the veil had hidden, and the charge of whoredom fell on the woman whose design had been the levirate right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. Judah went to her father and brothers and said to bring her forth and burn her, for she had wrought uncleanness in Israel. The judge who had lain with her now sentenced her to fire, and Henry's warning about God's displeasure at sin falls first on the judge himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. When they brought Tamar forth to burn her, she sent to her father-in-law the ring, the necklace, and the staff, saying discern whose these are, for by him she was with child. The tokens, kept since Timnah, now spoke: the judge was the father, and the sentence collapsed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 57. Judah acknowledged the tokens and said Tamar was more righteous than he was. The confession is the chapter's turning point: the sinner judges himself, and Henry's grace shines here, for Christ came to save sinners, even the chief, and sprang from this very line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 58. Judah ordered that they should not burn her, reversing his own sentence. The reversal is justice done late: the woman condemned by the guilty judge is freed by his confession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 59. For that reason Tamar was not given to Shelah, and Judah did not again approach her. The separation closed the matter: the levirate claim was satisfied through Judah himself, and no further union followed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 60. Afterwards Tamar bore two sons, Perez and Zerah, in the seventh year of this second week. Henry's wonder crowns the chapter: from Perez, born of this union, the Lord Jesus would spring, showing God's choice is of grace and not of merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 61. Thereupon the seven years of fruitfulness were accomplished, of which Joseph spoke to Pharaoh. The chronology joins Judah's story to Joseph's: the plenty years end as the family story turns toward famine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 62. Judah acknowledged that the deed he had done was evil, for he had lain with his daughter-in-law, and esteemed it hateful in his eyes, and confessed he had transgressed and gone astray, for he had uncovered his son's skirt, and began to lament and supplicate before the Lord. Henry's counsel fits the repentance: seek grace from God to avoid every appearance of sin, and let the Redeemer's humiliation, in taking such ancestors, endear him to our hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 63. Judah was told in a dream that he was forgiven, because he supplicated earnestly, lamented, and did not again commit the sin. The forgiveness follows the pattern Henry celebrates in this chapter: Christ came into the world to save sinners, and his worthiness is of himself, not of his ancestors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 64. Judah received forgiveness because he turned from his sin and his ignorance, though he had transgressed greatly before God. Repentance, not merit, is the ground of the pardon, the grace that makes Judah's line the line of the Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry's counsel fits the repentance: seek grace from God to avoid every appearance of sin, and let the Redeemer's humiliation, in taking such ancestors, endear him to our hearts. The forgiveness follows the pattern Henry celebrates in this chapter: Christ came into the world to save sinners, and his worthiness is of himself, not of his ancestors.
