The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 44 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And Israel took his journey from Haran from his house on the new moon of the third month, and he went on the way of the Well of the Oath and he offered a sacrifice to the God of his father Isaac on the seventh of this month.

2 And Jacob remembered the dream that he had seen at Bethel and he feared to go down into Egypt.

3 And while he was thinking of sending word to Joseph to come to him, and that he would not go down, he remained there seven days, if perchance he should see a vision as to whether he should remain or go down.

4 And

5 he celebrated the harvest festival of the first-fruits with old grain, for in all the land of Canaan there was not a handful of seed (in the land), for the famine was over all the beasts and cattle and birds, and also over man.

6 And on the sixteenth the Lord appeared unto him, and said unto him, " Jacob, Jacob " ; and he said, " Here am I."

7 And He said unto him : " I am the God of thy fathers, the God of Abraham and Isaac ; fear not to go down into Egypt, for I will there make of thee a great nation.

8 I shall go down with thee, and I shall bring thee up (again), and in this land wilt thou be buried, and Joseph will put his hands upon thy eyes.

9 Fear not ; go down into Egypt." 7.

10 And his sons rose up, and his sons' sons, and they placed their father and their possessions upon wagons.

11 And Israel rose up from the Well of the Oath on the sixteenth of this third month, and he went to the land of Egypt.

12 And Israel sent Judah before him to his son Joseph to examine the Land of Goshen, for Joseph had told his brothers that they should come and dwell there that they might be near him.

13 And this was the goodliest (land) in the land of Egypt, and near to him, for all (of them) and also for the cattle.

14 And these are the names of the sons of Jacob who went into Egypt with Jacob their father. 12.

15 Reuben, the first-born of Israel ; and these are the names of his sons : Enoch, and Pallu, and Hezron and Carmi — five.

16 Simeon and his sons; and these are the names of his sons : Jemuel, and Jamin, and Ohad,

17 a " To show the way " (Gen. xlvi. 28).

18 and Jachin, and Zohar, and Shaul, the son of the Zephathite woman — seven.

19 Levi and his sons; and these are the names of his sons : Gershon, and Kohath, and Merari — four.

20 Judah and his sons ; and these are the names of his sons : Shela, and Perez, and Zerah — four.

21 Issachar and his sons ; and these, are the names of his sons : Tola, and Phua and Jasub and Shimron — five.

22 Zebulon and his sons ; and these are the names of his sons : Sered, and Elon, and Jahleel — four.

23 And these are the sons of Jacob, and their sons, whom Leah bore to Jacob in Mesopotamia, six, and their one sister, Dinah : and all the souls of the sons of Leah, and their sons, who went with Jacob their father into Egypt, were twenty-nine, and Jacob their father being with them, they were thirty.

24 And the sons of Zilpah, Leah's handmaid, the wife of Jacob, whom she bore unto Jacob, Gad and Asher.

25 And these are the names of their sons who went with him into Egypt : the sons of Gad : Ziphion, and Haggi, and Shuni, and Ezbon, (and Eri) and Areli, and Arodi — eight.

26 And the sons of Asher : Imnah, and Ishvah, (and Ishvi), and Beriah, and Serah, their one sister — six.

27 All the souls were fourteen, and all those of Leah were forty-four.

28 And the sons of Rachel, the wife of Jacob : Joseph and Benjamin.

29 And there were born to Joseph in Egypt before his father came into Egypt, those whom Asenath, daughter of Potiphar priest of Heliopolis bare unto him, Manasseh, and Ephraim — three.

30 And the sons of Benjamin : Bela and Becher, and Ashbel, Gera, and Naaman, and Ehi, and Rosh, and Muppim, and Huppim, and Ard — eleven.

31 And all the souls of Rachel were fourteen.

32 And the sons of Bilhah, the handmaid of Rachel, the wife of Jacob, whom she

33 bare to Jacob, were Dan and Naphtali.

34 And these are the names of their sons who went with them into Egypt.

35 And the sons of Dan were Hushim, and Samon, and Asudi, and 'Ijaka, and Salomon — six.

36 And they died the year in which they entered into Egypt, and there was left to Dan Hushim alone.

37 And these are the names of the sons of Naphtali : Jahziel, and Guni, and Jezer, and Shallum, and 'Iv.

38 And 'Iv, who was born after the years of famine, died in Egypt.

39 And all the souls of Rachel were twenty-six.

40 And all the souls of Jacob which went into Egypt were seventy souls.

41 These are his children and his children's children, in all seventy; but five died in Egypt before Joseph, and had no children.

42 And in the land of Canaan two sons of Judah died, Er and Onan, and they had no children, and the children of Israel buried those who perished, and they were reckoned among the seventy Gentile nations.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: Israel took his journey from his house on the new moon of the third month, went by the way of the Well of the Oath, and offered a sacrifice to the God of his father Isaac on the seventh of the month. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. Israel took his journey from his house on the new moon of the third month, went by the way of the Well of the Oath, and offered a sacrifice to the God of his father Isaac on the seventh of the month. Henry notes that even for undertakings that appear most joyful, we should seek counsel, assistance, and blessing from the Lord, attending his ordinances and receiving the pledges of his covenant love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jacob remembered the dream he had seen at Bethel and feared to go down into Egypt. The fear was not unbelief but the weight of leaving the promised land, and Henry notes that in all removals we should be reminded of our removal out of this world, encouraged only by God's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. While Jacob thought of sending word to Joseph to come to him instead of going down, he remained seven days, hoping to see a vision whether he should remain or go down. The waiting is itself the counsel Henry commends: seek the Lord's direction before a great removal, rather than rushing on fear or hope alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. On the sixteenth the Lord appeared to Jacob and said, Jacob, Jacob, and he answered, Here am I. The double call and the ready answer recall the patriarchs' encounters with God, and the vision Jacob waited seven days to receive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. God said he was the God of Jacob's fathers, Abraham and Isaac, and told him not to fear going down into Egypt, for there he would make of him a great nation. Henry notes that the promise to Abraham was 215 years old, and the family had only increased to seventy, yet the fulfilling of promises is always sure, though often slow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. God promised to go down with Jacob, bring him up again, bury him in this land, and let Joseph put his hands upon his eyes. The promise covers the whole Egyptian sojourn: presence in it, return from it, burial in Canaan, and a son's hand closing his eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The command is repeated: fear not, go down into Egypt. The repetition seals the vision's answer to Jacob's seven days of waiting, turning fear into obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jacob's sons and grandsons rose up and placed their father and their possessions upon wagons. Henry notes the particular account of Jacob's family going to Egypt, the seventy whose increase would show God's power in making them a vast multitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Israel rose up from the Well of the Oath on the sixteenth of the third month and went to Egypt. The departure from the place of sacrifice, dated precisely, begins the sojourn God had just promised to bless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Israel sent Judah before him to his son Joseph to examine the land of Goshen, for Joseph had told his brothers they should dwell there near him. Henry notes that Joseph contrived to settle his brethren to their advantage, rendering good for evil, and that Goshen lay nearest to Canaan, keeping them near the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Goshen was the goodliest land in Egypt and near to Joseph, for all of them and also for their cattle. Henry notes that Joseph would have his brethren live by themselves in Goshen, not ashamed of their shepherd calling, lest court preferment tempt them to forget Canaan and the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. These are the names of the sons of Jacob who went into Egypt with their father. Henry observes that a particular account of the family is kept, to show God's power in making these seventy become a vast multitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Reuben, the first-born of Israel, is listed with his sons Enoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi, five souls. The first-born's line opens the register, though the birthright had passed from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Simeon and his sons are listed: Jemuel, Jamin, and Ohad. The list continues tribe by tribe, the family counted as it enters Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. This unit is the translator's footnote, explaining the purpose of sending Judah: to show the way, citing Genesis 46:28. Charles's note glosses the mission, that Judah went ahead to prepare the road to Goshen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. Simeon's sons continue: Jachin, Zohar, and Shaul, son of the Zephathite woman, seven souls in all. The counting includes the son of the foreign woman, counted among Israel without distinction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Levi and his sons are listed: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari, four souls. The priestly tribe enters Egypt as a small family, whose descendants would carry the ark and the tabernacle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Judah and his sons are listed: Shela, Perez, and Zerah, four souls. The royal tribe enters with the twins born to Tamar, Perez the ancestor of the Messiah among them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Issachar and his sons are listed: Tola, Phua, Jasub, and Shimron, five souls. The tribe's quiet register gives no deeds, only names, the seed of a people not yet a nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Zebulon and his sons are listed: Sered, Elon, and Jahleel, four souls. Each tribe's brief entry builds the seventy, name by name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. These are the sons of Jacob and their sons whom Leah bore in Mesopotamia, six sons and their one sister Dinah, and all the souls of Leah's sons and their sons who went into Egypt were twenty-nine, and with Jacob their father, thirty. Henry notes the purpose of the particular account: to show the power of God in making these seventy a vast multitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. The sons of Zilpah, Leah's handmaid and Jacob's wife, whom she bore to Jacob, were Gad and Asher. The handmaid's sons are counted with the household, their lines continuing into Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The sons of Gad who went into Egypt are named: Ziphion, Haggi, Shuni, Ezbon, Eri, Areli, and Arodi, eight souls. Gad's line enters as a troop, as his name foretold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The sons of Asher are named: Imnah, Ishvah, Ishvi, Beriah, and Serah their one sister, six souls. Asher's register includes a daughter among the sons, the family counted whole. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. All the souls of Zilpah's line were fourteen, and all those of Leah were forty-four. The totals gather the tribes into their mothers' houses, Leah's house the largest of the four. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The sons of Rachel, Jacob's wife, were Joseph and Benjamin. The two sons of the beloved wife stand at the head of Rachel's house, one already in Egypt, one arriving with his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Joseph's sons, born in Egypt before his father came, were Manasseh and Ephraim, born to Asenath daughter of Potiphar priest of Heliopolis, three souls with Joseph. The Egyptian-born grandsons are counted among Israel, the fruit of Joseph's exile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The sons of Benjamin are named: Bela, Becher, Ashbel, Gera, Naaman, Ehi, Rosh, Muppim, Huppim, and Ard, eleven souls. Benjamin, the youngest, arrives with the largest list of sons, the tribe that would keep with Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. All the souls of Rachel were fourteen, the first reckoning of her house. The number counts the heads of the house, before the fuller reckoning that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The sons of Bilhah, Rachel's handmaid and Jacob's wife, whom she bore to Jacob, were Dan and Naphtali. The last mother's house completes the four houses of Jacob's family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. The sons of Bilhah are introduced: Dan and Naphtali, whose own sons follow. The introduction gathers the handmaid's lines under their mothers' names. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. These are the names of the sons of Bilhah's sons who went into Egypt, the register now descending to the grandsons. The naming continues the particular account Henry finds kept to show God's power in the seventy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The sons of Dan were Hushim, Samon, Asudi, Ijaka, and Salomon, six souls. Dan's line enters Egypt with six names, though Jubilees will add that only Hushim remained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. These are the names of the sons of Naphtali: Jahziel, Guni, Jezer, Shallum, and Iv. Naphtali's five names close the tribal registers, the last of the seventy's branches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. All the souls of Rachel were twenty-six, the fuller reckoning of her house including the grandsons. The two reckonings, fourteen and twenty-six, count the house at different depths, heads and branches together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. All the souls of Jacob who went into Egypt were seventy souls. Henry notes the point of the number: that branch of Abraham's seed to which the promise was made sure had only increased to seventy, yet God would make these seventy a vast multitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. These are his children and his children's children, seventy in all, but five died in Egypt before Joseph and had no children. The seventy is the reckoned number, though death thinned it even in Egypt, and the promise waited on God's power, not on arithmetic. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
