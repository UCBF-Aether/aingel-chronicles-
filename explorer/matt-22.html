The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Matthew 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Jesus answered and spake unto them again by parables, and said,

2 The kingdom of heaven is like unto a certain king, which made a marriage for his son,

3 And sent forth his servants to call them that were bidden to the wedding: and they would not come.

4 Again, he sent forth other servants, saying, Tell them which are bidden, Behold, I have prepared my dinner: my oxen and my fatlings are killed, and all things are ready: come unto the marriage.

5 But they made light of it, and went their ways, one to his farm, another to his merchandise:

6 And the remnant took his servants, and entreated them spitefully, and slew them.

7 But when the king heard thereof, he was wroth: and he sent forth his armies, and destroyed those murderers, and burned up their city.

8 Then saith he to his servants, The wedding is ready, but they which were bidden were not worthy.

9 Go ye therefore into the highways, and as many as ye shall find, bid to the marriage.

10 So those servants went out into the highways, and gathered together all as many as they found, both bad and good: and the wedding was furnished with guests.

11 And when the king came in to see the guests, he saw there a man which had not on a wedding garment:

12 And he saith unto him, Friend, how camest thou in hither not having a wedding garment? And he was speechless.

13 Then said the king to the servants, Bind him hand and foot, and take him away, and cast him into outer darkness, there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.

14 For many are called, but few are chosen.

15 Then went the Pharisees, and took counsel how they might entangle him in his talk.

16 And they sent out unto him their disciples with the Herodians, saying, Master, we know that thou art true, and teachest the way of God in truth, neither carest thou for any man: for thou regardest not the person of men.

17 Tell us therefore, What thinkest thou? Is it lawful to give tribute unto Caesar, or not?

18 But Jesus perceived their wickedness, and said, Why tempt ye me, ye hypocrites?

19 Shew me the tribute money. And they brought unto him a penny.

20 And he saith unto them, Whose is this image and superscription?

21 They say unto him, Caesar’s. Then saith he unto them, Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar’s; and unto God the things that are God’s.

22 When they had heard these words, they marvelled, and left him, and went their way.

23 The same day came to him the Sadducees, which say that there is no resurrection, and asked him,

24 Saying, Master, Moses said, If a man die, having no children, his brother shall marry his wife, and raise up seed unto his brother.

25 Now there were with us seven brethren: and the first, when he had married a wife, deceased, and, having no issue, left his wife unto his brother:

26 Likewise the second also, and the third, unto the seventh.

27 And last of all the woman died also.

28 Therefore in the resurrection whose wife shall she be of the seven? for they all had her.

29 Jesus answered and said unto them, Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God.

30 For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven.

31 But as touching the resurrection of the dead, have ye not read that which was spoken unto you by God, saying,

32 I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob? God is not the God of the dead, but of the living.

33 And when the multitude heard this, they were astonished at his doctrine.

34 But when the Pharisees had heard that he had put the Sadducees to silence, they were gathered together.

35 Then one of them, which was a lawyer, asked him a question, tempting him, and saying,

36 Master, which is the great commandment in the law?

37 Jesus said unto him, Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind.

38 This is the first and great commandment.

39 And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.

40 On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.

41 While the Pharisees were gathered together, Jesus asked them,

42 Saying, What think ye of Christ? whose son is he? They say unto him, The son of David.

43 He saith unto them, How then doth David in spirit call him Lord, saying,

44 The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand, till I make thine enemies thy footstool?

45 If David then call him Lord, how is he his son?

46 And no man was able to answer him a word, neither durst any man from that day forth ask him any more questions.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The parable of the wedding feast, with bidden guests refusing and a man without a wedding garment cast out, is followed by three traps: tribute to Caesar (“Render unto Caesar the things which are Caesar's”), the Sadducees' resurrection riddle of seven husbands, answered from “I am the God of Abraham”, and the lawyer's question, answered with love of God and love of neighbour as the two great commandments, on which hang all the law and the prophets. Jesus closes by asking how David calls the Christ Lord. Interpreted Reading of this chapter's text as printed above and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And Jesus answered and spake unto them again by parables, and said. The third parable of judgment in the temple follows the two sons and the husbandmen, pressing the same charge home in a new figure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The kingdom of heaven is like unto a certain king, which made a marriage for his son. In this parable we see how the Lord is revealing himself in ever clearer light as the central Person of the kingdom, giving here a far plainer hint than in the last parable of the nobility of his descent. There he was indeed the Son, the only and beloved one, of the Householder; but here his race is royal, and he appears as himself at once the King and the King's Son. The last was a parable of the Old Testament history; and Christ is rather the last and greatest of the line of its prophets and teachers than the founder of a new kingdom. In that, God appears demanding something from men; in this, a parable of grace, God appears more as giving something to them. Thus, as often, the two complete each other: this taking up the matter where the other left it. The “marriage” of Jehovah to his people Israel was familiar to Jewish ears; and in Psalm 45 this marriage is seen consummated in the Person of Messiah “THE KING,” himself addressed as “GOD” and yet as anointed by “HIS GOD” with the oil of gladness above his fellows. But observe carefully, that THE BRIDE does not come into view in this parable; its design being to teach certain truths under the figure of guests at a wedding feast, and the want of a wedding garment, which would not have harmonized with the introduction of the Bride. The provision made for perishing souls in the gospel is represented by a royal feast made by a king, with eastern liberality, on the marriage of his son. Our merciful God has not only provided food, but a royal feast, for the perishing souls of his rebellious creatures. There is enough and to spare, of every thing that can add to our present comfort and everlasting happiness, in the salvation of his Son Jesus Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And sent forth his servants to call them that were bidden to the wedding: and they would not come. The servants represent all preachers of the Gospel; those bidden here mean the Jews, who were “bidden,” from the first choice of them onwards through every summons addressed to them by the prophets to hold themselves in readiness for the appearing of their King. And they would not come — as the issue of the whole ministry of the Baptist, our Lord himself, and his apostles thereafter, too sadly showed. The guests first invited were the Jews. When the prophets of the Old Testament prevailed not, nor John the Baptist, nor Christ himself, who told them the kingdom of God was at hand, the apostles and ministers of the gospel were sent, after Christ's resurrection, to tell them it was come, and to persuade them to accept the offer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Again, he sent forth other servants, saying, Tell them which are bidden, Behold, I have prepared my dinner: my oxen and my fatlings are killed, and all things are ready: come unto the marriage. This points to those Gospel calls after Christ's death, resurrection, ascension, and effusion of the Spirit, to which the parable could not directly allude, but when only it could be said, with strict propriety, “that all things were ready.” Compare “Christ our Passover is sacrificed for us; therefore, let us keep the feast”; also “I am the living bread which came down from heaven: if any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever: and the bread which I will give is my flesh, which I will give for the life of the world.” Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. But they made light of it, and went their ways, one to his farm, another to his merchandise. The reason why sinners come not to Christ and salvation by him, is, not because they cannot, but because they will not. Making light of Christ, and of the great salvation wrought out by him, is the damning sin of the world. They were careless. Multitudes perish for ever through mere carelessness, who show no direct aversion, but are careless as to their souls. Also the business and profit of worldly employments hinder many in closing with the Saviour. Both farmers and merchants must be diligent; but whatever we have of the world in our hands, our care must be to keep it out of our hearts, lest it come between us and Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And the remnant took his servants, and entreated them spitefully, and slew them. These are two different classes of unbelievers: the one simply indifferent; the other absolutely hostile — the one, contemptuous scorners; the other, bitter persecutors. Persecution of Christ's faithful ministers fills up the measure of guilt of any people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But when the king heard thereof, he was wroth: and he sent forth his armies, and destroyed those murderers, and burned up their city. The king — the Great God, who is the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ — was wroth at the affront put both on his Son, and on himself who had deigned to invite them. He sent forth his armies — the Romans are here styled God's armies, just as the Assyrian is styled “the rod of his anger,” as being the executors of his judicial vengeance — and destroyed those murderers, and in what vast numbers did they do it! And burned up their city — Ah! Jerusalem, once “the city of the Great King,” and even up almost to this time; but now it is “their city” — just as our Lord, a day or two after this, said of the temple, where God had so long dwelt, “Behold your house is left unto you desolate”! The utter ruin coming upon the Jewish church and nation is here represented. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Then saith he to his servants, The wedding is ready, but they which were bidden were not worthy. For how should those be deemed worthy to sit down at his table who had affronted him by their treatment of his gracious invitation? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Go ye therefore into the highways, and as many as ye shall find, bid to the marriage. The highways are the great outlets and thoroughfares, whether of town or country, where human beings are to be found; and as many as ye shall find, bid to the marriage — that is, just as they are. The offer of Christ and salvation to the Gentiles was not expected; it was such a surprise as it would be to wayfaring men, to be invited to a royal wedding-feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. So those servants went out into the highways, and gathered together all as many as they found, both bad and good: and the wedding was furnished with guests. Without making any distinction between open sinners and the morally correct. The Gospel call fetched in Jews, Samaritans, and outlying heathen alike. The design of the gospel is to gather souls to Christ; all the children of God scattered abroad. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And when the king came in to see the guests, he saw there a man which had not on a wedding garment. Solemn expression this, of that omniscient inspection of every professed disciple of the Lord Jesus from age to age, in virtue of which his true character will hereafter be judicially proclaimed! This shows that it is the judgment of individuals which is intended in this latter part of the parable: the first part represents rather national judgment. The language here is drawn from Zephaniah 1: “Hold thy peace at the presence of the Lord God; for the day of the Lord is at hand: for the Lord hath prepared a sacrifice, he hath bid his guests. And it shall come to pass in the day of the Lord's sacrifice, that I will punish the princes, and the king's children, and all such as are clothed with strange apparel.” The custom in the East of presenting festival garments, even though not clearly proved, is certainly presupposed here. It undoubtedly means something which they bring not of their own — for how could they have any such dress who were gathered in from the highways indiscriminately? — but which they receive as their appropriate dress. And what can that be but what is meant by “putting on the Lord Jesus,” as “THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS?” Nor could such language be strange to those in whose ears had so long resounded those words of prophetic joy: “I will greatly rejoice in the Lord, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decketh himself with ornaments, and as a bride adorneth herself with her jewels.” The case of hypocrites is represented by the guest that had not on a wedding-garment. It concerns all to prepare for the scrutiny; and those, and those only, who put on the Lord Jesus, who have a Christian temper of mind, who live by faith in Christ, and to whom he is all in all, have the wedding-garment. The imputed righteousness of Christ, and the sanctification of the Spirit, are both alike necessary. No man has the wedding-garment by nature, or can form it for himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And he saith unto him, Friend, how camest thou in hither not having a wedding garment? And he was speechless. Being self-condemned. The day is coming, when hypocrites will be called to account for all their presumptuous intruding into gospel ordinances, and usurpation of gospel privileges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Then said the king to the servants, Bind him hand and foot, and take him away, and cast him into outer darkness; there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth. The servants are the angelic ministers of divine vengeance. Bind him hand and foot — putting it out of his power to resist — and cast him into outer darkness. The expression is emphatic — “the darkness which is outside.” To be “outside” at all — or, in the language of Revelation, to be “without” the heavenly city, excluded from its joyous nuptials and gladsome festivities — is sad enough of itself, without anything else. But to find themselves not only excluded from the brightness and glory and joy and felicity of the kingdom above, but thrust into a region of “darkness,” with all its horrors, this is the dismal retribution here announced, that awaits the unworthy at the great day. Take him away. Those that walk unworthy of Christianity, forfeit all the happiness they presumptuously claimed. Our Saviour here passes out of the parable into that which it teaches. Hypocrites go by the light of the gospel itself down to utter darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. For many are called, but few are chosen. Many are called to the wedding-feast, that is, to salvation, but few have the wedding-garment, the righteousness of Christ, the sanctification of the Spirit. Then let us examine ourselves whether we are in the faith, and seek to be approved by the King. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Then went the Pharisees, and took counsel how they might entangle him in his talk. The Pharisees sent their disciples with the Herodians, a party among the Jews, who were for full subjection to the Roman emperor. Though opposed to each other, they joined against Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And they sent out unto him their disciples with the Herodians, saying, Master, we know that thou art true, and teachest the way of God in truth, neither carest thou for any man: for thou regardest not the person of men. What they said of Christ was right; whether they knew it or not, blessed be God we know it. Jesus Christ was a faithful Teacher, and a bold reprover. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Tell us therefore, What thinkest thou? Is it lawful to give tribute unto Caesar, or not? The question is a trap either way: answer yes, and lose the people; answer no, and face Rome. The questioners care nothing for the answer, only for the entanglement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. But Jesus perceived their wickedness, and said, Why tempt ye me, ye hypocrites? Christ saw their wickedness. Whatever mask the hypocrite puts on, our Lord Jesus sees through it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Shew me the tribute money. And they brought unto him a penny. The coin itself will answer the question: it bears the emperor's image, and circulates by his authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And he saith unto them, Whose is this image and superscription? The question forces them to name Caesar as owner of the coin they carry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. They say unto him, Caesar's. Then saith he unto them, Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar's; and unto God the things that are God's. Christ did not interpose as a judge in matters of this nature, for his kingdom is not of this world, but he enjoins peaceable subjection to the powers that be. His adversaries were reproved, and his disciples were taught that the Christian religion is no enemy to civil government. The coin bears Caesar's image; man bears God's image, and belongs to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. When they had heard these words, they marvelled, and left him, and went their way. Christ is, and will be, the wonder, not only of his friends, but of his enemies. They admire his wisdom, but will not be guided by it; his power, but will not submit to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The same day came to him the Sadducees, which say that there is no resurrection, and asked him. The doctrines of Christ displeased the infidel Sadducees, as well as the Pharisees and Herodians. He carried the great truths of the resurrection and a future state, further than they had yet been revealed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Saying, Master, Moses said, If a man die, having no children, his brother shall marry his wife, and raise up seed unto his brother. The Sadducees cite the levirate law, intending to make resurrection absurd by multiplying husbands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Now there were with us seven brethren: and the first, when he had married a wife, deceased, and, having no issue, left his wife unto his brother. The case is built step by step, each brother in turn marrying the widow and dying childless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Likewise the second also, and the third, unto the seventh. Seven husbands in succession, and still no seed: the Sadducees' arithmetic is complete, and, they think, conclusive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And last of all the woman died also. The widow outlives them all, and dies last — the case now stands ready for their question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Therefore in the resurrection whose wife shall she be of the seven? for they all had her. There is no arguing from the state of things in this world, as to what will take place hereafter; the Sadducees' question assumes the resurrection life is only this life continued. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Jesus answered and said unto them, Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God. All errors arise from not knowing the Scriptures and the power of God. Let truth be set in a clear light, and it appears in full strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven. In this world death takes away one after another, and so ends all earthly hopes, joys, sorrows, and connexions. How wretched are those who look for nothing better beyond the grave! In the resurrection, marriage — an ordinance for this dying world — gives place to the angels' state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. But as touching the resurrection of the dead, have ye not read that which was spoken unto you by God, saying. Having thus silenced them, our Lord proceeded to show the truth of the doctrine of the resurrection from the books of Moses — the very books the Sadducees accepted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. God is not the God of the dead, but of the living. God declared to Moses that he was the God of the patriarchs, who had died long before; this shows that they were then in a state of being, capable of enjoying his favour, and proves that the doctrine of the resurrection is clearly taught in the Old Testament as well as in the New. But this doctrine was kept for a more full revelation, after the resurrection of Christ, who was the first-fruits of them that slept. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And when the multitude heard this, they were astonished at his doctrine. The crowd, who had watched the Sadducees' trap, is astonished at the doctrine that answers it from Moses himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. But when the Pharisees had heard that he had put the Sadducees to silence, they were gathered together. The Pharisees, hearing their rivals silenced, gather for a fresh attempt — enemies of Christ uniting in relays. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Then one of them, which was a lawyer, asked him a question, tempting him, and saying. An interpreter of the law asked our Lord a question, to try, not so much his knowledge, as his judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Master, which is the great commandment in the law? The lawyer asks for the greatest commandment, hoping to catch him ranking God's laws against each other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Jesus said unto him, Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. The love of God is the first and great commandment, and the sum of all the commands of the first table. Our love of God must be sincere, not in word and tongue only. All our love is too little to bestow upon him, therefore all the powers of the soul must be engaged for him, and carried out toward him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. This is the first and great commandment. First in order and great in weight: love of God heads the whole law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. To love our neighbour as ourselves is the second great commandment. There is a self-love which is corrupt, and the root of the greatest sins, and it must be put off and mortified; but there is a self-love which is the rule of the greatest duty: we must have a due concern for the welfare of our own souls and bodies. And we must love our neighbour as truly and sincerely as we love ourselves; in many cases we must deny ourselves for the good of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets. By these two commandments let our hearts be formed as by a mould: the whole law hangs on love of God and love of neighbour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. While the Pharisees were gathered together, Jesus asked them. When Christ baffled his enemies, he asked what thoughts they had of the promised Messiah? How he could be the Son of David and yet his Lord? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Saying, What think ye of Christ? whose son is he? They say unto him, The Son of David. The Pharisees answer readily: the Christ is David's son — true, but not the whole truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. He saith unto them, How then doth David in spirit call him Lord, saying. He quotes Psalm 110. If the Christ was to be a mere man, who would not exist till many ages after David's death, how could his forefather call him Lord? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. The Lord said unto my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand, till I make thine enemies thy footstool. David's Lord sits at Jehovah's right hand: the Messiah is enthroned above David, not merely descended from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. If David then call him Lord, how is he his son? The Pharisees could not answer it. Nor can any solve the difficulty except he allows the Messiah to be the Son of God, and David's Lord equally with the Father. He took upon him human nature, and so became God manifested in the flesh; in this sense he is the Son of man and the Son of David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. And no man was able to answer him a word, neither durst any man from that day forth ask him any more questions. It behoves us above all things seriously to inquire, “What think we of Christ?” Is he altogether glorious in our eyes, and precious to our hearts? May Christ be our joy, our confidence, our all. May we daily be made more like to him, and more devoted to his service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

He carried the great truths of the resurrection and a future state, further than they had yet been revealed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Saying, Master, Moses said, If a man die, having no children, his brother shall marry his wife, and raise up seed unto his brother. The Sadducees cite the levirate law, intending to make resurrection absurd by multiplying husbands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Now there were with us seven brethren: and the first, when he had married a wife, deceased, and, having no issue, left his wife unto his brother. The case is built step by step, each brother in turn marrying the widow and dying childless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Likewise the second also, and the third, unto the seventh. Seven husbands in succession, and still no seed: the Sadducees' arithmetic is complete, and, they think, conclusive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And last of all the woman died also. The widow outlives them all, and dies last — the case now stands ready for their question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Therefore in the resurrection whose wife shall she be of the seven? for they all had her. There is no arguing from the state of things in this world, as to what will take place hereafter; the Sadducees' question assumes the resurrection life is only this life continued. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Jesus answered and said unto them, Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God. All errors arise from not knowing the Scriptures and the power of God. Let truth be set in a clear light, and it appears in full strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven. In this world death takes away one after another, and so ends all earthly hopes, joys, sorrows, and connexions. How wretched are those who look for nothing better beyond the grave! In the resurrection, marriage — an ordinance for this dying world — gives place to the angels' state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. But as touching the resurrection of the dead, have ye not read that which was spoken unto you by God, saying. Having thus silenced them, our Lord proceeded to show the truth of the doctrine of the resurrection from the books of Moses — the very books the Sadducees accepted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. God is not the God of the dead, but of the living. God declared to Moses that he was the God of the patriarchs, who had died long before; this shows that they were then in a state of being, capable of enjoying his favour, and proves that the doctrine of the resurrection is clearly taught in the Old Testament as well as in the New. But this doctrine was kept for a more full revelation, after the resurrection of Christ, who was the first-fruits of them that slept. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And when the multitude heard this, they were astonished at his doctrine. The crowd, who had watched the Sadducees' trap, is astonished at the doctrine that answers it from Moses himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. But when the Pharisees had heard that he had put the Sadducees to silence, they were gathered together. The Pharisees, hearing their rivals silenced, gather for a fresh attempt — enemies of Christ uniting in relays. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Then one of them, which was a lawyer, asked him a question, tempting him, and saying. An interpreter of the law asked our Lord a question, to try, not so much his knowledge, as his judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Master, which is the great commandment in the law? The lawyer asks for the greatest commandment, hoping to catch him ranking God's laws against each other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Jesus said unto him, Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. The love of God is the first and great commandment, and the sum of all the commands of the first table. Our love of God must be sincere, not in word and tongue only. All our love is too little to bestow upon him, therefore all the powers of the soul must be engaged for him, and carried out toward him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. This is the first and great commandment. First in order and great in weight: love of God heads the whole law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. To love our neighbour as ourselves is the second great commandment. There is a self-love which is corrupt, and the root of the greatest sins, and it must be put off and mortified; but there is a self-love which is the rule of the greatest duty: we must have a due concern for the welfare of our own souls and bodies. And we must love our neighbour as truly and sincerely as we love ourselves; in many cases we must deny ourselves for the good of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets. By these two commandments let our hearts be formed as by a mould: the whole law hangs on love of God and love of neighbour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. While the Pharisees were gathered together, Jesus asked them. When Christ baffled his enemies, he asked what thoughts they had of the promised Messiah? How he could be the Son of David and yet his Lord? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Saying, What think ye of Christ? whose son is he? They say unto him, The Son of David. The Pharisees answer readily: the Christ is David's son — true, but not the whole truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. He saith unto them, How then doth David in spirit call him Lord, saying. He quotes Psalm 110. If the Christ was to be a mere man, who would not exist till many ages after David's death, how could his forefather call him Lord? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. The Lord said unto my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand, till I make thine enemies thy footstool. David's Lord sits at Jehovah's right hand: the Messiah is enthroned above David, not merely descended from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. If David then call him Lord, how is he his son? The Pharisees could not answer it. Nor can any solve the difficulty except he allows the Messiah to be the Son of God, and David's Lord equally with the Father. He took upon him human nature, and so became God manifested in the flesh; in this sense he is the Son of man and the Son of David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. And no man was able to answer him a word, neither durst any man from that day forth ask him any more questions. It behoves us above all things seriously to inquire, “What think we of Christ?” Is he altogether glorious in our eyes, and precious to our hearts? May Christ be our joy, our confidence, our all. May we daily be made more like to him, and more devoted to his service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 2. The kingdom of heaven is like unto a certain king, which made a marriage for his son. In this parable we see how the Lord is revealing himself in ever clearer light as the central Person of the kingdom, giving here a far plainer hint than in the last parable of the nobility of his descent. There he was indeed the Son, the only and beloved one, of the Householder; but here his race is royal, and he appears as himself at once the King and the King's Son. The last was a parable of the Old Testament history; and Christ is rather the last and greatest of the line of its prophets and teachers than the founder of a new kingdom. In that, God appears demanding something from men; in this, a parable of grace, God appears more as giving something to them. Thus, as often, the two complete each other: this taking up the matter where the other left it. The “marriage” of Jehovah to his people Israel was familiar to Jewish ears; and in Psalm 45 this marriage is seen consummated in the Person of Messiah “THE KING,” himself addressed as “GOD” and yet as anointed by “HIS GOD” with the oil of gladness above his fellows. But observe carefully, that THE BRIDE does not come into view in this parable; its design being to teach certain truths under the figure of guests at a wedding feast, and the want of a wedding garment, which would not have harmonized with the introduction of the Bride. The provision made for perishing souls in the gospel is represented by a royal feast made by a king, with eastern liberality, on the marriage of his son. Our merciful God has not only provided food, but a royal feast, for the perishing souls of his rebellious creatures. There is enough and to spare, of every thing that can add to our present comfort and everlasting happiness, in the salvation of his Son Jesus Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And sent forth his servants to call them that were bidden to the wedding: and they would not come. The servants represent all preachers of the Gospel; those bidden here mean the Jews, who were “bidden,” from the first choice of them onwards through every summons addressed to them by the prophets to hold themselves in readiness for the appearing of their King. And they would not come — as the issue of the whole ministry of the Baptist, our Lord himself, and his apostles thereafter, too sadly showed. The guests first invited were the Jews. When the prophets of the Old Testament prevailed not, nor John the Baptist, nor Christ himself, who told them the kingdom of God was at hand, the apostles and ministers of the gospel were sent, after Christ's resurrection, to tell them it was come, and to persuade them to accept the offer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Again, he sent forth other servants, saying, Tell them which are bidden, Behold, I have prepared my dinner: my oxen and my fatlings are killed, and all things are ready: come unto the marriage. This points to those Gospel calls after Christ's death, resurrection, ascension, and effusion of the Spirit, to which the parable could not directly allude, but when only it could be said, with strict propriety, “that all things were ready.” Compare “Christ our Passover is sacrificed for us; therefore, let us keep the feast”; also “I am the living bread which came down from heaven: if any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever: and the bread which I will give is my flesh, which I will give for the life of the world.” Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. But they made light of it, and went their ways, one to his farm, another to his merchandise. The reason why sinners come not to Christ and salvation by him, is, not because they cannot, but because they will not. Making light of Christ, and of the great salvation wrought out by him, is the damning sin of the world. They were careless. Multitudes perish for ever through mere carelessness, who show no direct aversion, but are careless as to their souls. Also the business and profit of worldly employments hinder many in closing with the Saviour. Both farmers and merchants must be diligent; but whatever we have of the world in our hands, our care must be to keep it out of our hearts, lest it come between us and Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And the remnant took his servants, and entreated them spitefully, and slew them. These are two different classes of unbelievers: the one simply indifferent; the other absolutely hostile — the one, contemptuous scorners; the other, bitter persecutors. Persecution of Christ's faithful ministers fills up the measure of guilt of any people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But when the king heard thereof, he was wroth: and he sent forth his armies, and destroyed those murderers, and burned up their city. The king — the Great God, who is the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ — was wroth at the affront put both on his Son, and on himself who had deigned to invite them. He sent forth his armies — the Romans are here styled God's armies, just as the Assyrian is styled “the rod of his anger,” as being the executors of his judicial vengeance — and destroyed those murderers, and in what vast numbers did they do it! And burned up their city — Ah! Jerusalem, once “the city of the Great King,” and even up almost to this time; but now it is “their city” — just as our Lord, a day or two after this, said of the temple, where God had so long dwelt, “Behold your house is left unto you desolate”! The utter ruin coming upon the Jewish church and nation is here represented. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Then saith he to his servants, The wedding is ready, but they which were bidden were not worthy. For how should those be deemed worthy to sit down at his table who had affronted him by their treatment of his gracious invitation? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Go ye therefore into the highways, and as many as ye shall find, bid to the marriage. The highways are the great outlets and thoroughfares, whether of town or country, where human beings are to be found; and as many as ye shall find, bid to the marriage — that is, just as they are. The offer of Christ and salvation to the Gentiles was not expected; it was such a surprise as it would be to wayfaring men, to be invited to a royal wedding-feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. So those servants went out into the highways, and gathered together all as many as they found, both bad and good: and the wedding was furnished with guests. Without making any distinction between open sinners and the morally correct. The Gospel call fetched in Jews, Samaritans, and outlying heathen alike. The design of the gospel is to gather souls to Christ; all the children of God scattered abroad. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And when the king came in to see the guests, he saw there a man which had not on a wedding garment. Solemn expression this, of that omniscient inspection of every professed disciple of the Lord Jesus from age to age, in virtue of which his true character will hereafter be judicially proclaimed! This shows that it is the judgment of individuals which is intended in this latter part of the parable: the first part represents rather national judgment. The language here is drawn from Zephaniah 1: “Hold thy peace at the presence of the Lord God; for the day of the Lord is at hand: for the Lord hath prepared a sacrifice, he hath bid his guests. And it shall come to pass in the day of the Lord's sacrifice, that I will punish the princes, and the king's children, and all such as are clothed with strange apparel.” The custom in the East of presenting festival garments, even though not clearly proved, is certainly presupposed here. It undoubtedly means something which they bring not of their own — for how could they have any such dress who were gathered in from the highways indiscriminately? — but which they receive as their appropriate dress. And what can that be but what is meant by “putting on the Lord Jesus,” as “THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS?” Nor could such language be strange to those in whose ears had so long resounded those words of prophetic joy: “I will greatly rejoice in the Lord, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decketh himself with ornaments, and as a bride adorneth herself with her jewels.” The case of hypocrites is represented by the guest that had not on a wedding-garment. It concerns all to prepare for the scrutiny; and those, and those only, who put on the Lord Jesus, who have a Christian temper of mind, who live by faith in Christ, and to whom he is all in all, have the wedding-garment. The imputed righteousness of Christ, and the sanctification of the Spirit, are both alike necessary. No man has the wedding-garment by nature, or can form it for himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And he saith unto him, Friend, how camest thou in hither not having a wedding garment? And he was speechless. Being self-condemned. The day is coming, when hypocrites will be called to account for all their presumptuous intruding into gospel ordinances, and usurpation of gospel privileges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Then said the king to the servants, Bind him hand and foot, and take him away, and cast him into outer darkness; there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth. The servants are the angelic ministers of divine vengeance. Bind him hand and foot — putting it out of his power to resist — and cast him into outer darkness. The expression is emphatic — “the darkness which is outside.” To be “outside” at all — or, in the language of Revelation, to be “without” the heavenly city, excluded from its joyous nuptials and gladsome festivities — is sad enough of itself, without anything else. But to find themselves not only excluded from the brightness and glory and joy and felicity of the kingdom above, but thrust into a region of “darkness,” with all its horrors, this is the dismal retribution here announced, that awaits the unworthy at the great day. Take him away. Those that walk unworthy of Christianity, forfeit all the happiness they presumptuously claimed. Our Saviour here passes out of the parable into that which it teaches. Hypocrites go by the light of the gospel itself down to utter darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. For many are called, but few are chosen. Many are called to the wedding-feast, that is, to salvation, but few have the wedding-garment, the righteousness of Christ, the sanctification of the Spirit. Then let us examine ourselves whether we are in the faith, and seek to be approved by the King. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
