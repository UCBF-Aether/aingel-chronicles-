The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 These also are the generations of Aaron and Moses in the day that the LORD spake with Moses in mount Sinai.

2 And these are the names of the sons of Aaron; Nadab the firstborn, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.

3 These are the names of the sons of Aaron, the priests which were anointed, whom he consecrated to minister in the priest’s office.

4 And Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD, when they offered strange fire before the LORD, in the wilderness of Sinai, and they had no children: and Eleazar and Ithamar ministered in the priest’s office in the sight of Aaron their father.

5 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

6 Bring the tribe of Levi near, and present them before Aaron the priest, that they may minister unto him.

7 And they shall keep his charge, and the charge of the whole congregation before the tabernacle of the congregation, to do the service of the tabernacle.

8 And they shall keep all the instruments of the tabernacle of the congregation, and the charge of the children of Israel, to do the service of the tabernacle.

9 And thou shalt give the Levites unto Aaron and to his sons: they are wholly given unto him out of the children of Israel.

10 And thou shalt appoint Aaron and his sons, and they shall wait on their priest’s office: and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death.

11 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

12 And I, behold, I have taken the Levites from among the children of Israel instead of all the firstborn that openeth the matrix among the children of Israel: therefore the Levites shall be mine;

13 Because all the firstborn are mine; for on the day that I smote all the firstborn in the land of Egypt I hallowed unto me all the firstborn in Israel, both man and beast: mine shall they be: I am the LORD.

14 And the LORD spake unto Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, saying,

15 Number the children of Levi after the house of their fathers, by their families: every male from a month old and upward shalt thou number them.

16 And Moses numbered them according to the word of the LORD, as he was commanded.

17 And these were the sons of Levi by their names; Gershon, and Kohath, and Merari.

18 And these are the names of the sons of Gershon by their families; Libni, and Shimei.

19 And the sons of Kohath by their families; Amram, and Izehar, Hebron, and Uzziel.

20 And the sons of Merari by their families; Mahli, and Mushi. These are the families of the Levites according to the house of their fathers.

21 Of Gershon was the family of the Libnites, and the family of the Shimites: these are the families of the Gershonites.

22 Those that were numbered of them, according to the number of all the males, from a month old and upward, even those that were numbered of them were seven thousand and five hundred.

23 The families of the Gershonites shall pitch behind the tabernacle westward.

24 And the chief of the house of the father of the Gershonites shall be Eliasaph the son of Lael.

25 And the charge of the sons of Gershon in the tabernacle of the congregation shall be the tabernacle, and the tent, the covering thereof, and the hanging for the door of the tabernacle of the congregation,

26 And the hangings of the court, and the curtain for the door of the court, which is by the tabernacle, and by the altar round about, and the cords of it for all the service thereof.

27 And of Kohath was the family of the Amramites, and the family of the Izeharites, and the family of the Hebronites, and the family of the Uzzielites: these are the families of the Kohathites.

28 In the number of all the males, from a month old and upward, were eight thousand and six hundred, keeping the charge of the sanctuary.

29 The families of the sons of Kohath shall pitch on the side of the tabernacle southward.

30 And the chief of the house of the father of the families of the Kohathites shall be Elizaphan the son of Uzziel.

31 And their charge shall be the ark, and the table, and the candlestick, and the altars, and the vessels of the sanctuary wherewith they minister, and the hanging, and all the service thereof.

32 And Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest shall be chief over the chief of the Levites, and have the oversight of them that keep the charge of the sanctuary.

33 Of Merari was the family of the Mahlites, and the family of the Mushites: these are the families of Merari.

34 And those that were numbered of them, according to the number of all the males, from a month old and upward, were six thousand and two hundred.

35 And the chief of the house of the father of the families of Merari was Zuriel the son of Abihail: these shall pitch on the side of the tabernacle northward.

36 And under the custody and charge of the sons of Merari shall be the boards of the tabernacle, and the bars thereof, and the pillars thereof, and the sockets thereof, and all the vessels thereof, and all that serveth thereto,

37 And the pillars of the court round about, and their sockets, and their pins, and their cords.

38 But those that encamp before the tabernacle toward the east, even before the tabernacle of the congregation eastward, shall be Moses, and Aaron and his sons, keeping the charge of the sanctuary for the charge of the children of Israel; and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death.

39 All that were numbered of the Levites, which Moses and Aaron numbered at the commandment of the LORD, throughout their families, all the males from a month old and upward, were twenty and two thousand.

40 And the LORD said unto Moses, Number all the firstborn of the males of the children of Israel from a month old and upward, and take the number of their names.

41 And thou shalt take the Levites for me (I am the LORD) instead of all the firstborn among the children of Israel; and the cattle of the Levites instead of all the firstlings among the cattle of the children of Israel.

42 And Moses numbered, as the LORD commanded him, all the firstborn among the children of Israel.

43 And all the firstborn males by the number of names, from a month old and upward, of those that were numbered of them, were twenty and two thousand two hundred and threescore and thirteen.

44 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

45 Take the Levites instead of all the firstborn among the children of Israel, and the cattle of the Levites instead of their cattle; and the Levites shall be mine: I am the LORD.

46 And for those that are to be redeemed of the two hundred and threescore and thirteen of the firstborn of the children of Israel, which are more than the Levites;

47 Thou shalt even take five shekels apiece by the poll, after the shekel of the sanctuary shalt thou take them: (the shekel is twenty gerahs:)

48 And thou shalt give the money, wherewith the odd number of them is to be redeemed, unto Aaron and to his sons.

49 And Moses took the redemption money of them that were over and above them that were redeemed by the Levites:

50 Of the firstborn of the children of Israel took he the money; a thousand three hundred and threescore and five shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary:

51 And Moses gave the money of them that were redeemed unto Aaron and to his sons, according to the word of the LORD, as the LORD commanded Moses.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter sets out the Levites' place among Israel. Aaron's sons are named, with Nadab and Abihu having died for offering strange fire, and the Levites are given to Aaron and his sons in place of every firstborn of Israel, whom the LORD claimed. The three Levitical families are stationed around the tabernacle: Gershon on the west, Kohath on the south, Merari on the north, with Moses and Aaron on the east. A census of firstborn males counts 22,273 against 22,000 Levites, and the 273 surplus are redeemed at five shekels each, paid to Aaron and his sons. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Firstborn. Every firstborn male of Israel, claimed by the LORD; the Levites serve in their place, and the surplus of 273 firstborn are redeemed at five shekels each. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These are the generations of Aaron and Moses from the day the Lord spoke with Moses on Sinai. JFB explains why Aaron is named before Moses here, contrary to usual style: the priesthood is the subject, and that dignity belonged exclusively to Aaron's posterity, while Moses' children stood in the private ranks of the Levites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Aaron's sons were four, all consecrated to the priest's office. JFB notes the two oldest enjoyed only a brief term of office, while Eleazar and Ithamar served dutifully during their father's lifetime as his assistants, under his superintendence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. These sons were the anointed priests, consecrated to minister. The anointing set them apart for the work alone: priesthood here is not inherited honour in general, but a specific consecration to a specific office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Nadab and Abihu died before the Lord when they offered strange fire in the wilderness of Sinai, leaving no children. Their brief term stands as the warning that consecration does not license carelessness about God's appointed worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Lord spoke to Moses about bringing Levi near, and JFB notes the Hebrew word for bringing near is a sacrificial term: presenting an offering to God. The Levites were thus themselves devoted as an offering to the sanctuary, no longer to be employed in common offices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Levi was presented before Aaron to minister to him, subordinate to the priests who alone entered the holy place. Their humbler duties were nevertheless of great utility and importance to the religion and morals of the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The Levites kept Aaron's charge and the charge of the whole congregation before the tabernacle. Henry's remark frames the whole chapter: those whom God finds work for, he will find help for, and Aaron, left with only two sons for much priestly work, was given the whole tribe as helpers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The Levites kept all the instruments of the tabernacle and the charge of Israel. Their service was practical and constant: the vessels and furniture of worship were in their hands, even though the priestly acts themselves were not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The Levites were wholly given to Aaron and his sons out of Israel. JFB puts it sharply: the priests hold the place of God, and the Levites are the servants of God in the obedience they render to the priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Aaron and his sons waited on the priest's office, and the stranger who came near would die. The severity guards the honour of holy things: none might see or touch them but those called of God to the service, as Henry had already stressed in chapter 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The Lord spoke again, and JFB stresses that Levi's consecration did not originate in Moses' legislative wisdom but in God's special appointment. The tribe was chosen partly as a tribute to Moses and Aaron, partly for its zeal in the golden calf affair, and partly because, being the smallest tribe, it could ill find other employment and support. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. God took the Levites instead of all the firstborn among Israel. The exchange recalled the last judgment on Egypt, from which Israelite households were miraculously exempt: all firstborn had been consecrated to God, and now one whole tribe stood in their place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. All the firstborn are God's because on the day he smote Egypt's firstborn he hallowed Israel's. Henry draws the redemption lesson: when he who made us also saves us, as the firstborn were saved, we are under further obligation to serve him faithfully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Levi was numbered in the wilderness of Sinai, but on a different principle from the other tribes. JFB explains: the other tribes were counted from twenty years upward for war, from which Levites were totally exempt, while Levites were counted from a month old upward, because they entered a work for which they were trained from childhood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Levi was numbered after fathers' houses and families, every male from a month old upward. The lower age reflects the nature of the service: Levi's work was not the battlefield but the sanctuary, entered as soon as a boy was capable of instruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Moses numbered them according to the word of the Lord, as he was commanded. The census of Levi, like that of Israel, was an act of obedience, not administration for its own sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Levi's sons were Gershon, Kohath and Merari, the three ancestral heads of the tribe's subdivisions. JFB notes their stations: Gershonites on the west, Kohathites on the south, Merarites on the north, with Moses and Aaron on the honoured east side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The Gershonite families pitched behind the tabernacle westward. Their western station, second in honour, matched Gershon's place as the eldest son, while the heavier drudgery fell to Merari. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The charge of Gershon's sons was the tabernacle itself: its coverings, hangings and cords. Their work was the fabric of the dwelling, the outer shelter of the holy place, carried and kept by their families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. The Kohathite families pitched on the south side of the tabernacle. They held the principal place about the sanctuary, JFB notes, probably because the Aaronic family belonged to this division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. The Kohathites' charge was the ark, the table, the candlestick, the altars and the vessels of the sanctuary. They carried the most precious and sacred things, the very furniture of God's presence, while the priests themselves covered them first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Eleazar, son of Aaron, was chief over the chief of the Levites, overseeing all who kept the charge of the sanctuary. JFB notes he was therefore called the second priest, and performed the high priest's duties in his absence from illness or necessity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. The Merarites' charge was the boards, bars, pillars, sockets, pins and cords of the tabernacle and court. Theirs was the heavy framework, the drudgery of the structure, without which the coverings Gershon carried had nothing to hang on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. Moses, Aaron and his sons encamped before the tabernacle eastward, on the entrance side. JFB notes this was the post of honour, reserved to Moses and the priestly family, while the sons of Moses himself had no station here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. The Levites numbered twenty-two thousand males from a month old upward. JFB notes the separate family numbers add to twenty-two thousand three hundred, and the missing three hundred is variously explained, most probably by a copyist's confusion of similar Hebrew letters used as figures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. The firstborn males of Israel were now numbered on the same principle, from a month old upward, so a comparison could be made with Levi. The two numbers came near each other, and Henry remarks that known unto God are all his works beforehand: there is an exact proportion between them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. The Levites were taken for the Lord instead of all the firstborn, and their cattle instead of the firstlings of Israel's cattle. The exchange covered beast as well as man, so that firstlings were not then brought to the altar as they afterwards were. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. The firstborn males numbered twenty-two thousand two hundred and seventy-three, leaving an excess of two hundred and seventy-three over the Levites. JFB notes the excess is so small that it is best explained by counting only firstborn still in their parents' households, or only those born since the departure from Egypt, when God claimed all the firstborn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. The Lord spoke again, confirming the exchange: Levites for firstborn, Levite cattle for firstlings. The repetition seals the substitution as God's own decree: JFB renders the divine I am the Lord here as, I decree it to be so, and expect full obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. The two hundred and seventy-three firstborn over the number of the Levites were to be redeemed. Since there were not Levites enough for an equal exchange, some parents had to incur the expense, and Jewish writers say the matter was settled by lot, with slips marked for a son of Levi or for five shekels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. The redemption price was five shekels apiece by the poll, after the shekel of the sanctuary. JFB reckons the shekel at half a crown, making each ransom twelve shillings and sixpence, appropriated to the use of the sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. The redemption money was given to Aaron and his sons. The payment went to the priesthood because the firstborn, had they not been exchanged, would have served the sanctuary themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. Moses took the redemption money of the firstborn, a thousand three hundred and threescore and five shekels. The exact sum, five shekels times two hundred and seventy-three, shows the redemption was reckoned man by man, not waved in round numbers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. Moses gave the money to Aaron and his sons according to the word of the Lord. Henry closes the chapter with the church lesson: the church is called the church of the firstborn, redeemed not with silver and gold but with the precious blood of the Son of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
