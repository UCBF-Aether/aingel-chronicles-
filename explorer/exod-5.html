The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And afterward Moses and Aaron went in, and told Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me in the wilderness.

2 And Pharaoh said, Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice to let Israel go? I know not the LORD, neither will I let Israel go.

3 And they said, The God of the Hebrews hath met with us: let us go, we pray thee, three days’ journey into the desert, and sacrifice unto the LORD our God; lest he fall upon us with pestilence, or with the sword.

4 And the king of Egypt said unto them, Wherefore do ye, Moses and Aaron, let the people from their works? get you unto your burdens.

5 And Pharaoh said, Behold, the people of the land now are many, and ye make them rest from their burdens.

6 And Pharaoh commanded the same day the taskmasters of the people, and their officers, saying,

7 Ye shall no more give the people straw to make brick, as heretofore: let them go and gather straw for themselves.

8 And the tale of the bricks, which they did make heretofore, ye shall lay upon them; ye shall not diminish ought thereof: for they be idle; therefore they cry, saying, Let us go and sacrifice to our God.

9 Let there more work be laid upon the men, that they may labour therein; and let them not regard vain words.

10 And the taskmasters of the people went out, and their officers, and they spake to the people, saying, Thus saith Pharaoh, I will not give you straw.

11 Go ye, get you straw where ye can find it: yet not ought of your work shall be diminished.

12 So the people were scattered abroad throughout all the land of Egypt to gather stubble instead of straw.

13 And the taskmasters hasted them, saying, Fulfil your works, your daily tasks, as when there was straw.

14 And the officers of the children of Israel, which Pharaoh’s taskmasters had set over them, were beaten, and demanded, Wherefore have ye not fulfilled your task in making brick both yesterday and to day, as heretofore?

15 Then the officers of the children of Israel came and cried unto Pharaoh, saying, Wherefore dealest thou thus with thy servants?

16 There is no straw given unto thy servants, and they say to us, Make brick: and, behold, thy servants are beaten; but the fault is in thine own people.

17 But he said, Ye are idle, ye are idle: therefore ye say, Let us go and do sacrifice to the LORD.

18 Go therefore now, and work; for there shall no straw be given you, yet shall ye deliver the tale of bricks.

19 And the officers of the children of Israel did see that they were in evil case, after it was said, Ye shall not minish ought from your bricks of your daily task.

20 And they met Moses and Aaron, who stood in the way, as they came forth from Pharaoh:

21 And they said unto them, The LORD look upon you, and judge; because ye have made our savour to be abhorred in the eyes of Pharaoh, and in the eyes of his servants, to put a sword in their hand to slay us.

22 And Moses returned unto the LORD, and said, LORD, wherefore hast thou so evil entreated this people? why is it that thou hast sent me?

23 For since I came to Pharaoh to speak in thy name, he hath done evil to this people; neither hast thou delivered thy people at all.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Moses and Aaron ask Pharaoh to let Israel go to hold a feast in the wilderness. Pharaoh refuses, accuses them of idling the people, and makes the bondage harder: straw is withheld while the same daily quota of bricks is still demanded. The Israelite officers are beaten and blame Moses, and Moses turns to the LORD in complaint. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Moses and Aaron went in to Pharaoh with the LORD's demand: Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me in the wilderness. Henry notes the first demand was modest and reasonable, a feast in the wilderness, not yet full departure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Pharaoh answered, Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice? I know not the LORD, neither will I let Israel go. Henry observes proud ignorance: Pharaoh owned no god above himself and treated the LORD as a stranger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They replied that the God of the Hebrews had met with them, and asked leave for three days' journey to sacrifice, lest God fall upon them with pestilence or sword. Henry notes the plea was framed humbly, as a religious duty, to leave Pharaoh without excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The king charged Moses and Aaron with letting the people from their works, bidding them return to their burdens. Henry observes tyrants call godly labour idleness and religious liberty a hindrance to profit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Pharaoh said the people of the land were now many, and Moses and Aaron made them rest from their burdens. Henry notes his fear was the people's numbers, and his remedy was more work, not justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The same day Pharaoh commanded the taskmasters and the officers of the people. Henry observes the speed of oppression: the answer to a prayer for liberty was a heavier yoke within the day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. No more straw was to be given for brick-making; the people must go and gather straw themselves. Henry notes the cruelty was deliberate: the material was withdrawn but the demand was not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The tale of bricks from former days was still to be laid upon them, undiminished. Henry observes this was oppression refined: the same output required with less supplied, so that failure itself became the crime. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. More work was to be laid upon the men, that they might labour therein and not regard vain words. Henry notes Pharaoh's policy: keep the people too weary to listen to Moses, and call God's word vain talk. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The taskmasters and officers went out and told the people Pharaoh's command: no straw would be given, yet the tale must be delivered. Henry observes the messengers of tyranny delivered the sentence as if it were justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The people were told to go and get straw where they could find it, yet nothing of their work would be diminished. Henry notes the impossible command was itself the punishment, designed to break their spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. So the people were scattered throughout Egypt to gather stubble instead of straw. Henry observes the whole land saw Israel's scattering, and the work that once was ordered became a desperate search. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The taskmasters hasted them, demanding their daily tasks as when straw was given. Henry notes oppression knows no Sabbath of mercy: the daily tale was exacted even when the means were gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The Israelite officers set over the people were beaten and asked why they had not fulfilled the brick tale. Henry observes the officers, caught between Pharaoh and their brethren, suffered for a failure they could not prevent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The officers cried to Pharaoh, asking why he dealt so with his servants. Henry notes they appealed to the very power that oppressed them, the common refuge of the helpless when no higher help is yet seen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. They pleaded that no straw was given, yet they were told to make brick, and were beaten as if at fault. Henry observes the plea was just, but justice was not to be had from a heart hardened against God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Pharaoh answered, Ye are idle, ye are idle, therefore ye say, Let us go and sacrifice to the LORD. Henry notes the charge was repeated for emphasis: religion itself was branded as idleness by a king who measured men by bricks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Go and work, said Pharaoh; no straw would be given, yet the tale of bricks must be delivered. Henry observes the sentence closed every plea, leaving Israel no remedy in man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The officers saw they were in evil case when the tale could not be diminished. Henry notes the moment when human hope failed was the moment God would begin to work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Coming from Pharaoh, the officers met Moses and Aaron standing in the way. Henry observes the messengers of deliverance were met not with thanks but with reproach, the common lot of those who bring hard news of freedom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The officers said, The LORD look upon you and judge, because ye have made our savour abhorred to Pharaoh and put a sword in his hand to slay us. Henry notes their complaint mistook the cause: Moses had not made the yoke heavier, Pharaoh had. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Moses returned unto the LORD and asked why he had so evil entreated this people, and why he had sent him at all. Henry does not excuse the complaint, but notes its honesty: Moses carried the people's grief straight to God rather than to Pharaoh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Since Moses came to Pharaoh in God's name, Pharaoh had done evil to Israel, and God had not delivered them at all. Henry observes the chapter ends in darkness, that the coming deliverance might shine the brighter against it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • tale. The required daily quota of bricks, still exacted in full after straw was withheld. Interpreted Study note on verse 13, summarizing Matthew Henry
