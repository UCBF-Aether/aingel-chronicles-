The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Jehoshaphat his son reigned in his stead, and strengthened himself against Israel.

2 And he placed forces in all the fenced cities of Judah, and set garrisons in the land of Judah, and in the cities of Ephraim, which Asa his father had taken.

3 And the LORD was with Jehoshaphat, because he walked in the first ways of his father David, and sought not unto Baalim;

4 But sought to the Lord God of his father, and walked in his commandments, and not after the doings of Israel.

5 Therefore the LORD stablished the kingdom in his hand; and all Judah brought to Jehoshaphat presents; and he had riches and honour in abundance.

6 And his heart was lifted up in the ways of the LORD: moreover he took away the high places and groves out of Judah.

7 Also in the third year of his reign he sent to his princes, even to Benhail, and to Obadiah, and to Zechariah, and to Nethaneel, and to Michaiah, to teach in the cities of Judah.

8 And with them he sent Levites, even Shemaiah, and Nethaniah, and Zebadiah, and Asahel, and Shemiramoth, and Jehonathan, and Adonijah, and Tobijah, and Tobadonijah, Levites; and with them Elishama and Jehoram, priests.

9 And they taught in Judah, and had the book of the law of the LORD with them, and went about throughout all the cities of Judah, and taught the people.

10 And the fear of the LORD fell upon all the kingdoms of the lands that were round about Judah, so that they made no war against Jehoshaphat.

11 Also some of the Philistines brought Jehoshaphat presents, and tribute silver; and the Arabians brought him flocks, seven thousand and seven hundred rams, and seven thousand and seven hundred he goats.

12 And Jehoshaphat waxed great exceedingly; and he built in Judah castles, and cities of store.

13 And he had much business in the cities of Judah: and the men of war, mighty men of valour, were in Jerusalem.

14 And these are the numbers of them according to the house of their fathers: Of Judah, the captains of thousands; Adnah the chief, and with him mighty men of valour three hundred thousand.

15 And next to him was Jehohanan the captain, and with him two hundred and fourscore thousand.

16 And next him was Amasiah the son of Zichri, who willingly offered himself unto the LORD; and with him two hundred thousand mighty men of valour.

17 And of Benjamin; Eliada a mighty man of valour, and with him armed men with bow and shield two hundred thousand.

18 And next him was Jehozabad, and with him an hundred and fourscore thousand ready prepared for the war.

19 These waited on the king, beside those whom the king put in the fenced cities throughout all Judah.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Jehoshaphat's reign: his strengthening against Israel, his sending teachers of the law through Judah, the fear of the Lord on surrounding kingdoms, and his great army. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And Jehoshaphat his son reigned in his stead, and strengthened himself against Israel. Henry notes Jehoshaphat, one of the best kings since David, good son of a good father, first strengthened himself against Israel, not to invade but to maintain his own, checking Ahab's growing greatness without bloodshed, so that Ahab soon courted his alliance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And he placed forces in all the fenced cities of Judah, and set garrisons in Judah, and in the cities of Ephraim which Asa his father had taken. Henry notes he fortified frontier cities and put stronger garrisons in Ephraim's cities, strengthening himself by fair and regular methods on which he might expect God's blessing, not by a Syrian league as his father did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And the Lord was with Jehoshaphat, because he walked in the first ways of his father David, and sought not unto Baalim. Henry notes the distinction between David's first ways and last: Jehoshaphat followed David's first, purest ways, before the fall in Uriah's matter, and followed him only as far as he followed God, abhorring all Baalim of neighbouring nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. But sought to the Lord God of his father, and walked in his commandments, and not after the doings of Israel. Henry notes he worshipped the Lord God of his father only, prayed and enquired of him only, and walked in God's commandments according to God's institution, not learning Israel's way despite civil dealings with its king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Therefore the Lord established the kingdom in his hand, and all Judah brought Jehoshaphat presents, and he had riches and honour in abundance. Henry notes those stand firmly who have God's presence, and Judah's presents acknowledged his kindness in sending preachers, for the more true religion among a people, the more conscientious loyalty, and piety is the best friend to outward prosperity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And his heart was lifted up in the ways of the Lord, moreover he took away the high places and groves out of Judah. Henry notes his heart was enlarged in good, lively and fervent, bold above difficulties, never thinking he could do enough for God, and he took away idolatrous high places and groves, teachers of lies, though places dedicated to the true God only were left. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Also in the third year of his reign he sent to his princes, even to Benhail, Obadiah, Zechariah, Nethaneel and Michaiah, to teach in the cities of Judah. Henry notes Jehoshaphat found his people generally very ignorant, and resolved to begin at the right end, having them well taught rather than led blindfold, sending princes to teach in the cities and, in administering justice, to teach people how to do better with reasons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And with them he sent Levites, Shemaiah, Nethaniah, Zebadiah, Asahel, Shemiramoth, Jehonathan, Adonijah, Tobijah and Tobadonijah, and with them Elishama and Jehoram, priests. Henry notes Levites and priests went with the princes, teachers by office, who had neglected teaching pretending people would not hear, but now princes' authority obliged people to come and hear, so that if they were not instructed it was the teachers' fault. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And they taught in Judah, and had the book of the law of the Lord with them, and went about throughout all the cities of Judah, and taught the people. Henry notes they had the book of the law for their own direction, fetching instructions from it and not men's commandments, and for the people's conviction, showing divine warrant, for ministers going to teach should have their Bibles with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And the fear of the Lord fell upon all the kingdoms round about Judah, so that they made no war against Jehoshaphat. Henry notes it was not Jehoshaphat's army that struck terror, but the fear of God falling on neighbours when he reformed his country and set up a preaching ministry, for God's ordinances are more a kingdom's strength than military force. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Also some of the Philistines brought Jehoshaphat presents and tribute silver, and the Arabians brought flocks, seven thousand seven hundred rams and seven thousand seven hundred he-goats. Henry notes neighbours brought presents to secure his friendship, perhaps tribute imposed by Asa, and with the rams and he-goats probably a proportion of ewes, lambs, she-goats and kids. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And Jehoshaphat waxed great exceedingly, and he built in Judah castles and cities of store. Henry notes he pulled down barns and built larger, castles and store cities for arms and victuals, a man of business aiming at public good, to preserve peace or prepare for war. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And he had much business in the cities of Judah, and the men of war, mighty men of valour, were in Jerusalem. Henry notes his militia was never in better order since David modelled it, with great commanders and serviceable men fit for war in their districts, ready at call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And these are the numbers according to their fathers' houses: Of Judah, captains of thousands, Adnah the chief, and with him mighty men of valour three hundred thousand. Henry notes three commanders in Judah and two in Benjamin are named, with numbers amounting to 1,160,000 in all, a vast number for Judah and Benjamin's lot, increased by long peace, Jerusalem's enlargement, and comers from Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And next to him was Jehohanan the captain, and with him two hundred and fourscore thousand. Henry's militia account applies: Jehohanan commanded the second great division of Judah's forces, part of the vast muster under Jehoshaphat's blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And next him was Amasiah son of Zichri, who willingly offered himself unto the Lord, and with him two hundred thousand mighty men of valour. Henry notes Amasiah was most eminent for religion, offering himself not to the king only but to the Lord, to glorify God in his post, accepting it not for honour, power or profit but for conscience toward God and his country's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And of Benjamin, Eliada a mighty man of valour, and with him armed men with bow and shield two hundred thousand. Henry notes Benjamin supplied two commanders, Eliada with bowmen and shield-bearers, part of the militia in good order under Jehoshaphat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And next him was Jehozabad, and with him a hundred and fourscore thousand ready prepared for the war. Henry notes Jehozabad commanded Benjamin's second division, ready prepared for war, completing the five great commanders of the muster. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. These waited on the king, beside those whom the king put in the fenced cities throughout all Judah. Henry notes the commanders waited on the king as court officers, privy-counsellors and ministers of state, besides the garrisons in fenced cities throughout Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
