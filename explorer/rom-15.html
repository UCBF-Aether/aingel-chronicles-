The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Romans 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 We then that are strong ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not to please ourselves.

2 Let every one of us please his neighbour for his good to edification.

3 For even Christ pleased not himself; but, as it is written, The reproaches of them that reproached thee fell on me.

4 For whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning, that we through patience and comfort of the scriptures might have hope.

5 Now the God of patience and consolation grant you to be likeminded one toward another according to Christ Jesus:

6 That ye may with one mind and one mouth glorify God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.

7 Wherefore receive ye one another, as Christ also received us to the glory of God.

8 Now I say that Jesus Christ was a minister of the circumcision for the truth of God, to confirm the promises made unto the fathers:

9 And that the Gentiles might glorify God for his mercy; as it is written, For this cause I will confess to thee among the Gentiles, and sing unto thy name.

10 And again he saith, Rejoice, ye Gentiles, with his people.

11 And again, Praise the Lord, all ye Gentiles; and laud him, all ye people.

12 And again, Esaias saith, There shall be a root of Jesse, and he that shall rise to reign over the Gentiles; in him shall the Gentiles trust.

13 Now the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that ye may abound in hope, through the power of the Holy Ghost.

14 And I myself also am persuaded of you, my brethren, that ye also are full of goodness, filled with all knowledge, able also to admonish one another.

15 Nevertheless, brethren, I have written the more boldly unto you in some sort, as putting you in mind, because of the grace that is given to me of God,

16 That I should be the minister of Jesus Christ to the Gentiles, ministering the gospel of God, that the offering up of the Gentiles might be acceptable, being sanctified by the Holy Ghost.

17 I have therefore whereof I may glory through Jesus Christ in those things which pertain to God.

18 For I will not dare to speak of any of those things which Christ hath not wrought by me, to make the Gentiles obedient, by word and deed,

19 Through mighty signs and wonders, by the power of the Spirit of God; so that from Jerusalem, and round about unto Illyricum, I have fully preached the gospel of Christ.

20 Yea, so have I strived to preach the gospel, not where Christ was named, lest I should build upon another man’s foundation:

21 But as it is written, To whom he was not spoken of, they shall see: and they that have not heard shall understand.

22 For which cause also I have been much hindered from coming to you.

23 But now having no more place in these parts, and having a great desire these many years to come unto you;

24 Whensoever I take my journey into Spain, I will come to you: for I trust to see you in my journey, and to be brought on my way thitherward by you, if first I be somewhat filled with your company.

25 But now I go unto Jerusalem to minister unto the saints.

26 For it hath pleased them of Macedonia and Achaia to make a certain contribution for the poor saints which are at Jerusalem.

27 It hath pleased them verily; and their debtors they are. For if the Gentiles have been made partakers of their spiritual things, their duty is also to minister unto them in carnal things.

28 When therefore I have performed this, and have sealed to them this fruit, I will come by you into Spain.

29 And I am sure that, when I come unto you, I shall come in the fulness of the blessing of the gospel of Christ.

30 Now I beseech you, brethren, for the Lord Jesus Christ’s sake, and for the love of the Spirit, that ye strive together with me in your prayers to God for me;

31 That I may be delivered from them that do not believe in Judaea; and that my service which I have for Jerusalem may be accepted of the saints;

32 That I may come unto you with joy by the will of God, and may with you be refreshed.

33 Now the God of peace be with you all. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The strong ought to bear the infirmities of the weak and please their neighbour for good, following Christ, who pleased not himself and bore reproaches, and the Scriptures give patience and comfort through hope. Paul explains his own ministry to the Gentiles, his plan to visit Rome on his way to Spain after delivering the collection to Jerusalem, and asks the Romans to pray for his deliverance and acceptance. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. We who are strong ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not please ourselves. Henry notes that strength is given for bearing, not for boasting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 3. Even Christ pleased not himself, but, as it is written, the reproaches of those who reproached you fell on me. Henry observes that Christ is the pattern of self-forgetting service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Whatever things were written before were written for our learning, that we through patience and comfort of the Scriptures might have hope. JFB notes that the Old Testament is written for Christian hope, not merely for history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The God of patience and consolation grant you to be like-minded one toward another according to Christ Jesus. Henry notes that unity is God's gift, prayed for, not man's achievement alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. That you may with one mind and one mouth glorify God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. JFB stresses that unity of mind issues in unity of worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Therefore receive one another, as Christ also received us to God's glory. Henry observes that Christ's reception of us is the measure of our reception of one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Henry observes that Gentile praise is the fruit of mercy, not of merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 21. As it is written, to whom he was not spoken of, they shall see, and they who have not heard shall understand. Henry observes that pioneer preaching fulfils prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

JFB notes that Rome is a stopping place on the way to Spain, not merely a destination. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 28. When Paul has performed this and sealed to them this fruit, he will come by them into Spain. JFB notes that Paul treats the collection as sealed fruit, delivered faithfully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. He knows that when he comes to them, he shall come in the fullness of the blessing of Christ's gospel. Henry stresses that Paul expects his coming to be a blessing, not a burden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Paul beseeches them, for the Lord Jesus Christ's sake and the Spirit's love, to strive together with him in prayers to God for him. JFB notes that prayer is striving together, a joint labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. He asks prayer that he may be delivered from unbelievers in Judea, and that his service for Jerusalem may be accepted by the saints. Henry observes that even an apostle asks prayer for safety and acceptance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. That he may come to them with joy by God's will, and may with them be refreshed. JFB notes that refreshment in fellowship is the hoped-for end of a perilous journey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. The God of peace be with you all. Amen. Henry observes that the prayer for peace fittingly closes a letter that began with grace and peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • infirmities of the weak. The scruples and limitations of weaker believers, which the strong are to bear rather than please themselves. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • minister of the circumcision. Paul's description of Christ as a servant to the Jews, to confirm the promises made to the fathers. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • Spain. The further westward mission Paul hopes to undertake after visiting Rome. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
