The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 59 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Deliver me from mine enemies, O my God: defend me from them that rise up against me.

2 Deliver me from the workers of iniquity, and save me from bloody men.

3 For, lo, they lie in wait for my soul: the mighty are gathered against me; not for my transgression, nor for my sin, O LORD.

4 They run and prepare themselves without my fault: awake to help me, and behold.

5 Thou therefore, O LORD God of hosts, the God of Israel, awake to visit all the heathen: be not merciful to any wicked transgressors. Selah.

6 They return at evening: they make a noise like a dog, and go round about the city.

7 Behold, they belch out with their mouth: swords are in their lips: for who, say they, doth hear?

8 But thou, O LORD, shalt laugh at them; thou shalt have all the heathen in derision.

9 Because of his strength will I wait upon thee: for God is my defence.

10 The God of my mercy shall prevent me: God shall let me see my desire upon mine enemies.

11 Slay them not, lest my people forget: scatter them by thy power; and bring them down, O Lord our shield.

12 For the sin of their mouth and the words of their lips let them even be taken in their pride: and for cursing and lying which they speak.

13 Consume them in wrath, consume them, that they may not be: and let them know that God ruleth in Jacob unto the ends of the earth. Selah.

14 And at evening let them return; and let them make a noise like a dog, and go round about the city.

15 Let them wander up and down for meat, and grudge if they be not satisfied.

16 But I will sing of thy power; yea, I will sing aloud of thy mercy in the morning: for thou hast been my defence and refuge in the day of my trouble.

17 Unto thee, O my strength, will I sing: for God is my defence, and the God of my mercy.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Night dogs round the city: enemies lie in wait without his fault, return at evening making dog-noise, belching swords from lips, asking, 'Who hears?' But God laughs, scatters rather than slays lest the people forget, consumes in wrath till men know God rules in Jacob to earth's ends, and he will sing aloud of power and mercy in the morning, God his defence. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Heading note: the text panel prints the verses only, without a heading. The title recalled in verse 1, Saul's men watching David's house to kill him, comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Deliver me from my enemies, O my God; defend me from those who rise up against me: deliverance and defence are asked together — the title recalls Saul's men watching David's house to kill him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Deliver me from the workers of iniquity, and save me from bloody men: workers of iniquity and bloody men are the two classes of enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For, lo, they lie in wait for my soul; the mighty are gathered against me, not for my transgression, nor for my sin, O Lord: lying in wait, mighty gathered, yet not for his transgression or sin — innocence is pleaded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They run and prepare themselves without my fault; awake to help me, and behold: running and preparing without his fault — God is bidden awake, help, and behold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. You therefore, O Lord God of hosts, the God of Israel, awake to visit all the heathen; be not merciful to any wicked transgressors: Lord of hosts and God of Israel is bidden awake to visit the heathen, with no mercy to wicked transgressors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They return at evening; they make a noise like a dog, and go round about the city: evening return, dog-like noise, circling the city — the enemies prowl like scavenger dogs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Behold, they belch out with their mouth; swords are in their lips, for who, say they, does hear? Belched speech, swords in lips, and the question 'who hears?' — they count on God's deafness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. But you, O Lord, shall laugh at them; you shall have all the heathen in derision: God's laughter and derision answer their question — he hears, and he scorns their scorn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Because of his strength will I wait upon you, for God is my defence: because of the enemy's strength — or, as some read, God's strength — he waits on God, his defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The God of my mercy shall prevent me; God shall let me see my desire upon my enemies: mercy going before him, and sight of his desire on enemies, are promised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Slay them not, lest my people forget; scatter them by your power, and bring them down, O Lord our shield: not slaying but scattering — a lasting, visible example lest the people forget — by power, brought down by the Lord their shield. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. For the sin of their mouth and the words of their lips let them even be taken in their pride, and for cursing and lying which they speak: mouth-sin, lip-words, pride, cursing, and lying are the snare that takes them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Consume them in wrath, consume them, that they may not be, and let them know that God rules in Jacob unto the ends of the earth: consuming in wrath, twice prayed, ends in knowledge — God rules in Jacob to earth's ends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. And at evening let them return, and let them make a noise like a dog, and go round about the city: the evening dog-noise returns as refrain — the enemies still prowl, but now as a picture of futility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Let them wander up and down for meat, and grudge if they be not satisfied: wandering for meat, grudging unsatisfied — hunger without satisfaction is their lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. But I will sing of your power; yea, I will sing aloud of your mercy in the morning, for you have been my defence and refuge in the day of my trouble: against their evening prowling stands his morning song of power and mercy, for God was defence and refuge in trouble's day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Unto you, O my strength, will I sing, for God is my defence, and the God of my mercy: to God his strength he sings, for God is defence and God of mercy — the psalm ends in the two names of its trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
