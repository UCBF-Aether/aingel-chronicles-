The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Thessalonians 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Wherefore when we could no longer forbear, we thought it good to be left at Athens alone;

2 And sent Timotheus, our brother, and minister of God, and our fellowlabourer in the gospel of Christ, to establish you, and to comfort you concerning your faith:

3 That no man should be moved by these afflictions: for yourselves know that we are appointed thereunto.

4 For verily, when we were with you, we told you before that we should suffer tribulation; even as it came to pass, and ye know.

5 For this cause, when I could no longer forbear, I sent to know your faith, lest by some means the tempter have tempted you, and our labour be in vain.

6 But now when Timotheus came from you unto us, and brought us good tidings of your faith and charity, and that ye have good remembrance of us always, desiring greatly to see us, as we also to see you:

7 Therefore, brethren, we were comforted over you in all our affliction and distress by your faith:

8 For now we live, if ye stand fast in the Lord.

9 For what thanks can we render to God again for you, for all the joy wherewith we joy for your sakes before our God;

10 Night and day praying exceedingly that we might see your face, and might perfect that which is lacking in your faith?

11 Now God himself and our Father, and our Lord Jesus Christ, direct our way unto you.

12 And the Lord make you to increase and abound in love one toward another, and toward all men, even as we do toward you:

13 To the end he may stablish your hearts unblameable in holiness before God, even our Father, at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ with all his saints.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Unable to bear the separation, Paul sent Timothy to strengthen and encourage their faith under affliction, fearing the tempter might have undone his work. Timothy's good report of their faith and love has comforted Paul in his own distress, and he prays night and day to see them again, that the Lord will increase their love and establish their hearts blameless at Christ's coming. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Wherefore when we could no longer forbear, we thought it good to be left at Athens alone. Though hindered from going himself, Paul's love could not forbear sending Timothy, and though Timothy was very useful to him, he was content to be left alone at Athens for the Thessalonians' good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. And sent Timothy, our brother, minister of God, and fellowlabourer in the gospel of Christ, to establish you and comfort you concerning your faith. Timothy, junior in age and lower in rank, is called brother by Paul in humility, and a minister of God and fellowlabourer, for ministers are labourers in the Lord's vineyard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. That no man should be moved by these afflictions, for you yourselves know we are appointed thereunto. The design was to establish and comfort them as to the object of faith, that Jesus Christ is the Saviour of the world, and as to faith's recompense, more than enough to make up all losses and reward all labours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. For verily, when we were with you, we told you before that we should suffer tribulation, even as it came to pass, and you know. It is God's will that we enter his kingdom through many afflictions, and the apostles, far from flattering people with worldly prosperity, told them plainly to count upon trouble in the flesh, following their Master. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. For this cause, when I could no longer forbear, I sent to know your faith, lest by some means the tempter have tempted you, and our labour be in vain. If the devil cannot hinder ministers from labouring, Henry warns, he will hinder the success of their labours if possible, and Christians forewarned of danger will be kept from the tempter's devices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. But now when Timothy came from you to us and brought good tidings of your faith and charity, and that you have good remembrance of us always, desiring greatly to see us, as we also to see you. The return brings the very news Paul feared he would not hear: faith standing and love alive, with mutual longing to meet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Therefore, brethren, we were comforted over you in all our affliction and distress by your faith. Another church's steadfast faith comforts an afflicted apostle, and one great end of ministry, Henry notes, is to help faith forward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. For now we live, if you stand fast in the Lord. The apostle's life is bound up with his converts' standing, and their perseverance is his vitality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. For what thanks can we render to God again for you, for all the joy with which we joy for your sakes before our God? Thankfulness in this present state is very imperfect, Henry observes, yet joy before God over converts is the offering ministry returns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Night and day praying exceedingly that we might see your face and might perfect that which is lacking in your faith. The instrument that obtained faith, the ordinances of God, is also the means of increasing and confirming it, for as faith comes by hearing, so it is confirmed by hearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Now God himself and our Father, and our Lord Jesus Christ, direct our way to you. Prayer is religious worship due to God only, Henry notes, offered to God as Father, and not only in Christ's name but up to Christ himself as Lord and Saviour; let us acknowledge God in all our ways, and he will direct our paths. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. And the Lord make you to increase and abound in love one toward another, and toward all men, even as we do toward you. Mutual love is required of all Christians, and love is of God, fulfilling gospel as well as law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. To the end he may establish your hearts unblameable in holiness before God, even our Father, at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ with all his saints. We need the Spirit's influences for growth in grace, Henry says, and the way to obtain them is prayer; holiness is required of all who would go to heaven, and the Lord Jesus will certainly come in glory with his saints, when without holiness no heart shall be established. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
