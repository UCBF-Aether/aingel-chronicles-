The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto Aaron and to his sons, that they separate themselves from the holy things of the children of Israel, and that they profane not my holy name in those things which they hallow unto me: I am the LORD.

3 Say unto them, Whosoever he be of all your seed among your generations, that goeth unto the holy things, which the children of Israel hallow unto the LORD, having his uncleanness upon him, that soul shall be cut off from my presence: I am the LORD.

4 What man soever of the seed of Aaron is a leper, or hath a running issue; he shall not eat of the holy things, until he be clean. And whoso toucheth any thing that is unclean by the dead, or a man whose seed goeth from him;

5 Or whosoever toucheth any creeping thing, whereby he may be made unclean, or a man of whom he may take uncleanness, whatsoever uncleanness he hath;

6 The soul which hath touched any such shall be unclean until even, and shall not eat of the holy things, unless he wash his flesh with water.

7 And when the sun is down, he shall be clean, and shall afterward eat of the holy things; because it is his food.

8 That which dieth of itself, or is torn with beasts, he shall not eat to defile himself therewith; I am the LORD.

9 They shall therefore keep mine ordinance, lest they bear sin for it, and die therefore, if they profane it: I the LORD do sanctify them.

10 There shall no stranger eat of the holy thing: a sojourner of the priest, or an hired servant, shall not eat of the holy thing.

11 But if the priest buy any soul with his money, he shall eat of it, and he that is born in his house: they shall eat of his meat.

12 If the priest’s daughter also be married unto a stranger, she may not eat of an offering of the holy things.

13 But if the priest’s daughter be a widow, or divorced, and have no child, and is returned unto her father’s house, as in her youth, she shall eat of her father’s meat: but there shall be no stranger eat thereof.

14 And if a man eat of the holy thing unwittingly, then he shall put the fifth part thereof unto it, and shall give it unto the priest with the holy thing.

15 And they shall not profane the holy things of the children of Israel, which they offer unto the LORD;

16 Or suffer them to bear the iniquity of trespass, when they eat their holy things: for I the LORD do sanctify them.

17 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

18 Speak unto Aaron, and to his sons, and unto all the children of Israel, and say unto them, Whatsoever he be of the house of Israel, or of the strangers in Israel, that will offer his oblation for all his vows, and for all his freewill offerings, which they will offer unto the LORD for a burnt offering;

19 Ye shall offer at your own will a male without blemish, of the beeves, of the sheep, or of the goats.

20 But whatsoever hath a blemish, that shall ye not offer: for it shall not be acceptable for you.

21 And whosoever offereth a sacrifice of peace offerings unto the LORD to accomplish his vow, or a freewill offering in beeves or sheep, it shall be perfect to be accepted; there shall be no blemish therein.

22 Blind, or broken, or maimed, or having a wen, or scurvy, or scabbed, ye shall not offer these unto the LORD, nor make an offering by fire of them upon the altar unto the LORD.

23 Either a bullock or a lamb that hath any thing superfluous or lacking in his parts, that mayest thou offer for a freewill offering; but for a vow it shall not be accepted.

24 Ye shall not offer unto the LORD that which is bruised, or crushed, or broken, or cut; neither shall ye make any offering thereof in your land.

25 Neither from a stranger’s hand shall ye offer the bread of your God of any of these; because their corruption is in them, and blemishes be in them: they shall not be accepted for you.

26 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

27 When a bullock, or a sheep, or a goat, is brought forth, then it shall be seven days under the dam; and from the eighth day and thenceforth it shall be accepted for an offering made by fire unto the LORD.

28 And whether it be cow, or ewe, ye shall not kill it and her young both in one day.

29 And when ye will offer a sacrifice of thanksgiving unto the LORD, offer it at your own will.

30 On the same day it shall be eaten up; ye shall leave none of it until the morrow: I am the LORD.

31 Therefore shall ye keep my commandments, and do them: I am the LORD.

32 Neither shall ye profane my holy name; but I will be hallowed among the children of Israel: I am the LORD which hallow you,

33 That brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God: I am the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives two related laws: priests in their uncleanness, and sacrifices without blemish. A priest who is unclean, whether by leprosy, an issue, or contact with the dead or a creeping thing, must not eat holy things until he has washed and the sun is down; holy food belongs to the priest's own household, not to a stranger, sojourner, or hired servant, and unwitting eating must be restored with a fifth added. Every burnt or peace offering, from native or stranger, must be a male without blemish, at least eight days old, and a cow or ewe must not be killed with her young on the same day. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the law of priests in their uncleanness and of blemish-free sacrifices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Aaron and his sons shall separate themselves, that is abstain, from holy things and not profane God's holy name in things Israel hallows, a careless use of consecrated things dishonouring God's name and worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Whoever of Aaron's seed in generations goes to holy things with uncleanness upon him shall be cut off from God's presence, minute restrictions keeping priests on guard and preserving awe and submission, lest familiarity lead them to trifle with religion under cloak of office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. A leper or one with a running issue shall not eat holy things until clean, and whoever touches anything unclean by the dead or a man whose seed goes from him is likewise barred, priests being under no greater license than the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Whoever touches a creeping thing making him unclean, or a man from whom he takes uncleanness, whatever it is, is barred until cleansed, the chief temporary defilements being specified to prevent priestly license. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The soul that touches any such shall be unclean until evening and shall not eat holy things unless he washes his flesh with water, washing being the outward sign of restoration to forfeited privileges, not the cleansing itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. When the sun is down he shall be clean and afterward eat holy things, because it is his food, the evening limit ending the exclusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. That which dies of itself or is torn with beasts he shall not eat to defile himself, nature revolting against such food, but an express precept being needed to show that meat with its blood is prohibited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They shall keep God's ordinance, lest they bear sin and die if they profane it, for the LORD sanctifies them, neglect of washing leaving them in uncleanness and cut off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. No stranger shall eat of the holy thing, neither a priest's sojourner nor hired servant, the priests' portion being restricted to his own family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. If the priest buys a soul with his money, or one born in his house, they shall eat of his meat, a bought or homeborn slave being a stated household member, unlike a temporary guest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. If a priest's daughter is married to a stranger, she may not eat of the holy offerings, having left her father's household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But if a priest's daughter is a widow or divorced, childless, and returned to her father's house as in her youth, she shall eat of her father's meat, being reinstated, whereas a mother must find support elsewhere since her children have no priestly privilege; and no stranger shall eat thereof, the interdict repeated to show its stringency. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. If a man eats of the holy thing unwittingly, he shall add a fifth part to it and give it to the priest with the holy thing, a common Israelite unconsciously partaking of tithes or first-fruits restoring the amount plus a fine for the sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They shall not profane Israel's holy things offered to the LORD, whether priests defiling offerings by presenting them while unclean, or Israel defiling them by touching or reserving what is appointed to priests alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Nor shall they suffer Israel to bear iniquity of trespass when they eat their holy things, for the LORD sanctifies them, the guilt falling through profanation of the priests' portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The LORD speaks to Moses again, now concerning sacrifices without blemish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Whoever of Israel or strangers offers an oblation for vows or freewill offerings as a burnt offering must observe the blemish law, which applies to native and stranger alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. They shall offer at their own will, to their being accepted, a male without blemish of beeves, sheep, or goats, sacrifices being gifts to a King, and no imperfect victim fitting praise, conciliation, or atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Whatever has a blemish shall not be offered, for it shall not be acceptable, a perfect victim being required as a fitting instrument for gratitude, worship, prayer, confession, and atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Whoever offers a peace offering to accomplish a vow or a freewill offering in beeves or sheep, it shall be perfect to be accepted, with no blemish in it, symbolising whole dedication with sincere heart and pointing typically to Him without whom no sacrifice is acceptable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Blind, broken, maimed, wen, scurvy, or scabbed animals shall not be offered to the LORD nor made an offering by fire on the altar, each blemish disqualifying the gift to the King. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. A bullock or lamb with anything superfluous or lacking may be offered for a freewill offering but for a vow shall not be accepted, the rendering being that for either vow or freewill a blemished victim is not accepted, since without blemish is required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. They shall not offer to the LORD what is bruised, crushed, broken, or cut, nor make any offering thereof in their land, the same perfection being required of every altar victim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Neither from a stranger's hand shall they offer God's bread of any of these, because their corruption is in them and blemishes in them, they shall not be accepted, a stranger's blemished gift being no better than a native's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The LORD speaks to Moses again, concerning the age and humanity rules for sacrifices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. A bullock, sheep, or goat shall be seven days under the dam, and from the eighth day onward it shall be accepted for an offering by fire, animals not being perfect nor good for food until the eighth day, and offering them earlier would indicate contempt of religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Whether cow or ewe, they shall not kill it and her young both in one day, a lesson of humanity and tenderness to the dam, securing sacrifices from appearance of unfeeling cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. When they offer a sacrifice of thanksgiving, they shall offer it at their own will, as a freewill gift to the King. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. On the same day it shall be eaten, leaving none until the morrow, the thanksgiving offering being confined to its day, unlike a freewill offering allowed a second day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Therefore they shall keep God's commandments and do them, for He is the LORD, the blemish and holiness laws ending in obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Neither shall they profane His holy name, but He will be hallowed among Israel, for He is the LORD who sanctifies them, careless use of holy things dishonouring the name that must be hallowed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. He brought them out of Egypt to be their God, for He is the LORD, the exodus grounding the holiness and sacrifice laws in deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
