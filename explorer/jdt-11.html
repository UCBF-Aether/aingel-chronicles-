The Text — Judith 11 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Then said Holofernes unto her, Woman, be of good comfort, fear not in thine heart: for I never hurt any that was willing to serve Nabuchodonosor, the king of all the earth.

2 Now therefore, if thy people that dwelleth in the mountains had not set light by me, I would not have lifted up my spear against them: but they have done these things to themselves.

3 But now tell me wherefore thou art fled from them, and art come unto us: for thou art come for safeguard; be of good comfort, thou shalt live this night, and hereafter:

4 For none shall hurt thee, but entreat thee well, as they do the servants of king Nabuchodonosor my lord.

5 Then Judith said unto him, Receive the words of thy servant, and suffer thine handmaid to speak in thy presence, and I will declare no lie to my lord this night.

6 And if thou wilt follow the words of thine handmaid, God will bring the thing perfectly to pass by thee; and my lord shall not fail of his purposes.

7 As Nabuchodonosor king of all the earth liveth, and as his power liveth, who hath sent thee for the upholding of every living thing: for not only men shall serve him by thee, but also the beasts of the field, and the cattle, and the fowls of the air, shall live by thy power under Nabuchodonosor and all his house.

8 For we have heard of thy wisdom and thy policies, and it is reported in all the earth, that thou only art excellent in all the kingdom, and mighty in knowledge, and wonderful in feats of war.

9 Now as concerning the matter, which Achior did speak in thy council, we have heard his words; for the men of Bethulia saved him, and he declared unto them all that he had spoken unto thee.

10 Therefore, O lord and governor, respect not his word; but lay it up in thine heart, for it is true: for our nation shall not be punished, neither can sword prevail against them, except they sin against their God.

11 And now, that my lord be not defeated and frustrate of his purpose, even death is now fallen upon them, and their sin hath overtaken them, wherewith they will provoke their God to anger whensoever they shall do that which is not fit to be done:

12 For their victuals fail them, and all their water is scant, and they have determined to lay hands upon their cattle, and purposed to consume all those things, that God hath forbidden them to eat by his laws:

13 And are resolved to spend the firstfruits of the the tenths of wine and oil, which they had sanctified, and reserved for the priests that serve in Jerusalem before the face of our God; the which things it is not lawful for any of the people so much as to touch with their hands.

14 For they have sent some to Jerusalem, because they also that dwell there have done the like, to bring them a licence from the senate.

15 Now when they shall bring them word, they will forthwith do it, and they shall be given to thee to be destroyed the same day.

16 Wherefore I thine handmaid, knowing all this, am fled from their presence; and God hath sent me to work things with thee, whereat all the earth shall be astonished, and whosoever shall hear it.

17 For thy servant is religious, and serveth the God of heaven day and night: now therefore, my lord, I will remain with thee, and thy servant will go out by night into the valley, and I will pray unto God, and he will tell me when they have committed their sins:

18 And I will come and shew it unto thee: then thou shalt go forth with all thine army, and there shall be none of them that shall resist thee.

19 And I will lead thee through the midst of Judea, until thou come before Jerusalem; and I will set thy throne in the midst thereof; and thou shalt drive them as sheep that have no shepherd, and a dog shall not so much as open his mouth at thee: for these things were told me according to my foreknowledge, and they were declared unto me, and I am sent to tell thee.

20 Then her words pleased Holofernes and all his servants; and they marvelled at her wisdom, and said,

21 There is not such a woman from one end of the earth to the other, both for beauty of face, and wisdom of words.

22 Likewise Holofernes said unto her. God hath done well to send thee before the people, that strength might be in our hands and destruction upon them that lightly regard my lord.

23 And now thou art both beautiful in thy countenance, and witty in thy words: surely if thou do as thou hast spoken thy God shall be my God, and thou shalt dwell in the house of king Nabuchodonosor, and shalt be renowned through the whole earth.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Holofernes comforts Judith and asks why she has fled, and she answers with a story: her people are about to eat the firstfruits and tithes sanctified for the priests, and so to sin and be delivered up; God has therefore sent her to show Holofernes the way to take all the hill country without losing a man. Delighted, Holofernes praises her beauty and wit, and promises that if she does as she has spoken, her God shall be his God. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Firstfruits and tithes. the sanctified portions reserved for the priests, which Judith says her people are resolved to eat in their extremity (verse 13). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Hill country. the fortified passes of Judea, which Judith promises to show Holofernes how to take (verse 19). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then said Holofernes unto her, "Woman, be of good comfort, fear not in thine heart: for I never hurt any that was willing to serve Nabuchodonosor"; the Greek adds, "of all the earth" to the king's title. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. But now tell me wherefore thou art fled from them, and art come unto us, for thou art come for safeguard; be of good comfort, thou shalt live this night and hereafter; the Greek reads, "And art come to us? For thou art come for thy own safety. Take courage, for thou shalt live this night and henceforth, as no one shall hurt thee, but shall do thee good, as the servants of my lord, Nabuchodonosor, the king, are treated." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. For none shall hurt thee, but entreat thee well, as they do the servants of king Nabuchodonosor my lord — the same Greek assurance continues: no one shall hurt thee, but shall do thee good, as the king's servants are treated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. Then Judith said unto him, "Receive the words of thy servant, and suffer thine handmaid to speak in thy presence, and I will declare no lie to my lord this night"; the Greek adds, "and I will tell no untruth to my lord this night, for," and so on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. And if thou wilt follow the words of thine handmaid, God will bring the thing perfectly to pass by thee, and my lord shall not fail of his purposes; the Greek adds, "and none of the projects of my lord shall fail." All that Judith said was true in her sense, though the enemy did not understand her properly — so Josue lawfully entrapped the citizens of Hai (St. Augustine). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. As Nabuchodonosor king of all the earth liveth, and as his power liveth, who hath sent thee for the upholding of every living thing; she imitates Joseph (Genesis 42:15) and Eliseus (2 Kings 2:4) in swearing by the king's life, and speaks the truth, as the general had subjected many to his master. Her speech was not superstitious, but showed a civil respect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. Therefore, O lord and governor, respect not his word, but lay it up in thine heart, for it is true: for our nation shall not be punished, neither can the sword prevail against them, except they sin against their God; the Greek intimates that she advised him to follow his counsel: "For our nation is not punished with the sword, unless they sin against their God." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. And now, that my lord be not defeated and frustrate of his purpose, even death is now fallen upon them, and their sin hath overtaken them, wherewith they have provoked their God to anger; the Greek continues, "But now let not my lord be at a loss what to do. For death has fallen upon them, and sin has taken possession of them, so that they have irritated God by their folly." This might also be true, though they had not fallen into idolatry (chapter 8:18) at Bethulia. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. For their victuals fail them, and all their water is scant, and they have determined to lay hands upon their cattle, and purposed to consume all those things, that God hath forbidden them to eat by his laws; they might have provisions, but they would be useless on account of excessive thirst. The Greek reads, "and all that God had forbidden them to eat by his laws, without making any distinction between the clean and unclean" — blood was prohibited even before the law (Genesis 9:4; Leviticus 17:14). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. And are resolved to spend the firstfruits of the tenths of wine and oil, which they had sanctified, and reserved for the priests that serve in Jerusalem; the Greek explains, "And the first-fruits of corn, and the tithes of wine and of oil, which they had reserved as sacred for the priests, standing in Jerusalem, before our God, they had decreed to consume; though none of the people ought so much as to touch these things." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. For they have sent some to Jerusalem, because they also that dwell there have done the like, to bring them a licence from the senate; the Greek continues, "Yet they have sent to obtain permission of the senate at Jerusalem, where the people have done the like." She insinuates that her countrymen are in the utmost distress and not much attached to religion, though in extreme want the distinction of meats is not to be enforced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. Now when they shall bring them word, they will forthwith do it, and they shall be given to thee to be destroyed the same day; the Greek continues, "And when they shall have obtained an answer, they will fall to work, and then they will be abandoned to be destroyed by thee." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. Wherefore I thine handmaid, knowing all this, am fled from their presence, and God hath sent me to work things with thee, whereat all the earth shall be astonished; the Greek reads, "To perform with thee a feat which will astonish all who shall hear of these things," and, "My foreknowledge; and I was sent to inform thee" — she flatters Holofernes with the idea that he is under the special care of heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. For thy servant is religious, and serveth the God of heaven day and night: now therefore, my lord, I will remain with thee, and thy servant will go out into the valley, and pray unto God; the Greek continues, "For thy handmaid is religious, and serveth the God of heaven day and night; and now my lord, I am" with thee, and adds, "in the night, in the valley" — she will go out by night into the valley to pray. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. And I will come and show it unto thee: then thou shalt go forth with all thine army, and there shall be none of them that shall resist thee; the Greek reads, "They shall have completed their sins," and adds, "and thou shalt go out with all thy forces, and none of them shall resist thee." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. And I will lead thee through the midst of Judea, until thou come before Jerusalem, and I will set thy throne in the midst thereof, and thou shalt drive them as sheep that have no shepherd, and a dog shall not so much as open his mouth at thee; the Greek adds, "and I will bring thee through Judea, over-against Jerusalem, and will place thy throne in the midst of it, and thou shalt drive them like sheep" — "dog" is proverbial (Exodus 11:7). All Israel was at rest after the death of Holofernes, and Judith brought his head in triumph through the country; the promise of dominion was ironical (1 Kings 22:15). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. Likewise Holofernes said unto her, "God hath done well to send thee before the people, that strength might be in our hands and destruction upon them that lightly esteem my lord"; the Greek reads, "To increase our strength, and to bring destruction on those who have despised my lord." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 23. And now thou art both beautiful in thy countenance, and witty in thy words: surely if thou do as thou hast spoken thy God shall be my God, and thou shalt dwell in the house of king Nabuchodonosor, and shalt be renowned through the whole earth; the Greek reads, "And now thou art comely in thy appearance, and good in thy discourse. If then thou perform what thou hast spoken, thy God shall," be my God. It is hardly probable that he spoke in earnest; perhaps he did not know that the true God allowed of no other, and being captivated with Judith's beauty and perceiving her attachment to religion, he had a mind to remove all her objections to his person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
