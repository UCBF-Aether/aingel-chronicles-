The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Kings 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Elijah the Tishbite, who was of the inhabitants of Gilead, said unto Ahab, As the LORD God of Israel liveth, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, but according to my word.

2 And the word of the LORD came unto him, saying,

3 Get thee hence, and turn thee eastward, and hide thyself by the brook Cherith, that is before Jordan.

4 And it shall be, that thou shalt drink of the brook; and I have commanded the ravens to feed thee there.

5 So he went and did according unto the word of the LORD: for he went and dwelt by the brook Cherith, that is before Jordan.

6 And the ravens brought him bread and flesh in the morning, and bread and flesh in the evening; and he drank of the brook.

7 And it came to pass after a while, that the brook dried up, because there had been no rain in the land.

8 And the word of the LORD came unto him, saying,

9 Arise, get thee to Zarephath, which belongeth to Zidon, and dwell there: behold, I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee.

10 So he arose and went to Zarephath. And when he came to the gate of the city, behold, the widow woman was there gathering of sticks: and he called to her, and said, Fetch me, I pray thee, a little water in a vessel, that I may drink.

11 And as she was going to fetch it, he called to her, and said, Bring me, I pray thee, a morsel of bread in thine hand.

12 And she said, As the LORD thy God liveth, I have not a cake, but an handful of meal in a barrel, and a little oil in a cruse: and, behold, I am gathering two sticks, that I may go in and dress it for me and my son, that we may eat it, and die.

13 And Elijah said unto her, Fear not; go and do as thou hast said: but make me thereof a little cake first, and bring it unto me, and after make for thee and for thy son.

14 For thus saith the LORD God of Israel, The barrel of meal shall not waste, neither shall the cruse of oil fail, until the day that the LORD sendeth rain upon the earth.

15 And she went and did according to the saying of Elijah: and she, and he, and her house, did eat many days.

16 And the barrel of meal wasted not, neither did the cruse of oil fail, according to the word of the LORD, which he spake by Elijah.

17 And it came to pass after these things, that the son of the woman, the mistress of the house, fell sick; and his sickness was so sore, that there was no breath left in him.

18 And she said unto Elijah, What have I to do with thee, O thou man of God? art thou come unto me to call my sin to remembrance, and to slay my son?

19 And he said unto her, Give me thy son. And he took him out of her bosom, and carried him up into a loft, where he abode, and laid him upon his own bed.

20 And he cried unto the LORD, and said, O LORD my God, hast thou also brought evil upon the widow with whom I sojourn, by slaying her son?

21 And he stretched himself upon the child three times, and cried unto the LORD, and said, O LORD my God, I pray thee, let this child’s soul come into him again.

22 And the LORD heard the voice of Elijah; and the soul of the child came into him again, and he revived.

23 And Elijah took the child, and brought him down out of the chamber into the house, and delivered him unto his mother: and Elijah said, See, thy son liveth.

24 And the woman said to Elijah, Now by this I know that thou art a man of God, and that the word of the LORD in thy mouth is truth.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Elijah announces a drought that will break only at his word, is fed by ravens at Cherith, then by a widow at Zarephath whose jar of meal and cruse of oil do not run out. When the widow's son dies, Elijah stretches himself over the boy and prays, and the child lives, the LORD's word through Elijah proved in famine and death. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Elijah the Tishbite, of Gilead, appeared suddenly before Ahab and swore by the living God of Israel, before whom he stood, that there would be neither dew nor rain but according to his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The word of the Lord came to Elijah, directing a prophet who had just pronounced judgment to his next hiding place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He was told to turn eastward and hide by the brook Cherith, before Jordan, a refuge east of the king's reach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. He would drink of the brook, and God had commanded the ravens to feed him there, unclean birds made carriers of a prophet's bread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He went and did according to the Lord's word, dwelling by Cherith, obedience following the command without delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The ravens brought bread and flesh morning and evening, and he drank of the brook, a daily miracle that fed him twice a day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. After a while the brook dried up, because there was no rain in the land, so that even the prophet felt the drought he had announced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The word of the Lord came to him again, sending him from a dried brook to a new provision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. He was told to arise and go to Zarephath, which belonged to Zidon, Jezebel's own country, and dwell there, for God had commanded a widow to sustain him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. He arose and went to Zarephath, and at the city gate he saw the widow gathering sticks, the very woman God had appointed, in the lowliest work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. As she went to fetch water, he called to her for a morsel of bread, testing her charity with a small request first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. She swore by the Lord his God that she had no cake, only a handful of meal in a barrel and a little oil, and she was gathering sticks to make a last meal for herself and her son, and then die. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Elijah said, Fear not, do as thou hast said, but make me a little cake first, and afterward make for thee and thy son, faith putting God's prophet before one's own need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The promise was that meal and oil would hold out until rain returned — Henry's gloss is that they multiplied not in the hoarding but in the spending: there is that scattereth and yet increaseth, and when God blesses a little it goes a great way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. She went and did according to Elijah's saying, and she, and he, and her house ate many days, her obedience opening the store she feared was empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The barrel of meal wasted not, nor did the cruse of oil fail, according to the Lord's word spoken by Elijah, a miracle that lasted as long as the famine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. After these things the widow's son fell sick, so sore that no breath was left in him — Henry warns we must not think it strange to meet sharp affliction even in the way of duty: miraculous feeding did not secure him from sickness, and our mountain never stands so strong but it may be moved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. In grief the widow charged Elijah with coming to call her sin to remembrance and slay her son — Henry reads the outburst two ways: passion venting sorrow without hope of relief, and penitence, since God remembers our sins against us precisely to make us remember them against ourselves and repent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Elijah answered no expostulation but took the boy from his mother's bosom to the loft where he lodged — Henry notes he brought the case straight to God, having taken a particular kindness to the child, so that the affliction was his own more than by sympathy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. He cried to the Lord, O Lord my God, hast thou also brought evil upon the widow with whom I sojourn, by slaying her son, a prayer that argues with God from his own goodness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. He stretched himself upon the child three times and cried, O Lord my God, let this child's soul come into him again, earnest, repeated prayer for life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The Lord heard Elijah's voice, and the child's soul came into him again, and he revived, the first resurrection in Israel's story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Elijah took the child down from the chamber and delivered him to his mother, saying, See, thy son liveth, the dead restored to the living. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. The woman said, Now by this I know thou art a man of God, and the Lord's word in thy mouth is truth, her faith confirmed by the life of her son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
