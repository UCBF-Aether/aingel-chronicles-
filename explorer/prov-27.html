The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 27 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.

2 Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips.

3 A stone is heavy, and the sand weighty; but a fool’s wrath is heavier than them both.

4 Wrath is cruel, and anger is outrageous; but who is able to stand before envy?

5 Open rebuke is better than secret love.

6 Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.

7 The full soul loatheth an honeycomb; but to the hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet.

8 As a bird that wandereth from her nest, so is a man that wandereth from his place.

9 Ointment and perfume rejoice the heart: so doth the sweetness of a man’s friend by hearty counsel.

10 Thine own friend, and thy father’s friend, forsake not; neither go into thy brother’s house in the day of thy calamity: for better is a neighbour that is near than a brother far off.

11 My son, be wise, and make my heart glad, that I may answer him that reproacheth me.

12 A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself; but the simple pass on, and are punished.

13 Take his garment that is surety for a stranger, and take a pledge of him for a strange woman.

14 He that blesseth his friend with a loud voice, rising early in the morning, it shall be counted a curse to him.

15 A continual dropping in a very rainy day and a contentious woman are alike.

16 Whosoever hideth her hideth the wind, and the ointment of his right hand, which bewrayeth itself.

17 Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.

18 Whoso keepeth the fig tree shall eat the fruit thereof: so he that waiteth on his master shall be honoured.

19 As in water face answereth to face, so the heart of man to man.

20 Hell and destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied.

21 As the fining pot for silver, and the furnace for gold; so is a man to his praise.

22 Though thou shouldest bray a fool in a mortar among wheat with a pestle, yet will not his foolishness depart from him.

23 Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds.

24 For riches are not for ever: and doth the crown endure to every generation?

25 The hay appeareth, and the tender grass sheweth itself, and herbs of the mountains are gathered.

26 The lambs are for thy clothing, and the goats are the price of the field.

27 And thou shalt have goats’ milk enough for thy food, for the food of thy household, and for the maintenance for thy maidens.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Boast not of tomorrow, let another praise you, and weigh friendship honestly: faithful are the wounds of a friend, deceitful the kisses of an enemy, and iron sharpens iron as one person sharpens another. The chapter ends with husbandry, a turn to work after talk: know the state of your flocks, for riches are not for ever, but lambs, goats' milk and mountain herbs provide food and clothing for the household. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Hell and destruction are never full, so the eyes of man are never satisfied, for two things are never satisfied, death and sin, and the carnal mind's appetites for profit or pleasure always desire more, while those whose eyes are toward the Lord are satisfied in him, and shall for ever be so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds, for we ought to have some business to do in this world, and not to live in idleness, nor meddle with what we do not understand, but be diligent and take pains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. For riches are not for ever, and does the crown endure to every generation, for we ought to have business to do and not live in idleness, and though the world cannot be secured to us, we must choose a more lasting portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. The hay appears, and the tender grass shows itself, and herbs of the mountains are gathered, for by God's blessing upon honest labours, we may expect to enjoy as much of earthly blessings as is good for us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. The lambs are for thy clothing, and the goats are the price of the field, for by God's blessing upon honest labours, we may expect to enjoy as much of earthly blessings as is good for us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
