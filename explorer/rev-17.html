The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And there came one of the seven angels which had the seven vials, and talked with me, saying unto me, Come hither; I will shew unto thee the judgment of the great whore that sitteth upon many waters:

2 With whom the kings of the earth have committed fornication, and the inhabitants of the earth have been made drunk with the wine of her fornication.

3 So he carried me away in the spirit into the wilderness: and I saw a woman sit upon a scarlet coloured beast, full of names of blasphemy, having seven heads and ten horns.

4 And the woman was arrayed in purple and scarlet colour, and decked with gold and precious stones and pearls, having a golden cup in her hand full of abominations and filthiness of her fornication:

5 And upon her forehead was a name written, MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF HARLOTS AND ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH.

6 And I saw the woman drunken with the blood of the saints, and with the blood of the martyrs of Jesus: and when I saw her, I wondered with great admiration.

7 And the angel said unto me, Wherefore didst thou marvel? I will tell thee the mystery of the woman, and of the beast that carrieth her, which hath the seven heads and ten horns.

8 The beast that thou sawest was, and is not; and shall ascend out of the bottomless pit, and go into perdition: and they that dwell on the earth shall wonder, whose names were not written in the book of life from the foundation of the world, when they behold the beast that was, and is not, and yet is.

9 And here is the mind which hath wisdom. The seven heads are seven mountains, on which the woman sitteth.

10 And there are seven kings: five are fallen, and one is, and the other is not yet come; and when he cometh, he must continue a short space.

11 And the beast that was, and is not, even he is the eighth, and is of the seven, and goeth into perdition.

12 And the ten horns which thou sawest are ten kings, which have received no kingdom as yet; but receive power as kings one hour with the beast.

13 These have one mind, and shall give their power and strength unto the beast.

14 These shall make war with the Lamb, and the Lamb shall overcome them: for he is Lord of lords, and King of kings: and they that are with him are called, and chosen, and faithful.

15 And he saith unto me, The waters which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues.

16 And the ten horns which thou sawest upon the beast, these shall hate the whore, and shall make her desolate and naked, and shall eat her flesh, and burn her with fire.

17 For God hath put in their hearts to fulfil his will, and to agree, and give their kingdom unto the beast, until the words of God shall be fulfilled.

18 And the woman which thou sawest is that great city, which reigneth over the kings of the earth.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

One of the vial angels shows John the judgment of the great whore seated on many waters, arrayed in purple and scarlet and gold, with MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT written on her forehead, drunken with the blood of saints and martyrs. The angel then explains the mystery, which Henry notes is new only in manner, not matter: the scarlet beast's seven heads are seven mountains and seven kings, its ten horns ten kings who give their power to the beast and make war with the Lamb — and the Lamb shall overcome them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings — while the waters are peoples, multitudes, nations and tongues, and the horns shall at last hate the whore and make her desolate. The woman, the angel says, is that great city which reigns over the kings of the earth. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. One of the seven angels who had the seven vials said to John, "Come hither; I will shew unto thee the judgment of the great whore that sitteth upon many waters." Henry notes the vision is new only in manner, not matter, being contemporary with the last three vials, and that a whore here is one married yet false to her husband's bed, having forsaken the guide of her youth and broken God's covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. With her the kings of the earth have committed fornication, and the inhabitants of the earth have been made drunk with the wine of her fornication. Henry notes she prostituted herself to the kings of the earth and intoxicated them with the wine of her fornication. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The angel carried John away in the spirit into the wilderness, where he saw a woman sit upon a scarlet-coloured beast, full of names of blasphemy, having seven heads and ten horns. Henry notes her principal seat is upon the beast, that is, Rome, the city on seven hills, infamous for idolatry, tyranny, and blasphemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The woman was arrayed in purple and scarlet colour, decked with gold, precious stones, and pearls, having a golden cup in her hand full of abominations and filthiness of her fornication. Henry notes the gay and gaudy appearance carried all the allurements of worldly honour, riches, pomp, and pride, suited to sensual and worldly minds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Upon her forehead was a name written, MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF HARLOTS AND ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH. Henry notes impudent harlots hung out signs with their names: she is named from her residence, Babylon, with a mystery in the name since it is not old Babylon literally, and from her practice, a mother breeding up harlots and nursing them to idolatry and lewdness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. John saw the woman drunken with the blood of the saints and with the blood of the martyrs of Jesus, and when he saw her, he wondered with great admiration. Henry notes her diet: she satiated herself with the saints' blood with such greediness that she intoxicated herself with it, satiated but never satisfied. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The angel said to John, "Wherefore didst thou marvel? I will tell thee the mystery of the woman, and of the beast that carrieth her, which hath the seven heads and ten horns." Henry notes the angel undertakes to open the vision, which is the key of the former visions, though explained in a way that still needs further explanation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The beast that thou sawest was, and is not, and shall ascend out of the bottomless pit, and go into perdition, and those dwelling on the earth whose names were not written in the book of life shall wonder when they behold the beast that was, and is not, and yet is. Henry notes it was a seat of idolatry and persecution, is not in the ancient pagan form, yet truly is the seat of idolatry and tyranny in another form, ascending from hell and returning thither. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Here is the mind which hath wisdom. The seven heads are seven mountains, on which the woman sitteth. Henry notes the double signification: seven mountains, the seven hills on which Rome stands, and seven kings, seven sorts of government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. There are seven kings: five are fallen, and one is, and the other is not yet come; and when he cometh, he must continue a short space. Henry notes Rome was governed by kings, consuls, tribunes, decemviri, dictators, pagan emperors, and Christian emperors: five extinct when the prophecy was written, one then in being, the pagan emperor, and the Christian emperor yet to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The beast that was, and is not, even he is the eighth, and is of the seven, and goeth into perdition. Henry notes the beast, the papacy, makes an eighth governor and sets up idolatry again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The ten horns which thou sawest are ten kings, which have received no kingdom as yet, but receive power as kings one hour with the beast. Henry notes they shall not rise until the Roman empire is broken, or near the end of antichrist's reign, reigning but one hour with her yet very unanimous and zealous in her interest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. These have one mind, and shall give their power and strength unto the beast. Henry notes they divest themselves of their prerogatives and revenues, things so dear to princes, out of an unaccountable fondness for the papacy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. These shall make war with the Lamb, and the Lamb shall overcome them: for he is Lord of lords, and King of kings: and they that are with him are called, and chosen, and faithful. Henry notes that to an eye of sense the beast's army appears much stronger than an army with a lamb at its head, yet Christ must reign till all enemies are put under his feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The angel said the waters thou sawest, where the whore sits, are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues. Henry notes she presided over vast multitudes of all languages, reigning not only over kingdoms but over kings, who were her tributaries and vassals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The ten horns and the beast shall hate the whore, and shall make her desolate and naked, and shall eat her flesh, and burn her with fire. Henry notes it was of God that their hearts were turned against her, so that seeing their folly and how they had been bewitched and enslaved, they become instruments of God's providence in her destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. For God hath put in their hearts to fulfil his will, and to agree, and give their kingdom unto the beast, until the words of God shall be fulfilled. Henry notes both turnings were of God: the kings were judicially blinded to give their kingdom to the beast, and afterwards turned against the whore, since their hearts were in his hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The woman which thou sawest is that great city, which reigneth over the kings of the earth. Henry notes the whore's vast obedience is itself the aggrandizement of the Lamb's victory, since she reigned over kings who were her tributaries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Babylon the great. The woman on the scarlet beast, mother of harlots and abominations, whose judgment the chapter shows; the angel explains her heads, horns and waters rather than leaving them bare symbols. Interpreted Study notes, verses 5 and 7, Matthew Henry
  • The waters. The angel himself explains them in verse 15: peoples, multitudes, nations and tongues on which the whore sits. Interpreted Chapter text, verse 15
