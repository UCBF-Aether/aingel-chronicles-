The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name was Job; and that man was perfect and upright, and one that feared God, and eschewed evil.

2 And there were born unto him seven sons and three daughters.

3 His substance also was seven thousand sheep, and three thousand camels, and five hundred yoke of oxen, and five hundred she asses, and a very great household; so that this man was the greatest of all the men of the east.

4 And his sons went and feasted in their houses, every one his day; and sent and called for their three sisters to eat and to drink with them.

5 And it was so, when the days of their feasting were gone about, that Job sent and sanctified them, and rose up early in the morning, and offered burnt offerings according to the number of them all: for Job said, It may be that my sons have sinned, and cursed God in their hearts. Thus did Job continually.

6 Now there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them.

7 And the LORD said unto Satan, Whence comest thou? Then Satan answered the LORD, and said, From going to and fro in the earth, and from walking up and down in it.

8 And the LORD said unto Satan, Hast thou considered my servant Job, that there is none like him in the earth, a perfect and an upright man, one that feareth God, and escheweth evil?

9 Then Satan answered the LORD, and said, Doth Job fear God for nought?

10 Hast not thou made an hedge about him, and about his house, and about all that he hath on every side? thou hast blessed the work of his hands, and his substance is increased in the land.

11 But put forth thine hand now, and touch all that he hath, and he will curse thee to thy face.

12 And the LORD said unto Satan, Behold, all that he hath is in thy power; only upon himself put not forth thine hand. So Satan went forth from the presence of the LORD.

13 And there was a day when his sons and his daughters were eating and drinking wine in their eldest brother’s house:

14 And there came a messenger unto Job, and said, The oxen were plowing, and the asses feeding beside them:

15 And the Sabeans fell upon them, and took them away; yea, they have slain the servants with the edge of the sword; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee.

16 While he was yet speaking, there came also another, and said, The fire of God is fallen from heaven, and hath burned up the sheep, and the servants, and consumed them; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee.

17 While he was yet speaking, there came also another, and said, The Chaldeans made out three bands, and fell upon the camels, and have carried them away, yea, and slain the servants with the edge of the sword; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee.

18 While he was yet speaking, there came also another, and said, Thy sons and thy daughters were eating and drinking wine in their eldest brother’s house:

19 And, behold, there came a great wind from the wilderness, and smote the four corners of the house, and it fell upon the young men, and they are dead; and I only am escaped alone to tell thee.

20 Then Job arose, and rent his mantle, and shaved his head, and fell down upon the ground, and worshipped,

21 And said, Naked came I out of my mother’s womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD.

22 In all this Job sinned not, nor charged God foolishly.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Job of Uz is blameless, upright, God-fearing, and the greatest man of the east, with seven sons, three daughters, and vast flocks. The scene then moves to heaven, where Satan, the adversary, questions whether Job fears God for nothing, and is permitted to take all Job has but not to touch Job himself. Four messengers bring ruin in quick succession — raiders, fire from heaven, Chaldeans, and a wind that kills his children — and Job answers by tearing his robe, worshipping, and blessing the name of the LORD who gave and has taken away. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Job of Uz is presented as blameless and upright, fearing God and turning from evil. Henry stresses that his piety, not merely his prosperity, is the foundation of the whole book. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Job's seven sons and three daughters show a full, flourishing household. The family stands complete before any trial touches it, which makes the losses that follow all the sharper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Job's vast flocks, herds, and household make him the greatest man of the east. Henry notes the rare point: he was prosperous and yet pious, since wealth's temptations had not overcome him by God's grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Job's sons feasted in turn in each other's houses, with their sisters invited. The picture is of a harmonious family enjoying its plenty together, not of riot or division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. After each round of feasting Job sent for his children, sanctified them, and offered a burnt offering for each one, fearing they might have sinned in their hearts. Henry reads this as a father's care for his children's souls and his dependence on God's appointed way of acceptance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The scene moves to heaven, where the sons of God present themselves before the LORD and Satan comes among them. JFB treats Satan here as the adversary who appears only by permission, not as an independent power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Asked where he has come from, Satan answers that he has been roaming the earth. The exchange shows him as a restless accuser already at work among men before he turns on Job. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The LORD himself points to Job as without equal on earth, perfect, upright, fearing God, and shunning evil. The commendation comes from God, not from Job's own claim, and it sets up the test that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Satan answers with a suspicion: does Job fear God for nothing? His charge is that Job's religion is bought by blessing, a bargain rather than love, and the whole trial will answer that charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Satan points to the hedge God has set around Job, his house, and all he owns, and to the blessing on his work. In Satan's telling, protection and prosperity are the only reasons for Job's obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Satan wagers that if God touches all Job has, Job will curse him to his face. The claim is that suffering will strip away the disguise and expose self-interest underneath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The LORD gives Satan power over all Job has, but forbids him to lay a hand on Job himself. JFB stresses the limit: Satan has no power against man until God gives it, and even then it runs only as far as God allows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. On a day when Job's children are feasting at their eldest brother's house, the first blow falls. Henry notes Satan chose the hour of greatest ease and pleasure, so the trouble would cut deepest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. A messenger reports the oxen plowing and the donkeys feeding beside them when disaster struck. The ordinary workday setting underlines how sudden the ruin is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Sabean raiders carry off the livestock and kill the servants with the sword, leaving one man alive to tell it. The human enemy is the first instrument of a trial permitted from above. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. While the first messenger is still speaking, a second reports fire from heaven consuming the sheep and servants. Blow follows blow without pause, so Job has no time to gather himself between losses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. A third messenger tells of Chaldean bands raiding the camels and killing the servants. Henry observes the design: manifold troubles arriving together, wave upon wave, to overwhelm rather than merely grieve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The fourth messenger brings the heaviest news: Job's sons and daughters were eating and drinking in their eldest brother's house when calamity struck. The loss of property was grievous; the loss of the children is of another order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. A great wind from the wilderness strikes the house's four corners, and it falls on the young people, killing them. The one survivor in each report exists only so Job hears the whole weight at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Job rises, tears his robe, shaves his head, falls to the ground, and worships. Henry reads this as humbling himself under God's hand: grief expressed openly, yet turned at once toward worship rather than rebellion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Job confesses he came naked into the world and will return naked, and blesses the name of the LORD who gave and has taken away. JFB notes that the mother's womb is used poetically for the earth, and that instead of cursing, Job blesses the name of Jehovah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. In all this Job did not sin or charge God foolishly. JFB explains the sense as Job committing no folly against God: hasty words against God in grief would themselves be folly, and Job refuses them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Sons of God. As the chapter presents them, heavenly beings who present themselves before the LORD; Satan comes among them. Jamieson-Fausset-Brown treats Satan here as the adversary who appears only by permission, not as an independent power. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Satan. The adversary and accuser in this scene: he questions whether Job fears God for nothing and wagers that loss will make Job curse God to his face. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Perfect. Blameless and upright, as the chapter itself words it — a wholehearted fear of God and turning from evil, not a claim of sinlessness. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
