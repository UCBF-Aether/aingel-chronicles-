The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the whole multitude of them arose, and led him unto Pilate.

2 And they began to accuse him, saying, We found this fellow perverting the nation, and forbidding to give tribute to Caesar, saying that he himself is Christ a King.

3 And Pilate asked him, saying, Art thou the King of the Jews? And he answered him and said, Thou sayest it.

4 Then said Pilate to the chief priests and to the people, I find no fault in this man.

5 And they were the more fierce, saying, He stirreth up the people, teaching throughout all Jewry, beginning from Galilee to this place.

6 When Pilate heard of Galilee, he asked whether the man were a Galilaean.

7 And as soon as he knew that he belonged unto Herod’s jurisdiction, he sent him to Herod, who himself also was at Jerusalem at that time.

8 And when Herod saw Jesus, he was exceeding glad: for he was desirous to see him of a long season, because he had heard many things of him; and he hoped to have seen some miracle done by him.

9 Then he questioned with him in many words; but he answered him nothing.

10 And the chief priests and scribes stood and vehemently accused him.

11 And Herod with his men of war set him at nought, and mocked him, and arrayed him in a gorgeous robe, and sent him again to Pilate.

12 And the same day Pilate and Herod were made friends together: for before they were at enmity between themselves.

13 And Pilate, when he had called together the chief priests and the rulers and the people,

14 Said unto them, Ye have brought this man unto me, as one that perverteth the people: and, behold, I, having examined him before you, have found no fault in this man touching those things whereof ye accuse him:

15 No, nor yet Herod: for I sent you to him; and, lo, nothing worthy of death is done unto him.

16 I will therefore chastise him, and release him.

17 (For of necessity he must release one unto them at the feast.)

18 And they cried out all at once, saying, Away with this man, and release unto us Barabbas:

19 (Who for a certain sedition made in the city, and for murder, was cast into prison.)

20 Pilate therefore, willing to release Jesus, spake again to them.

21 But they cried, saying, Crucify him, crucify him.

22 And he said unto them the third time, Why, what evil hath he done? I have found no cause of death in him: I will therefore chastise him, and let him go.

23 And they were instant with loud voices, requiring that he might be crucified. And the voices of them and of the chief priests prevailed.

24 And Pilate gave sentence that it should be as they required.

25 And he released unto them him that for sedition and murder was cast into prison, whom they had desired; but he delivered Jesus to their will.

26 And as they led him away, they laid hold upon one Simon, a Cyrenian, coming out of the country, and on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus.

27 And there followed him a great company of people, and of women, which also bewailed and lamented him.

28 But Jesus turning unto them said, Daughters of Jerusalem, weep not for me, but weep for yourselves, and for your children.

29 For, behold, the days are coming, in the which they shall say, Blessed are the barren, and the wombs that never bare, and the paps which never gave suck.

30 Then shall they begin to say to the mountains, Fall on us; and to the hills, Cover us.

31 For if they do these things in a green tree, what shall be done in the dry?

32 And there were also two other, malefactors, led with him to be put to death.

33 And when they were come to the place, which is called Calvary, there they crucified him, and the malefactors, one on the right hand, and the other on the left.

34 Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots.

35 And the people stood beholding. And the rulers also with them derided him, saying, He saved others; let him save himself, if he be Christ, the chosen of God.

36 And the soldiers also mocked him, coming to him, and offering him vinegar,

37 And saying, If thou be the king of the Jews, save thyself.

38 And a superscription also was written over him in letters of Greek, and Latin, and Hebrew, THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS.

39 And one of the malefactors which were hanged railed on him, saying, If thou be Christ, save thyself and us.

40 But the other answering rebuked him, saying, Dost not thou fear God, seeing thou art in the same condemnation?

41 And we indeed justly; for we receive the due reward of our deeds: but this man hath done nothing amiss.

42 And he said unto Jesus, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom.

43 And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.

44 And it was about the sixth hour, and there was a darkness over all the earth until the ninth hour.

45 And the sun was darkened, and the veil of the temple was rent in the midst.

46 And when Jesus had cried with a loud voice, he said, Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit: and having said thus, he gave up the ghost.

47 Now when the centurion saw what was done, he glorified God, saying, Certainly this was a righteous man.

48 And all the people that came together to that sight, beholding the things which were done, smote their breasts, and returned.

49 And all his acquaintance, and the women that followed him from Galilee, stood afar off, beholding these things.

50 And, behold, there was a man named Joseph, a counsellor; and he was a good man, and a just:

51 (The same had not consented to the counsel and deed of them;) he was of Arimathaea, a city of the Jews: who also himself waited for the kingdom of God.

52 This man went unto Pilate, and begged the body of Jesus.

53 And he took it down, and wrapped it in linen, and laid it in a sepulchre that was hewn in stone, wherein never man before was laid.

54 And that day was the preparation, and the sabbath drew on.

55 And the women also, which came with him from Galilee, followed after, and beheld the sepulchre, and how his body was laid.

56 And they returned, and prepared spices and ointments; and rested the sabbath day according to the commandment.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Pilate finds no fault in Jesus, sends him to Herod, who questions him and sends him back in gorgeous apparel, and Pilate, pressed by the crowd’s cry for Barabbas, delivers him to be crucified (vv. 1–25). Simon of Cyrene bears the cross, women lament on the way, and Jesus is crucified between two malefactors, one of whom hears “today shalt thou be with me in paradise”; darkness, the torn veil, and his final commendation of his spirit follow (vv. 26–49). Joseph of Arimathaea lays his body in a hewn tomb, and the women who followed from Galilee prepare spices and rest on the Sabbath (vv. 50–56). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 23

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And the whole multitude of them arose, and led him unto Pilate. The council's verdict becomes a procession to Pilate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. They began to accuse him: We found this fellow perverting the nation, forbidding tribute to Caesar, saying he himself is Christ a King. The religious charge is recast as political perversion and rival kingship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Pilate asked, Art thou the King of the Jews? And he answered, Thou sayest it. The answer neither denies nor pleads; it returns Pilate's own words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Then said Pilate to the chief priests and people, I find no fault in this man. Pilate's first finding is innocence, declared to accusers and people alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They were the more fierce, saying, He stirs up the people, teaching throughout all Jewry, beginning from Galilee to this place. Henry notes instead of being softened by Pilate's declaration of innocence, and considering whether they brought innocent blood's guilt on themselves, the Jews were the more angry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. When Pilate heard of Galilee, he asked whether the man were a Galilaean. Galilee offers Pilate a jurisdictional exit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. As soon as he knew he belonged unto Herod's jurisdiction, he sent him to Herod, who himself also was at Jerusalem at that time. The case is transferred to Herod, conveniently in Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When Herod saw Jesus, he was exceeding glad, for he was desirous to see him of a long season, because he had heard many things of him, and he hoped to have seen some miracle done by him. Henry notes Herod had heard many things and out of curiosity longed to see him; the poorest beggar asking a miracle for necessity was never denied, but this proud prince, asking only to gratify curiosity, is refused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Then he questioned with him in many words, but he answered him nothing. Many questions meet no answer, for Herod might have seen Christ's works in Galilee and would not, so now he would see them and shall not, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The chief priests and scribes stood and vehemently accused him. Before Herod, as before Pilate, accusation is vehement and standing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Herod with his men of war set him at nought, mocked him, arrayed him in a gorgeous robe, and sent him again to Pilate. Mockery in a gorgeous robe replaces the miracle Herod wanted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The same day Pilate and Herod were made friends together, for before they were at enmity between themselves. Henry notes the friendships of wicked men are often formed by union in wickedness, agreeing in little except enmity to God and contempt of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Pilate, when he had called together chief priests, rulers, and people. The second hearing gathers all parties together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Said unto them, Ye have brought this man as one that perverts the people, and behold, I, having examined him before you, have found no fault touching those things whereof ye accuse him. Examination before the accusers finds no fault in the accused charges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. No, nor yet Herod, for I sent you to him, and lo, nothing worthy of death is done unto him. Herod's return without a death sentence becomes Pilate's second witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. I will therefore chastise him, and release him. Henry asks, if no fault be found, why chastise him? Pilate, declaring innocence and minded to release, yet to please the people would punish him as an evildoer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. For of necessity he must release one unto them at the feast. The feast custom of release frames the coming choice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. They cried out all at once, Away with this man, and release unto us Barabbas. One voice replaces the hearing: away with Jesus, release Barabbas. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Who for a certain sedition made in the city, and for murder, was cast into prison. Barabbas, seditionist and murderer, is the man preferred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Pilate therefore, willing to release Jesus, spoke again to them. Pilate's willingness speaks a second time against the cry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. But they cried, Crucify him, crucify him. The cry hardens from away with him to crucify him, twice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. He said unto them the third time, Why, what evil has he done? I have found no cause of death in him: I will therefore chastise him, and let him go. The third declaration of innocence still offers chastisement instead of release. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. They were instant with loud voices, requiring that he might be crucified, and the voices of them and of the chief priests prevailed. Henry notes the fear of man brings many into the snare of doing an unjust thing against conscience rather than get into trouble, and Pilate yielded at length, lacking courage against so strong a stream. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Pilate gave sentence that it should be as they required. Sentence follows the voices, not the findings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. He released unto them him that for sedition and murder was cast into prison, whom they had desired, but he delivered Jesus to their will. Barabbas goes free, Jesus is delivered to their will, to be crucified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. As they led him away, they laid hold upon one Simon, a Cyrenian, coming out of the country, and on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus. Simon, from the country, bears the cross after Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. There followed him a great company of people, and of women, which also bewailed and lamented him. Henry notes though many reproached and reviled him, yet some pitied him, and the procession includes bewailing women. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. But Jesus turning unto them said, Daughters of Jerusalem, weep not for me, but weep for yourselves, and for your children. Henry notes Christ's death was his victory and our deliverance, purchase of eternal life, so weep not for him, but for our sins and our children's, which caused his death, and for fear of miseries brought by slighting his love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For behold, the days are coming, in which they shall say, Blessed are the barren, and the wombs that never bore, and the paps which never gave suck. Coming days will reverse blessing: barrenness will seem blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Then shall they begin to say to the mountains, Fall on us, and to the hills, Cover us. Mountains and hills are invoked as cover from coming judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. For if they do these things in a green tree, what shall be done in the dry? Henry notes if God delivered Christ, the green tree, to such sufferings as sacrifice for sin, what will he do with sinners themselves, dry trees, corrupt and good for nothing; the best saints compared with Christ are dry trees, and Christ's sufferings preach terror to obstinate transgressors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. There were also two other, malefactors, led with him to be put to death. Two malefactors share the procession, framing the crucifixion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. When they were come to the place called Calvary, there they crucified him, and the malefactors, one on the right hand, and the other on the left. Henry notes here is the Lamb of God led as a lamb to slaughter, to sacrifice, crucified between two thieves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots. Henry notes as soon as Christ was fastened to the cross, he prayed for his crucifiers, for the great thing he died to purchase is forgiveness of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The people stood beholding. The rulers also with them derided him, saying, He saved others, let him save himself, if he be Christ, the chosen of God. Beholding becomes derision from rulers: saving others is turned into a taunt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. The soldiers also mocked him, coming to him, and offering him vinegar. Soldiers join rulers in mockery, with vinegar offered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. And saying, If thou be the king of the Jews, save thyself. The kingship charge becomes a mocking condition: save thyself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. A superscription also was written over him in letters of Greek, Latin, and Hebrew, THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS. Three languages proclaim the kingship his enemies mock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. One of the malefactors hanged railed on him, saying, If thou be Christ, save thyself and us. Henry notes in the two thieves are shown the different effects the cross will have in gospel preaching: one hardened to the last, for no troubles of themselves will change a wicked heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. But the other answering rebuked him, saying, Dost not thou fear God, seeing thou art in the same condemnation? The other thief rebukes railing with fear of God and shared condemnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. And we indeed justly, for we receive the due reward of our deeds, but this man has done nothing amiss. Henry notes the penitent owned he deserved what was done, and believed Jesus suffered wrongfully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. And he said unto Jesus, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom. Henry notes his faith: Christ in deepest disgrace, suffering as a deceiver, not delivered by his Father, yet believed in a life to come, desiring happiness there, not merely rescue from the cross; and his humility: all his request is, Lord, remember me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, Today shalt thou be with me in paradise. Henry notes Christ on the cross is gracious like Christ on the throne, pitying a poor penitent in greatest agony, opening heaven to penitent believers; the case is singular, teaching despair of none, yet giving no encouragement to put off repentance, for late repentance is seldom true and none can be sure of deathbed time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. It was about the sixth hour, and there was darkness over all the earth until the ninth hour. Three hours of darkness cover the earth at midday. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. The sun was darkened, and the veil of the temple was rent in the midst. Darkened sun and rent veil together mark the death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. When Jesus had cried with a loud voice, he said, Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit, and having said thus, he gave up the ghost. Henry notes his death is magnified by attending wonders and explained by the words with which he breathed out his soul; he was willing to offer himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Now when the centurion saw what was done, he glorified God, saying, Certainly this was a righteous man. The centurion, seeing what was done, glorifies God with a verdict of righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. All the people that came together to that sight, beholding the things done, smote their breasts, and returned. Beholders return smiting their breasts, a different response from derision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. All his acquaintance, and the women that followed him from Galilee, stood afar off, beholding these things. Acquaintance and Galilean women stand afar off, beholding to the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Behold, there was a man named Joseph, a counsellor, and he was a good man, and a just. Joseph of Arimathea, counsellor, good and just, steps forward where others stood afar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. The same had not consented to the counsel and deed of them; he was of Arimathea, a city of the Jews, who also himself waited for the kingdom of God. Non-consent and waiting for the kingdom mark Joseph apart from the council's deed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. This man went unto Pilate, and begged the body of Jesus. Begging the body from Pilate is Joseph's public service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. He took it down, wrapped it in linen, and laid it in a sepulchre hewn in stone, wherein never man before was laid. Henry notes many who make no outward show, like Joseph, will be far readier for real service when occasion comes than noisier professors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. That day was the preparation, and the sabbath drew on. Burial is hastened because the sabbath draws on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. The women also, which came with him from Galilee, followed after, and beheld the sepulchre, and how his body was laid. Galilean women follow and behold the sepulchre and the laying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. They returned, and prepared spices and ointments, and rested the sabbath day according to the commandment. Henry notes weeping must not hinder sowing, and though in tears for their Lord, they must prepare to keep holy the sabbath; worldly affairs must be ordered not to hinder sabbath work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 35. The people stood beholding. The rulers also with them derided him, saying, He saved others, let him save himself, if he be Christ, the chosen of God. Beholding becomes derision from rulers: saving others is turned into a taunt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. The soldiers also mocked him, coming to him, and offering him vinegar. Soldiers join rulers in mockery, with vinegar offered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. And saying, If thou be the king of the Jews, save thyself. The kingship charge becomes a mocking condition: save thyself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. A superscription also was written over him in letters of Greek, Latin, and Hebrew, THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS. Three languages proclaim the kingship his enemies mock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. One of the malefactors hanged railed on him, saying, If thou be Christ, save thyself and us. Henry notes in the two thieves are shown the different effects the cross will have in gospel preaching: one hardened to the last, for no troubles of themselves will change a wicked heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. But the other answering rebuked him, saying, Dost not thou fear God, seeing thou art in the same condemnation? The other thief rebukes railing with fear of God and shared condemnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. And we indeed justly, for we receive the due reward of our deeds, but this man has done nothing amiss. Henry notes the penitent owned he deserved what was done, and believed Jesus suffered wrongfully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. And he said unto Jesus, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom. Henry notes his faith: Christ in deepest disgrace, suffering as a deceiver, not delivered by his Father, yet believed in a life to come, desiring happiness there, not merely rescue from the cross; and his humility: all his request is, Lord, remember me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, Today shalt thou be with me in paradise. Henry notes Christ on the cross is gracious like Christ on the throne, pitying a poor penitent in greatest agony, opening heaven to penitent believers; the case is singular, teaching despair of none, yet giving no encouragement to put off repentance, for late repentance is seldom true and none can be sure of deathbed time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. It was about the sixth hour, and there was darkness over all the earth until the ninth hour. Three hours of darkness cover the earth at midday. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. The sun was darkened, and the veil of the temple was rent in the midst. Darkened sun and rent veil together mark the death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. When Jesus had cried with a loud voice, he said, Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit, and having said thus, he gave up the ghost. Henry notes his death is magnified by attending wonders and explained by the words with which he breathed out his soul; he was willing to offer himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Now when the centurion saw what was done, he glorified God, saying, Certainly this was a righteous man. The centurion, seeing what was done, glorifies God with a verdict of righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. All the people that came together to that sight, beholding the things done, smote their breasts, and returned. Beholders return smiting their breasts, a different response from derision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. All his acquaintance, and the women that followed him from Galilee, stood afar off, beholding these things. Acquaintance and Galilean women stand afar off, beholding to the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Behold, there was a man named Joseph, a counsellor, and he was a good man, and a just. Joseph of Arimathea, counsellor, good and just, steps forward where others stood afar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. The same had not consented to the counsel and deed of them; he was of Arimathea, a city of the Jews, who also himself waited for the kingdom of God. Non-consent and waiting for the kingdom mark Joseph apart from the council's deed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. This man went unto Pilate, and begged the body of Jesus. Begging the body from Pilate is Joseph's public service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. He took it down, wrapped it in linen, and laid it in a sepulchre hewn in stone, wherein never man before was laid. Henry notes many who make no outward show, like Joseph, will be far readier for real service when occasion comes than noisier professors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. That day was the preparation, and the sabbath drew on. Burial is hastened because the sabbath draws on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. The women also, which came with him from Galilee, followed after, and beheld the sepulchre, and how his body was laid. Galilean women follow and behold the sepulchre and the laying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. They returned, and prepared spices and ointments, and rested the sabbath day according to the commandment. Henry notes weeping must not hinder sowing, and though in tears for their Lord, they must prepare to keep holy the sabbath; worldly affairs must be ordered not to hinder sabbath work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
