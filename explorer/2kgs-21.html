The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 21 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Manasseh was twelve years old when he began to reign, and reigned fifty and five years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Hephzibah.

2 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, after the abominations of the heathen, whom the LORD cast out before the children of Israel.

3 For he built up again the high places which Hezekiah his father had destroyed; and he reared up altars for Baal, and made a grove, as did Ahab king of Israel; and worshipped all the host of heaven, and served them.

4 And he built altars in the house of the LORD, of which the LORD said, In Jerusalem will I put my name.

5 And he built altars for all the host of heaven in the two courts of the house of the LORD.

6 And he made his son pass through the fire, and observed times, and used enchantments, and dealt with familiar spirits and wizards: he wrought much wickedness in the sight of the LORD, to provoke him to anger.

7 And he set a graven image of the grove that he had made in the house, of which the LORD said to David, and to Solomon his son, In this house, and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen out of all tribes of Israel, will I put my name for ever:

8 Neither will I make the feet of Israel move any more out of the land which I gave their fathers; only if they will observe to do according to all that I have commanded them, and according to all the law that my servant Moses commanded them.

9 But they hearkened not: and Manasseh seduced them to do more evil than did the nations whom the LORD destroyed before the children of Israel.

10 And the LORD spake by his servants the prophets, saying,

11 Because Manasseh king of Judah hath done these abominations, and hath done wickedly above all that the Amorites did, which were before him, and hath made Judah also to sin with his idols:

12 Therefore thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Behold, I am bringing such evil upon Jerusalem and Judah, that whosoever heareth of it, both his ears shall tingle.

13 And I will stretch over Jerusalem the line of Samaria, and the plummet of the house of Ahab: and I will wipe Jerusalem as a man wipeth a dish, wiping it, and turning it upside down.

14 And I will forsake the remnant of mine inheritance, and deliver them into the hand of their enemies; and they shall become a prey and a spoil to all their enemies;

15 Because they have done that which was evil in my sight, and have provoked me to anger, since the day their fathers came forth out of Egypt, even unto this day.

16 Moreover Manasseh shed innocent blood very much, till he had filled Jerusalem from one end to another; beside his sin wherewith he made Judah to sin, in doing that which was evil in the sight of the LORD.

17 Now the rest of the acts of Manasseh, and all that he did, and his sin that he sinned, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

18 And Manasseh slept with his fathers, and was buried in the garden of his own house, in the garden of Uzza: and Amon his son reigned in his stead.

19 Amon was twenty and two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned two years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Meshullemeth, the daughter of Haruz of Jotbah.

20 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, as his father Manasseh did.

21 And he walked in all the way that his father walked in, and served the idols that his father served, and worshipped them:

22 And he forsook the LORD God of his fathers, and walked not in the way of the LORD.

23 And the servants of Amon conspired against him, and slew the king in his own house.

24 And the people of the land slew all them that had conspired against king Amon; and the people of the land made Josiah his son king in his stead.

25 Now the rest of the acts of Amon which he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

26 And he was buried in his sepulchre in the garden of Uzza: and Josiah his son reigned in his stead.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Manasseh rebuilds everything Hezekiah tore down, fills Jerusalem with idols and innocent blood, and reigns fifty-five years; his son Amon follows him for two and is murdered by his servants. The chapter records the judgment pronounced on Judah for Manasseh's sins, to be carried out in a later generation. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Manasseh was twelve when he began to reign and reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem, his mother Hephzibah. JFB notes he was born three years after his father's recovery, and his minority under guardians hostile to his father's reforming policy helps explain his anti-theocratic principles, as degenerate leaders who had him in their power trained him in their views and seduced him into open patronage of idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Manasseh did evil in the LORD's sight, after the abominations of the heathen the LORD cast out before Israel. JFB notes Hezekiah's reformation was only partially accomplished, the true fear of God had vanished from the masses, and corruption openly practised by degenerate leaders shaped the young prince. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Manasseh built up again the high places Hezekiah had destroyed, reared altars for Baal, made a grove as Ahab had, and worshipped all the host of heaven. JFB explains this as the restoration of high places and Baal pillars, with the Asherah representing all the host of heaven in pure star-worship of Chaldaic and Assyrian origin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Manasseh built altars in the LORD's house, of which the LORD had said, "In Jerusalem will I put my name." JFB's account of his idolatry places these altars within the temple dedicated to the true God's worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He built altars for all the host of heaven in the two courts of the LORD's house. JFB identifies the two courts as the priests' court and the large court of the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Manasseh made his son pass through the fire, observed times, used enchantments, dealt with familiar spirits and wizards, and wrought much wickedness in provoking the LORD. JFB explains observing times came from watching clouds, enchantments were jugglery and spells, familiar spirits were ventriloquists pretending to consult a spirit, and wizards were knowing ones pretending to reveal secrets and interpret dreams, a Chaldaean influx Manasseh liberally patronized and even practised himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He set a graven image of the grove he had made in the house of which the LORD had spoken to David and Solomon, saying there he would put his name for ever. JFB dwells on placing the Asherah within the temple precincts as the most aggravated outrage of the royal idolater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The LORD had said he would not make Israel's feet move any more from the land given their fathers, only if they would observe all he commanded and the law Moses commanded. JFB alludes this promise to the covenant with David, noting the condition was expressed from Israel's first plantation in Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. But they hearkened not, and Manasseh seduced them to do more evil than the nations the LORD destroyed before Israel. JFB notes that through Manasseh's pernicious influence they were seduced into greater excesses of idolatrous corruption than even the original Canaanites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The LORD spoke by his servants the prophets. JFB identifies these prophets as Hosea, Joel, Nahum, Habakkuk, and Isaiah, whose counsels and warnings were recorded in the national chronicles and now form part of the sacred canon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Because Manasseh had done these abominations, acting more wickedly than the Amorites before him and making Judah sin with his idols, the LORD would bring evil on Jerusalem and Judah. JFB's section on the prophetic sentence frames this indictment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The LORD declared he was bringing such evil upon Jerusalem and Judah that whoever heard of it, both his ears would tingle. JFB calls this a strong metaphorical form announcing an extraordinary and appalling event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The LORD would stretch over Jerusalem the line of Samaria and the plummet of Ahab's house, wiping Jerusalem as a man wipes a dish, turning it upside down. JFB explains captives doomed to destruction were marked off by measuring-line and plummet, so the line meant Samaria's destruction and the plummet Ahab's family's extermination, threatening Judah with utter destruction like both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The LORD would forsake the remnant of his inheritance and deliver them into their enemies' hands, to become a prey and spoil to all their enemies. JFB notes the remnant was Judah, alone remaining of the chosen people, and forsaking them meant falling into enemy power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. This was because they had done evil in God's sight and provoked him to anger since their fathers came out of Egypt, even unto this day. JFB's section on the prophetic judgment covers this long provocation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Moreover Manasseh shed innocent blood very much, till he filled Jerusalem from one end to another, besides his sin in making Judah sin. JFB notes that not content with idolatrous abomination, he cruelly persecuted all nonconformists, deluging the land with good men's blood, among whom tradition says Isaiah was sawn asunder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Manasseh slept with his fathers, was buried in the garden of his own house, and Amon his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section on Amon's reign follows this burial and succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Amon was twenty-two when he began to reign and reigned two years in Jerusalem, his mother Meshullemeth daughter of Haruz of Jotbah. JFB notes Amon continued his father's idolatrous policy, and after an inglorious two-year reign was massacred by his own domestics. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Amon did evil in the LORD's sight, as his father Manasseh did. JFB presents Amon as continuing the idolatrous policy of his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Amon walked in all the way his father walked in, served the idols his father served, and worshipped them. JFB's section on Amon frames this as the continuation of Manasseh's idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Amon forsook the LORD God of his fathers and walked not in the LORD's way. JFB notes Amon's reign simply continued his father's apostasy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Amon's servants conspired against him and slew the king in his own house. JFB notes he was massacred by some of his own domestics. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The people of the land slew all who had conspired against King Amon and made Josiah his son king in his stead. JFB notes the people slew the regicide conspirators and placed Josiah on the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Amon was buried in his sepulchre in the garden of Uzza, and Josiah his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section closes Amon's reign with this burial and Josiah's accession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
