The Text — Jubilees 48 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the sixth year of the third week of the forty-ninth jubilee thou didst depart and dwell in the land of Midian five weeks and one year.

2 And thou didst return into Egypt 2 in the second week in the second year in the fiftieth jubilee.

3 And thou thyself knowest what He spake unto thee on Mount Sinai, and what prince Mastema. 3 desired to do with thee when thou wast returning into Egypt on the way when thou didst meet him at the lodgingplace.

4 Did he not with all his power seek to slay thee and deliver the Egyptians out of thy hand when he saw that thou wast sent to execute judgment and vengeance on the Egyptians ? 4 4.

5 And I delivered thee out of his hand, and thou didst perform the signs and wonders which thou wast sent to perform in Egypt against Pharaoh, and against all his house, and against his servants and his people. ,

6 And the Lord executed a great vengeance on them for Israel's sake, and smote them through (the plagues of) blood and frogs, lice and dog-flies, and malignant boils breaking forth in blains ; and their cattle by death; and by hail-stones, thereby He destroyed everything that grew for them ; and by locusts which devoured the residue which had been left by the hail, and by darkness ; and (by the death) of the first-born of men and animals, and on all their idols the Lord took vengeance and burned them with fire.

7 And everything was sent through thy hand,

8 that thou shouldst declare (these things) before they were done, and thou didst speak with the king of Egypt before all his servants and before his people.

9 And everything took place according to thy words ; ten great and terrible judgments came on the land of Egypt that thou mightest execute vengeance on it for Israel.

10 And the Lord did everything for Israel's sake, and according to His covenant, which He had ordained with Abraham that He would take vengeance on them as they had brought them by force into bondage.

11 And the prince of the Mastema stood up against thee, and sought to cast thee into the hands of Pharaoh, and he helped the Egyptian sorcerers, and they stood up and wrought before thee.

12 The evils indeed we permitted them to work, but the remedies we did not allow to be wrought by their hands.

13 And the Lord smote them with malignant ulcers, arid they were not able to stand for we destroyed them so that they could not perform a single sign.

14 And notwithstanding all (these) signs and wonders the prince of the Mastema. was not put to shame because he took courage and cried to the Egyptians to pursue after thee with all the powers of the Egyptians, with their chariots, and with their horses, and with all the hosts of the peoples of Egypt.

15 And I stood between the Egyptians and Israel, and we delivered Israel out of his hand, and out of the hand of his people, and the Lord brought them through the midst of the sea as if it were dry land.

16 And all the peoples whom he brought to pursue after Israel, the Lord our God cast them into the midst of the sea, into the depths of the abyss beneath the children of Israel, even as the people of Egypt had cast their cluldren into the river.

17 He took vengeance on 1,000,000 of them, and one thousand strong and energetic men were

18 destroyed on account of one suckling of the children of thy people which they had thrown into the river.

19 And on the fourteenth day and on the fifteenth and on the sixteenth and on the seventeenth and on the eighteenth the prince of the Mastema. was bound and imprisoned behind the children of Israel that he might not accuse them.

20 And on the nineteenth we let them loose that they might help the Egyptians and pursue the children of Israel.

21 And he 2 hardened their hearts and made them stubborn, and the device was devised by the Lord our God that He might smite the Egyptians and cast them into the sea.

22 And on the fourteenth we bound him that he might not accuse the children of Israel on the clay when they asked the Egyptians for vessels and garments, vessels of silver, and vessels of gold, and vessels of bronze, in order to despoil the Egyptians 3 in return for the bondage in which they had forced them to serve.

23 And we did not lead forth the children of Israel from Egypt empty handed.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: In the sixth year of the third week of the forty-ninth jubilee Moses departed and dwelt in Midian five weeks and one year. Israel was not led forth from Egypt empty-handed. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the sixth year of the third week of the forty-ninth jubilee Moses departed and dwelt in Midian five weeks and one year. Henry notes that Moses was obliged to flee into Midian, that God ordered this for wise and holy ends, and that his life falls into three forties: prince in Egypt, shepherd in Midian, and king in Jeshurun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Moses returned into Egypt in the second week, in the second year, in the fiftieth jubilee. Henry notes that after God appeared in the bush, he often spoke to Moses, and that Moses left Midian with God's message to Pharaoh, to demand Israel's discharge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Moses himself knew what God spoke to him on Mount Sinai, and what prince Mastema desired to do with him when returning into Egypt, meeting him at the lodging-place. Henry notes that God met Moses in anger at the lodging-place, threatening him with death for neglecting to circumcise his son, and that when God shows us what is amiss, we must amend it speedily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Mastema sought with all his power to slay Moses and deliver the Egyptians out of his hand, when he saw Moses was sent to execute judgment and vengeance on the Egyptians. Henry's account of the lodging-place anger fits Jubilees' reading: a spiritual adversary sought Moses' death on the very road of his mission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The angel delivered Moses out of Mastema's hand, and Moses performed the signs and wonders he was sent to perform in Egypt against Pharaoh, his house, his servants, and his people. Henry notes that God gave Moses power to work miracles, so that the people would take his word, and that Moses, strengthened in faith, went about his work with courage and perseverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The Lord executed great vengeance on Egypt for Israel's sake, smiting them through blood, frogs, lice, dog-flies, malignant boils breaking forth in blains, cattle by death, hail-stones destroying all that grew, locusts devouring the residue left by hail, darkness, the death of the first-born of men and animals, and on all their idols he took vengeance and burned them with fire. Henry calls the first plague, the river turned to blood, a righteous plague, since the Egyptians had stained the river with Hebrew children's blood, and notes that the last plague slew the first-born from throne to dungeon, because they had slain the Hebrews' children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Everything was sent through Moses' hand, that he should declare these things before they were done. Henry notes that Moses, as Jehovah's ambassador, laid commands upon Pharaoh, denounced threatenings, and called for judgments upon him, declaring God's word before God's hand struck. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Moses was to declare the judgments before they were done, and he spoke with the king of Egypt before all his servants and before his people. The public declaration is the point: God's true miracles were not performed in corners, but in the sight of Pharaoh and his attendants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Everything took place according to Moses' words, and ten great and terrible judgments came on Egypt, that he might execute vengeance on it for Israel. Henry counts the plagues as ten, beginning with blood and ending with the first-born, each a righteous judgment on the oppressors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The Lord did everything for Israel's sake, according to his covenant with Abraham, that he would take vengeance on Egypt as they had brought Israel by force into bondage. Henry notes that God remembered his covenant, of which he is ever mindful, and looked upon Israel to help them, and that Exodus plainly fulfils the promises and prophecies made to Abraham respecting his seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The prince of Mastema stood up against Moses, sought to cast him into Pharaoh's hands, and helped the Egyptian sorcerers, who stood up and wrought before him. Henry notes that the magicians were cheats trying to copy Moses' real miracles by secret sleights, succeeding to a small extent so as to deceive bystanders, and that God often permits a snare wherein the disobedient are entangled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The evils indeed were permitted to the sorcerers to work, but the remedies were not allowed to be wrought by their hands. Henry's account agrees: the magicians could imitate to a point, but were at length obliged to confess they could no longer imitate the effects of divine power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The Lord smote the sorcerers with malignant ulcers, and they were not able to stand, for they were destroyed so that they could not perform a single sign. Henry notes the same defeat: the counterfeit wonders failed, and the magicians confessed they could not imitate divine power, for Satan is most to be dreaded when transformed into an angel of light, yet his imitations always fail at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Notwithstanding all these signs and wonders, the prince of Mastema was not put to shame, but took courage and cried to the Egyptians to pursue Israel with all Egypt's powers, chariots, horses, and hosts of the peoples of Egypt. Henry notes that Pharaoh, having let Israel go, was angry with himself for it, and that God would be honoured upon Pharaoh, whose envy and rage became a torment to himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The angel stood between the Egyptians and Israel, and delivered Israel out of Mastema's hand and his people's hand, and the Lord brought them through the midst of the sea as if it were dry land. Henry notes that the pillar of cloud and fire came behind Israel where they needed a guard, a wall between them and their enemies, and that they walked upon dry land in the midst of the sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. All the peoples brought to pursue Israel, the Lord cast into the midst of the sea, into the depths of the abyss beneath Israel, even as the Egyptians had cast Israel's children into the river. Henry notes the just and righteous wrath of God upon his people's enemies: the waters returned and overwhelmed all Pharaoh's host, not one escaping, and the ruin of sinners is brought on by their own rage and presumption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Mastema hardened the Egyptians' hearts and made them stubborn, and the device was devised by the Lord God that he might smite the Egyptians and cast them into the sea. Henry notes that Pharaoh had hardened his own heart against the oppressed, and now God, in righteous judgment, hardened his heart against the teaching of the miracles, so that he would not let Israel alone and fell with his host. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. On the fourteenth the angel bound Mastema, that he might not accuse Israel on the day when they asked the Egyptians for vessels and garments, silver, gold, and bronze, in order to despoil the Egyptians in return for the bondage they had forced them to serve. Henry notes that the Egyptians, in terror, would purchase Israel's favour and speedy departure, and that the Lord took care that their hard-earned wages should be paid and the people provided for their journey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Israel was not led forth from Egypt empty-handed. Henry notes the same provision: the Egyptians furnished Israel with riches at their departure, and the people who had served in bondage left with the wages of their years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
