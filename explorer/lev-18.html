The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, I am the LORD your God.

3 After the doings of the land of Egypt, wherein ye dwelt, shall ye not do: and after the doings of the land of Canaan, whither I bring you, shall ye not do: neither shall ye walk in their ordinances.

4 Ye shall do my judgments, and keep mine ordinances, to walk therein: I am the LORD your God.

5 Ye shall therefore keep my statutes, and my judgments: which if a man do, he shall live in them: I am the LORD.

6 None of you shall approach to any that is near of kin to him, to uncover their nakedness: I am the LORD.

7 The nakedness of thy father, or the nakedness of thy mother, shalt thou not uncover: she is thy mother; thou shalt not uncover her nakedness.

8 The nakedness of thy father’s wife shalt thou not uncover: it is thy father’s nakedness.

9 The nakedness of thy sister, the daughter of thy father, or daughter of thy mother, whether she be born at home, or born abroad, even their nakedness thou shalt not uncover.

10 The nakedness of thy son’s daughter, or of thy daughter’s daughter, even their nakedness thou shalt not uncover: for theirs is thine own nakedness.

11 The nakedness of thy father’s wife’s daughter, begotten of thy father, she is thy sister, thou shalt not uncover her nakedness.

12 Thou shalt not uncover the nakedness of thy father’s sister: she is thy father’s near kinswoman.

13 Thou shalt not uncover the nakedness of thy mother’s sister: for she is thy mother’s near kinswoman.

14 Thou shalt not uncover the nakedness of thy father’s brother, thou shalt not approach to his wife: she is thine aunt.

15 Thou shalt not uncover the nakedness of thy daughter in law: she is thy son’s wife; thou shalt not uncover her nakedness.

16 Thou shalt not uncover the nakedness of thy brother’s wife: it is thy brother’s nakedness.

17 Thou shalt not uncover the nakedness of a woman and her daughter, neither shalt thou take her son’s daughter, or her daughter’s daughter, to uncover her nakedness; for they are her near kinswomen: it is wickedness.

18 Neither shalt thou take a wife to her sister, to vex her, to uncover her nakedness, beside the other in her life time.

19 Also thou shalt not approach unto a woman to uncover her nakedness, as long as she is put apart for her uncleanness.

20 Moreover thou shalt not lie carnally with thy neighbour’s wife, to defile thyself with her.

21 And thou shalt not let any of thy seed pass through the fire to Molech, neither shalt thou profane the name of thy God: I am the LORD.

22 Thou shalt not lie with mankind, as with womankind: it is abomination.

23 Neither shalt thou lie with any beast to defile thyself therewith: neither shall any woman stand before a beast to lie down thereto: it is confusion.

24 Defile not ye yourselves in any of these things: for in all these the nations are defiled which I cast out before you:

25 And the land is defiled: therefore I do visit the iniquity thereof upon it, and the land itself vomiteth out her inhabitants.

26 Ye shall therefore keep my statutes and my judgments, and shall not commit any of these abominations; neither any of your own nation, nor any stranger that sojourneth among you:

27 (For all these abominations have the men of the land done, which were before you, and the land is defiled;)

28 That the land spue not you out also, when ye defile it, as it spued out the nations that were before you.

29 For whosoever shall commit any of these abominations, even the souls that commit them shall be cut off from among their people.

30 Therefore shall ye keep mine ordinance, that ye commit not any one of these abominable customs, which were committed before you, and that ye defile not yourselves therein: I am the LORD your God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter forbids Israel to follow the practices of Egypt, where they dwelt, or of Canaan, where they are being brought. It lists prohibited unions by kinship and by marriage, covering parents, siblings, aunts, daughters-in-law, a wife's sister in her lifetime, and others, and then forbids adultery, giving seed to Molech, lying with mankind as with womankind, and lying with a beast. The warning is that the nations were cast out for these abominations and the land itself vomited them out, and Israel will be spewed out likewise if they defile themselves in the same way. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the law of unlawful marriages after the blood law, as a law widely different from Egyptian and Canaanite custom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Israel is addressed as the LORD's people, I am the LORD your God, a renewed mention of divine sovereignty bearing particularly on these laws. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They shall not do according to Egypt, where they dwelt, nor according to Canaan, where they are being brought, nor walk in their ordinances, since the enormities prohibited here were freely practised or publicly sanctioned in both countries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They shall do the LORD's judgments and keep His ordinances to walk in them, for He is the LORD their God, in place of Egyptian and Canaanite ordinances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Keeping His statutes and judgments brings life in them, a blessing verified in eras of pure religion by public prosperity and domestic happiness, and with a higher reference to spiritual life as applied by Christ and Paul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. None shall approach any near of kin to uncover nakedness, a general summary of all particular prohibitions, against Egyptian laxity that sanctioned brother-sister and even parent-child marriages, forming the basis of later Christian marriage law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The nakedness of father or mother shall not be uncovered, she being his mother, the first particular case under the general summary, a consanguinity prohibition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The nakedness of his father's wife shall not be uncovered, for it is his father's nakedness, an affinity prohibition included in the general summary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The nakedness of his sister, father's daughter or mother's daughter, born at home or elsewhere, shall not be uncovered, a consanguinity case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The nakedness of son's daughter or daughter's daughter shall not be uncovered, for theirs is his own nakedness, a consanguinity case in the descending line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The nakedness of his father's wife's daughter, begotten by his father, she being his sister, shall not be uncovered, a half-sister case under the same summary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The nakedness of his father's sister shall not be uncovered, for she is near of kin to his father, an aunt case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The nakedness of his mother's sister shall not be uncovered, for she is near of kin to his mother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The nakedness of his father's brother shall not be uncovered, nor shall he approach his wife, for she is his aunt, an uncle's-wife affinity case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The nakedness of his daughter-in-law, his son's wife, shall not be uncovered, an affinity case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The nakedness of his brother's wife shall not be uncovered, for it is his brother's nakedness, an affinity case completing the eleven affinity cases. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. A woman and her daughter, or her son's daughter or daughter's daughter, shall not be taken to uncover nakedness, for they are near kinswomen, a wicked consanguinity case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Neither shall he take a wife to her sister to vex her, beside the other in her lifetime, a practice common among Egyptians and Chaldeans, the marginal reading also prohibiting polygamy as contrary to the original marriage law, though Moses tolerated it for hardness of heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. He shall not approach a woman to uncover her nakedness as long as she is put apart for her uncleanness, the separation law of chapter 15 applying within the marriage prohibitions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He shall not lie carnally with his neighbour's wife to defile himself with her, an adultery case among the enumerated enormities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. He shall not let any seed pass through the fire to Molech, the Ammonite idol, king, whose brass calf-headed statue was heated and infants passed through its arms for lustration, nor profane God's name by giving foreigners occasion to call Him cruel for demanding human victims. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. He shall not lie with mankind as with womankind, for it is abomination, among the unnatural crimes enumerated with incest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Neither shall he lie with any beast to defile himself, nor shall a woman stand before a beast to lie down thereto, for it is confusion, an unnatural enormity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Israel shall not defile themselves in any of these things, for in all these the nations are defiled which God casts out before them, seventeen express incest cases, eleven of affinity and six of consanguinity, with aggravated unnatural crimes, being the cause of Canaan's expulsion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The land is defiled, therefore God visits its iniquity upon it, and the land itself vomits out her inhabitants, figurative language showing the hopeless depth of Canaanite moral corruption and the judicial character of their extermination. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Israel shall keep His statutes and judgments and commit none of these abominations, neither native nor sojourning stranger, since the law binds the stranger dwelling among them as well. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. For all these abominations have the men of the land done, who were before them, and the land is defiled, the prior inhabitants having already polluted it by the same practices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. That the land does not spew Israel out also, as it spewed out the nations before them, is the condition of their keeping, since the same defilement would bring the same expulsion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. For whosoever commits any of these abominations, even the souls that commit them shall be cut off from among their people, death being sternly denounced without distinction on incest, affinity, consanguinity, and bestiality alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Therefore they shall keep His ordinance, committing none of these abominable customs practised before them, and not defiling themselves, for God is only re-delivering the law imprinted on the natural heart, forbidden to all men from the beginning or at least from the flood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
