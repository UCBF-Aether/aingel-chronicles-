The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And David made him houses in the city of David, and prepared a place for the ark of God, and pitched for it a tent.

2 Then David said, None ought to carry the ark of God but the Levites: for them hath the LORD chosen to carry the ark of God, and to minister unto him for ever.

3 And David gathered all Israel together to Jerusalem, to bring up the ark of the LORD unto his place, which he had prepared for it.

4 And David assembled the children of Aaron, and the Levites:

5 Of the sons of Kohath; Uriel the chief, and his brethren an hundred and twenty:

6 Of the sons of Merari; Asaiah the chief, and his brethren two hundred and twenty:

7 Of the sons of Gershom; Joel the chief and his brethren an hundred and thirty:

8 Of the sons of Elizaphan; Shemaiah the chief, and his brethren two hundred:

9 Of the sons of Hebron; Eliel the chief, and his brethren fourscore:

10 Of the sons of Uzziel; Amminadab the chief, and his brethren an hundred and twelve.

11 And David called for Zadok and Abiathar the priests, and for the Levites, for Uriel, Asaiah, and Joel, Shemaiah, and Eliel, and Amminadab,

12 And said unto them, Ye are the chief of the fathers of the Levites: sanctify yourselves, both ye and your brethren, that ye may bring up the ark of the LORD God of Israel unto the place that I have prepared for it.

13 For because ye did it not at the first, the LORD our God made a breach upon us, for that we sought him not after the due order.

14 So the priests and the Levites sanctified themselves to bring up the ark of the LORD God of Israel.

15 And the children of the Levites bare the ark of God upon their shoulders with the staves thereon, as Moses commanded according to the word of the LORD.

16 And David spake to the chief of the Levites to appoint their brethren to be the singers with instruments of musick, psalteries and harps and cymbals, sounding, by lifting up the voice with joy.

17 So the Levites appointed Heman the son of Joel; and of his brethren, Asaph the son of Berechiah; and of the sons of Merari their brethren, Ethan the son of Kushaiah;

18 And with them their brethren of the second degree, Zechariah, Ben, and Jaaziel, and Shemiramoth, and Jehiel, and Unni, Eliab, and Benaiah, and Maaseiah, and Mattithiah, and Elipheleh, and Mikneiah, and Obededom, and Jeiel, the porters.

19 So the singers, Heman, Asaph, and Ethan, were appointed to sound with cymbals of brass;

20 And Zechariah, and Aziel, and Shemiramoth, and Jehiel, and Unni, and Eliab, and Maaseiah, and Benaiah, with psalteries on Alamoth;

21 And Mattithiah, and Elipheleh, and Mikneiah, and Obededom, and Jeiel, and Azaziah, with harps on the Sheminith to excel.

22 And Chenaniah, chief of the Levites, was for song: he instructed about the song, because he was skilful.

23 And Berechiah and Elkanah were doorkeepers for the ark.

24 And Shebaniah, and Jehoshaphat, and Nethaneel, and Amasai, and Zechariah, and Benaiah, and Eliezer, the priests, did blow with the trumpets before the ark of God: and Obededom and Jehiah were doorkeepers for the ark.

25 So David, and the elders of Israel, and the captains over thousands, went to bring up the ark of the covenant of the LORD out of the house of Obededom with joy.

26 And it came to pass, when God helped the Levites that bare the ark of the covenant of the LORD, that they offered seven bullocks and seven rams.

27 And David was clothed with a robe of fine linen, and all the Levites that bare the ark, and the singers, and Chenaniah the master of the song with the singers: David also had upon him an ephod of linen.

28 Thus all Israel brought up the ark of the covenant of the LORD with shouting, and with sound of the cornet, and with trumpets, and with cymbals, making a noise with psalteries and harps.

29 And it came to pass, as the ark of the covenant of the LORD came to the city of David, that Michal, the daughter of Saul looking out at a window saw king David dancing and playing: and she despised him in her heart.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells how David, having learned from the first attempt, brings the ark up properly — carried by Levites, with musicians before it — to the tent he pitched in Jerusalem, while Michal despises his rejoicing. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David made houses for himself in Jerusalem, and prepared a place for the ark, pitching a tent for it. Henry notes the correction of the first attempt: wherever we build for ourselves, we must make room for God's ark, a church in the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. None ought to carry the ark but the Levites, for the Lord chose them to carry it and minister forever. Henry notes David now remembered the express rule: Kohathites bore it on shoulders, with no wagons, and even knowing men may overlook a rule until corrected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. David gathered all Israel to Jerusalem to bring up the ark to its place. Henry notes he not only summoned Levites as before, but saw that they assembled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. David assembled the children of Aaron and the Levites. Henry stresses the chiefs especially were summoned, since those advanced in dignity should go before others in duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. David called Zadok and Abiathar the priests, and the Levite chiefs Uriel, Asaiah, Joel, Shemaiah, Eliel, and Amminadab. Henry notes the sons of Aaron were called with the chiefs to receive the solemn charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Ye are chief of the fathers of the Levites: sanctify yourselves, that ye may bring up the ark of the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes chiefs bear more expectation, both in service and influence, and their first neglect had cost a breach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. For because ye did it not at the first, the Lord made a breach upon us, for we sought him not after the due order. Henry makes this the key confession: it is not enough to do good, we must do it well, seeking God in due order, and learn regularity from chastisement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The priests and Levites sanctified themselves to bring up the ark. Henry notes the breach on Uzza made them careful to cleanse from ceremonial pollution and compose themselves, so as to strike reverence on the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The Levites bore the ark on their shoulders with staves, as Moses commanded according to the Lord's word. Henry notes many remiss in duty will reform when faithfully told, and the Levites now carried it by law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. David spake to the chief Levites to appoint their brethren singers with psalteries, harps, and cymbals, sounding aloud with joy. Henry notes officers were appointed to welcome the ark with every expression of joy, proficients nominated for the service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The Levites appointed Heman son of Joel, Asaph son of Berechiah, and Ethan son of Kushaiah. Henry notes Heman, Asaph, and Ethan were now first appointed as the three chief singers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. With them their brethren of the second degree, Zechariah and others, and Obed-edom and Jeiel the porters. Henry notes some were appointed porters, and Obed-edom, the ark's former housekeeper, counted doorkeeping an honour and recompense. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The singers Heman, Asaph, and Ethan were appointed to sound with cymbals of brass. Henry notes they undertook to sound with symbols, the chief musicians leading the welcome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Zechariah and others were with psalteries on Alamoth. Henry notes others sang with psalteries, in the concert's order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Mattithiah and others were with harps on the Sheminith to excel. Henry notes harps on the eighth, eight notes higher or lower, according to the rules of the concert. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Chenaniah, chief of the Levites, was for song, because he was skilful. Henry notes one was appointed for song who could sing well and instruct others: those excelling in a gift should teach others, not grudge making them as wise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Berechiah and Elkanah were doorkeepers for the ark. Henry lists door-keepers for the ark among the appointed offices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Shebaniah and other priests blew trumpets before the ark, and Obed-edom and Jehiah were doorkeepers. Henry notes priests blew trumpets as usual at the ark's removal and solemn feasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. David, Israel's elders, and captains went to bring up the ark from Obed-edom's house with joy. Henry marks this second conveyance as done more successfully: no mistake in the work, and no damp on the joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. It came to pass, when God helped the Levites that bare the ark, that they offered seven bullocks and seven rams. Henry notes God helped trembling Levites, encouraging them and silencing fears, and they offered sacrifices in atonement for the former error and acknowledgment of help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. David was clothed with a robe of fine linen, and all Levites that bare the ark, and singers, and Chenaniah master of song, and David had a linen ephod. Henry notes great expressions of rejoicing: sacred music, David dancing, singers singing, people shouting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. All Israel brought up the ark with shouting, sound of cornet, trumpets, cymbals, psalteries, and harps. Henry calls times of public reformation times of public rejoicing: those unworthy of the ark are not glad of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. As the ark came into David's city, Michal daughter of Saul looked out a window, saw David dancing and playing, and despised him in her heart. Henry notes her despising did not make David despicable, nor did the wise and good think worse of him for zealous devotion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
