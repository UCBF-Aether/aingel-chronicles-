The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Zechariah 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the angel that talked with me came again, and waked me, as a man that is wakened out of his sleep.

2 And said unto me, What seest thou? And I said, I have looked, and behold a candlestick all of gold, with a bowl upon the top of it, and his seven lamps thereon, and seven pipes to the seven lamps, which are upon the top thereof:

3 And two olive trees by it, one upon the right side of the bowl, and the other upon the left side thereof.

4 So I answered and spake to the angel that talked with me, saying, What are these, my lord?

5 Then the angel that talked with me answered and said unto me, Knowest thou not what these be? And I said, No, my lord.

6 Then he answered and spake unto me, saying, This is the word of the LORD unto Zerubbabel, saying, Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.

7 Who art thou, O great mountain? before Zerubbabel thou shalt become a plain: and he shall bring forth the headstone thereof with shoutings, crying, Grace, grace unto it.

8 Moreover the word of the LORD came unto me, saying,

9 The hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house; his hands shall also finish it; and thou shalt know that the LORD of hosts hath sent me unto you.

10 For who hath despised the day of small things? for they shall rejoice, and shall see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel with those seven; they are the eyes of the LORD, which run to and fro through the whole earth.

11 Then answered I, and said unto him, What are these two olive trees upon the right side of the candlestick and upon the left side thereof?

12 And I answered again, and said unto him, What be these two olive branches which through the two golden pipes empty the golden oil out of themselves?

13 And he answered me and said, Knowest thou not what these be? And I said, No, my lord.

14 Then said he, These are the two anointed ones, that stand by the LORD of the whole earth.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Woken as a man from sleep, Zechariah sees a candlestick all of gold with a bowl on top, seven lamps, seven pipes, and two olive trees beside it. The word to Zerubbabel is the chapter's centre: not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit, says the LORD of hosts, so the great mountain before Zerubbabel will become a plain and he will bring forth the headstone with shoutings of grace. Whoever despised the day of small things will rejoice to see the plummet in Zerubbabel's hand, and the two olive trees are the two anointed ones standing by the LORD of the whole earth. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And the angel that talked with me came again, and waked me, as a man that is wakened out of his sleep, because the fifth vision begins with a waking, as if the prophet had slept between visions and must be roused to see. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And said unto me, what seest thou, and I said, I have looked, and behold a candlestick all of gold, with a bowl upon the top of it, and his seven lamps thereon, and seven pipes to the seven lamps, which are upon the top thereof, because the golden candlestick, fed from a bowl by seven pipes, pictures the church supplied with oil without human tending. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And two olive trees by it, one upon the right side of the bowl, and the other upon the left side thereof, because the two olive trees stand beside the bowl, feeding it continually, as living sources of the oil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. So I answered and spoke to the angel that talked with me, saying, what are these, my lord, because the prophet asks the meaning of the whole apparatus, and the question opens the interpretation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Then the angel that talked with me answered and said unto me, knowest thou not what these be, and I said, no, my lord, because the prophet confesses ignorance, and the angel's question humbles him before the answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Then he answered and spoke unto me, saying, this is the word of the Lord unto Zerubbabel, saying, not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, says the Lord of hosts, because the vision's word to Zerubbabel is that the temple will be finished not by might or power, but by God's Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Who art thou, O great mountain, before Zerubbabel thou shalt become a plain, and he shall bring forth the headstone thereof with shoutings, crying, grace, grace unto it, because every mountain of opposition becomes a plain before Zerubbabel, and the headstone is brought forth with shouts of grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Moreover the word of the Lord came unto me, saying, because a second word follows, confirming the first with a personal pledge about the builder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house, his hands shall also finish it, and thou shalt know that the Lord of hosts has sent me unto you, because the same hands that laid the foundation will finish the house, and the finishing will prove the sending. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For who has despised the day of small things, for they shall rejoice, and shall see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel with those seven, they are the eyes of the Lord, which run to and fro through the whole earth, because the day of small things is not to be despised, and the seven eyes are the Lord's watchful providence running through the whole earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Then answered I, and said unto him, what are these two olive trees upon the right side of the candlestick and upon the left side thereof, because the prophet returns to the olive trees, asking their meaning apart from the candlestick. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And I answered again, and said unto him, what be these two olive branches which through the two golden pipes empty the golden oil out of themselves, because the question narrows to the branches and pipes, asking how the golden oil reaches the lamps. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. And he answered me and said, knowest thou not what these be, and I said, no, my lord, because again the prophet confesses he does not know, and the repeated question marks the mystery of the symbol. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Then said he, these are the two anointed ones, that stand by the Lord of the whole earth, because the two olive trees are the two anointed ones, Zerubbabel the governor and Joshua the priest, standing by the Lord of the whole earth, supplying the church through their offices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
