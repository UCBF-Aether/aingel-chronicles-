The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 33 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD said unto Moses, Depart, and go up hence, thou and the people which thou hast brought up out of the land of Egypt, unto the land which I sware unto Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, saying, Unto thy seed will I give it:

2 And I will send an angel before thee; and I will drive out the Canaanite, the Amorite, and the Hittite, and the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite:

3 Unto a land flowing with milk and honey: for I will not go up in the midst of thee; for thou art a stiffnecked people: lest I consume thee in the way.

4 And when the people heard these evil tidings, they mourned: and no man did put on him his ornaments.

5 For the LORD had said unto Moses, Say unto the children of Israel, Ye are a stiffnecked people: I will come up into the midst of thee in a moment, and consume thee: therefore now put off thy ornaments from thee, that I may know what to do unto thee.

6 And the children of Israel stripped themselves of their ornaments by the mount Horeb.

7 And Moses took the tabernacle, and pitched it without the camp, afar off from the camp, and called it the Tabernacle of the congregation. And it came to pass, that every one which sought the LORD went out unto the tabernacle of the congregation, which was without the camp.

8 And it came to pass, when Moses went out unto the tabernacle, that all the people rose up, and stood every man at his tent door, and looked after Moses, until he was gone into the tabernacle.

9 And it came to pass, as Moses entered into the tabernacle, the cloudy pillar descended, and stood at the door of the tabernacle, and the Lord talked with Moses.

10 And all the people saw the cloudy pillar stand at the tabernacle door: and all the people rose up and worshipped, every man in his tent door.

11 And the LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend. And he turned again into the camp: but his servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man, departed not out of the tabernacle.

12 And Moses said unto the LORD, See, thou sayest unto me, Bring up this people: and thou hast not let me know whom thou wilt send with me. Yet thou hast said, I know thee by name, and thou hast also found grace in my sight.

13 Now therefore, I pray thee, if I have found grace in thy sight, shew me now thy way, that I may know thee, that I may find grace in thy sight: and consider that this nation is thy people.

14 And he said, My presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest.

15 And he said unto him, If thy presence go not with me, carry us not up hence.

16 For wherein shall it be known here that I and thy people have found grace in thy sight? is it not in that thou goest with us? so shall we be separated, I and thy people, from all the people that are upon the face of the earth.

17 And the LORD said unto Moses, I will do this thing also that thou hast spoken: for thou hast found grace in my sight, and I know thee by name.

18 And he said, I beseech thee, shew me thy glory.

19 And he said, I will make all my goodness pass before thee, and I will proclaim the name of the LORD before thee; and will be gracious to whom I will be gracious, and will shew mercy on whom I will shew mercy.

20 And he said, Thou canst not see my face: for there shall no man see me, and live.

21 And the LORD said, Behold, there is a place by me, and thou shalt stand upon a rock:

22 And it shall come to pass, while my glory passeth by, that I will put thee in a clift of the rock, and will cover thee with my hand while I pass by:

23 And I will take away mine hand, and thou shalt see my back parts: but my face shall not be seen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After the calf, God tells Israel to go up to the land, but says his presence will not go with them lest he consume them on the way, and the people mourn and strip off their ornaments. Moses pitches his tent outside the camp, speaks with the LORD face to face as a man speaks to his friend, and pleads until God promises his presence will go with them and give them rest. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And the LORD said unto Moses, Depart, and go up hence, thou and the people which thou hast brought up out of the land of Egypt, unto the land which I sware unto Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, saying, Unto thy seed will I give it. Henry notes those whom God pardons must be made to know what their sin deserved, and though he promised to make good his covenant with Abraham in giving them Canaan, yet he denied them the tokens of his presence they had been blessed with. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And I will send an angel before thee, and I will drive out the Canaanite, the Amorite, and the Hittite, and the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite. Henry observes the angel would still go before them and the nations would still be driven out, for the covenant's outward promise stood, even while the presence was withdrawn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Unto a land flowing with milk and honey, for I will not go up in the midst of thee, for thou art a stiffnecked people, lest I consume thee in the way. Henry notes the sentence was very expressive of God's displeasure: Let them go forward as they are, with an angel, but without God himself in their midst. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And when the people heard these evil tidings, they mourned, and no man did put on him his ornaments. Henry observes the people mourned for their sin, and of all the bitter fruits of sin, true penitents most lament and dread most God's departure from them, for Canaan itself would be no pleasant land without the Lord's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. For the LORD had said unto Moses, Say unto the children of Israel, Ye are a stiffnecked people, and I will come up into the midst of thee in a moment, and consume thee, and therefore now put off thy ornaments from thee, that I may know what to do unto thee. Henry notes the command to put off ornaments matched the sin: those who parted with ornaments to maintain sin could do no less than lay aside ornaments in token of sorrow and shame for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And the children of Israel stripped themselves of their ornaments by the mount Horeb. Henry observes the stripping was done at Horeb itself, where the calf had been made, that the place of sin might become the place of repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And Moses took the tabernacle, and pitched it without the camp, afar off from the camp, and called it the Tabernacle of the congregation, and it came to pass, that every one which sought the LORD went out unto the tabernacle of the congregation, which was without the camp. Henry notes Moses took the tabernacle, a temporary building set up for worship and for judging disputes, and pitched it without the camp, for the camp polluted with idolatry could not hold God's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And it came to pass, when Moses went out unto the tabernacle, that all the people rose up, and stood every man at his tent door, and looked after Moses, until he was gone into the tabernacle. Henry observes the people looked after him, very desirous to be at peace with God, and concerned to know what would come to pass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And it came to pass, as Moses entered into the tabernacle, the cloudy pillar descended, and stood at the door of the tabernacle, and the LORD talked with Moses. Henry notes the cloudy pillar, which had withdrawn from the camp when it was polluted, now returned, for if our hearts go forth toward God to meet him, he will graciously come to meet us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And all the people saw the cloudy pillar stand at the tabernacle door, and all the people rose up and worshipped, every man in his tent door. Henry observes the whole camp worshipped at the sight: the pillar at the door was proof that God still talked with Moses, even after the calf. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And the LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend. And he turned again into the camp, but his servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man, departed not out of the tabernacle. Henry notes the friendship of the speech: face to face, as a man speaks to his friend, and Joshua's staying behind showed a young man who would not leave the place of God's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And Moses said unto the LORD, See, thou sayest unto me, Bring up this people, and thou hast not let me know whom thou wilt send with me. Yet thou hast said, I know thee by name, and thou hast also found grace in my sight. Henry notes Moses was very earnest with God, pleading the charge laid on him and the grace already given, for God's promises should not only encourage faith but excite fervency in prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Now therefore, I pray thee, if I have found grace in thy sight, show me now thy way, that I may know thee, that I may find grace in thy sight, and consider that this nation is thy people. Henry observes the plea ran both ways: show me thy way, that I may know thee, and remember that this nation is thy people, for Moses would not lead a people God had disowned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And he said, My presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest. Henry notes the answer restored what the sentence had withdrawn: God's presence would go, and rest would follow, for presence and rest come together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And he said unto him, If thy presence go not with me, carry us not up hence. Henry observes Moses spoke as one who dreaded the thought of going forward without the Lord's presence, for Canaan without God was no promise at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. For wherein shall it be known here that I and thy people have found grace in thy sight? Is it not in that thou goest with us? So shall we be separated, I and thy people, from all the people that are upon the face of the earth. Henry notes the argument: God's presence was the mark of grace and the seal of separation, for without it Israel was no different from any nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And the LORD said unto Moses, I will do this thing also that thou hast spoken, for thou hast found grace in my sight, and I know thee by name. Henry observes how he speeds: see, in a type, Christ's intercession, which he ever lives to make for all that come to God by him, and it is not by anything in those for whom he intercedes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And he said, I beseech thee, show me thy glory. Henry notes Moses, having won the presence, now entreated a sight of the glory itself, pressing from grace to glory in one prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And he said, I will make all my goodness pass before thee, and I will proclaim the name of the LORD before thee, and will be gracious to whom I will be gracious, and will show mercy on whom I will show mercy. Henry observes the Lord granted what would abundantly satisfy: God's goodness is his glory, and he will have us know him by the glory of his mercy more than by the glory of his majesty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And he said, Thou canst not see my face, for there shall no man see me, and live. Henry notes a full discovery of God's glory would overwhelm even Moses himself, for man is mean and unworthy of it, weak and could not bear it, guilty and could not but dread it, and the merciful display made in Christ Jesus alone can be borne by us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And the LORD said, Behold, there is a place by me, and thou shalt stand upon a rock. Henry observes upon the rock there was a fit place for Moses to view God's goodness and glory, and the rock in Horeb was typical of Christ the Rock, the Rock of refuge, salvation, and strength, and happy are they who stand upon this Rock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And it shall come to pass, while my glory passeth by, that I will put thee in a clift of the rock, and will cover thee with my hand while I pass by. Henry notes the cleft may be an emblem of Christ, as smitten, crucified, wounded, and slain, for Moses was hidden in the Rock while glory passed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And I will take away mine hand, and thou shalt see my back parts, but my face shall not be seen. Henry closes on the imperfect knowledge of God in the present state, even as revealed in Christ, for this, when compared with the heavenly sight of him, is but like seeing a man that is gone by, whose back only is to be seen, and the fullest displays of his glory, grace, and goodness are reserved to another state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms
