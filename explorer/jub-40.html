The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 40 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in those days Pharaoh dreamed two dreams in one night concerning a famine which was to be in all the land, and he awoke from his sleep and called all the interpreters of dreams that were in Egypt, and magicians, and told them his two dreams,

2 and they were not able to declare (them).

3 And then the chief butler remembered Joseph and spake of him to the king, and he brought him forth from the prison, and he told his two dreams before him.

4 And he said before Pharaoh that his two dreams were one, and he said unto him : " Seven years will come (in which there will be) plenty over all the land of Egypt, and after that seven years of famine, such a famine as hath not been in all the land.

5 And now let Pharaoh appoint overseers in all the land of Egypt, and let them store up food in every city throughout the days of the years of plenty, and there will be food for the seven years of famine, and the land will not perish through the famine, for it will be very severe." 5.

6 And the Lord gave Joseph favour and mercy in the eyes of Pharaoh, and Pharaoh said unto his servants : " We shall not find such a wise and discreet man as this man, for the spirit of the Lord is with him." 6.

7 And he appointed him the second in all his kingdom and gave him authority over all Egypt, and caused him to ride in the second chariot of Pharaoh.

8 And he clothed him with byssus garments, and he put a gold chain upon his neck, and (a herald) proclaimed before him " 'El 'El wa 'Abirer," and he placed a ring on his hand and made him ruler over all his house, and magnified him, and said unto him : " Only on the throne shall I be greater than thou." 8.

9 And Joseph ruled over all the land of Egypt, and all the princes of Pharaoh, and all his servants, and all who did the king's business loved him, for he walked in uprightness, for he was without pride and arrogance, and he had no respect of persons, and did not accept gifts, but he judged in uprightness all the people of the land 9.

10 And the

11 land of Egypt was at peace before Pharaoh because of Joseph, for the Lord was with him, and gave him favour and mercy for all his generations before all those who knew him and those who heard concerning him, and Pharaoh's kingdom was well ordered, and there was no Satan and no evil person (therein).

12 And the king called Joseph's name Sephantiphans and gave Joseph to wife the daughter of Potiphar, the daughter of the priest of Heliopolis, the chief cook.

13 And on the day that Joseph stood before Pharaoh he was thirty years old [when he stood before Pharaoh].

14 And in that year Isaac died.

15 And it came to pass as Joseph had said in the interpretation of his two dreams, according as he had said it, there were seven years of plenty over all the land of Egypt, and the land of Egypt produced abundantly, one measure (producing) eighteen hundred measures.

16 And Joseph gathered food into every city until they were full of corn until they could no longer count and measure it for its multitude.

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In those days Pharaoh dreamed two dreams in one night concerning a famine to come in all the land, awoke, and called all Egypt's interpreters and magicians to tell them his dreams. Henry notes that the dreams showed they were sent of God, since Pharaoh awoke with his spirit troubled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The interpreters and magicians were not able to declare Pharaoh's dreams. Human wisdom failed where God's meaning was hidden, preparing the way for the prisoner who gave honour to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Then the chief butler remembered Joseph, spoke of him to the king, and Joseph was brought forth from prison and told the two dreams before Pharaoh. Henry notes that God's time for the enlargement of his people is the fittest time, and that if the butler had freed Joseph earlier, he would likely have gone back to the Hebrews and never become the blessing to his family that he proved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Joseph said before Pharaoh that the two dreams were one: seven years of plenty would come over all Egypt, and after them seven years of famine such as had not been in the land. Henry stresses the goodness of God in sending the plenty before the famine, that provision might be made, and warns that we cannot be sure tomorrow shall be as today. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Joseph counselled Pharaoh to appoint overseers in all Egypt to store up food in every city through the years of plenty, so that there would be food for the seven years of famine and the land would not perish, for the famine would be very severe. Henry notes that fair warning should always be followed by good counsel, and that we must provide accordingly for the day of trial before us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The Lord gave Joseph favour and mercy in Pharaoh's eyes, and Pharaoh said to his servants that no man so wise and discreet could be found, for the spirit of the Lord was with him. Henry records Pharaoh's honourable testimony: he is a man in whom the spirit of God is, and such men ought to be valued. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Pharaoh appointed Joseph second in all his kingdom, gave him authority over all Egypt, and caused him to ride in Pharaoh's second chariot. The preferment encourages all to trust in God, Henry notes, since the prisoner of yesterday becomes the ruler of today. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Pharaoh clothed Joseph with byssus garments, put a gold chain on his neck, had a herald proclaim before him, placed a ring on his hand, made him ruler over all his house, and said only on the throne would he be greater. Henry notes the marks of honour Pharaoh put upon Joseph, including a new name speaking the value he had for him, a revealer of secrets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Joseph ruled over all Egypt, and all Pharaoh's princes, servants, and king's business loved him, for he walked in uprightness, without pride or arrogance, with no respect of persons, accepting no gifts, and judging all the people in uprightness. The character of the ruler matches the wisdom of the interpreter: the same uprightness governed both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The land of Egypt was at peace before Pharaoh because of Joseph, the sentence beginning here and completed in the next verse. Peace under Joseph's rule was the fruit of the stored plenty and the just judgments that distributed it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Egypt was at peace because the Lord was with Joseph, giving him favour and mercy for all his generations before all who knew him and heard of him, and Pharaoh's kingdom was well ordered, with no Satan and no evil person therein. Henry's account of the plenty years agrees: Joseph was diligent in laying up while plenty lasted, and prudent in giving out when famine came. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The king called Joseph's name Sephantiphans and gave him as wife the daughter of Potiphar, priest of Heliopolis, the chief cook. Henry notes the new name given by Pharaoh, Zaphnath-paaneah, a revealer of secrets, and some translate it saviour of the world, a title Henry applies in its highest sense to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. On the day Joseph stood before Pharaoh he was thirty years old. Thirteen years had passed since he was sold at seventeen, and Henry's remark stands: God's time for enlargement is the fittest time, neither early nor late. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. In that year Isaac died, the same year Joseph stood before Pharaoh. The death of the patriarch in Canaan and the exaltation of his grandson in Egypt fall together, closing one generation's story as another's opens. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. As Joseph had said in interpreting his two dreams, there were seven years of plenty over all Egypt, and the land produced abundantly, one measure producing eighteen hundred measures. Henry notes that the seven plenteous years came and were ended, and that we must not be secure in prosperity nor slothful in making good use of opportunity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Joseph gathered food into every city until they were full of corn beyond counting and measuring for its multitude. Henry observes that Joseph was diligent in laying up while the plenty lasted, and that the great harvests of plenty, though they seemed very much, would only just serve to keep the people alive through famine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
