The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 25 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then answered Bildad the Shuhite, and said,

2 Dominion and fear are with him, he maketh peace in his high places.

3 Is there any number of his armies? and upon whom doth not his light arise?

4 How then can man be justified with God? or how can he be clean that is born of a woman?

5 Behold even to the moon, and it shineth not; yea, the stars are not pure in his sight.

6 How much less man, that is a worm? and the son of man, which is a worm?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Bildad answers briefly, the friends’ shortest speech: dominion and fear are with God, who makes peace in his high places, and his armies cannot be numbered. Before such majesty, how can man be justified with God, or he that is born of a woman be clean? Even the moon has no brightness and the stars are not pure in his sight — how much less man, that is a worm. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Bildad the Shuhite answers briefly, trying to show Job's rashness in wishing to come before God by arguments borrowed from Eliphaz. The short speech is the friends' last attempt before Job's long reply. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Dominion and fear are with God, who makes peace in his high places. JFB explains dominion and fear as terror-inspiring power, with peace in high places implying power quelling all opposition on high and on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Is there any number of God's armies, and upon whom does not his light arise? JFB explains armies as angels and stars, countless, with his light rising upon all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. How then can man be justified with God, or he born of a woman be clean? JFB refers back to Eliphaz's revelation and Job's own words on born of a woman: the question is the friends' standing challenge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Behold, even to the moon it shines not, and the stars are not pure in God's sight. JFB notes the moon answers to the heavens in Eliphaz's version, and stars to saints or angels: even the most dazzling objects, and angels of whom stars are emblems, are imperfect before the Creator, whose light and purity are not creaturely. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. How much less man, that is a worm, and the son of man, which is a worm? JFB notes two distinct Hebrew words: the first a worm bred in putridity, alluding to corruption, the second a crawling worm, implying weakness and grovelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
