The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Sirach 30 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 He that loveth his son causeth him oft to feel the rod, that he may have joy of him in the end.

2 He that chastiseth his son shall have joy in him, and shall rejoice of him among his acquaintance.

3 He that teacheth his son grieveth the enemy: and before his friends he shall rejoice of him.

4 Though his father die, yet he is as though he were not dead: for he hath left one behind him that is like himself.

5 While he lived, he saw and rejoiced in him: and when he died, he was not sorrowful.

6 He left behind him an avenger against his enemies, and one that shall requite kindness to his friends.

7 He that maketh too much of his son shall bind up his wounds; and his bowels will be troubled at every cry.

8 An horse not broken becometh headstrong: and a child left to himself will be wilful.

9 Cocker thy child, and he shall make thee afraid: play with him, and he will bring thee to heaviness.

10 Laugh not with him, lest thou have sorrow with him, and lest thou gnash thy teeth in the end.

11 Give him no liberty in his youth, and wink not at his follies.

12 Bow down his neck while he is young, and beat him on the sides while he is a child, lest he wax stubborn, and be disobedient unto thee, and so bring sorrow to thine heart.

13 Chastise thy son, and hold him to labour, lest his lewd behaviour be an offence unto thee.

14 Better is the poor, being sound and strong of constitution, than a rich man that is afflicted in his body.

15 Health and good estate of body are above all gold, and a strong body above infinite wealth.

16 There is no riches above a sound body, and no joy above the joy of the heart.

17 Death is better than a bitter life or continual sickness.

18 Delicates poured upon a mouth shut up are as messes of meat set upon a grave.

19 What good doeth the offering unto an idol? for neither can it eat nor smell: so is he that is persecuted of the Lord.

20 He seeth with his eyes and groaneth, as an eunuch that embraceth a virgin and sigheth.

21 Give not over thy mind to heaviness, and afflict not thyself in thine own counsel.

22 The gladness of the heart is the life of man, and the joyfulness of a man prolongeth his days.

23 Love thine own soul, and comfort thy heart, remove sorrow far from thee: for sorrow hath killed many, and there is no profit therein.

24 Envy and wrath shorten the life, and carefulness bringeth age before the time.

25 A cheerful and good heart will have a care of his meat and diet.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A father who loves his son causes him often to feel the rod, that he may have joy of him in the end, joining chastisement to labour lest lewd behaviour become an offence. The chapter closes more gently: a cheerful and good heart will have a care even of meat and diet. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

  • Verse 11. Give him no liberty in his youth, and do not wink at his follies. Youth should be given no loose liberty, and follies must not be winked at. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

  • Verse 14. Better is the poor, being sound and strong of constitution, than a rich man afflicted in his body. Sound, strong poverty is better than bodily afflicted riches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

  • Verse 16. There is no riches above a sound body, and no joy above the joy of the heart. No riches exceed a sound body, and no joy exceeds heart-joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. Death is better than a bitter life or continual sickness. Death is preferable to a bitter life or unending sickness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. Delicates poured upon a mouth shut up are as messes of meat set upon a grave. Delicacies for a shut mouth are like meat set on a grave: useless to the dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. What good does the offering to an idol do? For neither can it eat nor smell; so is he who is persecuted of the Lord. An idol's offering is useless, since it cannot eat or smell, and so is the man persecuted by God, unable to enjoy what is set before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 20. He sees with his eyes and groans, as a eunuch embracing a virgin and sighing. He sees and groans, like a eunuch embracing a virgin and sighing, desiring what he cannot enjoy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 21. Do not give over your mind to heaviness, and do not afflict yourself in your own counsel. The mind must not be surrendered to heaviness, nor the self afflicted by its own counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 22. The gladness of the heart is the life of man, and the joyfulness of a man prolongs his days. Heart-gladness is man's life, and joyfulness lengthens days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 23. Love your own soul, comfort your heart, remove sorrow far from you, for sorrow has killed many, and there is no profit therein. Love of one's own soul, comforted heart, and sorrow removed are urged, since sorrow has killed many and profits nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 24. Envy and wrath shorten life, and carefulness brings age before the time. Envy and wrath shorten life, and anxious care ages a person prematurely. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 25. A cheerful and good heart will have a care of his meat and diet. A cheerful, good heart takes care even of meat and diet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
