The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — John 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now before the feast of the passover, when Jesus knew that his hour was come that he should depart out of this world unto the Father, having loved his own which were in the world, he loved them unto the end.

2 And supper being ended, the devil having now put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon’s son, to betray him;

3 Jesus knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he was come from God, and went to God;

4 He riseth from supper, and laid aside his garments; and took a towel, and girded himself.

5 After that he poureth water into a bason, and began to wash the disciples’ feet, and to wipe them with the towel wherewith he was girded.

6 Then cometh he to Simon Peter: and Peter saith unto him, Lord, dost thou wash my feet?

7 Jesus answered and said unto him, What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter.

8 Peter saith unto him, Thou shalt never wash my feet. Jesus answered him, If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me.

9 Simon Peter saith unto him, Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head.

10 Jesus saith to him, He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit: and ye are clean, but not all.

11 For he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean.

12 So after he had washed their feet, and had taken his garments, and was set down again, he said unto them, Know ye what I have done to you?

13 Ye call me Master and Lord: and ye say well; for so I am.

14 If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another’s feet.

15 For I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you.

16 Verily, verily, I say unto you, The servant is not greater than his lord; neither he that is sent greater than he that sent him.

17 If ye know these things, happy are ye if ye do them.

18 I speak not of you all: I know whom I have chosen: but that the scripture may be fulfilled, He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me.

19 Now I tell you before it come, that, when it is come to pass, ye may believe that I am he.

20 Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that receiveth whomsoever I send receiveth me; and he that receiveth me receiveth him that sent me.

21 When Jesus had thus said, he was troubled in spirit, and testified, and said, Verily, verily, I say unto you, that one of you shall betray me.

22 Then the disciples looked one on another, doubting of whom he spake.

23 Now there was leaning on Jesus’ bosom one of his disciples, whom Jesus loved.

24 Simon Peter therefore beckoned to him, that he should ask who it should be of whom he spake.

25 He then lying on Jesus’ breast saith unto him, Lord, who is it?

26 Jesus answered, He it is, to whom I shall give a sop, when I have dipped it. And when he had dipped the sop, he gave it to Judas Iscariot, the son of Simon.

27 And after the sop Satan entered into him. Then said Jesus unto him, That thou doest, do quickly.

28 Now no man at the table knew for what intent he spake this unto him.

29 For some of them thought, because Judas had the bag, that Jesus had said unto him, Buy those things that we have need of against the feast; or, that he should give something to the poor.

30 He then having received the sop went immediately out: and it was night.

31 Therefore, when he was gone out, Jesus said, Now is the Son of man glorified, and God is glorified in him.

32 If God be glorified in him, God shall also glorify him in himself, and shall straightway glorify him.

33 Little children, yet a little while I am with you. Ye shall seek me: and as I said unto the Jews, Whither I go, ye cannot come; so now I say to you.

34 A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another.

35 By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another.

36 Simon Peter said unto him, Lord, whither goest thou? Jesus answered him, Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now; but thou shalt follow me afterwards.

37 Peter said unto him, Lord, why cannot I follow thee now? I will lay down my life for thy sake.

38 Jesus answered him, Wilt thou lay down thy life for my sake? Verily, verily, I say unto thee, The cock shall not crow, till thou hast denied me thrice.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Before the Passover feast, knowing his hour has come, Jesus rises from supper, girds himself with a towel, and washes the disciples’ feet, including Peter, who first refuses and then asks for hands and head as well (vv. 1–17). He foretells betrayal — “he that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me” — gives the sop to Judas, who goes out into the night, and then gives the new commandment: love one another as I have loved you (vv. 18–35). Peter’s boast that he will lay down his life is answered with the prediction that the cock will not crow before Peter denies him three times (vv. 36–38). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, John 13

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry: his love for his own does not wear out at the hour of departure — the end of his earthly course is where that love is shown to hold, unseparated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And supper being ended, the devil having now put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him, a sin so exceedingly sinful, with so little temptation from the world and flesh, that it was plainly directly from Satan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Jesus, knowing the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he was come from God, and went to God, acted from the full knowledge of his dignity, not in spite of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. He rose from supper, laid aside his garments, took a towel, and girded himself, laying aside everything that would hinder the service he would do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. After that he poured water into a basin, and began to wash the disciples' feet, and to wipe them with the towel wherewith he was girded, teaching us to think nothing below us wherein we may promote God's glory and our brethren's good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Then he came to Simon Peter, and Peter said, Lord, dost thou wash my feet, startled at the Master's lowliness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Jesus answered, What I do thou knowest not now, but thou shalt know hereafter, for our Lord does many things whose meaning his disciples do not for the present know, but shall know afterward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Peter said, Thou shalt never wash my feet, and Jesus answered, If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me, for it is not humility but unbelief to put away the offers of the gospel as too good to be true, and only those spiritually washed by Christ have a part in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Simon Peter said, Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head, for he who truly desires sanctification desires it throughout, the whole man made pure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jesus said, He who is washed needs not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit, and you are clean, but not all, for the justified believer's daily care is to wash his feet, cleansing daily guilt and watching against defilement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. For he knew who should betray him, therefore he said, You are not all clean, and when hypocrites are discovered, it should be no surprise or stumbling to us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Henry: the question opens the lesson — he asks what he has done so they will observe, not merely receive, the example he has set. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Henry: they call him Master and Lord, and say well — the titles are owned, not flattered, and his example as Master and Lord is what authorises the command that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet, for duties are mutual, and we must both accept help from brethren and afford it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. For I have given you an example, that you should do as I have done to you, and when we see our Master serving, we see how ill it becomes us to domineer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Verily, verily, the servant is not greater than his lord, neither he who is sent greater than he who sent him, so no disciple may refuse lowly service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. If you know these things, happy are you if you do them, for knowledge without practice brings no blessedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. I speak not of you all, he said; I know whom I have chosen, but that the Scripture may be fulfilled, He that eats bread with me has lifted up his heel against me, for the prophecy of treachery may apply to all who meet God's mercies with ingratitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Now I tell you before it comes, that when it comes to pass, you may believe that I am he, for foreknowledge confirms faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Verily, verily, he who receives whomsoever I send receives me, and he who receives me receives him who sent me, so the apostles' mission carries Christ's own authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. When Jesus had thus said, he was troubled in spirit, and testified, and said, Verily, verily, one of you shall betray me, for our Lord had often spoken of his sufferings without such trouble as he now showed at Judas's treachery, and the sins of Christians are the grief of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Then the disciples looked one on another, doubting of whom he spoke, each fearing himself rather than accusing another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Henry: one disciple leans on Jesus' bosom — the place of nearness given to the disciple Jesus loved, the same nearness Peter will use to ask his question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Simon Peter therefore beckoned to him, that he should ask who it should be of whom he spoke, seeking the answer through the beloved disciple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. He then, lying on Jesus' breast, said, Lord, who is it, asking in the confidence of nearness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The dipped sop identifies the traitor among those who shared his bread; Henry warns that the prophecy reaches beyond Judas to all who partake of God's mercies and meet them with ingratitude, lifting up the heel against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And after the sop Satan entered into him, and then Jesus said to him, That thou doest, do quickly, for those whose deeds are evil love darkness rather than light, and Judas went out as one weary of Jesus and his apostles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Now no man at the table knew for what intent he spoke this unto him, for the sign was understood by none but John. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For some thought, because Judas had the bag, that Jesus had said to him, Buy those things we have need of against the feast, or that he should give something to the poor, a natural misreading of an ordinary errand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Henry: Judas goes out weary of Jesus and his company, into the night — evil deeds choose darkness over light, and the hour fits the deed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Therefore, when he was gone out, Jesus said, Now is the Son of man glorified, and God is glorified in him, for Christ had been glorified in many miracles, yet he spoke of his sufferings as a greater glory, since satisfaction was thereby made for the wrong done to God by man's sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. If God be glorified in him, God shall also glorify him in himself, and shall straightway glorify him, for the cross and the crown belong together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Little children, yet a little while I am with you, he said; you shall seek me, and as I said to the Jews, Whither I go, ye cannot come, so now I say to you, for we cannot yet follow him to heavenly happiness, but if we believe, we shall follow hereafter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. A new commandment I give unto you, that you love one another, as I have loved you, that you also love one another, for they were to love for Christ's sake and according to his example, seeking others' benefit as one body animated by one soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. By this shall all men know you are my disciples, if you have love one to another, for if followers of Christ do not show love, they give cause to suspect their sincerity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Simon Peter said, Lord, whither goest thou, and Jesus answered, Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now, but thou shalt follow me afterwards, for Peter overlooked brotherly love to ask about secret things that belong to God only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Peter said, Lord, why cannot I follow thee now, I will lay down my life for thy sake, eager to know and to promise more than he could perform. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Jesus answered, Wilt thou lay down thy life for my sake, verily, verily, I say unto thee, The cock shall not crow, till thou hast denied me thrice, for Christ knows us better than we know ourselves, and will hide pride from those he loves by discovering them to themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
