The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Peter 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Likewise, ye wives, be in subjection to your own husbands; that, if any obey not the word, they also may without the word be won by the conversation of the wives;

2 While they behold your chaste conversation coupled with fear.

3 Whose adorning let it not be that outward adorning of plaiting the hair, and of wearing of gold, or of putting on of apparel;

4 But let it be the hidden man of the heart, in that which is not corruptible, even the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price.

5 For after this manner in the old time the holy women also, who trusted in God, adorned themselves, being in subjection unto their own husbands:

6 Even as Sara obeyed Abraham, calling him lord: whose daughters ye are, as long as ye do well, and are not afraid with any amazement.

7 Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.

8 Finally, be ye all of one mind, having compassion one of another, love as brethren, be pitiful, be courteous:

9 Not rendering evil for evil, or railing for railing: but contrariwise blessing; knowing that ye are thereunto called, that ye should inherit a blessing.

10 For he that will love life, and see good days, let him refrain his tongue from evil, and his lips that they speak no guile:

11 Let him eschew evil, and do good; let him seek peace, and ensue it.

12 For the eyes of the Lord are over the righteous, and his ears are open unto their prayers: but the face of the Lord is against them that do evil.

13 And who is he that will harm you, if ye be followers of that which is good?

14 But and if ye suffer for righteousness’ sake, happy are ye: and be not afraid of their terror, neither be troubled;

15 But sanctify the Lord God in your hearts: and be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you with meekness and fear:

16 Having a good conscience; that, whereas they speak evil of you, as of evildoers, they may be ashamed that falsely accuse your good conversation in Christ.

17 For it is better, if the will of God be so, that ye suffer for well doing, than for evil doing.

18 For Christ also hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God, being put to death in the flesh, but quickened by the Spirit:

19 By which also he went and preached unto the spirits in prison;

20 Which sometime were disobedient, when once the longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing, wherein few, that is, eight souls were saved by water.

21 The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us (not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God,) by the resurrection of Jesus Christ:

22 Who is gone into heaven, and is on the right hand of God; angels and authorities and powers being made subject unto him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Wives and husbands are addressed in turn, the chapter valuing gentle, quiet character over outward adornment and calling husbands to honour their wives, before a general call to unity, compassion, blessing rather than retaliation, and a ready, gentle defence of the hope within. Christ suffered once for sins, the just for the unjust, and the chapter connects his proclamation to the spirits in prison with Noah's days, baptism's saving figure, and Christ now at God's right hand with all powers subject to him. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Likewise, wives are to be in subjection to their own husbands, so that, if any do not obey the word, they may without the word be won by the wives' conduct. Henry notes how narrowly evil people watch the ways of professors of religion, and a godly life may persuade where argument cannot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Husbands may be won while beholding a chaste conversation coupled with fear, that is, conduct governed by reverence for God rather than dread of man. Religious people should take care that all their behaviour answers to their profession, since conduct is watched more closely than words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Adorning is not to be the outward kind, plaiting of hair, wearing of gold, or putting on of apparel. Henry clarifies that apparel itself is not forbidden, but vanity and costliness in ornament are, and few know the right measure of food and clothing without desiring something beyond what is good for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The true ornament is the hidden person of the heart, in what is incorruptible, even a meek and quiet spirit, which in God's sight is of great price. Henry directs Christian women to put on the graces of the Holy Spirit, since right ordering of one's own spirit fixes affection and excites esteem more than studied ornaments with a froward temper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. After this manner in old time the holy women who trusted in God adorned themselves, being in subjection to their own husbands. Their example shows that godly subjection and trust in God, not display, were the ancient pattern of beauty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord, and Christian wives are her daughters as long as they do well and are not afraid with any amazement. Henry stresses subjection from desire to do well and please God, not from dread and amazement, and that doing duty from a willing mind in obedience to God is the whole point. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Likewise, husbands are to dwell with their wives according to knowledge, giving honour to the wife as the weaker vessel and as heirs together of the grace of life, so that prayers are not hindered. The husband's duty implies respect, protection, and trust, living peaceably together, since husband and wife should pray together by themselves and with their children, and holiness and prayer feed each other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Finally, be all of one mind, having compassion one toward another, loving as brethren, pitiful and courteous. Though Christians cannot always be exactly of the same mind, they should have compassion and love as brethren, and this agreement in affection is the mark of the community. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Do not render evil for evil or railing for railing, but contrariwise blessing, knowing you are called to this, that you should inherit a blessing. Returning blessing for insult is not weakness but the calling itself, and the blessing inherited answers the blessing given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Whoever would love life and see good days must refrain the tongue from evil and lips from speaking guile. Henry's rule for a comfortable life on earth and eternal life in heaven begins with bridling the tongue from wicked, abusive, or deceitful words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Let such a person eschew evil and do good, seeking peace and pursuing it. Forsaking evil actions is not enough: positive good must be done, and peace must be sought with all people, not merely waited for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. For the eyes of the Lord are over the righteous and his ears open to their prayers, but the face of the Lord is against those who do evil. God, all-wise and everywhere present, watches over the righteous and takes care of them, and that watchfulness is the ground of the previous exhortations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Who will harm you if you are followers of what is good? None could or should harm those who copy the example of Christ, who is perfect goodness, and do good to others as his followers, though harm may still come in this world, it cannot touch the blessing promised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Even if you suffer for righteousness' sake, happy are you, and do not be afraid of their terror, nor troubled. Where the great fear of God is, there is no room for other fears, and it disturbs not, since reverence for God steadies the heart against human threats. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Sanctify the Lord God in your hearts, and be ready always to give an answer to everyone who asks a reason for the hope in you, with meekness and fear. We sanctify God before others when our conduct invites them to glorify him, and we should be able to defend our religion with meekness, in the fear of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Keep a good conscience, so that those who speak evil of you as evildoers may be ashamed, falsely accusing your good conversation in Christ. The conscience is good when it does its office well, and a life that contradicts slander is itself the answer to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. It is better, if God's will be so, to suffer for well-doing than for evil-doing. A person on whom sin and suffering meet is in a sad condition, since sin makes suffering extreme and comfortless, while suffering for good has a clear conscience behind it, whatever natural impatience suggests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. For Christ also once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God, being put to death in the flesh but quickened by the Spirit. The blessed end and design of his sufferings was to reconcile us to God and bring us to eternal glory, and his example is the argument for patience under suffering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. By the Spirit Christ also went and preached to the spirits in prison. Henry reads this with the next verse: as to the old world, Christ sent his Spirit and gave warning by Noah, and God's patience waited long, so the preaching belongs to the days of Noah's warning, not to a later mission after death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Those spirits were disobedient when God's longsuffering waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was being prepared, in which few, eight souls, were saved by water. Though God's patience waits long, it will cease at last, and the spirits of disobedient sinners, once out of their bodies, are committed to prison, from which there is no redemption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The like figure, baptism, now also saves us, not the putting away of the filth of the flesh but the answer of a good conscience toward God, by the resurrection of Jesus Christ. Henry warns against resting on outward forms: Noah's salvation in the ark on the water was a type of believers' eternal salvation by baptism of the Holy Spirit, meaning regeneration, repentance, faith, and a new life professed uprightly before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Christ has gone into heaven and is at the right hand of God, angels, authorities, and powers being made subject to him. His resurrection is what assures believers of purifying and peace, and his exaltation over every power is the ground of their confidence under suffering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • spirits in prison. a difficult passage: Henry reads it with the next verse, as Christ’s Spirit preaching through Noah’s warning to the old world, not as a later mission after death (verses 19–20) Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
