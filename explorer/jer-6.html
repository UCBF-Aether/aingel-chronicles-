The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 O ye children of Benjamin, gather yourselves to flee out of the midst of Jerusalem, and blow the trumpet in Tekoa, and set up a sign of fire in Bethhaccerem: for evil appeareth out of the north, and great destruction.

2 I have likened the daughter of Zion to a comely and delicate woman.

3 The shepherds with their flocks shall come unto her; they shall pitch their tents against her round about; they shall feed every one in his place.

4 Prepare ye war against her; arise, and let us go up at noon. Woe unto us! for the day goeth away, for the shadows of the evening are stretched out.

5 Arise, and let us go by night, and let us destroy her palaces.

6 For thus hath the LORD of hosts said, Hew ye down trees, and cast a mount against Jerusalem: this is the city to be visited; she is wholly oppression in the midst of her.

7 As a fountain casteth out her waters, so she casteth out her wickedness: violence and spoil is heard in her; before me continually is grief and wounds.

8 Be thou instructed, O Jerusalem, lest my soul depart from thee; lest I make thee desolate, a land not inhabited.

9 Thus saith the LORD of hosts, They shall throughly glean the remnant of Israel as a vine: turn back thine hand as a grapegatherer into the baskets.

10 To whom shall I speak, and give warning, that they may hear? behold, their ear is uncircumcised, and they cannot hearken: behold, the word of the LORD is unto them a reproach; they have no delight in it.

11 Therefore I am full of the fury of the LORD; I am weary with holding in: I will pour it out upon the children abroad, and upon the assembly of young men together: for even the husband with the wife shall be taken, the aged with him that is full of days.

12 And their houses shall be turned unto others, with their fields and wives together: for I will stretch out my hand upon the inhabitants of the land, saith the LORD.

13 For from the least of them even unto the greatest of them every one is given to covetousness; and from the prophet even unto the priest every one dealeth falsely.

14 They have healed also the hurt of the daughter of my people slightly, saying, Peace, peace; when there is no peace.

15 Were they ashamed when they had committed abomination? nay, they were not at all ashamed, neither could they blush: therefore they shall fall among them that fall: at the time that I visit them they shall be cast down, saith the LORD.

16 Thus saith the LORD, Stand ye in the ways, and see, and ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein, and ye shall find rest for your souls. But they said, We will not walk therein.

17 Also I set watchmen over you, saying, Hearken to the sound of the trumpet. But they said, We will not hearken.

18 Therefore hear, ye nations, and know, O congregation, what is among them.

19 Hear, O earth: behold, I will bring evil upon this people, even the fruit of their thoughts, because they have not hearkened unto my words, nor to my law, but rejected it.

20 To what purpose cometh there to me incense from Sheba, and the sweet cane from a far country? your burnt offerings are not acceptable, nor your sacrifices sweet unto me.

21 Therefore thus saith the LORD, Behold, I will lay stumblingblocks before this people, and the fathers and the sons together shall fall upon them; the neighbour and his friend shall perish.

22 Thus saith the LORD, Behold, a people cometh from the north country, and a great nation shall be raised from the sides of the earth.

23 They shall lay hold on bow and spear; they are cruel, and have no mercy; their voice roareth like the sea; and they ride upon horses, set in array as men for war against thee, O daughter of Zion.

24 We have heard the fame thereof: our hands wax feeble: anguish hath taken hold of us, and pain, as of a woman in travail.

25 Go not forth into the field, nor walk by the way; for the sword of the enemy and fear is on every side.

26 O daughter of my people, gird thee with sackcloth, and wallow thyself in ashes: make thee mourning, as for an only son, most bitter lamentation: for the spoiler shall suddenly come upon us.

27 I have set thee for a tower and a fortress among my people, that thou mayest know and try their way.

28 They are all grievous revolters, walking with slanders: they are brass and iron; they are all corrupters.

29 The bellows are burned, the lead is consumed of the fire; the founder melteth in vain: for the wicked are not plucked away.

30 Reprobate silver shall men call them, because the LORD hath rejected them.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This is a siege warning for Jerusalem and its surrounding towns. Benjamin is told to flee, the trumpet is blown at Tekoa, and the invader from the north is described as setting up siege works against a city whose leaders will not listen. The chapter ends by naming the people reprobate silver, because the Lord has rejected them. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. O children of Benjamin, gather to flee out of Jerusalem, blow the trumpet in Tekoa, and set up a sign of fire in Bethhaccerem, for evil appears out of the north and great destruction. Henry warns it is vain to contend with God's judgments: the Chaldean army shall break in upon Judah and in a little time devour all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The daughter of Zion is likened to a comely and delicate woman. Henry's section makes the delicacy the point: the more Judah indulges in the pleasures of this life, the more she unfits herself for its troubles, and the day is coming when the careless and secure in sinful ways will be visited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Shepherds with their flocks shall come against her, pitching tents round about and feeding each in his place. Henry notes the invaders are eager, not to fulfil God's counsels but to fill their own treasures, yet God thereby serves his own purposes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Prepare war against her; arise, let us go up at noon, though woe follows, for the day goes away and evening shadows stretch out. Henry calls it folly to trifle when eternal salvation must be worked out and its enemies fought: the noon assault leaves no time to trifle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Arise, let us go by night and destroy her palaces. Henry's warning stands: the siege presses day and night, and those secure in sinful ways will be visited without respite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Hew down trees and cast a mount against Jerusalem, for this is the city to be visited, wholly oppression in the midst of her. Henry notes the siege works are God's visitation upon a city whose whole midst is oppression. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. As a fountain casts out her waters, so she casts out her wickedness, with violence and spoil heard in her and grief and wounds continually before God. Henry compares the corrupt heart in its natural state to such a fountain: always flowing, yet always full of evil thoughts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Be thou instructed, O Jerusalem, lest God's soul depart from thee and he make thee desolate, a land not inhabited. Henry adds that the God of mercy is loth to depart even from a provoking people, and is earnest with them that repentance and reformation may prevent things from coming to extremity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry's next section warns that when the Lord arises to take vengeance, no sinners of any age or rank, or of either sex, escape the gleaning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. To whom shall Jeremiah speak and give warning that they may hear? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Go not forth into the field, nor walk by the way, for the sword of the enemy and fear is on every side. Henry notes the prey is surrounded: the sins of God's professing people leave them nowhere to walk safely. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. O daughter of God's people, gird with sackcloth and wallow in ashes, making mourning as for an only son, most bitter lamentation, for the spoiler shall suddenly come upon us. Henry's section calls for mourning proportioned to the judgment: sudden spoiling deserves the bitterness of losing an only son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Jeremiah is set for a tower and a fortress among God's people, that he may know and try their way. Henry compares the prophet's testing work to assaying ore: the tower watches and proves what the people truly are. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. They are all grievous revolters, walking with slanders, brass and iron, all corrupters. Henry calls them the worst of revolters: sinners soon become tempters, corrupting others as well as themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. The bellows are burned, the lead is consumed of the fire, the founder melts in vain, for the wicked are not plucked away. Henry notes the ore supposed to have good metal proves all dross: nothing prevails to part them from their sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Reprobate silver shall men call them, because the Lord has rejected them. Henry closes that when warnings, corrections, rebukes, and all means of grace leave men unrenewed, they will be left, as rejected of God, to everlasting misery; our prayer must be to be refined by the Lord as silver is refined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
