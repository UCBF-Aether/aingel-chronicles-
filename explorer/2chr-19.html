The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Jehoshaphat the king of Judah returned to his house in peace to Jerusalem.

2 And Jehu the son of Hanani the seer went out to meet him, and said to king Jehoshaphat, Shouldest thou help the ungodly, and love them that hate the LORD? therefore is wrath upon thee from before the LORD.

3 Nevertheless there are good things found in thee, in that thou hast taken away the groves out of the land, and hast prepared thine heart to seek God.

4 And Jehoshaphat dwelt at Jerusalem: and he went out again through the people from Beersheba to mount Ephraim, and brought them back unto the LORD God of their fathers.

5 And he set judges in the land throughout all the fenced cities of Judah, city by city,

6 And said to the judges, Take heed what ye do: for ye judge not for man, but for the LORD, who is with you in the judgment.

7 Wherefore now let the fear of the LORD be upon you; take heed and do it: for there is no iniquity with the LORD our God, nor respect of persons, nor taking of gifts.

8 Moreover in Jerusalem did Jehoshaphat set of the Levites, and of the priests, and of the chief of the fathers of Israel, for the judgment of the LORD, and for controversies, when they returned to Jerusalem.

9 And he charged them, saying, Thus shall ye do in the fear of the LORD, faithfully, and with a perfect heart.

10 And what cause soever shall come to you of your brethren that dwell in your cities, between blood and blood, between law and commandment, statutes and judgments, ye shall even warn them that they trespass not against the LORD, and so wrath come upon you, and upon your brethren: this do, and ye shall not trespass.

11 And, behold, Amariah the chief priest is over you in all matters of the LORD; and Zebadiah the son of Ishmael, the ruler of the house of Judah, for all the king’s matters: also the Levites shall be officers before you. Deal courageously, and the LORD shall be with the good.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Jehoshaphat's return to Jerusalem, the prophet Jehu's rebuke for helping the wicked, and Jehoshaphat's appointment of judges with a charge to judge in the fear of God. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And Jehoshaphat king of Judah returned to his house in peace to Jerusalem. Henry notes this was a great favour: he fared better than he expected after imminent peril, better than he deserved being out of duty's way, and better than Ahab, brought home slain, for God distinguishes the righteous from the ungodly, one taken and the other left. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And Jehu son of Hanani the seer went out to meet him, and said, Shouldest thou help the ungodly, and love them that hate the Lord? Therefore is wrath upon thee from before the Lord. Henry notes it is great mercy to be told our faults in time: Jehu, son of Hanani who was stocked for plain dealing under Asa, was not afraid to reprove another king, and told Jehoshaphat plainly he had done very ill in joining Ahab, for God's people must not debase themselves by intimacy with haters of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Nevertheless there are good things found in thee, in that thou hast taken away the groves out of the land, and hast prepared thine heart to seek God. Henry notes reproof should notice what is praiseworthy: his abolishing idolatry with a heart fixed to seek God was a good thing God accepted, so though displeased, God would not cast him off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And Jehoshaphat dwelt at Jerusalem, and he went out again through the people from Beersheba to Mount Ephraim, and brought them back unto the Lord God of their fathers. Henry notes he took reproof well, unlike his father with the seer, dwelt at home minding his own business, and made a pious visitation of his kingdom to atone for his visit to Ahab, reviving reformation and repairing damage his example may have done, for true repentance recovers those hardened by our example. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And he set judges in the land throughout all the fenced cities of Judah, city by city. Henry notes having sent preachers to instruct, Jehoshaphat now sent judges to execute laws and be a terror to evil-doers, new-modelling courts whose judges had neglected business or been slighted, with new men and a new charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And said to the judges, Take heed what ye do: for ye judge not for man, but for the Lord, who is with you in the judgment. Henry notes the charge prescribes caution, for judges above all must be cautious since so much depends on correctness, and piety, for the fear of God restrains from wrong and engages to duty, with motives from God: their commission is from him, his eye is upon them, and he is the example of justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Wherefore now let the fear of the Lord be upon you; take heed and do it: for there is no iniquity with the Lord our God, nor respect of persons, nor taking of gifts. Henry notes magistrates are called gods and must resemble God, with whom there is no iniquity, bribery or respect of persons, and the fear of God will make them a terror to none but evil-doers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Moreover in Jerusalem did Jehoshaphat set of the Levites, and of the priests, and of the chief of the fathers of Israel, for the judgment of the Lord, and for controversies, when they returned to Jerusalem. Henry notes he erected a supreme court at Jerusalem, under the king's inspection, for pleas of the crown, the judgment of the Lord, and common pleas, controversies between party and party, with learned Levites and priests and chief fathers as judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And he charged them, saying, Thus shall ye do in the fear of the Lord, faithfully, and with a perfect heart. Henry notes the supreme court's charge: act from a good principle, in the fear of the Lord always before them, then faithfully, conscientiously, and with a perfect upright heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And what cause soever shall come to you of your brethren in your cities, between blood and blood, between law and commandment, statutes and judgments, ye shall even warn them that they trespass not against the Lord, and so wrath come upon you and your brethren: this do, and ye shall not trespass. Henry notes their great care must be to prevent sin, warning people not to trespass against the Lord, for wrath would come on people if they sinned and on magistrates if they did not punish it, and those with power contract guilt if they do not use it to restrain sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And behold, Amariah the chief priest is over you in all matters of the Lord, and Zebadiah son of Ishmael, ruler of Judah, for all the king's matters, also Levites shall be officers before you. Deal courageously, and the Lord shall be with the good. Henry notes Amariah presided in ecclesiastical causes and Zebadiah in civil, Levites served as officers bringing causes and executing sentences, and the charge closed with resolution: deal courageously, fear not man's face, for the Lord shall be with the good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
