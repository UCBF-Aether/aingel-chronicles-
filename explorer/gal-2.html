The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Galatians 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then fourteen years after I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, and took Titus with me also.

2 And I went up by revelation, and communicated unto them that gospel which I preach among the Gentiles, but privately to them which were of reputation, lest by any means I should run, or had run, in vain.

3 But neither Titus, who was with me, being a Greek, was compelled to be circumcised:

4 And that because of false brethren unawares brought in, who came in privily to spy out our liberty which we have in Christ Jesus, that they might bring us into bondage:

5 To whom we gave place by subjection, no, not for an hour; that the truth of the gospel might continue with you.

6 But of these who seemed to be somewhat, (whatsoever they were, it maketh no matter to me: God accepteth no man’s person:) for they who seemed to be somewhat in conference added nothing to me:

7 But contrariwise, when they saw that the gospel of the uncircumcision was committed unto me, as the gospel of the circumcision was unto Peter;

8 (For he that wrought effectually in Peter to the apostleship of the circumcision, the same was mighty in me toward the Gentiles:)

9 And when James, Cephas, and John, who seemed to be pillars, perceived the grace that was given unto me, they gave to me and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship; that we should go unto the heathen, and they unto the circumcision.

10 Only they would that we should remember the poor; the same which I also was forward to do.

11 But when Peter was come to Antioch, I withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed.

12 For before that certain came from James, he did eat with the Gentiles: but when they were come, he withdrew and separated himself, fearing them which were of the circumcision.

13 And the other Jews dissembled likewise with him; insomuch that Barnabas also was carried away with their dissimulation.

14 But when I saw that they walked not uprightly according to the truth of the gospel, I said unto Peter before them all, If thou, being a Jew, livest after the manner of Gentiles, and not as do the Jews, why compellest thou the Gentiles to live as do the Jews?

15 We who are Jews by nature, and not sinners of the Gentiles,

16 Knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Jesus Christ, even we have believed in Jesus Christ, that we might be justified by the faith of Christ, and not by the works of the law: for by the works of the law shall no flesh be justified.

17 But if, while we seek to be justified by Christ, we ourselves also are found sinners, is therefore Christ the minister of sin? God forbid.

18 For if I build again the things which I destroyed, I make myself a transgressor.

19 For I through the law am dead to the law, that I might live unto God.

20 I am crucified with Christ: neverthless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me.

21 I do not frustrate the grace of God: for if righteousness come by the law, then Christ is dead in vain.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Fourteen years on, Paul lays his Gentile gospel before the Jerusalem leaders, who add nothing to it and give him and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship, asking only that the poor be remembered. Yet at Antioch he confronts Peter face to face for withdrawing from Gentile believers, and states the letter's core: a person is justified by faith in Christ, not by works of the law. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Fourteen years after, Paul went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus also, faithfully giving a full account of the doctrine he preached among the Gentiles, free from all mixture of Judaism. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He went up by revelation and communicated privately to those of reputation the gospel he preaches among the Gentiles, using proper caution lest his past labors be lessened or his future usefulness hindered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. But neither Titus, a Greek, was compelled to be circumcised, since what may lawfully be complied with must be refused when it would betray the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. This was because of false brethren secretly brought in, who came privily to spy out the liberty believers have in Christ Jesus, that they might bring them into bondage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. To them Paul gave place by subjection not for an hour, that the truth of the gospel might continue with the Galatians, since the gospel is God's, not men's, and men are only its keepers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Of those who seemed to be somewhat, Paul adds that whatever they were makes no matter, since God accepts no man's person, and they added nothing to his knowledge or authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Contrariwise, when they saw the gospel of the uncircumcision committed to Paul as the gospel of the circumcision was to Peter, they acknowledged his apostleship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. For he who worked effectually in Peter for the circumcision was mighty in Paul toward the Gentiles, the same God authoring both missions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. When James, Cephas, and John, who seemed to be pillars, perceived the grace given to Paul, they gave him and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship, acknowledging him designed to the same apostolic office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Only they asked that they remember the poor, which Paul was forward to do, a pattern of Christian charity extending to all Christ's disciples regardless of differences of opinion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. But when Peter came to Antioch, Paul withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed, not fearing to reprove even Peter when the truth of the gospel and the church's peace were hurt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Before certain men came from James, Peter ate with the Gentiles, but when they came he withdrew and separated himself, fearing those of the circumcision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The other Jews dissembled likewise with him, so that even Barnabas was carried away with their dissimulation, Peter's timid conduct leading others to think the ceremonies necessary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. When Paul saw they did not walk uprightly according to the truth of the gospel, he said to Peter before them all that if he, a Jew, lived as a Gentile and not as a Jew, he could not compel Gentiles to live as Jews, since by Christ's death the partition wall was taken down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Paul and Peter are Jews by nature, not sinners of the Gentiles, yet even they must be justified the same way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Knowing a man is not justified by works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ, even they believed in Christ to be justified by faith, not by works, since the argument holds as strongly against dependence on moral works as on ceremonial ones. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. If, while seeking justification by Christ, believers themselves are found sinners, is Christ therefore the minister of sin? God forbid: that would dishonor Christ and hurt believers, and taking occasion from free grace to live in sin is what Christian hearts shudder at. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. For if Paul builds again the things he destroyed, he makes himself a transgressor, returning to a law whose sacrifices and cleansings are done away in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. For through the law Paul is dead to the law, that he might live to God, no more hoping or fearing anything from it, any more than a dead man from enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Paul is crucified with Christ, yet he lives, yet not he, but Christ lives in him, the old man crucified and the new man living, grace quickened and sin mortified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. He does not frustrate God's grace, for if righteousness came by the law, then Christ died in vain, and grace cannot stand with man's merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms
