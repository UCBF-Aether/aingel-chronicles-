Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A lament addressed in turn to the earth and to Death. Earth, it says, has gathered kings and rulers, the great and honoured, beautiful women and fair virgins, strong hands, knowledge, fair voices and songs like the harp — and Death has taken their souls by the will of the Lord God. The chapter then sets up an exchange between humanity and the earth: we ate the earth’s fruit and it ate our flesh; we drank its dew and it drank our blood; we ate from its earth and it ate our bones — and Death, fearing neither greatness nor terror, spared no one: not the mighty and strong, not the poor, not those who thought good and kept the commandment, not those who thought evil and became as beasts, not the perfect in beauty or the bitter of word. Death gathered their souls into treasuries of light or darkness, and the earth gathered their flesh, until the blowing of the horn, when the dead shall rise — and the doers shall be judged according to their deeds, the good rejoicing.
