The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hosea 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt.

2 As they called them, so they went from them: they sacrificed unto Baalim, and burned incense to graven images.

3 I taught Ephraim also to go, taking them by their arms; but they knew not that I healed them.

4 I drew them with cords of a man, with bands of love: and I was to them as they that take off the yoke on their jaws, and I laid meat unto them.

5 He shall not return into the land of Egypt, and the Assyrian shall be his king, because they refused to return.

6 And the sword shall abide on his cities, and shall consume his branches, and devour them, because of their own counsels.

7 And my people are bent to backsliding from me: though they called them to the most High, none at all would exalt him.

8 How shall I give thee up, Ephraim? how shall I deliver thee, Israel? how shall I make thee as Admah? how shall I set thee as Zeboim? mine heart is turned within me, my repentings are kindled together.

9 I will not execute the fierceness of mine anger, I will not return to destroy Ephraim: for I am God, and not man; the Holy One in the midst of thee: and I will not enter into the city.

10 They shall walk after the LORD: he shall roar like a lion: when he shall roar, then the children shall tremble from the west.

11 They shall tremble as a bird out of Egypt, and as a dove out of the land of Assyria: and I will place them in their houses, saith the LORD.

12 Ephraim compasseth me about with lies, and the house of Israel with deceit: but Judah yet ruleth with God, and is faithful with the saints.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God remembers loving Israel as a child and calling his son out of Egypt, teaching Ephraim to walk and holding him by the arms, yet they sacrificed to Baalim and would not return. Torn between judgment and compassion, God says he will not execute the fierceness of his anger or destroy Ephraim, for he is God and not man, and his children will tremble home from Egypt and Assyria to their houses. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt, because God's love chose Israel in its childhood, and the exodus was the calling of a son, not merely the freeing of a people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. As they called them, so they went from them, they sacrificed unto Baalim, and burned incense to graven images, because the more the prophets called, the farther the people went, turning the call itself into an occasion for Baal worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. I taught Ephraim also to go, taking them by their arms, but they knew not that I healed them, because God led Israel as a child is led by the arms, healing every fall, while Israel never knew the hand that healed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. I drew them with cords of a man, with bands of love, and I was to them as they that take off the yoke on their jaws, and I laid meat unto them, because God drew with human cords of love, lifting the yoke to feed them, as a man lifts the yoke from an ox's jaws to let it eat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He shall not return into the land of Egypt, but the Assyrian shall be his king, because they refused to return, because Egypt is not the sentence, but Assyria will be king over a people who refused to return to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And the sword shall abide on his cities, and shall consume his branches, and devour them, because of their own counsels, because the sword will settle upon the cities and eat up their defences, and the cause is their own counsels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And my people are bent to backsliding from me, though they called them to the most High, none at all would exalt him, because the people are bent, like a bow warped toward backsliding, and even when called upward, none will exalt God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. How shall I give thee up, Ephraim, how shall I deliver thee, Israel, how shall I make thee as Admah, how shall I set thee as Zeboim, mine heart is turned within me, my repentings are kindled together, because God pauses over the sentence, naming the cities of the plain that were overthrown, and his heart turns within him at the thought of doing the same to Ephraim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. I will not execute the fierceness of mine anger, I will not return to destroy Ephraim, for I am God, and not man, the Holy One in the midst of thee, and I will not enter into the city, because God's holiness, unlike man's anger, restrains the fierceness, and he will not come as a destroyer into the city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They shall walk after the Lord, he shall roar like a lion, when he shall roar, then the children shall tremble from the west, because the people will follow the roaring lion, and his children will come trembling from the west at the sound. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They shall tremble as a bird out of Egypt, and as a dove out of the land of Assyria, and I will place them in their houses, says the Lord, because the exiles will flutter home like birds from Egypt and doves from Assyria, and God himself will place them in their houses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Ephraim compasses me about with lies, and the house of Israel with deceit, but Judah yet rules with God, and is faithful with the saints, because Ephraim surrounds God with lies while Judah, though not innocent, still holds to God and to the faithful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Baal and Baalim. the Canaanite god, and the plural form for the Baals, whose worship the prophets charge God's people with, crediting idols with gifts God himself gave. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Ephraim. the leading tribe of the northern kingdom, used in the prophets for the northern kingdom of Israel itself, as distinct from Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
