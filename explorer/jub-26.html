The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 26 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the seventh year of this week Isaac called Esau, his elder son, and said unto him : " I am old, my son, and behold my eyes are dim in seeing, and I know not the day of my death.

2 And now take thy hunting weapons, thy quiver and thy bow, and go out to the field, and hunt and catch me (venison), my son, and make me savoury meat, such as my soul loveth, and bring it to me that I may eat, and that my soul may bless thee before I die.

3 But Rebecca heard Isaac speaking to Esau.

4 And Esau went forth early to the field to hunt and catch and bring home to his father.

5 And Rebecca called Jacob, her son, and said unto him : " Behold, I heard Isaac, thy father, speak unto Esau, thy brother, saying : " Hunt for me, and make me savoury meat, and bring (it) to me that I may eat and bless thee before the Lord before I die." And now, my son, obey my voice in that which I command thee : Go to thy flock and fetch me two good kids of the goats, and I will make them savoury meat for thy father, such as he loves, and thou shalt bring (it) to thy father that he may eat and bless thee before the Lord before he die, and that thou mayst be blessed." And Jacob said to Rebecca his mother : " Mother, I shall not withhold

6 anything which my father would eat, and which would please him : only I fear, my mother, that he will recognise my voice and wish to touch me.

7 And thou knowest that I am smooth, and Esau, my brother, is hairy, and I shall appear before his eyes as an evildoer, and shall do a deed which he had not commanded me, and he will be wroth with me, and I shall bring upon myself a curse, and not a blessing." 9.

8 And Rebecca, his mother, said unto him :

9 " Upon me be thy curse, my son, only obey my voice. ' 10.

10 And Jacob obeyed the voice of Rebecca, his mother, and went and fetched two good and fat kids of the goats, and brought them to his mother, and his mother made them (savoury meat) such as he loved.

11 And Rebecca took the goodly raiment of Esau, her elder son, which was with her in the house, and she clothed Jacob, her younger son, (with them), and she put the skins of the kids upon his hands and on the exposed parts of his neck.

12 And she gave the meat and the bread which she had prepared into the hand of her son Jacob.

13 And Jacob went in to his father and said : " I am thy son : I have done according as thou badest me : arise and sit and eat of that which I have caught, father, that thy soul may bless me." 14.

14 And Isaac said to his son : " How hast thou found so quickly, my son?" 15.

15 And Jacob said : " Because (the Lord) thy God caused me to find." 16.

16 And Isaac said unto him : " Come near, that I may feel thee, my son, if thou art my son Esau or not." 17.

17 And Jacob went near to Isaac, his father, and he felt him and said : "The voice is Jacob's voice, but the hands are the hands "of Esau," and he discerned him not, because it was a dispensation from heaven to remove his power of perception and Isaac discerned not, for his hands were hairy as (his brother) Esau's, so that he blessed him.

18 And he said : " Art thou my son, Esau?

19 " and he said : " I am thy son " : and he said, " Bring

20 near to me that I may eat of that which thou hast caught, my son, that my soul may bless thee." 20.

21 And he brought near to him, and he did eat, and he brought him wine and he drank.

22 And Isaac, his father, said unto him : " Come near and kiss me, my son."

23 And he came near and kissed him.

24 And he smelled the smell of his raiment, and he blessed him and said : " Behold, the smell of my son is as the smell of a (full) field which the Lord hath blessed.

25 And may the Lord give thee of the dew of heaven And of the dew of the earth, and plenty of corn

26 and oil : Let nations serve thee, And peoples bow down to thee.

27 Be lord over thy brethren,

28 And let thy mother's sons bow down to thee ; And may all the blessings wherewith the Lord

29 hath blessed me and blessed Abraham, my

30 father Be imparted to thee and to thy seed for ever : Cursed be he that curseth thee, And blessed be he that blesseth thee."

31 And it came to pass as soon as Isaac had made an end of blessing his son Jacob, and Jacob had gone forth from Isaac his father he hid himself and Esau, his brother, came in from his hunting.

32 And he also made savoury meat, and brought (it) to his father, and said unto his father : " Let my father arise, and eat of my venison that thy soul may bless me." 27.

33 And Isaac, his father, said unto him: " Who art thou? " And he said unto him : " I am thy first-born, thy son Esau : I have done as thou hast commanded me." And Isaac was very

34 greatly astonished, and said : " Who is he that hath hunted and caught and brought (it) to me, and I have eaten of all before thou camest, and have blessed him : (and) he shall be blessed, and all his seed for ever." 29.

35 And it came to pass when Esau heard the words of his father Isaac that he cried with an exceeding great and bitter cry, and said unto his father: "Bless me, (even) me also, father." 30.

36 And he said unto him : " Thy brother came with guile, and hath taken away thy blessing."

37 And he said : " Now I know why his name is named Jacob : behold, he hath supplanted me these two times : he took away my birth-right, and now he hath taken away my blessing." 31.

38 And he said : " Hast thou not reserved a blessing for me, father?

39 " and Isaac answered and said unto Esau :

40 " Behold, I have made him thy lord,

41 And all his brethren have I given to him for servants,

42 And with plenty of corn and wine and oil have I strengthened him :

43 And what now shall I do for thee, my son ?

44 And Esau said to Isaac, his father :

45 " Hast thou but one blessing, O father ?

46 Bless me, (even) me also, father " : And Esau lifted up his voice and wept.

47 And Isaac answered and said unto him :

48 " Behold, far from the dew of the earth shall be thy dwelling,

49 And far from the dew of heaven from above.

50 And by thy sword wilt thou live,

51 And thou wilt serve thy brother.

52 And it shall come to pass when thou becomest great

53 And dost shake his yoke from off thy neck,

54 Thou wilt sin a complete sin unto death,

55 And thy seed will be rooted out from under heaven." 35.

56 And Esau kept threatening Jacob because of the blessing wherewith his father blessed him, and he said in his heart : " May the days of mourning for my father now come, so that I may slay my brother Jacob."

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: Isaac, now old and dim of sight, set out to bless his elder son before he died. Esau kept threatening Jacob because of the blessing, and said in his heart that he would slay his brother once the days of mourning for his father had come. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. Isaac, now old and dim of sight, set out to bless his elder son before he died. Henry notes that Isaac meant to put the honour of the promise on Esau, without duly weighing the divine word that the elder should serve the younger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Isaac sent Esau hunting for venison to make the savoury meat he loved, so that he might eat and bless him before death. The blessing Isaac planned was the solemn passing on of the covenant promise to the next head of the family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Rebecca overheard Isaac speaking to Esau. Henry stresses that she knew the blessing was intended for Jacob, yet chose a crooked way to secure it rather than leaving the matter to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Esau went out early to hunt and bring home what his father had asked. His prompt obedience makes the deception that follows all the sharper, since the blessing was sought behind his back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Rebecca told Jacob what she had heard and ordered him to fetch two good kids so she could prepare the savoury meat Isaac loved. Henry warns that she wronged Isaac by the cheat, wronged Jacob by tempting him, and put a stumbling-block in Esau's way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Jacob hesitated, fearing his father would recognise his voice and wish to touch him. His fear shows he knew the plan depended on deceiving a blind old man, not on any honest claim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Jacob pointed out the plain difference between himself and his hairy brother, fearing he would seem an evildoer and bring a curse instead of a blessing. Henry's warning applies: we must not do evil that good may come, even to further a divine promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Rebecca answered Jacob's fear by taking the risk on herself. Henry calls her words a very rash speech, too daring for any creature to say of the curse of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Rebecca's words, that any curse should fall on her, were meant to quiet Jacob's conscience. Henry notes that Christ alone has borne the curse of the law for those who take up his yoke; no creature may claim that place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jacob obeyed his mother, fetched two good fat kids, and brought them to her to prepare as Isaac loved. Obedience to a parent did not make the deceit right, and Henry observes that all parties in the affair were to be blamed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Rebecca dressed Jacob in Esau's goodly raiment and covered his hands and neck with kid skins, so that touch and smell would both point to Esau. Every sense was enlisted in the disguise, which is why Henry calls the whole device a cheat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Rebecca put the prepared meat and bread into Jacob's hands to carry to his father. The meal was a mother's cooking passed off as a hunter's catch, the centrepiece of the deception. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Jacob went in claiming to be Esau and to have done as his father bade him, asking Isaac to eat so that he might bless him. Henry notes that Jacob gained his point only with difficulty, and by falsehood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Isaac wondered how his son had found game so quickly. The question was a natural one, and it gave Jacob the chance to turn back from the lie, which he did not take. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Jacob answered that the Lord Isaac's God had caused him to find the game. Henry's account of this chapter warns against using God's name to cover wrong means, since the end never justifies them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Isaac proposed the test of touch, to learn whether the man before him was Esau or not. His blindness forced him to judge by hands rather than eyes, and the disguise was made to meet exactly that test. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Isaac felt Jacob and said the voice was Jacob's voice but the hands were Esau's hands, yet he did not discern him. Henry observes that Isaac's attachment to Esau, set against the mind of God, had weakened his own faith and perception in the matter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Isaac asked directly whether the man before him was his son Esau. The direct question pressed Jacob to confirm the falsehood in plain words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Jacob answered that he was Isaac's son, and Isaac asked him to bring the food near so he might eat and bless him. The blessing was now within reach of the deception, though not yet given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Isaac's request to eat of what his son had caught set the meal before the blessing, as he had planned with Esau. In the patriarchal order, the father's eating and blessing went together as one solemn act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Jacob brought the food near, Isaac ate, and Jacob brought wine which he drank. The shared meal sealed the scene: Isaac believed he was eating Esau's venison and was ready to bless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Isaac called Jacob near to kiss him, adding the test of smell to the tests of voice and touch. Each test was met by the disguise, and Henry notes how God overruled even these bad actions to fulfil his purposes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Jacob came near and kissed his father. The kiss of a son, given under a false name, completed the deception that the blessing would then ratify. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Isaac smelled the raiment and blessed Jacob, saying his smell was as the smell of a full field which the Lord had blessed. Henry notes the blessing was given in very general terms, with no mention of the distinguishing mercies of the covenant with Abraham. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The blessing promised the dew of heaven and of earth, with plenty of corn and oil. These are the fruits of a favoured land, the outward plenty that attends God's blessing on the heir of the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The blessing continued that nations should serve Jacob and peoples bow down to him. Henry reads this dominion as the lot appointed to Jacob's seed, a sovereignty that would reach beyond his own household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Jacob was made lord over his brethren, reversing the natural order of elder and younger. The words confirmed what God had said before the twins were born, that the elder should serve the younger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. His mother's sons were to bow down to him, binding the whole family under Jacob's headship. The blessing thus settled the leadership of the house, not merely a share of its goods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Isaac prayed that all the blessings given to him and to Abraham his father might be imparted to Jacob and his seed for ever. This clause tied Jacob to the covenant line, which is why Henry stresses that Jacob's privilege was to convey spiritual blessings to all nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The blessing closed with the ancient formula, cursed be he that curseth thee and blessed be he that blesseth thee. The same words guarded Abraham, and now they guarded Jacob as Abraham's heir. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. As soon as Isaac had finished blessing Jacob, Jacob went out and hid himself, and Esau came in from his hunting. The timing is the hinge of the story: the blessing was given and could not be taken back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Esau also made savoury meat and brought it to his father, asking him to arise, eat, and bless him. He came in full confidence that the blessing was still waiting for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Isaac asked who Esau was, and Esau answered that he was the first-born, who had done as commanded. Henry notes that Isaac, made sensible of the deceit practised on him, trembled exceedingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Isaac, greatly astonished, asked who had hunted and brought food before Esau came, and confirmed that the one he had blessed would indeed be blessed, with all his seed for ever. Henry stresses that Isaac soon recovered and confirmed the blessing, saying he had blessed him and he shall be blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. When Esau heard his father's words he cried with an exceeding great and bitter cry, begging a blessing for himself also. Henry warns that those who sell their title to spiritual blessings for what is of no value will one day ask urgently for them in vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Isaac told Esau that his brother had come with guile and taken away the blessing. The word guile names the means plainly: the blessing was gained by deceit, even though God overruled it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Esau said he now knew why his brother was named Jacob, for he had supplanted him twice, taking first the birthright and now the blessing. The name Jacob, supplanter, had proved painfully true in Esau's experience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Esau asked whether his father had reserved no blessing for him. The question shows he still hoped some part of the covenant blessing might remain to be given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Isaac answered Esau, beginning the lesser blessing that remained. What followed was a common blessing of earthly good, not the covenant blessing already given to Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Isaac told Esau that he had made Jacob his lord, a settled fact that could not be undone. The headship of the family had passed, and Esau's blessing had to be framed within it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. All Jacob's brethren had been given to him for servants, completing the transfer of dominion. Henry notes that Esau's blessing, unlike Jacob's, contained nothing pointing to Christ, and earthly fatness alone would stand him in little stead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Isaac added that Jacob had been strengthened with plenty of corn, wine, and oil, the same plenty named in Jacob's blessing. The store of the blessing was already spent on Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Isaac asked what he could now do for Esau, his son. The question is not unkindness but the plain arithmetic of a blessing already given away entire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Esau spoke again to his father, pressing his plea. His persistence shows how deeply he felt the loss once it was irreversible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Esau asked whether his father had but one blessing, and begged to be blessed also. Henry observes that faint desires of happiness, without right choice of the end and right use of the means, deceive many to their ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Esau lifted up his voice and wept. His tears were real grief for a lost inheritance, though Henry warns they came after he had himself despised the best things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Isaac answered Esau with the blessing that remained to him. What follows is a genuine blessing of a lesser kind, not a curse, though it lacks the covenant promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Esau's dwelling was to be far from the dew of the earth, the reverse of Jacob's fruitful portion. His lot would be a harder land, away from the fatness promised to his brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. Esau was also to be far from the dew of heaven from above, completing the picture of a drier, less favoured dwelling. The blessing measured his portion against Jacob's at every point. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Esau would live by his sword, a life of arms and roaming rather than settled plenty. Henry notes this as the common blessing Esau desired, earthly strength without the covenant's spiritual promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. Esau would serve his brother, confirming the elder's subjection to the younger. The service was the appointed order, however unwelcome, and it matched the word spoken before their birth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Isaac added that when Esau became great he would shake his brother's yoke from off his neck. The yoke would not last for ever: Esau's descendants would in time break free of Jacob's dominion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. The shaking off of the yoke marked the limit of Jacob's dominion over Esau in Isaac's blessing. Henry reads Esau's blessing as a whole as a common, earthly portion, without the promise of Christ that made Jacob's blessing great. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Esau kept threatening Jacob because of the blessing, and said in his heart that he would slay his brother once the days of mourning for his father had come. Henry compares Esau here to Cain, who slew his brother because he had gained the acceptance of which he had rendered himself unworthy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 36. Isaac told Esau that his brother had come with guile and taken away the blessing. The word guile names the means plainly: the blessing was gained by deceit, even though God overruled it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Esau said he now knew why his brother was named Jacob, for he had supplanted him twice, taking first the birthright and now the blessing. The name Jacob, supplanter, had proved painfully true in Esau's experience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Esau asked whether his father had reserved no blessing for him. The question shows he still hoped some part of the covenant blessing might remain to be given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Isaac answered Esau, beginning the lesser blessing that remained. What followed was a common blessing of earthly good, not the covenant blessing already given to Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Isaac told Esau that he had made Jacob his lord, a settled fact that could not be undone. The headship of the family had passed, and Esau's blessing had to be framed within it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. All Jacob's brethren had been given to him for servants, completing the transfer of dominion. Henry notes that Esau's blessing, unlike Jacob's, contained nothing pointing to Christ, and earthly fatness alone would stand him in little stead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Isaac added that Jacob had been strengthened with plenty of corn, wine, and oil, the same plenty named in Jacob's blessing. The store of the blessing was already spent on Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Isaac asked what he could now do for Esau, his son. The question is not unkindness but the plain arithmetic of a blessing already given away entire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Esau spoke again to his father, pressing his plea. His persistence shows how deeply he felt the loss once it was irreversible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Esau asked whether his father had but one blessing, and begged to be blessed also. Henry observes that faint desires of happiness, without right choice of the end and right use of the means, deceive many to their ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Esau lifted up his voice and wept. His tears were real grief for a lost inheritance, though Henry warns they came after he had himself despised the best things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Isaac answered Esau with the blessing that remained to him. What follows is a genuine blessing of a lesser kind, not a curse, though it lacks the covenant promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Esau's dwelling was to be far from the dew of the earth, the reverse of Jacob's fruitful portion. His lot would be a harder land, away from the fatness promised to his brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. Esau was also to be far from the dew of heaven from above, completing the picture of a drier, less favoured dwelling. The blessing measured his portion against Jacob's at every point. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Esau would live by his sword, a life of arms and roaming rather than settled plenty. Henry notes this as the common blessing Esau desired, earthly strength without the covenant's spiritual promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. Esau would serve his brother, confirming the elder's subjection to the younger. The service was the appointed order, however unwelcome, and it matched the word spoken before their birth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Isaac added that when Esau became great he would shake his brother's yoke from off his neck. The yoke would not last for ever: Esau's descendants would in time break free of Jacob's dominion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. The shaking off of the yoke marked the limit of Jacob's dominion over Esau in Isaac's blessing. Henry reads Esau's blessing as a whole as a common, earthly portion, without the promise of Christ that made Jacob's blessing great. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Esau kept threatening Jacob because of the blessing, and said in his heart that he would slay his brother once the days of mourning for his father had come. Henry compares Esau here to Cain, who slew his brother because he had gained the acceptance of which he had rendered himself unworthy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
