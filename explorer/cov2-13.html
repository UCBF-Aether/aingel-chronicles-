*THE AGAPE*

1 In the supper or feast, let those who have come together receive [a portion ] thus from the shepherd, as for a blessing. But let not a catechumen receive.

2 If any one be of the household of, or related to, one who is a teacher of heathenism,’ let him not accord with him and give praise with him, also let him not eat with him because of relationship or for concord, lest he deliver ineffable things to a wolf and he receive judgment.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the supper or feast — the Agape — those who have come together receive a portion thus from the shepherd, as for a blessing; but a catechumen is not to receive. If anyone is of the household of, or related to, one who is a teacher of heathenism, he is not to accord with him and give praise with him, nor eat with him because of relationship or for concord, lest he deliver ineffable things to a wolf and receive judgment. Those called with the bishop to the house of one who is faithful are to eat with gravity and knowledge, not with drunkenness or debauchery, and not so that he who is present may laugh or so as to annoy the household of him who called them; but so let them enter that he who called them may pray that the saints may enter into his house, for ye are the salt of the earth, as ye have heard. When they eat, they are to eat abundantly, but so that there may be left over both for them and for those to whom he who called them wishes to send, so that he may have them as foods left over by the saints and rejoice at what remains over. Those who come to a feast, being called, are not to stretch out a hand before those who are elder; the last are to eat when the first have done. Those who eat are not to strive in speech, but to eat in silence; if anyone desires, or the bishop or presbyter asks a question, he is to return answer.
