The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And if a soul sin, and hear the voice of swearing, and is a witness, whether he hath seen or known of it; if he do not utter it, then he shall bear his iniquity.

2 Or if a soul touch any unclean thing, whether it be a carcase of an unclean beast, or a carcase of unclean cattle, or the carcase of unclean creeping things, and if it be hidden from him; he also shall be unclean, and guilty.

3 Or if he touch the uncleanness of man, whatsoever uncleanness it be that a man shall be defiled withal, and it be hid from him; when he knoweth of it, then he shall be guilty.

4 Or if a soul swear, pronouncing with his lips to do evil, or to do good, whatsoever it be that a man shall pronounce with an oath, and it be hid from him; when he knoweth of it, then he shall be guilty in one of these.

5 And it shall be, when he shall be guilty in one of these things, that he shall confess that he hath sinned in that thing:

6 And he shall bring his trespass offering unto the LORD for his sin which he hath sinned, a female from the flock, a lamb or a kid of the goats, for a sin offering; and the priest shall make an atonement for him concerning his sin.

7 And if he be not able to bring a lamb, then he shall bring for his trespass, which he hath committed, two turtledoves, or two young pigeons, unto the LORD; one for a sin offering, and the other for a burnt offering.

8 And he shall bring them unto the priest, who shall offer that which is for the sin offering first, and wring off his head from his neck, but shall not divide it asunder:

9 And he shall sprinkle of the blood of the sin offering upon the side of the altar; and the rest of the blood shall be wrung out at the bottom of the altar: it is a sin offering.

10 And he shall offer the second for a burnt offering, according to the manner: and the priest shall make an atonement for him for his sin which he hath sinned, and it shall be forgiven him.

11 But if he be not able to bring two turtledoves, or two young pigeons, then he that sinned shall bring for his offering the tenth part of an ephah of fine flour for a sin offering; he shall put no oil upon it, neither shall he put any frankincense thereon: for it is a sin offering.

12 Then shall he bring it to the priest, and the priest shall take his handful of it, even a memorial thereof, and burn it on the altar, according to the offerings made by fire unto the LORD: it is a sin offering.

13 And the priest shall make an atonement for him as touching his sin that he hath sinned in one of these, and it shall be forgiven him: and the remnant shall be the priest’s, as a meat offering.

14 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

15 If a soul commit a trespass, and sin through ignorance, in the holy things of the LORD; then he shall bring for his trespass unto the LORD a ram without blemish out of the flocks, with thy estimation by shekels of silver, after the shekel of the sanctuary, for a trespass offering.

16 And he shall make amends for the harm that he hath done in the holy thing, and shall add the fifth part thereto, and give it unto the priest: and the priest shall make an atonement for him with the ram of the trespass offering, and it shall be forgiven him.

17 And if a soul sin, and commit any of these things which are forbidden to be done by the commandments of the LORD; though he wist it not, yet is he guilty, and shall bear his iniquity.

18 And he shall bring a ram without blemish out of the flock, with thy estimation, for a trespass offering, unto the priest: and the priest shall make an atonement for him concerning his ignorance wherein he erred and wist it not, and it shall be forgiven him.

19 It is a trespass offering: he hath certainly trespassed against the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter continues the sin and trespass laws with particular cases: a witness who withholds testimony, a person who touches an unclean thing, and a person who swears a rash oath. Guilt must be met with confession, and the offering is graded to means: a female lamb, or two birds, or even fine flour for the very poor. The second half deals with trespass against holy things, where restitution must be made with a fifth added, and with doubtful cases where conscience suspects wrong though the facts are uncertain. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Trespass offering. An offering for a wrong that also requires putting right, here covering withheld testimony, defilement, rash oaths, and sacrilege against holy things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • A fifth part. The extra fifth added to full restitution when a wrong against a holy thing or a neighbour is made good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hearing the voice of swearing means hearing the words of adjuration read in a Jewish court, where witnesses were adjured rather than sworn directly, and the offence here is neglecting an opportunity to lodge information one could give. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. A person who unknowingly touches an unclean thing, then neglects purification or joins religious service while defiled, may afterwards be convinced of the offence and so become guilty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The same principle covers touching the uncleanness of man: the defilement may be hidden at first, but once it is known to him he bears guilt for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. A rash oath is sworn without duly considering its nature and consequences, perhaps binding oneself to do wrong or neglecting a vow to do good, and only afterwards is the transgression recognised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Guilt must be met with voluntary confession from the impulse of conscience, before the matter comes to the knowledge of the world, since spontaneous confession releases the sinner from punishment a prior discovery might have brought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The trespass offering differs from a sin offering in being appointed for one who did evil unwittingly, doubted his own criminality, or stood in a special situation requiring such a sacrifice, and here it is a female lamb or kid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. If he cannot bring a lamb, the law accepts two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering, grading the offering to his means. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The priest offers the sin-offering bird first, wringing off its head from its neck without dividing it completely, following the graduated trespass scale. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Blood of the sin offering is sprinkled on the side of the altar and the rest drained at its base, marking it as a sin offering within the graduated scale. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The second bird is offered as a burnt offering according to the prescribed manner, and by this pair the priest makes atonement and the sin is forgiven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. If even two birds are beyond his ability, the tenth part of an ephah of fine flour without oil or frankincense satisfies the law, so that poverty bars no one from atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The priest takes a handful of the flour as its memorial and burns it on the altar according to the offerings made by fire, the flour offering standing in the same graduated scale as lamb and birds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. By this flour offering the priest makes atonement for sin in any of these matters, it is forgiven him, and the rest belongs to the priest as a meat offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. A further trespass law is introduced concerning the holy things of the LORD, moving from concealment, defilement, and rash oaths to sacrilege. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Ignorant sacrilege, such as withholding full tithes or first-fruits or eating meats belonging to the priests alone, requires a ram without blemish valued in sanctuary shekels, as soon as the involuntary fraud is known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Along with the ram, restitution must be made for the harm done to the holy thing, with a fifth added and given to the priest, the amount being determined by the priest, and by this atonement he is forgiven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. This case also concerns holy things but differs as a doubtful case, where conscience suspects wrong though the understanding is in doubt whether criminality has been committed, yet the person is still guilty and bears iniquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. For such a doubtful trespass he brings a ram without blemish at the priest's valuation, and the priest makes atonement for the ignorance in which he erred and did not know it, and he is forgiven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The rite is expressly a trespass offering, because he has certainly trespassed against the LORD, and these provisions are designed to impress responsibility to God and a salutary fear of secret wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
