Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter continues the resurrection argument as a vision of judgment day, built on a long repeated formula: “in the day of…” darkness, fear, lightning, requital — a catalogue in which many terms are left untranslated or marked [?], so individual days in the list cannot be glossed with confidence. Its point is plain, however: on that day deeds themselves judge, and rank is erased — master above servant, king above poor, father above son, proud above humble, none honoured above another, for it is the day of justice and requital according to what each did. Those who heard and did good shall rejoice, the chapter says, and because they transgressed not their Creator’s commandment and knew they should rise, they shall inherit in the heavens the land of life sworn to their fathers, in joy that shall not fail for ever. Those who denied the resurrection and remembered not its day shall grieve when they see the risen, finding sorrow with no comfort, an undying ʿəṣe [?] and unquenched fire, and torments upon their flesh.
