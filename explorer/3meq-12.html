Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A woe against the mighty men of Israel who kept not what was commanded in the tabernacle but did their own will — pride, fornication, drunkenness, and other evils the chapter names in part with untranslated words. God’s wrath, it says, will burn upon them like stubble before fire, like fire on the mountains, like a whirlwind lifting the ṣäbäl dəkum [?] from the earth and scattering it until no trace of it is found, and all evildoers will be destroyed from before His face. Against that stands mercy: He loves those who love Him, forgives those who walk in His commandment, and promises rescue from enemies in the day of tribulation to those who make straight their souls, trust in Him, and transgress not His law — His wrath restrained because He knows they are flesh and blood. The chapter then turns to origins and return: souls created from where they were not, returning to earth, and to be brought again into being by His will. It closes with Ṣiruṣayədan, who boasts that his days shall be as the days of heaven and that he makes the sun rise, never to die — until the angel of death, Ṭələmyakos, smites his heart and he dies in that hour, stripped of dwelling and pride, and the Chaldean army destroys and burns his city. The chapter does not explain who Ṣiruṣayədan is or how his story joins the opening woe, and its middle and closing verses are heavily marked [?].
