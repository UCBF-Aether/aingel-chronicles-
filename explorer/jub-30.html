Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 30 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the first year of the sixth week he went up to Salem, to the east of Shechem, in peace in the fourth month.

2 And there they carried off Dinah, the daughter of Jacob, into the house of Shechem, the son of Hamor, the Hivite, the prince of the land, and he lay with her and defiled her, and she was a little girl, a child of twelve years.

3 And he besought his father and her brothers that she might be given to him to wife.

4 And Jacob and his sons were wroth because of the men of Shechem;

5 ' Our author omits all reference to the circumcision of the Shechemites, because he approves of the conduct of Simeon and Levi, and extols it.

6 On the other hand, their conduct in the matter. is severely reprobated in Gen. xlix. 5-7.

7 Our author's view seems to reflect a bitter feeling against the people of Shechem which prevailed in his time.

8 for they had defiled Dinah, their sister, and they spake to them with evil intent and dealt deceitfully with them and beguiled them.

9 And Simeon and Levi came unexpectedly to Shechem and executed judgment on all the men of Shechem, and slew all the men whom they found in it, and left not a single one remaining in it : they slew all in torments because they had dishonoured their sister Dinah.

10 And thus let it not again be done from henceforth that a daughter of Israel be defiled ; for judgment is ordained in heaven against them that they should destroy with the sword all the men of the Shechemites because they had wrought shame in Israel.

11 And the Lord delivered them into the hands of the sons of Jacob that they might exterminate them with the sword and execute judgment upon them, and that it might not thus again be done in Israel that a virgin of Israel should be defiled.

12 And if there is any man who wisheth in Israel to give his daughter or his sister to any man who is of the seed of the Gentiles he shall surely die, and they shall stone him with stones ; for he hath wrought shame in Israel ; and they shall burn the woman with fire, because she hath dishonoured the name of the house of her father, and she shall be rooted out of Israel.

13 And let not an adulteress and no uncleanness be found in Israel throughout all the days of the generations of the earth ; for Israel is holy unto the Lord, and every man who hath defiled (it) shall surely die : they shall stone him with stones.

14 For thus hath it been ordained and written in the heavenly tables regarding all the seed of Israel : he who defileth (it) shall surely die, and he shall be stoned with stones.

15 And to this law there is no limit of days,,and no remission, nor any atonement : but the man who hath defiled his daughter shall be rooted out in the midst of all Israel, because he hath

16 given of his seed to Moloch and wrought impiously so as to defile it.

17 And do thou, Moses, command the children of Israel and exhort them not to give their daughters to the Gentiles, and not to take for their sons any of the daughters of the Gentiles, for this is abominable before the Lord.

18 For this reason I have written for thee in the words of the Law all the deeds of the Shechemites, which they wrought against Dinah, and how the sons of Jacob spake, saying : " We shall not give our daughter to a man who is uncircumcised ; for that were a reproach unto us." 13.

19 And it is a reproach to Israel, to those who give, and to those who take the daughters of the Gentiles ; for this is unclean and abominable to Israel. 14.

20 And Israel will not be free from this uncleanness if it hath a wife of the daughters of the Gentiles, or hath given any of its daughters to a man who is of any of the Gentiles. .15.

21 For there will be plague upon plague, and curse upon curse, and every judgment and plague and curse will come (upon him) : if he do this thing, or hide his eyes from those who commit uncleanness, or those who defile the sanctuary of the Lord, or those who profane His holy name (then) will the whole nation together be judged for all the uncleanness and profanation of this (man).

22 And there will be no respect of persons [and no consideration of persons],' and no receiving at his hands of fruits and offerings and burnt-offerings and fat, nor the fragrance of sweet savour, so as to accept it: and so fare every man or woman in Israel who defileth the sanctuary.

23 For

24 this reason I have commanded thee, saying : " Testify this testimony to Israel : see how the Shechemites fared and their sons : how they were delivered into the hands of two sons of Jacob, and they slew them under tortures, and it was (reckoned) unto them for righteousness, and it is written down to them for righteousness.

25 And the seed of Levi was chosen for the priesthood, and to be Levites, that they might minister before the Lord, as we, continually, and that Levi and his sons may be blessed for ever ; for he was zealous to execute righteousness and judgment and vengeance on all those who arose against Israel.

26 And so they inscribe as a testimony in his favour on the heavenly tables blessing and righteousness before the God of all : And we remember the righteousness which the man fulfilled during his life, at all periods of the year; until a thousand generations they will record it, and it will come to him and to his descendants after him, and he hath been recorded on the heavenly tables as a friend and a righteous man.

27 All this account I have written for thee, and have commanded thee to say to the children of Israel, that they should not commit sin nor transgress the ordinances nor break the covenant which hath been ordained for them, (but) that they should fulfil it and be recorded as friends.

28 But if they transgress and work uncleanness in every way, they will be recorded on the heavenly tables as adversaries, and they will be destroyed out of the book of life and they will be recorded in the book of those who will be destroyed and with those who will be rooted out of the earth.

29 And on the

31 day when the sons of Jacob slew Shechem a writing was recorded in their favour in heaven that they had executed righteousness and uprightness and vengeance on the sinners, and it was written for a blessing.

32 And they brought Dinah, their sister, out of the house of Shechem, and they took captive everything that was in Shechem, their sheep and their oxen and their asses, and all their wealth, and all their flocks, and brought them all to Jacob their father.

33 And he reproached them because they had put the city to the sword ; for he feared those who dwelt in the land, the Canaanites and the Perizzites.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the first year of the sixth week Jacob went up to Salem, east of Shechem, in peace, in the fourth month. Henry notes that Jacob came to Shalem and built an altar there, keeping up religion and the worship of God in his family wherever he pitched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Dinah, Jacob's daughter, was carried off into the house of Shechem, son of Hamor the Hivite, prince of the land, who lay with her and defiled her; she was a little girl of twelve. Henry warns that young persons are never so safe as under the care of pious parents, and that Dinah's gadding abroad among strangers to true religion exposed her to great danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Shechem besought his father and her brothers that Dinah might be given to him as wife. The request after the deed could not undo the defilement, and Henry traces the chain that follows: foolish pleasures lead to seduction, seduction to wrath, wrath to revenge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Jacob and his sons were wroth because of the men of Shechem. The anger was shared by the whole house, since the shame touched the family name, not only the girl herself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. This unit is not narrative but the translator's footnote: Charles explains that Jubilees omits all reference to the circumcision of the Shechemites because the author approves of Simeon and Levi's conduct and extols it. The note is essential to reading what follows, since it states the book's stance outright. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 6. This unit continues Charles's footnote, contrasting Jubilees with Genesis: the brothers' conduct is severely reprobated in Genesis 49:5-7. The same deed is thus judged in opposite ways by the two books, and the reader should not mistake Jubilees' praise for the canonical verdict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. This unit closes Charles's footnote, suggesting the author's view reflects a bitter feeling against the people of Shechem prevailing in his time. The praise of the vengeance is therefore historically situated, not a timeless moral rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. The sons spoke to the Shechemites with evil intent, dealing deceitfully and beguiling them. Henry's account of the chapter stresses the treachery: the Shechemites submitted to the sacred rite only to serve a turn, while Simeon and Levi answered deceit with a worse deceit ending in murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Simeon and Levi came unexpectedly to Shechem, executed judgment on all its men, and left not one remaining, slaying them in torments because they had dishonoured Dinah. Henry judges the brothers most unrighteous, warning that those who act wickedly under pretext of religion are the worst enemies of the truth; Jubilees, by contrast, records the slaughter as judgment, and the note must hold both verdicts in view. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jubilees turns the event into a standing ordinance: let it not again be done that a daughter of Israel be defiled, for judgment is ordained in heaven against such men. Henry's warning underlies the ordinance's concern, that acquaintance with the Canaanites and their ways ends in blood, though Henry himself condemns the brothers' revenge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The Lord is said to have delivered the Shechemites into the sons of Jacob, that they might exterminate them and execute judgment, so that no virgin of Israel should again be defiled. The verse states Jubilees' doctrine plainly: the vengeance was God's judgment delivered by human hands, a claim Henry's commentary on Genesis does not endorse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Let no adulteress and no uncleanness be found in Israel through all generations, for Israel is holy to the Lord. Henry's warning against needless exposure to danger and against learning Canaanite ways grounds the call to holiness, even where the penalties Jubilees attaches are its own law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Moses is commanded to exhort Israel not to give their daughters to the Gentiles nor take Gentile daughters for their sons, for this is abominable before the Lord. Henry's rule from the Jacob stories points the same way: those who profess religion should not marry with those that care not for religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The Law is said to record all the deeds of the Shechemites against Dinah, and the sons' words, that they would not give their daughter to an uncircumcised man, since that would be a reproach. The verse anchors Jubilees' marriage law in the Shechem story itself, as a warning written into the Law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. It is a reproach to Israel, to givers and takers alike, to exchange daughters with the Gentiles, for this is unclean and abominable to Israel. Henry's account of Dinah's gadding shows the same fear: acquaintance with the daughters of the land led to defilement and then to blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Israel will not be free from this uncleanness if it has a Gentile wife or has given a daughter to a Gentile. Henry's chain applies: one sin leads on to another, and unlawful commerce between the sexes ends, more than any other sin, in blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Moses is told to testify to Israel how the Shechemites fared: they were delivered into the hands of two sons of Jacob, slain under tortures, and it was reckoned to them for righteousness. The verse is Jubilees' own verdict, opposite to Henry's judgment that Simeon and Levi were most unrighteous, and the study note records the disagreement rather than hiding it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. All this account is written and commanded to the children of Israel, that they should not sin, transgress the ordinances, or break the covenant, but fulfil it and be recorded as friends. Henry's general warning fits the exhortation: carefully avoid all occasions of sin and approaches to it, for the beginning of sin is as the letting forth of water. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. On the day the sons of Jacob slew Shechem, a writing was recorded in their favour in heaven, that they had executed righteousness, uprightness, and vengeance on the sinners, and it was written for a blessing. Jubilees thus closes the episode with a heavenly record of approval, where Genesis closes it with Jacob's rebuke and a dying father's curse on their anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The brothers brought Dinah out of Shechem's house and took captive everything in the city, sheep, oxen, asses, wealth, and flocks, bringing them all to Jacob. Henry notes that murder in this chapter was followed by other lawless actions, the plunder completing the desolation that began with seduction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Jacob reproached his sons because they had put the city to the sword, fearing the Canaanites and Perizzites who dwelt in the land. Henry's account of the next chapter explains the sequel: though the Canaanites were angry, they were kept back by divine power, and the way of duty proved the way of safety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The dread of the Lord fell on all the cities around Shechem, and they did not rise to pursue Jacob's sons, for terror had fallen on them. Henry notes that God governs the world more by secret terrors on men's minds than we are aware of, and that while Jacob was about God's work he was under special protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Reasonable disagreements, side by side

The study notes for this chapter record a genuine disagreement about the Shechem deed, and this page does not hide it:

  • Jubilees' verdict. The deed is reckoned to Simeon and Levi for righteousness, and Moses is told to testify to Israel how the Shechemites fared at their hands. Interpreted Study note for verse 24, summarizing Matthew Henry (reporting Jubilees' verdict)
  • The Genesis verdict, as Charles's footnote states it. The brothers' conduct is severely reprobated in Genesis 49:5-7, so the same deed is judged in opposite ways by the two books, and the reader should not mistake Jubilees' praise for the canonical verdict. Interpreted Study note for verse 6, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Henry's judgement. Henry judges Simeon and Levi most unrighteous for the deed, opposite to Jubilees' verdict. Interpreted Study note for verse 24, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unresolved Which verdict a reader adopts is not adjudicated on this page; the disagreement itself is the documented fact, and it rests on the two books' different judgements of one deed.
