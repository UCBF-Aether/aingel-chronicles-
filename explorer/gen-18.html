The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD appeared unto him in the plains of Mamre: and he sat in the tent door in the heat of the day;

2 And he lift up his eyes and looked, and, lo, three men stood by him: and when he saw them, he ran to meet them from the tent door, and bowed himself toward the ground,

3 And said, My LORD, if now I have found favour in thy sight, pass not away, I pray thee, from thy servant:

4 Let a little water, I pray you, be fetched, and wash your feet, and rest yourselves under the tree:

5 And I will fetch a morsel of bread, and comfort ye your hearts; after that ye shall pass on: for therefore are ye come to your servant. And they said, So do, as thou hast said.

6 And Abraham hastened into the tent unto Sarah, and said, Make ready quickly three measures of fine meal, knead it, and make cakes upon the hearth.

7 And Abraham ran unto the herd, and fetcht a calf tender and good, and gave it unto a young man; and he hasted to dress it.

8 And he took butter, and milk, and the calf which he had dressed, and set it before them; and he stood by them under the tree, and they did eat.

9 And they said unto him, Where is Sarah thy wife? And he said, Behold, in the tent.

10 And he said, I will certainly return unto thee according to the time of life; and, lo, Sarah thy wife shall have a son. And Sarah heard it in the tent door, which was behind him.

11 Now Abraham and Sarah were old and well stricken in age; and it ceased to be with Sarah after the manner of women.

12 Therefore Sarah laughed within herself, saying, After I am waxed old shall I have pleasure, my lord being old also?

13 And the LORD said unto Abraham, Wherefore did Sarah laugh, saying, Shall I of a surety bear a child, which am old?

14 Is any thing too hard for the LORD? At the time appointed I will return unto thee, according to the time of life, and Sarah shall have a son.

15 Then Sarah denied, saying, I laughed not; for she was afraid. And he said, Nay; but thou didst laugh.

16 And the men rose up from thence, and looked toward Sodom: and Abraham went with them to bring them on the way.

17 And the LORD said, Shall I hide from Abraham that thing which I do;

18 Seeing that Abraham shall surely become a great and mighty nation, and all the nations of the earth shall be blessed in him?

19 For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do justice and judgment; that the LORD may bring upon Abraham that which he hath spoken of him.

20 And the LORD said, Because the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and because their sin is very grievous;

21 I will go down now, and see whether they have done altogether according to the cry of it, which is come unto me; and if not, I will know.

22 And the men turned their faces from thence, and went toward Sodom: but Abraham stood yet before the LORD.

23 And Abraham drew near, and said, Wilt thou also destroy the righteous with the wicked?

24 Peradventure there be fifty righteous within the city: wilt thou also destroy and not spare the place for the fifty righteous that are therein?

25 That be far from thee to do after this manner, to slay the righteous with the wicked: and that the righteous should be as the wicked, that be far from thee: Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?

26 And the LORD said, If I find in Sodom fifty righteous within the city, then I will spare all the place for their sakes.

27 And Abraham answered and said, Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the LORD, which am but dust and ashes:

28 Peradventure there shall lack five of the fifty righteous: wilt thou destroy all the city for lack of five? And he said, If I find there forty and five, I will not destroy it.

29 And he spake unto him yet again, and said, Peradventure there shall be forty found there. And he said, I will not do it for forty’s sake.

30 And he said unto him, Oh let not the LORD be angry, and I will speak: Peradventure there shall thirty be found there. And he said, I will not do it, if I find thirty there.

31 And he said, Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the LORD: Peradventure there shall be twenty found there. And he said, I will not destroy it for twenty’s sake.

32 And he said, Oh let not the LORD be angry, and I will speak yet but this once: Peradventure ten shall be found there. And he said, I will not destroy it for ten’s sake.

33 And the LORD went his way, as soon as he had left communing with Abraham: and Abraham returned unto his place.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The LORD appears to Abraham at Mamre in the form of three visitors, who are given food and promise that Sarah will bear a son within the year, at which Sarah laughs. As the visitors turn toward Sodom, Abraham pleads with God, bargaining down from fifty righteous to ten, that the city might be spared for their sake. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 11. Abraham and Sarah were both old and well advanced in years, and Sarah had long passed childbearing age, so the promise faced every ordinary impossibility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Sarah laughed to herself at the thought of pleasure and a child in her old age, with her lord also old, a laugh Henry reads as mingled wonder and doubt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. God asked why Sarah laughed and whether anything was too hard for him, turning her private laugh into a public lesson about God's power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The question, whether anything is too hard for God, stands as the chapter's key: the promise did not depend on Sarah's age but on God's ability. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Sarah denied laughing, because she was afraid, but was told plainly that she had laughed, showing that nothing spoken or thought in the tent was hidden from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The men rose and looked toward Sodom, and Abraham went with them to send them on their way, as a host escorting honoured guests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. God asked whether he should hide from Abraham what he was about to do, treating his friend as a confidant rather than keeping judgment secret. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The reason given was Abraham's future: he would become a great nation and all nations would be blessed in him, so he was fit to know God's dealings with the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. God said he knew Abraham would command his household after him to keep God's way in justice and righteousness, so that the promises could be fulfilled through a taught and obedient family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah was great and their sin very grave, a cry to heaven for justice that demanded God's personal inquiry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. God said he would go down and see whether the outcry matched the facts, a human way of speaking that pictures God's judgment as careful and first-hand, never hasty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The two men turned toward Sodom while Abraham remained standing before God, left alone with the judge of all the earth to plead for the cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Abraham drew near and asked whether God would sweep away the righteous with the wicked, the bold question on which his whole intercession rests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. He asked whether fifty righteous people in the city would save it, arguing that a just judge would spare a whole city for the sake of the good within it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. He pressed that it would be far from God to slay the righteous with the wicked, treating them alike, since the judge of all the earth must do right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. God agreed to spare the city for fifty righteous, showing that Abraham's reasoning about justice was right and that God welcomes bold, humble pleading. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Abraham answered that he was only dust and ashes, yet ventured to speak, a model of intercession that joins lowliness with persistence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. He asked whether five fewer, forty-five righteous, would still save the city, and God agreed, so the pleading moved downward step by step. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. He went on to forty, and God agreed again, and Henry notes that each concession encouraged Abraham to ask further rather than presuming too far at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. He asked for thirty, and God agreed, the number falling while God's patience in hearing held steady. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. He asked for twenty, and God agreed, so the intercession kept narrowing toward the small remnant that might yet be found. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. He asked one last time, for ten, and God agreed to spare the city for ten righteous, the lowest number Abraham dared to name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. God left off speaking and Abraham returned home, and JFB notes he stopped either because he thought the cities now safe, or because God restrained further pleading. In fact, as the next chapter shows, there were not ten righteous there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
