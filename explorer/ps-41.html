The Text — Psalms 41 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Blessed is he that considereth the poor: the LORD will deliver him in time of trouble.

2 The LORD will preserve him, and keep him alive; and he shall be blessed upon the earth: and thou wilt not deliver him unto the will of his enemies.

3 The LORD will strengthen him upon the bed of languishing: thou wilt make all his bed in his sickness.

4 I said, LORD, be merciful unto me: heal my soul; for I have sinned against thee.

5 Mine enemies speak evil of me, When shall he die, and his name perish?

6 And if he come to see me, he speaketh vanity: his heart gathereth iniquity to itself; when he goeth abroad, he telleth it.

7 All that hate me whisper together against me: against me do they devise my hurt.

8 An evil disease, say they, cleaveth fast unto him: and now that he lieth he shall rise up no more.

9 Yea, mine own familiar friend, in whom I trusted, which did eat of my bread, hath lifted up his heel against me.

10 But thou, O LORD, be merciful unto me, and raise me up, that I may requite them.

11 By this I know that thou favourest me, because mine enemy doth not triumph over me.

12 And as for me, thou upholdest me in mine integrity, and settest me before thy face for ever.

13 Blessed be the LORD God of Israel from everlasting, and to everlasting. Amen, and Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Blessed is the one who considers the poor, preserved, kept alive, tended by God himself on the sickbed, set against enemies who wish his name to perish, whispering conspirators, and even a familiar friend who ate his bread lifting his heel. Raised up, the enemy does not triumph, and the first book of Psalms closes: Blessed be the Lord God of Israel from everlasting to everlasting. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Blessed is he who considers the poor; the Lord will deliver him in time of trouble: consideration of the poor is blessed, and its reward is deliverance in trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The Lord will preserve him and keep him alive; he shall be blessed upon the earth, and you will not deliver him to the will of his enemies: preservation, life, earthly blessing, and safety from enemies' will are the fourfold reward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The Lord will strengthen him on the bed of languishing; you will make all his bed in his sickness: God himself tends the sickbed, making the bed of the languishing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. I said, Lord, be merciful to me; heal my soul, for I have sinned against you: mercy and healing of the soul are asked, with sin against God confessed as the sickness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. My enemies speak evil of me: When shall he die, and his name perish? Enemies wish not only death but the perishing of his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And if he comes to see me, he speaks vanity; his heart gathers iniquity to itself; when he goes abroad, he tells it: the visiting enemy is a spy — vanity spoken, iniquity gathered, gossip spread abroad. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. All who hate me whisper together against me; against me they devise my hurt: whispering together is conspiracy, and its object is his hurt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. An evil disease, say they, cleaves fast to him; and now that he lies he shall rise up no more: they pronounce his disease incurable and his lying down final. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Yea, my own familiar friend, in whom I trusted, who ate of my bread, has lifted up his heel against me: the trusted bread-eating friend's lifted heel is the deepest treachery — the words Jesus applies to Judas. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. But you, O Lord, be merciful to me, and raise me up, that I may requite them: mercy and raising up are asked, with requital — just repayment — as the stated end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. By this I know that you favour me, because my enemy does not triumph over me: the enemy's failure to triumph is the sign of God's favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And as for me, you uphold me in my integrity, and set me before your face for ever: upheld integrity ends in God's face for ever — presence as the final reward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Blessed be the Lord God of Israel from everlasting to everlasting. Amen, and Amen: the first book of Psalms closes in blessing, from everlasting to everlasting, with a double Amen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
