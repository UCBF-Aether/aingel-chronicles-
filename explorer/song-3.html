The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Song of Songs 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 By night on my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not.

2 I will rise now, and go about the city in the streets, and in the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not.

3 The watchmen that go about the city found me: to whom I said, Saw ye him whom my soul loveth?

4 It was but a little that I passed from them, but I found him whom my soul loveth: I held him, and would not let him go, until I had brought him into my mother’s house, and into the chamber of her that conceived me.

5 I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, and by the hinds of the field, that ye stir not up, nor awake my love, till he please.

6 Who is this that cometh out of the wilderness like pillars of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense, with all powders of the merchant?

7 Behold his bed, which is Solomon’s; threescore valiant men are about it, of the valiant of Israel.

8 They all hold swords, being expert in war: every man hath his sword upon his thigh because of fear in the night.

9 King Solomon made himself a chariot of the wood of Lebanon.

10 He made the pillars thereof of silver, the bottom thereof of gold, the covering of it of purple, the midst thereof being paved with love, for the daughters of Jerusalem.

11 Go forth, O ye daughters of Zion, and behold king Solomon with the crown wherewith his mother crowned him in the day of his espousals, and in the day of the gladness of his heart.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

By night the beloved searches her bed, the streets and the watchmen for the one her soul loves, finds him, and will not let him go. The scene then shifts to a royal procession out of the wilderness, Solomon's guarded bed and his chariot of Lebanon wood, silver and gold, crowned on his wedding day. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. By night on my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth; I sought him, but I found him not. The night is a time of coldness, darkness, and drowsiness, Henry says, and at first, when uneasy, only feeble efforts are made to obtain the comfort of communion with Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. I will rise now and go about the city in the streets and broad ways to seek him whom my soul loveth; I sought him, but I found him not. The believer is then roused to increased diligence, Henry notes, and the streets and broad-ways imply the means of grace in which the Lord is to be sought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The watchmen that go about the city found me, and I asked, saw ye him whom my soul loveth? Application is made to those who watch for men's souls, Henry says, but immediate satisfaction is not found, for we must not rest in any means but by faith apply directly to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. It was but a little that I passed from them, but I found him whom my soul loveth; I held him and would not let him go until I had brought him into my mother's house. The holding of Christ and not letting him go denotes earnest cleaving to him, Henry says, and what prevails is humble, ardent suing by prayer with lively faith on his promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, that ye stir not up nor awake my love till he please. Wherever we find Christ, Henry says, we must take him home with us to our houses, especially to our hearts, and beware of grieving our holy Comforter and provoking the departure of the Beloved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Who is this that comes out of the wilderness like pillars of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense? A wilderness is an emblem of the world, Henry explains; the believer comes out of it when delivered from the love of its sinful pleasures and pursuits, to seek happiness in communion with the Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Behold his bed, which is Solomon's; threescore valiant men are about it, of the valiant of Israel. He who is the Peace of his people, the King of the heavenly Zion, has provided for the safe conveyance of his redeemed through the wilderness of this world, Henry says. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. They all hold swords, being expert in war, every man with his sword upon his thigh because of fear in the night. The church is well guarded, Henry notes; more are with her than are against her, and believers reposing in Christ, though they have fears in the night, are yet safe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. King Solomon made himself a chariot of the wood of Lebanon. The chariot denotes the covenant of redemption, the way of our salvation, Henry says: the work of Christ which makes him loved and admired in the eyes of believers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. He made its pillars of silver, its bottom of gold, its covering of purple, the midst thereof being paved with love, for the daughters of Jerusalem. The blood of the covenant, that rich purple, is the cover of this chariot, Henry explains, sheltering believers from divine wrath and the troubles of this world, while its midst is the love of Christ which passes knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Go forth, O ye daughters of Zion, and behold King Solomon with the crown wherewith his mother crowned him in the day of his espousals, and in the day of the gladness of his heart. Take special notice of his crown, Henry urges: applied to Christ, it speaks the honour put upon him and his power and dominion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
