The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then he called his twelve disciples together, and gave them power and authority over all devils, and to cure diseases.

2 And he sent them to preach the kingdom of God, and to heal the sick.

3 And he said unto them, Take nothing for your journey, neither staves, nor scrip, neither bread, neither money; neither have two coats apiece.

4 And whatsoever house ye enter into, there abide, and thence depart.

5 And whosoever will not receive you, when ye go out of that city, shake off the very dust from your feet for a testimony against them.

6 And they departed, and went through the towns, preaching the gospel, and healing every where.

7 Now Herod the tetrarch heard of all that was done by him: and he was perplexed, because that it was said of some, that John was risen from the dead;

8 And of some, that Elias had appeared; and of others, that one of the old prophets was risen again.

9 And Herod said, John have I beheaded: but who is this, of whom I hear such things? And he desired to see him.

10 And the apostles, when they were returned, told him all that they had done. And he took them, and went aside privately into a desert place belonging to the city called Bethsaida.

11 And the people, when they knew it, followed him: and he received them, and spake unto them of the kingdom of God, and healed them that had need of healing.

12 And when the day began to wear away, then came the twelve, and said unto him, Send the multitude away, that they may go into the towns and country round about, and lodge, and get victuals: for we are here in a desert place.

13 But he said unto them, Give ye them to eat. And they said, We have no more but five loaves and two fishes; except we should go and buy meat for all this people.

14 For they were about five thousand men. And he said to his disciples, Make them sit down by fifties in a company.

15 And they did so, and made them all sit down.

16 Then he took the five loaves and the two fishes, and looking up to heaven, he blessed them, and brake, and gave to the disciples to set before the multitude.

17 And they did eat, and were all filled: and there was taken up of fragments that remained to them twelve baskets.

18 And it came to pass, as he was alone praying, his disciples were with him: and he asked them, saying, Whom say the people that I am?

19 They answering said, John the Baptist; but some say, Elias; and others say, that one of the old prophets is risen again.

20 He said unto them, But whom say ye that I am? Peter answering said, The Christ of God.

21 And he straitly charged them, and commanded them to tell no man that thing;

22 Saying, The Son of man must suffer many things, and be rejected of the elders and chief priests and scribes, and be slain, and be raised the third day.

23 And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.

24 For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: but whosoever will lose his life for my sake, the same shall save it.

25 For what is a man advantaged, if he gain the whole world, and lose himself, or be cast away?

26 For whosoever shall be ashamed of me and of my words, of him shall the Son of man be ashamed, when he shall come in his own glory, and in his Father’s, and of the holy angels.

27 But I tell you of a truth, there be some standing here, which shall not taste of death, till they see the kingdom of God.

28 And it came to pass about an eight days after these sayings, he took Peter and John and James, and went up into a mountain to pray.

29 And as he prayed, the fashion of his countenance was altered, and his raiment was white and glistering.

30 And, behold, there talked with him two men, which were Moses and Elias:

31 Who appeared in glory, and spake of his decease which he should accomplish at Jerusalem.

32 But Peter and they that were with him were heavy with sleep: and when they were awake, they saw his glory, and the two men that stood with him.

33 And it came to pass, as they departed from him, Peter said unto Jesus, Master, it is good for us to be here: and let us make three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elias: not knowing what he said.

34 While he thus spake, there came a cloud, and overshadowed them: and they feared as they entered into the cloud.

35 And there came a voice out of the cloud, saying, This is my beloved Son: hear him.

36 And when the voice was past, Jesus was found alone. And they kept it close, and told no man in those days any of those things which they had seen.

37 And it came to pass, that on the next day, when they were come down from the hill, much people met him.

38 And, behold, a man of the company cried out, saying, Master, I beseech thee, look upon my son: for he is mine only child.

39 And, lo, a spirit taketh him, and he suddenly crieth out; and it teareth him that he foameth again, and bruising him hardly departeth from him.

40 And I besought thy disciples to cast him out; and they could not.

41 And Jesus answering said, O faithless and perverse generation, how long shall I be with you, and suffer you? Bring thy son hither.

42 And as he was yet a coming, the devil threw him down, and tare him. And Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, and healed the child, and delivered him again to his father.

43 And they were all amazed at the mighty power of God. But while they wondered every one at all things which Jesus did, he said unto his disciples,

44 Let these sayings sink down into your ears: for the Son of man shall be delivered into the hands of men.

45 But they understood not this saying, and it was hid from them, that they perceived it not: and they feared to ask him of that saying.

46 Then there arose a reasoning among them, which of them should be greatest.

47 And Jesus, perceiving the thought of their heart, took a child, and set him by him,

48 And said unto them, Whosoever shall receive this child in my name receiveth me: and whosoever shall receive me receiveth him that sent me: for he that is least among you all, the same shall be great.

49 And John answered and said, Master, we saw one casting out devils in thy name; and we forbad him, because he followeth not with us.

50 And Jesus said unto him, Forbid him not: for he that is not against us is for us.

51 And it came to pass, when the time was come that he should be received up, he stedfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem,

52 And sent messengers before his face: and they went, and entered into a village of the Samaritans, to make ready for him.

53 And they did not receive him, because his face was as though he would go to Jerusalem.

54 And when his disciples James and John saw this, they said, Lord, wilt thou that we command fire to come down from heaven, and consume them, even as Elias did?

55 But he turned, and rebuked them, and said, Ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of.

56 For the Son of man is not come to destroy men’s lives, but to save them. And they went to another village.

57 And it came to pass, that, as they went in the way, a certain man said unto him, Lord, I will follow thee whithersoever thou goest.

58 And Jesus said unto him, Foxes have holes, and birds of the air have nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head.

59 And he said unto another, Follow me. But he said, Lord, suffer me first to go and bury my father.

60 Jesus said unto him, Let the dead bury their dead: but go thou and preach the kingdom of God.

61 And another also said, Lord, I will follow thee; but let me first go bid them farewell, which are at home at my house.

62 And Jesus said unto him, No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The twelve are sent to preach and heal with nothing for the journey (vv. 1–6); Herod wonders who this is, since John has been beheaded (vv. 7–9). Jesus feeds about five thousand from five loaves and two fishes (vv. 10–17), Peter confesses him as the Christ of God, and Jesus predicts his suffering and teaches daily cross-bearing (vv. 18–27). About eight days later Peter, John, and James see him transfigured on a mountain, talking with Moses and Elijah (vv. 28–36). A demon-possessed boy is healed, greatness is corrected with a child, a stranger casting out devils is left alone, Samaritans reject him, and would-be followers are warned what following costs (vv. 37–62). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 9

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He called the twelve together and gave them power and authority over all devils, and to cure diseases. Henry notes they were now able to teach others what they had received, and Jesus is the fountain of power and authority to whom all creatures must be subject. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He sent them to preach the kingdom of God and to heal the sick. Preaching and healing go together as the mission's two hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Take nothing for the journey, neither staves, scrip, bread, money, nor two coats apiece. Henry notes they must not commend themselves by outward appearance, but go as they were, trusting that he who goes with his ministers in power will care for their wants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Whatever house ye enter, there abide, and thence depart. Contentment with one lodging is part of the mission's discipline. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Whoever will not receive you, shake off the very dust from your feet for a testimony against them when ye leave that city. Henry warns that where truth and love go together yet the message is rejected, it leaves men without excuse and turns to testimony against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. They departed, went through towns, preaching the gospel and healing everywhere. The mission in practice matches the commission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Herod heard all that was done and was perplexed, because some said John was risen from the dead. Henry notes Herod's guilty conscience was ready to conclude John risen, since he had killed him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Some said Elijah had appeared, others that one of the old prophets was risen again. Rumour tries to fit Jesus into familiar prophetic shapes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Herod said, John have I beheaded, but who is this of whom I hear such things? And he desired to see him. Henry asks why he did not go and see him: probably pride, or unwillingness to meet another reprover of sin, and delay hardened his heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The apostles returned and told him all they had done, and he took them aside privately to a desert place belonging to Bethsaida. Report and retreat follow mission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The people followed him, and he received them, spoke of the kingdom of God, and healed those needing healing. Henry notes though they came unseasonably, he gave them what they came for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. When day wore away, the twelve said, send the multitude away to lodge and get victuals in towns round about, for we are in a desert place. The disciples' practical solution is dismissal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Give ye them to eat. They answered, we have no more but five loaves and two fishes, except we buy meat for all this people. The command exposes the gap between need and resource. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. They were about five thousand men, and he said, make them sit down by fifties in a company. Order precedes miracle: the crowd is arranged before it is fed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. They did so and made them all sit down. Obedience in arrangement is the disciples' part of the feeding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He took the five loaves and two fishes, looked up to heaven, blessed, broke, and gave to the disciples to set before the multitude. Henry notes creature-comforts must be acknowledged as received from God, unworthily received, and owed wholly to Christ's mediation, by whom the curse is taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. They ate and were all filled, and twelve baskets of fragments remained. Henry notes Christ's blessing makes a little go a great way, fills every hungry soul abundantly, and in our Father's house there is bread enough and to spare; we are not straitened in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. As he was alone praying, with disciples by, he asked, whom say the people that I am? Prayer precedes the great confession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. They answered, John the Baptist, some say Elijah, others one of the old prophets risen again. Public opinion repeats Herod's rumours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. But whom say ye that I am? Peter answered, The Christ of God. Henry calls it unspeakable comfort that Jesus is God's Anointed, appointed to be Messiah and qualified for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. He straitly charged and commanded them to tell no man that thing. The confession is true but premature for proclamation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The Son of man must suffer many things, be rejected by elders, chief priests, and scribes, be slain, and be raised the third day. Henry notes Jesus discourses concerning his own sufferings, and disciples must prepare for their own, not plot to prevent his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross daily, and follow me. Henry notes crosses meet us in the way of duty: not to be pulled upon our heads, but when laid for us, to be taken up and carried after Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Whosoever will save his life shall lose it, but whosoever will lose his life for my sake shall save it. Saving and losing are reversed by his sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. What is a man advantaged, if he gain the whole world and lose himself, or be cast away? Henry notes it is well or ill with us according as it is well or ill with our souls; the body cannot be happy if the soul is miserable in the other world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Whosoever shall be ashamed of me and my words, of him shall the Son of man be ashamed, when he comes in his own glory, his Father's, and the holy angels'. Henry adds we must never be ashamed of Christ and his gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Some standing here shall not taste death till they see the kingdom of God. The kingdom's coming is near enough for hearers to witness it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. About eight days after, he took Peter, John, and James up into a mountain to pray. The transfiguration is framed, like the confession, by prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. As he prayed, the fashion of his countenance was altered, and his raiment was white and glistering. Henry notes prayer is a transfiguring, transforming duty, which makes the face shine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Behold, two men talked with him, Moses and Elijah. Law and prophets stand with the Son on the mountain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. They appeared in glory and spoke of his decease to be accomplished at Jerusalem. Henry notes even in transfiguration Jesus was willing to speak concerning his death; in our greatest earthly glories, remember we have no continuing city here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Peter and they with him were heavy with sleep, but when awake they saw his glory and the two men standing with him. Henry notes the disciples, after a while awake, could give a full account of what passed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. As they departed, Peter said, Master, it is good for us to be here; let us make three tabernacles, for thee, Moses, and Elijah, not knowing what he said. Henry notes those who talk of making tabernacles on earth for glorified saints in heaven know not what they say. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. While he spoke, a cloud overshadowed them, and they feared as they entered the cloud. Glory that invites tabernacles also inspires fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. A voice out of the cloud said, This is my beloved Son: hear him. The Father's command replaces Peter's plan: listen, do not build. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. When the voice was past, Jesus was found alone, and they kept it close and told no man in those days what they had seen. Transfiguration is a specimen, Henry says, of the glory in which he will come to judge the world, and an encouragement to suffer for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. On the next day, coming down from the hill, much people met him. Mountain glory descends to a waiting crowd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. A man cried out, Master, look upon my son, for he is mine only child. The father's plea names the child's uniqueness, as Jairus's did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. A spirit takes him, he suddenly cries out, it tears him till he foams, and bruising him hardly departs. Henry notes how deplorable the child's case, under an evil spirit, more frightful than natural disease, and what mischief Satan does where he gets possession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. I besought thy disciples to cast him out, and they could not. The disciples' failure is confessed openly before the crowd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. O faithless and perverse generation, how long shall I be with you and suffer you? Bring thy son hither. The rebuke falls on the generation, and the command brings the boy to the one who can heal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. As he was yet coming, the devil threw him down and tore him; Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, healed the child, and delivered him again to his father. Henry notes a word from Christ healed the child, and happy are those with access to Christ, who can do what his disciples cannot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. All were amazed at the mighty power of God, but while they wondered at all Jesus did, he said to his disciples, let these sayings sink into your ears, for the Son of man shall be delivered into men's hands. Wonder at power must make room for the prediction of suffering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Let these sayings sink down into your ears: for the Son of man shall be delivered into the hands of men. The saying is repeated because it will not sink in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. They understood not this saying, it was hid from them that they perceived it not, and they feared to ask him. Henry notes the prediction was plain enough, but they would not understand it because it agreed not with their notions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Then there arose a reasoning among them, which of them should be greatest. From a prediction of suffering they turn at once to a contest for greatness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Jesus, perceiving the thought of their heart, took a child and set him by him. The answer to ambition is a child placed beside him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Whosoever receives this child in my name receives me, and whosoever receives me receives him that sent me; for he that is least among you all, the same shall be great. Henry notes a little child is the emblem by which Christ teaches simplicity and humility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. John answered, Master, we saw one casting out devils in thy name, and we forbade him, because he follows not with us. Exclusiveness follows ambition: outsiders doing the work must be stopped. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Forbid him not, for he that is not against us is for us. Henry notes that even the twelve, with reason to silence outsiders if any had, were warned not to; faithful followers may be found who do not follow with us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. When the time came that he should be received up, he steadfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem. The journey to the cross begins with a fixed face. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. He sent messengers before his face, who entered a Samaritan village to make ready for him. Preparation precedes the rejected welcome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. They did not receive him, because his face was as though he would go to Jerusalem. National and religious prejudice, not enmity to God's word alone, shapes the refusal, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. James and John asked, Lord, wilt thou that we command fire from heaven to consume them, even as Elijah did? Zeal borrows Elijah's fire for a Samaritan village. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. He turned and rebuked them, saying, ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of. Henry notes they were ignorant of their own hearts' motives, which were pride and carnal ambition, and the gospel dispensation is marked by miracles of mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. For the Son of man is not come to destroy men's lives, but to save them. And they went to another village. The rebuke's reason is the mission itself: saving, not destroying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 57. As they went, a man said, Lord, I will follow thee whithersoever thou goest. Forward willingness is tested by the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 58. Foxes have holes, and birds have nests, but the Son of man has not where to lay his head. Henry notes the hasty follower had not counted the cost, and must lay aside thoughts of great things in the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 59. He said to another, Follow me; but he said, Lord, suffer me first to go and bury my father. The call is direct, the delay is pious and reasonable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 60. Let the dead bury their dead, but go thou and preach the kingdom of God. Henry notes religion teaches kindness and piety at home, but we must not make these an excuse for neglecting duty to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 61. Another said, I will follow thee, but let me first go bid farewell to those at home. Farewell seems small, yet it holds the heart back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 62. No man, having put his hand to the plough and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God. Henry warns that looking back leads to drawing back, and drawing back to perdition; he only that endures to the end shall be saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Investigation (editorial — a parallel examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a possible parallel. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

Feeding and transfiguration — Luke 9, Mark 6, and Mark 9

Luke 9:10–17 retells the feeding of about five thousand from five loaves and two fishes, with twelve baskets left — the same outline as Mark 6:30–44, though Luke sets it near Bethsaida and omits the night’s sea-walking that follows in Mark. Luke 9:28–36 then retells the transfiguration, agreeing with Mark 9:2–8 on Peter, John, and James, Moses and Elijah, and the voice “This is my beloved Son: hear him,” while differing on the date (“about an eight days” against Mark’s “after six days”) and on the mountain’s purpose (to pray) and the sleep of the disciples. No source consulted for this page establishes a historical relationship between the accounts, and none is claimed here.
