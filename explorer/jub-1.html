How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language, this chapter opens the book on Mount Sinai: in the first year of the exodus, in the third month, God summons Moses up the Mount to receive the tables of the law, and tells him to write down the earlier and later history of the division of the days of the law and of the testimony, warning beforehand of Israel's coming rebellion, forgetfulness, captivity, and eventual recognition that God has been truly with them. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse . The book presents itself as the history of the division of the days, weeks and jubilees from creation to Sinai, spoken to Moses on the Mount. Charles's introduction explains its design: a Pharisaic retelling of Genesis and Exodus showing the law was already in force before its republication on Sinai. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 1. God summons Moses in the first year of the exodus, on the sixteenth day of the third month, for the two tables of the law. Charles notes the author supplies a date where Exodus 19:1 gives none, and distinguishes this sixteenth from the fifteenth, the date of the feast of firstfruits and other patriarchal events. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 2. Moses goes up and the glory of the Lord abides on Sinai under a cloud for six days. Charles traces the passage to Exodus 24:15-18, with the two tables drawn from Exodus 31:18. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 3. On the seventh day God calls Moses from the cloud, and the glory appears like flaming fire on the mountain top. Charles notes that a change of one vowel would give 'devouring' fire, as in Exodus 24:17. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 4. Moses spends forty days and forty nights on the Mount being taught the earlier and the later history of the division of the days. This taught history is the book itself, which claims to record what Moses learned there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 5. God commands Moses to incline his heart to every word and write them in a book, so that later generations may see God had not forsaken them despite their evil. Charles compares Exodus 34:27 and the rabbinic picture of Moses shown all the generations to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 6. When these things come upon them, Israel will recognise that God is more righteous than they in all his judgments and actions. Charles traces the verse to Deuteronomy 30:1: calamity itself will teach them God's justice and his abiding presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. Moses is told to write all these words because God already knows Israel's rebellion and stiff neck before they enter the promised land. Charles cites Deuteronomy 31:27 for the stiff neck and Deuteronomy 30:20 for the land sworn to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. This verse opens the quoted promise of the land sworn to the fathers, the 'saying' that introduces the next verse. The promise of a land flowing with milk and honey comes from Exodus 33:1, 3 and Deuteronomy 31:20. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. The promise itself: to Israel's seed God will give a land flowing with milk and honey. It stands here as the pledge against which the coming apostasy will be measured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. Israel will eat, be satisfied, and turn to strange gods that cannot deliver them from tribulation, and this written witness will testify against them. Charles cites Deuteronomy 31:20 for the pattern of satiety leading to apostasy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. They will forget all God's commandments, walk after the Gentiles and their uncleanness, and serve gods that become an offence, tribulation, affliction and snare. Charles cites Exodus 23:33 and Deuteronomy 7:16 for the snare of serving foreign gods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. This verse is the hinge into the judgment sentence: the apostasy just described now brings perishing and captivity, as the next verse spells out. It opens the first of the two great catastrophes Charles sees depicted in verses 10-18. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. Many will perish and be taken captive and fall to the enemy because they forsook God's ordinances, the festivals of the covenant, the sabbaths, and the holy place, tabernacle and sanctuary. Charles reads this as the partial destruction and captivity of Israel, the northern tribes having forsaken the law, the festivals and the Jerusalem temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. Judah's turn follows: high places, groves and graven images, each worshipping his own image, and children sacrificed to demons. Charles cites Ezekiel 20:28, 2 Chronicles 33:3 and Deuteronomy 32:17 for these idolatries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. God will send witnesses to testify against them, but they will not hear, will slay the witnesses, persecute those who seek the law, and abrogate and change everything to work evil. Charles cites 2 Chronicles 24:19 and 36:15-16 for the rejected prophets, and Nehemiah 9:26 for the slain witnesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. God will hide his face and deliver them to the Gentiles for captivity, prey and devouring, removing and scattering them from the land. Charles cites 2 Kings 21:14 for the delivery into Gentile hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. In captivity they will forget all the law, commandments and judgments, and go astray over new moons, sabbaths, festivals, jubilees and ordinances. Charles notes this is Judah forgetting God's service in captivity, and it is the calendar apostasy the book's own 364-day reckoning is written to correct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. After this they will turn to God from among the Gentiles with all their heart, soul and strength, and he will gather them and be found by them when they seek him. Charles traces the return to Deuteronomy 4:29-30 and Jeremiah 29:13-14. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. This verse completes the return promise of verse 18: seeking God with all their heart and all their soul. Charles cites Jeremiah 29:13-14, where seeking with the whole heart is the condition of being found. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. God will disclose abounding peace with righteousness, plant them as the plant of uprightness, and make them a blessing and not a curse, the head and not the tail. Charles flags the 'plant' wording as corrupt but traces it to Jeremiah 32:41, with Deuteronomy 28:13 and Zechariah 8:13 behind the blessing and headship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. God will build his sanctuary among them, dwell with them, and be their God while they are his people in truth and righteousness. Charles identifies this as the second Temple, citing Exodus 25:8 and 29:45 and Leviticus 26:12. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. The promise closes with God's pledge not to forsake or fail them, because he is the Lord their God. Charles cites Deuteronomy 31:6 for the pledge that frames the whole restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. Moses falls on his face and prays God not to forsake his people and inheritance, nor deliver them to Gentile enemies who would rule them and cause them to sin. Charles compares Deuteronomy 9:26, Psalm 106:41 and 2 Kings 21:14 for the plea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. Moses asks for mercy lifted up on the people, an upright spirit created in them, and the spirit of Beliar kept from ruling over them to accuse and ensnare them from righteousness. Charles notes Beliar here as the accusing spirit, treated further at 15:33, and compares Psalm 51:10 for the created spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. Moses pleads that they are God's people and inheritance, delivered from Egypt with great power, and asks for a clean heart and holy spirit so they will not be ensnared in sin henceforth to eternity. Charles cites Deuteronomy 9:26, 29 for the deliverance plea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. God answers that he knows their contrariness, thoughts and stiffneckedness, and they will not be obedient until they confess their own sin and their fathers' sin. Charles cites Deuteronomy 31:27 for the stiff neck and Leviticus 26:40 and Nehemiah 9:2 for the confession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. This verse completes the condition of verse 27: confession must cover the sin of the fathers as well as their own. Charles cites Leviticus 26:40, where confession of both sins precedes restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. After confession they will turn to God in all uprightness, heart and soul, and he will circumcise the foreskin of their heart and their seed's heart, create a holy spirit in them, and cleanse them to be his people. Charles cites Deuteronomy 10:16 and 30:6 for the circumcised heart and 2 Chronicles 6:38 for the whole-hearted turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. Their souls will cleave to God and all his commandments, they will fulfil them, and he will be their Father and they his children. This is the inward fulfilment of the restoration promised in the preceding verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. They will all be called children of the living God, and every angel and spirit will know them as God's children, with God their Father in uprightness and righteousness who loves them. The restoration ends in a publicly recognised sonship before the whole heavenly host. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. Moses is commanded to write down all these words, first and last, covering all the divisions of days in the law, testimony, weeks and jubilees unto eternity, until God descends and dwells with them. This is the book's own commission, matching the writing command of verses 5 and 27 in Charles's notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. This unit is the section heading marking the turn from prophecy to dictation: God now commands the angel to write. It divides the Sinai prologue from the history the angel will dictate to Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. The angel of the presence is commanded to write for Moses from the beginning of creation until God's sanctuary is built among them for all eternity. That span, creation to Sinai, is exactly the scope of the book that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. The Lord will appear to the eyes of all, opening the universal recognition that completes the verse. The prologue's restoration ends not in private blessing but in God's open manifestation to the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. All will know that he is the God of Israel, the Father of all the children of Jacob, and King on Mount Zion for all eternity. The titles gather the chapter's promises: covenant God, Father of the restored people, and everlasting King in Zion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. Zion and Jerusalem will be holy, the closing note of the prologue vision. The restored sanctuary of verse 22 finds its counterpart in a holy city, fit for the King who dwells there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. The angel of the presence who went before Israel's camp took the tables of the divisions of the years, from creation, of the law and testimony, of weeks and jubilees, by individual years. Charles's introduction explains the claim: the division of days was ordained on the heavenly tablets, so the book's chronology is revealed, not devised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

Unfamiliar terms

  • Jubilee; week of years. Interpreted The book dates its history by divisions of days, weeks, and jubilees (verse 1 and the chapter's opening summary): a “week” here means a week of years, and a jubilee is one of the long periods by which those weeks are grouped. This page states no more about the length of a jubilee than the chapter's own dating language supports. Chapter text, verse 1, above; study notes for this chapter

  • Verse 15. God will send witnesses to testify against them, but they will not hear, will slay the witnesses, persecute those who seek the law, and abrogate and change everything to work evil. Charles cites 2 Chronicles 24:19 and 36:15-16 for the rejected prophets, and Nehemiah 9:26 for the slain witnesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. God will hide his face and deliver them to the Gentiles for captivity, prey and devouring, removing and scattering them from the land. Charles cites 2 Kings 21:14 for the delivery into Gentile hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. In captivity they will forget all the law, commandments and judgments, and go astray over new moons, sabbaths, festivals, jubilees and ordinances. Charles notes this is Judah forgetting God's service in captivity, and it is the calendar apostasy the book's own 364-day reckoning is written to correct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. After this they will turn to God from among the Gentiles with all their heart, soul and strength, and he will gather them and be found by them when they seek him. Charles traces the return to Deuteronomy 4:29-30 and Jeremiah 29:13-14. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. This verse completes the return promise of verse 18: seeking God with all their heart and all their soul. Charles cites Jeremiah 29:13-14, where seeking with the whole heart is the condition of being found. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. God will disclose abounding peace with righteousness, plant them as the plant of uprightness, and make them a blessing and not a curse, the head and not the tail. Charles flags the 'plant' wording as corrupt but traces it to Jeremiah 32:41, with Deuteronomy 28:13 and Zechariah 8:13 behind the blessing and headship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. God will build his sanctuary among them, dwell with them, and be their God while they are his people in truth and righteousness. Charles identifies this as the second Temple, citing Exodus 25:8 and 29:45 and Leviticus 26:12. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. The promise closes with God's pledge not to forsake or fail them, because he is the Lord their God. Charles cites Deuteronomy 31:6 for the pledge that frames the whole restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. Moses falls on his face and prays God not to forsake his people and inheritance, nor deliver them to Gentile enemies who would rule them and cause them to sin. Charles compares Deuteronomy 9:26, Psalm 106:41 and 2 Kings 21:14 for the plea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. Moses asks for mercy lifted up on the people, an upright spirit created in them, and the spirit of Beliar kept from ruling over them to accuse and ensnare them from righteousness. Charles notes Beliar here as the accusing spirit, treated further at 15:33, and compares Psalm 51:10 for the created spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. Moses pleads that they are God's people and inheritance, delivered from Egypt with great power, and asks for a clean heart and holy spirit so they will not be ensnared in sin henceforth to eternity. Charles cites Deuteronomy 9:26, 29 for the deliverance plea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. God answers that he knows their contrariness, thoughts and stiffneckedness, and they will not be obedient until they confess their own sin and their fathers' sin. Charles cites Deuteronomy 31:27 for the stiff neck and Leviticus 26:40 and Nehemiah 9:2 for the confession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. This verse completes the condition of verse 27: confession must cover the sin of the fathers as well as their own. Charles cites Leviticus 26:40, where confession of both sins precedes restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. After confession they will turn to God in all uprightness, heart and soul, and he will circumcise the foreskin of their heart and their seed's heart, create a holy spirit in them, and cleanse them to be his people. Charles cites Deuteronomy 10:16 and 30:6 for the circumcised heart and 2 Chronicles 6:38 for the whole-hearted turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. Their souls will cleave to God and all his commandments, they will fulfil them, and he will be their Father and they his children. This is the inward fulfilment of the restoration promised in the preceding verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. They will all be called children of the living God, and every angel and spirit will know them as God's children, with God their Father in uprightness and righteousness who loves them. The restoration ends in a publicly recognised sonship before the whole heavenly host. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. Moses is commanded to write down all these words, first and last, covering all the divisions of days in the law, testimony, weeks and jubilees unto eternity, until God descends and dwells with them. This is the book's own commission, matching the writing command of verses 5 and 27 in Charles's notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. This unit is the section heading marking the turn from prophecy to dictation: God now commands the angel to write. It divides the Sinai prologue from the history the angel will dictate to Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. The angel of the presence is commanded to write for Moses from the beginning of creation until God's sanctuary is built among them for all eternity. That span, creation to Sinai, is exactly the scope of the book that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. The Lord will appear to the eyes of all, opening the universal recognition that completes the verse. The prologue's restoration ends not in private blessing but in God's open manifestation to the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. All will know that he is the God of Israel, the Father of all the children of Jacob, and King on Mount Zion for all eternity. The titles gather the chapter's promises: covenant God, Father of the restored people, and everlasting King in Zion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. Zion and Jerusalem will be holy, the closing note of the prologue vision. The restored sanctuary of verse 22 finds its counterpart in a holy city, fit for the King who dwells there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. The angel of the presence who went before Israel's camp took the tables of the divisions of the years, from creation, of the law and testimony, of weeks and jubilees, by individual years. Charles's introduction explains the claim: the division of days was ordained on the heavenly tablets, so the book's chronology is revealed, not devised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H.
