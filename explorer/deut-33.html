The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Deuteronomy 33 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And this is the blessing, wherewith Moses the man of God blessed the children of Israel before his death.

2 And he said, The LORD came from Sinai, and rose up from Seir unto them; he shined forth from mount Paran, and he came with ten thousands of saints: from his right hand went a fiery law for them.

3 Yea, he loved the people; all his saints are in thy hand: and they sat down at thy feet; every one shall receive of thy words.

4 Moses commanded us a law, even the inheritance of the congregation of Jacob.

5 And he was king in Jeshurun, when the heads of the people and the tribes of Israel were gathered together.

6 Let Reuben live, and not die; and let not his men be few.

7 And this is the blessing of Judah: and he said, Hear, LORD, the voice of Judah, and bring him unto his people: let his hands be sufficient for him; and be thou an help to him from his enemies.

8 And of Levi he said, Let thy Thummim and thy Urim be with thy holy one, whom thou didst prove at Massah, and with whom thou didst strive at the waters of Meribah;

9 Who said unto his father and to his mother, I have not seen him; neither did he acknowledge his brethren, nor knew his own children: for they have observed thy word, and kept thy covenant.

10 They shall teach Jacob thy judgments, and Israel thy law: they shall put incense before thee, and whole burnt sacrifice upon thine altar.

11 Bless, LORD, his substance, and accept the work of his hands; smite through the loins of them that rise against him, and of them that hate him, that they rise not again.

12 And of Benjamin he said, The beloved of the LORD shall dwell in safety by him; and the Lord shall cover him all the day long, and he shall dwell between his shoulders.

13 And of Joseph he said, Blessed of the LORD be his land, for the precious things of heaven, for the dew, and for the deep that coucheth beneath,

14 And for the precious fruits brought forth by the sun, and for the precious things put forth by the moon,

15 And for the chief things of the ancient mountains, and for the precious things of the lasting hills,

16 And for the precious things of the earth and fulness thereof, and for the good will of him that dwelt in the bush: let the blessing come upon the head of Joseph, and upon the top of the head of him that was separated from his brethren.

17 His glory is like the firstling of his bullock, and his horns are like the horns of unicorns: with them he shall push the people together to the ends of the earth: and they are the ten thousands of Ephraim, and they are the thousands of Manasseh.

18 And of Zebulun he said, Rejoice, Zebulun, in thy going out; and, Issachar, in thy tents.

19 They shall call the people unto the mountain; there they shall offer sacrifices of righteousness: for they shall suck of the abundance of the seas, and of treasures hid in the sand.

20 And of Gad he said, Blessed be he that enlargeth Gad: he dwelleth as a lion, and teareth the arm with the crown of the head.

21 And he provided the first part for himself, because there, in a portion of the lawgiver, was he seated; and he came with the heads of the people, he executed the justice of the LORD, and his judgments with Israel.

22 And of Dan he said, Dan is a lion’s whelp: he shall leap from Bashan.

23 And of Naphtali he said, O Naphtali, satisfied with favour, and full with the blessing of the LORD: possess thou the west and the south.

24 And of Asher he said, Let Asher be blessed with children; let him be acceptable to his brethren, and let him dip his foot in oil.

25 Thy shoes shall be iron and brass; and as thy days, so shall thy strength be.

26 There is none like unto the God of Jeshurun, who rideth upon the heaven in thy help, and in his excellency on the sky.

27 The eternal God is thy refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms: and he shall thrust out the enemy from before thee; and shall say, Destroy them.

28 Israel then shall dwell in safety alone: the fountain of Jacob shall be upon a land of corn and wine; also his heavens shall drop down dew.

29 Happy art thou, O Israel: who is like unto thee, O people saved by the LORD, the shield of thy help, and who is the sword of thy excellency! and thine enemies shall be found liars unto thee; and thou shalt tread upon their high places.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Before his death Moses blesses each tribe in turn, as Jacob once did, but in his own voice: Reuben shall live, Judah's hands defended, Levi teaching law, Benjamin dwelling safely, Joseph blessed with earth's bounty, Zebulun and Issachar rejoicing, Gad enlarged, Dan leaping, Naphtali satisfied, Asher dipped in oil. The blessing closes with praise of the God who rides the heavens to Israel's help. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And this is the blessing, with which Moses the man of God blessed the children of Israel before his death; man of God is a common designation of a prophet, here applied to Moses as, like Jacob, he delivers a prophetic benediction before dying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And he said, The Lord came from Sinai, and rose up from Seir to them; he shined forth from Mount Paran, and he came with ten thousands of saints; from his right hand went a fiery law for them; under a metaphor borrowed from the dawn, God's majesty is described as light appearing at Sinai and scattering beams over the region on Israel's march, the fiery law being so called for the thunder and lightning of its promulgation and the fierce curse against its violation, yet given in kindness and love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Love precedes law: the people are loved, the saints held in God's hand, seated at his feet to receive his words, so the blessing's opening makes obedience a posture of sitting and listening, not of bargaining. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Moses commanded us a law, even the inheritance of the congregation of Jacob; the law was Israel's grand distinction and peculiar privilege, not only a hereditary obligation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. JFB heads this whole blessing (vv 1-28) with the majesty of God, displayed at Sinai as a divine light scattering its beams; here that majesty presides as king over Jeshurun when the heads of the people and the tribes are gathered together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Let Reuben live, and not die, and let not his men be few; though deprived of primogeniture, Reuben was still to hold rank as a tribe, gradually sinking into a nomadic tribe with enough merely to live and not die. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And this is the blessing of Judah: and he said, Hear, Lord, the voice of Judah, and bring him to his people; let his hands be sufficient for him, and be a help to him from his enemies; its general purport points to Judah's great power and independence, and its lead in all military expeditions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. And of Levi he said, Let your Thummim and your Urim be with your holy one, whom you proved at Massah, and with whom you strove at the waters of Meribah; the blessing appoints the Levites to the priesthood, a reward for their zeal for God and severity even against relatives who joined the calf idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Levi's zeal is measured by kinship refused: father, mother, brethren and children unacknowledged where God's word and covenant are at stake, for the priesthood's severity at the calf is the ground of its blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Levi's double office follows its zeal: teaching Jacob judgments and Israel law, and putting incense and whole burnt sacrifice on the altar, so word and worship are the priesthood's two hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. For Levi the blessing is twofold in JFB's reading: prosperity for the tribe's substance, and a final end to its opponents, for the prayer does not ask for victory in one battle but for enemies who cannot rise a second time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And of Benjamin he said, The beloved of the Lord shall dwell in safety by him, and the Lord shall cover him all the day long, and he shall dwell between his shoulders; a distinguishing favour was Benjamin's portion near the temple, Mount Moriah lying within its borders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Joseph's blessing begins with heaven and depth: precious things above in dew, and the deep couching beneath, so the land's bounty is drawn from both skies and subterranean waters, not from husbandry alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Joseph's portion, diversified by hill and dale, wood and water, is rich in all productions, JFB notes; the precious fruits of sun and moon are part of that promised plenty of a mountainous and level land alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The chief things of the ancient mountains and the lasting hills belong to the same Joseph blessing: a territory rich in olives, grapes, figs and grain, whose emblems of the bullock's firstling and the unicorn's horns signify glory and strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. And for the precious things of the earth and fulness of it, and for the good will of him who dwelt in the bush; let the blessing come upon the head of Joseph, and upon the top of the head of him who was separated from his brethren; Joseph's diversified territory of hill, dale, wood, and water would be rich in olives, grapes, figs, grain, and herbs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. His glory is like the firstling of his bullock, and his horns are like the horns of unicorns; with them he shall push the people together to the ends of the earth, and they are the ten thousands of Ephraim, and the thousands of Manasseh; glory and strength are shadowed here, in emblems thought to point to Joshua and Jeroboam, both of Ephraim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. And of Zebulun he said, Rejoice, Zebulun, in your going out, and, Issachar, in your tents; Zebulun rejoices in commercial enterprises and voyages by sea, Issachar preferring to reside in maritime towns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. They shall call the people to the mountain; there they shall offer sacrifices of righteousness, for they shall suck of the abundance of the seas, and of treasures hid in the sand, trafficking with the Phoenicians in gold, silver, pearl, coral, Tyrian dye from the murex, and glass from the sand of the river Belus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. And of Gad he said, Blessed be he who enlarges Gad; he dwells as a lion, and tears the arm with the crown of the head; Gad's possessions were larger than if west of Jordan, settled by Moses himself in the first conquered land, and dwelling as a lion south of the Jabbok. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. And he provided the first part for himself, because there, in a portion of the lawgiver, was he seated, and he came with the heads of the people, he executed the justice of the Lord, and his judgments with Israel; Gad faithfully kept its engagement to join the heads of the people in invading Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. And of Dan he said, Dan is a lion's whelp; he shall leap from Bashan; his southern settlement being too small, Dan by a sudden successful irruption established a colony in the northern extremity, like a young lion's leap. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. And of Naphtali he said, O Naphtali, satisfied with favour, and full with the blessing of the Lord, possess the west and the south; its pleasant, fertile territory lay west on the lakes of Merom and Chinnereth, and south of the northern Danites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. And of Asher he said, Let Asher be blessed with children; let him be acceptable to his brethren, and let him dip his foot in oil; Asher's condition combines all elements of earthly felicity, its district being fertile for the olive, whether by foot presses or by culture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Your shoes shall be iron and brass, and as your days, so shall your strength be; such shoes suited Asher's rocky coast from Carmel to Sidon, and warriors wore metallic greaves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. There is none like to the God of Jeshurun, who rides upon the heaven in your help, and in his excellency on the sky; the chapter concludes with congratulation on Israel's happiness in having Jehovah as God and protector, the cloud and fire being both guide and shelter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Refuge is eternal and arms everlasting: the God who shelters from above also thrusts the enemy out before Israel and commands destruction, so protection and expulsion are one act of the same arms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Safety alone is the blessing's end: the fountain of Jacob rests on corn, wine and dew from heaven, so dwelling alone is not isolation but provision under God's own sky. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Happiness is defined by rescue: a people saved by the Lord, shielded in help and sworded in excellency, whose enemies are found liars and whose feet tread high places, so the blessing ends not with wealth but with vindication. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
