The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover he made an altar of brass, twenty cubits the length thereof, and twenty cubits the breadth thereof, and ten cubits the height thereof.

2 Also he made a molten sea of ten cubits from brim to brim, round in compass, and five cubits the height thereof; and a line of thirty cubits did compass it round about.

3 And under it was the similitude of oxen, which did compass it round about: ten in a cubit, compassing the sea round about. Two rows of oxen were cast, when it was cast.

4 It stood upon twelve oxen, three looking toward the north, and three looking toward the west, and three looking toward the south, and three looking toward the east: and the sea was set above upon them, and all their hinder parts were inward.

5 And the thickness of it was an handbreadth, and the brim of it like the work of the brim of a cup, with flowers of lilies; and it received and held three thousand baths.

6 He made also ten lavers, and put five on the right hand, and five on the left, to wash in them: such things as they offered for the burnt offering they washed in them; but the sea was for the priests to wash in.

7 And he made ten candlesticks of gold according to their form, and set them in the temple, five on the right hand, and five on the left.

8 He made also ten tables, and placed them in the temple, five on the right side, and five on the left. And he made an hundred basons of gold.

9 Furthermore he made the court of the priests, and the great court, and doors for the court, and overlaid the doors of them with brass.

10 And he set the sea on the right side of the east end, over against the south.

11 And Huram made the pots, and the shovels, and the basons. And Huram finished the work that he was to make for king Solomon for the house of God;

12 To wit, the two pillars, and the pommels, and the chapiters which were on the top of the two pillars, and the two wreaths to cover the two pommels of the chapiters which were on the top of the pillars;

13 And four hundred pomegranates on the two wreaths; two rows of pomegranates on each wreath, to cover the two pommels of the chapiters which were upon the pillars.

14 He made also bases, and lavers made he upon the bases;

15 One sea, and twelve oxen under it.

16 The pots also, and the shovels, and the fleshhooks, and all their instruments, did Huram his father make to king Solomon for the house of the LORD of bright brass.

17 In the plain of Jordan did the king cast them, in the clay ground between Succoth and Zeredathah.

18 Thus Solomon made all these vessels in great abundance: for the weight of the brass could not be found out.

19 And Solomon made all the vessels that were for the house of God, the golden altar also, and the tables whereon the shewbread was set;

20 Moreover the candlesticks with their lamps, that they should burn after the manner before the oracle, of pure gold;

21 And the flowers, and the lamps, and the tongs, made he of gold, and that perfect gold;

22 And the snuffers, and the basons, and the spoons, and the censers, of pure gold: and the entry of the house, the inner doors thereof for the most holy place, and the doors of the house of the temple, were of gold.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter describes the temple's furnishings: the bronze altar, the great basin called the molten sea, the lavers, candlesticks, and tables, all finished by Huram for Solomon. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Moreover he made a bronze altar twenty cubits long, twenty broad and ten high. Henry notes it was far larger than Moses' five-cubit altar, because Israel had grown numerous and rich, and returns should bear proportion to receivings; its height let worshippers see the sacrifice and be moved to sorrow for sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He made a molten sea, ten cubits brim to brim, round in compass, five cubits high, with a line of thirty cubits compassing it. Henry notes it stood at the entrance to the priests' court like a font at a church door, signifying the fountain opened for washing from sin, and JFB notes the knops of Kings are here given as oxen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Under it was the similitude of oxen, ten in a cubit, compassing the sea, in two rows cast with it when it was cast. JFB notes the basin and its supporting oxen were cast in one and the same mould, all of one piece. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. It stood upon twelve oxen, three looking north, west, south and east, with the sea set above and their hinder parts inward. JFB's treatment of the sea's supports covers this arrangement: the one-piece casting carried the great basin on the twelve oxen, facing every quarter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Its thickness was a handbreadth, its brim like a cup with flowers of lilies, and it received and held three thousand baths. Henry notes the sea signified both gospel privilege, cleansing by Christ's blood, and gospel duty, cleansing ourselves by repentance; JFB reconciles the three thousand here with two thousand in Kings as the basin filled with all its fountain accompaniments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He made ten lavers, five on the right and five on the left, to wash the burnt offerings in, while the sea was for the priests to wash in. Henry notes that as priests must be washed, so must sacrifices: we must put away vain thoughts and corrupt aims that pollute our performances themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He made ten candlesticks of gold according to their form, five on the right and five on the left in the temple. Henry notes the written word is a lamp shining in a dark place, and the increase from one candlestick to ten signified light growing, with further books of Scripture to come; JFB adds it was a standing emblem that growing light was needed against growing darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He made ten tables, five on each side, and a hundred basins of gold. Henry notes that as the house was enlarged, so was its housekeeping, and God's table is well furnished, with bread enough for the whole family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Furthermore he made the court of the priests and the great court, with doors overlaid with brass. Henry notes the doors were overlaid both for strength and beauty, and that they might not rot with exposure to the weather. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. He set the sea on the right side of the east end, over against the south. Henry's placement of the sea at the entrance to the priests' court explains the position: it stood ready for the priests to wash as they entered to minister. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Huram made the pots, shovels and basins, and finished the work he was to make for Solomon for the house of God. JFB refers this completion to the parallel account in Kings, marking Huram's brass work as the finished furnishing of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The work included the two pillars, the pommels, the chapiters on the pillars, and the two wreaths covering the pommels. Henry's chapter overview places these brass vessels among the court furniture, while the pillars themselves, Jachin and Boaz, were treated in chapter 3 as establishment and strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. There were four hundred pomegranates on the two wreaths, two rows on each wreath, covering the pommels. Henry's overview groups these with the pillar ornaments, the rich brass work of the open court, in view of all the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He made also bases, and lavers upon the bases. Henry notes the ten lavers stood between porch and altar for washing the offerings, and these bases carried them in their service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. One sea, and twelve oxen under it, completed the great washing vessel. Henry's sea typology applies again: the one great basin for the priests' cleansing stood on the twelve oxen, a single provision for all who ministered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The pots, shovels, fleshhooks and all their instruments Huram made for Solomon of bright brass. Henry groups these with the vessels of the altar in the open court, the brass furniture by which the sacrifices were handled in the people's view. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. In the plain of Jordan the king cast them, in the clay ground between Succoth and Zeredathah. Henry's account of the brass furniture's abundance fits this casting place: the great vessels were cast where clay ground served the foundry, in the Jordan plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Thus Solomon made all these vessels in great abundance, for the weight of the brass could not be found out. Henry's point on the enlarged altar applies to the whole furnishing: Israel, greatly enlarged in borders and wealth, enlarged God's house accordingly, and returns bore proportion to receivings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Solomon made all the vessels for the house of God, the golden altar also, and the tables whereon the showbread was set. Henry notes that within the house all was gold, for the nearer we come to God the purer we must be, and the golden altar of incense points to Christ ever living to make intercession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Moreover the candlesticks with their lamps, that they should burn after the manner before the oracle, of pure gold. Henry's chapter overview names these as appurtenances of the golden furnishings, and his candlestick typology applies: lamps burning before the oracle are the word shining in a dark place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The flowers, lamps and tongs he made of gold, and that perfect gold. Henry's overview again covers these appurtenances of the candlesticks and altar, all of pure gold within the house, where all must be pure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The snuffers, basins, spoons and censers were of pure gold, and the entry, the inner doors for the most holy place, and the doors of the temple, were of gold. Henry notes all within was gold, and the golden doors marked the passage into God's nearer presence, where purity is required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • molten sea. A great bronze basin at the entrance to the priests’ court, for washing: Henry likens it to a font at a church door. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • bath. A measure of liquid capacity used for the sea’s contents. The study note reconciles the figures given here and in Kings but does not state a modern equivalent, so none is given. Unresolved Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages

  • The study note for verse 2 connects this passage to the Kings account of the sea, whose knops are given here as oxen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • The study note for verse 5 connects this passage to the Kings figure of two thousand baths for the sea, against three thousand here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • The study note for verse 11 connects this passage to the parallel Kings account of Huram's finished brass work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
