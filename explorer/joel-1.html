The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Joel 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word of the LORD that came to Joel the son of Pethuel.

2 Hear this, ye old men, and give ear, all ye inhabitants of the land. Hath this been in your days, or even in the days of your fathers?

3 Tell ye your children of it, and let your children tell their children, and their children another generation.

4 That which the palmerworm hath left hath the locust eaten; and that which the locust hath left hath the cankerworm eaten; and that which the cankerworm hath left hath the caterpiller eaten.

5 Awake, ye drunkards, and weep; and howl, all ye drinkers of wine, because of the new wine; for it is cut off from your mouth.

6 For a nation is come up upon my land, strong, and without number, whose teeth are the teeth of a lion, and he hath the cheek teeth of a great lion.

7 He hath laid my vine waste, and barked my fig tree: he hath made it clean bare, and cast it away; the branches thereof are made white.

8 Lament like a virgin girded with sackcloth for the husband of her youth.

9 The meat offering and the drink offering is cut off from the house of the LORD; the priests, the LORD’s ministers, mourn.

10 The field is wasted, the land mourneth; for the corn is wasted: the new wine is dried up, the oil languisheth.

11 Be ye ashamed, O ye husbandmen; howl, O ye vinedressers, for the wheat and for the barley; because the harvest of the field is perished.

12 The vine is dried up, and the fig tree languisheth; the pomegranate tree, the palm tree also, and the apple tree, even all the trees of the field, are withered: because joy is withered away from the sons of men.

13 Gird yourselves, and lament, ye priests: howl, ye ministers of the altar: come, lie all night in sackcloth, ye ministers of my God: for the meat offering and the drink offering is withholden from the house of your God.

14 Sanctify ye a fast, call a solemn assembly, gather the elders and all the inhabitants of the land into the house of the LORD your God, and cry unto the LORD,

15 Alas for the day! for the day of the LORD is at hand, and as a destruction from the Almighty shall it come.

16 Is not the meat cut off before our eyes, yea, joy and gladness from the house of our God?

17 The seed is rotten under their clods, the garners are laid desolate, the barns are broken down; for the corn is withered.

18 How do the beasts groan! the herds of cattle are perplexed, because they have no pasture; yea, the flocks of sheep are made desolate.

19 O LORD, to thee will I cry: for the fire hath devoured the pastures of the wilderness, and the flame hath burned all the trees of the field.

20 The beasts of the field cry also unto thee: for the rivers of waters are dried up, and the fire hath devoured the pastures of the wilderness.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Joel calls the old men and all the inhabitants of the land to hear of a disaster without precedent in their days or their fathers' days: wave after wave of locusts, palmerworm, locust, cankerworm, caterpillar, stripping vine, fig, wheat, barley, and every tree of the field. Offerings are cut off from the house of the LORD, priests mourn in sackcloth, beasts groan without pasture, and the chapter ends with a fast, a solemn assembly, and a cry to the LORD, for the day of the LORD is at hand. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The word of the Lord came to Joel the son of Pethuel, and Henry dismisses the fancy that this Joel was Samuel's son, noting he lived long after and spoke of a sore judgment brought upon Judah for its sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The old men and all inhabitants are asked whether such a thing had been in their days or their fathers', and Henry stresses the judgment was without precedent in memory or record, as those who outdo their predecessors in sin may expect sorer judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They are told to tell their children, and their children their children, so that a judgment without precedent in the past would not be forgotten in the ages to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. What the palmerworm left the locust ate, and what the locust left the cankerworm and caterpillar ate, and Henry reads the succession as armies of insects coming to eat the land's fruits, an image that also applies to destruction by a foreign enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Drunkards are told to awake and weep because the new wine is cut off from their mouths, and Henry notes that God, Lord of hosts, can humble a proud people by the weakest and most contemptible creatures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. A nation strong and without number has come up, with teeth like a lion's, and the insect army is thus described in the language of invasion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The invader has laid the vine waste and barked the fig tree, making it clean bare and casting it away, so that the branches are white, stripped trees standing as the visible ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The people must lament like a virgin girded with sackcloth for the husband of her youth, and Henry explains the image as grief for a lover lost between espousal and marriage, or a young husband suddenly taken, the sharpest private sorrow picturing a public loss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The meat and drink offerings are cut off from the house of the Lord, and the priests mourn, since the locusts have destroyed both the people's food and the material of their thanksgiving offerings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The field is wasted, the land mourns, the corn is wasted, the new wine dried up, and the oil languishes, and Henry notes the people had not been thankful for bread, wine, and oil, and are now justly brought to lament their loss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Husbandmen are ashamed and vinedressers howl for wheat and barley, because the harvest has perished, and Henry warns that those who labour only for the meat that perishes will sooner or later be ashamed of their labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Vine, fig, pomegranate, palm, apple, and all the trees of the field are withered, because joy is withered away from the sons of men, so that the ruin of the orchards is also the ruin of the people's gladness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Priests and ministers of the altar must gird themselves, lament, and lie all night in sackcloth, since the offerings are withholden from God's house, and Henry brings in clergymen alongside countrymen as sharers in the lamentation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. A fast is to be sanctified, a solemn assembly called, and elders and inhabitants gathered into the house of the Lord to cry to him, turning lamentation into a directed appeal to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Alas for the day, for the day of the Lord is at hand, a destruction from the Almighty, and the locust plague is thus named as a foretaste of the great day itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The meat is cut off before their eyes, with joy and gladness from the house of God, so that even worship loses its festal character when the gifts that sustained it are gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The seed is rotten under the clods, the garners are desolate, and the barns broken down because the corn is withered, carrying the devastation from the standing crop into the storehouse and the seed for next year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The beasts groan, the herds are perplexed for want of pasture, and the flocks are made desolate, so that the judgment reaches the dumb creatures that share man's dependence on the field. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Joel himself cries to the Lord, for fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness and flame has burned all the trees, the prophet turning from describing ruin to pleading against it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Even the beasts of the field cry to God, for the rivers are dried up and fire has devoured the pastures, and the whole creation's cry becomes the prophet's closing argument for mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • The day of the LORD. the day of God's visitation and judgment, pictured in the prophets as darkness, trumpet alarm, and distress, and also as deliverance for those who call on his name. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Palmerworm, locust, cankerworm, caterpillar. four names for successive waves of locusts in Joel 1, each eating what the last left, an image the study note also applies to destruction by a foreign enemy. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
