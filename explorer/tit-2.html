The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Titus 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 But speak thou the things which become sound doctrine:

2 That the aged men be sober, grave, temperate, sound in faith, in charity, in patience.

3 The aged women likewise, that they be in behaviour as becometh holiness, not false accusers, not given to much wine, teachers of good things;

4 That they may teach the young women to be sober, to love their husbands, to love their children,

5 To be discreet, chaste, keepers at home, good, obedient to their own husbands, that the word of God be not blasphemed.

6 Young men likewise exhort to be sober minded.

7 In all things shewing thyself a pattern of good works: in doctrine shewing uncorruptness, gravity, sincerity,

8 Sound speech, that cannot be condemned; that he that is of the contrary part may be ashamed, having no evil thing to say of you.

9 Exhort servants to be obedient unto their own masters, and to please them well in all things; not answering again;

10 Not purloining, but shewing all good fidelity; that they may adorn the doctrine of God our Saviour in all things.

11 For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men,

12 Teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly, in this present world;

13 Looking for that blessed hope, and the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ;

14 Who gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works.

15 These things speak, and exhort, and rebuke with all authority. Let no man despise thee.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Speak what fits sound doctrine, Paul tells Titus, with fitting conduct for older men and women, younger women and men, and servants, while Titus himself is to be a pattern of good works and sound speech that cannot be condemned. The ground is grace: God's grace has appeared bringing salvation, teaching believers to deny ungodliness and worldly passions and live self-controlled, upright and godly lives, awaiting the blessed hope and Christ who gave himself to redeem and purify a people of his own. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In contrast to the false teachers, Titus must speak what fits sound doctrine. JFB notes the stress falls on doing: wandering curiosity is best cured by recalling believers to the holy life they ought to practise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Aged men are to be sober, dignified, self-controlled, and sound in faith, love, and patient endurance. Henry adds that the decays of age excuse no excess; comfort is to be sought in nearer communion with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Aged women likewise are to behave as befits holiness, not as slanderers or slaves to much wine, but as teachers of good things. JFB notes this teaching was private, by example and counsel, not public preaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The older women are to school the younger women in self-restraint and in love for their husbands and children. Titus, a young man, wisely teaches the younger women through the older women rather than directly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Young women are to be discreet, chaste, busy at home, kind, and submissive to their own husbands, so that God's word is not spoken evil of. Failures in household duty, Henry warns, greatly reproach Christianity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Young men likewise are to be exhorted to be sober-minded, self-restrained in pleasures and follies. Henry observes that more young people are ruined by pride than by any other sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Titus himself must be a pattern of good works in everything, showing uncorruptness, dignity, and sincerity in his teaching. All teaching is useless, JFB notes, unless the teacher's example confirms his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. His speech must be sound and beyond condemnation, so that opponents are put to shame, having no evil thing to say. A blameless life stops the mouths of adversaries, which is itself a glory for a Christian. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Servants are to obey their own masters and seek to please them well in all things, not answering back. JFB explains the frequent charge: Christian freedom was easily misunderstood as licence to forget one's station. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They must not pilfer but show all good faithfulness, so that they adorn the doctrine of God our Saviour in everything. Even the lowest station, Henry notes, can honour the gospel, and service to an earthly master done for Christ serves him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The ground of all these duties is that God's grace bringing salvation has appeared to all people, of every rank. Grace has now been embodied and made to shine forth in Jesus, after long being hidden in God's counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. That grace disciplines us: it teaches us to deny ungodliness and worldly lusts once for all, and to live soberly toward ourselves, righteously toward neighbours, and godly toward God in this present age. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. We live this way looking for the blessed hope, the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ. JFB notes the single article joins God and Saviour as one Being, and that this hope is the antidote to worldly lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Christ gave himself for us to redeem us from all iniquity, not merely its penalty, and to purify a people peculiarly his own, zealous for good works. Redemption and sanctification, Henry stresses, go together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. These things Titus must speak, exhort, and rebuke with all authority, and let no one despise him. Speaking with such vigour commands respect and leaves no one above the need of admonition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • purloining. pilfering, stealing small things, forbidden to servants who should show all good faithfulness (verse 10) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
