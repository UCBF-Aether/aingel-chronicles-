The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The proverbs of Solomon the son of David, king of Israel;

2 To know wisdom and instruction; to perceive the words of understanding;

3 To receive the instruction of wisdom, justice, and judgment, and equity;

4 To give subtilty to the simple, to the young man knowledge and discretion.

5 A wise man will hear, and will increase learning; and a man of understanding shall attain unto wise counsels:

6 To understand a proverb, and the interpretation; the words of the wise, and their dark sayings.

7 The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.

8 My son, hear the instruction of thy father, and forsake not the law of thy mother:

9 For they shall be an ornament of grace unto thy head, and chains about thy neck.

10 My son, if sinners entice thee, consent thou not.

11 If they say, Come with us, let us lay wait for blood, let us lurk privily for the innocent without cause:

12 Let us swallow them up alive as the grave; and whole, as those that go down into the pit:

13 We shall find all precious substance, we shall fill our houses with spoil:

14 Cast in thy lot among us; let us all have one purse:

15 My son, walk not thou in the way with them; refrain thy foot from their path:

16 For their feet run to evil, and make haste to shed blood.

17 Surely in vain the net is spread in the sight of any bird.

18 And they lay wait for their own blood; they lurk privily for their own lives.

19 So are the ways of every one that is greedy of gain; which taketh away the life of the owners thereof.

20 Wisdom crieth without; she uttereth her voice in the streets:

21 She crieth in the chief place of concourse, in the openings of the gates: in the city she uttereth her words, saying,

22 How long, ye simple ones, will ye love simplicity? and the scorners delight in their scorning, and fools hate knowledge?

23 Turn you at my reproof: behold, I will pour out my spirit unto you, I will make known my words unto you.

24 Because I have called, and ye refused; I have stretched out my hand, and no man regarded;

25 But ye have set at nought all my counsel, and would none of my reproof:

26 I also will laugh at your calamity; I will mock when your fear cometh;

27 When your fear cometh as desolation, and your destruction cometh as a whirlwind; when distress and anguish cometh upon you.

28 Then shall they call upon me, but I will not answer; they shall seek me early, but they shall not find me:

29 For that they hated knowledge, and did not choose the fear of the LORD:

30 They would none of my counsel: they despised all my reproof.

31 Therefore shall they eat of the fruit of their own way, and be filled with their own devices.

32 For the turning away of the simple shall slay them, and the prosperity of fools shall destroy them.

33 But whoso hearkeneth unto me shall dwell safely, and shall be quiet from fear of evil.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The book states its purpose: to teach wisdom, justice, judgment and equity, and to give the simple shrewdness and the young knowledge. A father's warning follows against joining a gang that lures with shared spoil, since greed takes the life of its owners. Then Wisdom herself cries in the streets and gates: those who refuse her call will eat the fruit of their own way, while whoever listens will dwell safely. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These are Solomon's proverbs, and Henry notes their principles are piety, charity, justice, benevolence and true prudence, whose universal purity proves them the word of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The book teaches wisdom, piety toward God, instruction and moral discipline, giving the reader wise and prudent counsels for conduct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Its instruction in wisdom is meant to be shown in the conduct of life, above all in righteousness toward our fellow creatures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The simple are given sagacity to discern right through just principles and correct views of virtue and vice, and the young gain knowledge and discretion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Even the wise will listen and increase in learning, since these lessons benefit those who feel their own ignorance and need to be taught. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The reader is led to understand proverbs, interpretations, and the words and dark sayings of the wise, pondering the precepts well so as not to err through obstinacy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge; fools, Henry says, are those with no true wisdom who follow their own devices without reverence for God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. A child is to heed a father's instruction and a mother's law, with reasons given, since children are reasonable creatures who still need law because they are corrupt and wilful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Such instruction is an ornament of grace to the head and chains about the neck: divine truths valued highly become honourable to us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. When sinners entice, the answer is a flat refusal: do not say as they say, do as they do, or have fellowship with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The gang's lure is to lie in wait for blood and lurk privily for the innocent, a pleasure in destroying another that Henry calls unthinkable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. They promise to swallow victims alive like the grave, whole and unresisting, but sinners love company in sin and will have the more to answer for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The bait is precious substance and houses filled with spoil, yet Henry warns that such wealth is neither substance nor precious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. They offer one purse shared alike, but men promise themselves in vain that sin will turn to their advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The counsel is to walk not in their way and to refrain the foot from their path, refusing even one step on these destructive paths. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Their feet run to evil and make haste to shed blood, for the way of sin is downhill and men cannot stop themselves once started. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. A net spread in the sight of any bird is spread in vain, yet these sinners rush on heedlessly toward their own ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. In lying in wait for others' blood they lurk privily for their own lives, since greediness of gain hurries men into practices that cut short their days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. So are the ways of everyone greedy of gain: it takes away the life of its owners, for what is a man profited though he gain the world if he lose his soul? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Wisdom herself cries without in the streets and lifts her voice in the chief places of concourse, and Henry identifies this Wisdom with Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. She cries at the gates and at the entry of the city, so that no one can plead that the call of God never reached them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Three sorts are called: simple ones fond of their simple notions, scorners who jest at everything sacred, and fools who hate to be taught. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. The precept is plain, "Turn you at my reproof": God promises to pour out his Spirit on those who turn, for special grace is needful to sincere conversion and never denied to any who seek it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Because Wisdom called and men refused, stretching out her hand with no one regarding, the danger now is not hearkening to the calls of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. They set at nought all counsel and would none of reproof, rejecting the love of Christ and the promises mingled with his reproofs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Wisdom will laugh at their calamity and mock when their fear comes, since sinners now at ease will find their calamity come upon them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. When fear comes as desolation and destruction as a whirlwind, with distress and anguish, the God once ready to hear will be sought too late. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Then they shall call upon Wisdom but not be answered, and seek her early but not find her, for now God is ready to hear their prayers but then they shall cry in vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. This is because they hated knowledge and did not choose the fear of the LORD, flattering themselves in their wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. They would none of counsel and despised all reproof, making no right use of reproofs, which is to turn from evil to good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. Therefore they shall eat of the fruit of their own way and be filled with their own devices, reaping what their choices have sown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. The turning away of the simple shall slay them and the prosperity of fools shall destroy them, since the uncertainty of life and the consequences of dying without Christ make the perilous path deadly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. But whoever hearkens to Wisdom shall dwell safely and be quiet from fear of evil, enjoying peace of conscience and confidence in God, free from evil in life, in death, and for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words in this chapter that modern readers may trip over. They are editorial explanation, not Scripture.

  • subtilty. Shrewdness and good sense in seeing through things; the notes read it as sagacity to discern right through just principles. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • scorner. One who mocks and scoffs at correction; the notes describe scorners as those who jest at everything sacred. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
