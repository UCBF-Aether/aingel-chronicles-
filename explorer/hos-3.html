The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hosea 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then said the LORD unto me, Go yet, love a woman beloved of her friend, yet an adulteress, according to the love of the LORD toward the children of Israel, who look to other gods, and love flagons of wine.

2 So I bought her to me for fifteen pieces of silver, and for an homer of barley, and an half homer of barley:

3 And I said unto her, Thou shalt abide for me many days; thou shalt not play the harlot, and thou shalt not be for another man: so will I also be for thee.

4 For the children of Israel shall abide many days without a king, and without a prince, and without a sacrifice, and without an image, and without an ephod, and without teraphim:

5 Afterward shall the children of Israel return, and seek the LORD their God, and David their king; and shall fear the LORD and his goodness in the latter days.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Hosea is told to love again a woman who has been unfaithful, and he buys her back for fifteen pieces of silver and barley, setting her apart for many days. The chapter reads Israel's coming exile the same way: many days without king, prince, sacrifice, image, ephod, or teraphim, and afterward a return to seek the LORD their God and David their king in the latter days. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then said the Lord unto me, go yet, love a woman beloved of her friend, yet an adulteress, according to the love of the Lord toward the children of Israel, who look to other gods, and love flagons of wine, because the prophet's renewed love pictures God's love persisting toward a people still turned to idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. So I bought her to me for fifteen pieces of silver, and for a homer of barley, and a half homer of barley, because the price is that of a slave, showing how low the adulteress had fallen and how real the purchase of her back would be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And I said unto her, thou shalt abide for me many days, thou shalt not play the harlot, and thou shalt not be for another man, so will I also be for thee, because the discipline is a season of chaste separation, waiting without lovers and without husband, until restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For the children of Israel shall abide many days without a king, and without a prince, and without a sacrifice, and without an image, and without an ephod, and without teraphim, because the long exile strips away both lawful and unlawful worship, leaving the people with neither throne nor altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Afterward shall the children of Israel return, and seek the Lord their God, and David their king, and shall fear the Lord and his goodness in the latter days, because the end of the discipline is return, seeking God and the Davidic king together, in the last days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Ephod and teraphim. the priestly garment used in seeking God's guidance, and household idols; Hosea 3 says exile will strip Israel of both lawful and unlawful worship alike. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
