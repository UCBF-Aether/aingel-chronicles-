The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Timothy 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 This is a true saying, If a man desire the office of a bishop, he desireth a good work.

2 A bishop then must be blameless, the husband of one wife, vigilant, sober, of good behaviour, given to hospitality, apt to teach;

3 Not given to wine, no striker, not greedy of filthy lucre; but patient, not a brawler, not covetous;

4 One that ruleth well his own house, having his children in subjection with all gravity;

5 (For if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God?)

6 Not a novice, lest being lifted up with pride he fall into the condemnation of the devil.

7 Moreover he must have a good report of them which are without; lest he fall into reproach and the snare of the devil.

8 Likewise must the deacons be grave, not doubletongued, not given to much wine, not greedy of filthy lucre;

9 Holding the mystery of the faith in a pure conscience.

10 And let these also first be proved; then let them use the office of a deacon, being found blameless.

11 Even so must their wives be grave, not slanderers, sober, faithful in all things.

12 Let the deacons be the husbands of one wife, ruling their children and their own houses well.

13 For they that have used the office of a deacon well purchase to themselves a good degree, and great boldness in the faith which is in Christ Jesus.

14 These things write I unto thee, hoping to come unto thee shortly:

15 But if I tarry long, that thou mayest know how thou oughtest to behave thyself in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth.

16 And without controversy great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen of angels, preached unto the Gentiles, believed on in the world, received up into glory.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter sets out the character required of overseers and deacons: blameless, self-controlled, hospitable, able to teach and manage a household well, tested before serving, holding the faith with a clear conscience. Paul writes so Timothy will know how to behave in God's household, the church of the living God, pillar and foundation of the truth, and closes with the great mystery of godliness. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. It is a true saying that desiring the office of a bishop is desiring a good work, and Henry approves the desire when it springs from love to Christ and to souls, ready to deny self and undergo hardship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. A bishop must be blameless, the husband of one wife, vigilant, sober, of good behaviour, given to hospitality, and apt to teach, and Henry notes that sobriety and watchfulness assist one another, and that a minister must give as little occasion for blame as can be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He must be not given to wine, no striker, not greedy of filthy lucre, but patient, not a brawler, not covetous, so that temperance in appetite, temper, and money all mark the office together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. He must rule his own house well, having his children in subjection with all gravity, and Henry notes that ministers' families ought to be examples of good to all other families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. For if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God, and the household becomes the visible proof of fitness for the larger household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He must not be a novice, lest being lifted up with pride he fall into the condemnation of the devil, and Henry warns that pride is the sin that turned angels into devils. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He must also have a good report of them that are without, lest he fall into reproach and the snare of the devil, so that his former life must stand examination by neighbours as well as by the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Deacons likewise must be grave, not doubletongued, not given to much wine, not greedy of filthy lucre, and Henry notes that deacons were first appointed to distribute the church's charity and manage its concerns, a great trust not to be lodged in unfit hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. They must hold the mystery of the faith in a pure conscience, joining sound belief to a clean conscience, since doctrine held in an impure conscience soon corrupts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They must first be proved, and then, if found blameless, serve, and Henry stresses that public trusts should not be given until men are found fit for the business entrusted to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Their wives likewise must be grave, not slanderers, sober, faithful in all things, and Henry extends the warning to all related to ministers, who must walk as becomes the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Deacons must be husbands of one wife, ruling their children and houses well, the same household test applied to the second office as to the first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Those who use the office of deacon well purchase a good degree and great boldness in the faith, so that faithful service in a lower trust prepares for greater confidence in the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Paul writes these things hoping to come shortly, yet providing instruction in case he tarries, so that the church is not left without order while its apostle is absent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The aim is that Timothy know how to behave in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, and Henry notes that God dwells there, and that when a church ceases to be the pillar and ground of truth, regard for truth must come first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Great is the mystery of godliness: God manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen of angels, preached to the Gentiles, believed on in the world, received up into glory, and Henry sums it up: the mystery is Christ himself, come to take away sins and purify a peculiar people zealous of good works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • bishop. overseer, the office whose character the chapter sets out in verses 1–7 Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
  • filthy lucre. shameful gain, which an overseer must not be greedy for (verse 3) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
