The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Timothy 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Let as many servants as are under the yoke count their own masters worthy of all honour, that the name of God and his doctrine be not blasphemed.

2 And they that have believing masters, let them not despise them, because they are brethren; but rather do them service, because they are faithful and beloved, partakers of the benefit. These things teach and exhort.

3 If any man teach otherwise, and consent not to wholesome words, even the words of our Lord Jesus Christ, and to the doctrine which is according to godliness;

4 He is proud, knowing nothing, but doting about questions and strifes of words, whereof cometh envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings,

5 Perverse disputings of men of corrupt minds, and destitute of the truth, supposing that gain is godliness: from such withdraw thyself.

6 But godliness with contentment is great gain.

7 For we brought nothing into this world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out.

8 And having food and raiment let us be therewith content.

9 But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition.

10 For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.

11 But thou, O man of God, flee these things; and follow after righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, meekness.

12 Fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life, whereunto thou art also called, and hast professed a good profession before many witnesses.

13 I give thee charge in the sight of God, who quickeneth all things, and before Christ Jesus, who before Pontius Pilate witnessed a good confession;

14 That thou keep this commandment without spot, unrebukable, until the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ:

15 Which in his times he shall shew, who is the blessed and only Potentate, the King of kings, and Lord of lords;

16 Who only hath immortality, dwelling in the light which no man can approach unto; whom no man hath seen, nor can see: to whom be honour and power everlasting. Amen.

17 Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy;

18 That they do good, that they be rich in good works, ready to distribute, willing to communicate;

19 Laying up in store for themselves a good foundation against the time to come, that they may lay hold on eternal life.

20 O Timothy, keep that which is committed to thy trust, avoiding profane and vain babblings, and oppositions of science falsely so called:

21 Which some professing have erred concerning the faith. Grace be with thee. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Servants are to honour their masters, and teachers who stir up controversy for gain are to be recognised for what they are, Paul says. Godliness with contentment is great gain, since nothing was brought into the world and nothing can be taken out, while those determined to be rich fall into temptation and the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. Timothy is to flee these things, fight the good fight of faith, and charge the rich to be generous and lay up a good foundation. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Servants under the yoke must count their masters worthy of all honour, lest God's name and doctrine be blasphemed, and Henry warns that religious knowledge gives no right to despise masters, disobey lawful commands, or expose their faults. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Servants with believing masters must not despise them because they are brethren, but rather serve them, since they are faithful and beloved partakers of the benefit, and Henry notes they must serve with double diligence and cheerfulness because of their shared faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Whoever teaches otherwise and consents not to the wholesome words of Christ and the doctrine of godliness has departed from the only words to which unfeigned consent is due. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Such a man is proud, knowing nothing, doting about questions and strifes of words, and Henry observes that commonly those are most proud who know least, for they do not know themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. From such disputes come envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings, and perverse disputings of corrupt minds destitute of the truth, supposing gain is godliness, and Henry calls these disputes all subtlety and no solidity, pursued for worldly advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Godliness with contentment is great gain, and Henry contrasts those who make a trade of Christianity for this world, who will be disappointed, with those who mind it as their calling and find its promise for this life and the next. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. We brought nothing into this world, and can carry nothing out, and Henry notes that in the greatest straits we cannot be poorer than when we came in, and that a shroud, a coffin, and a grave are all the richest man can have from his wealth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Having food and raiment, let us be therewith content, since nature should be content with a little and grace with less, and the necessaries of life bound a true Christian's desires. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Those who will be rich fall into temptation and a snare and many foolish and hurtful lusts that drown men in destruction, and Henry stresses it is not the rich but those determined to be rich who place their happiness in wealth and give Satan opportunity to tempt them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The love of money is the root of all evil, and some, coveting after it, have erred from the faith and pierced themselves with many sorrows, and Henry says every sort of wickedness grows from the love of money, as daily experience proves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. But thou, O man of God, flee these things, and follow righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, meekness, and Henry notes it ill becomes any man, but especially a man of God, to set his heart on the things of this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Fight the good fight of faith and lay hold on eternal life, to which Timothy was called and professed a good profession before many witnesses, and Henry sets eternal life as the crown proposed for encouragement in the conflict with corruption and the powers of darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Timothy is charged in the sight of God who quickens all things and of Christ who witnessed a good confession before Pontius Pilate to keep the commandment without spot until Christ's appearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. That appearing God will show in his own time, he who is the blessed and only Potentate, King of kings and Lord of lords, and Henry notes the appearing is certain though its time is not for us to know. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. He alone has immortality, dwelling in unapproachable light, whom no man has seen nor can see, and Henry notes that mortal eyes cannot bear the brightness of divine glory, and God is known to sinners only in and through Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. To him be honour and power everlasting, and Henry adores the Godhead here without distinction of Persons, since all these things are properly spoken of Father, Son, or Holy Ghost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The rich in this world must not be highminded nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God who gives richly all things to enjoy, and Henry notes that being rich toward God is wholly different from being rich in this world, and that wealth is among the most uncertain of things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. They must do good, be rich in good works, ready to distribute and willing to communicate, since what is an estate worth except as it gives opportunity to do the more good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. So they lay up a good foundation against the time to come, that they may lay hold on eternal life, showing faith by fruits of love while the self-indulgent around them lift up their eyes in torment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. O Timothy, keep that which is committed to thy trust, avoiding profane and vain babblings and oppositions of science falsely so called, and Henry warns that learning which opposes the gospel is not true science, and that advancing reason above faith endangers faith itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Some professing that false knowledge have erred concerning the faith, and the epistle closes with grace be with thee, and Henry closes likewise: grace includes all that is good and is an earnest and beginning of glory, and where God gives grace he will give glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
