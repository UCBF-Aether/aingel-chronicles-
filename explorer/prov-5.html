The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 My son, attend unto my wisdom, and bow thine ear to my understanding:

2 That thou mayest regard discretion, and that thy lips may keep knowledge.

3 For the lips of a strange woman drop as an honeycomb, and her mouth is smoother than oil:

4 But her end is bitter as wormwood, sharp as a two-edged sword.

5 Her feet go down to death; her steps take hold on hell.

6 Lest thou shouldest ponder the path of life, her ways are moveable, that thou canst not know them.

7 Hear me now therefore, O ye children, and depart not from the words of my mouth.

8 Remove thy way far from her, and come not nigh the door of her house:

9 Lest thou give thine honour unto others, and thy years unto the cruel:

10 Lest strangers be filled with thy wealth; and thy labours be in the house of a stranger;

11 And thou mourn at the last, when thy flesh and thy body are consumed,

12 And say, How have I hated instruction, and my heart despised reproof;

13 And have not obeyed the voice of my teachers, nor inclined mine ear to them that instructed me!

14 I was almost in all evil in the midst of the congregation and assembly.

15 Drink waters out of thine own cistern, and running waters out of thine own well.

16 Let thy fountains be dispersed abroad, and rivers of waters in the streets.

17 Let them be only thine own, and not strangers’ with thee.

18 Let thy fountain be blessed: and rejoice with the wife of thy youth.

19 Let her be as the loving hind and pleasant roe; let her breasts satisfy thee at all times; and be thou ravished always with her love.

20 And why wilt thou, my son, be ravished with a strange woman, and embrace the bosom of a stranger?

21 For the ways of man are before the eyes of the LORD, and he pondereth all his goings.

22 His own iniquities shall take the wicked himself, and he shall be holden with the cords of his sins.

23 He shall die without instruction; and in the greatness of his folly he shall go astray.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The strange woman's lips drop honey and her mouth is smoother than oil, but her end is bitter as wormwood and her steps take hold on death. The chapter warns of honour, wealth and years handed to strangers and of mourning at the last for hated instruction. Against this it sets faithfulness at home: drink from your own cistern, rejoice with the wife of your youth, for every way lies before the eyes of the LORD. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The son is urged to attend to wisdom and bow his ear to understanding, for Henry's opening caution to young men rests on heeding instruction before temptation speaks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Discretion is to be regarded and knowledge kept on the lips, since wisdom guarded in speech is the defence Henry sets against fleshly lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The strange woman's lips drop as a honeycomb and her mouth is smoother than oil, the flattering charm Henry warns must not be listened to, lest its sweetness hide its ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Her end is bitter as wormwood and sharp as a two-edged sword, for Henry stresses that however luscious the sin seems, take it any way and it wounds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Her feet go down to death and her steps take hold on hell, the direct tendency of this sin being, in Henry's words, the destruction of body and soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Her ways are movable so the path of life is not pondered, for the tempter's shifting disguises keep the victim from soberly weighing where the road ends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The children are called to hear and not depart from the father's words, since Henry's caution is addressed to all young men as his children, to be treasured before assault comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The way is to be removed far from her and her door not approached, for those who would be kept from harm must keep out of harm's way and not thrust themselves into temptation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Honour given to others and years to the cruel are the price Henry names for this sin, which blasts reputation and wastes time and estate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Strangers filled with wealth and labours housed by a stranger show the same ruin, for the sin wastes the estate and hands its fruits to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Mourning at the last, when flesh and body are consumed, is the sorrow Henry says follows merriment sooner or later, since the sin is destructive to health. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The sinner reproaches himself for hating instruction and despising reproof, making no excuse for his folly once the rooted habit has done its work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Not obeying teachers nor inclining the ear to instructors is the refusal of the very counsel Henry's caution offers, leaving the sinner without defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Being almost in all evil amid congregation and assembly shows how far the sin carries a man, for by frequent acts its habits become rooted and confirmed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Drinking waters from one's own cistern and well pictures lawful marriage, which Henry calls a means God appointed to keep from these destructive vices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Fountains dispersed abroad and rivers in the streets warn against scattering what belongs to the marriage bond, for the remedy is faithfulness kept within God's appointed means. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Letting the waters be only one's own and not strangers' with thee guards the marriage against the stranger, since Henry ties proper union to attending God's word with affection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. The fountain blessed and rejoicing with the wife of youth is the affectionate union Henry commends, sought under God's direction and blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Being ravished always with her love, as with the loving hind and pleasant roe, is the delight Henry sets within lawful marriage against the strange woman's lure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. To be ravished with a strange woman and embrace a stranger's bosom is the folly Henry asks why any son would choose, when the blessed fountain stands open at home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. A man's ways are before the eyes of the LORD, who ponders all his goings, so secret sins that escape fellow creatures never escape God, who sees and weighs them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. His own iniquities take the wicked and hold him with the cords of his sins, for those foolish enough to choose sin's way are justly left of God to themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Dying without instruction and going astray in great folly is the miserable end of the wicked, left to go on in the way to destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
