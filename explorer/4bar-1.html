The Text — 4 Baruch 1 (Ge'ez text (project Ge'ez corpus))

1 And it came to pass, when the king of the Chaldeans had taken the children of Israel captive, that the Lord spake unto Jeremiah, saying:

2 Jeremiah, my chosen, arise and go forth out of this city, thou and Baruch; for I will destroy it because of the multitude of the sins of them that dwell therein. For thy prayer is as a strong pillar in the midst of the city, and as a wall of adamant round about it. Now therefore arise ye, and go and depart, before the host of the Chaldeans come and compass the city.

3 And Jeremiah answered, saying: I beseech thee, my Lord, suffer thy servant to speak before thee.

4 And the Lord said unto him: Speak, my chosen Jeremiah.

5 And Jeremiah answered and said: O Lord of all, wilt thou deliver this chosen city into the hands of the Chaldeans, that the king with his people may boast and say, I have prevailed against the city of God?

6 Nay, my Lord; but if it please thee, destroy it with thine own hands.

7 And the Lord said unto Jeremiah: Because thou art my chosen, arise and go forth, thou and Baruch; for I will destroy it because of the sins of them that dwell therein. And neither the king nor his host shall be able to enter into the city, except I first open and enlarge the gates thereof.

8 Arise now, and go unto Baruch, and tell him this word; and when the sixth hour of the night is come, enter ye within the walls of the city, and I will shew you: for except I first destroy the city, they shall not be able to enter into it.

9 And when the Lord had spoken these things, he departed from Jeremiah.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

When the king of the Chaldeans has taken Israel captive, the Lord tells Jeremiah, his chosen, to arise and leave the city with Baruch before the Chaldean host compasses it, for the city will be destroyed for the multitude of its sins. Jeremiah's prayer, the Lord says, is as a strong pillar in the midst of the city and as a wall of adamant round about it — yet even that prayer will not hold back the destruction now decreed. Investigation (editorial — a question, not a conclusion)

This section investigates a question; it does not conclude it. Interpreted Editorial framing for this section

Parallel. 4 Baruch opens where Jeremiah and Baruch leave off: the Chaldeans have taken Israel captive, the Lord tells Jeremiah and Baruch to leave Jerusalem before it is destroyed, and the two figures at the centre are the prophet and scribe of the books of Jeremiah and Baruch. A reader of those books will recognise the same fall, the same city, and the same two men. Interpreted Chapter text of 4 Baruch 1; chapter text of Jeremiah 39 and Baruch 1

Differences. 4 Baruch then goes where Jeremiah and Baruch do not: an angel opens the gates to the Chaldeans, Abimelech sleeps sixty-six years under a tree and wakes to fresh figs, Baruch abides in a tomb, and an eagle carries messages — legendary expansions with no counterpart in Jeremiah or Baruch, and the book closes with a man supposed to be Jeremiah stoned while Jeremiah stands among them.
