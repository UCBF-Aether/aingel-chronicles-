The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 34 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 I will bless the LORD at all times: his praise shall continually be in my mouth.

2 My soul shall make her boast in the LORD: the humble shall hear thereof, and be glad.

3 O magnify the LORD with me, and let us exalt his name together.

4 I sought the LORD, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.

5 They looked unto him, and were lightened: and their faces were not ashamed.

6 This poor man cried, and the LORD heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles.

7 The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.

8 O taste and see that the LORD is good: blessed is the man that trusteth in him.

9 O fear the LORD, ye his saints: for there is no want to them that fear him.

10 The young lions do lack, and suffer hunger: but they that seek the LORD shall not want any good thing.

11 Come, ye children, hearken unto me: I will teach you the fear of the LORD.

12 What man is he that desireth life, and loveth many days, that he may see good?

13 Keep thy tongue from evil, and thy lips from speaking guile.

14 Depart from evil, and do good; seek peace, and pursue it.

15 The eyes of the LORD are upon the righteous, and his ears are open unto their cry.

16 The face of the LORD is against them that do evil, to cut off the remembrance of them from the earth.

17 The righteous cry, and the LORD heareth, and delivereth them out of all their troubles.

18 The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.

19 Many are the afflictions of the righteous: but the LORD delivereth him out of them all.

20 He keepeth all his bones: not one of them is broken.

21 Evil shall slay the wicked: and they that hate the righteous shall be desolate.

22 The LORD redeemeth the soul of his servants: and none of them that trust in him shall be desolate.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Blessing at all times: sought, heard, and delivered from all fears. The poor man cried and was saved, the angel encamps round the fearing, 'taste and see.' Then the psalm turns teacher, keep the tongue, depart from evil, pursue peace, for the Lord is near the brokenhearted, keeps all his bones, and redeems his servants from desolation. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. I will bless the Lord at all times; his praise shall continually be in my mouth: praise at all times, not only in deliverance, and continually, not occasionally. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. My soul shall make her boast in the Lord; the humble shall hear and be glad: boasting in God, unlike boasting in self, gladdens the humble who hear it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. O magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name together: praise is invited to become shared — magnification is a company act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. I sought the Lord, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears: seeking, hearing, and deliverance from all fears — not some — is the testimony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. They looked to him and were lightened, and their faces were not ashamed: looking to God lightens the face, and shame is removed by the look. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. This poor man cried, and the Lord heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles: the poor man's cry, not his worth, moved God to save from all troubles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The angel of the Lord encamps round about those who fear him, and delivers them: a single angel encamping like an army pictures God's surrounding guard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. O taste and see that the Lord is good; blessed is the man who trusts in him: goodness is to be tasted by experience, not merely heard of, and trust is the tasting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. O fear the Lord, you his saints, for there is no want to those who fear him: fear of God and freedom from want are joined — reverence is provision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The young lions lack and suffer hunger, but those who seek the Lord shall not want any good thing: even the strongest hunters hunger, while seekers lack no good thing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Come, you children, hearken to me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord: the psalm turns teacher, and the lesson is the fear of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. What man desires life, and loves many days, that he may see good? The question invites everyone who wants a good, long life to listen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Keep your tongue from evil, and your lips from speaking guile: the first lesson is the tongue — evil speech and guile must be kept, not merely regretted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Depart from evil, and do good; seek peace, and pursue it: the second lesson joins turning from evil to doing good, and seeking peace to pursuing it actively. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The eyes of the Lord are upon the righteous, and his ears open to their cry: God's watch and hearing are the encouragement for the life just described. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The face of the Lord is against evildoers, to cut off their remembrance from the earth: the same face that favours the righteous opposes the wicked, even to erasing their memory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The righteous cry, and the Lord hears, and delivers them out of all their troubles: the cry-hearing-deliverance pattern of verse 4 is repeated as a rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The Lord is near to the brokenhearted, and saves those of a contrite spirit: nearness is promised precisely to the broken and contrite, not to the proud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but the Lord delivers him out of them all: afflictions are many — righteousness does not exempt — but deliverance is out of them all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He keeps all his bones; not one of them is broken: preservation extends to the bones, and John applies this to Christ, whose bones were not broken on the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Evil shall slay the wicked, and those who hate the righteous shall be desolate: evil itself becomes the wicked's executioner, and hatred of the righteous ends in desolation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The Lord redeems the soul of his servants, and none who trust in him shall be desolate: redemption of the soul is the final answer to desolation — the trusting are never desolate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
