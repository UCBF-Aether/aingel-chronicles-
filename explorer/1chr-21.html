The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 21 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Satan stood up against Israel, and provoked David to number Israel.

2 And David said to Joab and to the rulers of the people, Go, number Israel from Beersheba even to Dan; and bring the number of them to me, that I may know it.

3 And Joab answered, The LORD make his people an hundred times so many more as they be: but, my lord the king, are they not all my lord’s servants? why then doth my lord require this thing? why will he be a cause of trespass to Israel?

4 Nevertheless the king’s word prevailed against Joab. Wherefore Joab departed, and went throughout all Israel, and came to Jerusalem.

5 And Joab gave the sum of the number of the people unto David. And all they of Israel were a thousand thousand and an hundred thousand men that drew sword: and Judah was four hundred threescore and ten thousand men that drew sword.

6 But Levi and Benjamin counted he not among them: for the king’s word was abominable to Joab.

7 And God was displeased with this thing; therefore he smote Israel.

8 And David said unto God, I have sinned greatly, because I have done this thing: but now, I beseech thee, do away the iniquity of thy servant; for I have done very foolishly.

9 And the LORD spake unto Gad, David’s seer, saying,

10 Go and tell David, saying, Thus saith the LORD, I offer thee three things: choose thee one of them, that I may do it unto thee.

11 So Gad came to David, and said unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Choose thee

12 Either three years’ famine; or three months to be destroyed before thy foes, while that the sword of thine enemies overtaketh thee; or else three days the sword of the LORD, even the pestilence, in the land, and the angel of the LORD destroying throughout all the coasts of Israel. Now therefore advise thyself what word I shall bring again to him that sent me.

13 And David said unto Gad, I am in a great strait: let me fall now into the hand of the LORD; for very great are his mercies: but let me not fall into the hand of man.

14 So the LORD sent pestilence upon Israel: and there fell of Israel seventy thousand men.

15 And God sent an angel unto Jerusalem to destroy it: and as he was destroying, the LORD beheld, and he repented him of the evil, and said to the angel that destroyed, It is enough, stay now thine hand. And the angel of the LORD stood by the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite.

16 And David lifted up his eyes, and saw the angel of the LORD stand between the earth and the heaven, having a drawn sword in his hand stretched out over Jerusalem. Then David and the elders of Israel, who were clothed in sackcloth, fell upon their faces.

17 And David said unto God, Is it not I that commanded the people to be numbered? even I it is that have sinned and done evil indeed; but as for these sheep, what have they done? let thine hand, I pray thee, O LORD my God, be on me, and on my father’s house; but not on thy people, that they should be plagued.

18 Then the angel of the LORD commanded Gad to say to David, that David should go up, and set up an altar unto the LORD in the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite.

19 And David went up at the saying of Gad, which he spake in the name of the LORD.

20 And Ornan turned back, and saw the angel; and his four sons with him hid themselves. Now Ornan was threshing wheat.

21 And as David came to Ornan, Ornan looked and saw David, and went out of the threshingfloor, and bowed himself to David with his face to the ground.

22 Then David said to Ornan, Grant me the place of this threshingfloor, that I may build an altar therein unto the LORD: thou shalt grant it me for the full price: that the plague may be stayed from the people.

23 And Ornan said unto David, Take it to thee, and let my lord the king do that which is good in his eyes: lo, I give thee the oxen also for burnt offerings, and the threshing instruments for wood, and the wheat for the meat offering; I give it all.

24 And king David said to Ornan, Nay; but I will verily buy it for the full price: for I will not take that which is thine for the LORD, nor offer burnt offerings without cost.

25 So David gave to Ornan for the place six hundred shekels of gold by weight.

26 And David built there an altar unto the LORD, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings, and called upon the LORD; and he answered him from heaven by fire upon the altar of burnt offering.

27 And the LORD commanded the angel; and he put up his sword again into the sheath thereof.

28 At that time when David saw that the LORD had answered him in the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite, then he sacrificed there.

29 For the tabernacle of the LORD, which Moses made in the wilderness, and the altar of the burnt offering, were at that season in the high place at Gibeon.

30 But David could not go before it to enquire of God: for he was afraid because of the sword of the angel of the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells David's numbering of Israel, the plague that followed, and David's purchase of Ornan's threshingfloor, where he built an altar and the plague was stayed — the site of the future temple. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Satan stood up against Israel and provoked David to number Israel. Henry notes the tempter's aim was to weaken Israel by tempting its best friend to pride, the sin most offensive to God, and even the best saints are not out of Satan's reach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. David said to Joab and the rulers, go, number Israel from Beer-sheba to Dan, and bring the number to me that I may know it. Henry notes numbering seemed innocent, as a shepherd knowing his flock, but done in pride of heart it was a great provocation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Joab answered, the Lord make his people a hundred times so many more, but why doth my lord require this thing? Why will he be a cause of trespass to Israel? Henry notes Joab's remonstrance: it was needless, since God promised multiplication, and dangerous, since it might provoke God against Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Nevertheless the king's word prevailed against Joab, so Joab departed, went throughout Israel, and came to Jerusalem. Henry notes Joab obeyed only because forced, doing the work with the greatest reluctance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Joab gave the sum of the number to David: all Israel were 1,100,000 men that drew sword, and Judah 470,000. Henry notes the muster was really very great, yet Joab had no heart to perfect it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. But Levi and Benjamin counted he not among them, for the king's word was abominable to Joab. Henry notes Joab left two considerable tribes unnumbered, and was perhaps inexact elsewhere, which may explain differences from Samuel's sums. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. God was displeased with this thing, therefore he smote Israel. Henry notes God's dearest children must smart for wrongdoing, and no sin is more displeasing than pride of heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. David said unto God, I have sinned greatly, because I have done this thing, but now take away the iniquity of thy servant, for I have done very foolishly. Henry notes David's penitent confession: he owned he had sinned greatly and foolishly, and prayed the iniquity be done away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The word of the Lord came unto Gad, David's seer, saying. Henry introduces Gad as the prophet through whom David is put to his choice of punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Go and tell David, thus saith the Lord, I offer thee three things, choose thee one of them, that I may do it unto thee. Henry notes David must be punished by one of three judgments, for his further humiliation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. So Gad came to David and said, thus saith the Lord, choose thee. Henry presents Gad delivering the trilemma to David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Either three years' famine, or three months to be destroyed before thy foes, or else three days the sword of the Lord, even pestilence in the land, and the angel destroying throughout Israel. Henry lists the three rods: war, famine, or pestilence, with the terror of all three impressed on David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. David said, I am in a great strait: let me fall now into the hand of the Lord, for very great are his mercies, but let me not fall into the hand of man. Henry praises David casting himself on God's mercy even while God frowned: though he slay me, yet will I trust him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. So the Lord sent pestilence upon Israel, and there fell of Israel seventy thousand men. Henry notes divine justice made the people fewer, whom David was proud of numbering: God numbered them to the sword, a black bill of mortality against his muster-roll. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. God sent an angel unto Jerusalem to destroy it, and as he was destroying, the Lord beheld, and repented him of the evil, and said to the angel, it is enough, stay now thine hand. And the angel stood by the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite. Henry notes when David repented, God repented of the judgment and stayed the angel's hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. David lifted up his eyes and saw the angel of the Lord stand between earth and heaven, having a drawn sword stretched out over Jerusalem, and David and the elders fell upon their faces. Henry notes the terrible sight: while we lie under God's wrath, holy angels are armed against us, though unseen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. David said, is it not I that commanded the people to be numbered? Even I it is that have sinned and done evil indeed; but as for these sheep, what have they done? Let thine hand be upon me and my father's house, but not on thy people, that they should be plagued. Henry notes David accepted punishment, asked to be the sufferer as the sinner, and showed tender concern for the people plagued for his transgression. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Then the angel of the Lord commanded Gad to say to David that he should go up and set up an altar unto the Lord in the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite. Henry notes the same angel who carried on the war set on foot the treaty of peace, commanding the altar through the seer to honour the prophetic office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. David went up at the saying of Gad, which he spake in the name of the Lord. Henry notes David immediately obeyed the direction to build the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Ornan turned back and saw the angel, and his four sons with him hid themselves. Henry notes Ornan had himself seen the angel and was terrified by its brightness and drawn sword, so he hid with his sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. As David came to Ornan, Ornan looked and saw David, and went out of the threshingfloor and bowed himself to David with his face to the ground. Henry presents Ornan's reverent meeting of the king on the threshingfloor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. David said to Ornan, grant me the place of this threshingfloor, that I may build an altar unto the Lord, for the full price, that the plague may be stayed from the people. Henry notes David's bargain: he would not serve God at other people's charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Ornan said unto David, take it to thee, and let my lord the king do that which is good in his eyes: lo, I give thee the oxen also for burnt offerings, and the threshing instruments for wood, and the wheat for the meat offering; I give it all. Henry notes Ornan generously offered it gratis, willing to do anything toward atonement after seeing the angel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. King David said to Ornan, nay, but I will verily buy it for the full price, for I will not take that which is thine for the Lord, nor offer burnt offerings without cost. Henry stresses David's principle: he would not serve God at another's charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. So David gave to Ornan for the place six hundred shekels of gold by weight. Henry records the full-price purchase of the threshingfloor, the future temple site. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. David built there an altar unto the Lord, offered burnt offerings and peace offerings, and called upon the Lord, and he answered him from heaven by fire upon the altar of burnt offering. Henry notes God's acceptance: the fire that might have fastened on the sinner fastened on the sacrifice, signifying anger turned away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And the Lord commanded the angel, and he put up his sword again into the sheath thereof. Henry notes the destroying sword returned to its sheath once atonement was accepted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. At that time when David saw that the Lord had answered him in the threshingfloor of Ornan, he sacrificed there. Henry notes David continued sacrificing where God had graciously met him, expecting to meet him there still. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For the tabernacle of the Lord, which Moses made in the wilderness, and the altar of burnt offering, were at that season in the high place at Gibeon. Henry notes the Mosaic altar remained at Gibeon, while David's new altar stood at Ornan's floor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. But David could not go before it to enquire of God, for he was afraid because of the sword of the angel of the Lord. Henry notes David dared not leave the angel with drawn sword over Jerusalem, and the business required haste, so God dispensed with one altar in the present distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Investigation (editorial — a question, not a conclusion)

This section investigates a question; it does not conclude it. The study note for verse 6 observes that Joab left Levi and Benjamin unnumbered and was perhaps inexact elsewhere, which may explain differences from Samuel's sums for the numbering.

Parallels. Both accounts tell the same numbering of Israel under David. Differences. The totals differ, and this chapter's own account shows the numbering incomplete, with two tribes left out.

Whether Joab's incompleteness fully accounts for the difference, and which total is correct, is not established by the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
