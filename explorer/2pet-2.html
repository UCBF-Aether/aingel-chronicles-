The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Peter 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 But there were false prophets also among the people, even as there shall be false teachers among you, who privily shall bring in damnable heresies, even denying the Lord that bought them, and bring upon themselves swift destruction.

2 And many shall follow their pernicious ways; by reason of whom the way of truth shall be evil spoken of.

3 And through covetousness shall they with feigned words make merchandise of you: whose judgment now of a long time lingereth not, and their damnation slumbereth not.

4 For if God spared not the angels that sinned, but cast them down to hell, and delivered them into chains of darkness, to be reserved unto judgment;

5 And spared not the old world, but saved Noah the eighth person, a preacher of righteousness, bringing in the flood upon the world of the ungodly;

6 And turning the cities of Sodom and Gomorrha into ashes condemned them with an overthrow, making them an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly;

7 And delivered just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked:

8 (For that righteous man dwelling among them, in seeing and hearing, vexed his righteous soul from day to day with their unlawful deeds;)

9 The Lord knoweth how to deliver the godly out of temptations, and to reserve the unjust unto the day of judgment to be punished:

10 But chiefly them that walk after the flesh in the lust of uncleanness, and despise government. Presumptuous are they, selfwilled, they are not afraid to speak evil of dignities.

11 Whereas angels, which are greater in power and might, bring not railing accusation against them before the Lord.

12 But these, as natural brute beasts, made to be taken and destroyed, speak evil of the things that they understand not; and shall utterly perish in their own corruption;

13 And shall receive the reward of unrighteousness, as they that count it pleasure to riot in the day time. Spots they are and blemishes, sporting themselves with their own deceivings while they feast with you;

14 Having eyes full of adultery, and that cannot cease from sin; beguiling unstable souls: an heart they have exercised with covetous practices; cursed children:

15 Which have forsaken the right way, and are gone astray, following the way of Balaam the son of Bosor, who loved the wages of unrighteousness;

16 But was rebuked for his iniquity: the dumb ass speaking with man’s voice forbad the madness of the prophet.

17 These are wells without water, clouds that are carried with a tempest; to whom the mist of darkness is reserved for ever.

18 For when they speak great swelling words of vanity, they allure through the lusts of the flesh, through much wantonness, those that were clean escaped from them who live in error.

19 While they promise them liberty, they themselves are the servants of corruption: for of whom a man is overcome, of the same is he brought in bondage.

20 For if after they have escaped the pollutions of the world through the knowledge of the Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, they are again entangled therein, and overcome, the latter end is worse with them than the beginning.

21 For it had been better for them not to have known the way of righteousness, than, after they have known it, to turn from the holy commandment delivered unto them.

22 But it is happened unto them according to the true proverb, The dog is turned to his own vomit again; and the sow that was washed to her wallowing in the mire.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Just as false prophets arose in Israel, false teachers will arise among readers, Peter warns, secretly bringing destructive heresies, denying the Master who bought them, and leading many into shameful ways through greed. Judgment is illustrated by fallen angels, the flood with Noah preserved, and Sodom with Lot rescued, showing the Lord knows how to rescue the godly and hold the unjust for judgment, while the teachers themselves are described as spots, wells without water and clouds without rain, promising freedom while slaves of corruption. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. As false prophets once rose among the people, so false teachers will secretly bring in destructive heresies, even denying the Lord who bought them. Henry warns that the way of error is a hurtful way, yet many are always ready to walk in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Many will follow the seducers' pernicious ways, and the way of truth will be evil spoken of because of them. Henry urges believers to give the enemy no occasion to blaspheme the holy name by which they are called. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Through covetousness the seducers will use feigned words to make merchandise of their followers, but their judgment does not linger. Henry notes such deceivers are condemned already, and the wrath of God abides upon them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. God did not spare the angels that sinned, but cast them down to hell in chains of darkness reserved for judgment. Henry draws the lesson that if creatures sin even in heaven, they must suffer in hell, for sin is the work of darkness and darkness its wages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. God did not spare the old world, but brought the flood on the ungodly while saving Noah, the eighth person and a preacher of righteousness. Henry notes that neither the number nor the quality of offenders procures favour: where sin is universal, punishment likewise extends to all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Sodom and Gomorrha were turned into ashes and condemned with an overthrow, as an ensample to those who would live ungodly afterward. Henry observes that God can at once turn a fruitful land into barrenness, and no plans or politics can keep judgments from a sinful people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked, was delivered out of Sodom. Henry notes that when God sends destruction on the ungodly, he commands deliverance for the righteous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Dwelling among the wicked, the righteous man vexed his soul day by day with their unlawful deeds, in seeing and hearing. Henry observes that in bad company we cannot but get either guilt or grief, yet a child of the Lord may retain integrity among the most profane, since the grace of Christ in him is stronger than temptation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The Lord knows how to deliver the godly out of temptations and to reserve the unjust for punishment at the day of judgment. Henry adds that wicked men often escape suffering here precisely because they are kept to that day, to be punished with the devil and his angels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Chiefly punished are those who walk after the flesh in the lust of uncleanness and despise government, presumptuous and self-willed, unafraid to speak evil of dignities. Henry describes such seducers as giving themselves up to their own fleshly minds, refusing to bring every thought to the obedience of Christ, and despising those whom God has set in authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Angels, greater in power and might, bring no railing accusation against dignities before the Lord, in contrast to the seducers' boldness. Henry's point is that those who despise God-set authority presume further than even mightier angels dare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Like natural brute beasts made to be taken and destroyed, the seducers speak evil of things they understand not and shall perish in their own corruption. Henry notes they walk after the flesh, going on in sinful courses and increasing to greater degrees of impurity and wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. They shall receive the reward of unrighteousness, counting it pleasure to riot in the daytime, spots and blemishes sporting in their own deceivings while they feast with believers. Henry observes that outward temporal good things are the wages such sinners expect and promise themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Having eyes full of adultery that cannot cease from sin, they beguile unstable souls, with hearts exercised in covetous practices, cursed children. Henry warns that none have more cause to tremble than those bold to gratify sinful lusts by presuming on divine grace and mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Having forsaken the right way, they have gone astray following the way of Balaam, who loved the wages of unrighteousness. Henry's account of the seducers fits here: they leave the straight road for the wages sin promises, speaking lightly of the restraints of God's law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Balaam was rebuked for his iniquity when the dumb ass, speaking with a man's voice, forbade the madness of the prophet. Henry's warning stands behind the example: Christians should stand at a distance from such presumptuous teachers rather than follow their folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. These are wells without water, clouds carried with a tempest, for whom the mist of darkness is reserved for ever. Henry explains that deceivers are empty because there is no truth in them, and as they increase darkness in this world, the mist of darkness is justly their portion in the next. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. With great swelling words of vanity they allure through fleshly lusts and wantonness those who had clean escaped from error. Henry notes that as clouds hinder the light of the sun, so these darken counsel by words wherein there is no truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. While they promise liberty, they are themselves servants of corruption, for whoever is overcome is brought into bondage. Henry observes that in the midst of their talk of liberty, these men are the vilest slaves, their own lusts gaining complete victory over them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. If those who escaped the pollutions of the world through knowing Christ are again entangled and overcome, their latter end is worse than the beginning. Henry warns that when men are entangled they are easily overcome, so Christians should keep close to the word of God and watch against all who seek to bewilder them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. It had been better not to have known the way of righteousness than, having known it, to turn from the holy commandment delivered to them. Henry states plainly that a state of apostasy is worse than a state of ignorance, and bringing an evil report on the good way exposes a person to the heaviest condemnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. The true proverb has happened to them: the dog returns to his own vomit, and the washed sow to her wallowing in the mire. Henry calls the state dreadful, yet adds that such a case is not utterly hopeless: the leper may be made clean, and the backslider who grieves should believe in the Lord Jesus and be saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
