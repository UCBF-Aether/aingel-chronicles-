The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — John 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 These things have I spoken unto you, that ye should not be offended.

2 They shall put you out of the synagogues: yea, the time cometh, that whosoever killeth you will think that he doeth God service.

3 And these things will they do unto you, because they have not known the Father, nor me.

4 But these things have I told you, that when the time shall come, ye may remember that I told you of them. And these things I said not unto you at the beginning, because I was with you.

5 But now I go my way to him that sent me; and none of you asketh me, Whither goest thou?

6 But because I have said these things unto you, sorrow hath filled your heart.

7 Nevertheless I tell you the truth; It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you.

8 And when he is come, he will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:

9 Of sin, because they believe not on me;

10 Of righteousness, because I go to my Father, and ye see me no more;

11 Of judgment, because the prince of this world is judged.

12 I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now.

13 Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth: for he shall not speak of himself; but whatsoever he shall hear, that shall he speak: and he will shew you things to come.

14 He shall glorify me: for he shall receive of mine, and shall shew it unto you.

15 All things that the Father hath are mine: therefore said I, that he shall take of mine, and shall shew it unto you.

16 A little while, and ye shall not see me: and again, a little while, and ye shall see me, because I go to the Father.

17 Then said some of his disciples among themselves, What is this that he saith unto us, A little while, and ye shall not see me: and again, a little while, and ye shall see me: and, Because I go to the Father?

18 They said therefore, What is this that he saith, A little while? we cannot tell what he saith.

19 Now Jesus knew that they were desirous to ask him, and said unto them, Do ye enquire among yourselves of that I said, A little while, and ye shall not see me: and again, a little while, and ye shall see me?

20 Verily, verily, I say unto you, That ye shall weep and lament, but the world shall rejoice: and ye shall be sorrowful, but your sorrow shall be turned into joy.

21 A woman when she is in travail hath sorrow, because her hour is come: but as soon as she is delivered of the child, she remembereth no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world.

22 And ye now therefore have sorrow: but I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice, and your joy no man taketh from you.

23 And in that day ye shall ask me nothing. Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whatsoever ye shall ask the Father in my name, he will give it you.

24 Hitherto have ye asked nothing in my name: ask, and ye shall receive, that your joy may be full.

25 These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs: but the time cometh, when I shall no more speak unto you in proverbs, but I shall shew you plainly of the Father.

26 At that day ye shall ask in my name: and I say not unto you, that I will pray the Father for you:

27 For the Father himself loveth you, because ye have loved me, and have believed that I came out from God.

28 I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world: again, I leave the world, and go to the Father.

29 His disciples said unto him, Lo, now speakest thou plainly, and speakest no proverb.

30 Now are we sure that thou knowest all things, and needest not that any man should ask thee: by this we believe that thou camest forth from God.

31 Jesus answered them, Do ye now believe?

32 Behold, the hour cometh, yea, is now come, that ye shall be scattered, every man to his own, and shall leave me alone: and yet I am not alone, because the Father is with me.

33 These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jesus warns that the disciples will be put out of the synagogues and killed by men who think they do God service, and promises that it is expedient he go away, so the Comforter will come to convict the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment (vv. 1–15). “A little while, and ye shall not see me” becomes sorrow turned to joy, like a woman in travail, and the promise that whatever they ask the Father in his name will be given (vv. 16–28). The chapter closes with plain speaking about leaving the Father and returning to him, the disciples’ confession, and his final word of peace: in the world they will have tribulation, but he has overcome the world (vv. 29–33). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, John 16

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These things have I spoken unto you, that you should not be offended, he said, for notice of trouble is given so terror will not surprise his disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. They shall put you out of the synagogues, he said; yea, the time comes, that whoever kills you will think he does God service, for real enemies of God's service may pretend zeal for it, and villainy is not changed by putting God's name to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And these things will they do unto you, because they have not known the Father, nor me, for ignorance of God is the root of persecution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. But these things I have told you, that when the time shall come, you may remember I told you of them, and these things I said not at the beginning, because I was with you, for while he was with them to teach, guide, and comfort, they did not yet need this promise of the Spirit's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. But now I go my way to him who sent me, he said, and none of you asks me, Whither goest thou, for sorrow had silenced their questions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. But because I have said these things unto you, sorrow has filled your heart, for too great affection for this present life hinders joy in God, and melancholy Christians look only on the dark side of the cloud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Nevertheless I tell you the truth, he said; it is expedient for you that I go away, for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you, but if I depart, I will send him unto you, for his bodily presence could be in only one place at a time, but his Spirit is everywhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And when he is come, he will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment, for convincing work is the Spirit's work, and none but he can do it effectually. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Of sin, because they believe not on me, for the Spirit convinces of the fact, fault, folly, filth, fountain, and fruit of sin, proving all the world guilty before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Of righteousness, because I go to my Father, and you see me no more, for Christ's ascension proves the ransom was accepted and righteousness finished, through which believers are justified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Of judgment, because the prince of this world is judged, for when Satan's power is broken, all will be well, and no other power can stand before Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. I have yet many things to say unto you, but you cannot bear them now, for their capacity was not yet enlarged by the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. However, when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth, for he shall not speak of himself, but whatsoever he shall hear, that shall he speak, and he will show you things to come, for the Spirit is our Guide, not only to show the way but to go with us by continued aids. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He shall glorify me, he said, for he shall receive of mine, and shall show it unto you, for all the gifts and graces of the Spirit, all apostolic preaching and writing, were to glorify Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. All things that the Father has are mine, he said; therefore I said, he shall take of mine, and shall show it unto you, for Father and Son share all things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. A little while, and you shall not see me, he said, and again, a little while, and you shall see me, because I go to the Father, for it is good to consider how near our seasons of grace are to an end, that we may improve them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Then some of his disciples said among themselves, What is this that he says unto us, A little while, and you shall not see me, and again, a little while, and you shall see me, and, Because I go to the Father, for the saying perplexed them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. They said therefore, What is this that he says, A little while, for we cannot tell what he says, repeating the riddle to one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Now Jesus knew they were desirous to ask him, and said unto them, Do you inquire among yourselves of that I said, A little while, and you shall not see me, and again, a little while, and you shall see me, answering their unspoken question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Verily, verily, I say unto you, that you shall weep and lament, but the world shall rejoice, and you shall be sorrowful, but your sorrow shall be turned into joy, for their sorrows would soon be turned into joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. A woman when she is in travail has sorrow, because her hour is come, but as soon as she is delivered of the child, she remembers no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world, the very image of sorrow turned to joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And you now therefore have sorrow, he said, but I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice, and your joy no man takes from you, for neither men nor devils, neither sufferings in life nor in death, can deprive believers of their joy in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And in that day you shall ask me nothing, he said; verily, verily, whatever you shall ask the Father in my name, he will give it you, for asking of the Father shows a sense of spiritual wants and conviction they are to be had from God only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Hitherto you have asked nothing in my name, he said; ask, and you shall receive, that your joy may be full, for asking in Christ's name acknowledges our unworthiness and full dependence on him as our Righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs, he said, but the time comes, when I shall no more speak unto you in proverbs, but I shall show you plainly of the Father, for after his resurrection he intended plainly to teach them of the Father and the way to him through his intercession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. At that day you shall ask in my name, he said, and I say not unto you, that I will pray the Father for you, for the frequency of this command shows the great end of Christ's mediation: to impress us with our sinfulness and the merit and power of his death, whereby we have access to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. For the Father himself loves you, because you have loved me, and have believed that I came out from God, for to address the Father in Christ's name and to address the Son as God in human nature reconciling the world are the same, as Father and Son are one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world, he said; again, I leave the world, and go to the Father, a plain declaration of his coming from the Father and his return to him, God manifest in the flesh at his entrance and received up into glory at his departure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. His disciples said, Lo, now speakest thou plainly, and speakest no proverb, for by this saying they improved in knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Now are we sure thou knowest all things, they said, and needest not that any man should ask thee; by this we believe thou camest forth from God, for they improved also in faith, saying, Now are we sure, though they knew not their own weakness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Jesus answered them, Do ye now believe, questioning a faith that would so soon be tested. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Behold, the hour comes, yea, is now come, that you shall be scattered, every man to his own, and shall leave me alone, and yet I am not alone, because the Father is with me, for the divine nature did not desert the human, but supported it and put comfort and value into his sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. These things I have spoken unto you, that in me you might have peace, he said; in the world you shall have tribulation, but be of good cheer, I have overcome the world, for peace in Christ is the only true peace, through him we have peace with God and in our own minds, and while we think we stand, let us take heed lest we fall, watching and praying without ceasing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
