The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Peter 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Simon Peter, a servant and an apostle of Jesus Christ, to them that have obtained like precious faith with us through the righteousness of God and our Saviour Jesus Christ:

2 Grace and peace be multiplied unto you through the knowledge of God, and of Jesus our Lord,

3 According as his divine power hath given unto us all things that pertain unto life and godliness, through the knowledge of him that hath called us to glory and virtue:

4 Whereby are given unto us exceeding great and precious promises: that by these ye might be partakers of the divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world through lust.

5 And beside this, giving all diligence, add to your faith virtue; and to virtue knowledge;

6 And to knowledge temperance; and to temperance patience; and to patience godliness;

7 And to godliness brotherly kindness; and to brotherly kindness charity.

8 For if these things be in you, and abound, they make you that ye shall neither be barren nor unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.

9 But he that lacketh these things is blind, and cannot see afar off, and hath forgotten that he was purged from his old sins.

10 Wherefore the rather, brethren, give diligence to make your calling and election sure: for if ye do these things, ye shall never fall:

11 For so an entrance shall be ministered unto you abundantly into the everlasting kingdom of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.

12 Wherefore I will not be negligent to put you always in remembrance of these things, though ye know them, and be established in the present truth.

13 Yea, I think it meet, as long as I am in this tabernacle, to stir you up by putting you in remembrance;

14 Knowing that shortly I must put off this my tabernacle, even as our Lord Jesus Christ hath shewed me.

15 Moreover I will endeavour that ye may be able after my decease to have these things always in remembrance.

16 For we have not followed cunningly devised fables, when we made known unto you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, but were eyewitnesses of his majesty.

17 For he received from God the Father honour and glory, when there came such a voice to him from the excellent glory, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.

18 And this voice which came from heaven we heard, when we were with him in the holy mount.

19 We have also a more sure word of prophecy; whereunto ye do well that ye take heed, as unto a light that shineth in a dark place, until the day dawn, and the day star arise in your hearts:

20 Knowing this first, that no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation.

21 For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Simon Peter writes grace and peace through knowledge of God and Jesus, whose divine power has given everything needed for life and godliness through precious promises, by which believers partake of the divine nature and escape the world's corruption. To faith they are to add virtue, knowledge, self-control, endurance, godliness, brotherly kindness and love, making their calling and election sure. Peter, knowing his departure is near, stresses he writes as an eyewitness of Christ's majesty on the holy mountain, and that prophecy comes not by private interpretation but as holy men spoke moved by the Spirit. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Simon Peter, servant and apostle of Jesus Christ, writes to those who have obtained like precious faith through the righteousness of God and our Saviour Jesus Christ. Henry notes faith unites the weak believer to Christ as really as the strong, and every sincere believer is justified by it in God's sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Grace and peace are multiplied to believers through the knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord. Henry stresses that in Christ all fulness dwells, and pardon, peace, grace, and knowledge are given through the Holy Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. God's divine power has given us all things that pertain to life and godliness, through the knowledge of him who called us to glory and virtue. Henry urges turning these promises into prayers for the transforming grace of the Holy Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. By exceeding great and precious promises we become partakers of the divine nature, escaping the corruption in the world through lust. Henry says these promises should make us inquire whether we are really renewed in the spirit of our minds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Beside this, believers are to give all diligence to add virtue to their faith. Henry notes faith worketh godliness and produces effects no other grace in the soul can produce. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. To virtue we must add knowledge, increasing acquaintance with the whole truth and will of God; to knowledge, temperance, moderation about worldly things; to temperance, patience, cheerful submission to the will of God; and to patience, godliness. Henry explains that tribulation worketh patience, whereby we bear calamities and crosses with silence and submission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. To godliness we must add brotherly kindness, tender affection to fellow Christians as children of the same Father, servants of the same Master, and heirs of the same inheritance; and to brotherly kindness, charity. Henry stresses this family bond among believers, travellers to the same country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. If these graces be in us and abound, we shall be neither barren nor unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ. Henry presents this fruitfulness as the proof that faith is alive and working godliness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Whoever lacks these things is blind and cannot see afar off, having forgotten he was purged from his old sins. Henry contrasts such forgetfulness with the diligence that keeps the believer mindful of cleansing grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. We are therefore to give diligence to make our calling and election sure, for by doing these things we shall never fall. Henry says labouring for assurance by believing and well-doing is itself a firm argument of God's grace upholding us so we shall not utterly fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. So an entrance shall be ministered abundantly into the everlasting kingdom of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. Henry promises the diligent a triumphant entrance into that kingdom where Christ reigns and they shall reign with him for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Peter will therefore always put his readers in remembrance of these things, though they know them and are established in the present truth. Henry notes we must be established in the truth, especially the truth necessary for our own day, so as not to be shaken by every wind of doctrine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. As long as Peter is in this tabernacle, he thinks it meet to stir his readers up by putting them in remembrance. Henry pictures the body as but a tabernacle, or tent, of the soul — a mean and movable dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Peter knows he must shortly put off this tabernacle, as the Lord Jesus Christ has showed him. Henry observes the nearness of death made the apostle diligent in the business of life, and nothing gives composure in death's prospect like knowing we have faithfully followed the Lord Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Peter will endeavour that after his decease his readers may always have these things in remembrance. Henry notes that by the written word, those who fear the Lord and talk of his loving-kindness are enabled to spread the knowledge of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Peter insists they did not follow cunningly devised fables when they made known Christ's power and coming, but were eyewitnesses of his majesty. Henry declares the gospel is no weak thing but comes in power, and it is the sight of Jesus crucified that heals the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Christ received honour and glory from God the Father when the voice from the excellent glory said, "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased." Henry notes God is well pleased with Christ, and with us in him, the promised Messiah through whom believers are accepted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Peter and his companions heard this voice when they were with Christ in the holy mount. Henry treats this eyewitness testimony, joined to prophecy, as the firm ground of faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. We have also a more sure word of prophecy, to which we do well to take heed, as to a light shining in a dark place until the day dawn and the day star arise in our hearts. Henry likens Scripture's light, darted into the blind mind by the Holy Spirit, to daybreak diffusing itself through the soul till it makes perfect day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. No prophecy of Scripture is of any private interpretation. Henry explains that the prophets and penmen of the Old Testament spoke and wrote under the influence and direction of the Spirit of God, so every man ought to search Scripture to understand its sense and meaning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Prophecy came not in old time by the will of man, but holy men of God spoke as they were moved by the Holy Ghost. Henry concludes the Scriptures are to be accounted the words of the Holy Ghost, to be esteemed and reverenced as a book written by holy men taught by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

  • tabernacle. a tent, the chapter’s image for the body as the soul’s temporary dwelling, soon to be put off (verses 13–14) Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
