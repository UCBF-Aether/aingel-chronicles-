The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Unto the angel of the church of Ephesus write; These things saith he that holdeth the seven stars in his right hand, who walketh in the midst of the seven golden candlesticks;

2 I know thy works, and thy labour, and thy patience, and how thou canst not bear them which are evil: and thou hast tried them which say they are apostles, and are not, and hast found them liars:

3 And hast borne, and hast patience, and for my name’s sake hast laboured, and hast not fainted.

4 Nevertheless I have somewhat against thee, because thou hast left thy first love.

5 Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works; or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will remove thy candlestick out of his place, except thou repent.

6 But this thou hast, that thou hatest the deeds of the Nicolaitanes, which I also hate.

7 He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God.

8 And unto the angel of the church in Smyrna write; These things saith the first and the last, which was dead, and is alive;

9 I know thy works, and tribulation, and poverty, (but thou art rich) and I know the blasphemy of them which say they are Jews, and are not, but are the synagogue of Satan.

10 Fear none of those things which thou shalt suffer: behold, the devil shall cast some of you into prison, that ye may be tried; and ye shall have tribulation ten days: be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life.

11 He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; He that overcometh shall not be hurt of the second death.

12 And to the angel of the church in Pergamos write; These things saith he which hath the sharp sword with two edges;

13 I know thy works, and where thou dwellest, even where Satan’s seat is: and thou holdest fast my name, and hast not denied my faith, even in those days wherein Antipas was my faithful martyr, who was slain among you, where Satan dwelleth.

14 But I have a few things against thee, because thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel, to eat things sacrificed unto idols, and to commit fornication.

15 So hast thou also them that hold the doctrine of the Nicolaitanes, which thing I hate.

16 Repent; or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will fight against them with the sword of my mouth.

17 He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the hidden manna, and will give him a white stone, and in the stone a new name written, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it.

18 And unto the angel of the church in Thyatira write; These things saith the Son of God, who hath his eyes like unto a flame of fire, and his feet are like fine brass;

19 I know thy works, and charity, and service, and faith, and thy patience, and thy works; and the last to be more than the first.

20 Notwithstanding I have a few things against thee, because thou sufferest that woman Jezebel, which calleth herself a prophetess, to teach and to seduce my servants to commit fornication, and to eat things sacrificed unto idols.

21 And I gave her space to repent of her fornication; and she repented not.

22 Behold, I will cast her into a bed, and them that commit adultery with her into great tribulation, except they repent of their deeds.

23 And I will kill her children with death; and all the churches shall know that I am he which searcheth the reins and hearts: and I will give unto every one of you according to your works.

24 But unto you I say, and unto the rest in Thyatira, as many as have not this doctrine, and which have not known the depths of Satan, as they speak; I will put upon you none other burden.

25 But that which ye have already hold fast till I come.

26 And he that overcometh, and keepeth my works unto the end, to him will I give power over the nations:

27 And he shall rule them with a rod of iron; as the vessels of a potter shall they be broken to shivers: even as I received of my Father.

28 And I will give him the morning star.

29 He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Four letters follow one shape: Christ names himself by a title fitting the church, commends what is good, reproves what is wrong, calls for repentance, and promises the overcomer. Ephesus has lost its first love yet hates the Nicolaitans' deeds; Smyrna is outwardly poor yet rich, and must be faithful unto death; Pergamos holds fast in a hard place yet tolerates Balaam's doctrine and the Nicolaitans; Thyatira's works grow, yet it suffers the seducing prophetess Jezebel. Henry's notes stress that what is said to one church concerns all. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The message to Ephesus comes from him who holds the seven stars in his right hand and walks among the seven golden candlesticks. Henry notes ministers are in Christ's hand, directed, filled with light, and supported, while Christ himself is intimately present in his churches, observing what is amiss and what they lack. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Christ knows Ephesus' works, labour, and patience, and that they cannot bear evil men, having tried those who claimed to be apostles and found them liars. Henry commends their diligence, their bearing and waiting patience, and their discreet zeal, which tried pretences before casting anyone off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They have borne up and have patience, labouring for Christ's name without fainting. Henry notes Christ keeps an account of every day's and hour's work his servants do for him, and their labour shall not be in vain in the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Nevertheless Christ has somewhat against them: they have left their first love. Henry explains they had not forsaken its object but lost its fervent degree, and that lively first affections cool without constant care, grieving Christ, who will faithfully reprove what he sees amiss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They are told to remember from where they have fallen, repent, and do the first works, or Christ will come quickly and remove their candlestick unless they repent. Henry urges them to compare present with former state, grieve inwardly, and begin again step by step, reviving first zeal, tenderness, and seriousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Yet Ephesus retains this: they hate the deeds of the Nicolaitans, which Christ also hates. Henry notes the Nicolaitans were a loose sect sheltering under Christianity with hateful doctrines and deeds, and praises Ephesus' just abhorrence, since indifference between truth and error is not pleasing to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches, and to the overcomer Christ will give to eat of the tree of life in the midst of the paradise of God. Henry notes what is said to one church concerns all, and that persevering victors receive from Christ the perfection and confirmation in holiness Adam would have had. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The message to Smyrna comes from the first and the last, who was dead and is alive. Henry notes this title suits a suffering church: Christ is from everlasting to everlasting, died to purchase salvation, and ever lives to apply it and make intercession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Christ knows Smyrna's works, tribulation, and poverty, yet says, "but thou art rich," and knows the blasphemy of those who say they are Jews and are not, but are the synagogue of Satan. Henry contrasts outward poverty with spiritual riches in faith, works, privileges, and hope, and warns that assemblies opposing gospel truth and worship are Satan's synagogue, not God's church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They are told to fear none of the coming sufferings: the devil will cast some of them into prison to be tried, with tribulation ten days, but they must be faithful unto death to receive a crown of life. Henry notes the trial is limited, not universal and not perpetual, meant to prove faith, patience, and courage, and the crown suits those who part with life itself in fidelity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says, and the overcomer shall not be hurt of the second death. Henry explains the second death as eternal, the dying of the soul without mixture of support, from which Christ saves all his faithful servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The message to Pergamos comes from him who has the sharp sword with two edges. Henry notes the title suits a church infested with corrupt teachers: Christ will fight them with the sword of his word, which cuts every way, with the edge of law and the edge of gospel, wounding and then opening wounds to heal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Christ knows Pergamos' works and where they dwell, even where Satan's seat is, yet they hold fast his name and have not denied his faith, even when Antipas, his faithful martyr, was slain among them. Henry notes Christ makes gracious allowance for hard places, and that their steadfastness shone brighter for Antipas' martyrdom in Satan's very seat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Yet Christ has a few things against them: they have those who hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balak to put a stumblingblock before Israel, to eat things sacrificed to idols and commit fornication. Henry observes corrupt doctrine and corrupt worship lead to corrupt conduct, as Balaam drew Israel by impure worship into impure practice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. So they also have those who hold the doctrine of the Nicolaitans, which Christ hates. Henry warns that continuing in communion with corrupt principles displeases God and draws guilt on the whole society, which must exclude such persons from communion or face Christ's displeasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. They must repent, or Christ will come quickly and fight against them with the sword of his mouth. Henry notes repentance is the duty of churches as well as persons, including repentance for others' sins so far as they were accessory by connivance, and that no sword cuts so deep as the sword of Christ's mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. To the overcomer Christ will give hidden manna, a white stone, and a new name written on it, known only to the receiver. Henry explains the manna as the Spirit's comforts in communion with Christ, hidden from the world, and the white stone as absolution, after the ancient custom of acquittal, with the new name as the name of adoption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The message to Thyatira comes from the Son of God, whose eyes are like a flame of fire and whose feet are like fine brass. Henry notes the eyes signify piercing, perfect knowledge that searches hearts and reins, and the brass feet signify providence steady, awful, pure, and holy in judgment and action. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Christ knows Thyatira's works, charity, service, faith, patience, and works, the last more than the first. Henry praises their growing fruitfulness: while others left their first love, these grew wiser and better, and Christians should ambition that their last works be their best. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Yet Christ has a few things against them: they suffer the woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess, to teach and seduce his servants to commit fornication and eat things sacrificed to idols. Henry compares her to Jezebel, persecutor of prophets and patroness of idolatry, and charges the church with connivance, since it neglected its ministerial power to censure and excommunicate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Christ gave her space to repent of her fornication, and she repented not. Henry notes repentance requires time and is a great work, and that where God gives space he expects fruits meet for repentance, while lost space brings double destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Behold, Christ will cast her into a bed, and those who commit adultery with her into great tribulation, unless they repent of their deeds. Henry reads the bed as a bed of pain and flames, not pleasure, yet still escapable by repentance for her partners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. He will kill her children with death, and all the churches shall know that he searches the reins and hearts, giving to each according to his works. Henry notes this judgment teaches Christ's infallible knowledge of secret inclinations and his impartial justice, since neither Christian name nor church membership shelters sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. To the rest in Thyatira who have not this doctrine nor known the depths of Satan, Christ will put no other burden. Henry contrasts the seducers' boasted "depths," profound mysteries, with Christ's name for them, depths of Satan, and stresses Christ's tenderness: he will not overburden faith with new mysteries or consciences with new laws. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. But what they already have, they are to hold fast till Christ comes. Henry notes Christ is coming to end all his people's temptations, and holding fast faith and a good conscience until then leaves all difficulty and danger over. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. To the overcomer who keeps Christ's works to the end, he will give power over the nations. Henry reads this as dominion either when the empire turned Christian or in the world to come, when believers sit with Christ on his throne of judgment over his enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. He shall rule them with a rod of iron, breaking them to shivers as a potter's vessels, even as Christ received of his Father. Henry pairs this dominion with the next verse's gift, as power requiring suitable wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Christ will give the overcomer the morning star. Henry identifies the star as Christ himself, bringing day into the soul, with the perfection of light and wisdom requisite for the dignity believers will hold in the morning of the resurrection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. Henry notes that in this and the following epistles the call to attention comes after the promise, teaching us to attend to promises as well as precepts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Nicolaitans. A loose sect sheltering under Christianity with hateful doctrines and deeds, whose deeds Ephesus hates and Christ hates, in Henry's note. Interpreted Study note, verse 6, Matthew Henry
  • Second death. Eternal death, the dying of the soul without support, from which the overcomer shall not be hurt, in Henry's note. Interpreted Study note, verse 11, Matthew Henry
  • Hidden manna, white stone, new name. The overcomer's gifts at Pergamos: Henry reads the manna as the Spirit's hidden comforts in communion with Christ, and the white stone as absolution, after the ancient custom of a white stone for acquittal. Interpreted Study note, verse 17, Matthew Henry
