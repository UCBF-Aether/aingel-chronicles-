The Text — Jubilees 14 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 After these things in the fourth year of this week, on the new moon of the third month the word of the Lord came to Abram in a dream, saying : " Fear not, Abram ; I am thy defender, and thy reward will be exceeding great." 2.

2 And he said: "Lord, Lord, what wilt thou give me, seeing I go

3 hence childless, and the son of Maseq, the son of

4 my handmaid, is the Dammasek Eliezer : he will be

5 my heir, and to me thou hast given no seed." 3.

6 And

7 He said unto him : " This (man) will not be thy heir,

8 but one that will come out of thine own bowels ;

9 he will be thine heir." 4.

10 And He brought him

11 forth abroad, and said unto him : " Look toward

12 heaven and number the stars, if thou art able to

13 number them." 5.

14 And he looked toward heaven,

15 and beheld the stars.

16 And He said unto him :

17 " So shall thy seed be." 6.

18 And he believed in the

19 Lord, and it was counted to him for righteousness.

20 And He said unto him : "I am the Lord that

21 brought thee out of Ur of the Chaldees, to give thee

22 the land of the Canaanites to possess it for ever;

23 and I shall be God unto thee and to thy seed after

24 thee." 8.

25 And he said: "Lord, Lord, whereby

26 shall I know that I shall inherit (it) ?

27 And he

28 said unto him : "

29 Take Me an heifer of three years,

30 and a goat of three years, and a sheep of three years,

31 and a turtle-dove, and a pigeon." 10.

32 And he took

33 all these in the middle of the month ; and he dwelt

34 at the oak of Mamre, which is near Hebron.

35 And

36 he built there an altar, and sacrificed all these ; and

37 he poured their blood upon the altar, and divided

38 them in the midst, and laid them over against each

39 other; but the birds divided he not.

40 And birds

41 came down upon the pieces, and Abram drove them

42 away, and did not suffer the birds to touch them.

43 And it came to pass, when the sun had set, that

44 an ecstasy fell upon Abram, and lo ! an horror of

45 great darkness fell upon him, and it was said unto

46 Abram : " Know of a surety that thy seed shall be a

47 stranger in a land (that is) not theirs, and they will

48 bring them into bondage, and afflict them four

49 hundred years.

50 And the nation also to whom they will be in bondage shall I judge, and after that they will come forth thence with much substance.

51 And thou wilt go to thy fathers in peace, and be buried in a good old age.

52 But in the fourth generation they will return hither ; for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full." 17.

53 And he awoke from his sleep, and he arose, and the sun had set ; and there was a flame, and behold ! a furnace was smoking, and a flame of fire passed between the pieces.

54 And on that day the Lord made a covenant with Abram, saying : " To thy seed will I give this land, from the river of Egypt unto the great river, the river Euphrates, the Kenites, the Kenizzites, the Kadmonites, the Perizzites, and the Rephaim, the Phakorites and the Hivites and the Amorites, and the Canaanites, and the Girgashites, and the Jebusites."

55 And the day passed, and Abram offered the pieces, and the birds, and their fruit offerings, and their drink offerings, and the fire devoured them.

56 And on that day we made a covenant with Abram, according as we had covenanted with Noah in this month ; and Abram renewed the festival and ordinance for himself for ever.

57 The Birth of Ishmael.

58 And Abram rejoiced, and made all these things known to Sarai his wife ; and he believed that he would have seed, but she did not bear.

59 And Sarai advised her husband Abram, and said unto him : " Go in unto Hagar, my Egyptian maid : it may be that I shall build up seed unto thee by her.'

60 And Abram hearkened unto the voice of Sarai his wife, and said unto her, "Do (so)."

61 And Sarai took Hagar, her maid, the Egyptian, and gave her to Abram, her husband, to be his wife.

62 And he went in unto her, and she conceived and bare him a son, and he called his name Ishmael, in the fifth year of this week ; and this was the eighty-sixth year in the life of Abram.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: After these things, in the fourth year of this week, on the new moon of the third month, the word of the Lord came to Abram in a dream, saying: 'Fear not, Abram; I am thy defender, and thy reward will be exceeding great.' Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. After these things, in the fourth year of this week, on the new moon of the third month, the word of the Lord came to Abram in a dream, saying: 'Fear not, Abram; I am thy defender, and thy reward will be exceeding great.' JFB on Genesis 15:1 notes that when the excitement of the conquest was over, Abram had become prey to despondency and terror at probable revenge, and the word, connected with a vision, denotes a prophetic message given to dispel his fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. This verse opens Abram's reply: 'Lord, Lord, what wilt thou give me, seeing I go hence childless.' The sentence is completed by the Eliezer complaint of the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. This verse continues the complaint: the son of Maseq, the son of his handmaid, is the Damascene Eliezer. The heir in view is a household figure, not a son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. This verse continues the complaint: Eliezer will be his heir. JFB on Genesis 15:3 notes that by nomadic usage the chief confidential servant would be heir to his possessions and honours, but only by adoption, falling sadly short of the parental hopes Abram had been encouraged to entertain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. 'And to me thou hast given no seed.' The complaint closes: no seed has been given, so the household servant stands as heir by default. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. This verse is the hinge to God's answer: and he said unto him. The reply reverses the complaint in the next verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. 'This man will not be thy heir.' The servant-heir is refused outright. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. 'But one that will come out of thine own bowels.' The heir will be of Abram's own body, not of his household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. 'He will be thine heir.' The promise closes: the own-bowels son will be the heir. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. This verse opens the star sign: God brought him forth abroad and said unto him. The sentence is completed by the numbering command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. 'Look toward heaven and number the stars, if thou art able to number them.' The stars are set as the measure of the promised seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. This verse completes the command: number the stars, if thou art able to number them. The condition already implies innumerability. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. This verse completes the star command: if thou art able to number them. Human numbering fails before the stars, as it will before the seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. He looked toward heaven and beheld the stars. Abram obeys the sign, looking up to behold what he cannot number. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. This verse completes the looking: he beheld the stars. The beholding prepares the promise of the next verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. God said unto him. The promise follows the beholding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. 'So shall thy seed be.' Seed like stars: innumerable as the host Abram just beheld. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. He believed in the Lord. Abram's belief answers the star promise with trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. It was counted to him for righteousness. Belief itself is reckoned as righteousness, the chapter's centre: Abram is righteous by trusting the promise before any son is born. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. God said unto him: 'I am the Lord that brought thee out of Ur of the Chaldees, to give thee the land of the Canaanites to possess it forever.' The self-identification recalls the exodus from Ur and joins it to the perpetual land gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. This verse continues the identification: the Lord brought Abram out of Ur to give him the Canaanites' land forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. This verse continues the land promise: the land is to be possessed forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. 'And I shall be God unto thee and to thy seed after thee.' The land promise is sealed by a God-promise extending to Abram and his seed after him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. This verse completes the God-promise: God unto Abram and his seed after him. The covenant's personal bond frames the land gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. He said: 'Lord, Lord, whereby shall I know that I shall inherit it?' JFB on Genesis 15:8 notes Abram, having renewed his address in a spirit of more becoming submission, asked whereby he would know he would inherit it, and was delighted by a most explicit promise of Canaan, immediately confirmed by a remarkable ceremony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. This verse completes the question: whereby shall I know that I shall inherit it? The sign Abram asks for is answered by the sacrifice of the next verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. This verse opens the answer: and he said unto him. The sacrifice command follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. This verse opens the command: take me an heifer. The list is completed by the goat, sheep and birds of the next verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. 'Take me an heifer of three years, and a goat of three years, and a sheep of three years, and a turtle-dove, and a pigeon.' The fivefold offering is specified: three three-year-old animals and two birds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. This verse completes the list: a turtle-dove and a pigeon. Birds close the offering, and they alone will remain undivided. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. This verse completes the offering list: a turtle-dove and a pigeon. The five kinds are now all commanded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. He took all these in the middle of the month. The taking is dated to mid-month, the book's precise reckoning for covenant acts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. This verse continues the preparation: he dwelt at the oak of Mamre, which is near Hebron. The covenant place is Mamre's oak near Hebron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. This verse completes the place notice: the oak of Mamre near Hebron. Hebron frames the covenant as Shechem framed the first altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. This verse opens the sacrifice: he built there an altar and sacrificed all these. The sentence is completed by the blood and division of the next verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. This verse continues the sacrifice: he poured their blood upon the altar. Blood on the altar precedes the division of the pieces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. He divided them in the midst and laid them over against each other, but the birds divided he not. JFB on Genesis 15:9-21 notes that on great occasions when parties joined a compact they observed precisely these rites, and the Lord condescended to enter into covenant with Abram, while the patriarch did not pass between the sacrifice, because in this transaction he was bound to nothing, whereas God gave a sign binding himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. This verse continues the division: the pieces were laid over against each other. The divided halves face each other, awaiting the passing sign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. This verse completes the division notice: the birds he divided not. Birds alone stay whole, as in the offering list's distinction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. Birds came down upon the pieces. The covenant pieces are threatened from above, as Abram's seed was threatened by ravens in chapter 11. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. Abram drove them away and did not suffer the birds to touch them. Abram guards the pieces as he once guarded the seed, driving the birds away untouched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. This verse completes the guarding: Abram did not suffer the birds to touch the pieces. The covenant sacrifice is kept intact until sunset. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. When the sun had set, an ecstasy fell upon Abram, and lo, an horror of great darkness fell upon him. Sunset brings ecstasy and horror of great darkness, the dread that precedes the prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. This verse continues the sunset dread: an horror of great darkness fell upon him. The darkness is the veil for the word that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. This verse opens the prophecy: it was said unto Abram. The sentence is completed by the surety of strangerhood and bondage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. 'Know of a surety that thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs.' The prophecy begins with sure knowledge: strangerhood in a foreign land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. This verse continues the prophecy: they will bring them into bondage and afflict them. Strangerhood becomes bondage and affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 48. This verse gives the bondage its length: four hundred years. The affliction is measured, not endless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 49. This verse completes the bondage sentence: affliction for four hundred years. The measured years bound the darkness of the prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 50. 'And the nation also to whom they will be in bondage shall I judge, and after that they will come forth thence with much substance.' The oppressor nation will be judged, and the seed will come out with much substance, as Abram came out of Egypt enriched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 51. 'And thou wilt go to thy fathers in peace, and be buried in a good old age.' Abram's own end is peaceful: to his fathers in peace, buried in a good old age, while his seed's bondage still lies ahead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 52. 'But in the fourth generation they will return hither; for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.' Return waits on the fourth generation, when Amorite iniquity reaches its fullness: judgment on Canaan is timed by iniquity, not by haste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 53. He awoke from his sleep, and he arose, and the sun had set, and there was a flame, and behold, a furnace was smoking, and a flame of fire passed between the pieces. The smoking furnace and flame of fire pass between the pieces, the divine sign passing where Abram did not, binding God himself to the covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. On that day the Lord made a covenant with Abram, saying: 'To thy seed will I give this land, from the river of Egypt unto the great river, the river Euphrates, the Kenites, the Kenizzites, the Kadmonites, the Perizzites, and the Rephaim, the Phakorites and the Hivites and the Amorites, and the Canaanites, and the Girgashites, and the Jebusites.' The covenant's land runs from Egypt's river to the Euphrates, over ten peoples named in full. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 55. The day passed, and Abram offered the pieces, and the birds, and their fruit offerings, and their drink offerings, and the fire devoured them. After the covenant, the pieces, birds, fruit offerings and drink offerings are offered and devoured by fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 56. On that day the angels made a covenant with Abram, according as they had covenanted with Noah in this month, and Abram renewed the festival and ordinance for himself forever. The covenant falls in the third month, as Noah's did, and Abram renews the festival and ordinance forever: the feast of weeks renewed in Abram's covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 57. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the birth of Ishmael. It divides the covenant dream from the Hagar story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 58. Abram rejoiced and made all these things known to Sarai his wife, and he believed that he would have seed, but she did not bear. Joy and belief meet barrenness: Abram believes he will have seed, yet Sarai bears none. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 59. Sarai advised her husband Abram, saying: 'Go in unto Hagar, my Egyptian maid: it may be that I shall build up seed unto thee by her.' Sarai's plan builds seed through her Egyptian maid, the human device beside the star promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 60. Abram hearkened unto the voice of Sarai his wife, and said unto her, 'Do so.' Abram agrees to Sarai's voice, as Adam once hearkened to his wife's voice in chapter 3. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 61. Sarai took Hagar, her maid, the Egyptian, and gave her to Abram her husband to be his wife. JFB on Genesis 16:1 notes the handmaid was a female slave, one of those obtained in Egypt; Sarai gives her to Abram as wife. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 62. He went in unto her, and she conceived and bare him a son, and he called his name Ishmael, in the fifth year of this week, and this was the eighty-sixth year in the life of Abram. Ishmael is born when Abram is eighty-six, dated to the fifth year of the week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
