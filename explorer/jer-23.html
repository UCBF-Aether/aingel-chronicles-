The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Woe be unto the pastors that destroy and scatter the sheep of my pasture! saith the LORD.

2 Therefore thus saith the LORD God of Israel against the pastors that feed my people; Ye have scattered my flock, and driven them away, and have not visited them: behold, I will visit upon you the evil of your doings, saith the LORD.

3 And I will gather the remnant of my flock out of all countries whither I have driven them, and will bring them again to their folds; and they shall be fruitful and increase.

4 And I will set up shepherds over them which shall feed them: and they shall fear no more, nor be dismayed, neither shall they be lacking, saith the LORD.

5 Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper, and shall execute judgment and justice in the earth.

6 In his days Judah shall be saved, and Israel shall dwell safely: and this is his name whereby he shall be called, THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS.

7 Therefore, behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that they shall no more say, The LORD liveth, which brought up the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt;

8 But, The LORD liveth, which brought up and which led the seed of the house of Israel out of the north country, and from all countries whither I had driven them; and they shall dwell in their own land.

9 Mine heart within me is broken because of the prophets; all my bones shake; I am like a drunken man, and like a man whom wine hath overcome, because of the LORD, and because of the words of his holiness.

10 For the land is full of adulterers; for because of swearing the land mourneth; the pleasant places of the wilderness are dried up, and their course is evil, and their force is not right.

11 For both prophet and priest are profane; yea, in my house have I found their wickedness, saith the LORD.

12 Wherefore their way shall be unto them as slippery ways in the darkness: they shall be driven on, and fall therein: for I will bring evil upon them, even the year of their visitation, saith the LORD.

13 And I have seen folly in the prophets of Samaria; they prophesied in Baal, and caused my people Israel to err.

14 I have seen also in the prophets of Jerusalem an horrible thing: they commit adultery, and walk in lies: they strengthen also the hands of evildoers, that none doth return from his wickedness; they are all of them unto me as Sodom, and the inhabitants thereof as Gomorrah.

15 Therefore thus saith the LORD of hosts concerning the prophets; Behold, I will feed them with wormwood, and make them drink the water of gall: for from the prophets of Jerusalem is profaneness gone forth into all the land.

16 Thus saith the LORD of hosts, Hearken not unto the words of the prophets that prophesy unto you: they make you vain: they speak a vision of their own heart, and not out of the mouth of the LORD.

17 They say still unto them that despise me, The LORD hath said, Ye shall have peace; and they say unto every one that walketh after the imagination of his own heart, No evil shall come upon you.

18 For who hath stood in the counsel of the LORD, and hath perceived and heard his word? who hath marked his word, and heard it?

19 Behold, a whirlwind of the LORD is gone forth in fury, even a grievous whirlwind: it shall fall grievously upon the head of the wicked.

20 The anger of the LORD shall not return, until he have executed, and till he have performed the thoughts of his heart: in the latter days ye shall consider it perfectly.

21 I have not sent these prophets, yet they ran: I have not spoken to them, yet they prophesied.

22 But if they had stood in my counsel, and had caused my people to hear my words, then they should have turned them from their evil way, and from the evil of their doings.

23 Am I a God at hand, saith the LORD, and not a God afar off?

24 Can any hide himself in secret places that I shall not see him? saith the LORD. Do not I fill heaven and earth? saith the LORD.

25 I have heard what the prophets said, that prophesy lies in my name, saying, I have dreamed, I have dreamed.

26 How long shall this be in the heart of the prophets that prophesy lies? yea, they are prophets of the deceit of their own heart;

27 Which think to cause my people to forget my name by their dreams which they tell every man to his neighbour, as their fathers have forgotten my name for Baal.

28 The prophet that hath a dream, let him tell a dream; and he that hath my word, let him speak my word faithfully. What is the chaff to the wheat? saith the LORD.

29 Is not my word like as a fire? saith the LORD; and like a hammer that breaketh the rock in pieces?

30 Therefore, behold, I am against the prophets, saith the LORD, that steal my words every one from his neighbour.

31 Behold, I am against the prophets, saith the LORD, that use their tongues, and say, He saith.

32 Behold, I am against them that prophesy false dreams, saith the LORD, and do tell them, and cause my people to err by their lies, and by their lightness; yet I sent them not, nor commanded them: therefore they shall not profit this people at all, saith the LORD.

33 And when this people, or the prophet, or a priest, shall ask thee, saying, What is the burden of the LORD? thou shalt then say unto them, What burden? I will even forsake you, saith the LORD.

34 And as for the prophet, and the priest, and the people, that shall say, The burden of the LORD, I will even punish that man and his house.

35 Thus shall ye say every one to his neighbour, and every one to his brother, What hath the LORD answered? and, What hath the LORD spoken?

36 And the burden of the LORD shall ye mention no more: for every man’s word shall be his burden; for ye have perverted the words of the living God, of the LORD of hosts our God.

37 Thus shalt thou say to the prophet, What hath the LORD answered thee? and, What hath the LORD spoken?

38 But since ye say, The burden of the LORD; therefore thus saith the LORD; Because ye say this word, The burden of the LORD, and I have sent unto you, saying, Ye shall not say, The burden of the LORD;

39 Therefore, behold, I, even I, will utterly forget you, and I will forsake you, and the city that I gave you and your fathers, and cast you out of my presence:

40 And I will bring an everlasting reproach upon you, and a perpetual shame, which shall not be forgotten.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Woe is pronounced on the pastors who destroy and scatter the sheep, and a promise follows: the Lord will gather the remnant, raise up shepherds, and raise up a righteous Branch from David who will reign wisely. The second half turns on false prophets — their dreams, their stolen words, and their use of the burden of the Lord — ending in everlasting reproach and perpetual shame. Henry warns the people are exhorted not to listen to false promises: hearken not to prophets who speak visions of their own heart, not out of the Lord's mouth, for they make you vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. They say still unto them that despise me: The Lord has said, Ye shall have peace; and they say unto every one that walks after the imagination of his own heart, No evil shall come upon you. Henry notes those who are resolved to go on in evil ways will justly be given up to believe strong delusions: peace promised to despisers, and no evil to heart-walkers, is the strong delusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. For who has stood in the counsel of the Lord, and has perceived and heard his word? Who has marked his word, and heard it? Henry asks: but which of them had received any revelation of God, or understood anything of his word? None stood in counsel, none perceived or heard his word, yet all prophesy peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Behold, a whirlwind of the Lord is gone forth in fury, even a grievous whirlwind: it shall fall grievously upon the head of the wicked. Henry's whirlwind gone forth in fury, falling grievously on the wicked's head, is the indignation the false prophets' peace-promises cannot turn aside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. The anger of the Lord shall not return, until he has executed, and till he has performed the thoughts of his heart: in the latter days ye shall consider it perfectly. Henry notes there was a time coming when they would reflect on their folly and unbelief with remorse: anger unreturned until thoughts are performed, considered perfectly in latter days, is that remorse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. I have not sent these prophets, yet they ran: I have not spoken to them, yet they prophesied. Henry's unsent runners, unspoken prophets, are the false teachers: they ran without sending, and prophesied without speaking from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. But if they had stood in my counsel, and had caused my people to hear my words, then they should have turned them from their evil way, and from the evil of their doings. Henry contrasts true and false: the teaching and example of the true prophets led men to repentance, faith, and righteousness, while the false prophets led men to rest in forms and notions, and to be quiet in their sins. Had they stood in counsel, the people would have turned from evil ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Am I a God at hand, says the Lord, and not a God afar off? Henry notes men cannot be hidden from God's all-seeing eye: a God at hand and afar off alike sees the pretenders to inspiration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Can any hide himself in secret places that I shall not see him? Says the Lord. Do not I fill heaven and earth? Says the Lord. Henry asks: will they never see what judgements they prepare for themselves? None can hide in secret places from him who fills heaven and earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. I have heard what the prophets said, that prophesy lies in my name, saying, I have dreamed, I have dreamed. Henry warns let them not call their foolish dreams divine oracles: I have dreamed, I have dreamed, is the lie prophesied in God's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. How long shall this be in the heart of the prophets that prophesy lies? Yea, they are prophets of the deceit of their own heart. Henry's prophets of deceit of their own heart prophesy lies from within: how long shall this be in their hearts is the question their judgement answers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Which think to cause my people to forget my name by their dreams which they tell every man to his neighbour, as their fathers have forgotten my name for Baal. Henry notes the promises of peace these prophets make are no more to be compared to God's promises than chaff to wheat: dreams told neighbour to neighbour, causing forgetfulness of God's name as fathers forgot it for Baal, are chaff, not wheat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. The prophet that has a dream, let him tell a dream; and he that has my word, let him speak my word faithfully. What is the chaff to the wheat? Says the Lord. Henry's chaff-to-wheat distinction is the test: dreams told as dreams, and God's word spoken faithfully, for what is chaff to wheat? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Is not my word like as a fire? Says the Lord; and like a hammer that breaks the rock in pieces? Henry notes the unhumbled heart of man is like a rock: if not melted by the word of God as a fire, it will be broken to pieces by it as a hammer, for the word is both fire and hammer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. Therefore, behold, I am against the prophets, says the Lord, that steal my words every one from his neighbour. Henry asks how can they be long safe, or at all easy, who have a God of almighty power against them? The word-stealers, every one from his neighbour, have God against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. Behold, I am against the prophets, says the Lord, that use their tongues, and say, He says. Henry's tongue-users, saying He says when he has not said, have God against them: the word of God is no smooth, lulling, deceitful message, and by its faithfulness it may certainly be distinguished from false doctrines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. Behold, I am against them that prophesy false dreams, says the Lord, and do tell them, and cause my people to err by their lies, and by their lightness; yet I sent them not, nor commanded them: therefore they shall not profit this people at all, says the Lord. Henry's false-dream prophets, causing error by lies and lightness, unsent and uncommanded, shall not profit this people at all, for God is against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. And when this people, or the prophet, or a priest, shall ask thee, saying, What is the burden of the Lord? Thou shalt then say unto them, What burden? I will even forsake you, says the Lord. Henry notes those are miserable indeed who are forsaken and forgotten of God: the scoffers' question, What is the burden of the Lord, is answered with forsaking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. And as for the prophet, and the priest, and the people, that shall say, The burden of the Lord, I will even punish that man and his house. Henry notes men's jesting at God's judgements will not baffle them: prophet, priest, and people who say The burden of the Lord shall be punished, man and house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. Thus shall ye say every one to his neighbour, and every one to his brother, What has the Lord answered? And, What has the Lord spoken? Henry's right speech replaces the jest: neighbour and brother ask, What has the Lord answered, and what has the Lord spoken, instead of mocking with burdens. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. And the burden of the Lord shall ye mention no more: for every man's word shall be his burden; for ye have perverted the words of the living God, of the Lord of hosts our God. Henry notes it is a mark of great and daring impiety for men to jest with the words of God: the burden mentioned no more, for every man's word shall be his burden, since they perverted the living God's words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. Thus shalt thou say to the prophet, What has the Lord answered thee? And, What has the Lord spoken? Henry's prophet is asked rightly: What has the Lord answered thee, and what has the Lord spoken, for the jesting burden is forbidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. But since ye say, The burden of the Lord; therefore thus says the Lord: Because ye say this word, The burden of the Lord, and I have sent unto you, saying, Ye shall not say, The burden of the Lord. Henry's since-ye-say is the indictment: because they say the forbidden word after being sent not to say it, the sentence follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. Therefore, behold, I, even I, will utterly forget you, and I will forsake you, and the city that I gave to you and to your fathers, and cast you out of my presence. Henry notes God had taken Israel to be a people near to him, but they shall now be cast out of his presence: utterly forgotten, forsaken, city and fathers' gift cast out of his presence, is the scoffers' portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. And I will bring an everlasting reproach upon you, and a perpetual shame, which shall not be forgotten. Henry closes: every idle and profane word will add to the sinner's burden in the day of judgement, when everlasting shame will be his portion, for the reproach is everlasting and the shame perpetual, never forgotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Thus says the Lord of hosts: Hearken not unto the words of the prophets that prophesy unto you: they make you vain: they speak a vision of their own heart, and not out of the mouth of the Lord. Henry warns the people are exhorted not to listen to false promises: hearken not to prophets who speak visions of their own heart, not out of the Lord's mouth, for they make you vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. They say still unto them that despise me: The Lord has said, Ye shall have peace; and they say unto every one that walks after the imagination of his own heart, No evil shall come upon you. Henry notes those who are resolved to go on in evil ways will justly be given up to believe strong delusions: peace promised to despisers, and no evil to heart-walkers, is the strong delusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. For who has stood in the counsel of the Lord, and has perceived and heard his word? Who has marked his word, and heard it? Henry asks: but which of them had received any revelation of God, or understood anything of his word? None stood in counsel, none perceived or heard his word, yet all prophesy peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Behold, a whirlwind of the Lord is gone forth in fury, even a grievous whirlwind: it shall fall grievously upon the head of the wicked. Henry's whirlwind gone forth in fury, falling grievously on the wicked's head, is the indignation the false prophets' peace-promises cannot turn aside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. The anger of the Lord shall not return, until he has executed, and till he has performed the thoughts of his heart: in the latter days ye shall consider it perfectly. Henry notes there was a time coming when they would reflect on their folly and unbelief with remorse: anger unreturned until thoughts are performed, considered perfectly in latter days, is that remorse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. I have not sent these prophets, yet they ran: I have not spoken to them, yet they prophesied. Henry's unsent runners, unspoken prophets, are the false teachers: they ran without sending, and prophesied without speaking from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. But if they had stood in my counsel, and had caused my people to hear my words, then they should have turned them from their evil way, and from the evil of their doings. Henry contrasts true and false: the teaching and example of the true prophets led men to repentance, faith, and righteousness, while the false prophets led men to rest in forms and notions, and to be quiet in their sins. Had they stood in counsel, the people would have turned from evil ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Am I a God at hand, says the Lord, and not a God afar off? Henry notes men cannot be hidden from God's all-seeing eye: a God at hand and afar off alike sees the pretenders to inspiration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Can any hide himself in secret places that I shall not see him? Says the Lord. Do not I fill heaven and earth? Says the Lord. Henry asks: will they never see what judgements they prepare for themselves? None can hide in secret places from him who fills heaven and earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. I have heard what the prophets said, that prophesy lies in my name, saying, I have dreamed, I have dreamed. Henry warns let them not call their foolish dreams divine oracles: I have dreamed, I have dreamed, is the lie prophesied in God's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. How long shall this be in the heart of the prophets that prophesy lies? Yea, they are prophets of the deceit of their own heart. Henry's prophets of deceit of their own heart prophesy lies from within: how long shall this be in their hearts is the question their judgement answers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Which think to cause my people to forget my name by their dreams which they tell every man to his neighbour, as their fathers have forgotten my name for Baal. Henry notes the promises of peace these prophets make are no more to be compared to God's promises than chaff to wheat: dreams told neighbour to neighbour, causing forgetfulness of God's name as fathers forgot it for Baal, are chaff, not wheat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. The prophet that has a dream, let him tell a dream; and he that has my word, let him speak my word faithfully. What is the chaff to the wheat? Says the Lord. Henry's chaff-to-wheat distinction is the test: dreams told as dreams, and God's word spoken faithfully, for what is chaff to wheat? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Is not my word like as a fire? Says the Lord; and like a hammer that breaks the rock in pieces? Henry notes the unhumbled heart of man is like a rock: if not melted by the word of God as a fire, it will be broken to pieces by it as a hammer, for the word is both fire and hammer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. Therefore, behold, I am against the prophets, says the Lord, that steal my words every one from his neighbour. Henry asks how can they be long safe, or at all easy, who have a God of almighty power against them? The word-stealers, every one from his neighbour, have God against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. Behold, I am against the prophets, says the Lord, that use their tongues, and say, He says. Henry's tongue-users, saying He says when he has not said, have God against them: the word of God is no smooth, lulling, deceitful message, and by its faithfulness it may certainly be distinguished from false doctrines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. Behold, I am against them that prophesy false dreams, says the Lord, and do tell them, and cause my people to err by their lies, and by their lightness; yet I sent them not, nor commanded them: therefore they shall not profit this people at all, says the Lord. Henry's false-dream prophets, causing error by lies and lightness, unsent and uncommanded, shall not profit this people at all, for God is against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. And when this people, or the prophet, or a priest, shall ask thee, saying, What is the burden of the Lord? Thou shalt then say unto them, What burden? I will even forsake you, says the Lord. Henry notes those are miserable indeed who are forsaken and forgotten of God: the scoffers' question, What is the burden of the Lord, is answered with forsaking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. And as for the prophet, and the priest, and the people, that shall say, The burden of the Lord, I will even punish that man and his house. Henry notes men's jesting at God's judgements will not baffle them: prophet, priest, and people who say The burden of the Lord shall be punished, man and house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. Thus shall ye say every one to his neighbour, and every one to his brother, What has the Lord answered? And, What has the Lord spoken? Henry's right speech replaces the jest: neighbour and brother ask, What has the Lord answered, and what has the Lord spoken, instead of mocking with burdens. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. And the burden of the Lord shall ye mention no more: for every man's word shall be his burden; for ye have perverted the words of the living God, of the Lord of hosts our God. Henry notes it is a mark of great and daring impiety for men to jest with the words of God: the burden mentioned no more, for every man's word shall be his burden, since they perverted the living God's words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. Thus shalt thou say to the prophet, What has the Lord answered thee? And, What has the Lord spoken? Henry's prophet is asked rightly: What has the Lord answered thee, and what has the Lord spoken, for the jesting burden is forbidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. But since ye say, The burden of the Lord; therefore thus says the Lord: Because ye say this word, The burden of the Lord, and I have sent unto you, saying, Ye shall not say, The burden of the Lord. Henry's since-ye-say is the indictment: because they say the forbidden word after being sent not to say it, the sentence follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. Therefore, behold, I, even I, will utterly forget you, and I will forsake you, and the city that I gave to you and to your fathers, and cast you out of my presence. Henry notes God had taken Israel to be a people near to him, but they shall now be cast out of his presence: utterly forgotten, forsaken, city and fathers' gift cast out of his presence, is the scoffers' portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. And I will bring an everlasting reproach upon you, and a perpetual shame, which shall not be forgotten. Henry closes: every idle and profane word will add to the sinner's burden in the day of judgement, when everlasting shame will be his portion, for the reproach is everlasting and the shame perpetual, never forgotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
