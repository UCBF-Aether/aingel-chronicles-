The Text — Joshua 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, when Jabin king of Hazor had heard those things, that he sent to Jobab king of Madon, and to the king of Shimron, and to the king of Achshaph,

2 And to the kings that were on the north of the mountains, and of the plains south of Chinneroth, and in the valley, and in the borders of Dor on the west,

3 And to the Canaanite on the east and on the west, and to the Amorite, and the Hittite, and the Perizzite, and the Jebusite in the mountains, and to the Hivite under Hermon in the land of Mizpeh.

4 And they went out, they and all their hosts with them, much people, even as the sand that is upon the sea shore in multitude, with horses and chariots very many.

5 And when all these kings were met together, they came and pitched together at the waters of Merom, to fight against Israel.

6 And the LORD said unto Joshua, Be not afraid because of them: for to morrow about this time will I deliver them up all slain before Israel: thou shalt hough their horses, and burn their chariots with fire.

7 So Joshua came, and all the people of war with him, against them by the waters of Merom suddenly; and they fell upon them.

8 And the LORD delivered them into the hand of Israel, who smote them, and chased them unto great Zidon, and unto Misrephothmaim, and unto the valley of Mizpeh eastward; and they smote them, until they left them none remaining.

9 And Joshua did unto them as the LORD bade him: he houghed their horses, and burnt their chariots with fire.

10 And Joshua at that time turned back, and took Hazor, and smote the king thereof with the sword: for Hazor beforetime was the head of all those kingdoms.

11 And they smote all the souls that were therein with the edge of the sword, utterly destroying them: there was not any left to breathe: and he burnt Hazor with fire.

12 And all the cities of those kings, and all the kings of them, did Joshua take, and smote them with the edge of the sword, and he utterly destroyed them, as Moses the servant of the LORD commanded.

13 But as for the cities that stood still in their strength, Israel burned none of them, save Hazor only; that did Joshua burn.

14 And all the spoil of these cities, and the cattle, the children of Israel took for a prey unto themselves; but every man they smote with the edge of the sword, until they had destroyed them, neither left they any to breathe.

15 As the LORD commanded Moses his servant, so did Moses command Joshua, and so did Joshua; he left nothing undone of all that the LORD commanded Moses.

16 So Joshua took all that land, the hills, and all the south country, and all the land of Goshen, and the valley, and the plain, and the mountain of Israel, and the valley of the same;

17 Even from the mount Halak, that goeth up to Seir, even unto Baalgad in the valley of Lebanon under mount Hermon: and all their kings he took, and smote them, and slew them.

18 Joshua made war a long time with all those kings.

19 There was not a city that made peace with the children of Israel, save the Hivites the inhabitants of Gibeon: all other they took in battle.

20 For it was of the LORD to harden their hearts, that they should come against Israel in battle, that he might destroy them utterly, and that they might have no favour, but that he might destroy them, as the LORD commanded Moses.

21 And at that time came Joshua, and cut off the Anakims from the mountains, from Hebron, from Debir, from Anab, and from all the mountains of Judah, and from all the mountains of Israel: Joshua destroyed them utterly with their cities.

22 There was none of the Anakims left in the land of the children of Israel: only in Gaza, in Gath, and in Ashdod, there remained.

23 So Joshua took the whole land, according to all that the LORD said unto Moses; and Joshua gave it for an inheritance unto Israel according to their divisions by their tribes. And the land rested from war.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The northern kings gather a host as numerous as sand on the seashore at the waters of Merom, and Joshua falls on them suddenly and routs them to Sidon and the valley of Mizpeh. Hazor, head of those kingdoms, is burned, its king put to the sword, and the campaign ends with the land resting from war. The chapter stresses obedience: Joshua left nothing undone of what Moses commanded, and the Anakim are cut off except in Gaza, Gath and Ashdod. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry: a fresh confederacy is a fresh danger, and fresh dangers drive God's people back to fresh supports from his word — the trial is proportioned to the strength given for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Henry: the wonders already wrought are meant to make Israel act vigorously themselves, not to sit still before a host counted like sand on the shore. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The Lord told Joshua not to be afraid because of them, for tomorrow about this time he would deliver them all slain before Israel, and Joshua was to hough their horses and burn their chariots with fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Henry: encouragement turns into action — Joshua falls on the confederacy at Merom, acting vigorously on the promise rather than waiting on the enemy's numbers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Joshua did to them as the Lord bade him, houghing their horses and burning their chariots with fire, showing self-denial in compliance with God's command, since things the carnal heart depends on are hurtful to the life of faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Henry: Hazor's fall is a judgment whose measure is full — a people left to the pride and obstinacy of their hearts meet the vengeance they merited, with Israel as executioner by command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Henry: Hazor's utter destruction is the judgment of a people whose iniquity is full — vengeance merited, not mere conquest, and Israel the appointed executioner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Henry: the taking of cities and kings is execution of a command, not spoil of ambition — Israel acts under the command given to Moses, and the judgment is the Lord's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Henry: duty kept and promise performed answer each other — Joshua leaves nothing undone, and God's word and his works are found to agree together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Joshua's long war with all those kings is Henry's picture of the believer's warfare: tedious, sharp and difficult, with no lasting peace to be expected until death, yet a rest from war remains for the people of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Henry: the cities would not seek peace, left to the pride and obstinacy of their hearts — Gibeon alone stands as the exception to a refusal shared by all the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Henry: the hardening is a judgment of abandonment — restraints withdrawn, hearts left to their own pride and enmity, driving them to battle rather than to peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. At that time Joshua came and cut off the Anakims from the mountains, from Hebron, Debir, Anab, all the mountains of Judah, and all the mountains of Israel, destroying them utterly with their cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. None of the Anakims were left in Israel's land, only in Gaza, Gath, and Ashdod there remained, and Henry notes the sons of Anak need never be a terror to God's Israel, for their day to fall will come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Henry: the land rests from war, not in peace with the Canaanites — a rest that foreshadows the rest remaining for God's people when their warfare is accomplished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
