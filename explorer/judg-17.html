The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And there was a man of mount Ephraim, whose name was Micah.

2 And he said unto his mother, The eleven hundred shekels of silver that were taken from thee, about which thou cursedst, and spakest of also in mine ears, behold, the silver is with me; I took it. And his mother said, Blessed be thou of the LORD, my son.

3 And when he had restored the eleven hundred shekels of silver to his mother, his mother said, I had wholly dedicated the silver unto the LORD from my hand for my son, to make a graven image and a molten image: now therefore I will restore it unto thee.

4 Yet he restored the money unto his mother; and his mother took two hundred shekels of silver, and gave them to the founder, who made thereof a graven image and a molten image: and they were in the house of Micah.

5 And the man Micah had an house of gods, and made an ephod, and teraphim, and consecrated one of his sons, who became his priest.

6 In those days there was no king in Israel, but every man did that which was right in his own eyes.

7 And there was a young man out of Bethlehemjudah of the family of Judah, who was a Levite, and he sojourned there.

8 And the man departed out of the city from Bethlehemjudah to sojourn where he could find a place: and he came to mount Ephraim to the house of Micah, as he journeyed.

9 And Micah said unto him, Whence comest thou? And he said unto him, I am a Levite of Bethlehemjudah, and I go to sojourn where I may find a place.

10 And Micah said unto him, Dwell with me, and be unto me a father and a priest, and I will give thee ten shekels of silver by the year, and a suit of apparel, and thy victuals. So the Levite went in.

11 And the Levite was content to dwell with the man; and the young man was unto him as one of his sons.

12 And Micah consecrated the Levite; and the young man became his priest, and was in the house of Micah.

13 Then said Micah, Now know I that the LORD will do me good, seeing I have a Levite to my priest.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This and the following chapters form an appendix to Judges, from a period of great disorder, and the refrain of the appendix is given at once: in those days there was no king in Israel, and every man did what was right in his own eyes. Micah of Mount Ephraim confesses stolen silver, his mother dedicates it to the LORD for a graven and a molten image, and Micah sets up a house of gods with an ephod and teraphim, consecrating first his own son and then a wandering Levite from Bethlehem-judah as his priest, on wages of ten shekels a year, apparel and food. Micah concludes that now he knows the LORD will do him good, seeing he has a Levite as priest. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. There was a man of Mount Ephraim named Micah, and JFB notes this and the following narratives form an appendix to Judges, from a period of great disorder and corruption, connected with chapter 1, in which Micah founded a miniature Shiloh of his own, sincere in intending to honour God yet blended with ignorance and delusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Micah told his mother the eleven hundred shekels taken from her, about which she had cursed, were with him, for he had taken them, and she blessed him, so that theft, curse, and blessing meet in a single household exchange. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. When he restored the silver, his mother said she had wholly dedicated it to the Lord for her son to make a graven image and a molten image, and JFB explains the graven image as carved wood or stone plated with silver, and the molten as solid metal cast in a mould, the two hundred shekels given to the founder sufficing for small figures with their pedestals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He restored the silver to his mother, who gave two hundred shekels to the founder, who made a graven and a molten image, which were placed in Micah's house, so that the restored theft became the material of idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Micah had a house of gods, made an ephod and teraphim, and consecrated one of his sons as priest, and JFB notes the house was a domestic chapel, the teraphim household tutelary gods, and the assumption of priesthood outside Aaron's family a direct violation of divine law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. In those days there was no king in Israel, and every man did that which was right in his own eyes, and JFB notes that for want of settled government no one called Micah to account, and no punishment followed any crime. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. There was a young man out of Bethlehem-judah, of the family of Judah, who was a Levite and sojourned there, and JFB notes Bethlehem-judah is distinguished from Bethlehem in Zebulun, and the Levite belonged to Judah by his mother's side, which accounts for his being there rather than in a Levitical city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The man departed from Bethlehem-judah to sojourn where he could find a place, and came to Micah's house in Mount Ephraim, and JFB notes his wandering, despite the provision secured for Levites, showed a roving disposition, and Micah, learning what he was, engaged his permanent services. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Micah asked the Levite whence he came, and he answered he was a Levite of Bethlehem-judah going to sojourn where he could find a place, the wandering priest meeting the private chapel-keeper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Micah said, Dwell with me, and be unto me a father and a priest, offering ten shekels of silver yearly, a suit of apparel, and victuals, and JFB notes father means spiritual father to conduct the establishment's services, and the apparel included vestments for priestly functions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The Levite was content to dwell with Micah, and the young man became to him as one of his sons, replacing the son Micah had first consecrated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Micah consecrated the Levite, filling his hand, and the young man became his priest and was in Micah's house, and JFB notes this consecration was no less unlawful for Micah to perform than for the Levite to receive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Then Micah said, Now I know the Lord will do me good, seeing I have a Levite as priest, and JFB notes the Levite's installation satisfied his conscience that orderly ministrations would bring prosperity, an expression evincing the united influence of ignorance and superstition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a question, not a conclusion)

This section investigates a parallel that a study note itself raises; it does not conclude anything about dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty. Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, on Judges 17:1, notes that this and the following narratives form an appendix to Judges, connected with chapter 1, in which Micah founded a miniature Shiloh of his own, sincere in intending to honour God yet blended with ignorance and delusion. Interpreted Study note on Judges 17:1, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Parallels. Micah's house mirrors the sanctuary's outward furniture: a house of gods, an ephod, teraphim, a consecrated priest, and language of dedication to the LORD, set against the house of God at Shiloh, where the Danites' stolen image remains set up all the time the house of God is in Shiloh (Judges 18:31). Interpreted Study notes on Judges 17:3-5, 17:12-13 and 18:31

Differences. The Shiloh sanctuary is the national house of God; Micah's is a private household shrine funded by restored stolen silver, furnished with a graven image and a molten image, and served first by Micah's own son and then by a hired wandering Levite whom Micah himself consecrates, which the notes call a direct violation of divine law. Sincerity of intention and legitimacy of worship are not the same thing in the note's account. Interpreted Study notes on Judges 17:3-5, 17:12-13
