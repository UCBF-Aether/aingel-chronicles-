The Text — 1 Book of the Covenant 19 (J. Cooper & A. J. Maclean, The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1902), public domain (archive.org cu31924029296170))

*OF THE CHURCH BUILDINGS*

1 Then within the fore-court let there be a place [to serve] for a baptistery, its length twenty-one cubits as a general type of the prophets, and its width twelve cubits as a type of those who have been determined to preach the Gospel,’ with one entrance and three exits.

2 Let the Church have a house of the catechumens, which shall be also the house of the exorcists. Let it not be detached from the Church, but so that those who enter and are in it may hear the lections and spiritual hymns of praise and psalms.

3 Let there be a throne by the altar ; on the right and on the left [let there be] the places of the presbyters, so that on the right may sit those who are more exalted and honoured, and those who labour in the word’; but those who are of middle age on the left hand.

4 But that place where the throne is, let it be raised three steps, for there the altar ought to be.

5 Let that house have two porches, on the right and on the left, for men and for women.

6 Let all the places be lighted, both for a type, and also for reading.

7 Let the altar have a veil of pure linen, for it is without spot.

8 Also the baptistery likewise, let it be under a veil.

9 Let a place be built as for commemoration, so that the priest and chief deacon sitting with the readers may write the names of those who offer the oblations, or of those for whom they have offered [them], so that when the holy things are offered by the bishop, the reader or chief deacon may name them by way of commemoration, which the priests’ and people offer for them with supplication.

10 For there is thistype also in heaven.?

11 Let the place of the. presbyters be within the veil, beside that place of commemoration.

12 Let the house of the offering and the treasury be quite beside the diaconicum.

13 But let the place of the lection be a little outside the altar.

14 Let the house of the bishop be beside that place which is called the fore-court.

15 Also that of those widows who are called “those that sit in front.”

16 Also let that of the presbyters and deacons be behind the baptistery.

17 Let the deaconesses abide beside the door of the Lord’s house.

18 Let the Church have a house for entertaining near by, where the chief deacon shall entertain strangers.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This is the church-buildings chapter: within the fore-court a baptistery, twenty-one cubits long as a type of the prophets and twelve cubits wide as a type of those determined to preach the Gospel, with one entrance and three exits; a house of the catechumens, which is also the house of the exorcists, not detached from the church, so that those in it may hear the lections, hymns and psalms. By the altar a throne, raised three steps, with places for the presbyters on right and left — the more exalted and those who labour in the word on the right, those of middle age on the left — and two porches, for men and for women. All places are to be lighted, both for a type and for reading; the altar is to have a veil of pure linen, and the baptistery likewise under a veil. A place of commemoration is to be built where the priest and chief deacon with the readers write the names of those who offer oblations and those for whom they are offered, to be named when the bishop offers; the presbyters' place is within the veil beside it, the house of offering and treasury beside the diaconicum, the place of lection a little outside the altar, the bishop's house beside the fore-court, the widows who sit in front nearby, the presbyters' and deacons' house behind the baptistery, the deaconesses beside the door of the Lord's house, and a guest house nearby where the chief deacon entertains strangers. Unfamiliar terms

  • catechumens. People under instruction before baptism; the chapter gives them a house of their own, joined to the church so that they can hear the readings, hymns and psalms.
  • presbyters. The elders seated by the throne beside the altar; the chapter seats the more honoured, and those who labour in the word, on the right.
