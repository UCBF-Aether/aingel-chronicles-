The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 28 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And he went on his journey, and came to the land of the east, to Laban, the brother of Rebecca, and he was with him, and served him for Rachel his daughter one week. And in the first year of the third week he said unto him : " Give me my wife, for whom I have served thee seven years; " and Laban said unto Jacob.

2 " I will give thee thy wife." 3.

3 And Laban made a feast, and took Leah his elder daughter, and gave (her) to Jacob as a wife, and gave her Zilpah his handmaid for an handmaid ; and Jacob did not know, for he thought that she was Rachel.

4 And he went in unto her, and behold, she was Leah; and Jacob was angry with Laban, and said unto him : " Why hast thou dealt thus with me ?

5 Did not I serve thee for Rachel and not for Leah?

6 Why hast thou wronged me?

7 Take thy daughter, and I will go ; for thou hast done evil to me." 5.

8 For Jacob loved Rachel more than Leah ; for Leah's eyes were weak, but her form was very handsome ; but Rachel had beautiful eyes and a beautiful and very handsome form.

9 And Laban said to Jacob : "It is not so done in our country, to give the younger before the elder."

10 And it is not right to do this ; for thus it is ordained and written in the heavenly tables, that no one should give his younger daughter before the elder — but the elder one giveth first and after her the younger —and the man who doeth so, they set down guilt against him in heaven, and none is righteous that doeth this thing, for this deed is evil before the Lord.

11 And command thou the children of Israel that they do not this thing ; let them neither take nor give the younger before they have given the elder, for it is very wicked. And Laban said to Jacob : " Let the seven days of the feast of this one pass by, and I shall give thee Rachel that thou mayest serve me another seven years, that thou mayest pasture my sheep as thou didst in the former week." 9.

12 And on the day when the seven days of the feast of Leah had passed, Laban gave Rachel to Jacob, that he might serve him another seven years, and he gave to Rachel Bilhah, the sister of Zilpah, as a handmaid.

13 And he served yet other seven years for Rachel, for Leah had been given to him for nothing.

14 And the Lord opened the womb of Leah, and she conceived and bare Jacob a son, and he called his name

15 Reuben on the fourteenth day of the ninth month, in the first year of the third week.

16 But the womb of Rachel was closed, for the Lord saw that Leah was hated and Rachel loved.

17 And again Jacob went in unto Leah, and she conceived, and bare Jacob a second son, and he called his name

18 Simeon, on the twenty-first of the tenth month, and in the third year of this week.

19 And again Jacob went in unto Leah, and she conceived, and bare him a third son, and he called his name Levi, in the new

20 moon of the first month in the sixth year of this week.

21 And again Jacob went in unto her, and she conceived, and bare him a fourth son, and he called his name Judah, on the fifteenth of the third month, in the first year of the fourth week.

22 And on account of all this Rachel envied Leah, for she did not bear, and she said to Jacob : " Give me children " ; and Jacob said : " Have I withheld from thee the fruits of thy womb?

23 Have I forsaken thee?

24 And when Rachel saw that Leah had borne four sons to Jacob, Reuben and Simeon and Levi and Judah, she said unto him : " Go in unto Bilhah my handmaid, and she will conceive, and bear a son unto me." 18.

25 (And she gave (him) Bilhah her handmaid to wife.) And he went in unto her, and she conceived, and bare him a son, and he called his name Dan, on the ninth of the sixth month, in the sixth year of the third week.

26 And Jacob went in again unto Bilhah a second time, and she conceived, and bare Jacob another son, and Rachel called his name Naphtali, on the fifth of the seventh month, in the second year of the fourth week.

27 And when Leah saw that she had become sterile and did not bear, she envied (Rachel) and she also gave her handmaid Zilpah to Jacob to wife, and she conceived, and bare a son, and Leah called his name Gad, on the twelfth of the eighth month, in the third year of the fourth week.

28 And he went in again unto her, and she conceived, and bare him a second son, and Leah called his name Asher, on the second of the eleventh month, in the fifth year of the fourth week.

29 And Jacob went in unto Leah, and she conceived, and bare a son, and she called his name Issachar, on the fourth of the fifth month, in the fourth year of the fourth week, and she gave him to a nurse.

30 And Jacob went in again unto her, and she conceived, and bare two (children), a son and a daughter, and she called the name of the son Zebulon, and the name of the daughter Dinah, in the seventh of the seventh month, in the sixth year of the fourth week.

31 And the Lord was gracious to Rachel, and opened her womb, and she

32 conceived, and bare a son, and she called his name Joseph, on the new moon of the fourth month, in the sixth year in this fourth week.

33 And in the days when Joseph was born, Jacob said to Laban : " Give me my wives and sons, and let me go to my father Isaac, and let me make me an house ; for I have completed the years in which I have served thee for thy two daughters, and I will go to the house of my father." 26.

34 And Laban said to Jacob : " Tarry with me for thy wages, and pasture my flock for me again, and take thy wages."

35 And they agreed with one another that he should give him as his wages those of the lambs and kids which were born black and spotted and white (these) were to be his wages.

36 And all the sheep brought forth spotted and speckled and black, variously marked and they brought forth again lambs like themselves, and all that were spotted were Jacob's and those which were not were Laban's.

37 And Jacob's possessions multiplied exceedingly, and he possessed oxen and sheep and asses and camels, and menservants and maidservants.

38 And Laban and his sons envied Jacob, and Laban took back his sheep from him, and he observed him with evil intent.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: Jacob came to Laban and served him one week of years for Rachel, then asked for his wife when the seven years were done. Laban and his sons envied Jacob, Laban took back his sheep, and observed him with evil intent. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. Jacob came to Laban and served him one week of years for Rachel, then asked for his wife when the seven years were done. Henry notes that love makes long and hard services short and easy, and that Jacob's labour of love is the pattern of service for heaven's happiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Laban answered that he would give Jacob his wife, agreeing to the bargain. The ready answer hid the deceit that the wedding feast would reveal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Laban made a feast but gave Jacob his elder daughter Leah, with Zilpah as her handmaid, and Jacob did not know, thinking she was Rachel. Henry observes that Jacob, who had imposed upon his father, was now imposed upon by Laban in like manner, and that the Lord was righteous in the recompense. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Jacob went in to Leah and discovered the deception, then angrily asked Laban why he had dealt so with him. The anger was just, though Henry notes that even the righteous, if they take a false step, are sometimes thus recompensed in the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Jacob reminded Laban that he had served for Rachel, not for Leah. The service had a named price, and Laban had taken the labour while withholding the promised wife. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Jacob asked why Laban had wronged him, pressing the plain injustice of the exchange. Henry adds that there is reason to believe Laban's excuse was not true, and that his way of settling the matter made bad worse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Jacob told Laban to take his daughter back and let him go, since Laban had done him evil. The offer to walk away showed how completely the bargain had been broken, though Jacob would in the end stay for Rachel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Jacob loved Rachel more than Leah, for Leah's eyes were weak though her form was very handsome, while Rachel was beautiful in eyes and form. Henry notes that the sisters' contest over children and favour grew from the desire for the promised Seed and the reproach of barrenness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Laban excused himself by the custom of his country, that the younger must not be given before the elder. Henry observes that Laban's excuse was probably untrue, a cover for a deceit already carried out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Laban proposed that the seven days of Leah's feast should pass, and then Rachel would be given if Jacob served another seven years pasturing his sheep. Henry notes that Jacob was thus drawn into the disquiet of multiplying wives, and that Laban's settlement made bad worse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. When Leah's feast days had passed, Laban gave Rachel to Jacob for another seven years' service, with Bilhah as her handmaid. Jacob could not refuse Rachel, for he had espoused her, nor Leah, who was now his wife. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Jacob served yet another seven years for Rachel, for Leah had been given to him for nothing. Henry's remark stands: love makes long service seem short, and an age of work is but as a few days to those who love God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The Lord opened Leah's womb, and she conceived and bore Jacob a son. Henry notes that the names Leah gave her children expressed her regard both to God and to her husband, and that God's providence in hearing her must be owned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Leah called her first son Reuben, born on the fourteenth day of the ninth month in the first year of the third week. Henry explains the name as See a son, with Leah's thought, now will my husband love me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Rachel's womb was closed, for the Lord saw that Leah was hated and Rachel loved. Henry stresses that God made the difference between the sisters, and that Rachel had the advantage in love while Leah had it in children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Jacob went in to Leah again, and she bore a second son. The births follow one another as the record of a household divided by favour, where each child was named for the mother's hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Leah called her second son Simeon, born on the twenty-first of the tenth month in the third year of the week. Henry gives the name as joined, with Leah's expectation, now will my husband be joined unto me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Leah bore a third son and called his name Levi, in the new moon of the first month in the sixth year of the week. Henry notes Levi as joined, the name that would later belong to the tribe set apart for God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The birth of Levi is dated precisely, marking the third son in the sequence of Leah's children. In the larger story Levi's line would become the priestly tribe, though here he is simply Leah's third-born. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Leah bore a fourth son and called him Judah, on the fifteenth of the third month in the first year of the fourth week. Henry notes that Judah means praise, from Leah's words, now will I praise the Lord, and that Christ descended after the flesh from him whose name was Praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Rachel envied Leah because she did not bear, and said to Jacob, give me children. Henry warns that envy is grieving at the good of another, a sin hateful to God and hurtful to neighbours and to ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Jacob answered Rachel sharply, asking whether he had withheld from her the fruits of the womb. Henry notes that Jacob loved Rachel and therefore reproved her for what she said amiss, since faithful reproofs show true affection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Rachel, seeing Leah's four sons, told Jacob to go in to her handmaid Bilhah, that she might bear a son for Rachel. Henry explains that by the usage of those times, the handmaid's children would be owned as her mistress's children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Bilhah was given to Jacob, conceived, and bore a son whom Rachel called Dan, on the ninth of the sixth month in the sixth year of the third week. Henry notes that Rachel took pleasure in giving names carrying marks of rivalry with her sister, and warns what roots of bitterness envy and strife are among relations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Jacob went in to Bilhah a second time, and she bore another son, whom Rachel called Naphtali, on the fifth of the seventh month in the second year of the fourth week. The second son completed Rachel's pair through her handmaid, matching Leah's growing household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Henry observes the power of jealousy and rivalship in the household, and admires the divine appointment joining one man and one woman only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Jacob went in to Zilpah again, and she bore a second son, whom Leah called Asher, on the second of the eleventh month in the fifth year of the fourth week. The contest of the sisters now ran through their handmaids, child against child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 33. When Joseph was born, Jacob asked Laban to give him his wives and sons and let him go to his father Isaac, to make a house of his own.
