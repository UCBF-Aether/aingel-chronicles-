The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Romans 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:

2 By whom also we have access by faith into this grace wherein we stand, and rejoice in hope of the glory of God.

3 And not only so, but we glory in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience;

4 And patience, experience; and experience, hope:

5 And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.

6 For when we were yet without strength, in due time Christ died for the ungodly.

7 For scarcely for a righteous man will one die: yet peradventure for a good man some would even dare to die.

8 But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.

9 Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him.

10 For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.

11 And not only so, but we also joy in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the atonement.

12 Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:

13 (For until the law sin was in the world: but sin is not imputed when there is no law.

14 Nevertheless death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over them that had not sinned after the similitude of Adam’s transgression, who is the figure of him that was to come.

15 But not as the offence, so also is the free gift. For if through the offence of one many be dead, much more the grace of God, and the gift by grace, which is by one man, Jesus Christ, hath abounded unto many.

16 And not as it was by one that sinned, so is the gift: for the judgment was by one to condemnation, but the free gift is of many offences unto justification.

17 For if by one man’s offence death reigned by one; much more they which receive abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness shall reign in life by one, Jesus Christ.)

18 Therefore as by the offence of one judgment came upon all men to condemnation; even so by the righteousness of one the free gift came upon all men unto justification of life.

19 For as by one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous.

20 Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound:

21 That as sin hath reigned unto death, even so might grace reign through righteousness unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Being justified by faith, believers have peace with God, stand in grace, and rejoice even in tribulation, which works patience, experience, and hope, because God's love is poured into their hearts. Paul then contrasts Adam, through whom sin and death entered, with Christ, through whom grace and the gift of righteousness abound all the more, reigning unto eternal life. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ. Henry notes that justification ends the war between God and the sinner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. By Christ we have access by faith into this grace in which we stand, and rejoice in hope of God's glory. JFB stresses that standing in grace is a present possession, not a future hope alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. We glory in tribulations also, knowing that tribulation works patience. Henry observes that affliction, in grace, becomes a workman rather than a destroyer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Patience works experience, and experience hope. JFB notes the chain: tried character is the evidence on which hope rests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Hope does not make ashamed, because God's love is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost given to us. Henry stresses that the Spirit makes God's love a felt reality, not a mere doctrine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. When we were yet without strength, in due time Christ died for the ungodly. JFB notes the timing and the objects: helpless sinners, not deserving saints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Scarcely for a righteous man will one die, yet perhaps for a good man some would dare to die. Henry observes that even the best human love stops short of Christ's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. God commends his love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us. JFB stresses that the proof of divine love is given at man's worst, not his best. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Much more, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him. Henry notes the argument from the greater to the less: if his death justifies, his life saves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. If when we were enemies we were reconciled to God by his Son's death, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life. JFB stresses that reconciliation is the ground of final salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. We also joy in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the atonement. Henry notes that joy in God himself, not only in his gifts, is the fruit of reconciliation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. As by one man sin entered the world, and death by sin, so death passed upon all men, for all have sinned. JFB stresses that Adam's fall is the fountain from which universal death flows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Until the law, sin was in the world, but sin is not imputed when there is no law. Henry observes that sin existed before Moses, even though it was not yet codified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those who had not sinned after Adam's transgression, Adam being the figure of him who was to come. JFB notes that universal death proves universal sin, inherited from Adam. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The free gift is not like the offence: if many died by one man's offence, much more God's grace and the gift by grace abounded to many by one man, Jesus Christ. Henry stresses that grace outmeasures the fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The gift is not as by one who sinned: judgment came by one offence to condemnation, but the free gift is of many offences to justification. JFB notes that grace covers a multitude of sins, not one alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. If by one man's offence death reigned, much more those who receive abundance of grace and the gift of righteousness shall reign in life by one, Jesus Christ. Henry observes that believers do not merely escape death; they reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. As by one offence judgment came on all men to condemnation, so by one righteousness the free gift came on all men to justification of life. JFB stresses the parallel of the two heads, Adam and Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. As by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by one man's obedience many shall be made righteous. Henry notes that representation, not imitation alone, is the ground in both cases. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The law entered that the offence might abound, but where sin abounded, grace did much more abound. JFB stresses that the law exposed sin's depth so that grace's height might be seen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. As sin reigned unto death, so grace reigns through righteousness unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord. Henry observes that grace now occupies sin's throne, ruling toward life instead of death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • justified. Declared or accounted righteous; Paul's word for the believer's standing before God through faith. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • atonement. Here, reconciliation received through Christ, by whom believers now joy in God. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • Adam and Christ. Paul's contrast: as sin and death reigned through the first man, grace and life reign through Jesus Christ. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
