The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Colossians 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, and Timotheus our brother,

2 To the saints and faithful brethren in Christ which are at Colosse: Grace be unto you, and peace, from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

3 We give thanks to God and the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, praying always for you,

4 Since we heard of your faith in Christ Jesus, and of the love which ye have to all the saints,

5 For the hope which is laid up for you in heaven, whereof ye heard before in the word of the truth of the gospel;

6 Which is come unto you, as it is in all the world; and bringeth forth fruit, as it doth also in you, since the day ye heard of it, and knew the grace of God in truth:

7 As ye also learned of Epaphras our dear fellowservant, who is for you a faithful minister of Christ;

8 Who also declared unto us your love in the Spirit.

9 For this cause we also, since the day we heard it, do not cease to pray for you, and to desire that ye might be filled with the knowledge of his will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding;

10 That ye might walk worthy of the Lord unto all pleasing, being fruitful in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God;

11 Strengthened with all might, according to his glorious power, unto all patience and longsuffering with joyfulness;

12 Giving thanks unto the Father, which hath made us meet to be partakers of the inheritance of the saints in light:

13 Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son:

14 In whom we have redemption through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins:

15 Who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature:

16 For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him:

17 And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.

18 And he is the head of the body, the church: who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead; that in all things he might have the preeminence.

19 For it pleased the Father that in him should all fulness dwell;

20 And, having made peace through the blood of his cross, by him to reconcile all things unto himself; by him, I say, whether they be things in earth, or things in heaven.

21 And you, that were sometime alienated and enemies in your mind by wicked works, yet now hath he reconciled

22 In the body of his flesh through death, to present you holy and unblameable and unreproveable in his sight:

23 If ye continue in the faith grounded and settled, and be not moved away from the hope of the gospel, which ye have heard, and which was preached to every creature which is under heaven; whereof I Paul am made a minister;

24 Who now rejoice in my sufferings for you, and fill up that which is behind of the afflictions of Christ in my flesh for his body’s sake, which is the church:

25 Whereof I am made a minister, according to the dispensation of God which is given to me for you, to fulfil the word of God;

26 Even the mystery which hath been hid from ages and from generations, but now is made manifest to his saints:

27 To whom God would make known what is the riches of the glory of this mystery among the Gentiles; which is Christ in you, the hope of glory:

28 Whom we preach, warning every man, and teaching every man in all wisdom; that we may present every man perfect in Christ Jesus:

29 Whereunto I also labour, striving according to his working, which worketh in me mightily.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul gives thanks for the Colossians' faith and love and prays they will walk worthily, strengthened and fruitful. The chapter's centre is Christ: image of the invisible God, firstborn over creation, by whom all things were made and hold together, head of the church, in whom all fullness dwells, reconciling all things through the blood of his cross, and Paul rejoices in suffering for that gospel. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul writes as an apostle by God's will alone, with Timothy as a brother in the work, and Henry notes that all true Christians are brethren to one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The Colossians are addressed as saints and faithful brethren in Christ, and grace and peace are wished to them from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Paul gives thanks to God and prays always for the Colossians, since faith, hope, and love are the principal graces of the Christian life and proper matter for thanksgiving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Paul had heard of their faith in Christ and of their love to all the saints, and Henry notes that Christian love arises from the Holy Spirit and is full of holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Their hope is laid up in heaven, treasured where no enemy can take it, and they had heard of it before in the word of the truth of the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The gospel had come to them as it had come into all the world, bringing forth fruit in them since the day they heard it and knew the grace of God in truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They had learned the gospel from Epaphras, a dear fellowservant and faithful minister of Christ, whose teaching formed their principles and lives according to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Epaphras had declared to Paul their love in the Spirit, the kind of love that Henry distinguishes from worldly or carnal love because it is holy and unselfish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Paul was constant in prayer that they might be filled with the knowledge of God's will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. That knowledge was meant to show itself in a worthy walk, fruitful in every good work and increasing in the knowledge of God, since good words will not do without good works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. They were to be strengthened with all might according to God's glorious power, and Henry notes the special use of this strength was for sufferings, borne with patience and joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Amidst their trials they gave thanks to the Father, whose special grace fitted them to partake of the inheritance provided for the saints in light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. God had delivered them from the power of darkness and translated them into the kingdom of his dear Son, turning willing subjects of Satan into subjects of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. In Christ they have redemption through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins, purchased by his atoning death, with all other spiritual blessings bestowed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Christ in his human nature is the visible discovery of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature, born or begotten before all creation as the Scripture way of representing eternity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. All things were created by him, visible and invisible, thrones and dominions and powers, made by his power, according to his pleasure, and for his praise and glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. He is before all things, and by him all things consist, since he not only created them at first but upholds them by the word of his power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. As Mediator he is the Head of the body, the church, the beginning and firstborn from the dead, so that in all things he might have the preeminence, with all grace and strength coming from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. It pleased the Father that in him should all fulness dwell, a fulness of merit and righteousness, of strength and grace for believers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Having made peace through the blood of his cross, God reconciled all things to himself by Christ, a mode of redemption Henry calls most suitable, since God showed his justice in requiring full satisfaction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The Colossians had once been alienated and enemies in their minds by wicked works, yet now they were reconciled, notwithstanding God's hatred of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. They were reconciled in the body of his flesh through death, to be presented holy and unblameable and unreproveable in God's sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. This standing depends on continuing in the faith, grounded and settled and not moved away from the hope of the gospel preached to every creature, of which Paul was made a minister. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Paul rejoiced in his sufferings for them, filling up what remains of the afflictions of Christ for the church's sake, since the sufferings of the Head and of the members make up, as it were, one body of sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. He was made a minister according to the dispensation of God given to him for them, to fulfil the word of God among them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The mystery hidden from ages and generations is now made manifest to God's saints, and believers are to be thankful that mysteries so long hidden have now been made known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. God would make known among the Gentiles the riches of the glory of this mystery, which is Christ in them, the hope of glory, and the test of that hope is whether Christ dwells and reigns in us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Paul preached Christ, warning and teaching every man in all wisdom, that he might present every man perfect in Christ Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For this he laboured, striving according to Christ's working in him mightily, and Henry urges believers likewise to be faithful to death through all trials. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
