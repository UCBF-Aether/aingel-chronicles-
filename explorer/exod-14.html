The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, that they turn and encamp before Pihahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, over against Baalzephon: before it shall ye encamp by the sea.

3 For Pharaoh will say of the children of Israel, They are entangled in the land, the wilderness hath shut them in.

4 And I will harden Pharaoh’s heart, that he shall follow after them; and I will be honoured upon Pharaoh, and upon all his host; that the Egyptians may know that I am the LORD. And they did so.

5 And it was told the king of Egypt that the people fled: and the heart of Pharaoh and of his servants was turned against the people, and they said, Why have we done this, that we have let Israel go from serving us?

6 And he made ready his chariot, and took his people with him:

7 And he took six hundred chosen chariots, and all the chariots of Egypt, and captains over every one of them.

8 And the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt, and he pursued after the children of Israel: and the children of Israel went out with an high hand.

9 But the Egyptians pursued after them, all the horses and chariots of Pharaoh, and his horsemen, and his army, and overtook them encamping by the sea, beside Pihahiroth, before Baalzephon.

10 And when Pharaoh drew nigh, the children of Israel lifted up their eyes, and, behold, the Egyptians marched after them; and they were sore afraid: and the children of Israel cried out unto the LORD.

11 And they said unto Moses, Because there were no graves in Egypt, hast thou taken us away to die in the wilderness? wherefore hast thou dealt thus with us, to carry us forth out of Egypt?

12 Is not this the word that we did tell thee in Egypt, saying, Let us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians? For it had been better for us to serve the Egyptians, than that we should die in the wilderness.

13 And Moses said unto the people, Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD, which he will shew to you to day: for the Egyptians whom ye have seen to day, ye shall see them again no more for ever.

14 The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace.

15 And the LORD said unto Moses, Wherefore criest thou unto me? speak unto the children of Israel, that they go forward:

16 But lift thou up thy rod, and stretch out thine hand over the sea, and divide it: and the children of Israel shall go on dry ground through the midst of the sea.

17 And I, behold, I will harden the hearts of the Egyptians, and they shall follow them: and I will get me honour upon Pharaoh, and upon all his host, upon his chariots, and upon his horsemen.

18 And the Egyptians shall know that I am the LORD, when I have gotten me honour upon Pharaoh, upon his chariots, and upon his horsemen.

19 And the angel of God, which went before the camp of Israel, removed and went behind them; and the pillar of the cloud went from before their face, and stood behind them:

20 And it came between the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel; and it was a cloud and darkness to them, but it gave light by night to these: so that the one came not near the other all the night.

21 And Moses stretched out his hand over the sea; and the LORD caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night, and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided.

22 And the children of Israel went into the midst of the sea upon the dry ground: and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand, and on their left.

23 And the Egyptians pursued, and went in after them to the midst of the sea, even all Pharaoh’s horses, his chariots, and his horsemen.

24 And it came to pass, that in the morning watch the LORD looked unto the host of the Egyptians through the pillar of fire and of the cloud, and troubled the host of the Egyptians,

25 And took off their chariot wheels, that they drave them heavily: so that the Egyptians said, Let us flee from the face of Israel; for the LORD fighteth for them against the Egyptians.

26 And the LORD said unto Moses, Stretch out thine hand over the sea, that the waters may come again upon the Egyptians, upon their chariots, and upon their horsemen.

27 And Moses stretched forth his hand over the sea, and the sea returned to his strength when the morning appeared; and the Egyptians fled against it; and the LORD overthrew the Egyptians in the midst of the sea.

28 And the waters returned, and covered the chariots, and the horsemen, and all the host of Pharaoh that came into the sea after them; there remained not so much as one of them.

29 But the children of Israel walked upon dry land in the midst of the sea; and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand, and on their left.

30 Thus the LORD saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians; and Israel saw the Egyptians dead upon the sea shore.

31 And Israel saw that great work which the LORD did upon the Egyptians: and the people feared the LORD, and believed the LORD, and his servant Moses.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Pursued to the sea by Pharaoh's chariots, Israel panics and Moses tells them to stand still and see the salvation of the LORD. A strong east wind divides the sea all night, Israel crosses on dry ground, and when the Egyptians follow, the waters return and drown Pharaoh's whole army. Israel sees the work, fears the LORD, and believes Moses. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD spake to Moses, directing the march after the departure. Henry notes the direction itself was part of the design: God ordered the route that would become the trap for Pharaoh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Israel was to turn and encamp before Pihahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, over against Baalzephon, encamping by the sea. Henry observes the place seemed a dead end, sea before them and wilderness behind, chosen precisely that Pharaoh might think them entangled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Pharaoh would say of Israel, They are entangled in the land, the wilderness hath shut them in. Henry notes what seems to tend to the church's ruin is often overruled to the ruin of the church's enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. God would harden Pharaoh's heart to follow after them, and be honoured upon Pharaoh, his chariots, and his horsemen, that the Egyptians might know he is the LORD, and Israel did so. Henry observes all men are made for their Maker's honour: those whom he is not honoured by, he will be honoured upon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. It was told the king of Egypt that the people fled, and the heart of Pharaoh and his servants was turned, asking why they had let Israel go from serving them. Henry notes that though with the greatest reason he had let them go, Pharaoh was now angry with himself for it, for envy and rage torment those who harbour them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Pharaoh made ready his chariot and took his people with him. Henry observes the king who had begged Israel to leave now armed himself to fetch them back: he would not tamely part with any out of his service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He took six hundred chosen chariots, and all the chariots of Egypt, and captains over every one of them. Henry notes the whole military strength of Egypt was mustered against an unarmed people on foot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he pursued after Israel, while Israel went out with a high hand. Henry observes the two motions together: Pharaoh driven by his own rage, Israel marching out boldly under God's hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The Egyptians pursued with all Pharaoh's horses, chariots, and horsemen, and his army, and overtook Israel encamping by the sea beside Pihahiroth before Baalzephon. Henry notes the trap was now sprung from Pharaoh's side, exactly as God had said. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. When Pharaoh drew nigh, Israel lifted up their eyes and saw the Egyptians marching after them, and they were sore afraid and cried unto the LORD. Henry observes some cried out to the Lord, and their fear led them to pray, which was well: God brings us into straits that he may bring us to our knees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. They said to Moses, Because there were no graves in Egypt, hast thou taken us away to die in the wilderness? Henry notes the bitter absurdity of unbelief: they quarrelled with Moses for bringing them out, and so were angry with God for the greatest kindness ever done them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Is not this the word we told thee in Egypt, saying, Let us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians? For it had been better to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness. Henry observes fear rewrote their history: they now claimed they had never wanted deliverance at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Moses said, Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD, which he will show you today, for the Egyptians ye have seen today ye shall see again no more forever. Henry notes the counsel: think not to save yourselves by fighting or flying; wait God's orders and observe them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace. Henry observes it is always our duty and interest, when we cannot get out of troubles, yet to get above our fears, and let them quicken prayer without silencing faith and hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The LORD said to Moses, Wherefore criest thou unto me? Speak unto Israel, that they go forward. Henry notes Moses's silent prayers of faith prevailed more with God than Israel's loud outcries of fear, and the command now was not to stand but to march. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Moses was to lift up his rod and stretch out his hand over the sea and divide it, and Israel would go on dry ground through the midst of the sea. Henry observes God can force a way where he does not find one, and open a passage through waters some miles over. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. God would harden the Egyptians' hearts, and they would follow Israel, and he would get honour upon Pharaoh, his chariots, and his horsemen. Henry notes the ruin of sinners is brought on by their own rage and presumption: they might have let Israel alone, and would not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The Egyptians would know that God is the LORD when he had gotten honour upon Pharaoh, his chariots, and his horsemen. Henry observes the sea would teach Egypt what the plagues had not: the name Pharaoh had scorned, written in the drowning of his army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The angel of God, which went before Israel's camp, removed and went behind them, and the pillar of cloud moved from before their face to behind them. Henry notes the guard came where it was needed: the guide became the rearguard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The pillar came between the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel, and was a cloud and darkness to the Egyptians, but gave light by night to Israel, so that the one came not near the other all night. Henry observes the word and providence of God have a dark side toward sinners and a bright side toward his people, as he who divided light from darkness allotted darkness to Egypt and light to Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the LORD caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night, and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided. Henry notes the means were natural, a wind all night, yet the result was wholly miraculous: a sea parted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Israel went into the midst of the sea upon dry ground, and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand and on their left. Henry observes they walked upon dry land in the midst of the sea, an encouragement to God's people in all ages to trust him in the greatest straits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The Egyptians pursued and went in after them to the midst of the sea, even all Pharaoh's horses, his chariots, and his horsemen. Henry notes they followed where they had no right to follow, presuming upon a path opened for others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. In the morning watch the LORD looked unto the host of the Egyptians through the pillar of fire and of the cloud, and troubled the host. Henry observes the look of God was itself a weapon: the same pillar that lit Israel now troubled Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. He took off their chariot wheels, so that they drave heavily, and the Egyptians said, Let us flee from the face of Israel, for the LORD fighteth for them against the Egyptians. Henry notes the confession came too late: they would flee from Israel's face, and could not, for the sea held them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The LORD told Moses to stretch out his hand over the sea, that the waters might come again upon the Egyptians, their chariots, and their horsemen. Henry observes the same hand that divided the sea was commanded to close it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Moses stretched forth his hand, and the sea returned to his strength when the morning appeared, and the Egyptians fled against it, and the LORD overthrew the Egyptians in the midst of the sea. Henry notes the timing: at dawn, when flight seemed possible, the waters returned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The waters returned and covered the chariots and the horsemen and all the host of Pharaoh that came into the sea after them, so that there remained not so much as one of them. Henry observes Pharaoh and his servants, who had hardened one another in sin, now fell together, not one escaping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. But Israel walked upon dry land in the midst of the sea, and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand and on their left. Henry notes the contrast is the point: the same waters were a wall of safety to one people and a grave to the other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Thus the LORD saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians, and Israel saw the Egyptians dead upon the sea shore. Henry observes the sight very much affected them: while men see God's works and feel the benefit, they fear him and trust in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Israel saw that great work which the LORD did upon the Egyptians, and the people feared the LORD and believed the LORD and his servant Moses. Henry closes on the frame Israel was in: how well were it for us if we were always in as good a frame as sometimes, holding fast by God while our enemies rage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
