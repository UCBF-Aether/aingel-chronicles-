The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Matthew 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now when Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judaea in the days of Herod the king, behold, there came wise men from the east to Jerusalem,

2 Saying, Where is he that is born King of the Jews? for we have seen his star in the east, and are come to worship him.

3 When Herod the king had heard these things, he was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him.

4 And when he had gathered all the chief priests and scribes of the people together, he demanded of them where Christ should be born.

5 And they said unto him, In Bethlehem of Judaea: for thus it is written by the prophet,

6 And thou Bethlehem, in the land of Juda, art not the least among the princes of Juda: for out of thee shall come a Governor, that shall rule my people Israel.

7 Then Herod, when he had privily called the wise men, enquired of them diligently what time the star appeared.

8 And he sent them to Bethlehem, and said, Go and search diligently for the young child; and when ye have found him, bring me word again, that I may come and worship him also.

9 When they had heard the king, they departed; and, lo, the star, which they saw in the east, went before them, till it came and stood over where the young child was.

10 When they saw the star, they rejoiced with exceeding great joy.

11 And when they were come into the house, they saw the young child with Mary his mother, and fell down, and worshipped him: and when they had opened their treasures, they presented unto him gifts; gold, and frankincense and myrrh.

12 And being warned of God in a dream that they should not return to Herod, they departed into their own country another way.

13 And when they were departed, behold, the angel of the Lord appeareth to Joseph in a dream, saying, Arise, and take the young child and his mother, and flee into Egypt, and be thou there until I bring thee word: for Herod will seek the young child to destroy him.

14 When he arose, he took the young child and his mother by night, and departed into Egypt:

15 And was there until the death of Herod: that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Out of Egypt have I called my son.

16 Then Herod, when he saw that he was mocked of the wise men, was exceeding wroth, and sent forth, and slew all the children that were in Bethlehem, and in all the coasts thereof, from two years old and under, according to the time which he had diligently enquired of the wise men.

17 Then was fulfilled that which was spoken by Jeremy the prophet, saying,

18 In Rama was there a voice heard, lamentation, and weeping, and great mourning, Rachel weeping for her children, and would not be comforted, because they are not.

19 But when Herod was dead, behold, an angel of the Lord appeareth in a dream to Joseph in Egypt,

20 Saying, Arise, and take the young child and his mother, and go into the land of Israel: for they are dead which sought the young child’s life.

21 And he arose, and took the young child and his mother, and came into the land of Israel.

22 But when he heard that Archelaus did reign in Judaea in the room of his father Herod, he was afraid to go thither: notwithstanding, being warned of God in a dream, he turned aside into the parts of Galilee:

23 And he came and dwelt in a city called Nazareth: that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophets, He shall be called a Nazarene.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Wise men from the east come to Jerusalem asking for the king of the Jews, Herod is troubled, the chief priests answer from Micah that the Christ is to be born in Bethlehem, and the star leads the wise men to the child, to whom they offer gold, frankincense and myrrh. Warned in dreams, they return another way, Joseph flees with Mary and the child into Egypt, Herod slays the children of Bethlehem, and after Herod's death the family returns and settles in Nazareth. Interpreted Reading of this chapter's text as printed above and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea, about six miles southwest of Jerusalem, in the days of Herod the Great, an Edomite made king by Rome — the sceptre departing from Judah as a sign the Messiah was at hand. The wise men were Magi, a learned class from the east, come to Jerusalem as the Jewish metropolis. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. “Where is he that is born King of the Jews?” is the language of strangers, not of Jews, and the star is safest regarded as a luminous meteor appearing under special laws for a special purpose. Their coming “to worship him” means religious homage, not mere civil respect, as the whole narrative shows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Herod was troubled as seeing a danger to his own throne, his guilty conscience perhaps adding other grounds of fear. All Jerusalem was troubled with him, dreading both revolutionary commotion and Herod's rage — a dead faith that hates truths which interfere with ambition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Herod gathered the chief priests — the current and former high priests with heads of the priestly courses — and the scribes, once mere transcribers of the law, now its interpreters, lawyers and divines. Together with the elders they formed the Sanhedrim, and Herod demanded of them, as authorized interpreters, where Christ should be born. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Their answer was prompt: in Bethlehem of Judea, for thus it is written by Micah. JFB calls it an involuntary testimony from the highest tribunal in the nation — the same tribunal that would at length condemn him to die. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. “Thou Bethlehem, art not the least among the princes of Judah” differs verbally from Micah yet agrees in substance: though little, Bethlehem is lifted from lowest to highest rank because out of it shall come a Governor. “Rule” is “feed,” kings pictured as shepherds of God's people Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Having the place from the Sanhedrim, Herod privately called the wise men to fix the other point, the date, inquiring precisely what time the star appeared. The unsuspecting strangers told him all, presuming the star's appearance would mark the child's age. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Herod sent them to Bethlehem to search diligently for the child and bring him word, “that I may come and worship him also” — the cunning hypocrite's cover. Yet the royal mandate meanwhile served the strangers as a safe conduct on their journey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The star they had seen in the east, apparently vanished in the interval, went before them again and stood over where the child was — hardly possible except by a luminous meteor, and not very high. JFB notes the Jewish ecclesiastics who could name the birthplace never trooped to Bethlehem, and God overruled even that cold indifference lest the news reach the tyrant too soon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. When they saw the star they rejoiced with exceeding great joy, language of exuberant transport. Henry compares it to the joy of those who, after a long night of temptation and desertion, receive the Spirit of adoption witnessing that they are children of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They came into a house, not the stable, and saw the child with Mary his mother — the Babe named first, then the mother, Joseph unnoticed as merely head of the house. Falling down to worship was lofty spiritual homage, and their gifts were religious offerings; the gold at least would prove serviceable for the unexpected journey to Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Warned of God in a dream not to return to Herod, they departed to their own country another way. It must have surprised them, just as they prepared to carry glad news to the “pious” king, but the Lord knew the bloody old tyrant better than to let them see his face again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The angel appeared to Joseph in a dream: arise, take the child and his mother — a form repeated, hinting Joseph was no more than the Child's guardian — and flee into Egypt. Egypt, near at hand, a Roman province independent of Herod and much inhabited by Jews, was an easy refuge; the house of bondage had returned to its first use as a house of refuge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Joseph arose the same night and departed into Egypt, without delay. Henry notes this was a trial of the faith of Joseph and Mary, and their faith, being tried, was found firm — a comfort to any in trouble with their infants, remembering Christ's own straits as an infant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He remained there until Herod's death, which followed not long after, of a horrible disease described by Josephus. Thus was fulfilled Hosea's word, “Out of Egypt have I called my son,” quoted from the Hebrew: Israel was God's son and first-born called out of Egypt, and now the Only-begotten Son is called out in the same pattern. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Seeing he was mocked by the wise men — truly mocked by a Higher than they, for he that sits in heaven laughs at the crafty — Herod was exceeding wroth and sent hired murderers to slay all male children in Bethlehem and its environs, two years old and under, by the time he had diligently inquired. He took a large sweep not to miss his mark, but the Babe was gone; Bethlehem was so small that Josephus's silence on the massacre is no argument against it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Then was fulfilled what was spoken by Jeremiah the prophet, the quotation differing only verbally from its source. Matthew's fulfilment citations anchor each turn of the story in prophecy his Jewish readers would recognize. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. In Ramah a voice was heard, Rachel weeping for her children and refusing comfort because they are not. In Jeremiah the words concern the Babylonian captivity; Rachel, buried near Bethlehem, is pictured rising to lament first a bitter captivity and now a bloody death, her babes the ram caught in the thicket while Isaac escapes — until the Babe of Bethlehem himself becomes the Ram caught in another thicket. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. When Herod was dead — he who thought himself safe from a dreaded Rival, when it was the Child who was safe from him — the angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph in Egypt. JFB notes the same angel seems employed on all these high occasions, most likely Gabriel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Arise and go into the land of Israel, in its most general sense, the particular province not yet indicated — so Joseph and Mary, like Abraham, went out not knowing whither they went until further direction came. “They are dead which sought the young child's life” echoes the Exodus case of Moses, though only Herod is meant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Joseph arose and came into the land of Israel, plainly intending to return to Bethlehem of Judea to rear the Infant King in his own royal city, until the time should come for him to occupy Jerusalem, the city of the Great King. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Hearing Archelaus reigned in Judea in place of his father Herod, Joseph was afraid to go there — Archelaus held Judea, Samaria and Idumea as ethnarch, and after ten years of tyranny was banished to Gaul. Warned in a dream, Joseph turned aside to Galilee under the less cruel Antipas, where secluded Nazareth lay far from the seat of government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. He came and dwelt in Nazareth, a small town in Lower Galilee in Zebulun's territory, held in bad esteem — so that Christ would bear the reproach of being called a Nazarene. Henry adds the comfort: wherever Providence allots our habitation, we may glory in being called by his name, sure that if we suffer with him we shall be glorified with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms
