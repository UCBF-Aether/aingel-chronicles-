Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter opens with the censers that were brought into the house of God — hallowed, it says, by the offering of the bodies of those who bore them — and a prayer for forgiveness, before turning to Phinehas, who was zealous and, the chapter says, loved exceedingly above the sons of the Levites — and the priesthood appointed to them [?]. Against him stand the sons of Korah: the earth swallowed them alive into Sheol, in soul and flesh, with their households and cattle, while those who transgress not God’s commandment are promised that God will do their desire and keep their kingdom. The sons of Korah and Asaph, the chapter says, murmured against Moses, claimed the priesthood as sons of Levi, and took censers to burn incense — but God accepted not their prayer, and they were burned by the fire of their own censers like wax in fire, none left. God then commands Moses and Aaron to gather the censers into the tabernacle, and the chapter closes with a list of tabernacle furnishings and sacrifices, the covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, the tabernacle of Moses and Aaron, of Samuel and Elias, and of Solomon’s house — and a final warning that those who are like them shall be as the sons of Korah, cast into the judgment of the burning fire, from which there is no escape. Much of the chapter is hard to follow: many verses are lists of untranslated Ge’ez words, several break off mid-thought, and nearly every line carries [?] marks, so the details should be read as the text gives them, not as settled.
