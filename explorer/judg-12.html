The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the men of Ephraim gathered themselves together, and went northward, and said unto Jephthah, Wherefore passedst thou over to fight against the children of Ammon, and didst not call us to go with thee? we will burn thine house upon thee with fire.

2 And Jephthah said unto them, I and my people were at great strife with the children of Ammon; and when I called you, ye delivered me not out of their hands.

3 And when I saw that ye delivered me not, I put my life in my hands, and passed over against the children of Ammon, and the LORD delivered them into my hand: wherefore then are ye come up unto me this day, to fight against me?

4 Then Jephthah gathered together all the men of Gilead, and fought with Ephraim: and the men of Gilead smote Ephraim, because they said, Ye Gileadites are fugitives of Ephraim among the Ephraimites, and among the Manassites.

5 And the Gileadites took the passages of Jordan before the Ephraimites: and it was so, that when those Ephraimites which were escaped said, Let me go over; that the men of Gilead said unto him, Art thou an Ephraimite? If he said, Nay;

6 Then said they unto him, Say now Shibboleth: and he said Sibboleth: for he could not frame to pronounce it right. Then they took him, and slew him at the passages of Jordan: and there fell at that time of the Ephraimites forty and two thousand.

7 And Jephthah judged Israel six years. Then died Jephthah the Gileadite, and was buried in one of the cities of Gilead.

8 And after him Ibzan of Bethlehem judged Israel.

9 And he had thirty sons, and thirty daughters, whom he sent abroad, and took in thirty daughters from abroad for his sons. And he judged Israel seven years.

10 Then died Ibzan, and was buried at Bethlehem.

11 And after him Elon, a Zebulonite, judged Israel; and he judged Israel ten years.

12 And Elon the Zebulonite died, and was buried in Aijalon in the country of Zebulun.

13 And after him Abdon the son of Hillel, a Pirathonite, judged Israel.

14 And he had forty sons and thirty nephews, that rode on threescore and ten ass colts: and he judged Israel eight years.

15 And Abdon the son of Hillel the Pirathonite died, and was buried in Pirathon in the land of Ephraim, in the mount of the Amalekites.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ephraim, jealous again, complains that Jephthah did not call them against Ammon and threatens to burn his house; Jephthah answers that he did call and they did not deliver him, and civil war follows. Gilead holds the fords of Jordan and tests fugitives with the word Shibboleth, which Ephraimites cannot pronounce, and forty-two thousand fall. Jephthah judges six years, and the chapter closes with the minor judges Ibzan of Bethlehem, Elon the Zebulonite and Abdon the Pirathonite, remembered chiefly by their families, tribes and terms. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The men of Ephraim gathered and went northward to Jephthah, complaining he had not called them to fight Ammon, and JFB calls this a fresh development of their jealous, rash, and irritable temper, desiring the credit of patriotism without sharing the victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Jephthah answered that he had called them and they had not delivered him, and JFB notes his straightforward answer shows the charge was false and their boast came with ill grace from people who had delayed appearing until the crisis was past. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Seeing they would not deliver him, Jephthah put his life in his hands and passed over against Ammon, and JFB explains the phrase as undertaking a duty of imminent peril, which he had done with Gileadite volunteers alone, so Ephraim ought rather to have been thankful the war ended without their labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Jephthah gathered Gilead and fought Ephraim, because Ephraim called Gileadites fugitives of Ephraim, and JFB notes the reasonable remonstrances were met with insulting sneers that Gileadites were outcasts, the scum of the common stock, and a feud immediately ensued. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Gilead took the passages of Jordan, and when an Ephraimite fugitive said he would pass over, they asked whether he was an Ephraimite, and JFB notes the Gileadites, having defeated Ephraim, rushed to the fords to intercept every fugitive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They told him to say Shibboleth, and he said Sibboleth, for he could not frame to pronounce it rightly, and they slew him at the fords, and JFB explains Shibboleth means a stream and Sibboleth a burden, and the Ephraimites could not bring their organs to pronounce the Eastern dialect's sound. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Jephthah judged Israel six years, died, and was buried in a city of Gilead, and JFB notes that however difficult some passages of his history are, apostolic authority ranks him among the worthies of the ancient church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. After him Ibzan of Bethlehem judged Israel, the first of the minor judges of whom JFB notes the only memorials preserved relate to the number of their families and their state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Ibzan had thirty sons and thirty daughters, whom he sent abroad and took in from abroad, and judged Israel seven years, a household spread by marriage alliances across the tribes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Ibzan died and was buried at Bethlehem, closing a judgeship remembered, as JFB notes, chiefly by family and place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. After him Elon a Zebulonite judged Israel ten years, another of the minor judges whose memorial is his tribe and term. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Elon died and was buried in Aijalon in Zebulon, his burial place preserving his tribal home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. After him Abdon son of Hillel, a Pirathonite, judged Israel, continuing the succession of minor judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Abdon had forty sons and thirty nephews who rode on seventy ass colts, and judged Israel eight years, a family train that displayed his state as JFB notes of these judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Abdon died and was buried in Pirathon in Ephraim, in the mount of the Amalekites, and the notice of place closes the roll of minor judges before Samson's story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
