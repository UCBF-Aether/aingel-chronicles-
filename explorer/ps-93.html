The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 93 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 The LORD reigneth, he is clothed with majesty; the LORD is clothed with strength, wherewith he hath girded himself: the world also is stablished, that it cannot be moved.

2 Thy throne is established of old: thou art from everlasting.

3 The floods have lifted up, O LORD, the floods have lifted up their voice; the floods lift up their waves.

4 The LORD on high is mightier than the noise of many waters, yea, than the mighty waves of the sea.

5 Thy testimonies are very sure: holiness becometh thine house, O LORD, for ever.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A short song of kingship: the LORD reigns, clothed with majesty and girded with strength, and the world is established so that it cannot be moved. His throne is established of old; he is from everlasting. Though the floods lift up their voice and waves, the LORD on high is mightier than the noise of many waters and the mighty waves of the sea. The psalm closes by tying that power to trust: his testimonies are very sure, and holiness becomes his house for ever. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Majesty as robe and strength as armour distinguish God's reign: earthly majesty is a glow-worm to the sun, and the world's standing is continued creation, providence upholding what creation founded, so the church, even more, cannot be moved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. The throne established of old rests on two grounds: God's right to rule is founded in his making the world, so his title is without flaw, ancient before any other rule, principality or power was erected and continuing when all others shall be put down; and the whole administration of his government was settled in his eternal counsels before all worlds — because God himself was from everlasting, his throne and all its determinations were so too, for in an eternal mind there could not but be eternal thoughts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. The floods lifting up are a threatening storm supposed: their voice speaks terror and their waves speak real danger, like a tempestuous sea — the heathen raging to ruin the church, overwhelm it like a deluge, sink it like a ship at sea, as the church is tossed with tempests and the floods of ungodly men make saints afraid. Henry applies it inwardly too, to the tumults in our own bosoms through prevailing passions and frights, which put the soul into disorder and are ready to overthrow its graces and comforts — but if the Lord reign there, even the winds and seas shall obey him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Against that storm the Lord on high is an immovable anchor. That he is on high denotes his safety, that the floods cannot reach him, and his sovereignty, that they are ruled by him, overruled, and wherein they rebel, overcome; and that he is mightier means he does more wondrous things than the noise of many waters, which cannot disturb his rest or rule nor defeat his designs. The enemies' power is but noise — more sound than substance, Pharaoh king of Egypt but a noise — so the church's friends are commonly more frightened than hurt, and God can command peace to the church and peace in the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Thy testimonies are very sure: holiness becometh thine house, O LORD, for ever, is the King reigning in truth and holiness: first, all his promises are inviolably faithful: thy testimonies are very sure. As God is able to protect his church, so he is true to the promises he has made of its safety and victory. His word is passed, and all the saints may rely upon it. Whatever was foretold concerning the kingdom of the Messiah would certainly have its accomplishment in due time. Those testimonies upon which the faith and hope of the Old-Testament saints were built were very sure, and would not fail them. Second, all his people ought to be conscientiously pure: holiness becomes thy house, O Lord! for ever. God's church is his house; it is a holy house, cleansed from sin, consecrated by God, and employed in his service. The holiness of it is its beauty — nothing better becomes the saints than conformity to God's image and an entire devotedness to his honour — and it is its strength and safety; it is the holiness of God's house that secures it against the many waters and their noise. Where there is purity there shall be peace. Fashions change, and that which is becoming at one time is not so at another; but holiness always becomes God's house and family, and those who belong to it; it is perpetually decent; and nothing so ill becomes the worshippers of the holy God as unholiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
