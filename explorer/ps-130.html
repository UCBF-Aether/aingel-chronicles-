The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 130 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Out of the depths have I cried unto thee, O LORD.

2 Lord, hear my voice: let thine ears be attentive to the voice of my supplications.

3 If thou, LORD, shouldest mark iniquities, O Lord, who shall stand?

4 But there is forgiveness with thee, that thou mayest be feared.

5 I wait for the LORD, my soul doth wait, and in his word do I hope.

6 My soul waiteth for the Lord more than they that watch for the morning: I say, more than they that watch for the morning.

7 Let Israel hope in the LORD: for with the LORD there is mercy, and with him is plenteous redemption.

8 And he shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

"Out of the depths have I cried unto thee, O LORD" — a short psalm of waiting and forgiveness. If the LORD should mark iniquities, who shall stand? But there is forgiveness with him, that he may be feared. The speaker waits for the LORD, his soul waits, and in God's word he hopes, more than watchmen wait for the morning — the image doubled for emphasis. The waiting widens to all Israel: let Israel hope in the LORD, for with him there is mercy and plenteous redemption, and he shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry opens a penitential psalm wholly on affairs of soul, one of seven used by penitents on admission into church, all concerned to apply it: best men sometimes in depths — great trouble, at loss what to do, depths of distress almost despair, spirit low dark sinking drooping cast down disquieted — yet in greatest depths privilege to cry unto God and be heard; prayer reaches heights of heaven out of greatest trouble in this world, Jeremiah out of dungeon, Daniel out of den, Jonah out of fish's belly; duty and interest to cry, likeliest way both to prevent sinking lower and recover out of horrible pit and miry clay (Ps 40:1-2). Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry prays in faith for answer of peace: Lord hear my voice, my complaint and prayer, let ears be attentive to voice both of afflictions and of supplications — assurance while continuing to call, not mere crying into silence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry humbles before justice: calling God Lord twice in few words, Jah and Adonai, very emphatic, awful sense of glorious majesty and dread of wrath; acknowledge iniquities cannot justify or plead Not guilty, remarkable liable to animadversion; own power and justice — if extreme to mark what we do amiss no hopes of coming off, eye discovers enough in best man to ground condemnation, cannot stand but certainly cast, shut out from favour shut up under wrath, cannot escape resist nor bear avenging hand; admire patience forbearance, undone if he marked, knows it therefore bears, of mercy we are not consumed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry casts on pardoning mercy: unspeakable comfort in all approaches that forgiveness with him, what we need; put himself into capacity to pardon, declared gracious merciful ready to forgive (Exod 34:6-7), promised forgive repenting, never any dealing found him implacable but easy entreated swift to mercy; with us iniquity therefore well with him forgiveness; propitiation reading — Jesus Christ great propitiation, ransom God found, ever with him as advocate, through him hope forgiveness; not that thou mayest be made bold with presumed upon, but that thou mayest be feared — worshipped served by children of men who sinners could have no dealings if not Master passing many faults, encourages not turned off for every misdemeanour nor any if truly repent, invites sinners repent return to fear, gracious merciful receives on repentance (Joel 2:13; Matt 3:2), holy awe of pardoning mercy (Hos 3:5, fear Lord and his goodness), then expect benefit when holy fear its object. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry waits in climax, song of degrees ascents: I wait for Lord — expect relief comfort believing will come, longing till comes, patiently bearing delay, resolving look for it from no other hand; my soul doth wait — sincerity not profession only, expectant, for Lord soul waits, gifts of grace operations of power; ground — in his word do I hope, hope only what promised in word not creatures of fancy, because promised not opinion of merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry waits more than watchers for morning: well-assured morning will come, so am I God will return in mercy according to promise, covenant more firm than ordinances of day and night for they shall end but that everlasting; very desirous it would come — sentinels on walls, watchers with sick, travellers abroad long before day wish dawning, but more earnestly good man longs tokens of favour visits of grace, more readily aware of first appearances than they of day; Hammond reads soul hastens to Lord from guards in morning, daily betaking early addressing prayers and soul before priests' morning sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry encourages all people of God, body and every good man surnaming himself by name of Israel (Isa 44:5), cheerfully stay upon him, for two reasons: light of nature discovers mercy with him, God of Israel merciful Father of mercies, mercy inherent delight darling attribute with him in all works counsels; light of gospel discovers redemption with him contrived by him wrought in fulness of time hidden in God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry redeems Israel from all iniquities: redemption from sin from all sin, eternal redemption Jesus Christ author of, saves people from sins (Matt 1:21), redeems from all iniquity (Titus 2:14), turns ungodliness from Jacob (Rom 11:26), both condemning and commanding power of sin; plenteous redemption — all-sufficient fulness of merit and grace in Redeemer, enough for all enough for each enough for me; redemption from sin includes redemption from all other evils therefore plenteous; persons — Israel according to spirit, all in covenant as Israel was, Israelites indeed in whom no guile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
