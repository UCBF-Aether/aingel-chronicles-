The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Sirach 9 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Be not jealous over the wife of thy bosom, and teach her not an evil lesson against thyself.

2 Give not thy soul unto a woman to set her foot upon thy substance.

3 Meet not with an harlot, lest thou fall into her snares.

4 Use not much the company of a woman that is a singer, lest thou be taken with her attempts.

5 Gaze not on a maid, that thou fall not by those things that are precious in her.

6 Give not thy soul unto harlots, that thou lose not thine inheritance.

7 Look not round about thee in the streets of the city, neither wander thou in the solitary place thereof.

8 Turn away thine eye from a beautiful woman, and look not upon another's beauty; for many have been deceived by the beauty of a woman; for herewith love is kindled as a fire.

9 Sit not at all with another man's wife, nor sit down with her in thine arms, and spend not thy money with her at the wine; lest thine heart incline unto her, and so through thy desire thou fall into destruction.

10 Forsake not an old friend; for the new is not comparable to him: a new friend is as new wine; when it is old, thou shalt drink it with pleasure.

11 Envy not the glory of a sinner: for thou knowest not what shall be his end.

12 Delight not in the thing that the ungodly have pleasure in; but remember they shall not go unpunished unto their grave.

13 Keep thee far from the man that hath power to kill; so shalt thou not doubt the fear of death: and if thou come unto him, make no fault, lest he take away thy life presently: remember that thou goest in the midst of snares, and that thou walkest upon the battlements of the city.

14 As near as thou canst, guess at thy neighbour, and consult with the wise.

15 Let thy talk be with the wise, and all thy communication in the law of the most High.

16 And let just men eat and drink with thee; and let thy glorying be in the fear of the Lord.

17 For the hand of the artificer the work shall be commended: and the wise ruler of the people for his speech.

18 A man of an ill tongue is dangerous in his city; and he that is rash in his talk shall be hated.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Household conduct, friendship, and speech are the chapter's subjects. Jealousy over a wife teaches an evil lesson against oneself, an old friend is like old wine and not to be forsaken for a new one, and a man of ill tongue is dangerous in his city, rash talk earning hatred. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Do not be jealous over the wife of your bosom, and do not teach her an evil lesson against yourself. Jealousy over a wife teaches her the very evil it fears, turned against the husband himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Do not give your soul to a woman to set her foot upon your substance. Surrendering yourself to a woman lets her trample your substance underfoot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. Do not meet with a harlot, lest you fall into her snares. Meeting a harlot is entering snares already set. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. Do not use much the company of a woman who is a singer, lest you be taken with her attempts. Frequent company with a singing woman exposes a man to her practised allurements. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Do not gaze on a maid, lest you fall by those things that are precious in her. Gazing at a young woman leads to falling through attraction to her charms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. Do not give your soul to harlots, lest you lose your inheritance. Giving oneself to harlots costs the inheritance itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. Do not look round about in the streets of the city, nor wander in its solitary places. Roving eyes in streets and lonely corners invite the very temptations this chapter warns against. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Turn your eye from a beautiful woman, and do not look upon another's beauty, for many have been deceived by a woman's beauty, and love is kindled by it as a fire. Beauty deceives many, and desire kindled by looking burns like fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. Do not sit at all with another man's wife, nor sit down with her in your arms, nor spend your money with her at the wine, lest your heart incline to her and through desire you fall into destruction. Intimacy with another man's wife, with wine and money spent on it, bends the heart until desire ends in destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Do not forsake an old friend, for the new is not comparable to him; a new friend is like new wine, and when it is old you will drink it with pleasure. Old friendship outvalues new, as old wine outvalues new; the new must age before it gives pleasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. Do not envy the glory of a sinner, for you do not know what his end will be. A sinner's present glory should not be envied, since his end is unknown and may be ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. Do not delight in what the ungodly take pleasure in, but remember they will not go unpunished to their grave. Pleasures of the ungodly end in punishment before the grave, so they are no delight for the wise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. Keep far from the man who has power to kill, so you will not doubt the fear of death; and if you come to him, make no fault, lest he take away your life presently, remembering you go amid snares and walk upon the battlements of the city. Nearness to a man with power of death is walking among snares on high battlements, where a single fault costs life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. As near as you can, guess at your neighbour, and consult with the wise. Judge your neighbour as best you can, and take counsel with the wise rather than trusting guesswork alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. Let your talk be with the wise, and all your communication in the law of the Most High. Conversation should be kept among the wise and centred on the law of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. Let just men eat and drink with you, and let your glorying be in the fear of the Lord. Table company should be just men, and boasting should be in reverence for God, not in anything else. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. For the hand of the artificer the work will be commended, and the wise ruler of the people for his speech. A craftsman is praised by his work, and a wise ruler by his speech; each is known by his proper product. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. A man of an ill tongue is dangerous in his city, and he who is rash in his talk will be hated. An evil tongue endangers a whole city, and rash talk earns hatred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
