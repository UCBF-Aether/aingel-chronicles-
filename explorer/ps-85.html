The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 85 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Lord, thou hast been favourable unto thy land: thou hast brought back the captivity of Jacob.

2 Thou hast forgiven the iniquity of thy people, thou hast covered all their sin. Selah.

3 Thou hast taken away all thy wrath: thou hast turned thyself from the fierceness of thine anger.

4 Turn us, O God of our salvation, and cause thine anger toward us to cease.

5 Wilt thou be angry with us for ever? wilt thou draw out thine anger to all generations?

6 Wilt thou not revive us again: that thy people may rejoice in thee?

7 Shew us thy mercy, O LORD, and grant us thy salvation.

8 I will hear what God the LORD will speak: for he will speak peace unto his people, and to his saints: but let them not turn again to folly.

9 Surely his salvation is nigh them that fear him; that glory may dwell in our land.

10 Mercy and truth are met together; righteousness and peace have kissed each other.

11 Truth shall spring out of the earth; and righteousness shall look down from heaven.

12 Yea, the LORD shall give that which is good; and our land shall yield her increase.

13 Righteousness shall go before him; and shall set us in the way of his steps.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Thanksgiving for a past restoration — the captivity of Jacob brought back, sins forgiven and covered, wrath turned away — becomes a present plea: turn us again, revive us, show us mercy and grant salvation. The speaker resolves to hear what God will speak, for he will speak peace to his people if they do not turn again to folly; then mercy and truth meet, righteousness and peace kiss, truth springs from the earth, and righteousness goes before God to set the people in the way of his steps. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Favour to the land as God's own, smiling on it with distinguishing favours, is the spring-head of national good: possession of Canaan itself came by favour, and without its continuance ruin would have come many times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Forgiven iniquity and covered sin are paired with return from captivity: Henry notes the return counts as favour only when accompanied by pardon, since God covers sin wholly, not in part. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Wrath taken away follows sin covered: God pacified when we are purified, anger turned by an intercessor standing in the gap, not stirred to consume. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Turn us, O God of our salvation, and cause thine anger toward us to cease — past favour is the plea for present restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Anger forever and anger drawn to all generations are deprecations, not predictions: Henry contrasts impenitent sinners, for whom wrath is endless, with God's people, for whom God will not contend forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Revival is prayed for as a second gathering, like the return from Babylon as life from the dead: God must revive his people with comforts spoken and deliverances wrought, as he once gave the captives some reviving in bondage. Henry draws the double end — that the people may rejoice, and that they may rejoice in God himself, who is both fountain of all mercies and centre of all joys. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Shew us thy mercy, O Lord, and grant us thy salvation, is prayer for the manifestation of God's good-will: Show us thy mercy, O Lord! show thyself merciful to us; not only have mercy on us, but let us have the comfortable evidences of that mercy; let us know that thou hast mercy on us and mercy in store for us. And they pray that God would, graciously to them and gloriously to himself, appear on their behalf: Grant us thy salvation; grant it by thy promise, and then, no doubt, thou wilt work it by thy providence. The vessels of God's mercy are the heirs of his salvation; he shows mercy to those to whom he grants salvation; for salvation is of mere mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Watch-tower listening after prayer: God speaks peace to saints, but peace is spoken only to those who turn from sin, since backsliding after peace is egregious folly, and sin itself is the war against the peace God offers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Surely his salvation is nigh them that fear him, that glory may dwell in our land, is help at hand and honour secured: Surely his salvation is nigh, nigh to us, nigher than we think it is: it will soon be effected, how great soever our difficulties and distresses are, when God's time shall come, and that time is not far off. When the tale of bricks is doubled, then Moses comes. It is nigh to all who fear him; when trouble is nigh salvation is nigh, for God is a very present help in time of trouble to all who are his; whereas salvation is far from the wicked. This may fitly be applied to Christ the author of eternal salvation: it was the comfort of the Old-Testament saints that, though they lived not to see that redemption in Jerusalem which they waited for, yet they were sure it was nigh, and would be welcome, to all that fear God. And honour is secured: that glory may dwell in our land, that we may have the worship of God settled and established among us; for that is the glory of a land. When that goes, Ichabod — the glory has departed; when that stays glory dwells. This may refer to the Messiah, who was to be the glory of his people Israel, and who came and dwelt among them, for which reason their land is called Immanuel's land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry reads the meeting and kissing of the graces three ways at once: as reformation, where rulers and ruled alike are merciful, true, righteous and peaceable; as returned favour, where man's truth meets God's mercy and man's righteousness meets God's peace; and chiefly as the Messiah's undertaking, where salvation is so contrived that God may pardon sinners and be at peace with them without any wrong to his truth and righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Truth springing from the earth is, in Henry's reading, truth grown in its proper soil, the hearts of men — the inward truth God desires, spread like grass over the earth — while righteousness looking down from heaven is God's mercy shining as the sun upon what it cherishes. Through the Mediator, heaven and earth meet again: God is just, and the justifier of those who believe in Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. The Lord gives what is good, everything he himself sees to be good for us, for all good comes from his goodness. Henry ties the land's increase to the graces going before it: when mercy, truth and righteousness govern hearts, seek first the kingdom and other things are added, and soul-prosperity either brings outward prosperity with it or sweetens the want of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Righteousness shall go before him, and shall set us in the way of his steps, is a sure guidance in the good way: the righteousness of his promise which he has made to us, assuring us of happiness, and the righteousness of sanctification, that good work which he has wrought in us, these shall go before him to prepare his way, both to raise our expectations of his favour and to qualify us for it; and these shall go before us also, and be our guide to set us in the way of his steps, that is, to encourage our hopes and guide our practice, that we may go forth to meet him when he is coming towards us in ways of mercy. Christ, the sun of righteousness, shall bring us to God, and put us into the way that leads to him. John Baptist, a preacher of righteousness, shall go before Christ to prepare his way. Righteousness is a sure guide both in meeting God and in following him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
