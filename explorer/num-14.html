The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And all the congregation lifted up their voice, and cried; and the people wept that night.

2 And all the children of Israel murmured against Moses and against Aaron: and the whole congregation said unto them, Would God that we had died in the land of Egypt! or would God we had died in this wilderness!

3 And wherefore hath the LORD brought us unto this land, to fall by the sword, that our wives and our children should be a prey? were it not better for us to return into Egypt?

4 And they said one to another, Let us make a captain, and let us return into Egypt.

5 Then Moses and Aaron fell on their faces before all the assembly of the congregation of the children of Israel.

6 And Joshua the son of Nun, and Caleb the son of Jephunneh, which were of them that searched the land, rent their clothes:

7 And they spake unto all the company of the children of Israel, saying, The land, which we passed through to search it, is an exceeding good land.

8 If the LORD delight in us, then he will bring us into this land, and give it us; a land which floweth with milk and honey.

9 Only rebel not ye against the LORD, neither fear ye the people of the land; for they are bread for us: their defence is departed from them, and the LORD is with us: fear them not.

10 But all the congregation bade stone them with stones. And the glory of the LORD appeared in the tabernacle of the congregation before all the children of Israel.

11 And the LORD said unto Moses, How long will this people provoke me? and how long will it be ere they believe me, for all the signs which I have shewed among them?

12 I will smite them with the pestilence, and disinherit them, and will make of thee a greater nation and mightier than they.

13 And Moses said unto the LORD, Then the Egyptians shall hear it, (for thou broughtest up this people in thy might from among them;)

14 And they will tell it to the inhabitants of this land: for they have heard that thou LORD art among this people, that thou LORD art seen face to face, and that thy cloud standeth over them, and that thou goest before them, by day time in a pillar of a cloud, and in a pillar of fire by night.

15 Now if thou shalt kill all this people as one man, then the nations which have heard the fame of thee will speak, saying,

16 Because the LORD was not able to bring this people into the land which he sware unto them, therefore he hath slain them in the wilderness.

17 And now, I beseech thee, let the power of my LORD be great, according as thou hast spoken, saying,

18 The LORD is longsuffering, and of great mercy, forgiving iniquity and transgression, and by no means clearing the guilty, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation.

19 Pardon, I beseech thee, the iniquity of this people according unto the greatness of thy mercy, and as thou hast forgiven this people, from Egypt even until now.

20 And the LORD said, I have pardoned according to thy word:

21 But as truly as I live, all the earth shall be filled with the glory of the LORD.

22 Because all those men which have seen my glory, and my miracles, which I did in Egypt and in the wilderness, and have tempted me now these ten times, and have not hearkened to my voice;

23 Surely they shall not see the land which I sware unto their fathers, neither shall any of them that provoked me see it:

24 But my servant Caleb, because he had another spirit with him, and hath followed me fully, him will I bring into the land whereinto he went; and his seed shall possess it.

25 (Now the Amalekites and the Canaanites dwelt in the valley.) Tomorrow turn you, and get you into the wilderness by the way of the Red sea.

26 And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron, saying,

27 How long shall I bear with this evil congregation, which murmur against me? I have heard the murmurings of the children of Israel, which they murmur against me.

28 Say unto them, As truly as I live, saith the LORD, as ye have spoken in mine ears, so will I do to you:

29 Your carcases shall fall in this wilderness; and all that were numbered of you, according to your whole number, from twenty years old and upward which have murmured against me.

30 Doubtless ye shall not come into the land, concerning which I sware to make you dwell therein, save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun.

31 But your little ones, which ye said should be a prey, them will I bring in, and they shall know the land which ye have despised.

32 But as for you, your carcases, they shall fall in this wilderness.

33 And your children shall wander in the wilderness forty years, and bear your whoredoms, until your carcases be wasted in the wilderness.

34 After the number of the days in which ye searched the land, even forty days, each day for a year, shall ye bear your iniquities, even forty years, and ye shall know my breach of promise.

35 I the LORD have said, I will surely do it unto all this evil congregation, that are gathered together against me: in this wilderness they shall be consumed, and there they shall die.

36 And the men, which Moses sent to search the land, who returned, and made all the congregation to murmur against him, by bringing up a slander upon the land,

37 Even those men that did bring up the evil report upon the land, died by the plague before the LORD.

38 But Joshua the son of Nun, and Caleb the son of Jephunneh, which were of the men that went to search the land, lived still.

39 And Moses told these sayings unto all the children of Israel: and the people mourned greatly.

40 And they rose up early in the morning, and gat them up into the top of the mountain, saying, Lo, we be here, and will go up unto the place which the LORD hath promised: for we have sinned.

41 And Moses said, Wherefore now do ye transgress the commandment of the LORD? but it shall not prosper.

42 Go not up, for the LORD is not among you; that ye be not smitten before your enemies.

43 For the Amalekites and the Canaanites are there before you, and ye shall fall by the sword: because ye are turned away from the LORD, therefore the LORD will not be with you.

44 But they presumed to go up unto the hill top: nevertheless the ark of the covenant of the LORD, and Moses, departed not out of the camp.

45 Then the Amalekites came down, and the Canaanites which dwelt in that hill, and smote them, and discomfited them, even unto Hormah.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the rebellion that follows the spies' report. All night the congregation weeps, murmurs against Moses and Aaron, and proposes to return to Egypt and choose a captain for the journey back. Joshua and Caleb tear their clothes and urge the people not to rebel, but the congregation threatens to stone them, and the glory of the LORD appears. Moses intercedes, and the sentence is pronounced: the numbered men from twenty years old and upward will die in the wilderness over forty years, their children will wander with them and then enter the land, and the ten spies die by plague at once. A rash attack the next morning, against Moses' warning, is beaten back by the Amalekites and Canaanites. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. All the congregation lifted up their voice and cried, and the people wept that night. JFB notes not literally all, for there were exceptions, but the night's weeping set the tone: a whole camp surrendered to despair at the spies' report. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. All Israel murmured against Moses and Aaron, saying, would God we had died in Egypt, or in this wilderness. JFB calls this insolence to generous leaders and base ingratitude to God, showing the deep degradation that made the decree debarring that generation necessary, and notes they were punished by their wishes being granted, to die in that wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They asked why the Lord had brought them to this land, to fall by the sword, with wives and children as a prey, and said it were better to return to Egypt. Henry calls this the madness of unbridled passion, prodigal of what nature counts most dear: they wished rather to die criminals under God's justice than live conquerors in his favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They said to one another, let us make a captain and return into Egypt. The mutiny had reached the point of organising a rival leadership, and JFB notes a leader to reconduct them to Egypt is spoken of in Nehemiah as actually nominated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Moses and Aaron fell on their faces before all the assembly of the congregation. JFB reads them as humble and earnest suppliants, either entreating the people to desist or, more likely, turning to God as the usual and only refuge from a tumultuous and stiff-necked rabble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Joshua and Caleb, who were among those who searched the land, rent their clothes. JFB notes the rending testified grief and horror at the mutiny against Moses and blasphemy against God, while they also tried by truthful statement to persuade the people how easily so desirable a country might be possessed if they did not provoke God to abandon them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They spoke to all the company, saying, the land we passed through to search it is an exceeding good land. Against the evil report, the two faithful spies reasserted the land's goodness from their own eyes, not from hearsay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. If the Lord delights in us, he will bring us into this land and give it to us, a land flowing with milk and honey. JFB notes milk and honey are a general expression for a rich and fertile country, its principal products, and the condition is God's delight, not Israel's strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Only rebel not against the Lord, neither fear the people of the land, for they are bread for us, their defence is departed from them, and the Lord is with us: fear them not. JFB explains their defence as their shadow, the favour of God now lost to a people whose iniquities were full and transferred to Israel, and Henry adds that though Israel dwelt in tents, they were fortified while God's presence was with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. But all the congregation bade stone them with stones, and the glory of the Lord appeared in the tabernacle before all Israel. JFB notes the glory was seasonably manifested on this emergency to rescue God's ambassadors from peril, and Henry adds that those who faithfully expose themselves for God shall be hid from men's rage, under heaven or in heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The Lord asked Moses, how long will this people provoke me, and how long will it be before they believe me, for all the signs I have shown among them? The question counts the signs: after Egypt and the wilderness, unbelief was no longer ignorance but provocation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. God said, I will smite them with pestilence and disinherit them, and make of you a greater and mightier nation. JFB stresses this was not a final decree but a threatening, suspended, as the issue showed, on Moses' intercession and Israel's repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Moses answered, then the Egyptians shall hear it, for you brought up this people in your might from among them. The argument is God's reputation among the nations: destroying Israel would be heard first in Egypt, where his might had been displayed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The Egyptians will tell the inhabitants of this land, for they have heard that you, Lord, are among this people, seen face to face, your cloud standing over them, going before them by day in cloud and by night in fire. The nations already knew the signs of God's presence, so Israel's fate would be read as a verdict on God's power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Now if you kill all this people as one man, the nations who have heard your fame will speak, saying, because the Lord was not able to bring this people into the land he swore to them, therefore he slew them in the wilderness. Moses puts the slander plainly: the nations would not say God was just, but that God was unable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Because the Lord was not able to bring this people into the land which he swore to them, therefore he has slain them in the wilderness. This is the speech Moses feared the nations would make, turning God's judgment into a tale of divine failure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Now, I beseech you, let the power of my Lord be great, according as you have spoken. JFB renders the prayer as, let your power be magnified: the plea is that God's power should be shown in mercy, not only in destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The Lord is longsuffering, and of great mercy, forgiving iniquity and transgression, and by no means clearing the guilty, visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children to the third and fourth generation. Henry notes Moses argued consistently with God's character in his abundant mercies: the God who proclaimed himself longsuffering could forgive, without clearing the guilty outright. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Pardon, I beseech you, the iniquity of this people according to the greatness of your mercy, and as you have forgiven this people from Egypt even until now. Henry calls Moses a type of Christ here, praying for those who despitefully used him, and notes the pardon of a nation's sin is the turning away of the nation's punishment, for which Moses is so earnest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The Lord said, I have pardoned according to your word. The pardon was granted as Moses asked, yet, as the following verses show, pardon from immediate destruction was not entrance into the land: the sentence was commuted, not cancelled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. But as truly as I live, all the earth shall be filled with the glory of the Lord. JFB reads this restrictively for the occasion: the report would spread over all the land of the terrible things in righteousness God would do in inflicting the doom now pronounced, while in its full sense it awaits the universal prevalence of his glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Because all those men who have seen my glory and my miracles in Egypt and the wilderness, and have tempted me now ten times, and not hearkened to my voice, shall not see the land. JFB notes ten times means very frequently: the generation had seen glory and miracles repeatedly, and still refused to listen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Surely they shall not see the land which I swore to their fathers, neither shall any of them that provoked me see it. The oath to the fathers stood, but the provokers were cut out of it by their own provocation: the promise passed to their children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. But my servant Caleb, because he had another spirit with him and has followed me fully, him will I bring into the land whereinto he went, and his seed shall possess it. JFB describes Caleb, under God's Spirit, as a man of bold, generous, heroic courage, above worldly anxieties and fears, and notes Joshua was also excepted though not named here, being Moses' constant attendant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Now the Amalekites and Canaanites dwelt in the valley: tomorrow turn and get into the wilderness by the way of the Red Sea. JFB insists this is no mere parenthesis: those nomad tribes then occupied the valley determined to oppose Israel, so God commanded a safe and timely retreat, lest a people who had forfeited his presence fall a helpless prey with wives and children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. How long shall I bear with this evil congregation, which murmur against me? I have heard the murmurings of Israel, which they murmur against me. The renewed question, spoken to Moses and Aaron together, turns the murmuring back on the murmurers: God had heard every word, and the sentence answers it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Say to them, as truly as I live, says the Lord, as you have spoken in my ears, so will I do to you. Henry's summary is exact: God took them at their word, making their sin their ruin, for they wished to die in the wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Your carcases shall fall in this wilderness, and all that were numbered of you, according to your whole number, from twenty years old and upward, which have murmured against me. The census of chapter 1 becomes the death roll: the fighting men who were counted for Canaan would die outside it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Doubtless you shall not come into the land concerning which I swore to make you dwell in it, save Caleb son of Jephunneh and Joshua son of Nun. JFB notes these two are specially mentioned as honourable exceptions among the scouts and future leaders, while some of the old generation who did not join the mutiny, including the whole order of priests, were also spared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. But your little ones, which you said should be a prey, them will I bring in, and they shall know the land which you have despised. Henry notes God put a difference between guilty and innocent, cutting off the guilty without touching their children, so that he did not utterly take away his loving kindness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. But as for you, your carcases, they shall fall in this wilderness. The repetition seals the sentence: the generation that refused the land would not merely be delayed from it, but buried outside it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And your children shall wander in the wilderness forty years, and bear your whoredoms, until your carcases be wasted in the wilderness. The children bore the wandering their parents' unfaithfulness caused, until the guilty generation was wasted away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. After the number of the days in which you searched the land, even forty days, each day for a year, shall you bear your iniquities, even forty years, and you shall know my breach of promise. JFB explains the breach: by breaking the covenant's terms, they made it null and void on God's part, so he withheld the blessings promised on condition of obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. I the Lord have said, I will surely do it to all this evil congregation, that are gathered together against me: in this wilderness they shall be consumed, and there they shall die. The sentence is spoken as already certain: gathered against God, they would be consumed where they stood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And the men Moses sent to search the land, who returned and made all the congregation murmur against him by bringing up a slander upon the land, died by the plague before the Lord. Henry notes those who misrepresent religion and raise dislike toward it greatly provoke God, and the ten spies were struck dead on the spot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Even those men that brought up the evil report upon the land died by the plague before the Lord. JFB stresses the great and appalling mortality clearly betokened the hand of the Lord, distinguishing the spies' immediate judgment from the generation's forty-year sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. But Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Jephunneh, who were of the men that went to search the land, lived still. The two faithful spies lived through the plague that killed their ten companions, the first proof that God's exception of them was real. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. Moses told these sayings to all Israel, and the people mourned greatly. Henry notes justly are murmurers made mourners, but their mourning was for the judgment only, not for the sin, and so it did them no service: tears will not quench flames nor cool the tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. They rose up early in the morning, got up to the top of the mountain, saying, lo, we are here, and will go up to the place the Lord has promised, for we have sinned. JFB notes the impression of grief was brief: they rushed from one extreme of rashness to another, claiming repentance, or trusting God would yet give the land, while preparing to ascend against divine warning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Moses said, wherefore now do you transgress the commandment of the Lord? But it shall not prosper. Henry's rule applies exactly: that which has been duty in its season, when mistimed, may be turned into sin, and God bade them go and they would not, he forbade them and they would go. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Go not up, for the Lord is not among you, that you be not smitten before your enemies. Without God's presence the army was no army: the warning named the exact danger, defeat before enemies they had just declared too strong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. For the Amalekites and Canaanites are there before you, and you shall fall by the sword, because you are turned away from the Lord, therefore the Lord will not be with you. The enemies were already posted on the heights, and the people's turning away had removed the only strength that could have carried them through. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. But they presumed to go up to the hill top, nevertheless the ark of the covenant of the Lord and Moses departed not out of the camp. The presumption is marked by what stayed behind: ark and Moses remained in camp, so the climbers went up visibly without God and without their leader. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. Then the Amalekites came down, and the Canaanites who dwelt in that hill, smote them and discomfited them, even unto Hormah. JFB notes the enemies lay in ambuscade expecting the movement and rushed down from the heights, and Hormah took its name afterwards from the immense slaughter on this occasion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Even those men that brought up the evil report upon the land died by the plague before the Lord. JFB stresses the great and appalling mortality clearly betokened the hand of the Lord, distinguishing the spies' immediate judgment from the generation's forty-year sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. But Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Jephunneh, who were of the men that went to search the land, lived still. The two faithful spies lived through the plague that killed their ten companions, the first proof that God's exception of them was real. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. They rose up early in the morning, got up to the top of the mountain, saying, lo, we are here, and will go up to the place the Lord has promised, for we have sinned. JFB notes the impression of grief was brief: they rushed from one extreme of rashness to another, claiming repentance, or trusting God would yet give the land, while preparing to ascend against divine warning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. For the Amalekites and Canaanites are there before you, and you shall fall by the sword, because you are turned away from the Lord, therefore the Lord will not be with you. The enemies were already posted on the heights, and the people's turning away had removed the only strength that could have carried them through. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. But they presumed to go up to the hill top, nevertheless the ark of the covenant of the Lord and Moses departed not out of the camp. The presumption is marked by what stayed behind: ark and Moses remained in camp, so the climbers went up visibly without God and without their leader. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. Then the Amalekites came down, and the Canaanites who dwelt in that hill, smote them and discomfited them, even unto Hormah. JFB notes the enemies lay in ambuscade expecting the movement and rushed down from the heights, and Hormah took its name afterwards from the immense slaughter on this occasion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Matthew Henry

  • Verse 3. They asked why the Lord had brought them to this land, to fall by the sword, with wives and children as a prey, and said it were better to return to Egypt. Henry calls this the madness of unbridled passion, prodigal of what nature counts most dear: they wished rather to die criminals under God's justice than live conquerors in his favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The Lord is longsuffering, and of great mercy, forgiving iniquity and transgression, and by no means clearing the guilty, visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children to the third and fourth generation. Henry notes Moses argued consistently with God's character in his abundant mercies: the God who proclaimed himself longsuffering could forgive, without clearing the guilty outright. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Pardon, I beseech you, the iniquity of this people according to the greatness of your mercy, and as you have forgiven this people from Egypt even until now. Henry calls Moses a type of Christ here, praying for those who despitefully used him, and notes the pardon of a nation's sin is the turning away of the nation's punishment, for which Moses is so earnest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The Lord said, I have pardoned according to your word. The pardon was granted as Moses asked, yet, as the following verses show, pardon from immediate destruction was not entrance into the land: the sentence was commuted, not cancelled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Surely they shall not see the land which I swore to their fathers, neither shall any of them that provoked me see it. The oath to the fathers stood, but the provokers were cut out of it by their own provocation: the promise passed to their children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. How long shall I bear with this evil congregation, which murmur against me? I have heard the murmurings of Israel, which they murmur against me. The renewed question, spoken to Moses and Aaron together, turns the murmuring back on the murmurers: God had heard every word, and the sentence answers it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Say to them, as truly as I live, says the Lord, as you have spoken in my ears, so will I do to you. Henry's summary is exact: God took them at their word, making their sin their ruin, for they wished to die in the wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Your carcases shall fall in this wilderness, and all that were numbered of you, according to your whole number, from twenty years old and upward, which have murmured against me. The census of chapter 1 becomes the death roll: the fighting men who were counted for Canaan would die outside it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. But your little ones, which you said should be a prey, them will I bring in, and they shall know the land which you have despised. Henry notes God put a difference between guilty and innocent, cutting off the guilty without touching their children, so that he did not utterly take away his loving kindness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. But as for you, your carcases, they shall fall in this wilderness. The repetition seals the sentence: the generation that refused the land would not merely be delayed from it, but buried outside it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And your children shall wander in the wilderness forty years, and bear your whoredoms, until your carcases be wasted in the wilderness. The children bore the wandering their parents' unfaithfulness caused, until the guilty generation was wasted away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. I the Lord have said, I will surely do it to all this evil congregation, that are gathered together against me: in this wilderness they shall be consumed, and there they shall die. The sentence is spoken as already certain: gathered against God, they would be consumed where they stood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Moses told these sayings to all Israel, and the people mourned greatly. Henry notes justly are murmurers made mourners, but their mourning was for the judgment only, not for the sin, and so it did them no service: tears will not quench flames nor cool the tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Moses said, wherefore now do you transgress the commandment of the Lord? But it shall not prosper. Henry's rule applies exactly: that which has been duty in its season, when mistimed, may be turned into sin, and God bade them go and they would not, he forbade them and they would go. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Go not up, for the Lord is not among you, that you be not smitten before your enemies. Without God's presence the army was no army: the warning named the exact danger, defeat before enemies they had just declared too strong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 9. Only rebel not against the Lord, neither fear the people of the land, for they are bread for us, their defence is departed from them, and the Lord is with us: fear them not. JFB explains their defence as their shadow, the favour of God now lost to a people whose iniquities were full and transferred to Israel, and Henry adds that though Israel dwelt in tents, they were fortified while God's presence was with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. But all the congregation bade stone them with stones, and the glory of the Lord appeared in the tabernacle before all Israel. JFB notes the glory was seasonably manifested on this emergency to rescue God's ambassadors from peril, and Henry adds that those who faithfully expose themselves for God shall be hid from men's rage, under heaven or in heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. And the men Moses sent to search the land, who returned and made all the congregation murmur against him by bringing up a slander upon the land, died by the plague before the Lord. Henry notes those who misrepresent religion and raise dislike toward it greatly provoke God, and the ten spies were struck dead on the spot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
