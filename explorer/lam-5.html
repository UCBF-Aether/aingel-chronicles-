The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Lamentations 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Remember, O LORD, what is come upon us: consider, and behold our reproach.

2 Our inheritance is turned to strangers, our houses to aliens.

3 We are orphans and fatherless, our mothers are as widows.

4 We have drunken our water for money; our wood is sold unto us.

5 Our necks are under persecution: we labour, and have no rest.

6 We have given the hand to the Egyptians, and to the Assyrians, to be satisfied with bread.

7 Our fathers have sinned, and are not; and we have borne their iniquities.

8 Servants have ruled over us: there is none that doth deliver us out of their hand.

9 We gat our bread with the peril of our lives because of the sword of the wilderness.

10 Our skin was black like an oven because of the terrible famine.

11 They ravished the women in Zion, and the maids in the cities of Judah.

12 Princes are hanged up by their hand: the faces of elders were not honoured.

13 They took the young men to grind, and the children fell under the wood.

14 The elders have ceased from the gate, the young men from their musick.

15 The joy of our heart is ceased; our dance is turned into mourning.

16 The crown is fallen from our head: woe unto us, that we have sinned!

17 For this our heart is faint; for these things our eyes are dim.

18 Because of the mountain of Zion, which is desolate, the foxes walk upon it.

19 Thou, O LORD, remainest for ever; thy throne from generation to generation.

20 Wherefore dost thou forget us for ever, and forsake us so long time?

21 Turn thou us unto thee, O LORD, and we shall be turned; renew our days as of old.

22 But thou hast utterly rejected us; thou art very wroth against us.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The book ends not in description but in prayer: remember, O Lord, what is come upon us. Inheritance turned to strangers, houses to aliens, orphans and widows, a yoke on the neck with no rest — the whole community lays its reproach before God. The final plea is to be turned and renewed as in days of old, held against the fear that closes the book, that God has utterly rejected them and is very wroth. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Remember, O Lord, what has come upon us, consider and behold our reproach, the afflicted pouring out complaint to God, not of evils feared but of evils felt, and finding ease at the throne of grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Their inheritance is turned to strangers and their houses to aliens, the land and homes of God's people in foreign hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They are orphans and fatherless, their mothers as widows, the nation broken at the level of the family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They drink their own water for money and their wood is sold to them, paying for what was once their own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Their necks are under persecution, they labor and have no rest, bondage without respite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. They have given their hand to Egyptians and Assyrians to be satisfied with bread, submitting to former enemies for mere food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Their fathers sinned and are no more, and they have borne their iniquities, penitent and patient under what they suffer for their fathers' sins, hoping he who punishes will return in mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Servants have ruled over them, and none delivers them out of their hand, the lowest ruling the once-princely people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. They get their bread with peril of their lives because of the sword of the wilderness, every loaf fetched at risk of death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Their skin is black like an oven because of the terrible famine, hunger scorching them from within. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Enemies ravished the women in Zion and the maids in Judah's cities, violation added to conquest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Princes are hanged up by their hand, and elders' faces were not honored, rank and age alike dishonored. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Young men were taken to grind, and children fell under loads of wood, strength spent on servile labor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Elders have ceased from the gate, and young men from their music, public counsel and public joy both silenced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The joy of their heart has ceased, their dance turned into mourning, gladness itself extinguished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The crown has fallen from their head, and they cry, woe unto us, for we have sinned, acknowledging that all woes are owing to their own sin and folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. For this their heart is faint, and for these things their eyes are dim, grief weakening heart and sight alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Because Mount Zion is desolate, foxes walk upon it, the temple mount itself overrun by wild creatures, a ruin deeper than any other calamity to God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Thou, O Lord, remainest forever, thy throne from generation to generation, whatever changes on earth, God remaining wise, holy, just, and good, without variableness or shadow of turning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Wherefore dost thou forget them forever and forsake them so long, the prayer honestly voicing the fear of abandonment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Turn thou them unto thee, O Lord, and they shall be turned, renew their days as of old, since God never leaves any till they first leave him, and if he turns them in duty he will quickly return in mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. But thou hast utterly rejected us, thou art very wroth against us, the prayer ending on the edge of despair, yet still addressed to God, trusting in the end it shall be well with all who trust, fear, love, and serve the Lord, and watching against repining and despondency. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
