The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 John 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:

2 And he is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world.

3 And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments.

4 He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.

5 But whoso keepeth his word, in him verily is the love of God perfected: hereby know we that we are in him.

6 He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked.

7 Brethren, I write no new commandment unto you, but an old commandment which ye had from the beginning. The old commandment is the word which ye have heard from the beginning.

8 Again, a new commandment I write unto you, which thing is true in him and in you: because the darkness is past, and the true light now shineth.

9 He that saith he is in the light, and hateth his brother, is in darkness even until now.

10 He that loveth his brother abideth in the light, and there is none occasion of stumbling in him.

11 But he that hateth his brother is in darkness, and walketh in darkness, and knoweth not whither he goeth, because that darkness hath blinded his eyes.

12 I write unto you, little children, because your sins are forgiven you for his name’s sake.

13 I write unto you, fathers, because ye have known him that is from the beginning. I write unto you, young men, because ye have overcome the wicked one. I write unto you, little children, because ye have known the Father.

14 I have written unto you, fathers, because ye have known him that is from the beginning. I have written unto you, young men, because ye are strong, and the word of God abideth in you, and ye have overcome the wicked one.

15 Love not the world, neither the things that are in the world. If any man love the world, the love of the Father is not in him.

16 For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world.

17 And the world passeth away, and the lust thereof: but he that doeth the will of God abideth for ever.

18 Little children, it is the last time: and as ye have heard that antichrist shall come, even now are there many antichrists; whereby we know that it is the last time.

19 They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us: but they went out, that they might be made manifest that they were not all of us.

20 But ye have an unction from the Holy One, and ye know all things.

21 I have not written unto you because ye know not the truth, but because ye know it, and that no lie is of the truth.

22 Who is a liar but he that denieth that Jesus is the Christ? He is antichrist, that denieth the Father and the Son.

23 Whosoever denieth the Son, the same hath not the Father: he that acknowledgeth the Son hath the Father also.

24 Let that therefore abide in you, which ye have heard from the beginning. If that which ye have heard from the beginning shall remain in you, ye also shall continue in the Son, and in the Father.

25 And this is the promise that he hath promised us, even eternal life.

26 These things have I written unto you concerning them that seduce you.

27 But the anointing which ye have received of him abideth in you, and ye need not that any man teach you: but as the same anointing teacheth you of all things, and is truth, and is no lie, and even as it hath taught you, ye shall abide in him.

28 And now, little children, abide in him; that, when he shall appear, we may have confidence, and not be ashamed before him at his coming.

29 If ye know that he is righteous, ye know that every one that doeth righteousness is born of him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The writer writes so readers may not sin, yet if anyone does sin, there is an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous, the propitiation for sins, not for readers only but for the whole world. Knowing God is shown by keeping his commandments, and the old commandment to love is also new in Christ; loving a brother abides in light, hating a brother walks in darkness. Addresses to children, fathers and young men follow, with warnings not to love the world, and about antichrists, the anointing that teaches, and abiding in Christ until he appears. Interpreted Editorial overview of the chapter text; verse-by-verse study-note summaries follow

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John writes so that his readers may not sin at all, not so that grace becomes a licence for sin. If a believer does sin, he has an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous, who pleads on the ground of his own perfect obedience and endurance of the penalty, not on any goodness in us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Christ is himself the propitiation, the sacrifice that removes estrangement and appeases righteous wrath against sin. His advocacy is for believers, but his propitiation extends as widely as sin extends, to the sins of the whole world, so that no reader need think the Lord died for others but not for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The test of knowing God is keeping his commandments, guarding them as a precious thing rather than faultlessly conforming at every point. JFB notes that this answers the boasted knowledge of those who set obedience aside: hearty acceptance of God's whole revealed will is the mark of real knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. A claim to know God, joined to disobedience, is a lie, and the truth is not in such a person. Henry puts it as a question: what knowledge of Christ can that be which does not see him as most worthy of entire obedience, and what honesty is there in a disobedient profession? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Whoever keeps his word shows the love of God perfected in him, growing toward its full maturity, and hereby we know we are in him. JFB traces a gradation here, from knowing him, to being in him, to abiding in him, as love and knowledge grow together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Claiming to abide in him obliges a person to walk as he walked, with deeds consistent with words. JFB stresses that the model is Christ's ordinary walk on earth, above all in love, rather than his wonders, and Henry adds that such obedience, if universal, would make earth resemble heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The commandment is no new invention: it is the old word of love heard from the beginning, when the readers first heard the gospel preached. Henry notes it had been in force from the beginning of the world, though it comes to Christians with new motives, rules, and obligations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The same commandment is new because Christianity first promulgated love on its highest motive, loving as Christ loved us, and because it is now being realised in believers. The darkness is passing away and the true light already shines, so the command is new in experience as well as in promulgation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Whoever claims to be in the light while hating his brother is in darkness even until now, for there is no middle ground between light and darkness, love and hatred. JFB notes that where spiritual life exists, however weak, love supplants hatred, and where love is absent hatred prevails. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Loving one's brother is abiding in the light, and in such a person there is no occasion of stumbling. Gospel light not only illumines the understanding but warms the heart into love, giving an unimpeded path, whereas hatred brings both blindness and stumbling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Hatred keeps a person in darkness and walking in darkness, not knowing where he goes, because darkness has blinded his eyes. Henry warns that where spiritual darkness dwells, judgment and conscience are darkened and mistake the way to heavenly life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. John addresses all his readers as little children in the sense of dear sons and daughters, and assures them their sins are forgiven for his name's sake. JFB treats this forgiveness as the universal first privilege of all God's children, shared alike by every stage of growth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Fathers are addressed for their settled knowledge of him who is from the beginning, young men for having overcome the wicked one, and little children for knowing the Father. Henry observes that even the longest-standing Christians still need advice and instruction, and none are too old to learn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The address is repeated with a difference: young men are strong because the word of God abides in them, and by that word they have overcome the wicked one. Henry makes it their glory to be strong in Christ and his grace, breaking off bad habits and connections and entering the strait gate of conversion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Love for the world and love for the Father cannot grow together: the more world-love prevails, the more Father-love decays. Henry allows that worldly things may be used for God's purposes and to his glory, but not sought or valued for the ends to which sin abuses them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. All that is in the world is summed up in three ruling inclinations of depraved nature: the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life. Henry glosses these as sensual appetite, delight in riches and possessions, and craving for grandeur, honour, and applause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The world passes away, and its lust with it, but the one who does the will of God abides forever. Henry contrasts fading vanities, whose desire itself will soon fail, with holy affection, which is not like the lust that passes away, and with the love of God, which shall never fail. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. It is the last time, and the presence of many antichrists, as foretold, is itself the sign of it. Henry treats every denier of Christ's person or offices as an antichrist, and the prophecy of seducers rising in the Christian world as a guard meant to keep believers from being seduced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Those who went out from the church were not of it, for had they been, they would have continued; their departure made manifest that they were not all of the body. Henry notes the church does not always know its true members, and that such separations prove true Christians and render them more watchful and humble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. True Christians have an unction from the Holy One, an anointing with grace, gifts, and spiritual privileges by the Holy Spirit, and so know all things needful to the truth. Henry insists this unction alone keeps believers from delusions, especially the falsehoods spread about the person of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. John has not written because his readers do not know the truth, but because they do know it, and no lie is of the truth. Henry's counsel follows from this: judge favourably all who trust in Christ as the divine Saviour and obey his word, while pitying and praying for deniers of his Godhead and atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The liar, in the pointed sense, is the one who denies that Jesus is the Christ, and such a one is antichrist, denying the Father and the Son together. Henry warns that the most hurtful lies the father of lies spreads are falsehoods about the person of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Whoever denies the Son does not have the Father, while whoever acknowledges the Son has the Father also. Denying the Son forfeits all part in the Father's favour while the great salvation is rejected, since the Father is known only in and through the Son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Let what was heard from the beginning abide in you, and if it remains, you will continue in the Son and in the Father. Henry calls the truth of Christ abiding in us the means that severs us from sin and unites us to the Son of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The promise attached to abiding in the original gospel is eternal life itself, a promise suited to God's own greatness, power, and goodness. Henry urges readers to prize gospel truth accordingly, since thereby the promise of eternal life is made sure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. These things are written concerning those who would seduce the readers, naming the danger plainly rather than leaving it vague. The warning belongs with the encouragement that follows: the readers already possess, in the anointing, the safeguard against being led astray. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The anointing received from Christ abides in believers and teaches them all things, being truth and no lie, so that they need no seducing teacher to replace it. Henry says the Spirit of truth teaches all things necessary to the knowledge of God in Christ in this dispensation, and will not lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Abide in him, so that when he appears we may have confidence and not be ashamed before him at his coming. Henry repeats the affectionate address, little children, and notes that gospel privileges oblige gospel duties, and that constancy in religion in trying times shows birth from above. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. If we know that he is righteous, we know that everyone who does righteousness is born of him. Henry's closing test for the chapter is the same: only those born of God bear his holy image and walk in his most righteous ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
