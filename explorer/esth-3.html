The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Esther 3 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–10; King James Version, Apocrypha (the Rest of Esther) for the Greek additions)

1 After these things did king Ahasuerus promote Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, and advanced him, and set his seat above all the princes that were with him.

2 And all the king’s servants, that were in the king’s gate, bowed, and reverenced Haman: for the king had so commanded concerning him. But Mordecai bowed not, nor did him reverence.

3 Then the king’s servants, which were in the king’s gate, said unto Mordecai, Why transgressest thou the king’s commandment?

4 Now it came to pass, when they spake daily unto him, and he hearkened not unto them, that they told Haman, to see whether Mordecai’s matters would stand: for he had told them that he was a Jew.

5 And when Haman saw that Mordecai bowed not, nor did him reverence, then was Haman full of wrath.

6 And he thought scorn to lay hands on Mordecai alone; for they had shewed him the people of Mordecai: wherefore Haman sought to destroy all the Jews that were throughout the whole kingdom of Ahasuerus, even the people of Mordecai.

7 In the first month, that is, the month Nisan, in the twelfth year of king Ahasuerus, they cast Pur, that is, the lot, before Haman from day to day, and from month to month, to the twelfth month, that is, the month Adar.

8 And Haman said unto king Ahasuerus, There is a certain people scattered abroad and dispersed among the people in all the provinces of thy kingdom; and their laws are diverse from all people; neither keep they the king’s laws: therefore it is not for the king’s profit to suffer them.

9 If it please the king, let it be written that they may be destroyed: and I will pay ten thousand talents of silver to the hands of those that have the charge of the business, to bring it into the king’s treasuries.

10 And the king took his ring from his hand, and gave it unto Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the Jews’ enemy.

11 And the king said unto Haman, The silver is given to thee, the people also, to do with them as it seemeth good to thee.

12 Then were the king’s scribes called on the thirteenth day of the first month, and there was written according to all that Haman had commanded unto the king’s lieutenants, and to the governors that were over every province, and to the rulers of every people of every province according to the writing thereof, and to every people after their language; in the name of king Ahasuerus was it written, and sealed with the king’s ring.

13 And the letters were sent by posts into all the king’s provinces, to destroy, to kill, and to cause to perish, all Jews, both young and old, little children and women, in one day, even upon the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is the month Adar, and to take the spoil of them for a prey.

14 The copy of the writing for a commandment to be given in every province was published unto all people, that they should be ready against that day.

15 The posts went out, being hastened by the king’s commandment, and the decree was given in Shushan the palace. And the king and Haman sat down to drink; but the city Shushan was perplexed.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Haman the Agagite is promoted above all the princes, and when Mordecai refuses to bow to him or show him reverence, Haman resolves to destroy not Mordecai alone but all the Jews throughout the empire. He casts Pur, the lot, to fix the day, offers the king ten thousand talents of silver, and with the king's ring seals letters ordering the destruction of all Jews, young and old, little children and women, on the thirteenth day of the month Adar. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After these things did king Ahasuerus promote Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, and advanced him, and set his seat above all the princes with him. JFB notes the Agagite descends from Agag, Saul's enemy, so the old Amalekite enmity rises again in a new courtier. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And all the king's servants in the king's gate bowed and reverenced Haman: for the king had so commanded concerning him. But Mordecai bowed not, nor did him reverence. Clarke notes the refusal was not mere civil respect, which Mordecai could not refuse, but the prostration that implied divine adoration, which the Persian kings received and the Jews reserved for God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke
  • Verse 3. Then the king's servants in the king's gate said unto Mordecai, Why transgressest thou the king's commandment? The daily question presses conformity, as if the king's command settled the conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Now when they spake daily unto him, and he hearkened not unto them, that they told Haman, to see whether Mordecai's matters would stand: for he had told them that he was a Jew. Henry notes Mordecai's stated reason is his Jewishness: his religion, not his pride, forbids the bow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. And when Haman saw that Mordecai bowed not, nor did him reverence, then was Haman full of wrath. JFB notes one man's refusal unmans a favourite who commands an empire's bows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And he thought scorn to lay hands on Mordecai alone; for they had showed him the people of Mordecai: wherefore Haman sought to destroy all the Jews throughout the whole kingdom of Ahasuerus, even the people of Mordecai. Henry notes the leap from one man to a whole people is the mark of Haman's pride: private wrath becomes genocide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. In the first month, the month Nisan, in the twelfth year of Ahasuerus, they cast Pur, that is, the lot, before Haman from day to day, and from month to month, to the twelfth month, the month Adar. JFB notes the lot is cast to choose the killing day, and providence lets it fall almost a year away, giving time for deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. And Haman said unto the king, There is a certain people scattered abroad and dispersed among the people in all the provinces; and their laws are diverse from all people; neither keep they the king's laws: therefore it is not for the king's profit to suffer them. Henry notes the charge mixes truth and lie: the Jews' laws were diverse, but they did keep the king's laws, and their dispersion is turned into a threat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. If it please the king, let it be written that they may be destroyed: and I will pay ten thousand talents of silver to the hands of those that have charge of the business, to bring it into the king's treasuries. JFB notes Haman offers to buy the massacre, pricing a people at ten thousand talents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. And the king took his ring from his hand, and gave it unto Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the Jews' enemy. Henry notes the ring is the empire's seal: with it, Haman's will becomes the king's law, and the narrator already names him the Jews' enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And the king said unto Haman, The silver is given to thee, the people also, to do with them as it seems good to thee. JFB notes the king gives away both the bribe and the people, signing a death warrant he has not read with care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Then were the king's scribes called on the thirteenth day of the first month, and there was written according to all that Haman had commanded unto the lieutenants, governors and rulers of every province, each in its own writing and language; in Ahasuerus's name was it written, and sealed with the king's ring. Henry notes the empire's scribes and posts now serve murder, in every language, under the king's own seal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. And the letters were sent by posts into all the provinces, to destroy, to kill, and to cause to perish, all Jews, both young and old, little children and women, in one day, even upon the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, Adar, and to take the spoil of them for a prey. JFB notes the decree spares no age or sex, and promises the plunder as the murderers' pay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The copy of the writing for a commandment to be given in every province was published unto all people, that they should be ready against that day. The publication gives the enemies months to prepare, and the Jews months to dread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The posts went out, hastened by the king's commandment, and the decree was given in Shushan the palace. And the king and Haman sat down to drink; but the city Shushan was perplexed. Henry notes the closing contrast: the authors of the decree drink, while the city that must live under it is perplexed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Pur, that is, the lot. The chapter itself explains the word: lots cast before Haman day by day and month by month to fix the day of destruction. Documented Chapter text, verse 7
  • Agagite. Haman is called the son of Hammedatha the Agagite; the study note, following JFB, traces the name to Agag, Saul's enemy, so an old enmity rises again in a new courtier. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • The king's ring. The king's seal of authority: the king takes his ring from his hand and gives it to Haman, and letters written and sealed with it carry the king's name. Documented Chapter text, verses 10, 12
