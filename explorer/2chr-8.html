The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass at the end of twenty years, wherein Solomon had built the house of the LORD, and his own house,

2 That the cities which Huram had restored to Solomon, Solomon built them, and caused the children of Israel to dwell there.

3 And Solomon went to Hamathzobah, and prevailed against it.

4 And he built Tadmor in the wilderness, and all the store cities, which he built in Hamath.

5 Also he built Bethhoron the upper, and Bethhoron the nether, fenced cities, with walls, gates, and bars;

6 And Baalath, and all the store cities that Solomon had, and all the chariot cities, and the cities of the horsemen, and all that Solomon desired to build in Jerusalem, and in Lebanon, and throughout all the land of his dominion.

7 As for all the people that were left of the Hittites, and the Amorites, and the Perizzites, and the Hivites, and the Jebusites, which were not of Israel,

8 But of their children, who were left after them in the land, whom the children of Israel consumed not, them did Solomon make to pay tribute until this day.

9 But of the children of Israel did Solomon make no servants for his work; but they were men of war, and chief of his captains, and captains of his chariots and horsemen.

10 And these were the chief of king Solomon’s officers, even two hundred and fifty, that bare rule over the people.

11 And Solomon brought up the daughter of Pharaoh out of the city of David unto the house that he had built for her: for he said, My wife shall not dwell in the house of David king of Israel, because the places are holy, whereunto the ark of the LORD hath come.

12 Then Solomon offered burnt offerings unto the LORD on the altar of the LORD, which he had built before the porch,

13 Even after a certain rate every day, offering according to the commandment of Moses, on the sabbaths, and on the new moons, and on the solemn feasts, three times in the year, even in the feast of unleavened bread, and in the feast of weeks, and in the feast of tabernacles.

14 And he appointed, according to the order of David his father, the courses of the priests to their service, and the Levites to their charges, to praise and minister before the priests, as the duty of every day required: the porters also by their courses at every gate: for so had David the man of God commanded.

15 And they departed not from the commandment of the king unto the priests and Levites concerning any matter, or concerning the treasures.

16 Now all the work of Solomon was prepared unto the day of the foundation of the house of the LORD, and until it was finished. So the house of the LORD was perfected.

17 Then went Solomon to Eziongeber, and to Eloth, at the sea side in the land of Edom.

18 And Huram sent him by the hands of his servants ships, and servants that had knowledge of the sea; and they went with the servants of Solomon to Ophir, and took thence four hundred and fifty talents of gold, and brought them to king Solomon.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter, set after twenty years of building, tells Solomon's cities and fortifications, the labour imposed on the remaining Canaanites, his offerings, and the ships sent to Ophir with Huram's servants. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And it came to pass at the end of twenty years, wherein Solomon had built the house of the Lord and his own house. Henry notes Solomon spent his days not in contemplation but in action, in his country building cities, and having begun with God's house and made quick work of it, he prospered in all his undertakings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. That the cities which Huram had restored to Solomon, Solomon built them, and caused Israel to dwell there. Henry's building account applies: Solomon, a man of business who did not consult his own ease, built and settled cities in a time of peace, preparing for war as in summer one provides food for winter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And Solomon went to Hamathzobah, and prevailed against it. Henry groups this with Solomon's building and fortifying work, the action of a king securing his dominion while at peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And he built Tadmor in the wilderness, and all the store cities which he built in Hamath. Henry notes Solomon built all he desired to build, and divine providence gratified even his humour, giving success not only in what he needed but in what he had a mind to build. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Also he built Bethhoron the upper and Bethhoron the nether, fenced cities, with walls, gates and bars. Henry notes Solomon fortified cities in peace against war, a man's business as much as providing food in summer for winter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And Baalath, and all the store cities, and all the chariot cities, and the cities of the horsemen, and all that Solomon desired to build in Jerusalem, in Lebanon, and throughout his dominion. Henry notes Solomon knew how to set bounds to his desires: he finished all he desired and then desired no more, unlike those who enlarge desires endlessly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. As for all the people left of the Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites, which were not of Israel. Henry notes there were a great many strangers in Israel, remnants of the Canaanites, welcome to live there but not to live and do nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. But of their children, left after them in the land, whom Israel consumed not, them did Solomon make to pay tribute until this day. Henry notes it is a state's interest to promote industry and keep subjects from idleness, and these strangers were set to work and tribute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. But of the children of Israel did Solomon make no servants for his work; but they were men of war, and chief of his captains, and captains of his chariots and horsemen. Henry's contrast applies: Israelites were not made servants for the work, but served as warriors and captains, while strangers supplied the labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And these were the chief of King Solomon's officers, even two hundred and fifty, that bare rule over the people. Henry notes Solomon, a man of business himself, employed a great many hands and kept abundance of people to work, under officers ruling over them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And Solomon brought up the daughter of Pharaoh out of the city of David unto the house he had built for her, for he said, My wife shall not dwell in the house of David, because the places are holy, whereunto the ark of the Lord hath come. Henry notes Solomon consulted his father's memory: the place where the ark had been so long and publicly attended was too venerable for the gaiety, not to say iniquity, of Pharaoh's daughter's court, and the ancient landmarks between sacred and common ought to be kept up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Then Solomon offered burnt offerings unto the Lord on the altar he had built before the porch. Henry notes the building of the temple was in order to its service: in vain had the altar been built and fire come down, if sacrifices had not been constantly brought as food of the altar and fuel of that fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Even after a certain rate every day, offering according to Moses' commandment, on sabbaths, new moons, and solemn feasts three times in the year, unleavened bread, weeks, and tabernacles. Henry notes daily sacrifices as duly as the day came, weekly double on the sabbath, monthly on new moons, and yearly at the three feasts, a settled method of devotion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And he appointed, according to David his father's order, the courses of the priests to their service, and Levites to their charges, to praise and minister before the priests, as every day required, and porters by courses at every gate, for so had David the man of God commanded. Henry notes Solomon, though builder of the temple, did not amend or add to what David the man of God commanded, but closely adhered to it and used his authority to have it duly observed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And they departed not from the commandment of the king unto the priests and Levites concerning any matter, or concerning the treasures. Henry notes Solomon observed God's laws, and then all obeyed his orders, none departing from the king's commandment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Now all the work of Solomon was prepared unto the day of the foundation of the house of the Lord, and until it was finished. So the house of the Lord was perfected. Henry notes the work was the main matter, not the place: the temple was unfinished till the service was put into good order, and then the house was perfected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Then went Solomon to Eziongeber and to Eloth, at the seaside in the land of Edom. Henry notes Solomon visited the seaport towns in person, for those who deal much in the world will find it their interest to inspect their affairs themselves and see with their own eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And Huram sent him by his servants ships, and servants that had knowledge of the sea, and they went with Solomon's servants to Ophir, and took thence four hundred and fifty talents of gold, and brought them to King Solomon. Henry notes Canaan, though rich, must send to Ophir for gold, and Israel, though wise, must be beholden to Tyre for seamen, teaching that grace, not gold, is the best riches, and acquaintance with God and his law the best knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
