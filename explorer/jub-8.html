The Text — Jubilees 8 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 In the twenty-ninth jubilee, in the first week, in the beginning thereof Arpachshad took to himself a wife and her name was Rasu'eja [the daughter of Susan,] the daughter of Elam, and she bare him a son in the third year in this week, and he called his name Kainam.

2 And the son grew, and his father taught

3 . 1 i. e. the priests.

4 Later Judaism directed that the rest of the fruit should be eaten by the owners within the walls of Jerusalem.

5 The view of the text is supported by the Samaritans, the Karaite Jews and Ibn Ezra.

6 him writing, and he went to seek for himself a place where he might seize for himself a city.

7 And he found a writing which former (generations) had carved on the rock, and he read what was thereon, and he transcribed it and sinned owing to it ; for it contained the teaching of the Watchers in accordance with which they used to observe the omens of the sun and moon and stars in all the signs of heaven.

8 And he wrote it down and said nothing regarding it ; for he was afraid to speak to Noah about it lest he should be angry with him on account of it.

9 And in the thirtieth jubilee, in the second week, in the first year thereof, he took to himself a wife, and her name was Melka the daughter of Madai, the son of Japheth, and in the fourth year he begat a son, and called his name Shelah ; for he said : " Truly I have been sent." 6.

10 [And in the fourth year he was born], and Shelah grew up and took to himself a wife, and her name was Mu'ak, the daughter of Kesed, his father's brother, in the one and thirtieth jubilee, in the fifth week, in the first year thereof.

11 And she bare him a son in the fifth year thereof, and he called his name Eber : and he took unto himself a wife, and her name was 'Azurad the daughter of Nebrod, in the thirty-second Jubilee, in the seventh week, in the third year thereof.

12 And in the sixth year thereof, she bare him a son, and he called his name Peleg; for in the days when he was born the children of Noah began to divide the earth amongst themselves : for this reason he called his name Peleg.

13 And they divided (it) secretly amongst themselves,

14 and told it to Noah.

15 And it came to pass in the beginning of the thirty-third jubilee that they divided the earth into three parts, for Shem and Ham and Japheth, according to the inheritance of each, in the first year in the first week, when one of us who had been sent, was with them.

16 And he called his sons, and they drew nigh to him, they and their children, and he divided the earth into the lots, which his three sons were to take in possession, and they reached forth their hands, and took the writing out of the bosom of Noah, their father.

17 And there came forth on the writing as Shem's lot the middle of the earth which he should take as an inheritance for himself and for his sons for the generations of eternity, from the middle of the mountain range of Rafa from the mouth of the water from the river Tina and his portion goeth towards the west through the midst of this river, and it extendeth till it reacheth the water of the abysses, out of which this river goeth forth and poureth its waters into the sea Me'at and this river floweth into the great sea.

18 And all that is towards the north is Japheth's, and all that is towards the south belongeth to Shem.

19 And it extendeth till it reacheth Karaso: this is in the bosom of the tongue which looketh towards the south. 14.

20 And his portion extendeth along the great sea, and it extendeth in a straight line till it reacheth the west of the tongue which looketh towards the south ; for this sea is named the tongue of the Egyptian Sea.

21 And it turneth from here towards the south towards the mouth of the great sea on the shore of (its) waters, and it extendeth to the west to 'Afra, and it extendeth till it reacheth the waters of the river Gihon, and to the south of the waters of Gihon, to the banks of this river.

22 And it extendeth towards the east, till it reacheth the Garden of Eden, to the south thereof, [to the south] and from the east of the whole land of Eden and of the whole east, it turneth to the east, and proceedeth till it reacheth the east of the mountain named Rafa, and it descendeth to the bank of the mouth of the river Tina.

23 This portion came forth by lot for Shem and his sons, that they should possess it for ever unto his generations for evermore.

24 And Noah rejoiced that this portion came forth for Shem

25 and for his sons,

26 and he remembered all that he had spoken with his mouth in prophecy; for he had said:

27 " Blessed be the Lord God of Shem, And may the Lord dwell in the dwelling of Shem."

28 And he knew that the Garden of Eden is the holy of holies, and the dwelling of the Lord, and Mount Sinai the centre of the desert, and Mount Zion — the centre of the navel of the earth : these three were created as holy places facing each other.

29 And he blessed the God of gods, who had put the word of the Lord into his mouth, and the Lord for evermore.

30 And he knew that a blessed portion

31 and a blessing had come to Shem and his sons unto the generations for ever — the whole land of Eden and the whole land of the Red Sea, and the whole land of the east, and India, and on the Red Sea and the mountains thereof, and all the land of Bashan, and all the land of Lebanon and the islands of Kaftur and all the mountains of Sanir and 'Amana and the mountains of Asshur in the north, and all the land of Elam, Asshur, and Babel, and Susan and Ma'edai and all the mountains of Ararat, and all the region beyond the sea, which is beyond the mountains of Asshur towards the north, a blessed and spacious land, and all that is in it is very good. 22.

32 And for Ham came forth the second portion, beyond the Gihon towards the south to the right of the Garden, and it extendeth towards the south and it extendeth to all the mountains of fire and it extendeth towards the west to the sea of 'Atel and it extendeth towards the west till it reacheth the sea of Ma'uk — that (sea) into which everything which is not destroyed descendeth.

33 And it goeth forth towards the north to the limits of Gadir, and it goeth forth to the coast of the waters of the sea to the waters of the great sea till it draweth near to the river Gihon, and goeth along the river Gihon till it reacheth the right of the Garden of Eden.

34 And this is the land which came forth for Ham as the portion which he was to occupy for ever for himself and his sons unto their

35 Crete.

37 22-24 give details of Ham's portion, which includes all Africa and certain parts of Asia.

43 And for Japheth came forth the third portion beyond the river Tina to the north of the outflow of its waters, and it extendeth north-easterly to the whole region of Gog and to all the country east thereof.

44 And it extendeth northerly to the north, and it extendeth to the mountains of Qelt towards the north, and towards the sea of Ma'uk, and it goeth forth to the east of Gadir as far as the region of the waters of the sea.

45 And it extendeth until it approacheth the west of Fara and it returneth towards 'Aferag and it extendeth easterly to the waters of the sea of Me'at.

46 And it extendeth to the region of the river Tina in a northeasterly direction until it approacheth the boundary of its waters towards the mountain Rafa and it turneth round towards the north. 29.

47 This is the land which came forth for Japheth and his sons as the portion of his inheritance which he should possess for himself and his sons, for their generations for ever; five great islands, and a great land in the north.

48 But it is cold, and the land of Ham is hot, and the land of Shem is neither hot nor cold, but it is of blended cold and heat.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

In plain language: In the twenty-ninth jubilee, in the first week, in the beginning thereof, Arpachshad took Rasu'eja, daughter of Susan, daughter of Elam, to wife, and she bare him a son in the third year in this week, and he called his name Kainam. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the twenty-ninth jubilee, in the first week, in the beginning thereof, Arpachshad took Rasu'eja, daughter of Susan, daughter of Elam, to wife, and she bare him a son in the third year in this week, and he called his name Kainam. The Shemite line continues through Arpachshad, married into Elam, with Kainam's birth dated to week and year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 2. This verse opens Kainam's upbringing: the son grew, and his father taught him writing. The sentence is completed by verse 6, where the taught youth goes seeking a city to seize. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 6. He went to seek for himself a place where he might seize for himself a city. The youth taught writing turns to seizure: literacy and ambition arrive together in Kainam. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. He found a writing which former generations had carved on the rock, and he read what was thereon, and he transcribed it and sinned owing to it, for it contained the teaching of the Watchers, in accordance with which they used to observe the omens of the sun, moon and stars in all the signs of heaven. Kainam's sin is the recovery of forbidden Watcher teaching, astrology by sun, moon and stars, in contrast to Enoch's licit heavenly writing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. He wrote it down and said nothing regarding it, for he was afraid to speak to Noah about it lest Noah should be angry with him on account of it. Concealment betrays knowledge of guilt: the transcription is hidden from the patriarch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. In the thirtieth jubilee, in the second week, in the first year thereof, he took Melka, daughter of Madai, son of Japheth, to wife, and in the fourth year he begat a son and called his name Shelah, saying, 'Truly I have been sent.' Shelah's name, 'sent,' is explained by his father's own words, and the marriage crosses into Japheth's line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. In the fourth year he was born, and Shelah grew up and took Mu'ak, daughter of Kesed, his father's brother, in the thirty-first jubilee, in the fifth week, in the first year thereof. The bracketed birth year and the dated marriage carry the line from Kainam to Shelah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. She bare him a son in the fifth year thereof, and he called his name Eber, and he took unto himself a wife, Azurad, daughter of Nebrod, in the thirty-second jubilee, in the seventh week, in the third year thereof. Eber's birth and marriage continue the dated genealogy toward Peleg. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. In the sixth year thereof she bare him a son, and he called his name Peleg, for in the days when he was born the children of Noah began to divide the earth amongst themselves, and for this reason he called his name Peleg. JFB on Genesis 10:25 notes Peleg's name was given in memory of the division, made by divine instruction probably through Eber, and cites Deuteronomy 32:8 and Acts 17:26 for God apportioning the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They divided the earth secretly amongst themselves. A first, secret division precedes the public, lot-cast division under Noah in verse 15. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. They told it to Noah. The secret division is disclosed to the patriarch, who will then oversee the division by lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. In the beginning of the thirty-third jubilee they divided the earth into three parts, for Shem, Ham and Japheth, according to the inheritance of each, in the first year in the first week, when one of the angels who had been sent was with them. JFB on Genesis 10:32 notes the division was made in the most orderly manner, the sons ranged by nations, every nation assigned its territory; Jubilees dates the formal division and sets an angel as witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He called his sons, and they drew nigh to him, they and their children, and he divided the earth into the lots which his three sons were to take in possession, and they reached forth their hands and took the writing out of the bosom of Noah, their father. The lots are drawn as a writing from Noah's bosom, so the division is received, not seized. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. There came forth on the writing as Shem's lot the middle of the earth, which he should take as an inheritance for himself and his sons for the generations of eternity, from the middle of the mountain range of Rafa, from the mouth of the water from the river Tina, his portion going west through the midst of this river until it reaches the water of the abysses, out of which this river goes forth and pours its waters into the sea Me'at, and this river flows into the great sea. Shem receives the middle earth, bounded by the Rafa range and the Tina river system running to the great sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. All that is towards the north is Japheth's, and all that is towards the south belongs to Shem. The verse fixes the orientation of Shem's middle lot: Japheth to the north, the south to Shem's side of the division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. His portion extends till it reaches Karaso, in the bosom of the tongue which looks towards the south. Karaso on the southward tongue marks a boundary point of Shem's lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. His portion extends along the great sea, in a straight line till it reaches the west of the tongue which looks towards the south, for this sea is named the tongue of the Egyptian Sea. The western boundary runs along the great sea to the tongue of the Egyptian Sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. It turns from here towards the south, towards the mouth of the great sea on the shore of its waters, and extends west to 'Afra, and till it reaches the waters of the river Gihon, and to the south of the waters of Gihon, to the banks of this river. The southern boundary runs by 'Afra to the Gihon river's banks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. It extends towards the east till it reaches the Garden of Eden, to the south thereof, and from the east of the whole land of Eden and of the whole east, it turns to the east and proceeds till it reaches the east of the mountain named Rafa, and descends to the bank of the mouth of the river Tina. The eastern boundary returns by Eden to Rafa and the Tina's mouth, closing Shem's circuit where it began. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. This portion came forth by lot for Shem and his sons, that they should possess it forever unto his generations forevermore. The lot makes Shem's portion a perpetual inheritance, not a temporary camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. This verse opens Noah's joy: he rejoiced that this portion came forth for Shem and his sons. The sentence is completed by the prophecy remembered in the next verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. This verse continues Noah's rejoicing over Shem's portion and his sons. The joy is explained by the remembered prophecy that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. He remembered all that he had spoken with his mouth in prophecy, for he had said, 'Blessed be the Lord God of Shem, and may the Lord dwell in the dwelling of Shem.' The lot fulfils Noah's blessing of chapter 7: Shem's middle portion is the dwelling where the Lord will dwell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. This verse gives the prophecy itself, quoted from chapter 7: blessed be the Lord God of Shem, and may the Lord dwell in Shem's dwelling. The blessing is now seen fulfilled in the lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. He knew that the Garden of Eden is the holy of holies and the dwelling of the Lord, and Mount Sinai the centre of the desert, and Mount Zion the centre of the navel of the earth, and these three were created as holy places facing each other. Eden, Sinai and Zion form a triad of holy places, centres of garden, desert and earth, facing one another; chapter 4 adds the Mount of the East as a fourth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. He blessed the God of gods, who had put the word of the Lord into his mouth, and the Lord forevermore. Noah blesses God for the prophetic word now fulfilled in the lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. This verse opens the catalogue: he knew that a blessed portion and a blessing had come to Shem and his sons unto the generations forever. The sentence is completed by the lands list of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. The blessed portion is the whole land of Eden and the whole land of the Red Sea, the whole land of the east, India, the Red Sea and its mountains, all the land of Bashan, Lebanon, the islands of Kaftur, the mountains of Sanir and 'Amana, the mountains of Asshur in the north, all the land of Elam, Asshur, Babel, Susan and Ma'edai, all the mountains of Ararat, and all the region beyond the sea beyond the mountains of Asshur towards the north, a blessed and spacious land, all in it very good. Shem's catalogue runs from Eden and the Red Sea east to India, and north through Bashan, Lebanon, Elam, Asshur, Babel, Susan, Media and Ararat to the beyond-sea north, a spacious land pronounced very good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. For Ham came forth the second portion, beyond the Gihon towards the south to the right of the Garden, extending south to all the mountains of fire, west to the sea of 'Atel, and west till it reaches the sea of Ma'uk, that sea into which everything which is not destroyed descends. Ham receives the south beyond the Gihon, to the mountains of fire and the western seas; Charles's notes gloss the sea of Ma'uk as the great ocean stream in the extreme west. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. It goes forth towards the north to the limits of Gadir, and to the coast of the waters of the sea, to the waters of the great sea, till it draws near to the river Gihon, and goes along the river Gihon till it reaches the right of the Garden of Eden. Ham's boundary returns north by Gadir, which Charles's note identifies with Cadiz, along the great sea to the Gihon and Eden's right side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. This verse introduces Ham's portion as the land which came forth for him to occupy forever for himself and his sons unto their generations. The sentence is completed by verse 42's 'forever.' Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. This verse completes Ham's portion: for himself and his sons unto their generations forever. Like Shem's, Ham's lot is a perpetual inheritance, and Charles's summary notes it covers all Africa and certain parts of Asia. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. For Japheth came forth the third portion beyond the river Tina to the north of the outflow of its waters, extending north-easterly to the whole region of Gog and to all the country east thereof. Japheth receives the north beyond the Tina, toward Gog's region and the eastern country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. It extends northerly to the north, to the mountains of Qelt towards the north, towards the sea of Ma'uk, and goes forth to the east of Gadir as far as the region of the waters of the sea. Japheth's boundary runs by the Qelt mountains and the Ma'uk sea to the east of Gadir, Cadiz's region. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. It extends until it approaches the west of Fara, and returns towards 'Aferag, and extends easterly to the waters of the sea of Me'at. The circuit turns at Fara and 'Aferag back east to the Me'at sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. It extends to the region of the river Tina in a north-easterly direction until it approaches the boundary of its waters towards the mountain Rafa, and turns round towards the north. The circuit closes at the Tina and Rafa, mirroring Shem's starting point and turning north. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. This is the land which came forth for Japheth and his sons as the portion of his inheritance which he should possess for himself and his sons, for their generations forever: five great islands, and a great land in the north. Japheth's perpetual portion is five great islands and a great northern land, matching JFB's isles of the Gentiles for Japheth's settlements. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. But it is cold, and the land of Ham is hot, and the land of Shem is neither hot nor cold, but of blended cold and heat. Climate matches portion: Japheth's north is cold, Ham's south hot, and Shem's middle temperate, blended cold and heat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 23. This portion came forth by lot for Shem and his sons, that they should possess it forever unto his generations forevermore. The lot makes Shem's portion a perpetual inheritance, not a temporary camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. This verse opens Noah's joy: he rejoiced that this portion came forth for Shem and his sons. The sentence is completed by the prophecy remembered in the next verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. This verse continues Noah's rejoicing over Shem's portion and his sons. The joy is explained by the remembered prophecy that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. He remembered all that he had spoken with his mouth in prophecy, for he had said, 'Blessed be the Lord God of Shem, and may the Lord dwell in the dwelling of Shem.' The lot fulfils Noah's blessing of chapter 7: Shem's middle portion is the dwelling where the Lord will dwell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. This verse gives the prophecy itself, quoted from chapter 7: blessed be the Lord God of Shem, and may the Lord dwell in Shem's dwelling. The blessing is now seen fulfilled in the lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. He knew that the Garden of Eden is the holy of holies and the dwelling of the Lord, and Mount Sinai the centre of the desert, and Mount Zion the centre of the navel of the earth, and these three were created as holy places facing each other. Eden, Sinai and Zion form a triad of holy places, centres of garden, desert and earth, facing one another; chapter 4 adds the Mount of the East as a fourth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. He blessed the God of gods, who had put the word of the Lord into his mouth, and the Lord forevermore. Noah blesses God for the prophetic word now fulfilled in the lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. This verse opens the catalogue: he knew that a blessed portion and a blessing had come to Shem and his sons unto the generations forever. The sentence is completed by the lands list of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. The blessed portion is the whole land of Eden and the whole land of the Red Sea, the whole land of the east, India, the Red Sea and its mountains, all the land of Bashan, Lebanon, the islands of Kaftur, the mountains of Sanir and 'Amana, the mountains of Asshur in the north, all the land of Elam, Asshur, Babel, Susan and Ma'edai, all the mountains of Ararat, and all the region beyond the sea beyond the mountains of Asshur towards the north, a blessed and spacious land, all in it very good. Shem's catalogue runs from Eden and the Red Sea east to India, and north through Bashan, Lebanon, Elam, Asshur, Babel, Susan, Media and Ararat to the beyond-sea north, a spacious land pronounced very good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. For Ham came forth the second portion, beyond the Gihon towards the south to the right of the Garden, extending south to all the mountains of fire, west to the sea of 'Atel, and west till it reaches the sea of Ma'uk, that sea into which everything which is not destroyed descends. Ham receives the south beyond the Gihon, to the mountains of fire and the western seas; Charles's notes gloss the sea of Ma'uk as the great ocean stream in the extreme west. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. It goes forth towards the north to the limits of Gadir, and to the coast of the waters of the sea, to the waters of the great sea, till it draws near to the river Gihon, and goes along the river Gihon till it reaches the right of the Garden of Eden. Ham's boundary returns north by Gadir, which Charles's note identifies with Cadiz, along the great sea to the Gihon and Eden's right side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. This verse introduces Ham's portion as the land which came forth for him to occupy forever for himself and his sons unto their generations. The sentence is completed by verse 42's 'forever.' Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. This verse completes Ham's portion: for himself and his sons unto their generations forever. Like Shem's, Ham's lot is a perpetual inheritance, and Charles's summary notes it covers all Africa and certain parts of Asia. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. For Japheth came forth the third portion beyond the river Tina to the north of the outflow of its waters, extending north-easterly to the whole region of Gog and to all the country east thereof. Japheth receives the north beyond the Tina, toward Gog's region and the eastern country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. It extends northerly to the north, to the mountains of Qelt towards the north, towards the sea of Ma'uk, and goes forth to the east of Gadir as far as the region of the waters of the sea. Japheth's boundary runs by the Qelt mountains and the Ma'uk sea to the east of Gadir, Cadiz's region. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. It extends until it approaches the west of Fara, and returns towards 'Aferag, and extends easterly to the waters of the sea of Me'at. The circuit turns at Fara and 'Aferag back east to the Me'at sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. It extends to the region of the river Tina in a north-easterly direction until it approaches the boundary of its waters towards the mountain Rafa, and turns round towards the north. The circuit closes at the Tina and Rafa, mirroring Shem's starting point and turning north. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 48. But it is cold, and the land of Ham is hot, and the land of Shem is neither hot nor cold, but of blended cold and heat. Climate matches portion: Japheth's north is cold, Ham's south hot, and Shem's middle temperate, blended cold and heat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
