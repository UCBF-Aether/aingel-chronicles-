The Text — Sirach 4 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 My son, defraud not the poor of his living, and make not the needy eyes to wait long.

2 Make not an hungry soul sorrowful; neither provoke a man in his distress.

3 Add not more trouble to an heart that is vexed; and defer not to give to him that is in need.

4 Reject not the supplication of the afflicted; neither turn away thy face from a poor man.

5 Turn not away thine eye from the needy, and give him none occasion to curse thee:

6 For if he curse thee in the bitterness of his soul, his prayer shall be heard of him that made him.

7 Get thyself the love of the congregation, and bow thy head to a great man.

8 Let it not grieve thee to bow down thine ear to the poor, and give him a friendly answer with meekness.

9 Deliver him that suffereth wrong from the hand of the oppressor; and be not fainthearted when thou sittest in judgment.

10 Be as a father unto the fatherless, and instead of an husband unto their mother: so shalt thou be as the son of the most High, and he shall love thee more than thy mother doth.

11 Wisdom exalteth her children, and layeth hold of them that seek her.

12 He that loveth her loveth life; and they that seek to her early shall be filled with joy.

13 He that holdeth her fast shall inherit glory; and wheresoever she entereth, the Lord will bless.

14 They that serve her shall minister to the Holy One: and them that love her the Lord doth love.

15 Whoso giveth ear unto her shall judge the nations: and he that attendeth unto her shall dwell securely.

16 If a man commit himself unto her, he shall inherit her; and his generation shall hold her in possession.

17 For at the first she will walk with him by crooked ways, and bring fear and dread upon him, and torment him with her discipline, until she may trust his soul, and try him by her laws.

18 Then will she return the straight way unto him, and comfort him, and shew him her secrets.

19 But if he go wrong, she will forsake him, and give him over to his own ruin.

20 Observe the opportunity, and beware of evil; and be not ashamed when it concerneth thy soul.

21 For there is a shame that bringeth sin; and there is a shame which is glory and grace.

22 Accept no person against thy soul, and let not the reverence of any man cause thee to fall.

23 And refrain not to speak, when there is occasion to do good, and hide not thy wisdom in her beauty.

24 For by speech wisdom shall be known: and learning by the word of the tongue.

25 In no wise speak against the truth; but be abashed of the error of thine ignorance.

26 Be not ashamed to confess thy sins; and force not the course of the river.

27 Make not thyself an underling to a foolish man; neither accept the person of the mighty.

28 Strive for the truth unto death, and the Lord shall fight for thee.

29 Be not hasty in thy tongue, and in thy deeds slack and remiss.

30 Be not as a lion in thy house, nor frantick among thy servants.

31 Let not thine hand be stretched out to receive, and shut when thou shouldest repay.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Justice to the poor must be prompt, not deferred: do not defraud them of their living or make needy eyes wait. Wisdom's discipline at first feels harsh, but wholehearted commitment inherits her, while the chapter also warns against grasping hands that take eagerly and refuse to repay. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Do not defraud the poor of his living, and do not make needy eyes wait long. Justice to the poor is urgent: withholding what they live on, and delaying help, are both wrongs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Do not make a hungry soul sorrowful, nor provoke a man in his distress. Hunger and distress already weigh heavily, and adding sorrow or provocation to them is cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. Do not add trouble to a vexed heart, and do not defer giving to him who is in need. A troubled heart needs relief, not more weight, and need calls for prompt giving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. Do not reject the supplication of the afflicted, nor turn your face from a poor man. Affliction's plea deserves a hearing, and turning away the face is itself a refusal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Do not turn your eye from the needy, and give him no occasion to curse you. Looking away from need invites the needy person's bitter curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. If he curses you in the bitterness of his soul, his prayer will be heard by him who made him. The Maker hears the poor man's bitter prayer, so wronging the needy is dangerous before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. Win the love of the congregation, and bow your head to a great man. A good name among the people and deference to those in authority both belong to a well-ordered life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Do not grudge bowing your ear to the poor, but give him a friendly answer with meekness. Listening to the poor is a duty, and the answer should be friendly and gentle, not grudging. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. Deliver him who suffers wrong from the oppressor's hand, and do not be fainthearted when you sit in judgment. Rescuing the wronged takes courage, especially when judging against power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Be as a father to the fatherless and instead of a husband to their mother, and you will be as a son of the Most High, loved by him more than by your mother. Fatherly care for the fatherless is the way to sonship with God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. Wisdom exalts her children and lays hold of those who seek her. Wisdom lifts those who belong to her and takes hold of seekers rather than waiting passively. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. He who loves wisdom loves life, and those who seek her early will be filled with joy. Loving wisdom is loving life itself, and seeking her early fills life with joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. He who holds her fast will inherit glory, and wherever she enters, the Lord will bless. Clinging to wisdom brings glory as an inheritance, and her presence brings God's blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. Those who serve wisdom minister to the Holy One, and those who love her are loved by the Lord. Serving wisdom is service of God, and loving her draws God's love in return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. Whoever gives ear to wisdom will judge the nations, and he who attends to her will dwell securely. Listening to wisdom qualifies a person for great judgment and gives secure dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. If a man commits himself to wisdom, he will inherit her, and his generation will hold her in possession. Wholehearted commitment makes wisdom an inheritance passed down through the family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. At first wisdom walks with a man by crooked ways, bringing fear, dread, and torment with her discipline, until she can trust his soul and try him by her laws. Wisdom's first schooling feels harsh and winding, because it tests whether the learner can be trusted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. Then she will return by the straight way to him, comfort him, and show him her secrets. Once tested and trusted, the learner finds wisdom coming straight to him with comfort and hidden knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. But if he goes wrong, she will forsake him and give him over to his own ruin. Turning aside forfeits wisdom's company, and the deserter is left to the ruin he chose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 20. Observe the opportunity, beware of evil, and do not be ashamed when your soul is concerned. Right timing matters, evil must be watched for, and shame should never silence a matter of conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 21. There is a shame that brings sin, and a shame that is glory and grace. False shame leads into sin, while the shame that owns fault before God brings honour and grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 22. Accept no person against your soul, and let no man's reverence cause you to fall. Neither favour nor fear of any person should be allowed to work against your own soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 23. Do not refrain from speaking when there is occasion to do good, and do not hide your wisdom in her beauty. Wisdom kept silent when good could be spoken is wisdom hidden, not wisdom used. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 24. By speech wisdom is known, and learning by the word of the tongue. Wisdom becomes visible in speech, and learning is proved by what the tongue can rightly say. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 25. In no way speak against the truth, but be abashed at the error of your ignorance. Truth must never be contradicted, and ignorance should bring embarrassment rather than bold speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 26. Do not be ashamed to confess your sins, and do not force the course of the river. Confession should be unashamed, and no one should fight against the natural course of things as if forcing a river. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 27. Do not make yourself an underling to a foolish man, nor accept the person of the mighty. Subservience to folly and favouritism toward power both corrupt judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 28. Strive for the truth unto death, and the Lord will fight for you. Truth is worth contending for to the end, and God himself takes up the fight of those who do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 29. Do not be hasty in your tongue, and slack and remiss in your deeds. Speech should not outrun action, nor action lag behind speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 30. Do not be as a lion in your house, nor frantic among your servants. A tyrant at home, raging over servants, is the opposite of a governed household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 31. Do not let your hand be stretched out to receive and shut when you should repay. Taking eagerly while refusing to give back in turn is a grasping, dishonest pattern. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
