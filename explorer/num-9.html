Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, in the first month of the second year after they were come out of the land of Egypt, saying,

2 Let the children of Israel also keep the passover at his appointed season.

3 In the fourteenth day of this month, at even, ye shall keep it in his appointed season: according to all the rites of it, and according to all the ceremonies thereof, shall ye keep it.

4 And Moses spake unto the children of Israel, that they should keep the passover.

5 And they kept the passover on the fourteenth day of the first month at even in the wilderness of Sinai: according to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so did the children of Israel.

6 And there were certain men, who were defiled by the dead body of a man, that they could not keep the passover on that day: and they came before Moses and before Aaron on that day:

7 And those men said unto him, We are defiled by the dead body of a man: wherefore are we kept back, that we may not offer an offering of the LORD in his appointed season among the children of Israel?

8 And Moses said unto them, Stand still, and I will hear what the LORD will command concerning you.

9 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

10 Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, If any man of you or of your posterity shall be unclean by reason of a dead body, or be in a journey afar off, yet he shall keep the passover unto the LORD.

11 The fourteenth day of the second month at even they shall keep it, and eat it with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.

12 They shall leave none of it unto the morning, nor break any bone of it: according to all the ordinances of the passover they shall keep it.

13 But the man that is clean, and is not in a journey, and forbeareth to keep the passover, even the same soul shall be cut off from among his people: because he brought not the offering of the LORD in his appointed season, that man shall bear his sin.

14 And if a stranger shall sojourn among you, and will keep the passover unto the LORD; according to the ordinance of the passover, and according to the manner thereof, so shall he do: ye shall have one ordinance, both for the stranger, and for him that was born in the land.

15 And on the day that the tabernacle was reared up the cloud covered the tabernacle, namely, the tent of the testimony: and at even there was upon the tabernacle as it were the appearance of fire, until the morning.

16 So it was alway: the cloud covered it by day, and the appearance of fire by night.

17 And when the cloud was taken up from the tabernacle, then after that the children of Israel journeyed: and in the place where the cloud abode, there the children of Israel pitched their tents.

18 At the commandment of the LORD the children of Israel journeyed, and at the commandment of the LORD they pitched: as long as the cloud abode upon the tabernacle they rested in their tents.

19 And when the cloud tarried long upon the tabernacle many days, then the children of Israel kept the charge of the LORD, and journeyed not.

20 And so it was, when the cloud was a few days upon the tabernacle; according to the commandment of the LORD they abode in their tents, and according to the commandment of the LORD they journeyed.

21 And so it was, when the cloud abode from even unto the morning, and that the cloud was taken up in the morning, then they journeyed: whether it was by day or by night that the cloud was taken up, they journeyed.

22 Or whether it were two days, or a month, or a year, that the cloud tarried upon the tabernacle, remaining thereon, the children of Israel abode in their tents, and journeyed not: but when it was taken up, they journeyed.

23 At the commandment of the LORD they rested in the tents, and at the commandment of the LORD they journeyed: they kept the charge of the LORD, at the commandment of the LORD by the hand of Moses.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the second passover and the guiding cloud. In the first month of the second year, Israel keeps the passover in the wilderness of Sinai at its appointed season. Men defiled by the dead or away on a journey, who cannot keep it then, keep it in the second month instead, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs; but a clean man at home who neglects it is cut off. The second half describes the cloud over the tabernacle: when it lifted they journeyed, when it stayed they camped, whether for days, a month, or a year, at the commandment of the LORD. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Second passover. The passover kept in the second month by those defiled by the dead or away on a journey at the appointed season, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. Interpreted Chapter text in the Scripture panel above

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the first month of the second year after the exodus, the Lord spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai. JFB notes the date places this command shortly after the tabernacle's erection, a month before the numbering of chapter 1, though it is narrated here to introduce the case that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Israel was to keep the passover at his appointed season. JFB notes this was the first observance since the exodus, kept on the exact anniversary of the departure, with the lamb and unleavened bread, though the hurried circumstances of girded loins, sandals and staff were not repeated, being incidental to the flight itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. On the fourteenth day of the first month at evening they were to keep it, according to all its rites and ceremonies. The exactness of the season mattered: the memorial was tied to the very day of deliverance, not left to convenience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Moses spoke to Israel that they should keep the passover. The command was delivered through Moses as law for the whole congregation, not as an optional remembrance for the willing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. They kept the passover on the fourteenth day of the first month at evening in the wilderness of Sinai, according to all the Lord commanded Moses. JFB adds it was probably the only observance of the feast during the forty years, since circumcision, required for eating it, was not practised in the wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Certain men were defiled by the dead body of a man and could not keep the passover on that day. JFB explains the dilemma: discharging the last offices to deceased relatives was imperative, yet funeral attendance brought seven days' exclusion, and Jewish writers say these men had carried out the bodies of Nadab and Abihu. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The defiled men came before Moses and Aaron asking why they were kept back from offering to the Lord at its appointed season. Henry observes with what trouble and concern they complained: it should be a trouble to us when by any occasion we are kept back from the solemnities of a sabbath or a sacrament. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Moses said, stand still, and I will hear what the Lord will command concerning you. Henry praises Moses' deliberation: ministers must ask counsel of God's mouth, not determine by their own fancy, and time taken to spread a difficult case before God by prayer will be directed in the good and right way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. If a man is unclean by reason of a dead body, or on a journey afar off, he shall keep the passover on the fourteenth day of the second month at evening. JFB calls this a special license by divine authority: the disqualified or distant kept it by themselves a month later, under all the same solemn formalities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They ate it with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. The second passover was no shortened rite: the same bread and herbs marked the same deliverance, only a month later. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They left none of it until the morning, nor broke any bone of it, according to all the ordinances of the passover. The unbroken bones and the nothing-left rule kept the second observance identical to the first in every particular. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But the man who is clean and not on a journey, yet forbears to keep the passover, shall be cut off from his people, because he brought not the Lord's offering in its season, and shall bear his sin. Henry contrasts the two absences: those forced to absent themselves may expect grace under affliction, but those who of choice absent themselves may expect God's wrath, for God is not mocked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. If a stranger sojourns among you and will keep the passover, he shall do so according to its ordinance, one ordinance for stranger and homeborn alike. JFB notes Gentile converts were admitted if circumcised, to the same privileges as native Israelites, and liable to excommunication if they neglected it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. On the day the tabernacle was reared, the cloud covered it, the tent of the testimony, and at evening it appeared as fire until morning. JFB describes the cloud as a visible token of God's special presence and guardian care, resting by day as a dark column and by night as fire over the ark's part of the sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. So it was always: the cloud covered it by day, and the appearance of fire by night. The sign never left them, teaching, as Henry says, that God is always near us, both night and day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. When the cloud was taken up from the tabernacle, then Israel journeyed. JFB explains the cloud rose higher so as to be conspicuous at the remotest extremities of the camp, and its rising was properly called the commandment of the Lord, since from it as a glorious throne he gave the order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. At the commandment of the Lord Israel journeyed, and at his commandment they pitched, as long as the cloud abode on the tabernacle. The cloud's motion regulated the commencement and termination of every journey, so that marching and resting were alike acts of obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. When the cloud tarried long on the tabernacle, Israel kept the charge of the Lord and journeyed not. JFB notes the Israelites had imbibed a nomad love of change and longed to hasten to Canaan, yet whatever irksomeness they felt in long encampment, they stayed while the cloud stayed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Sometimes the cloud was but a few days on the tabernacle, and then according to the Lord's command they abode, and according to his command they journeyed. Short stays and long stays alike were governed: there was no time lost while waiting God's time, as Henry puts it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Sometimes the cloud abode from evening to morning, and when it was taken up in the morning they journeyed, or by day or by night when it was taken up. The rule covered every pattern of stay, so that no hour of departure was left to human guessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Whether the cloud tarried two days, or a month, or a year, Israel abode in their tents and journeyed not, and when it was taken up they journeyed. The length of the stay made no difference to the duty: a year of waiting was obedience, just as a day's march was. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. At the commandment of the Lord they rested in their tents, and at his commandment they journeyed, keeping the charge of the Lord by the hand of Moses. Henry closes with the heart's response: at the Lord's command our hearts should always move and rest, saying, Father, thy will be done, dispose of me and mine as thou pleasest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
