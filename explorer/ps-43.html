The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 43 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Judge me, O God, and plead my cause against an ungodly nation: O deliver me from the deceitful and unjust man.

2 For thou art the God of my strength: why dost thou cast me off? why go I mourning because of the oppression of the enemy?

3 O send out thy light and thy truth: let them lead me; let them bring me unto thy holy hill, and to thy tabernacles.

4 Then will I go unto the altar of God, unto God my exceeding joy: yea, upon the harp will I praise thee, O God my God.

5 Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Judge me and plead my cause against an ungodly nation, send light and truth to lead to the holy hill and tabernacles, then to the altar of God, my exceeding joy, with harp praise, and the same refrain closes it: hope in God, for I shall yet praise him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Judge me, O God, and plead my cause against an ungodly nation; O deliver me from the deceitful and unjust man: the companion to Psalm 42 continues the plea — judgment, advocacy against an ungodly nation, and deliverance from one deceitful, unjust man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. For you are the God of my strength; why do you cast me off? Why go I mourning because of the enemy's oppression? The God of his strength is asked why he casts off, and why mourning under oppression continues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. O send out your light and your truth; let them lead me; let them bring me to your holy hill, and to your tabernacles: light and truth are prayed for as guides, to lead him home to God's hill and dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Then will I go to the altar of God, to God my exceeding joy; yea, upon the harp will I praise you, O God my God: the journey ends at the altar, where God himself is exceeding joy, praised on the harp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why disquieted within me? Hope in God, for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God: the refrain closes the pair of psalms as it closed Psalm 42 — hope, future praise, and God as health of countenance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
