The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 21 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then there was a famine in the days of David three years, year after year; and David enquired of the LORD. And the LORD answered, It is for Saul, and for his bloody house, because he slew the Gibeonites.

2 And the king called the Gibeonites, and said unto them; (now the Gibeonites were not of the children of Israel, but of the remnant of the Amorites; and the children of Israel had sworn unto them: and Saul sought to slay them in his zeal to the children of Israel and Judah.)

3 Wherefore David said unto the Gibeonites, What shall I do for you? and wherewith shall I make the atonement, that ye may bless the inheritance of the LORD?

4 And the Gibeonites said unto him, We will have no silver nor gold of Saul, nor of his house; neither for us shalt thou kill any man in Israel. And he said, What ye shall say, that will I do for you.

5 And they answered the king, The man that consumed us, and that devised against us that we should be destroyed from remaining in any of the coasts of Israel,

6 Let seven men of his sons be delivered unto us, and we will hang them up unto the LORD in Gibeah of Saul, whom the LORD did choose. And the king said, I will give them.

7 But the king spared Mephibosheth, the son of Jonathan the son of Saul, because of the LORD’s oath that was between them, between David and Jonathan the son of Saul.

8 But the king took the two sons of Rizpah the daughter of Aiah, whom she bare unto Saul, Armoni and Mephibosheth; and the five sons of Michal the daughter of Saul, whom she brought up for Adriel the son of Barzillai the Meholathite:

9 And he delivered them into the hands of the Gibeonites, and they hanged them in the hill before the LORD: and they fell all seven together, and were put to death in the days of harvest, in the first days, in the beginning of barley harvest.

10 And Rizpah the daughter of Aiah took sackcloth, and spread it for her upon the rock, from the beginning of harvest until water dropped upon them out of heaven, and suffered neither the birds of the air to rest on them by day, nor the beasts of the field by night.

11 And it was told David what Rizpah the daughter of Aiah, the concubine of Saul, had done.

12 And David went and took the bones of Saul and the bones of Jonathan his son from the men of Jabeshgilead, which had stolen them from the street of Bethshan, where the Philistines had hanged them, when the Philistines had slain Saul in Gilboa:

13 And he brought up from thence the bones of Saul and the bones of Jonathan his son; and they gathered the bones of them that were hanged.

14 And the bones of Saul and Jonathan his son buried they in the country of Benjamin in Zelah, in the sepulchre of Kish his father: and they performed all that the king commanded. And after that God was intreated for the land.

15 Moreover the Philistines had yet war again with Israel; and David went down, and his servants with him, and fought against the Philistines: and David waxed faint.

16 And Ishbibenob, which was of the sons of the giant, the weight of whose spear weighed three hundred shekels of brass in weight, he being girded with a new sword, thought to have slain David.

17 But Abishai the son of Zeruiah succoured him, and smote the Philistine, and killed him. Then the men of David sware unto him, saying, Thou shalt go no more out with us to battle, that thou quench not the light of Israel.

18 And it came to pass after this, that there was again a battle with the Philistines at Gob: then Sibbechai the Hushathite slew Saph, which was of the sons of the giant.

19 And there was again a battle in Gob with the Philistines, where Elhanan the son of Jaareoregim, a Bethlehemite, slew the brother of Goliath the Gittite, the staff of whose spear was like a weaver’s beam.

20 And there was yet a battle in Gath, where was a man of great stature, that had on every hand six fingers, and on every foot six toes, four and twenty in number; and he also was born to the giant.

21 And when he defied Israel, Jonathan the son of Shimeah the brother of David slew him.

22 These four were born to the giant in Gath, and fell by the hand of David, and by the hand of his servants.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A famine in David's days is traced to Saul's broken treaty with the Gibeonites, and David hands over seven of Saul's sons, whose bodies hang until Rizpah's vigil moves David to bury them with Saul and Jonathan. The chapter ends with four battles against Philistine giants, in which David's men fight and David himself is nearly killed. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. A famine lasts three years, year after year, and David enquires of the Lord, who answers, It is for Saul and his bloody house, because he slew the Gibeonites. Every affliction arises from sin, Henry notes, and some troubles especially show they are sent to bring sin to remembrance; God's judgments often look a great way back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The king calls the Gibeonites, who are not Israelites but a remnant of the Amorites, whom Israel had sworn to, and whom Saul sought to slay in his zeal for Israel and Judah. The old oath, broken by a dead king, still binds the living nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. David asks the Gibeonites what he shall do and wherewith he shall make atonement, that they may bless the inheritance of the Lord. The king seeks not a payment but a blessing from the wronged party. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The Gibeonites say they will have no silver or gold of Saul or his house, nor shall David kill any man in Israel for them, and David asks what they shall say. Money is no satisfaction for blood, Henry observes, and the Gibeonites refuse it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. They answer that the man who consumed them and devised their destruction from all Israel's coasts must be answered for in his sons. Saul's posterity trod in his steps, Henry notes, for it is called a bloody house, and they are reckoned with for his sin as well as their own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Let seven men of his sons be delivered to them, they say, to be hanged to the Lord in Gibeah of Saul, whom the Lord chose, and the king says, I will give them. The number seven matches the public scale of the wrong, and the place is Saul's own city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. But the king spares Mephibosheth son of Jonathan son of Saul, because of the Lord's oath between David and Jonathan. Covenant spares one son of Saul's house even while justice takes seven others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The king takes the two sons of Rizpah daughter of Aiah, whom she bore to Saul, Armoni and Mephibosheth, and the five sons of Michal daughter of Saul, whom she brought up for Adriel. The victims are named, not counted anonymously, and Rizpah's sons head the list. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. He delivers them to the Gibeonites, who hang them in the hill before the Lord, and all seven fall together, put to death in the days of harvest, at the beginning of barley harvest. They are sacrificed at harvest's beginning, Henry says, to turn away God's wrath, which had withheld harvest mercies for years past; in vain do we expect mercy unless we do justice upon our sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Rizpah takes sackcloth, spreads it on the rock, and stays from harvest's beginning until water drops on the bodies from heaven, suffering neither birds by day nor beasts by night to touch them. A mother's vigil guards the dishonoured dead through the whole dry season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. It is told to David what Rizpah, Saul's concubine, has done. The report of her devotion reaches the king who ordered the deaths. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. David goes and takes the bones of Saul and Jonathan from the men of Jabeshgilead, who stole them from the street of Bethshan where the Philistines hanged them. The bones of the first king and his son are recovered from the city that once honoured them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. He brings up the bones of Saul and Jonathan, and gathers the bones of those who were hanged. The honoured dead and the executed dead are gathered together, and both receive burial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The bones of Saul and Jonathan are buried in Benjamin in Zelah, in the sepulchre of Kish his father, and all the king commanded is performed, and after that God is entreated for the land. When God sends rain, Henry notes, the bodies are buried, for then it appears God is entreated; when justice is done on earth, vengeance from heaven ceases. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The Philistines have war again with Israel, and David goes down with his servants to fight, and David waxes faint. These events seem to be toward the end of David's reign, Henry observes, and even strong saints sometimes wax faint in spiritual conflict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Ishbibenob, of the sons of the giant, whose spear weighs three hundred shekels of brass, girded with a new sword, thinks to have slain David. The old king, faint in battle, faces a giant's son with a new sword. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. But Abishai succours David, smites the Philistine, and kills him, and David's men swear he shall go no more out with them to battle, lest he quench the light of Israel. David faints but does not flee, Henry says, and God sends help in time of need; those who stand their ground shall be relieved and made more than conquerors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Again there is battle at Gob, and Sibbechai the Hushathite slays Saph, of the sons of the giant. The giant brood is cut down one by one in successive battles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Again there is battle in Gob, where Elhanan son of Jaareoregim, a Bethlehemite, slays the brother of Goliath, whose spear staff is like a weaver's beam. The brother of the champion David slew as a youth falls to a Bethlehemite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Yet again there is battle in Gath, where a man of great stature has six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot, four and twenty in number, also born to the giant. The catalogue of giants grows stranger, as if the old enemy keeps producing new forms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. When he defies Israel, Jonathan son of Shimeah, David's brother, slays him. Defiance is answered by a kinsman of the king, not by the king himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. These four were born to the giant in Gath, and fell by the hand of David and his servants. Death, Henry reflects, is a Christian's last enemy and a son of Anak, but through him who triumphed for us, believers shall be more than conquerors at last, even over that enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
