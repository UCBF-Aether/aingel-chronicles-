The Text — Jeremiah 41 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now it came to pass in the seventh month, that Ishmael the son of Nethaniah the son of Elishama, of the seed royal, and the princes of the king, even ten men with him, came unto Gedaliah the son of Ahikam to Mizpah; and there they did eat bread together in Mizpah.

2 Then arose Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, and the ten men that were with him, and smote Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan with the sword, and slew him, whom the king of Babylon had made governor over the land.

3 Ishmael also slew all the Jews that were with him, even with Gedaliah, at Mizpah, and the Chaldeans that were found there, and the men of war.

4 And it came to pass the second day after he had slain Gedaliah, and no man knew it,

5 That there came certain from Shechem, from Shiloh, and from Samaria, even fourscore men, having their beards shaven, and their clothes rent, and having cut themselves, with offerings and incense in their hand, to bring them to the house of the LORD.

6 And Ishmael the son of Nethaniah went forth from Mizpah to meet them, weeping all along as he went: and it came to pass, as he met them, he said unto them, Come to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam.

7 And it was so, when they came into the midst of the city, that Ishmael the son of Nethaniah slew them, and cast them into the midst of the pit, he, and the men that were with him.

8 But ten men were found among them that said unto Ishmael, Slay us not: for we have treasures in the field, of wheat, and of barley, and of oil, and of honey. So he forbare, and slew them not among their brethren.

9 Now the pit wherein Ishmael had cast all the dead bodies of the men, whom he had slain because of Gedaliah, was it which Asa the king had made for fear of Baasha king of Israel: and Ishmael the son of Nethaniah filled it with them that were slain.

10 Then Ishmael carried away captive all the residue of the people that were in Mizpah, even the king’s daughters, and all the people that remained in Mizpah, whom Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard had committed to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam: and Ishmael the son of Nethaniah carried them away captive, and departed to go over to the Ammonites.

11 But when Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the captains of the forces that were with him, heard of all the evil that Ishmael the son of Nethaniah had done,

12 Then they took all the men, and went to fight with Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, and found him by the great waters that are in Gibeon.

13 Now it came to pass, that when all the people which were with Ishmael saw Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the captains of the forces that were with him, then they were glad.

14 So all the people that Ishmael had carried away captive from Mizpah cast about and returned, and went unto Johanan the son of Kareah.

15 But Ishmael the son of Nethaniah escaped from Johanan with eight men, and went to the Ammonites.

16 Then took Johanan the son of Kareah, and all the captains of the forces that were with him, all the remnant of the people whom he had recovered from Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, from Mizpah, after that he had slain Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, even mighty men of war, and the women, and the children, and the eunuchs, whom he had brought again from Gibeon:

17 And they departed, and dwelt in the habitation of Chimham, which is by Bethlehem, to go to enter into Egypt,

18 Because of the Chaldeans: for they were afraid of them, because Ishmael the son of Nethaniah had slain Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, whom the king of Babylon made governor in the land.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the seventh month Ishmael, of the seed royal, comes with ten men and murders Gedaliah at Mizpah, together with the Jews and Chaldeans with him, and carries away the remaining people. Johanan rescues the captives at Gibeon, but the people, afraid of the Chaldeans because Ishmael has slain the governor, set out toward Egypt. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry notes Ishmael, being of the seed royal, was jealous of Gedaliah's growing greatness and enraged that he should merit and accept a commission under the king of Babylon. His ten men, princes of the king too, were guided by the same peevish resentments, and came again to Mizpah to eat bread with the governor they meant to kill. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry stresses that those who ate bread with Gedaliah lifted up the heel against him: they picked no quarrel but watched an opportunity when they had him alone and assassinated him. Neither Babylon's commission nor Gedaliah's goodness and blessing to his country could secure him against such perfidious cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry notes Ishmael likewise put to the sword all found in arms there, both Jews and Chaldeans employed under Gedaliah or in any capacity to revenge his death. As if not enough Israelite blood had been shed by the Chaldeans, their own princes here mingled it with the blood of the Chaldeans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry notes the massacre was carried on and concealed so artfully that on the second day after Gedaliah was slain no man knew it, the vine-dressers and husbandmen being busy in the fields. Yet murder will out, as Johanan's later pursuit shows; concealment only delays the reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry notes these eighty men came from Shechem, Samaria, and Shiloh, places once famous but now reduced, belonging to the ten tribes yet retaining an affection for the worship of the God of Israel. They went toward the house of the Lord to pay their respects to its ashes and see its ruins, favouring its dust, in the habit of mourners with clothes rent and heads shaven, carrying offerings and incense in case any altar or priest remained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry notes Ishmael went out to meet the pilgrims with crocodile tears, pretending to bewail Jerusalem's desolations as much as they, and courted them into the town to try how they stood affected to Gedaliah. His invitation, Come to Gedaliah, pretended he would have them come and live with him, when really he intended they should come and die with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry notes that when Ishmael had the pilgrims in the midst of the town he fell upon them and slew them, casting them into the pit, and no doubt took their offerings and converted them to his own use. He who would not stick at such a murder, Henry says, would not stick at sacrilege. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry notes the ten who escaped worked not on their captors' compassion but on their covetousness, offering hidden treasures of wheat, barley, oil, and honey in exchange for their lives. Sometimes, he observes, the ransom of a man's life is his riches; but those who think thus to bribe death when it comes with commission will find death inexorable and themselves wretchedly deceived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry notes the pit was the one Asa king of Judah had dug when he built or fortified Mizpah as a frontier garrison for fear of Baasha king of Israel. Those who dig pits with a good intention, he warns, know not what bad use they may be put to; Ishmael slew so many he threw them all promiscuously into one pit rather than afford each a grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry notes Ishmael carried off prisoners the king's daughters and the poor of the land committed to Gedaliah's charge, leading them toward the Ammonites, probably intending to make a present of them as trophies to the king who set him on. This melancholy story warns us never to be secure in this world: the end of one trouble we fancy final may prove the beginning of a greater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry says it would have been well if Johanan, when he warned Gedaliah, had stayed with him, for he and his captains might have been a life-guard to Gedaliah and a terror to Ishmael, preventing the mischief without bloodshed. Instead they were out upon some expedition and out of the way when they should have been on the best service; at length, hearing of all the evil Ishmael had done, they resolved to try an after-game. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry notes Johanan gathered what forces he could and went to fight Ishmael, pursuing and overtaking him by the great waters of Gibeon. On Johanan's appearing with a force something like a match for him, Ishmael's heart failed him and his guilty conscience flew in his face: the most cruel, Henry observes, are often the most cowardly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry notes the poor captives were glad when they saw Johanan and his captains, looking upon them as their deliverers. Their gladness marks the turn of the whole episode: the prey Ishmael counted secure begins to slip from him the moment a rescuer appears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry notes the captives immediately found a way to wheel about and come over to Johanan, Ishmael not offering to detain them once he saw Johanan. Those who would be helped must help themselves, he says: they stayed not till their conquerors were beaten, but took the first opportunity to escape as soon as their friends appeared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry notes Ishmael quitted his prey to save his life and escaped with eight men, two of his ten assassins having deserted him or been killed in the engagement. He made his way to the Ammonites as a perfect renegade, abandoning all relation to the commonwealth of Israel though of the seed royal, and we hear no more of him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry notes Johanan now took the remnant he had recovered from Ishmael at Mizpah, mighty men of war and women and children and eunuchs, whom he had brought again from Gibeon, and so became their commander-in-chief. He prevailed only to rescue the captives, not to take revenge on the murderers, yet the prisoners were recovered and he now held command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry calls Johanan's resolution to enter Egypt very rash: instead of sitting down quietly and governing peaceably as Gedaliah did, he led the people toward Egypt, using his honestly won command little better than Ishmael would have used his. They encamped for a time in the habitation of Chimham by Bethlehem, David's city, steering their course toward Egypt from personal affection to that country or an ancient national confidence in the Egyptians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry judges the stated reason for going to Egypt very frivolous: he cannot think they really feared the Chaldeans would revenge Gedaliah's murder upon those who had appeared so vigorously against the murderers. They used this fear as a sham to cover their corrupt inclination to return into Egypt, and those who excuse themselves in sin with pretended fears will justly lose their comfort in real fears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
