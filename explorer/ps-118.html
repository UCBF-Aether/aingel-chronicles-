The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 118 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 O give thanks unto the LORD; for he is good: because his mercy endureth for ever.

2 Let Israel now say, that his mercy endureth for ever.

3 Let the house of Aaron now say, that his mercy endureth for ever.

4 Let them now that fear the LORD say, that his mercy endureth for ever.

5 I called upon the LORD in distress: the LORD answered me, and set me in a large place.

6 The LORD is on my side; I will not fear: what can man do unto me?

7 The LORD taketh my part with them that help me: therefore shall I see my desire upon them that hate me.

8 It is better to trust in the LORD than to put confidence in man.

9 It is better to trust in the LORD than to put confidence in princes.

10 All nations compassed me about: but in the name of the LORD will I destroy them.

11 They compassed me about; yea, they compassed me about: but in the name of the LORD I will destroy them.

12 They compassed me about like bees: they are quenched as the fire of thorns: for in the name of the LORD I will destroy them.

13 Thou hast thrust sore at me that I might fall: but the LORD helped me.

14 The LORD is my strength and song, and is become my salvation.

15 The voice of rejoicing and salvation is in the tabernacles of the righteous: the right hand of the LORD doeth valiantly.

16 The right hand of the LORD is exalted: the right hand of the LORD doeth valiantly.

17 I shall not die, but live, and declare the works of the LORD.

18 The LORD hath chastened me sore: but he hath not given me over unto death.

19 Open to me the gates of righteousness: I will go into them, and I will praise the LORD:

20 This gate of the LORD, into which the righteous shall enter.

21 I will praise thee: for thou hast heard me, and art become my salvation.

22 The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner.

23 This is the LORD’s doing; it is marvellous in our eyes.

24 This is the day which the LORD hath made; we will rejoice and be glad in it.

25 Save now, I beseech thee, O LORD: O LORD, I beseech thee, send now prosperity.

26 Blessed be he that cometh in the name of the LORD: we have blessed you out of the house of the LORD.

27 God is the LORD, which hath shewed us light: bind the sacrifice with cords, even unto the horns of the altar.

28 Thou art my God, and I will praise thee: thou art my God, I will exalt thee.

29 O give thanks unto the LORD; for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A thanksgiving built on the refrain "his mercy endureth for ever," first taken up in turn by Israel, the house of Aaron, and all who fear the LORD. The speaker called in distress and was answered and set in a large place; the LORD is on his side, so he will not fear what man can do, and it is better to trust in the LORD than to put confidence in man or in princes. Nations compassed him like bees and were quenched like fire of thorns in the name of the LORD; thrust sore that he might fall, he was helped, and the LORD became his strength, song and salvation. He shall not die but live and declare God's works, though chastened sore. The psalm moves through the gates of righteousness — "the stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner; this is the LORD's doing, marvellous in our eyes" — to "this is the day which the LORD hath made; we will rejoice and be glad in it," to the prayer "save now" and the blessing of him who comes in the name of the LORD, closing as it opened with thanks for a mercy that endures for ever. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry says David's heart was full of God's goodness — he loved to think and speak of it, soliciting praise for God and comfort for others — for God is good not only in himself but good to you, his mercy running in never-failing streams parallel with eternity's longest line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry calls Israel to confess from its own experience what it was called to trust in Psalm 115: priests and people alike join one thankful song, owning they have had experience of mercy all their days and confide in it for everlasting good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry extends the same call to Aaron's house: priests, like people, must own God's goodness and join the song, for the thanksgivings of all who truly fear the Lord please him as much as Israel's or Aaron's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry includes all who fear the Lord, Jews and proselytes: if they can say no more, let them say his mercy endures for ever, encouraging themselves to trust him by what they have experienced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry says God answered with enlargements, doing more than David could ask — enlarging his heart in prayer and giving more largely than desired — setting him in a large place with room to bestir, enjoy and thrive, the more comfortable for coming out of distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry grounds fearlessness in God's partisanship: the Lord is on my side as a righteous God espousing a righteous cause, so if we are for him he is for us; man is a depending creature of limited, subordinate power, and can do nothing but what God permits or turns to good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry reads the Lord taking his part with his helpers as God being all in all to them and to David, without whom neither he nor any friend could help him — and from this he is sure he shall see his desire upon haters, their designs defeated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry judges trust wiser, more comfortable and safer, with more reason and better speed, than confidence in man: devotion to God's guidance and government is better security than all kings undertaking to protect us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry repeats the point for princes: even the highest potentates are a poorer security than trust in the Lord, whose wisdom, power and goodness are entire dependence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry pictures adjacent nations — Philistines, Moabites, Syrians, Ammonites — confederate to cut off succours from newly enthroned David, yet in the Lord's name he will destroy them, going forth depending on his strength, by his warrant, for his glory, as against Goliath and as Christ triumphed over darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry stresses the repetition: compassed about, yea compassed about — enemies virulent, violent and for a time prevalent, rallying when disordered and pushing their design, yet still to be destroyed in the Lord's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry likens enemies to bees and thorn-fire: numerous, noisy and vexatious, they sting to their own destruction like the bee losing life with its sting, and blaze furiously a while then go out, unable to do the mischief threatened — fury and fool's laughter alike to be pitied, not feared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry distinguishes thrust from chastening: the enemy thrust sore, Satan tempting to cast us from excellency, that we might fall into sin and ruin, yet the Lord helped David keep his feet and ground — without that help spiritual enemies would long have ruined us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry makes the Lord both strength and song: without him we are weak and sad, on him we stay for doing and suffering and solace ourselves expressing joy and easing grief; God is sometimes strength when not song, but when both, he has become not only Saviour but salvation itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry hears rejoicing in the tabernacles of the righteous — mean, movable dwellings, no continuing city — yet more comfortable than wicked palaces, for where religion rules there is salvation, cause for continual joy, and the voice of rejoicing in daily family praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry exalts God's right hand doing valiantly and being exalted, itself exalting David: divine power acts vigorously and victoriously, with spirit as well as strength, and when it does valiantly for salvation it ought to be exalted in praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry makes life's purpose declaration: I shall not die by enemies' hands but live a monument of mercy and power, making it life's business to praise God, for it is not worth living for any other purpose than to declare his works for his honour and others' encouragement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry again distinguishes chastening from abandonment: men thrust for destruction, God chastens with a Father's love for instruction and holiness, and though he seems to turn men to destruction he says Return — chastened, not killed, as dying yet living, in Paul's phrase. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry seeks humble admission: temple gates are gates of righteousness, shut against the uncircumcised, so those entering communion must become suitors, and once admitted go in to praise — our business within God's gates, longing till heaven's gates open for endless praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry sees the gate opened on knocking: this is the Lord's appointed gate, into which the righteous shall enter — and some understand Christ himself, the way, the door of the sheep, by whom alone the righteous enter into God's righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry hears thanks for a favour already granted in prospect: distant saints praising God because he heard Old Testament prayers for Messiah's coming and would be their salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry applies the rejected stone principally to Christ, with David's preferment as shadow: Saul's court refused the stone, as Jewish rulers refused to own Messiah or build faith on him, trampling and stumbling at him — disgrace to Christ, ruin to rejecters, for rejecters of Christ are rejected of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry marvels at God's hand in both humiliation and exaltation: the rejected stone becomes headstone of the corner, chief foundation uniting Jew and Gentile and chief top-stone completing the building, pre-eminent as author and finisher of faith — the Lord's doing, product of his counsel, wonderful as his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry solemnizes the day the Lord has made: the whole gospel dispensation, the accepted time and day of salvation, a continual feast kept with joy — or fitly the Christian sabbath, made holy in remembrance of resurrection, when the rejected stone began exaltation, a day to rejoice in as his honour and our advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry hears hosanna as a loyal inauguration shout, Vivat rex: save me, let this Saviour be my ruler and protection, give me interest in his kingdom's prosperity, victory over lusts, grace conquering and to conquer — and preserve him, send his gospel prospering to pull down strongholds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry gives priests their part: bless the coming Prince — blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord, he that was to come and is to come again, commissioned to do God's will — welcome him into hearts, bless his ministers, and bless the people out of the Lord's house with spiritual blessings in heavenly places. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry pairs privilege and duty: God the Lord has shown us light — knowledge of himself, lifted countenance, prospect of everlasting light — and calls for ourselves as living sacrifices, bound with cords to the altar's horns in prayer and praise, hearts fixed and not starting back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry closes with personal exaltation: Thou art my God, on whom I depend and to whom I am devoted, who ownest me and art owned by me, therefore I will praise thee and exalt thee in my heart and others'. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry ends as he began, for God's glory is Alpha and Omega of all addresses: give thanks because in Christ God is good to man, his mercy endures for ever, the covenant made sure and everlasting — fit close to a prophecy of Christ, echoed by angels' glory in the highest and our hosannas. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
