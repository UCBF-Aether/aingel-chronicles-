The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Joshua 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now after the death of Moses the servant of the LORD it came to pass, that the LORD spake unto Joshua the son of Nun, Moses’ minister, saying,

2 Moses my servant is dead; now therefore arise, go over this Jordan, thou, and all this people, unto the land which I do give to them, even to the children of Israel.

3 Every place that the sole of your foot shall tread upon, that have I given unto you, as I said unto Moses.

4 From the wilderness and this Lebanon even unto the great river, the river Euphrates, all the land of the Hittites, and unto the great sea toward the going down of the sun, shall be your coast.

5 There shall not any man be able to stand before thee all the days of thy life: as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee: I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.

6 Be strong and of a good courage: for unto this people shalt thou divide for an inheritance the land, which I sware unto their fathers to give them.

7 Only be thou strong and very courageous, that thou mayest observe to do according to all the law, which Moses my servant commanded thee: turn not from it to the right hand or to the left, that thou mayest prosper whithersoever thou goest.

8 This book of the law shall not depart out of thy mouth; but thou shalt meditate therein day and night, that thou mayest observe to do according to all that is written therein: for then thou shalt make thy way prosperous, and then thou shalt have good success.

9 Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.

10 Then Joshua commanded the officers of the people, saying,

11 Pass through the host, and command the people, saying, Prepare you victuals; for within three days ye shall pass over this Jordan, to go in to possess the land, which the LORD your God giveth you to possess it.

12 And to the Reubenites, and to the Gadites, and to half the tribe of Manasseh, spake Joshua, saying,

13 Remember the word which Moses the servant of the LORD commanded you, saying, The LORD your God hath given you rest, and hath given you this land.

14 Your wives, your little ones, and your cattle, shall remain in the land which Moses gave you on this side Jordan; but ye shall pass before your brethren armed, all the mighty men of valour, and help them;

15 Until the LORD have given your brethren rest, as he hath given you, and they also have possessed the land which the LORD your God giveth them: then ye shall return unto the land of your possession, and enjoy it, which Moses the LORD’s servant gave you on this side Jordan toward the sunrising.

16 And they answered Joshua, saying, All that thou commandest us we will do, and whithersoever thou sendest us, we will go.

17 According as we hearkened unto Moses in all things, so will we hearken unto thee: only the LORD thy God be with thee, as he was with Moses.

18 Whosoever he be that doth rebel against thy commandment, and will not hearken unto thy words in all that thou commandest him, he shall be put to death: only be strong and of a good courage.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

With Moses dead, the Lord commissions Joshua to take Israel over the Jordan into the land promised to their fathers, from the wilderness to Lebanon and the Euphrates. The charge is repeated three times — be strong and of a good courage — and it is tied to the book of the law, which Joshua is to meditate on day and night and obey without turning right or left. The Transjordan tribes, already settled east of the river, pledge their fighting men to the campaign. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After the death of Moses the Lord spoke to Joshua the son of Nun, Moses' minister, and Henry notes Joshua had been trained up under command, since those fittest to rule are those who have learned to obey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Moses being dead, Joshua was commanded to arise and go over Jordan with all the people to the land God gave Israel, and Henry notes that though Jordan's banks were overflowed and Joshua had no bridge or boats, he must believe God would open a way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Every place the sole of their foot tread upon was given to them, as God had said to Moses, fulfilling the promise made to their fathers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Their territory was to run from the wilderness and Lebanon to the great river Euphrates, all the land of the Hittites, and to the Great Sea toward the going down of the sun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. No man would be able to stand before Joshua all his life, for as God was with Moses so he would be with him, and would not fail him nor forsake him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Joshua was to be strong and of good courage, for to this people he would divide as an inheritance the land sworn to their fathers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Only being strong and very courageous to observe all the law Moses commanded, turning neither to the right hand nor to the left, would Joshua prosper wherever he went, since no man's dignity sets him above the law of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The book of the law was not to depart from Joshua's mouth, but he was to meditate in it day and night and observe to do all written in it, for Henry notes he must make the law his rule and not neglect the one thing needful amid worldly affairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. God himself commanded Joshua to be strong and of good courage, not afraid nor dismayed, for the Lord his God would be with him whithersoever he went, and in the way of duty there is reason to be strong and very bold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Joshua then commanded the officers of the people, accepting the government and addressing himself immediately to the business of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The officers were to pass through the host commanding the people to prepare victuals, for within three days they would pass over Jordan to possess the land, and Henry notes we honour God's truth when we stagger not at his promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Joshua spoke particularly to the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half tribe of Manasseh, reminding them of their engagement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. They were to remember Moses' word that the Lord had given them rest and given them this land on the east side of Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Their wives, little ones, and cattle were to remain in the land Moses gave them, while all their mighty men of valour passed over armed before their brethren to help them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. They were to help their brethren until the Lord gave them rest as well and they possessed the land, and then return to their own possession, since those given rest by providence ought to consider what service they may do their brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The people answered that all Joshua commanded them they would do, and whithersoever he sent them they would go, engaging to obey without murmuring or disputing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. As they had hearkened to Moses in all things, so they would hearken to Joshua, praying only that the Lord his God would be with him as he was with Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Whoever rebelled against Joshua's commandment and would not hearken to his words would be put to death, and the people charged him only to be strong and of good courage, since the best we can ask for magistrates is God's presence with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
