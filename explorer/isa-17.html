The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The burden of Damascus. Behold, Damascus is taken away from being a city, and it shall be a ruinous heap.

2 The cities of Aroer are forsaken: they shall be for flocks, which shall lie down, and none shall make them afraid.

3 The fortress also shall cease from Ephraim, and the kingdom from Damascus, and the remnant of Syria: they shall be as the glory of the children of Israel, saith the LORD of hosts.

4 And in that day it shall come to pass, that the glory of Jacob shall be made thin, and the fatness of his flesh shall wax lean.

5 And it shall be as when the harvestman gathereth the corn, and reapeth the ears with his arm; and it shall be as he that gathereth ears in the valley of Rephaim.

6 Yet gleaning grapes shall be left in it, as the shaking of an olive tree, two or three berries in the top of the uppermost bough, four or five in the outmost fruitful branches thereof, saith the LORD God of Israel.

7 At that day shall a man look to his Maker, and his eyes shall have respect to the Holy One of Israel.

8 And he shall not look to the altars, the work of his hands, neither shall respect that which his fingers have made, either the groves, or the images.

9 In that day shall his strong cities be as a forsaken bough, and an uppermost branch, which they left because of the children of Israel: and there shall be desolation.

10 Because thou hast forgotten the God of thy salvation, and hast not been mindful of the rock of thy strength, therefore shalt thou plant pleasant plants, and shalt set it with strange slips:

11 In the day shalt thou make thy plant to grow, and in the morning shalt thou make thy seed to flourish: but the harvest shall be a heap in the day of grief and of desperate sorrow.

12 Woe to the multitude of many people, which make a noise like the noise of the seas; and to the rushing of nations, that make a rushing like the rushing of mighty waters!

13 The nations shall rush like the rushing of many waters: but God shall rebuke them, and they shall flee far off, and shall be chased as the chaff of the mountains before the wind, and like a rolling thing before the whirlwind.

14 And behold at eveningtide trouble; and before the morning he is not. This is the portion of them that spoil us, and the lot of them that rob us.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Damascus, allied with Samaria against Judah, will be taken away from being a city and left a ruinous heap, and Ephraim's glory will fade with it. A remnant will turn back to its Maker, and the chapter closes with the sudden overnight vanishing of the enemy who at eveningtide was a terror — the portion of all who spoil God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The burden of Damascus: Behold, Damascus is taken away from being a city, and it will be a ruinous heap. This prophecy concerns Damascus and its ally Samaria, Syria and Israel leagued together in chapters 7–8; Tiglath-pileser had already carried Damascus's people to Kir, but now in Hezekiah's reign a further overthrow is foretold, and Damascus is named before Israel because it was the prevailing power in the league, with Ephraim standing or falling with it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The cities of Aroer are forsaken, and they will be for flocks, which will lie down, and none will make them afraid. The cities of Aroer are the cities round Aroer and under its jurisdiction, near the river of Gad and founded by the Gadites, and the forsaken cities will become pasture like the waste places of chapter 5. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The fortress also will cease from Ephraim, and the kingdom from Damascus, and the remnant of Syria will be as the glory of the children of Israel, says the Lord of hosts. The strongholds will be pulled down, Samaria especially, and the remnant of Syria left after Tiglath-pileser's overthrow will meet the same fate as Israel, its ally. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. In that day the glory of Jacob will be made thin, and the fatness of his flesh will wax lean. The glory of Jacob is the kingdom of Ephraim and all they rely on, and its fatness will waste like the fat ones of Assyria in chapter 10, a figure for destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. It will be as when the harvestman gathers the corn and reaps the ears with his arm, and it will be as he that gathers ears in the valley of Rephaim. The inhabitants and wealth of Israel will be swept away with only a few left behind, as the husbandman gathers corn and fruit and leaves only a few gleaning ears, collecting the standing grain with one arm to cut it with the sickle in the other, in the fertile valley of Rephaim southwest of Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Yet gleaning grapes will be left in it, as the shaking of an olive tree, two or three berries in the top of the uppermost bough, four or five in the outmost fruitful branches, says the Lord God of Israel. A few poor inhabitants will be left in Israel, like the two or three olive berries left on the topmost boughs, not worth the trouble of reaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. At that day a man will look to his Maker, and his eyes will have respect to the Holy One of Israel. Instead of trusting in fortresses, the remnant will look to the God who made them, turning from the strongholds that failed them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He will not look to the altars, the work of his hands, neither will he respect that which his fingers have made, either the groves or the images. The groves are symbolical trees representing the hosts of heaven, Saba, answering to Astarte queen of heaven as Baal is king, and the images are literally images to the sun, to Baal, who answers to the sun as Astarte answers to the hosts of heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. In that day his strong cities will be as a forsaken bough and an uppermost branch, which they left because of the children of Israel, and there will be desolation. The forsaken bough is rather the leavings of woods, what the axeman leaves when he cuts down the grove, and only a few cities out of many will be left to Israel by God's purpose, executed by the Assyrian. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Because you have forgotten the God of your salvation and have not been mindful of the rock of your strength, therefore you will plant pleasant plants and set them with strange slips. The God of salvation and rock of strength are the titles of Deuteronomy 32, and the pleasant plants are rather nursery grounds or pleasure-grounds, set with strange slips, cuttings of plants from far and therefore valuable — the idolatrous plantings of a people who forgot God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. In the day you will make your plant to grow, and in the morning you will make your seed to flourish, but the harvest will be a heap in the day of grief and of desperate sorrow. In the day of planting, as soon as you plant it grows, and in the morning, immediately after, the seed flourishes, but promising as the prospect is, the harvest is gone in the day of expected possession, and the sorrow is desperate and irremediable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Woe to the multitude of many people, who make a noise like the noise of the seas, and to the rushing of nations, that make a rushing like the rushing of mighty waters! This begins a fragment on the sudden destruction of Sennacherib's great army in Judea: the prophet in vision perceives the vast and mixed Assyrian hosts on the hills of Judah, roaring like the seas, yet the connection is that notwithstanding calamities on Israel, God's people will not be utterly destroyed, and the Assyrian spoilers will perish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The nations will rush like the rushing of many waters, but God will rebuke them, and they will flee far off, and will be chased as the chaff of the mountains before the wind, and like a rolling thing before the whirlwind. God rebukes them and they flee, chased as chaff from threshing floors set on elevated places to catch the wind that separates chaff from wheat, and like stubble rolling before the whirlwind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. And behold, at eveningtide trouble, and before the morning he is not: this is the portion of them that spoil us, and the lot of them that rob us. The destruction before morning was fulfilled to the letter in the host that at eveningtide was such a terror to Judah, and this is the general declaration of the doom awaiting the foes of God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
