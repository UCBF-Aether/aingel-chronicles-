The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, and timber of cedars, with masons and carpenters, to build him an house.

2 And David perceived that the LORD had confirmed him king over Israel, for his kingdom was lifted up on high, because of his people Israel.

3 And David took more wives at Jerusalem: and David begat more sons and daughters.

4 Now these are the names of his children which he had in Jerusalem; Shammua, and Shobab, Nathan, and Solomon,

5 And Ibhar, and Elishua, and Elpalet,

6 And Nogah, and Nepheg, and Japhia,

7 And Elishama, and Beeliada, and Eliphalet.

8 And when the Philistines heard that David was anointed king over all Israel, all the Philistines went up to seek David. And David heard of it, and went out against them.

9 And the Philistines came and spread themselves in the valley of Rephaim.

10 And David enquired of God, saying, Shall I go up against the Philistines? And wilt thou deliver them into mine hand? And the LORD said unto him, Go up; for I will deliver them into thine hand.

11 So they came up to Baalperazim; and David smote them there. Then David said, God hath broken in upon mine enemies by mine hand like the breaking forth of waters: therefore they called the name of that place Baalperazim.

12 And when they had left their gods there, David gave a commandment, and they were burned with fire.

13 And the Philistines yet again spread themselves abroad in the valley.

14 Therefore David enquired again of God; and God said unto him, Go not up after them; turn away from them, and come upon them over against the mulberry trees.

15 And it shall be, when thou shalt hear a sound of going in the tops of the mulberry trees, that then thou shalt go out to battle: for God is gone forth before thee to smite the host of the Philistines.

16 David therefore did as God commanded him: and they smote the host of the Philistines from Gibeon even to Gazer.

17 And the fame of David went out into all lands; and the LORD brought the fear of him upon all nations.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells how Hiram of Tyre built David a house, lists David's children born at Jerusalem, and recounts two defeats of the Philistines, after which David's fame spread to all lands. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, with cedar trees, masons, and carpenters, to build him a house. Henry notes no man has such sufficiency that he needs no neighbours: great David still needed little Hiram's workmen and materials. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. David perceived the Lord had confirmed him king over Israel, for his kingdom was lifted up on high because of his people Israel. Henry notes a wise man is satisfied to be settled only when he sees God's special providence in it, and advancements are for usefulness, not self-glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. David took more wives at Jerusalem, and begat more sons and daughters. Henry calls this David's infirmity in settling, yet notes the numerous issue added to his honour and strength, for children are a heritage of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. These are the names of his children born in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, and Solomon. Henry notes David's children are recorded again here, as before, because it was their honour to have such a father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When the Philistines heard David was anointed king over all Israel, they sought him, and David heard and went out against them. Henry warns against security in any settlement: when Christ's kingdom advances, the serpent's seed insults it, and molestation should be expected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The Philistines came and spread themselves in the valley of Rephaim. Henry sets this invasion as the occasion for David's repeated enquiry of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. David enquired of God, saying, shall I go up against the Philistines, and wilt thou deliver them into my hand? And the Lord said, go up, for I will deliver them. Henry directs readers in all ways to acknowledge God: in distress fly to him, when wronged appeal to him, and when unsure ask counsel at his oracles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. They came up to Baal-perazim, and David smote them there, saying, God hath broken in upon my enemies by my hand like the breaking forth of waters, therefore they called the place Baal-perazim. Henry encourages resisting spiritual enemies in observance of divine directions and dependence on divine strength, as Philistines fled before David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. When the Philistines left their gods there, David gave commandment and they were burned with fire. Henry teaches holy indignation against idolatry and all its remains from this burning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The Philistines yet again spread themselves in the valley. Henry notes David enquired a second time, modelling repeated dependence rather than presuming on one victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. God said, go not up after them, but turn away and come upon them over against the mulberry trees. Henry presents the second, different direction as proof that guidance must be sought afresh, not assumed from past success. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. When thou shalt hear a sound of going in the tops of the mulberry trees, then go out to battle, for God is gone forth before thee to smite the Philistines. Henry urges attending God's motions in providence and Spirit: when God goes before us, gird up and follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. David did as God commanded him, and they smote the Philistine host from Gibeon even to Gazer. Henry makes obedience to the new direction the pattern of success. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The fame of David went out into all lands, and the Lord brought the fear of him upon all nations. Henry reads this reputation as a type of the Son of David, exalted and given a name above every name, formidable to enemies and desirable as an ally. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
