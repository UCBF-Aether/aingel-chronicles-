The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses and to Aaron, saying,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When any man hath a running issue out of his flesh, because of his issue he is unclean.

3 And this shall be his uncleanness in his issue: whether his flesh run with his issue, or his flesh be stopped from his issue, it is his uncleanness.

4 Every bed, whereon he lieth that hath the issue, is unclean: and every thing, whereon he sitteth, shall be unclean.

5 And whosoever toucheth his bed shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even.

6 And he that sitteth on any thing whereon he sat that hath the issue shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even.

7 And he that toucheth the flesh of him that hath the issue shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even.

8 And if he that hath the issue spit upon him that is clean; then he shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even.

9 And what saddle soever he rideth upon that hath the issue shall be unclean.

10 And whosoever toucheth any thing that was under him shall be unclean until the even: and he that beareth any of those things shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even.

11 And whomsoever he toucheth that hath the issue, and hath not rinsed his hands in water, he shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even.

12 And the vessel of earth, that he toucheth which hath the issue, shall be broken: and every vessel of wood shall be rinsed in water.

13 And when he that hath an issue is cleansed of his issue; then he shall number to himself seven days for his cleansing, and wash his clothes, and bathe his flesh in running water, and shall be clean.

14 And on the eighth day he shall take to him two turtledoves, or two young pigeons, and come before the LORD unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and give them unto the priest:

15 And the priest shall offer them, the one for a sin offering, and the other for a burnt offering; and the priest shall make an atonement for him before the LORD for his issue.

16 And if any man’s seed of copulation go out from him, then he shall wash all his flesh in water, and be unclean until the even.

17 And every garment, and every skin, whereon is the seed of copulation, shall be washed with water, and be unclean until the even.

18 The woman also with whom man shall lie with seed of copulation, they shall both bathe themselves in water, and be unclean until the even.

19 And if a woman have an issue, and her issue in her flesh be blood, she shall be put apart seven days: and whosoever toucheth her shall be unclean until the even.

20 And every thing that she lieth upon in her separation shall be unclean: every thing also that she sitteth upon shall be unclean.

21 And whosoever toucheth her bed shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even.

22 And whosoever toucheth any thing that she sat upon shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even.

23 And if it be on her bed, or on any thing whereon she sitteth, when he toucheth it, he shall be unclean until the even.

24 And if any man lie with her at all, and her flowers be upon him, he shall be unclean seven days; and all the bed whereon he lieth shall be unclean.

25 And if a woman have an issue of her blood many days out of the time of her separation, or if it run beyond the time of her separation; all the days of the issue of her uncleanness shall be as the days of her separation: she shall be unclean.

26 Every bed whereon she lieth all the days of her issue shall be unto her as the bed of her separation: and whatsoever she sitteth upon shall be unclean, as the uncleanness of her separation.

27 And whosoever toucheth those things shall be unclean, and shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even.

28 But if she be cleansed of her issue, then she shall number to herself seven days, and after that she shall be clean.

29 And on the eighth day she shall take unto her two turtles, or two young pigeons, and bring them unto the priest, to the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

30 And the priest shall offer the one for a sin offering, and the other for a burnt offering; and the priest shall make an atonement for her before the LORD for the issue of her uncleanness.

31 Thus shall ye separate the children of Israel from their uncleanness; that they die not in their uncleanness, when they defile my tabernacle that is among them.

32 This is the law of him that hath an issue, and of him whose seed goeth from him, and is defiled therewith;

33 And of her that is sick of her flowers, and of him that hath an issue, of the man, and of the woman, and of him that lieth with her that is unclean.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the laws of uncleanness from bodily discharges. A man's running issue, and a woman's issue of blood in her separation or prolonged beyond it, defile bed, seat, saddle, vessel, and anyone who touches them, with washing and uncleanness until evening, or seven days where the chapter so provides. After cleansing, seven days are counted and on the eighth day two birds are offered, one for a sin offering and one for a burnt offering. The chapter closes by warning that Israel must be separated from uncleanness lest they defile the tabernacle among them. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron, giving further forms of personal uncleanness after the leprosy laws, to be governed by the same separation principle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When any man has a running issue out of his flesh, because of his issue he is unclean, whether from natural causes or disease, and the stringent rules that follow are calculated to prevent contagion and discourage licentious excess. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. His uncleanness is defined by the issue itself, whether his flesh runs with it or is stopped from it, so the condition, not merely the flow, defiles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Every bed he lies on and everything he sits on shall be unclean, the separation extending from the person to anything connected with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Whoever touches his bed shall wash his clothes, bathe in water, and be unclean until evening, secondary contact requiring washing and a day's exclusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He who sits on anything on which the man with the issue sat shall wash his clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening, the sitting place carrying the defilement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He who touches the body of the man with the issue shall wash his clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening, direct contact defiling as surely as contact with his bed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. If the man with the issue spits on a clean person, that person shall wash his clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening, the issue's moisture itself conveying defilement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Whatever saddle he rides upon shall be unclean, the riding seat being treated like a bed or sitting place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Whoever touches anything that was under him shall be unclean until evening, and whoever bears such things shall wash clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening, bearing being a closer contact than touching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Whomever the man with the issue touches, if he has not rinsed his hands in water, shall wash clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening, unwashed hands carrying the defilement to the touched person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. An earthen vessel he touches shall be broken, since Israelite pottery, like Egyptian water jars, was unglazed and porous and liable to imbibe impure particles, while a wooden vessel shall be rinsed in water. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. When he is cleansed of his issue, he numbers seven days for his cleansing, washes his clothes, bathes his flesh in running water, and is clean, a week's probation like a leper's to make sure he is completely healed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. On the eighth day he takes two turtledoves or two young pigeons and comes before the LORD to the tabernacle door and gives them to the priest, as the offering that ends the probation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The priest offers one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering, making atonement for him before the LORD for his issue, removing ceremonial defilement and typically pardoning sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. If a man's seed goes from him, he shall wash all his flesh in water and be unclean until evening, the same evening limit applying to this involuntary issue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Every garment and skin on which seed is shall be washed with water and be unclean until evening, the defilement extending to cloth and leather. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. A woman with whom a man lies with seed shall both bathe in water and be unclean until evening, the shared act sharing the evening uncleanness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. If a woman has an issue of blood in her flesh, she shall be put apart seven days, and whoever touches her shall be unclean until evening, though this may be a natural affection, it was anciently considered contagious and typified moral impurity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Everything she lies upon in her separation shall be unclean, and everything she sits upon shall be unclean, the same bed and seat rule as for a man's issue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Whoever touches her bed shall wash his clothes, bathe in water, and be unclean until evening. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Whoever touches anything she sat upon shall wash his clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. If it is on her bed or on anything she sits upon, when he touches it he shall be unclean until evening, indirect contact through her bed or seat still defiling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. If any man lies with her and her flowers are upon him, he shall be unclean seven days, and all the bed he lies upon shall be unclean, sharing her full separation period. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. If a woman has an issue of blood many days out of her separation time, or beyond it, all the days of her unclean issue she shall be as in her separation days, the prolonged flow extending the separation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Every bed she lies on all the days of her issue shall be as the bed of her separation, and whatever she sits on shall be unclean as in her separation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Whoever touches those things shall be unclean, wash his clothes, bathe in water, and be unclean until evening, as with her ordinary separation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 30. The priest offers one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering, making atonement for her before the LORD for the issue of her uncleanness, the appointed expiation whose neglect would defile the tabernacle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Thus Israel shall be separated from their uncleanness, lest they die in it when they defile the tabernacle among them, God requiring complete purity from a people dwelling as priests in His presence, and debarring even involuntary impurities as disrespect to His majesty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. This is the law for one who has an issue, and for him whose seed goes from him and is unclean thereby, the whole men's-issue law summed up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. And for her who is sick of her flowers, and for one with an issue, man or woman, and for him who lies with her who is unclean, the women's law and the shared-lying law being summed up with the men's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
