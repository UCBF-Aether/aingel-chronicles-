The Text — 2 Book of the Covenant 11 (J. Cooper & A. J. Maclean, The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1902), public domain (archive.org cu31924029296170))

1 If any one receive any service to carry to a widow or poor woman or any one constantly engaged in a Church work, let him give it the same day; and if not, on the morrow, let him add something to it from his own [property] and so give it. For the bread of the poor hath been kept back in his possession.

2 But in the last week of Pascha, on the fifth day of the week, let the bread and the cup be offered. And he who suffered for that which he hath offered, he [it is] who draweth near.

3 Let the lamp be offered’ in the temple by the deacon, saying : The grace of our Lord [be] with you all. And let all the people say: And with Thy spirit.

4 And let the little boys say spiritual psalms and hymns of praise by the light of the lamp.’ Let all the people respond Hallelujah to the psalm and to the chant sung together, with one accord, with voices in harmony; and let no one kneel until he who speaketh cease. Similarly also when a lection is read or the word of doctrine is spoken.

5 If then the Name of the Lord be spoken, and the rest, as hath sufficiently been made known, let no one bow, having come creeping in.!

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

If anyone receives any service to carry to a widow, poor woman or anyone constantly engaged in a Church work, he is to give it the same day; if not, on the morrow he is to add something to it from his own property and so give it, for the bread of the poor has been kept back in his possession. In the last week of Pascha, on the fifth day of the week, the bread and cup are to be offered, and he who suffered for that which he has offered draws near. The lamp is to be offered in the temple by the deacon, saying, The grace of our Lord be with you all; and all the people say, And with Thy spirit. The little boys say spiritual psalms and hymns of praise by the light of the lamp; all the people respond Hallelujah to the psalm and chant sung together, with one accord, with voices in harmony, and no one is to kneel until he who speaks ceases — similarly also when a lection is read or the word of doctrine is spoken. If then the Name of the Lord is spoken, and the rest, as has sufficiently been made known, no one is to bow, having come creeping in.
