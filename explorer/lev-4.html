The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, If a soul shall sin through ignorance against any of the commandments of the LORD concerning things which ought not to be done, and shall do against any of them:

3 If the priest that is anointed do sin according to the sin of the people; then let him bring for his sin, which he hath sinned, a young bullock without blemish unto the LORD for a sin offering.

4 And he shall bring the bullock unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the LORD; and shall lay his hand upon the bullock’s head, and kill the bullock before the LORD.

5 And the priest that is anointed shall take of the bullock’s blood, and bring it to the tabernacle of the congregation:

6 And the priest shall dip his finger in the blood, and sprinkle of the blood seven times before the LORD, before the vail of the sanctuary.

7 And the priest shall put some of the blood upon the horns of the altar of sweet incense before the LORD, which is in the tabernacle of the congregation; and shall pour all the blood of the bullock at the bottom of the altar of the burnt offering, which is at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

8 And he shall take off from it all the fat of the bullock for the sin offering; the fat that covereth the inwards, and all the fat that is upon the inwards,

9 And the two kidneys, and the fat that is upon them, which is by the flanks, and the caul above the liver, with the kidneys, it shall he take away,

10 As it was taken off from the bullock of the sacrifice of peace offerings: and the priest shall burn them upon the altar of the burnt offering.

11 And the skin of the bullock, and all his flesh, with his head, and with his legs, and his inwards, and his dung,

12 Even the whole bullock shall he carry forth without the camp unto a clean place, where the ashes are poured out, and burn him on the wood with fire: where the ashes are poured out shall he be burnt.

13 And if the whole congregation of Israel sin through ignorance, and the thing be hid from the eyes of the assembly, and they have done somewhat against any of the commandments of the LORD concerning things which should not be done, and are guilty;

14 When the sin, which they have sinned against it, is known, then the congregation shall offer a young bullock for the sin, and bring him before the tabernacle of the congregation.

15 And the elders of the congregation shall lay their hands upon the head of the bullock before the LORD: and the bullock shall be killed before the LORD.

16 And the priest that is anointed shall bring of the bullock’s blood to the tabernacle of the congregation:

17 And the priest shall dip his finger in some of the blood, and sprinkle it seven times before the LORD, even before the vail.

18 And he shall put some of the blood upon the horns of the altar which is before the LORD, that is in the tabernacle of the congregation, and shall pour out all the blood at the bottom of the altar of the burnt offering, which is at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

19 And he shall take all his fat from him, and burn it upon the altar.

20 And he shall do with the bullock as he did with the bullock for a sin offering, so shall he do with this: and the priest shall make an atonement for them, and it shall be forgiven them.

21 And he shall carry forth the bullock without the camp, and burn him as he burned the first bullock: it is a sin offering for the congregation.

22 When a ruler hath sinned, and done somewhat through ignorance against any of the commandments of the LORD his God concerning things which should not be done, and is guilty;

23 Or if his sin, wherein he hath sinned, come to his knowledge; he shall bring his offering, a kid of the goats, a male without blemish:

24 And he shall lay his hand upon the head of the goat, and kill it in the place where they kill the burnt offering before the LORD: it is a sin offering.

25 And the priest shall take of the blood of the sin offering with his finger, and put it upon the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and shall pour out his blood at the bottom of the altar of burnt offering.

26 And he shall burn all his fat upon the altar, as the fat of the sacrifice of peace offerings: and the priest shall make an atonement for him as concerning his sin, and it shall be forgiven him.

27 And if any one of the common people sin through ignorance, while he doeth somewhat against any of the commandments of the LORD concerning things which ought not to be done, and be guilty;

28 Or if his sin, which he hath sinned, come to his knowledge: then he shall bring his offering, a kid of the goats, a female without blemish, for his sin which he hath sinned.

29 And he shall lay his hand upon the head of the sin offering, and slay the sin offering in the place of the burnt offering.

30 And the priest shall take of the blood thereof with his finger, and put it upon the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and shall pour out all the blood thereof at the bottom of the altar.

31 And he shall take away all the fat thereof, as the fat is taken away from off the sacrifice of peace offerings; and the priest shall burn it upon the altar for a sweet savour unto the LORD; and the priest shall make an atonement for him, and it shall be forgiven him.

32 And if he bring a lamb for a sin offering, he shall bring it a female without blemish.

33 And he shall lay his hand upon the head of the sin offering, and slay it for a sin offering in the place where they kill the burnt offering.

34 And the priest shall take of the blood of the sin offering with his finger, and put it upon the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and shall pour out all the blood thereof at the bottom of the altar:

35 And he shall take away all the fat thereof, as the fat of the lamb is taken away from the sacrifice of the peace offerings; and the priest shall burn them upon the altar, according to the offerings made by fire unto the LORD: and the priest shall make an atonement for his sin that he hath committed, and it shall be forgiven him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the law of the sin offering for sins committed through ignorance or inadvertence, not wilfully. The offering is graded by the offender: a bullock for the anointed priest and for the whole congregation, with blood carried into the sanctuary, and a male goat for a ruler or a female goat or lamb for a common person, with blood put on the horns of the outer altar. In each case the fat is burnt, atonement is made, and the sinner is forgiven. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Sin offering. The offering for sins committed through ignorance or inadvertence, as opposed to wilful sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Through ignorance. Unintentional breach, through haste or inadvertence, of a commandment concerning things not to be done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The sin offering law is addressed to Israel as a whole, introducing a rite for sins committed not wilfully but in ignorance or inadvertence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Sins through ignorance here are unintentional breaches of ceremonial law, made through haste or inadvertency of negative precepts, which if done knowingly and wilfully would have carried a capital penalty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The anointed priest means chiefly the high priest, whose office as typical mediator makes even an unconscious transgression grave, both for himself and by the influence of his example, and whose guilt is said to bring guilt on the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The priest brings the young bullock to the tabernacle door, lays his hand on its head, and kills it before the LORD, following the form prescribed for burnt offerings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The anointed priest himself takes the bullock's blood into the tabernacle, a solemn ceremonial reserved for very grave offences, signalling that his sin has vitiated his services until atoned for by blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Sprinkling the blood seven times before the veil shows that the priest's sin, though done in ignorance, has tainted the sanctuary service itself and requires expiation within the holy place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Blood is put on the horns of the golden altar of incense and the rest poured at the base of the burnt-offering altar, carrying the atonement from the inner sanctuary out to the court of the priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. All the fat of the bullock for the sin offering is taken off, as in the peace offering, the choice inward portions being reserved for the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The fat covering the inwards and lying upon them is removed with the rest of the altar portion, following the same pattern as the peace offering's fat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The kidneys with their fat and the caul above the liver are taken away and burnt on the altar of burnt offering, as was done with the bullock of the peace offerings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. In ordinary circumstances the skin and flesh would be priests' perquisites, but for a high priest's sin the whole carcass is dealt with outside the camp, to mark the enormity of the transgression and the horror with which it is regarded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The whole bullock is carried to a clean place without the camp where the ashes are poured out and burnt on wood with fire, its total combustion outside the camp underscoring the gravity of the priest's sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. When the whole congregation sins through ignorance, through culpable neglect or misapprehension of the law, national guilt is contracted and national expiation becomes necessary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Once the congregation's sin is known, it offers a young bullock for the sin, the same sacrifice as for the priest, since the guilt taints the whole assembly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The elders, as heads of the tribes representing the people and as the principal aggressors in misleading the congregation, lay their hands on the bullock's head before the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The anointed priest brings the bullock's blood into the tabernacle, as in the priest's own offering, because the congregation's sin likewise taints the sanctuary service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Dipping his finger in the blood, the priest sprinkles it seven times before the veil, the same inner-sanctuary expiation required when the ritual itself has been vitiated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Blood is put on the horns of the incense altar in the tabernacle and the rest poured at the base of the burnt-offering altar, completing the movement of atonement from sanctuary to court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. All the fat is taken from the bullock and burnt on the altar, the only part of the animal offered on the altar in this rite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The bullock is dealt with exactly as the priest's sin-offering bullock, and by this atonement the congregation is forgiven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The bullock is carried forth without the camp and burnt as the first bullock was, since it is a sin offering for the congregation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. A ruler, whether king, judge, or subordinate, is covered by this law, but his trespass is less serious in character and consequence than that of the high priest or the whole congregation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. When his sin comes to his knowledge, the ruler brings a kid of the goats, a male without blemish, a sin offering of inferior value to the priest's or congregation's bullock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The ruler lays his hand on the goat's head and kills it where the burnt offering is killed, the same laying-on and slaughter as in the other sin offerings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. For a ruler the blood is not carried into the sanctuary but is put by the priest's finger on the horns of the burnt-offering altar and poured at its base. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. All the fat is burnt on the altar as the fat of the peace offerings, and by this atonement the ruler's sin is forgiven, while the carcass, unlike the priest's bullock, is eaten by the priests-in-waiting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. A common person who sins through ignorance is covered by the same law as the ruler, with an offering of a female kid or a ewe-lamb without blemish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. When his sin comes to his knowledge, the common offerer brings a kid of the goats, a female without blemish, for the sin he has sinned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. He lays his hand on the sin offering's head and slays it in the place of the burnt offering, following the ruler's ceremony exactly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The priest puts blood with his finger on the horns of the burnt-offering altar and pours out all the blood at its base, since this sin, unlike the priest's or congregation's, does not taint the sanctuary ritual. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. All the fat is taken away as from the peace offering and burnt for a sweet savour, and by this atonement the common person is forgiven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. If a lamb is brought for a sin offering, it is to be a female without blemish, the same value and character as the female kid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. The offerer lays his hand on the lamb's head and slays it for a sin offering where the burnt offering is killed, under the same ceremony as the ruler's and the common person's kid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. The priest puts blood on the horns of the burnt-offering altar and pours out all the blood at its base, applying the atonement at the place where bloody sacrifices are appointed to be immolated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. The fat is burnt as an offering made by fire, and the sinner is forgiven, not because these sacrifices had intrinsic power to cleanse conscience, but as figurative representations of the full and perfect sin offering to be made by Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
