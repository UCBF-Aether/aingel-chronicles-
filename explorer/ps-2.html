The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 2 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing?

2 The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against the LORD, and against his anointed, saying,

3 Let us break their bands asunder, and cast away their cords from us.

4 He that sitteth in the heavens shall laugh: the LORD shall have them in derision.

5 Then shall he speak unto them in his wrath, and vex them in his sore displeasure.

6 Yet have I set my king upon my holy hill of Zion.

7 I will declare the decree: the LORD hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee.

8 Ask of me, and I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance, and the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession.

9 Thou shalt break them with a rod of iron; thou shalt dash them in pieces like a potter’s vessel.

10 Be wise now therefore, O ye kings: be instructed, ye judges of the earth.

11 Serve the LORD with fear, and rejoice with trembling.

12 Kiss the Son, lest he be angry, and ye perish from the way, when his wrath is kindled but a little. Blessed are all they that put their trust in him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Nations and kings rebel against the Lord and his Anointed, but God on his throne is unmoved, his King already set on Zion. The King declares God's decree of sonship and a kingdom reaching to the ends of the earth, and the psalm closes by urging rulers to submit and trust before wrath comes. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The psalm opens with astonishment at the nations' futile rage: their plotting against God is as vain as imagining what can never stand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Earthly kings and rulers combine against the Lord and his Anointed, the Messiah; JFB notes this is fulfilled in the opposition to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Their cry is for licence: they would break God's bands and cast off his cords, mistaking his government for bondage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. God in heaven laughs, not in cruelty but in sovereign contempt for rebellion that cannot touch his throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. His laughter gives way to wrathful speech: he troubles his enemies with the displeasure their defiance has earned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Yet God's decree stands unmoved: his King is already set on Zion, his holy hill, whatever men resolve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The King declares the decree: the Lord calls him his Son, begotten this day — the eternal Sonship and the resurrection dignity that Henry and JFB both trace here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The Son need only ask, and the nations become his inheritance, to the ends of the earth; his kingdom is universal, not local. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Rebellion will be broken with a rod of iron and dashed like a potter's vessel: judgment is certain, and resistance is brittle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Kings and judges are therefore counselled to be wise and instructed — to learn from the fate of all who fight God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. True service joins fear with trembling joy: reverence without gloom, and gladness without presumption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. To kiss the Son is to submit and honour him before wrath is kindled; those who trust in him, rather than in their own power, are blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
