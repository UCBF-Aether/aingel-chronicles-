The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 30 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The words of Agur the son of Jakeh, even the prophecy: the man spake unto Ithiel, even unto Ithiel and Ucal,

2 Surely I am more brutish than any man, and have not the understanding of a man.

3 I neither learned wisdom, nor have the knowledge of the holy.

4 Who hath ascended up into heaven, or descended? who hath gathered the wind in his fists? who hath bound the waters in a garment? who hath established all the ends of the earth? what is his name, and what is his son’s name, if thou canst tell?

5 Every word of God is pure: he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him.

6 Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar.

7 Two things have I required of thee; deny me them not before I die:

8 Remove far from me vanity and lies: give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with food convenient for me:

9 Lest I be full, and deny thee, and say, Who is the LORD? or lest I be poor, and steal, and take the name of my God in vain.

10 Accuse not a servant unto his master, lest he curse thee, and thou be found guilty.

11 There is a generation that curseth their father, and doth not bless their mother.

12 There is a generation that are pure in their own eyes, and yet is not washed from their filthiness.

13 There is a generation, O how lofty are their eyes! and their eyelids are lifted up.

14 There is a generation, whose teeth are as swords, and their jaw teeth as knives, to devour the poor from off the earth, and the needy from among men.

15 The horseleach hath two daughters, crying, Give, give. There are three things that are never satisfied, yea, four things say not, It is enough:

16 The grave; and the barren womb; the earth that is not filled with water; and the fire that saith not, It is enough.

17 The eye that mocketh at his father, and despiseth to obey his mother, the ravens of the valley shall pick it out, and the young eagles shall eat it.

18 There be three things which are too wonderful for me, yea, four which I know not:

19 The way of an eagle in the air; the way of a serpent upon a rock; the way of a ship in the midst of the sea; and the way of a man with a maid.

20 Such is the way of an adulterous woman; she eateth, and wipeth her mouth, and saith, I have done no wickedness.

21 For three things the earth is disquieted, and for four which it cannot bear:

22 For a servant when he reigneth; and a fool when he is filled with meat;

23 For an odious woman when she is married; and an handmaid that is heir to her mistress.

24 There be four things which are little upon the earth, but they are exceeding wise:

25 The ants are a people not strong, yet they prepare their meat in the summer;

26 The conies are but a feeble folk, yet make they their houses in the rocks;

27 The locusts have no king, yet go they forth all of them by bands;

28 The spider taketh hold with her hands, and is in kings’ palaces.

29 There be three things which go well, yea, four are comely in going:

30 A lion which is strongest among beasts, and turneth not away for any;

31 A greyhound; an he goat also; and a king, against whom there is no rising up.

32 If thou hast done foolishly in lifting up thyself, or if thou hast thought evil, lay thine hand upon thy mouth.

33 Surely the churning of milk bringeth forth butter, and the wringing of the nose bringeth forth blood: so the forcing of wrath bringeth forth strife.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Agur son of Jakeh confesses he is more brutish than any man, has not learned wisdom, and asks who has ascended to heaven or gathered the wind, before affirming that every word of God is pure and nothing may be added to it. His prayer asks for neither poverty nor riches, only convenient food. The rest is numerical wisdom: generations, insatiable things, wonderful ways, little creatures exceeding wise, and stately goings. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The words of Agur the son of Jakeh, even the prophecy, the man spoke unto Ithiel, even unto Ithiel and Ucal, for Agur speaks of himself as wanting righteousness and having done very foolishly, and it becomes us all to have low thoughts of ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Surely I am more brutish than any man, and have not the understanding of a man, for Agur speaks of himself as wanting righteousness and having done very foolishly, and it becomes us all to have low thoughts of ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. I neither learned wisdom, nor have the knowledge of the holy, for Agur speaks of himself as wanting revelation to guide him in truth and wisdom, and the more enlightened people are, the more they lament their ignorance and pray for clearer discoveries of God and his rich grace in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Who has ascended up into heaven, or descended, who has gathered the wind in his fists, who has bound the waters in a garment, who has established all the ends of the earth, what is his name, and what is his son's name, if thou canst tell, for in this verse there is a prophetic notice of him who came down from heaven to be our Instructor and Saviour, and then ascended to be our Advocate, the Messiah distinct from the Father, his name as yet secret, and the great Redeemer in providence and grace cannot be found out to perfection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Every word of God is pure, he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him, for had it not been for Christ, the foundations of the earth had sunk under the load of the curse upon the ground for man's sin, and who, and what is the mighty One who does all this. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar, for there is not the least ground to suspect anything wanting in God's word, and adding to his words opens the way to errors and corruptions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Two things have I required of thee, deny me them not before I die, for Agur wisely prayed for a middle state, that he might be kept at a distance from temptations, asking daily bread suited to his station, family, and real good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Remove far from me vanity and lies, give me neither poverty nor riches, feed me with food convenient for me, for Agur wisely prayed for a middle state, kept from temptations, asking daily bread suited to his station, and his prayer resembles several clauses of the Lord's prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Lest I be full, and deny thee, and say, Who is the LORD, or lest I be poor, and steal, and take the name of my God in vain, for when we consider how those with abundance are prone to abuse the gift, and what it is to suffer want, Agur's prayer will ever be found a wise one, though seldom offered, and our heavenly Father will supply all our need and not suffer us to want anything good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 12. There is a generation pure in their own eyes, and yet not washed from their filthiness, for many persuade themselves they are holy persons, whose hearts are full of sin, and who practise secret wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
