The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hosea 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Ephraim feedeth on wind, and followeth after the east wind: he daily increaseth lies and desolation; and they do make a covenant with the Assyrians, and oil is carried into Egypt.

2 The LORD hath also a controversy with Judah, and will punish Jacob according to his ways; according to his doings will he recompense him.

3 He took his brother by the heel in the womb, and by his strength he had power with God:

4 Yea, he had power over the angel, and prevailed: he wept, and made supplication unto him: he found him in Bethel, and there he spake with us;

5 Even the LORD God of hosts; the LORD is his memorial.

6 Therefore turn thou to thy God: keep mercy and judgment and wait on thy God continually.

7 He is a merchant, the balances of deceit are in his hand: he loveth to oppress.

8 And Ephraim said, Yet I am become rich, I have found me out substance: in all my labours they shall find none iniquity in me that were sin.

9 And I that am the LORD thy God from the land of Egypt will yet make thee to dwell in tabernacles, as in the days of the solemn feast.

10 I have also spoken by the prophets, and I have multiplied visions, and used similitudes, by the ministry of the prophets.

11 Is there iniquity in Gilead? surely they are vanity: they sacrifice bullocks in Gilgal; yea, their altars are as heaps in the furrows of the fields.

12 And Jacob fled into the country of Syria, and Israel served for a wife, and for a wife he kept sheep.

13 And by a prophet the LORD brought Israel out of Egypt, and by a prophet was he preserved.

14 Ephraim provoked him to anger most bitterly: therefore shall he leave his blood upon him, and his reproach shall his LORD return unto him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ephraim feeds on wind and chases the east wind, making covenants with Assyria while oil is carried into Egypt, and the LORD has a controversy with Judah and Jacob too. The chapter retells Jacob's story, grasping his brother's heel, wrestling with God, weeping and supplicating at Bethel, fleeing to Syria and serving for a wife, and draws the lesson: turn to God, keep mercy and judgment, and wait on God continually. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Ephraim feeds on wind, and follows after the east wind, he daily increases lies and desolation, and they do make a covenant with the Assyrians, and oil is carried into Egypt, because the nation feeds on empty alliances, chasing the scorching east wind, and carrying oil as tribute to Egypt while covenanting with Assyria. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The Lord has also a controversy with Judah, and will punish Jacob according to his ways, according to his doings will he recompense him, because the lawsuit reaches Judah too, and Jacob will be repaid according to his ways, not according to his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He took his brother by the heel in the womb, and by his strength he had power with God, because Jacob's life began with grasping and rose to wrestling, and both acts are held up as the pattern of his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Yea, he had power over the angel, and prevailed, he wept, and made supplication unto him, he found him in Bethel, and there he spoke with us, because Jacob prevailed not by strength but by weeping and supplication, and Bethel, where God spoke with him, is where God speaks with his people still. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Even the Lord God of hosts, the Lord is his memorial, because the God who met Jacob is the Lord of hosts, and his name itself is the memorial by which he is to be remembered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Therefore turn thou to thy God, keep mercy and judgment, and wait on thy God continually, because the answer to Jacob's story is return, with mercy and judgment kept, and continual waiting on God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He is a merchant, the balances of deceit are in his hand, he loves to oppress, because Ephraim has become a Canaanite merchant, weighing with false balances and loving the oppression that fills them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. And Ephraim said, yet I am become rich, I have found me out substance, in all my labours they shall find none iniquity in me that were sin, because wealth persuades itself that no iniquity can be found in it, and riches are taken as proof of innocence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And I that am the Lord thy God from the land of Egypt will yet make thee to dwell in tabernacles, as in the days of the solemn feast, because God, who brought them from Egypt, will bring them back to tabernacle life, as in the feast that remembers the wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. I have also spoken by the prophets, and I have multiplied visions, and used similitudes, by the ministry of the prophets, because God did not leave the people without witness, but multiplied visions and parables through prophet after prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Is there iniquity in Gilead, surely they are vanity, they sacrifice bullocks in Gilgal, yea, their altars are as heaps in the furrows of the fields, because Gilead is vanity itself, and Gilgal's altars stand like heaps of stones in the furrows, multiplying wherever the plough goes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And Jacob fled into the country of Syria, and Israel served for a wife, and for a wife he kept sheep, because Jacob's exile in Syria, serving for a wife and keeping sheep, is recalled to show how low the patriarch's beginnings were, and how much God raised him from. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. And by a prophet the Lord brought Israel out of Egypt, and by a prophet was he preserved, because the exodus and the preservation in the wilderness were both works done through a prophet, Moses, and the people owe both to God's prophetic word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Ephraim provoked him to anger most bitterly, therefore shall he leave his blood upon him, and his reproach shall his Lord return unto him, because bitter provocation leaves bloodguilt upon the provoker, and the reproach cast at God will be returned upon Ephraim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Ephraim. the leading tribe of the northern kingdom, used in the prophets for the northern kingdom of Israel itself, as distinct from Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
