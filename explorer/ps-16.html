The Text — Psalms 16 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Preserve me, O God: for in thee do I put my trust.

2 O my soul, thou hast said unto the LORD, Thou art my Lord: my goodness extendeth not to thee;

3 But to the saints that are in the earth, and to the excellent, in whom is all my delight.

4 Their sorrows shall be multiplied that hasten after another god: their drink offerings of blood will I not offer, nor take up their names into my lips.

5 The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance and of my cup: thou maintainest my lot.

6 The lines are fallen unto me in pleasant places; yea, I have a goodly heritage.

7 I will bless the LORD, who hath given me counsel: my reins also instruct me in the night seasons.

8 I have set the LORD always before me: because he is at my right hand, I shall not be moved.

9 Therefore my heart is glad, and my glory rejoiceth: my flesh also shall rest in hope.

10 For thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption.

11 Thou wilt shew me the path of life: in thy presence is fulness of joy; at thy right hand there are pleasures for evermore.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Trust as portion: God himself, not wealth, is the speaker's inheritance and goodly heritage. He delights in the saints, refuses idolatry, sets the Lord always before him, and ends on the path of life, fullness of joy in God's presence. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Preserve me, O God, for in you I put my trust: the prayer is short because trust itself is the argument. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. David says to the Lord, You are my Lord; my goodness extends not to you: God gains nothing from our good deeds, which is why verse 3 turns to the saints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. His delight is in the saints on earth, the excellent ones: goodness given to God is shown in love for his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Sorrows shall be multiplied for those who hasten after another god; their drink offerings of blood he will not offer, nor take their names on his lips: idolatry brings multiplied grief, and the godly refuse even its rituals and names. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Lord is the portion of his inheritance and of his cup, and maintains his lot: God himself, not land or wealth, is David's share. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The lines fall to him in pleasant places, and he has a goodly heritage: the lot God measures out is good, even when it is not the world's lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He blesses the Lord who gives him counsel, and his reins instruct him in the night: God's guidance continues in quiet, sleepless hours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He sets the Lord always before him, and because God is at his right hand he shall not be moved: constant awareness of God's presence is the secret of steadfastness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Therefore his heart is glad, his glory rejoices, and his flesh rests in hope: joy reaches body as well as soul, because hope is sure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. God will not leave his soul in hell, nor suffer his Holy One to see corruption: Peter and Paul apply this to Christ's resurrection, of which David's deliverance was a shadow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. God will show him the path of life; in his presence is fullness of joy, and at his right hand pleasures for evermore: the end of trust is not mere survival but endless joy in God's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages
