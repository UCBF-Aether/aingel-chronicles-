Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 But it came to pass within a while after, in the time of wheat harvest, that Samson visited his wife with a kid; and he said, I will go in to my wife into the chamber. But her father would not suffer him to go in.

2 And her father said, I verily thought that thou hadst utterly hated her; therefore I gave her to thy companion: is not her younger sister fairer than she? take her, I pray thee, instead of her.

3 And Samson said concerning them, Now shall I be more blameless than the Philistines, though I do them a displeasure.

4 And Samson went and caught three hundred foxes, and took firebrands, and turned tail to tail, and put a firebrand in the midst between two tails.

5 And when he had set the brands on fire, he let them go into the standing corn of the Philistines, and burnt up both the shocks, and also the standing corn, with the vineyards and olives.

6 Then the Philistines said, Who hath done this? And they answered, Samson, the son in law of the Timnite, because he had taken his wife, and given her to his companion. And the Philistines came up, and burnt her and her father with fire.

7 And Samson said unto them, Though ye have done this, yet will I be avenged of you, and after that I will cease.

8 And he smote them hip and thigh with a great slaughter: and he went down and dwelt in the top of the rock Etam.

9 Then the Philistines went up, and pitched in Judah, and spread themselves in Lehi.

10 And the men of Judah said, Why are ye come up against us? And they answered, To bind Samson are we come up, to do to him as he hath done to us.

11 Then three thousand men of Judah went to the top of the rock Etam, and said to Samson, Knowest thou not that the Philistines are rulers over us? what is this that thou hast done unto us? And he said unto them, As they did unto me, so have I done unto them.

12 And they said unto him, We are come down to bind thee, that we may deliver thee into the hand of the Philistines. And Samson said unto them, Swear unto me, that ye will not fall upon me yourselves.

13 And they spake unto him, saying, No; but we will bind thee fast, and deliver thee into their hand: but surely we will not kill thee. And they bound him with two new cords, and brought him up from the rock.

14 And when he came unto Lehi, the Philistines shouted against him: and the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him, and the cords that were upon his arms became as flax that was burnt with fire, and his bands loosed from off his hands.

15 And he found a new jawbone of an ass, and put forth his hand, and took it, and slew a thousand men therewith.

16 And Samson said, With the jawbone of an ass, heaps upon heaps, with the jaw of an ass have I slain a thousand men.

17 And it came to pass, when he had made an end of speaking, that he cast away the jawbone out of his hand, and called that place Ramathlehi.

18 And he was sore athirst, and called on the LORD, and said, Thou hast given this great deliverance into the hand of thy servant: and now shall I die for thirst, and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised?

19 But God clave an hollow place that was in the jaw, and there came water thereout; and when he had drunk, his spirit came again, and he revived: wherefore he called the name thereof Enhakkore, which is in Lehi unto this day.

20 And he judged Israel in the days of the Philistines twenty years.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In wheat harvest Samson visits his wife with a kid and finds her given to his companion, so he ties three hundred foxes, or jackals, tail to tail with firebrands and burns the Philistines' standing corn, vineyards and olives; the Philistines in revenge burn his wife and her father. Samson smites them hip and thigh, withdraws to the cleft of Etam, and is bound with new cords by three thousand men of Judah and delivered to Lehi, where the Spirit comes on him, the cords fall like burnt flax, and he slays a thousand men with a new jawbone of an ass, then drinks from the hollow place God opens and calls it En-hakkore. He judges Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In wheat harvest time Samson visited his wife with a kid, intending to go into her chamber, and JFB notes the harvest season left grain in a most combustible state, and the kid was a visitor's present, perhaps a token of reconciliation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Her father would not let him in, saying he had thought Samson utterly hated her and had given her to his companion, offering the younger sister instead, and JFB calls the allegation a mere sham and pretext, and the sister proposal an insult unlawful for an Israelite to accept. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Samson said he would now be more blameless than the Philistines though he did them a displeasure, and JFB notes this nefarious conduct provoked his just indignation and a resolve to take signal vengeance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He caught three hundred foxes, or rather jackals, which prowl in large packs in Palestine's mountains, and tied them tail to tail with firebrands between them, and JFB explains the slow-burning torches would blaze when blown by wind, and the pained animals would kindle one great conflagration in the Philistine corn plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He let them go into the Philistines' standing corn, and burnt up both the shocks and the standing corn with vineyards and olives, the general devastation and panic leaving no neighbour able to help another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The Philistines asked who had done this, learned Samson had done it because his father-in-law had given his wife to his companion, and burnt her and her father with fire, and JFB calls this remarkable retribution, since to avoid that very menace she had betrayed her husband. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Samson said that though they had done this he would yet be avenged of them, and then cease, and JFB notes that by this act the Philistines had been instruments in avenging his private wrong, but as divinely appointed judge his work of retribution was not yet accomplished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He smote them hip and thigh with a great slaughter, a proverbial expression for merciless slaughter, and then dwelt in the cleft of the rock Etam, a cave in the cliff. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The Philistines went up and spread themselves in Lehi, now El-Lekieh among limestone cliffs and caves, to apprehend Samson in revenge, and JFB notes Samson allowed himself to be bound and surrendered to free his countrymen from danger, then with a new jawbone of an ass slew a thousand men at Ramath-lehi, the hill of the jawbone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The men of Judah asked why the Philistines had come up, and were told, to bind Samson and do to him as he had done to them, stating the expedition's purpose plainly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Three thousand men of Judah went to Etam and asked Samson whether he knew the Philistines ruled over them, and what he had done, and he answered, As they did to me, so have I done to them, claiming his vengeance as requital. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They said they had come to bind him and deliver him to the Philistines, and asked him to swear they would not fall upon him themselves, and JFB's account of his willing surrender explains why he accepted bonds from his own countrymen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They bound him with two new cords and brought him up from the rock, and the new cords would make the coming breaking of them the more evidently miraculous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. When he came to Lehi the Philistines shouted against him, the Spirit of the Lord came mightily upon him, and the cords on his arms became as flax burnt with fire, and his bands loosed from his hands, the prelude to the jawbone slaughter JFB describes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He found a new jawbone of an ass, took it, and slew a thousand men with it, the weapon JFB notes as the instrument of the slaughter at Ramath-lehi. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Samson sang, With the jawbone of an ass, heaps upon heaps, with the jaw of an ass have I slain a thousand men, and JFB notes the weapon's inadequacy plainly shows a miraculous feat of supernatural strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. When he had finished speaking he cast away the jawbone and called the place Ramath-lehi, the hill of the jawbone, naming the battlefield after its weapon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Sore athirst, he called on the Lord, saying he had given this great deliverance, and now he would die for thirst and fall into the uncircumcised, and JFB notes his exhausted strength was about to be recruited by a refreshing draft, in a prayer that records his piety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. God clave a hollow place in the jaw, that is, in Lehi, and water came out, and when Samson drank his spirit returned, and he called the place En-hakkore, the supplication well, which JFB notes records the piety of this heroic champion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Samson judged Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines, closing the account of a judge whose deliverance, as chapter 13 foretold, was only begun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
