The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 A good name is rather to be chosen than great riches, and loving favour rather than silver and gold.

2 The rich and poor meet together: the LORD is the maker of them all.

3 A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished.

4 By humility and the fear of the LORD are riches, and honour, and life.

5 Thorns and snares are in the way of the froward: he that doth keep his soul shall be far from them.

6 Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.

7 The rich ruleth over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender.

8 He that soweth iniquity shall reap vanity: and the rod of his anger shall fail.

9 He that hath a bountiful eye shall be blessed; for he giveth of his bread to the poor.

10 Cast out the scorner, and contention shall go out; yea, strife and reproach shall cease.

11 He that loveth pureness of heart, for the grace of his lips the king shall be his friend.

12 The eyes of the LORD preserve knowledge, and he overthroweth the words of the transgressor.

13 The slothful man saith, There is a lion without, I shall be slain in the streets.

14 The mouth of strange women is a deep pit: he that is abhorred of the LORD shall fall therein.

15 Foolishness is bound in the heart of a child; but the rod of correction shall drive it far from him.

16 He that oppresseth the poor to increase his riches, and he that giveth to the rich, shall surely come to want.

17 Bow down thine ear, and hear the words of the wise, and apply thine heart unto my knowledge.

18 For it is a pleasant thing if thou keep them within thee; they shall withal be fitted in thy lips.

19 That thy trust may be in the LORD, I have made known to thee this day, even to thee.

20 Have not I written to thee excellent things in counsels and knowledge,

21 That I might make thee know the certainty of the words of truth; that thou mightest answer the words of truth to them that send unto thee?

22 Rob not the poor, because he is poor: neither oppress the afflicted in the gate:

23 For the LORD will plead their cause, and spoil the soul of those that spoiled them.

24 Make no friendship with an angry man; and with a furious man thou shalt not go:

25 Lest thou learn his ways, and get a snare to thy soul.

26 Be not thou one of them that strike hands, or of them that are sureties for debts.

27 If thou hast nothing to pay, why should he take away thy bed from under thee?

28 Remove not the ancient landmark, which thy fathers have set.

29 Seest thou a man diligent in his business? he shall stand before kings; he shall not stand before mean men.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A good name is to be chosen over great riches, and rich and poor meet together because the LORD made them all. The chapter teaches children, warns the borrower who becomes servant to the lender, and casts out the scorner so contention leaves with him. At verse 17 it turns to direct address: hear the words of the wise, trust in the LORD, rob not the poor, remove not the ancient landmark, and be diligent enough to stand before kings. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. A good name is rather to be chosen than great riches, and loving favour rather than silver and gold, for we should be more careful to get and keep a good name than to raise a great estate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The rich and poor meet together, and the LORD is the maker of them all, for Providence has ordered some rich and others poor, yet all are guilty before God, and at grace's throne the poor are as welcome as the rich. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. A prudent man foresees the evil and hides himself, but the simple pass on and are punished, for faith foresees evil coming upon sinners and looks to Jesus Christ as the sure refuge from the storm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. By humility and the fear of the LORD are riches, honour, and life, for where God's fear is, there will be humility, and much is to be enjoyed by it: spiritual riches and eternal life at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Thorns and snares are in the way of the froward, but he who keeps his soul shall be far from them, for sin's way is vexatious and dangerous, while duty's way is safe and easy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Train up a child in the way he should go, and when old he will not depart from it, for children must be trained not in the way they would go, of corrupt hearts, but in the way they should go, and as soon as possible led to knowledge of the Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender, showing how important it is to keep out of debt, though the poor should remember it is the Lord who made the difference. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. He who sows iniquity shall reap vanity, and the rod of his anger shall fail, for the power which many abuse will soon fail them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. He who has a bountiful eye shall be blessed, for he gives of his bread to the poor, for he who seeks to relieve others' wants and miseries shall be blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Cast out the scorner, and contention shall go out, and strife and reproach shall cease, for profane scoffers and revilers disturb the peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. He who loves pureness of heart, for the grace of his lips the king shall be his friend, for God will be the Friend of a man in whose spirit there is no guile, an honour all saints have. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The mouth of strange women is a deep pit, and he abhorred of the LORD shall fall therein, for the vile sin of licentiousness commonly besots the mind beyond recovery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Foolishness is bound in the heart of a child, but the rod of correction shall drive it far from him, for sin is foolishness in the heart, an inward inclination children bring into the world, cleaving close to the soul, and we all need correction by our heavenly Father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 24. Make no friendship with an angry man, and with a furious man thou shalt not go, for our corrupt hearts have so much tinder that it is dangerous to deal with those throwing about passion's sparks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Lest thou learn his ways, and get a snare to thy soul, for corrupt hearts have so much tinder that dealing with those throwing passion's sparks is dangerous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Be not thou one of them that strike hands, or of them that are sureties for debts, for every man ought to be just to himself and his family, and those are not who waste what they have by folly or carelessness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. If thou hast nothing to pay, why should he take away thy bed from under thee, for every man ought to be just to himself and his family, not wasting what he has by folly or carelessness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Remove not the ancient landmark which thy fathers have set, for we are taught not to trespass on another man's right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
