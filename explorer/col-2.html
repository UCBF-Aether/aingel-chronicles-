The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Colossians 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 For I would that ye knew what great conflict I have for you, and for them at Laodicea, and for as many as have not seen my face in the flesh;

2 That their hearts might be comforted, being knit together in love, and unto all riches of the full assurance of understanding, to the acknowledgement of the mystery of God, and of the Father, and of Christ;

3 In whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.

4 And this I say, lest any man should beguile you with enticing words.

5 For though I be absent in the flesh, yet am I with you in the spirit, joying and beholding your order, and the stedfastness of your faith in Christ.

6 As ye have therefore received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk ye in him:

7 Rooted and built up in him, and stablished in the faith, as ye have been taught, abounding therein with thanksgiving.

8 Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ.

9 For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily.

10 And ye are complete in him, which is the head of all principality and power:

11 In whom also ye are circumcised with the circumcision made without hands, in putting off the body of the sins of the flesh by the circumcision of Christ:

12 Buried with him in baptism, wherein also ye are risen with him through the faith of the operation of God, who hath raised him from the dead.

13 And you, being dead in your sins and the uncircumcision of your flesh, hath he quickened together with him, having forgiven you all trespasses;

14 Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross;

15 And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it.

16 Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days:

17 Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.

18 Let no man beguile you of your reward in a voluntary humility and worshipping of angels, intruding into those things which he hath not seen, vainly puffed up by his fleshly mind,

19 And not holding the Head, from which all the body by joints and bands having nourishment ministered, and knit together, increaseth with the increase of God.

20 Wherefore if ye be dead with Christ from the rudiments of the world, why, as though living in the world, are ye subject to ordinances,

21 (Touch not; taste not; handle not;

22 Which all are to perish with the using;) after the commandments and doctrines of men?

23 Which things have indeed a shew of wisdom in will worship, and humility, and neglecting of the body: not in any honour to the satisfying of the flesh.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul warns the Colossians not to be captured by philosophy and empty deceit, or judged over food, festivals and Sabbaths, or beguiled by angel worship and harsh treatment of the body. They are complete in Christ, circumcised without hands, buried and raised with him in baptism, the record of debt against them cancelled and nailed to the cross, while the old rules are only a shadow of things to come. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul would have them know what great conflict he had for them, for Laodicea, and for all who had not seen his face, since a soul prospers when it has clear knowledge of the truth as it is in Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. His aim was that their hearts might be comforted, knit together in love, and brought to the full assurance of understanding in acknowledging the mystery of God, of the Father, and of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. In Christ are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge, hid not from believers but for them, displayed in his person and redemption though hidden from proud unbelievers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Paul warned them lest any man beguile them with enticing words, since many are ruined by the false disguises and fair appearances of evil principles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Though absent in the flesh, Paul was with them in spirit, joying and beholding their order and the stedfastness of their faith in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. As they had received Christ Jesus the Lord, consenting to him and taking him for theirs, so they were to walk in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They were to be rooted and built up in him and stablished in the faith as taught, abounding therein with thanksgiving, since no one can be built up in Christ without first being founded upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. They were to beware lest any spoil them through philosophy and vain deceit after the tradition of men and the rudiments of the world, not after Christ, since vain philosophy pleases men's fancies while hindering their faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. In Christ dwells all the fulness of the Godhead bodily, so believers have in him the substance of all the shadows of the ceremonial law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Believers are complete in him, who is the head of all principality and power, and Henry notes that to be complete is to be furnished with all things necessary for salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. In him they are circumcised with the circumcision made without hands, putting off the body of the sins of the flesh, which Henry reads as the circumcision of the heart and crucifixion of the flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Buried with him in baptism, they are risen with him through faith in the operation of God who raised him from the dead, setting forth death and burial to sin and resurrection to newness of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Though dead in sins and the uncircumcision of their flesh, they have been quickened together with him, having all trespasses forgiven, since Christ's death was the death of their sins and his resurrection the quickening of their souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The handwriting of ordinances against them, a yoke to the Jews and a partition-wall to the Gentiles, was blotted out and taken out of the way, nailed to his cross, now that the substance had come and the shadows fled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a show of them openly, triumphing over them in the cross, so that the devil and all the powers of hell were conquered and disarmed by the dying Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Let no man therefore judge them in meat or drink, or in respect of a holyday, new moon, or sabbath days, since bigoted judgments about such things troubled believers needlessly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Those rites were a shadow of things to come, but the body is of Christ, and all the defects of the ceremonial law are made up in the gospel by his complete sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Let no man beguile them of their reward in voluntary humility and worshipping of angels, intruding into unseen things and vainly puffed up by a fleshly mind, since such seeming humility really hides pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Those who worship angels do not hold the Head, from whom the whole body, knit together by joints and bands, grows with the increase of God, and it is an insult to Christ, the only Mediator, to use any intercessors but him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. If they are dead with Christ from the rudiments of the world, why should they, as though living in the world, be subject to ordinances, since true believers cannot live in the fashions of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The ordinances of touch not, taste not, handle not are the commandments and doctrines of men, and Henry notes that what the Lord has left indifferent should be left to like freedom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Those things are all to perish with the using, and the commandments and doctrines of men cannot stand against the appointments of the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Such things have indeed a show of wisdom in will-worship and humility and neglecting of the body, but they are not in any honour to God, tending only to gratify self-will, self-wisdom, and self-righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • handwriting of ordinances. the written record of debt standing against its readers, which the chapter says was blotted out and nailed to the cross (verse 14) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
  • principalities and powers. spiritual powers which the chapter says Christ disarmed and triumphed over openly (verse 15) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
