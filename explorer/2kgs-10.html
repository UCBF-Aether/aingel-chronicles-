The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Ahab had seventy sons in Samaria. And Jehu wrote letters, and sent to Samaria, unto the rulers of Jezreel, to the elders, and to them that brought up Ahab’s children, saying,

2 Now as soon as this letter cometh to you, seeing your master’s sons are with you, and there are with you chariots and horses, a fenced city also, and armour;

3 Look even out the best and meetest of your master’s sons, and set him on his father’s throne, and fight for your master’s house.

4 But they were exceedingly afraid, and said, Behold, two kings stood not before him: how then shall we stand?

5 And he that was over the house, and he that was over the city, the elders also, and the bringers up of the children, sent to Jehu, saying, We are thy servants, and will do all that thou shalt bid us; we will not make any king: do thou that which is good in thine eyes.

6 Then he wrote a letter the second time to them, saying, If ye be mine, and if ye will hearken unto my voice, take ye the heads of the men your master’s sons, and come to me to Jezreel by to morrow this time. Now the king’s sons, being seventy persons, were with the great men of the city, which brought them up.

7 And it came to pass, when the letter came to them, that they took the king’s sons, and slew seventy persons, and put their heads in baskets, and sent him them to Jezreel.

8 And there came a messenger, and told him, saying, They have brought the heads of the king’s sons. And he said, Lay ye them in two heaps at the entering in of the gate until the morning.

9 And it came to pass in the morning, that he went out, and stood, and said to all the people, Ye be righteous: behold, I conspired against my master, and slew him: but who slew all these?

10 Know now that there shall fall unto the earth nothing of the word of the LORD, which the LORD spake concerning the house of Ahab: for the LORD hath done that which he spake by his servant Elijah.

11 So Jehu slew all that remained of the house of Ahab in Jezreel, and all his great men, and his kinsfolks, and his priests, until he left him none remaining.

12 And he arose and departed, and came to Samaria. And as he was at the shearing house in the way,

13 Jehu met with the brethren of Ahaziah king of Judah, and said, Who are ye? And they answered, We are the brethren of Ahaziah; and we go down to salute the children of the king and the children of the queen.

14 And he said, Take them alive. And they took them alive, and slew them at the pit of the shearing house, even two and forty men; neither left he any of them.

15 And when he was departed thence, he lighted on Jehonadab the son of Rechab coming to meet him: and he saluted him, and said to him, Is thine heart right, as my heart is with thy heart? And Jehonadab answered, It is. If it be, give me thine hand. And he gave him his hand; and he took him up to him into the chariot.

16 And he said, Come with me, and see my zeal for the LORD. So they made him ride in his chariot.

17 And when he came to Samaria, he slew all that remained unto Ahab in Samaria, till he had destroyed him, according to the saying of the LORD, which he spake to Elijah.

18 And Jehu gathered all the people together, and said unto them, Ahab served Baal a little; but Jehu shall serve him much.

19 Now therefore call unto me all the prophets of Baal, all his servants, and all his priests; let none be wanting: for I have a great sacrifice to do to Baal; whosoever shall be wanting, he shall not live. But Jehu did it in subtilty, to the intent that he might destroy the worshippers of Baal.

20 And Jehu said, Proclaim a solemn assembly for Baal. And they proclaimed it.

21 And Jehu sent through all Israel: and all the worshippers of Baal came, so that there was not a man left that came not. And they came into the house of Baal; and the house of Baal was full from one end to another.

22 And he said unto him that was over the vestry, Bring forth vestments for all the worshippers of Baal. And he brought them forth vestments.

23 And Jehu went, and Jehonadab the son of Rechab, into the house of Baal, and said unto the worshippers of Baal, Search, and look that there be here with you none of the servants of the LORD, but the worshippers of Baal only.

24 And when they went in to offer sacrifices and burnt offerings, Jehu appointed fourscore men without, and said, If any of the men whom I have brought into your hands escape, he that letteth him go, his life shall be for the life of him.

25 And it came to pass, as soon as he had made an end of offering the burnt offering, that Jehu said to the guard and to the captains, Go in, and slay them; let none come forth. And they smote them with the edge of the sword; and the guard and the captains cast them out, and went to the city of the house of Baal.

26 And they brought forth the images out of the house of Baal, and burned them.

27 And they brake down the image of Baal, and brake down the house of Baal, and made it a draught house unto this day.

28 Thus Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel.

29 Howbeit from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin, Jehu departed not from after them, to wit, the golden calves that were in Bethel, and that were in Dan.

30 And the LORD said unto Jehu, Because thou hast done well in executing that which is right in mine eyes, and hast done unto the house of Ahab according to all that was in mine heart, thy children of the fourth generation shall sit on the throne of Israel.

31 But Jehu took no heed to walk in the law of the LORD God of Israel with all his heart: for he departed not from the sins of Jeroboam, which made Israel to sin.

32 In those days the LORD began to cut Israel short: and Hazael smote them in all the coasts of Israel;

33 From Jordan eastward, all the land of Gilead, the Gadites, and the Reubenites, and the Manassites, from Aroer, which is by the river Arnon, even Gilead and Bashan.

34 Now the rest of the acts of Jehu, and all that he did, and all his might, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

35 And Jehu slept with his fathers: and they buried him in Samaria. And Jehoahaz his son reigned in his stead.

36 And the time that Jehu reigned over Israel in Samaria was twenty and eight years.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jehu wipes out Ahab's house: seventy heads sent to Jezreel, Ahaziah's kinsmen slain, and Baal's worshippers gathered into their temple and slaughtered, the temple itself made a draught-house. Jehu is commended for the deed but not for walking in God's law, and Hazael begins to cut Israel short. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Ahab had seventy sons in Samaria, and Jehu wrote letters to Samaria's rulers, elders, and the bringers-up of Ahab's children. JFB notes the seventy probably comprehended Ahab's whole posterity, assembled in the capital, and Jehu's challenge was a stroke of policy to discover whether they were pacific or hostile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Jehu's letter pointed out that his master's sons were with them, along with chariots, horses, a fenced city, and armour. JFB notes the tenor of the letters suggests their first intention was to select the fittest royal and set him up as king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Jehu challenged them to choose the best and meetest of their master's sons, set him on his father's throne, and fight for their master's house. JFB presents this challenge as designed to elicit their views and test their disposition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They were exceedingly afraid, saying two kings had not stood before Jehu, so how could they stand? JFB notes Jehu's bold character and rapid success terrified Samaria's and Jezreel's civic authorities into submission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The governor of the house, the governor of the city, the elders, and the bringers-up of the children sent to Jehu saying they were his servants and would do as he bid. JFB explains the bringers-up were principal grandees charged with the support and education of royal princes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Jehu's second letter demanded that if they were his and would hearken, they should take the heads of their master's sons and come to him at Jezreel by tomorrow. JFB notes the barbarous practice of a successful usurper slaughtering all who might claim the throne, frequently exemplified in Eastern history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. When the letter came, they took the king's sons, slew seventy persons, and put their heads in baskets to send to Jezreel. JFB's section on the beheading of Ahab's seventy children frames this wholesale slaughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. A messenger told Jehu the heads of the king's sons had been brought, and Jehu ordered them laid in two heaps at the gate's entrance until morning. JFB notes the exhibition of enemies' heads at a gate was always considered a glorious trophy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. In the morning Jehu told the people they were righteous, for he had conspired against his master and slain him, but who had slain all these? JFB observes Jehu's speech was artfully framed to impress the crowd that the massacre was the result of divine judgments on Ahab's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Jehu declared nothing of the LORD's word spoken against Ahab's house would fall to the earth, for the LORD had done what he spoke by Elijah. JFB notes the speech prepared the public mind to hear, without horror, of the further extinction of Ahab's friends and supporters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Jehu slew all that remained of Ahab's house in Jezreel, with his great men, kinsfolks, and priests, leaving him none remaining. JFB presents this as the further revolting tragedy prepared for by Jehu's speech: the extinction of all influential supporters of Ahab's dynasty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Jehu departed to Samaria, and at the shearing house in the way he met company. JFB's section carries the purge from Jezreel toward Samaria. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Jehu met the brethren of Ahaziah king of Judah, who said they were going down to salute the king's children and the queen's children. JFB explains they were not full but step-brothers, sons of Jehoram by concubines, ignorant of the revolution and travelling to visit royal relatives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Jehu ordered them taken alive, and they were slain at the pit of the shearing house, forty-two men, none left. JFB notes they were seized and put to death for fear they might stimulate the party still faithful to Ahab's dynasty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Jehu met Jehonadab son of Rechab, saluted him, and asked whether his heart was right as Jehu's heart was with his, and Jehonadab said it was. JFB describes Jehonadab as a man of piety and simple primitive life, highly esteemed and influential, whose friendship Jehu saw would advantage his cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Jehu said, "Come with me, and see my zeal for the LORD," and made Jehonadab ride in his chariot. JFB notes Jehu paid him the distinguished attention of a seat in his chariot, and the joining of hands was the recognized mode of striking a league and testifying fealty to a new sovereign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. When Jehu came to Samaria he slew all that remained unto Ahab there, till he had destroyed him according to the LORD's word to Elijah. JFB's section closes the purge of Ahab's house with this completion in Samaria. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Jehu gathered the people and said Ahab had served Baal a little, but Jehu would serve him much. JFB presents this as the opening of Jehu's deep-laid plot to destroy Baal's worshippers, done from political motives to secure the party that worshipped God in Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Jehu called for all Baal's prophets, servants, and priests, letting none be wanting, on pain of death. JFB notes Baal's votaries were classified as prophets, priests, and servants generally, and could easily be convened in one spacious temple, their number already diminished by Elijah's and Elisha's ministries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Jehu proclaimed a solemn assembly for Baal, and it was proclaimed. JFB explains the solemn sacrifice in Baal's honour was a deep-laid plot for the worshippers' extinction, in harmony with the Mosaic law and worthy of a constitutional king of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Jehu sent through all Israel, and all Baal's worshippers came, so the house of Baal was full from one end to another. JFB notes the summons gathered every worshipper into the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Jehu told the keeper of the vestry to bring forth vestments for all Baal's worshippers, and he brought them out. JFB explains Baal's priests were clad in robes of white byssus kept in a particular wardrobe under an officer's care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Jehu and Jehonadab went into Baal's house and told the worshippers to look that none of the LORD's servants were among them, but only Baal's worshippers. JFB presents this search as part of the plot's execution, ensuring only Baalites would be trapped. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. When they went in to offer sacrifices, Jehu appointed fourscore men outside, charging that if any escaped, the life of him who let him go would answer for it. JFB parallels this treacherous massacre's means with terrible tragedies in later Eastern history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. As soon as the burnt offering ended, Jehu ordered his guard and captains to go in, slay them, and let none come forth, casting them out. JFB's section "He destroys the worshippers of Baal" records this slaughter as the plot's fulfilment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. They brought out the images from Baal's house and burned them. JFB notes the temple's destruction completed the extinction of Baal worship in Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. They broke down Baal's image and house, making it a draught house to this day. JFB presents this desecration as the final end of Baal's house in Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Thus Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel. JFB's section closes with this summary of the purge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Howbeit Jehu departed not from the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin, keeping the golden calves in Bethel and Dan. JFB explains Jehu had no intention of carrying his zeal beyond a certain point, and, considering it impolitic to let subjects travel to Jerusalem, re-established the symbolic calf worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Jehu took no heed to walk in the law of the LORD God of Israel with all his heart, for he departed not from Jeroboam's sins. JFB's note on verse 29 applies here: Jehu's zeal stopped short, and he kept the calves for political reasons rather than following God's law fully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
