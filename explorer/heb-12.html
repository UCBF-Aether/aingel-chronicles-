The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hebrews 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us,

2 Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God.

3 For consider him that endured such contradiction of sinners against himself, lest ye be wearied and faint in your minds.

4 Ye have not yet resisted unto blood, striving against sin.

5 And ye have forgotten the exhortation which speaketh unto you as unto children, My son, despise not thou the chastening of the Lord, nor faint when thou art rebuked of him:

6 For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.

7 If ye endure chastening, God dealeth with you as with sons; for what son is he whom the father chasteneth not?

8 But if ye be without chastisement, whereof all are partakers, then are ye bastards, and not sons.

9 Furthermore we have had fathers of our flesh which corrected us, and we gave them reverence: shall we not much rather be in subjection unto the Father of spirits, and live?

10 For they verily for a few days chastened us after their own pleasure; but he for our profit, that we might be partakers of his holiness.

11 Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.

12 Wherefore lift up the hands which hang down, and the feeble knees;

13 And make straight paths for your feet, lest that which is lame be turned out of the way; but let it rather be healed.

14 Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord:

15 Looking diligently lest any man fail of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you, and thereby many be defiled;

16 Lest there be any fornicator, or profane person, as Esau, who for one morsel of meat sold his birthright.

17 For ye know how that afterward, when he would have inherited the blessing, he was rejected: for he found no place of repentance, though he sought it carefully with tears.

18 For ye are not come unto the mount that might be touched, and that burned with fire, nor unto blackness, and darkness, and tempest,

19 And the sound of a trumpet, and the voice of words; which voice they that heard intreated that the word should not be spoken to them any more:

20 (For they could not endure that which was commanded, And if so much as a beast touch the mountain, it shall be stoned, or thrust through with a dart:

21 And so terrible was the sight, that Moses said, I exceedingly fear and quake:)

22 But ye are come unto mount Sion, and unto the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to an innumerable company of angels,

23 To the general assembly and church of the firstborn, which are written in heaven, and to God the Judge of all, and to the spirits of just men made perfect,

24 And to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling, that speaketh better things than that of Abel.

25 See that ye refuse not him that speaketh. For if they escaped not who refused him that spake on earth, much more shall not we escape, if we turn away from him that speaketh from heaven:

26 Whose voice then shook the earth: but now he hath promised, saying, Yet once more I shake not the earth only, but also heaven.

27 And this word, Yet once more, signifieth the removing of those things that are shaken, as of things that are made, that those things which cannot be shaken may remain.

28 Wherefore we receiving a kingdom which cannot be moved, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear:

29 For our God is a consuming fire.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, readers are to lay aside every weight and the sin that clings, and run with endurance, looking to Jesus, author and finisher of faith, who endured the cross for the joy set before him. Hardship is presented as a father's discipline of sons, not abandonment; Esau's example warns against trading birthright for a meal; and readers have come not to terrifying Sinai but to Mount Zion, the heavenly Jerusalem, receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us — persevering obedience of faith in Christ is the race set before the Hebrews and before us, wherein we must either win the crown of glory or have everlasting misery; by the easily-besetting sin understand the sin to which we are most prone, which, while unsubdued, hinders the race, taking every motive for running and giving power to every discouragement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God — by stedfastly looking to Jesus, our thoughts strengthen holy affections and keep under carnal desires; let us frequently consider him, for what are our little trials to his agonies, or even to our deserts? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. For consider him that endured such contradiction of sinners against himself, lest ye be wearied and faint in your minds — when weary and faint in mind, recollect that holy Jesus suffered to save us from eternal misery; there is a proneness in believers to grow weary and faint under trials, from imperfection of grace and remains of corruption, but Christians should not faint under them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Ye have not yet resisted unto blood, striving against sin — you have not yet resisted unto blood, striving against sin, so your trials fall short of the utmost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. And ye have forgotten the exhortation which speaketh unto you as unto children, My son, despise not thou the chastening of the Lord, nor faint when thou art rebuked of him — do not make light of afflictions and be without feeling under them, for they are the hand and rod of God and his rebukes for sin; nor despond and sink under trials, nor fret and repine, but bear up with faith and patience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth — though enemies and persecutors may inflict sufferings, they are Divine chastisements, and our heavenly Father has his hand in all, with a wise end to answer; God may let others alone in their sins, but he will correct sin in his own children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. If ye endure chastening, God dealeth with you as with sons; for what son is he whom the father chasteneth not? — if you endure chastening, God deals with you as sons, for what son is there whom the father does not chasten? In this he acts as becomes a father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. But if ye be without chastisement, whereof all are partakers, then are ye bastards, and not sons — if you are without chastisement, of which all sons are partakers, then you are bastards and not sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Furthermore we have had fathers of our flesh which corrected us, and we gave them reverence: shall we not much rather be in subjection unto the Father of spirits, and live? — our earthly parents sometimes chastened to gratify passion rather than reform manners, but the Father of our souls never willingly grieves or afflicts his children, and it is always for our profit; shall we not much rather be subject to him and live? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. For they verily for a few days chastened us after their own pleasure; but he for our profit, that we might be partakers of his holiness — earthly fathers chastened for a few days after their own pleasure, but God chastens for our profit, that we might partake of his holiness; God's correction is not condemnation, and chastening borne with patience greatly promotes holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby — no chastening seems joyous for the present, but grievous; nevertheless afterward it yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness to those exercised by it; our whole life here is a state of childhood, imperfect in spiritual things, so we must submit to its discipline, and when we come to a perfect state we shall be fully reconciled to all God's chastisement now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Wherefore lift up the hands which hang down, and the feeble knees — a burden of affliction is apt to make hands hang down and knees feeble, dispiriting and discouraging the Christian; against this he must strive, that he may better run his spiritual race. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. And make straight paths for your feet, lest that which is lame be turned out of the way; but let it rather be healed — make straight paths for your feet, lest the lame be turned out of the way, but rather be healed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord — faith and patience enable believers to follow peace and holiness, as a man follows his calling constantly, diligently, and with pleasure; peace with men of all sects and parties favours our pursuit of holiness, and there can be no right peace without holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Looking diligently lest any man fail of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you, and thereby many be defiled — look diligently lest anyone fail of God's true grace, for where that fails, corruption prevails and breaks forth; beware lest any unmortified lust, seeming dead, spring up to trouble and disturb the whole body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Lest there be any fornicator, or profane person, as Esau, who for one morsel of meat sold his birthright — falling away from Christ is the fruit of preferring the delights of the flesh to God's blessing and the heavenly inheritance, as Esau did for one morsel of meat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. For ye know how that afterward, when he would have inherited the blessing, he was rejected: for he found no place of repentance, though he sought it carefully with tears — afterward Esau, seeking the blessing carefully with tears, was rejected and found no place of repentance; sinners will not always have such mean thoughts of the Divine blessing as they now have, yet God will neither sever the means from the blessing nor join the blessing with satisfying man's lusts, though mercy and blessing were never sought carefully and not obtained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. For ye are not come unto the mount that might be touched, and that burned with fire, nor unto blackness, and darkness, and tempest — you have not come to a mount that might be touched, burning with fire, blackness, darkness, and tempest; Mount Sinai, where the Jewish church state was formed, was tangible yet forbidden, and the Mosaic dispensation was much in outward and earthly things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. And the sound of a trumpet, and the voice of words; which voice they that heard intreated that the word should not be spoken to them any more — the trumpet's sound and voice of words were so dreadful that hearers entreated the word not be spoken to them any more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

(For they could not endure that which was commanded, And if so much as a beast touch the mountain, it shall be stoned, or thrust through with a dart: — they could not endure the command that even a beast touching the mountain be stoned or thrust through. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. And so terrible was the sight, that Moses said, I exceedingly fear and quake:) — so terrible was the sight that even Moses said, "I exceedingly fear and quake"; the most holy must despair if judged by the holy law from Sinai without a Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. But ye are come unto mount Sion, and unto the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to an innumerable company of angels — but you have come to Mount Zion, the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and an innumerable company of angels; the gospel church is called Mount Zion, where believers have clearer views of heaven and more heavenly tempers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. To the general assembly and church of the firstborn, which are written in heaven, and to God the Judge of all, and to the spirits of just men made perfect — to the general assembly and church of the firstborn written in heaven, to God the Judge of all, and to the spirits of just men made perfect; all God's children are heirs, and every one has the privileges of the firstborn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. And to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling, that speaketh better things than that of Abel — and to Jesus, Mediator of the new covenant, and the blood of sprinkling that speaks better things than Abel's; Christ brings God and man together in this covenant, keeps them together, pleads with God for us and with us for God, and at length brings God and his people together in heaven; this blood speaks in behalf of sinners, pleading not for vengeance but for mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. See that ye refuse not him that speaketh. For if they escaped not who refused him that spake on earth, much more shall not we escape, if we turn away from him that speaketh from heaven — see that you refuse not his gracious call and offered salvation, nor him who speaks from heaven with infinite tenderness and love; how can those escape who turn from God in unbelief or apostasy while he so graciously beseeches them to be reconciled and receive his everlasting favour? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Whose voice then shook the earth: but now he hath promised, saying, Yet once more I shake not the earth only, but also heaven — his voice then shook the earth, but now he has promised once more to shake not earth only but heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. And this word, Yet once more, signifieth the removing of those things that are shaken, as of things that are made, that those things which cannot be shaken may remain — "yet once more" signifies the removing of shaken, made things, that unshakeable things may remain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Wherefore we receiving a kingdom which cannot be moved, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear — we receiving an unmoveable kingdom, let us have grace to serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear; we cannot worship God acceptably unless with reverence and godly fear, and only God's grace enables us to worship aright. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. For our God is a consuming fire — for our God is a consuming fire; God is the same just and righteous God under the gospel as under the law, yet the inheritance of believers is secured, and all things pertaining to salvation are freely given in answer to prayer, so let us seek grace to serve him with reverence and godly fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
