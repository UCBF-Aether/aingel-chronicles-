The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hebrews 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets,

2 Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds;

3 Who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power, when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high:

4 Being made so much better than the angels, as he hath by inheritance obtained a more excellent name than they.

5 For unto which of the angels said he at any time, Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee? And again, I will be to him a Father, and he shall be to me a Son?

6 And again, when he bringeth in the firstbegotten into the world, he saith, And let all the angels of God worship him.

7 And of the angels he saith, Who maketh his angels spirits, and his ministers a flame of fire.

8 But unto the Son he saith, Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever: a sceptre of righteousness is the sceptre of thy kingdom.

9 Thou hast loved righteousness, and hated iniquity; therefore God, even thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows.

10 And, Thou, Lord, in the beginning hast laid the foundation of the earth; and the heavens are the works of thine hands:

11 They shall perish; but thou remainest; and they all shall wax old as doth a garment;

12 And as a vesture shalt thou fold them up, and they shall be changed: but thou art the same, and thy years shall not fail.

13 But to which of the angels said he at any time, Sit on my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool?

14 Are they not all ministering spirits, sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God spoke in many times and many ways through the prophets, the writer says, but in these last days has spoken by his Son. The Son is set far above the angels: appointed heir of all things, maker of the worlds, brightness of God's glory, seated at the right hand of Majesty, while angels are ministering spirits sent to serve those who will inherit salvation. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God spoke to his ancient people at sundry times and in divers manners, through successive generations — sometimes by personal direction, sometimes by dreams, visions, or Divine influence on the prophets' minds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The gospel revelation is excellent above the former, because God has now spoken by his Son, whom he appointed heir of all things and by whom he made the worlds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. In beholding the power, wisdom, and goodness of Christ we behold the Father's, for the fulness of the Godhead dwells really, not merely typically, in him; having by himself purged our sins, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The Son is made so much better than the angels, having by inheritance obtained a more excellent name than they — the name of Son and Messiah, anointed above all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. No angel was ever addressed as God addresses the Son: "Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee," and "I will be to him a Father, and he shall be to me a Son." Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. When God brings his firstbegotten into the world, he commands all the angels of God to worship him — proof that the Son is no mere angel, but the object of their worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Of the angels God says only that he makes them spirits and ministers, winds and flames of fire — servants sent to do his pleasure, not sons on a throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. But to the Son he says, "Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever," with a sceptre of righteousness — the Son is owned and honoured as God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The Son loved righteousness and hated iniquity, and therefore God anointed him with the oil of gladness above his fellows — above all prophets, priests, and kings ever employed in God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The Son is addressed as Lord who laid the foundation of the earth, and the heavens are the work of his hands — Creator, not creature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Heaven and earth shall perish, but the Son remains; they shall wax old as a garment, while he endures unchanged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. As a sovereign folds up garments of state and remains a sovereign still, so Christ shall fold up earth and heaven like a vesture and be still the same. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. To no angel did God ever say, "Sit on my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool" — that seat of honour belongs to the Son alone, whose enemies shall submit or be destroyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The most exalted angels are but ministering spirits, sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation — protecting, instructing, and comforting the saints under Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

  • sundry times and divers manners. various times and different ways, the chapter’s description of how God formerly spoke through the prophets (verse 1) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
  • firstbegotten. firstborn, the title under which the Son is brought into the world and worshipped by angels (verse 6) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
