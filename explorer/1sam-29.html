The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 29 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the Philistines gathered together all their armies to Aphek: and the Israelites pitched by a fountain which is in Jezreel.

2 And the lords of the Philistines passed on by hundreds, and by thousands: but David and his men passed on in the rereward with Achish.

3 Then said the princes of the Philistines, What do these Hebrews here? And Achish said unto the princes of the Philistines, Is not this David, the servant of Saul the king of Israel, which hath been with me these days, or these years, and I have found no fault in him since he fell unto me unto this day?

4 And the princes of the Philistines were wroth with him; and the princes of the Philistines said unto him, Make this fellow return, that he may go again to his place which thou hast appointed him, and let him not go down with us to battle, lest in the battle he be an adversary to us: for wherewith should he reconcile himself unto his master? should it not be with the heads of these men?

5 Is not this David, of whom they sang one to another in dances, saying, Saul slew his thousands, and David his ten thousands?

6 Then Achish called David, and said unto him, Surely, as the LORD liveth, thou hast been upright, and thy going out and thy coming in with me in the host is good in my sight: for I have not found evil in thee since the day of thy coming unto me unto this day: nevertheless the lords favour thee not.

7 Wherefore now return, and go in peace, that thou displease not the lords of the Philistines.

8 And David said unto Achish, But what have I done? and what hast thou found in thy servant so long as I have been with thee unto this day, that I may not go fight against the enemies of my lord the king?

9 And Achish answered and said to David, I know that thou art good in my sight, as an angel of God: notwithstanding the princes of the Philistines have said, He shall not go up with us to the battle.

10 Wherefore now rise up early in the morning with thy master’s servants that are come with thee: and as soon as ye be up early in the morning, and have light, depart.

11 So David and his men rose up early to depart in the morning, to return into the land of the Philistines. And the Philistines went up to Jezreel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The Philistines gather all their armies to Aphek and Israel pitches by a fountain in Jezreel, and David and his men pass on in the rereward with Achish, caught between the two hosts. The Philistine princes object, What do these Hebrews here, and warn Achish that David may become an adversary in battle and reconcile himself to Saul with their heads, quoting the song, Saul slew his thousands, and David his ten thousands. Achish, who has found no evil in David since he fell to him and calls him good as an angel of God, nevertheless must obey the lords: David is ordered to return and go in peace, to rise early and depart as soon as there is light. David protests his loyalty, is answered with the princes' word, and rises early with his men to return into Philistine country, while the Philistines go up to Jezreel, so that David is pulled back from the brink of fighting against his own people by the enemy's own suspicion. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Philistines gathered all their armies to Aphek, and the Israelites pitched by a fountain in Jezreel. The battle that would end Saul was assembling, with David caught between the two hosts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The lords of the Philistines passed on by hundreds and thousands, but David and his men passed on in the rereward with Achish. David waited with a secret hope that the Lord would help him out of his difficulty, Henry notes, but he seems to have been influenced too much by the fear of man in consenting to attend Achish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Then said the princes of the Philistines, What do these Hebrews here? And Achish said, Is not this David, the servant of Saul king of Israel, who hath been with me these days, or these years, and I have found no fault in him since he fell unto me? Achish vouched for his guest's long fidelity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The princes were wroth and said, Make this fellow return to his place, and let him not go down with us to battle, lest in the battle he be an adversary to us; for wherewith should he reconcile himself unto his master? Should it not be with the heads of these men? God inclined the princes to oppose David's being employed in the battle, Henry observes; thus their dislike befriended him, when no friend could do him such a kindness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Is not this David, of whom they sang one to another in dances, Saul slew his thousands, and David his ten thousands? The old song was quoted against him by the enemy's lords. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Then Achish called David and said, Surely, as the LORD liveth, thou hast been upright, and thy going out and coming in with me in the host is good in my sight, for I have not found evil in thee since thou camest unto me; nevertheless the lords favour thee not. David scarcely ever had a greater deliverance, Henry says, than when dismissed from such ensnaring service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Wherefore now return, and go in peace, that thou displease not the lords of the Philistines. The dismissal came as a command wrapped in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. David said to Achish, But what have I done? And what hast thou found in thy servant so long as I have been with thee, that I may not go fight against the enemies of my lord the king? His protest kept up the appearance of loyalty to Achish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Achish answered, I know that thou art good in my sight, as an angel of God; notwithstanding the princes have said, He shall not go up with us to the battle. God's people should always behave so, Henry counsels, as if possible to get the good word of all they have dealings with, and it is due to those who have acted well to speak well of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Wherefore now rise up early in the morning with thy master's servants that are come with thee, and as soon as ye be up early and have light, depart. The order was to leave at first light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. So David and his men rose up early to depart in the morning, to return into the land of the Philistines, and the Philistines went up to Jezreel. It is hard to come near to the brink of sin and not fall in, Henry warns — and David was pulled back from the brink by the enemy's own suspicion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
