The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then came all the tribes of Israel to David unto Hebron, and spake, saying, Behold, we are thy bone and thy flesh.

2 Also in time past, when Saul was king over us, thou wast he that leddest out and broughtest in Israel: and the LORD said to thee, Thou shalt feed my people Israel, and thou shalt be a captain over Israel.

3 So all the elders of Israel came to the king to Hebron; and king David made a league with them in Hebron before the LORD: and they anointed David king over Israel.

4 David was thirty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned forty years.

5 In Hebron he reigned over Judah seven years and six months: and in Jerusalem he reigned thirty and three years over all Israel and Judah.

6 And the king and his men went to Jerusalem unto the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land: which spake unto David, saying, Except thou take away the blind and the lame, thou shalt not come in hither: thinking, David cannot come in hither.

7 Nevertheless David took the strong hold of Zion: the same is the city of David.

8 And David said on that day, Whosoever getteth up to the gutter, and smiteth the Jebusites, and the lame and the blind that are hated of David’s soul, he shall be chief and captain. Wherefore they said, The blind and the lame shall not come into the house.

9 So David dwelt in the fort, and called it the city of David. And David built round about from Millo and inward.

10 And David went on, and grew great, and the LORD God of hosts was with him.

11 And Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, and cedar trees, and carpenters, and masons: and they built David an house.

12 And David perceived that the LORD had established him king over Israel, and that he had exalted his kingdom for his people Israel’s sake.

13 And David took him more concubines and wives out of Jerusalem, after he was come from Hebron: and there were yet sons and daughters born to David.

14 And these be the names of those that were born unto him in Jerusalem; Shammuah, and Shobab, and Nathan, and Solomon,

15 Ibhar also, and Elishua, and Nepheg, and Japhia,

16 And Elishama, and Eliada, and Eliphalet.

17 But when the Philistines heard that they had anointed David king over Israel, all the Philistines came up to seek David; and David heard of it, and went down to the hold.

18 The Philistines also came and spread themselves in the valley of Rephaim.

19 And David enquired of the LORD, saying, Shall I go up to the Philistines? wilt thou deliver them into mine hand? And the LORD said unto David, Go up: for I will doubtless deliver the Philistines into thine hand.

20 And David came to Baalperazim, and David smote them there, and said, The LORD hath broken forth upon mine enemies before me, as the breach of waters. Therefore he called the name of that place Baalperazim.

21 And there they left their images, and David and his men burned them.

22 And the Philistines came up yet again, and spread themselves in the valley of Rephaim.

23 And when David enquired of the LORD, he said, Thou shalt not go up; but fetch a compass behind them, and come upon them over against the mulberry trees.

24 And let it be, when thou hearest the sound of a going in the tops of the mulberry trees, that then thou shalt bestir thyself: for then shall the LORD go out before thee, to smite the host of the Philistines.

25 And David did so, as the LORD had commanded him; and smote the Philistines from Geba until thou come to Gazer.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

All the tribes come to Hebron and anoint David king over all Israel; he captures the Jebusite fortress of Jerusalem and makes it his capital. From there he twice defeats Philistine armies that come up against him, and his fame and household grow. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. All the tribes come to David at Hebron, calling themselves his bone and flesh. His advances were gradual, Henry notes, that his faith might be tried and he might gain experience, typifying the Messiah's kingdom coming to its height by degrees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The tribes recall that David led Israel out and in even while Saul was king, and that the Lord said he would feed his people and be their captain. Past service and divine promise together make the case for his kingship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The elders make a league with David before the Lord at Hebron and anoint him king over Israel. He is now anointed a third time, and the whole nation, not only Judah, owns him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. David was thirty when he began to reign, and reigned forty years. The round numbers frame a reign long enough to test him and to establish the kingdom he had waited for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Seven years and six months in Hebron over Judah, then thirty-three years in Jerusalem over all Israel and Judah. The divided reign gives way to a united one, by the same gradual steps Henry traces throughout. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The Jebusites taunt David that even the blind and lame could hold Jerusalem against him. Henry observes that God's people's enemies are often most confident and secure just when their day to fall draws near. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Nevertheless David takes the stronghold of Zion, the city of David. The pride and insolence of the Jebusites animated David, and the Lord God of hosts was with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. David promises chief command to whoever goes up by the gutter and smites the Jebusites, including the blind and lame he hates. The taunt is turned into the terms of the assault. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. David dwells in the fort, calls it the city of David, and builds round about from Millo inward. A captured stronghold becomes a capital, and a capital is built up as a permanent seat of government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. David went on and grew great, and the Lord God of hosts was with him. His growth is attributed not to his own craft but to God's presence, the same presence Saul had forfeited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Hiram of Tyre sends cedar, carpenters, and masons, and they build David a house. Henry notes the house was not less fit to be dedicated to God for being built by the sons of the stranger, as Isaiah prophesied of the gospel church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. David perceives the Lord established him king and exalted his kingdom for his people Israel's sake. Many have God's favour yet do not perceive it, Henry observes, but to be exalted and to perceive it is happiness; David owned it was for his people's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. David takes more concubines and wives in Jerusalem, and more sons and daughters are born to him. The expanding household is the outward sign of a government rooted and built up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The sons born in Jerusalem begin with Shammuah, Shobab, Nathan, and Solomon. The list records the royal house taking root in the new capital, with Solomon named among the first four. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg, and Japhia continue the Jerusalem-born sons. Each name marks the kingdom's establishment in Zion rather than in Hebron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Elishama, Eliada, and Eliphalet complete the list of sons born in Jerusalem. Never had Israel appeared so great, Henry notes, as it began now to be under an established king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The Philistines, hearing David is anointed king over Israel, come up to seek him, and David goes down to the hold. The Philistines considered not, Henry says, that David had God's presence, which Saul had lost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. The Philistines spread themselves in the valley of Rephaim. The attack on the newly set-up kingdom mirrors, Henry notes, how the Messiah's kingdom was attacked by the powers of darkness as soon as it appeared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. David enquires of the Lord whether to go up and whether God will deliver the Philistines, and the Lord answers, Go up, for I will doubtless deliver them. David owns dependence on God for victory and refers himself to God's good pleasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. At Baalperazim David smites them, saying the Lord broke forth on his enemies as the breach of waters. God performed his promise, and David did not fail to improve his advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. The Philistines leave their images behind, and David and his men burn them. The abandoned gods are destroyed rather than kept as trophies, showing whose victory it was. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. The Philistines come up yet again and spread in the valley of Rephaim. A second invasion tests whether David will again wait on God rather than trust his first victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. David enquires again, and the Lord tells him not to go up directly but to circle behind them against the mulberry trees. The changed orders show dependence is renewed each time, not presumed from the last battle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. When David hears the sound of a going in the tops of the mulberry trees, he is to bestir himself, for then the Lord goes out before him. Henry's lesson is that David waited till God moved, and stirred then, but not till then. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. David does as the Lord commanded and smites the Philistines from Geba to Gazer. Trained up in dependence on God and his providence, he turns a second invasion into a rout across the whole front. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
