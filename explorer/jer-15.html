The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then said the LORD unto me, Though Moses and Samuel stood before me, yet my mind could not be toward this people: cast them out of my sight, and let them go forth.

2 And it shall come to pass, if they say unto thee, Whither shall we go forth? then thou shalt tell them, Thus saith the LORD; Such as are for death, to death; and such as are for the sword, to the sword; and such as are for the famine, to the famine; and such as are for the captivity, to the captivity.

3 And I will appoint over them four kinds, saith the LORD: the sword to slay, and the dogs to tear, and the fowls of the heaven, and the beasts of the earth, to devour and destroy.

4 And I will cause them to be removed into all kingdoms of the earth, because of Manasseh the son of Hezekiah king of Judah, for that which he did in Jerusalem.

5 For who shall have pity upon thee, O Jerusalem? or who shall bemoan thee? or who shall go aside to ask how thou doest?

6 Thou hast forsaken me, saith the LORD, thou art gone backward: therefore will I stretch out my hand against thee, and destroy thee; I am weary with repenting.

7 And I will fan them with a fan in the gates of the land; I will bereave them of children, I will destroy my people since they return not from their ways.

8 Their widows are increased to me above the sand of the seas: I have brought upon them against the mother of the young men a spoiler at noonday: I have caused him to fall upon it suddenly, and terrors upon the city.

9 She that hath borne seven languisheth: she hath given up the ghost; her sun is gone down while it was yet day: she hath been ashamed and confounded: and the residue of them will I deliver to the sword before their enemies, saith the LORD.

10 Woe is me, my mother, that thou hast borne me a man of strife and a man of contention to the whole earth! I have neither lent on usury, nor men have lent to me on usury; yet every one of them doth curse me.

11 The LORD said, Verily it shall be well with thy remnant; verily I will cause the enemy to entreat thee well in the time of evil and in the time of affliction.

12 Shall iron break the northern iron and the steel?

13 Thy substance and thy treasures will I give to the spoil without price, and that for all thy sins, even in all thy borders.

14 And I will make thee to pass with thine enemies into a land which thou knowest not: for a fire is kindled in mine anger, which shall burn upon you.

15 O LORD, thou knowest: remember me, and visit me, and revenge me of my persecutors; take me not away in thy longsuffering: know that for thy sake I have suffered rebuke.

16 Thy words were found, and I did eat them; and thy word was unto me the joy and rejoicing of mine heart: for I am called by thy name, O LORD God of hosts.

17 I sat not in the assembly of the mockers, nor rejoiced; I sat alone because of thy hand: for thou hast filled me with indignation.

18 Why is my pain perpetual, and my wound incurable, which refuseth to be healed? wilt thou be altogether unto me as a liar, and as waters that fail?

19 Therefore thus saith the LORD, If thou return, then will I bring thee again, and thou shalt stand before me: and if thou take forth the precious from the vile, thou shalt be as my mouth: let them return unto thee; but return not thou unto them.

20 And I will make thee unto this people a fenced brasen wall: and they shall fight against thee, but they shall not prevail against thee: for I am with thee to save thee and to deliver thee, saith the LORD.

21 And I will deliver thee out of the hand of the wicked, and I will redeem thee out of the hand of the terrible.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Even Moses and Samuel standing before God, the chapter says, would not turn his mind toward this people; they are to be cast out to sword, famine, captivity and pestilence. Jeremiah complains of his own lot, is answered with a renewed commission to separate the precious from the vile, and is promised deliverance from the wicked and the terrible. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then said the Lord unto me: Though Moses and Samuel stood before me, yet my mind could not be toward this people: cast them out of my sight, and let them go forth. Henry notes the Lord declares that even Moses and Samuel must have pleaded in vain: the putting of this as a case, though they should stand before him, shows that they do not, and that saints in heaven do not pray for saints on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And it shall come to pass, if they say unto thee, Whither shall we go forth? Then thou shalt tell them, Thus says the Lord: Such as are for death, to death; and such as are for the sword, to the sword; and such as are for the famine, to the famine; and such as are for the captivity, to the captivity. Henry notes the Jews were condemned to different kinds of misery by the righteous judgement of God: death, sword, famine, and captivity, each appointed its own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And I will appoint over them four kinds, says the Lord: the sword to slay, and the dogs to tear, and the fowls of the heaven, and the beasts of the earth, to devour and destroy. Henry's four kinds are the righteous judgement's instruments: sword slays, dogs tear, fowls and beasts devour and destroy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And I will cause them to be removed into all kingdoms of the earth, because of Manasseh the son of Hezekiah king of Judah, for that which he did in Jerusalem. Henry warns that bad examples and misused authority often produce fatal effects, even after men are dead, or have repented of their crimes: Manasseh's deeds in Jerusalem remove his people into all kingdoms, and this should make all greatly dread being the occasion of sin in others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. For who shall have pity upon thee, O Jerusalem? Or who shall bemoan thee? Or who shall go aside to ask how thou doest? Henry's populous city made desolate finds no pity, no bemoaning, no passer-by to ask how she does: the desolation is complete and friendless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Thou hast forsaken me, says the Lord, thou art gone backward: therefore will I stretch out my hand against thee, and destroy thee; I am weary with repenting. Henry's forsaking is answered with a stretched-out hand: gone backward from God, Jerusalem meets destruction, for the Lord is weary with repenting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And I will fan them with a fan in the gates of the land; I will bereave them of children, I will destroy my people, since they return not from their ways. Henry notes the remnant would be driven away, like the chaff, into captivity: fanned at the gates, bereaved of children, destroyed for not returning from their ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Their widows are increased to me above the sand of the seas: I have brought upon them against the mother of the young men a spoiler at noonday: I have caused him to fall upon it suddenly, and terrors upon the city. Henry's widows above the sand of the seas, and the noonday spoiler falling suddenly with terrors, are the desolation of the populous city made plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. She that has borne seven languishes: she has given up the ghost; her sun is gone down while it was yet day: she has been ashamed and confounded: and the residue of them will I deliver to the sword before their enemies, says the Lord. Henry's mother of seven, languishing with sun gone down at midday, ashamed and confounded, leaves only a residue for the sword. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Woe is me, my mother, that thou hast borne me a man of strife and a man of contention to the whole earth! I have neither lent on usury, nor men have lent to me on usury; yet every one of them doth curse me. Henry notes Jeremiah met with much contempt and reproach, when they ought to have blessed him, and God for him: a man of strife and contention, cursed by all though he lent nothing on usury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The Lord said: Verily it shall be well with thy remnant; verily I will cause the enemy to entreat thee well in the time of evil and in the time of affliction. Henry notes it is a great and sufficient support to the people of God, that however troublesome their way may be, it shall be well with them in their latter end: the enemy himself shall entreat the prophet well in evil and affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Shall iron break the northern iron and the steel? Henry asks whether the most hardy and vigorous of their efforts can contend with the counsel of God, or with the army of the Chaldeans: iron cannot break northern iron, and Judah cannot break the counsel appointed against her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Thy substance and thy treasures will I give to the spoil without price, and that for all thy sins, even in all thy borders. Henry notes the people who had great estates would be used hardly: substance and treasures given to spoil without price, for all their sins in all their borders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And I will make thee to pass with thine enemies into a land which thou knowest not: for a fire is kindled in mine anger, which shall burn upon you. Henry notes all parts of the country had added to the national guilt, and let each take shame to itself: passing with enemies into an unknown land, under anger's kindled fire, is the shared doom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. O Lord, thou knowest: remember me, and visit me, and revenge me of my persecutors; take me not away in thy longsuffering: know that for thy sake I have suffered rebuke. Henry notes it is matter of comfort that we have a God, to whose knowledge of all things we may appeal: Jeremiah pleads with God for mercy and relief against his enemies, persecutors, and slanderers, appealing to the God who knows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Thy words were found, and I did eat them; and thy word was unto me the joy and rejoicing of mine heart: for I am called by thy name, O Lord God of hosts. Henry notes it will be a comfort to God's ministers, when men despise them, if they have the testimony of their own consciences: the eaten word, joy and rejoicing of the heart, is that testimony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. I sat not in the assembly of the mockers, nor rejoiced; I sat alone because of thy hand: for thou hast filled me with indignation. Henry notes he complains that he found little pleasure in his work: sitting alone because of God's hand, filled with indignation, apart from the mockers' assembly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Why is my pain perpetual, and my wound incurable, which refuses to be healed? Wilt thou be altogether unto me as a liar, and as waters that fail? Henry warns some good people lose much of the pleasantness of religion by the fretfulness and uneasiness of their natural temper, which they indulge: perpetual pain and an incurable wound become the fretful question whether God is as waters that fail. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Therefore thus says the Lord: If thou return, then will I bring thee again, and thou shalt stand before me: and if thou take forth the precious from the vile, thou shalt be as my mouth: let them return unto thee; but return not thou unto them. Henry notes the Lord called the prophet to cease from his distrust, and to return to his work: if he attended thereto, he might be assured the Lord would deliver him from his enemies, and taking the precious from the vile he shall be as God's mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And I will make thee unto this people a fenced brasen wall: and they shall fight against thee, but they shall not prevail against thee: for I am with thee to save thee and to deliver thee, says the Lord. Henry notes those who are with God, and faithful to him, he will deliver from trouble or carry through it: a fenced brasen wall, fought against but never prevailed against, because God is with him to save and deliver. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And I will deliver thee out of the hand of the wicked, and I will redeem thee out of the hand of the terrible. Henry closes that many things appear frightful, which do not at all hurt a real believer in Christ: delivered from the wicked and redeemed from the terrible, the faithful prophet is carried through the frightful things unhurt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
