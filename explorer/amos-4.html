The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Amos 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Hear this word, ye kine of Bashan, that are in the mountain of Samaria, which oppress the poor, which crush the needy, which say to their masters, Bring, and let us drink.

2 The Lord GOD hath sworn by his holiness, that, lo, the days shall come upon you, that he will take you away with hooks, and your posterity with fishhooks.

3 And ye shall go out at the breaches, every cow at that which is before her; and ye shall cast them into the palace, saith the LORD.

4 Come to Bethel, and transgress; at Gilgal multiply transgression; and bring your sacrifices every morning, and your tithes after three years:

5 And offer a sacrifice of thanksgiving with leaven, and proclaim and publish the free offerings: for this liketh you, O ye children of Israel, saith the Lord GOD.

6 And I also have given you cleanness of teeth in all your cities, and want of bread in all your places: yet have ye not returned unto me, saith the LORD.

7 And also I have withholden the rain from you, when there were yet three months to the harvest: and I caused it to rain upon one city, and caused it not to rain upon another city: one piece was rained upon, and the piece whereupon it rained not withered.

8 So two or three cities wandered unto one city, to drink water; but they were not satisfied: yet have ye not returned unto me, saith the LORD.

9 I have smitten you with blasting and mildew: when your gardens and your vineyards and your fig trees and your olive trees increased, the palmerworm devoured them: yet have ye not returned unto me, saith the LORD.

10 I have sent among you the pestilence after the manner of Egypt: your young men have I slain with the sword, and have taken away your horses; and I have made the stink of your camps to come up unto your nostrils: yet have ye not returned unto me, saith the LORD.

11 I have overthrown some of you, as God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah, and ye were as a firebrand plucked out of the burning: yet have ye not returned unto me, saith the LORD.

12 Therefore thus will I do unto thee, O Israel: and because I will do this unto thee, prepare to meet thy God, O Israel.

13 For, lo, he that formeth the mountains, and createth the wind, and declareth unto man what is his thought, that maketh the morning darkness, and treadeth upon the high places of the earth, The LORD, The God of hosts, is his name.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Hear this word, you kine of Bashan on the mountain of Samaria, who oppress the poor and crush the needy while calling for drink. God sent famine, drought, blasting, mildew, pestilence, and sword, and after each stroke comes the same refrain: yet have ye not returned unto me. The chapter ends with the summons to prepare to meet God. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hear this word, you kine of Bashan in Samaria's mountain, who oppress the poor, crush the needy, and say to your masters, bring and let us drink: wealth got by extortion used to provide for the flesh and fulfill its lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The Lord God has sworn by his holiness that days shall come when they will be taken away with hooks, and their posterity with fishhooks, oppressors dragged off like caught fish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They shall go out at the breaches, each straight before her, and cast themselves into the palace, says the Lord, escape routes becoming routes of exile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Come to Bethel and transgress, at Gilgal multiply transgression, bringing sacrifices every morning and tithes after three years, the prophet ironically bidding them do what they love, since confidence in unscriptural observances only proves they believe a lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Offer thanksgiving with leaven and proclaim and publish free offerings, for this pleases you, O children of Israel, says the Lord God, worship performed to please themselves rather than warranted by God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. God also gave them cleanness of teeth in all cities and want of bread in all places, yet they have not returned to him, says the Lord, famine as a first corrective refused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. God also withheld rain when three months remained to harvest, raining on one city and not on another, one piece rained on and another withering, yet they have not returned, carnal hearts wandering from creature to creature for satisfaction but not inclining to him in whom all wants could be met. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. So two or three cities wandered to one city to drink water but were not satisfied, yet they have not returned to him, says the Lord, thirst itself not teaching them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. God smote them with blasting and mildew, and when gardens, vineyards, fig trees, and olives increased, the palmerworm devoured them, yet they have not returned, preaching being as rain and everything withering where that rain is wanting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. God sent pestilence after Egypt's manner, slew their young men with the sword, took away horses, and made camp stink rise to their nostrils, yet they have not returned, repeated strokes not producing repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. God overthrew some of them as Sodom and Gomorrah, and they were as a firebrand plucked from the burning, yet they have not returned, deliverance itself not softening them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Therefore thus will God do to Israel, and because he will do this, prepare to meet thy God, O Israel, since ere long all must meet God in judgment and cannot stand if tried according to their doings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. For he who forms mountains, creates wind, declares to man his thought, makes morning darkness, and treads on earth's high places, the Lord, the God of hosts, is his name, and to meet him with comfort they must now meet him in Christ, who came to save lost sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Samaria. the capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, standing in the prophets for the ten tribes as Jerusalem stands for Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Kine of Bashan. the wealthy women of Samaria, pictured as well-fed cattle of the rich pasture land of Bashan, who oppress the poor and crush the needy. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
