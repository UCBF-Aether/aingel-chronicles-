The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Zechariah 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 I lifted up mine eyes again, and looked, and behold a man with a measuring line in his hand.

2 Then said I, Whither goest thou? And he said unto me, To measure Jerusalem, to see what is the breadth thereof, and what is the length thereof.

3 And, behold, the angel that talked with me went forth, and another angel went out to meet him,

4 And said unto him, Run, speak to this young man, saying, Jerusalem shall be inhabited as towns without walls for the multitude of men and cattle therein:

5 For I, saith the LORD, will be unto her a wall of fire round about, and will be the glory in the midst of her.

6 Ho, ho, come forth, and flee from the land of the north, saith the LORD: for I have spread you abroad as the four winds of the heaven, saith the LORD.

7 Deliver thyself, O Zion, that dwellest with the daughter of Babylon.

8 For thus saith the LORD of hosts; After the glory hath he sent me unto the nations which spoiled you: for he that toucheth you toucheth the apple of his eye.

9 For, behold, I will shake mine hand upon them, and they shall be a spoil to their servants: and ye shall know that the LORD of hosts hath sent me.

10 Sing and rejoice, O daughter of Zion: for, lo, I come, and I will dwell in the midst of thee, saith the LORD.

11 And many nations shall be joined to the LORD in that day, and shall be my people: and I will dwell in the midst of thee, and thou shalt know that the LORD of hosts hath sent me unto thee.

12 And the LORD shall inherit Judah his portion in the holy land, and shall choose Jerusalem again.

13 Be silent, O all flesh, before the LORD: for he is raised up out of his holy habitation.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A man with a measuring line sets out to measure Jerusalem, but the angel's message overturns the measuring: Jerusalem will be inhabited as towns without walls for the multitude of men and cattle in her, for the LORD himself will be a wall of fire round about and the glory in her midst. The exiles are called to flee from the land of the north and from Babylon, for he who touches God's people touches the apple of his eye, and the chapter ends with singing: the LORD comes to dwell in the midst of Zion, many nations will be joined to the LORD and be his people, and all flesh is to be silent before him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. I lifted up mine eyes again, and looked, and behold a man with a measuring line in his hand, because the third vision opens with a surveyor, measuring Jerusalem for rebuilding, as a city to be restored by line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Then said I, whither goest thou, and he said unto me, to measure Jerusalem, to see what is the breadth thereof, and what is the length thereof, because the measuring is of the city itself, breadth and length both to be known, as a city planned anew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And, behold, the angel that talked with me went forth, and another angel went out to meet him, because the interpreting angel goes out, and a second angel meets him, carrying the message that will correct the measuring. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. And said unto him, run, speak to this young man, saying, Jerusalem shall be inhabited as towns without walls for the multitude of men and cattle therein, because the city will be too full for walls, inhabited like open towns, since its multitude cannot be walled in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For I, says the Lord, will be unto her a wall of fire round about, and will be the glory in the midst of her, because God himself replaces the wall, a wall of fire without, and glory within, which is better than any wall of stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Ho, ho, come forth, and flee from the land of the north, says the Lord, for I have spread you abroad as the four winds of the heaven, says the Lord, because the exiles still in Babylon are summoned to flee, since they were scattered like the four winds and must now gather. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Deliver thyself, O Zion, that dwellest with the daughter of Babylon, because Zion, still dwelling with Babylon's daughter, is told to deliver herself by departure, leaving the place of captivity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For thus says the Lord of hosts, after the glory has he sent me unto the nations which spoiled you, for he that touches you touches the apple of his eye, because the nations that spoiled Israel touched the apple of God's eye, and after the glory, God sends against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. For, behold, I will shake mine hand upon them, and they shall be a spoil to their servants, and ye shall know that the Lord of hosts has sent me, because the shaking of God's hand reverses the spoil, making the spoilers a spoil to their own servants, which proves the sending. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Sing and rejoice, O daughter of Zion, for, lo, I come, and I will dwell in the midst of thee, says the Lord, because Zion is told to sing, since the coming is a dwelling in the midst, not a visit from afar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And many nations shall be joined to the Lord in that day, and shall be my people, and I will dwell in the midst of thee, and thou shalt know that the Lord of hosts has sent me unto thee, because the dwelling widens, and many nations are joined to the Lord and become his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And the Lord shall inherit Judah his portion in the holy land, and shall choose Jerusalem again, because Judah is God's own portion in the holy land, and Jerusalem is chosen again, as at the first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Be silent, O all flesh, before the Lord, for he is raised up out of his holy habitation, because all flesh must fall silent, since the Lord has risen from his holy dwelling to act, and silence is the only fitting answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Daughter of Zion. Zion, that is Jerusalem, pictured as a daughter told to sing and rejoice because the LORD comes to dwell in her midst. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Apple of his eye. the tenderly guarded centre of the eye; Zechariah 2 says he who touches God's people touches the apple of his eye. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
