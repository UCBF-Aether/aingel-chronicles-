The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, that, as he was praying in a certain place, when he ceased, one of his disciples said unto him, Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples.

2 And he said unto them, When ye pray, say, Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done, as in heaven, so in earth.

3 Give us day by day our daily bread.

4 And forgive us our sins; for we also forgive every one that is indebted to us. And lead us not into temptation; but deliver us from evil.

5 And he said unto them, Which of you shall have a friend, and shall go unto him at midnight, and say unto him, Friend, lend me three loaves;

6 For a friend of mine in his journey is come to me, and I have nothing to set before him?

7 And he from within shall answer and say, Trouble me not: the door is now shut, and my children are with me in bed; I cannot rise and give thee.

8 I say unto you, Though he will not rise and give him, because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needeth.

9 And I say unto you, Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you.

10 For every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and to him that knocketh it shall be opened.

11 If a son shall ask bread of any of you that is a father, will he give him a stone? or if he ask a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent?

12 Or if he shall ask an egg, will he offer him a scorpion?

13 If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?

14 And he was casting out a devil, and it was dumb. And it came to pass, when the devil was gone out, the dumb spake; and the people wondered.

15 But some of them said, He casteth out devils through Beelzebub the chief of the devils.

16 And others, tempting him, sought of him a sign from heaven.

17 But he, knowing their thoughts, said unto them, Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation; and a house divided against a house falleth.

18 If Satan also be divided against himself, how shall his kingdom stand? because ye say that I cast out devils through Beelzebub.

19 And if I by Beelzebub cast out devils, by whom do your sons cast them out? therefore shall they be your judges.

20 But if I with the finger of God cast out devils, no doubt the kingdom of God is come upon you.

21 When a strong man armed keepeth his palace, his goods are in peace:

22 But when a stronger than he shall come upon him, and overcome him, he taketh from him all his armour wherein he trusted, and divideth his spoils.

23 He that is not with me is against me: and he that gathereth not with me scattereth.

24 When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through dry places, seeking rest; and finding none, he saith, I will return unto my house whence I came out.

25 And when he cometh, he findeth it swept and garnished.

26 Then goeth he, and taketh to him seven other spirits more wicked than himself; and they enter in, and dwell there: and the last state of that man is worse than the first.

27 And it came to pass, as he spake these things, a certain woman of the company lifted up her voice, and said unto him, Blessed is the womb that bare thee, and the paps which thou hast sucked.

28 But he said, Yea rather, blessed are they that hear the word of God, and keep it.

29 And when the people were gathered thick together, he began to say, This is an evil generation: they seek a sign; and there shall no sign be given it, but the sign of Jonas the prophet.

30 For as Jonas was a sign unto the Ninevites, so shall also the Son of man be to this generation.

31 The queen of the south shall rise up in the judgment with the men of this generation, and condemn them: for she came from the utmost parts of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and, behold, a greater than Solomon is here.

32 The men of Nineve shall rise up in the judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: for they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here.

33 No man, when he hath lighted a candle, putteth it in a secret place, neither under a bushel, but on a candlestick, that they which come in may see the light.

34 The light of the body is the eye: therefore when thine eye is single, thy whole body also is full of light; but when thine eye is evil, thy body also is full of darkness.

35 Take heed therefore that the light which is in thee be not darkness.

36 If thy whole body therefore be full of light, having no part dark, the whole shall be full of light, as when the bright shining of a candle doth give thee light.

37 And as he spake, a certain Pharisee besought him to dine with him: and he went in, and sat down to meat.

38 And when the Pharisee saw it, he marvelled that he had not first washed before dinner.

39 And the Lord said unto him, Now do ye Pharisees make clean the outside of the cup and the platter; but your inward part is full of ravening and wickedness.

40 Ye fools, did not he that made that which is without make that which is within also?

41 But rather give alms of such things as ye have; and, behold, all things are clean unto you.

42 But woe unto you, Pharisees! for ye tithe mint and rue and all manner of herbs, and pass over judgment and the love of God: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.

43 Woe unto you, Pharisees! for ye love the uppermost seats in the synagogues, and greetings in the markets.

44 Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye are as graves which appear not, and the men that walk over them are not aware of them.

45 Then answered one of the lawyers, and said unto him, Master, thus saying thou reproachest us also.

46 And he said, Woe unto you also, ye lawyers! for ye lade men with burdens grievous to be borne, and ye yourselves touch not the burdens with one of your fingers.

47 Woe unto you! for ye build the sepulchres of the prophets, and your fathers killed them.

48 Truly ye bear witness that ye allow the deeds of your fathers: for they indeed killed them, and ye build their sepulchres.

49 Therefore also said the wisdom of God, I will send them prophets and apostles, and some of them they shall slay and persecute:

50 That the blood of all the prophets, which was shed from the foundation of the world, may be required of this generation;

51 From the blood of Abel unto the blood of Zacharias which perished between the altar and the temple: verily I say unto you, It shall be required of this generation.

52 Woe unto you, lawyers! for ye have taken away the key of knowledge: ye entered not in yourselves, and them that were entering in ye hindered.

53 And as he said these things unto them, the scribes and the Pharisees began to urge him vehemently, and to provoke him to speak of many things:

54 Laying wait for him, and seeking to catch something out of his mouth, that they might accuse him.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Asked by a disciple to teach them to pray as John taught his, Jesus gives the Lord’s Prayer and parables of the midnight friend and the asking child, urging persistence because the Father gives the Holy Spirit to those who ask (vv. 1–13). After casting out a dumb devil he answers the Beelzebub charge with the strong man bound, warns that an empty swept house invites seven worse spirits, and refuses a sign except the sign of Jonah (vv. 14–32). The lamp, the single eye, and a long series of woes against Pharisees and lawyers who tithe mint yet pass over judgment and love close the chapter (vv. 33–54). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 11

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. As he prayed in a certain place and ceased, a disciple asked, Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples. Henry calls this a good and very needful prayer, for only Christ by word and Spirit can teach us how to pray, what to pray for, and what to say. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. When ye pray, say, Our Father which art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come; thy will be done, as in heaven, so in earth. Henry notes Christ taught much the same prayer as in the Sermon on the Mount, differences in words being of no moment, and urges coming to our heavenly Father confiding in his power and goodness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Give us day by day our daily bread. Daily bread is asked daily, not stored in advance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Forgive us our sins, for we also forgive every one indebted to us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Forgiveness received and forgiveness given are bound together in the same petition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Which of you shall have a friend, and go to him at midnight saying, Friend, lend me three loaves? The parable begins with an awkward hour and a simple need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. For a friend of mine in his journey is come to me, and I have nothing to set before him. The midnight visitor's need is hospitality, not luxury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He from within shall answer, Trouble me not: the door is shut, my children are with me in bed; I cannot rise and give thee. Every excuse is respectable, and still the request stands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Though he will not rise because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needs. Henry's lesson is fervency and constancy: if God does not answer speedily, yet he will in due time if we continue to pray. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you. Asking, seeking, knocking are rising degrees of the same earnestness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. For every one that asks receives, he that seeks finds, and to him that knocks it shall be opened. The promise is stated universally, to encourage continuance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. If a son asks bread of a father, will he give him a stone? Or if he asks a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent? A father does not mock hunger with harm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Or if he asks an egg, will he offer him a scorpion? The third example completes the point: gifts may be small, but they are not cruel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him? Henry notes we must ask for the Holy Spirit above all, since all spiritual blessings are included in that one, and the Father is more ready to bestow than an indulgent parent to feed a hungry child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He was casting out a devil, and it was dumb; when the devil went out, the dumb spoke, and the people wondered. Casting out is here the destroying of the devil's power, not a mere display. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. But some said, he casts out devils through Beelzebub, chief of the devils. The miracle is admitted and its source is slandered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Others, tempting him, sought of him a sign from heaven. A sign already given is not enough for those who demand a different kind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Knowing their thoughts, he said, every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation, and a house divided against a house falls. A divided kingdom cannot be the explanation of its own defeat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. If Satan also be divided against himself, how shall his kingdom stand? Because ye say I cast out devils through Beelzebub. The charge collapses on its own logic. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. If I by Beelzebub cast out devils, by whom do your sons cast them out? Therefore shall they be your judges. Their own exorcists become witnesses against the charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. But if I with the finger of God cast out devils, no doubt the kingdom of God is come upon you. The exorcism is not a trick but an arrival: the kingdom has come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. When a strong man armed keeps his palace, his goods are in peace. Henry pictures the unconverted heart as the devil's palace, where he dwells and rules, and the sinner feels a false peace, secure with no dread of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. But when a stronger than he comes and overcomes him, he takes all his armour wherein he trusted, and divides his spoils. Conversion, Henry says, is Christ's victory over the devil in that soul, restoring it to liberty and recovering his own interest and power over it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. He that is not with me is against me, and he that gathers not with me scatters. Neutrality is not an option in this war. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. When the unclean spirit goes out, he walks through dry places seeking rest, finds none, and says, I will return to my house whence I came out. Expulsion without occupation leaves the house empty for return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. When he comes, he finds it swept and garnished. Henry warns the house may be swept from common sins by forced confession, feigned contrition, or partial reformation, yet not washed; sweeping takes only loose dirt, while the besetting sin is untouched, and garnish is paint, not true grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Then he takes seven other spirits more wicked than himself, and they enter and dwell there, and the last state is worse than the first. Henry urges us not to rest in what a man may have yet come short of heaven, and to pray earnestly to be delivered from such an awful state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. As he spoke, a woman lifted her voice: blessed is the womb that bore thee, and the paps thou hast sucked. Henry notes that while scribes despised his discourses, this woman admired them and his wisdom and power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. But he said, yea rather, blessed are they that hear the word of God, and keep it. Christ leads her to a higher consideration: hearing, kept in memory and kept as way and rule, is the true blessedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. When people gathered thick, he began: this is an evil generation; they seek a sign, and no sign shall be given but the sign of Jonah the prophet. Henry notes Christ promised one more sign, Jonah, explained in Matthew as the resurrection, and warned them to improve it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. For as Jonah was a sign to the Ninevites, so shall the Son of man be to this generation. The sign is a prophet delivered and preaching, not a spectacle on demand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. The queen of the south shall rise in judgment with this generation and condemn them, for she came from earth's utmost parts to hear Solomon's wisdom, and behold, a greater than Solomon is here. Distance travelled to hear wisdom condemns those who will not hear at home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation and condemn it, for they repented at Jonah's preaching, and behold, a greater than Jonah is here. Repentance at a lesser prophet condemns impenitence before a greater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. No man lighting a candle puts it in a secret place or under a bushel, but on a candlestick, that entrants may see the light. Light is given to be seen, not hidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The light of the body is the eye: when thine eye is single, thy whole body is full of light; but when thine eye is evil, thy body is full of darkness. The eye governs the body's light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Take heed therefore that the light in thee be not darkness. Henry warns especially that if our leading principles are wrong, judgment and practice must become more so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. If thy whole body be full of light, having no part dark, the whole shall be full of light, as when a candle's bright shining gives thee light. Undivided light makes the whole person luminous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. As he spoke, a Pharisee besought him to dine with him, and he went in and sat down to meat. The table becomes the next pulpit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. When the Pharisee saw it, he marvelled that he had not first washed before dinner. Outward washing is treated as the measure of piety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Now do ye Pharisees make clean the outside of cup and platter, but your inward part is full of ravening and wickedness. Henry's counsel for the whole section is to look to our hearts, that they may be cleansed and new-created, attending to great things of law and gospel without neglecting the smallest matter God appointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Ye fools, did not he that made what is without make what is within also? The Maker of outside is Maker of inside, so outside cleansing cannot excuse inside filth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. But rather give alms of such things as ye have, and behold, all things are clean unto you. Mercy, not ritual, cleanses what is held. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Woe unto you, Pharisees! For ye tithe mint, rue, and all herbs, and pass over judgment and the love of God; these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone. Small exactness cannot substitute for judgment and love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Woe unto you, Pharisees! For ye love the uppermost seats in synagogues, and greetings in markets. Honour-seeking is named as a woe, not a weakness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For ye are as graves which appear not, and men walking over them are not aware. Hidden corruption defiles the unaware, like an unmarked grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Then a lawyer answered, Master, thus saying thou reproachest us also. The lawyer hears his own profession in the woes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Woe unto you also, ye lawyers! For ye lade men with burdens grievous to be borne, and ye yourselves touch not the burdens with one finger. Burdens imposed on others, untouched by imposers, draw a woe of their own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Woe unto you! For ye build the sepulchres of the prophets, and your fathers killed them. Honouring dead prophets while sharing their killers' spirit is the indictment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Truly ye bear witness that ye allow your fathers' deeds, for they killed them, and ye build their sepulchres. Building tombs testifies to continuity, not to repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. Therefore also said the wisdom of God, I will send them prophets and apostles, and some they shall slay and persecute. The pattern continues into the present generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. That the blood of all prophets, shed from the foundation of the world, may be required of this generation. The generation that completes the pattern bears its reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. From Abel's blood unto Zacharias, who perished between altar and temple: verily, it shall be required of this generation. From first martyr to last, the whole account is charged to those who repeat it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Woe unto you, lawyers! For ye have taken away the key of knowledge: ye entered not in yourselves, and them entering in ye hindered. Keeping the key while barring the door is the lawyers' worst woe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. As he said these things, scribes and Pharisees began to urge him vehemently, and provoke him to speak of many things. Reproof provokes a cross-examination. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. Laying wait for him, seeking to catch something out of his mouth, that they might accuse him. Henry's prayer for such moments is for prudence and patience, that evil purposes be disappointed and the Spirit rest upon us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 33. No man lighting a candle puts it in a secret place or under a bushel, but on a candlestick, that entrants may see the light. Light is given to be seen, not hidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The light of the body is the eye: when thine eye is single, thy whole body is full of light; but when thine eye is evil, thy body is full of darkness. The eye governs the body's light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Take heed therefore that the light in thee be not darkness. Henry warns especially that if our leading principles are wrong, judgment and practice must become more so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. If thy whole body be full of light, having no part dark, the whole shall be full of light, as when a candle's bright shining gives thee light. Undivided light makes the whole person luminous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. As he spoke, a Pharisee besought him to dine with him, and he went in and sat down to meat. The table becomes the next pulpit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. When the Pharisee saw it, he marvelled that he had not first washed before dinner. Outward washing is treated as the measure of piety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Now do ye Pharisees make clean the outside of cup and platter, but your inward part is full of ravening and wickedness. Henry's counsel for the whole section is to look to our hearts, that they may be cleansed and new-created, attending to great things of law and gospel without neglecting the smallest matter God appointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Ye fools, did not he that made what is without make what is within also? The Maker of outside is Maker of inside, so outside cleansing cannot excuse inside filth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. But rather give alms of such things as ye have, and behold, all things are clean unto you. Mercy, not ritual, cleanses what is held. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Woe unto you, Pharisees! For ye tithe mint, rue, and all herbs, and pass over judgment and the love of God; these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone. Small exactness cannot substitute for judgment and love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Woe unto you, Pharisees! For ye love the uppermost seats in synagogues, and greetings in markets. Honour-seeking is named as a woe, not a weakness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For ye are as graves which appear not, and men walking over them are not aware. Hidden corruption defiles the unaware, like an unmarked grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Then a lawyer answered, Master, thus saying thou reproachest us also. The lawyer hears his own profession in the woes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Woe unto you also, ye lawyers! For ye lade men with burdens grievous to be borne, and ye yourselves touch not the burdens with one finger. Burdens imposed on others, untouched by imposers, draw a woe of their own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Woe unto you! For ye build the sepulchres of the prophets, and your fathers killed them. Honouring dead prophets while sharing their killers' spirit is the indictment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Truly ye bear witness that ye allow your fathers' deeds, for they killed them, and ye build their sepulchres. Building tombs testifies to continuity, not to repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. Therefore also said the wisdom of God, I will send them prophets and apostles, and some they shall slay and persecute. The pattern continues into the present generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. That the blood of all prophets, shed from the foundation of the world, may be required of this generation. The generation that completes the pattern bears its reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. From Abel's blood unto Zacharias, who perished between altar and temple: verily, it shall be required of this generation. From first martyr to last, the whole account is charged to those who repeat it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Woe unto you, lawyers! For ye have taken away the key of knowledge: ye entered not in yourselves, and them entering in ye hindered. Keeping the key while barring the door is the lawyers' worst woe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. As he said these things, scribes and Pharisees began to urge him vehemently, and provoke him to speak of many things. Reproof provokes a cross-examination. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. Laying wait for him, seeking to catch something out of his mouth, that they might accuse him. Henry's prayer for such moments is for prudence and patience, that evil purposes be disappointed and the Spirit rest upon us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
