The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, Go unto Pharaoh, and say unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Let my people go, that they may serve me.

2 And if thou refuse to let them go, behold, I will smite all thy borders with frogs:

3 And the river shall bring forth frogs abundantly, which shall go up and come into thine house, and into thy bedchamber, and upon thy bed, and into the house of thy servants, and upon thy people, and into thine ovens, and into thy kneadingtroughs:

4 And the frogs shall come up both on thee, and upon thy people, and upon all thy servants.

5 And the LORD spake unto Moses, Say unto Aaron, Stretch forth thine hand with thy rod over the streams, over the rivers, and over the ponds, and cause frogs to come up upon the land of Egypt.

6 And Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters of Egypt; and the frogs came up, and covered the land of Egypt.

7 And the magicians did so with their enchantments, and brought up frogs upon the land of Egypt.

8 Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron, and said, Intreat the LORD, that he may take away the frogs from me, and from my people; and I will let the people go, that they may do sacrifice unto the LORD.

9 And Moses said unto Pharaoh, Glory over me: when shall I intreat for thee, and for thy servants, and for thy people, to destroy the frogs from thee and thy houses, that they may remain in the river only?

10 And he said, To morrow. And he said, Be it according to thy word: that thou mayest know that there is none like unto the LORD our God.

11 And the frogs shall depart from thee, and from thy houses, and from thy servants, and from thy people; they shall remain in the river only.

12 And Moses and Aaron went out from Pharaoh: and Moses cried unto the LORD because of the frogs which he had brought against Pharaoh.

13 And the LORD did according to the word of Moses; and the frogs died out of the houses, out of the villages, and out of the fields.

14 And they gathered them together upon heaps: and the land stank.

15 But when Pharaoh saw that there was respite, he hardened his heart, and hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.

16 And the LORD said unto Moses, Say unto Aaron, Stretch out thy rod, and smite the dust of the land, that it may become lice throughout all the land of Egypt.

17 And they did so; for Aaron stretched out his hand with his rod, and smote the dust of the earth, and it became lice in man, and in beast; all the dust of the land became lice throughout all the land of Egypt.

18 And the magicians did so with their enchantments to bring forth lice, but they could not: so there were lice upon man, and upon beast.

19 Then the magicians said unto Pharaoh, This is the finger of God: and Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.

20 And the LORD said unto Moses, Rise up early in the morning, and stand before Pharaoh; lo, he cometh forth to the water; and say unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Let my people go, that they may serve me.

21 Else, if thou wilt not let my people go, behold, I will send swarms of flies upon thee, and upon thy servants, and upon thy people, and into thy houses: and the houses of the Egyptians shall be full of swarms of flies, and also the ground whereon they are.

22 And I will sever in that day the land of Goshen, in which my people dwell, that no swarms of flies shall be there; to the end thou mayest know that I am the LORD in the midst of the earth.

23 And I will put a division between my people and thy people: to morrow shall this sign be.

24 And the LORD did so; and there came a grievous swarm of flies into the house of Pharaoh, and into his servants’ houses, and into all the land of Egypt: the land was corrupted by reason of the swarm of flies.

25 And Pharaoh called for Moses and for Aaron, and said, Go ye, sacrifice to your God in the land.

26 And Moses said, It is not meet so to do; for we shall sacrifice the abomination of the Egyptians to the LORD our God: lo, shall we sacrifice the abomination of the Egyptians before their eyes, and will they not stone us?

27 We will go three days’ journey into the wilderness, and sacrifice to the LORD our God, as he shall command us.

28 And Pharaoh said, I will let you go, that ye may sacrifice to the LORD your God in the wilderness; only ye shall not go very far away: intreat for me.

29 And Moses said, Behold, I go out from thee, and I will intreat the LORD that the swarms of flies may depart from Pharaoh, from his servants, and from his people, to morrow: but let not Pharaoh deal deceitfully any more in not letting the people go to sacrifice to the LORD.

30 And Moses went out from Pharaoh, and intreated the LORD.

31 And the LORD did according to the word of Moses; and he removed the swarms of flies from Pharaoh, from his servants, and from his people; there remained not one.

32 And Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also, neither would he let the people go.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Three plagues fall in turn: frogs swarm over Egypt, then lice, then swarms of flies. The magicians fail at the lice and confess the finger of God, yet Pharaoh keeps bargaining, promising release and then hardening his heart each time the plague is lifted. Only Goshen, where Israel dwells, is spared the flies. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD told Moses to go to Pharaoh again with the demand: Let my people go, that they may serve me. Henry notes the demand never changed, though the judgments grew heavier, for God's claim on his people admits no compromise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. If Pharaoh refused, God would smite all his borders with frogs. Henry observes the second plague was fitted to Egypt's worship, for the frog was among their sacred creatures, now turned into a torment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The river would bring forth frogs abundantly, invading houses, bedchambers, beds, servants' houses, ovens, and kneadingtroughs. Henry notes no place was private or clean enough to escape: the plague entered where life was most domestic. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The frogs would come up upon Pharaoh himself, upon his people, and upon all his servants. Henry observes the plague honoured no rank: king and servant alike were overrun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Aaron was to stretch his hand with the rod over the streams, rivers, and ponds, that frogs might come up upon Egypt. Henry notes the same waters that had run blood now bred the next plague, judgment following judgment from one source. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Aaron stretched out his hand over Egypt's waters, and the frogs came up and covered the land. Henry observes the completeness: not a few frogs, but a covering, as the dust had covered the ground in the plague before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The magicians did so with their enchantments and brought up frogs upon Egypt. Henry notes the counterfeit again added to the plague without removing it, and Pharaoh still took imitation for power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron and asked them to intreat the LORD to take away the frogs, promising to let the people go and sacrifice. Henry observes this was the first yielding, wrung from him by discomfort rather than conviction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Moses answered, Glory over me: appoint when I shall intreat for thee, thy servants, and thy people. Henry notes Moses let Pharaoh name the time, that the removal might be plainly God's doing and not chance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Pharaoh said, To morrow, and Moses answered, Be it according to thy word, that thou mayest know there is none like the LORD our God. Henry observes Pharaoh's choice of tomorrow betrayed his heart: he would endure one more night of frogs rather than seek God at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The frogs would depart from Pharaoh, his houses, his servants, and his people, remaining only in the river. Henry notes the removal was as precise as the plague: the river kept its frogs, the houses were cleared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Moses and Aaron went out from Pharaoh, and Moses cried unto the LORD because of the frogs. Henry observes Moses prayed for his enemy at his enemy's request, the pattern of intercession for persecutors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The LORD did according to the word of Moses, and the frogs died out of the houses, villages, and fields. Henry notes the answer came exactly as prayed, that Pharaoh might know the LORD hearkens to his servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. They gathered the dead frogs together upon heaps, and the land stank. Henry observes the plague's aftermath was itself a judgment: deliverance left a stench, that Egypt might remember what it had endured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. When Pharaoh saw there was respite, he hardened his heart and hearkened not, as the LORD had said. Henry notes the pattern of all his repentances: they lasted exactly as long as the pain did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The LORD told Moses to have Aaron stretch out his rod and smite the dust of the land, that it might become lice. Henry observes this plague came without warning, for Pharaoh had been warned enough, and judgment now fell unannounced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Aaron smote the dust, and it became lice upon man and beast, all the dust of the land becoming lice. Henry notes the meanness of the instrument: dust underfoot became a torment, that Egypt might see God can arm the smallest things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The magicians tried with their enchantments to bring forth lice, but they could not. Henry observes the counterfeit failed here: imitation reached its limit, and the magicians stood exposed before their king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The magicians told Pharaoh, This is the finger of God, yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened and he hearkened not. Henry notes even his own wise men confessed what their king denied, and still denial held. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The LORD told Moses to rise early and stand before Pharaoh as he came forth to the water. Henry notes the morning confrontation repeated: Pharaoh was met at his daily worship before the day's business began. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Else, if Pharaoh would not let God's people go, God would send swarms of flies upon him, his servants, his people, and his houses. Henry observes the fourth plague invaded the houses again, but now with stinging swarms rather than creeping frogs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. God would sever the land of Goshen, where his people dwelt, so that no swarms would be there, that Pharaoh might know God was in the midst of the land. Henry notes the new feature: distinction, for the plagues now spared Israel while striking Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. God would put a division between his people and Pharaoh's people, and tomorrow this sign would be. Henry observes the division was itself the sign: the same land, the same air, yet one people plagued and the other untouched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. The LORD did so, and a grievous swarm came into Pharaoh's house and his servants' houses, and the land was corrupted by reason of the swarm. Henry notes the corruption spread beyond the houses to the whole land, a plague of ruin, not mere annoyance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron and said, Go ye, sacrifice to your God in the land. Henry observes the compromise offered: worship permitted, but only within Egypt, where Pharaoh could still control it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Moses answered it was not meet to sacrifice the abomination of the Egyptians before their eyes, lest they stone them. Henry explains the sacrifices Israel offered were animals Egypt worshipped, so public sacrifice in Egypt would provoke murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. They would go three days' journey into the wilderness and sacrifice as God commanded. Henry notes Moses held the line: the place and manner of worship were God's to appoint, not Pharaoh's to bargain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Pharaoh yielded so far: he would let them go to sacrifice in the wilderness, only they must not go very far away, and they must intreat for him. Henry observes the bargain still clung to a chain: leave, but stay within reach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Moses promised to intreat the LORD that the swarms would depart tomorrow, but warned Pharaoh not to deal deceitfully any more in not letting the people go. Henry notes the warning was plain: a second deceit would be worse than the first refusal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Moses went out from Pharaoh and intreated the LORD. Henry observes Moses again prayed for the king who had twice broken faith, and God again heard him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. The LORD did according to Moses's word and removed the swarms, so that not one remained. Henry notes the completeness of the removal left Pharaoh without even the excuse of a lingering fly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also, neither would he let the people go. Henry closes the chapter on the same sentence as before: mercy received, promise broken, heart harder than ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
