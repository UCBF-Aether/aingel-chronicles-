The Text — 1 Samuel 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now there was a certain man of Ramathaimzophim, of mount Ephraim, and his name was Elkanah, the son of Jeroham, the son of Elihu, the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph, an Ephrathite:

2 And he had two wives; the name of the one was Hannah, and the name of the other Peninnah: and Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children.

3 And this man went up out of his city yearly to worship and to sacrifice unto the LORD of hosts in Shiloh. And the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, the priests of the LORD, were there.

4 And when the time was that Elkanah offered, he gave to Peninnah his wife, and to all her sons and her daughters, portions:

5 But unto Hannah he gave a worthy portion; for he loved Hannah: but the LORD had shut up her womb.

6 And her adversary also provoked her sore, for to make her fret, because the LORD had shut up her womb.

7 And as he did so year by year, when she went up to the house of the LORD, so she provoked her; therefore she wept, and did not eat.

8 Then said Elkanah her husband to her, Hannah, why weepest thou? and why eatest thou not? and why is thy heart grieved? am not I better to thee than ten sons?

9 So Hannah rose up after they had eaten in Shiloh, and after they had drunk. Now Eli the priest sat upon a seat by a post of the temple of the LORD.

10 And she was in bitterness of soul, and prayed unto the LORD, and wept sore.

11 And she vowed a vow, and said, O LORD of hosts, if thou wilt indeed look on the affliction of thine handmaid, and remember me, and not forget thine handmaid, but wilt give unto thine handmaid a man child, then I will give him unto the LORD all the days of his life, and there shall no razor come upon his head.

12 And it came to pass, as she continued praying before the LORD, that Eli marked her mouth.

13 Now Hannah, she spake in her heart; only her lips moved, but her voice was not heard: therefore Eli thought she had been drunken.

14 And Eli said unto her, How long wilt thou be drunken? put away thy wine from thee.

15 And Hannah answered and said, No, my lord, I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit: I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but have poured out my soul before the LORD.

16 Count not thine handmaid for a daughter of Belial: for out of the abundance of my complaint and grief have I spoken hitherto.

17 Then Eli answered and said, Go in peace: and the God of Israel grant thee thy petition that thou hast asked of him.

18 And she said, Let thine handmaid find grace in thy sight. So the woman went her way, and did eat, and her countenance was no more sad.

19 And they rose up in the morning early, and worshipped before the LORD, and returned, and came to their house to Ramah: and Elkanah knew Hannah his wife; and the LORD remembered her.

20 Wherefore it came to pass, when the time was come about after Hannah had conceived, that she bare a son, and called his name Samuel, saying, Because I have asked him of the LORD.

21 And the man Elkanah, and all his house, went up to offer unto the LORD the yearly sacrifice, and his vow.

22 But Hannah went not up; for she said unto her husband, I will not go up until the child be weaned, and then I will bring him, that he may appear before the LORD, and there abide for ever.

23 And Elkanah her husband said unto her, Do what seemeth thee good; tarry until thou have weaned him; only the LORD establish his word. So the woman abode, and gave her son suck until she weaned him.

24 And when she had weaned him, she took him up with her, with three bullocks, and one ephah of flour, and a bottle of wine, and brought him unto the house of the LORD in Shiloh: and the child was young.

25 And they slew a bullock, and brought the child to Eli.

26 And she said, Oh my lord, as thy soul liveth, my lord, I am the woman that stood by thee here, praying unto the LORD.

27 For this child I prayed; and the LORD hath given me my petition which I asked of him:

28 Therefore also I have lent him to the LORD; as long as he liveth he shall be lent to the LORD. And he worshipped the LORD there.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Elkanah of Ramathaim-zophim has two wives, Peninnah with children and Hannah with none, and year by year at Shiloh Peninnah provokes Hannah sorely until she weeps and cannot eat. Hannah rises after eating and drinking, prays in bitterness of soul with only her lips moving, and Eli, thinking her drunken, rebukes her; she answers meekly that she has poured out her soul before the LORD, and Eli blesses her. She conceives and bears Samuel, because she asked him of the LORD, keeps him until he is weaned, then brings him to Shiloh with three bullocks, flour and wine, and lends him to the LORD as long as he lives. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Elkanah of Ramathaim-zophim in Mount Ephraim opens the book, and Henry's chapter heading frames the whole book as the story of Eli's house, Samuel, Saul's rise and fall, and David's patient suffering as a pattern for believers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Elkanah had two wives, Peninnah with children and Hannah with none, setting up the household sorrow that drives the chapter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Elkanah went up yearly to worship and sacrifice at Shiloh, keeping up his attendance at God's altar despite the unhappy divisions in his family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. At the sacrifice Elkanah gave portions to Peninnah and her children, the ordinary family share of the peace offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Hannah received a worthy portion because Elkanah loved her, though the LORD had shut up her womb; Henry warns against abating just love to a relation for an infirmity that is their affliction, not their fault. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Peninnah provoked Hannah sorely to make her fret over her childlessness. Henry calls it a base disposition to delight in grieving a sorrowful spirit, and says we ought to bear one another's burdens, not add to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The provocation repeated year by year at the house of the LORD, until Hannah wept and could not eat. Those of a fretful spirit who lay provocations too much to heart, Henry notes, strip themselves of many comforts of life and godliness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Elkanah tenderly asked why Hannah wept and would not eat, offering himself as better to her than ten sons. Henry's counsel here is to notice our comforts, and look at what is for us as well as what is against us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. After eating and drinking in Shiloh, Hannah rose to pray while Eli the priest sat by a post of the temple of the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Hannah prayed in bitterness of soul and wept sore. Henry observes that she mingled tears with her prayers, trusting a God who knows the troubled soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Hannah vowed that if God would look on her affliction and remember her, she would give the child to the LORD all his days. God gives us leave in prayer, Henry notes, not only to ask good things in general but to name the special good we most need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. As Hannah continued praying before the LORD, Eli marked her mouth, watching a prayer he could not hear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Hannah spoke in her heart, only her lips moving, with no voice heard, so Eli thought her drunken. Her silent prayer testified her belief in God's knowledge of the heart and its desires. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Eli rashly charged her with drunkenness and told her to put away her wine. Henry warns that it ill becomes us to be hasty in censuring others and to think people guilty while the matter is doubtful and unproved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Hannah answered meekly that she was a woman of a sorrowful spirit who had drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but had poured out her soul before the LORD. She did not retort the charge or upbraid Eli with his own sons' conduct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. She asked not to be counted a daughter of Belial, explaining that she had spoken out of the abundance of her complaint and grief. When unjustly censured, Henry counsels, we need a double watch before the door of our lips not to return censure for censure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Eli acknowledged his mistake and blessed her: go in peace, and may the God of Israel grant your petition. Hannah thought it enough to clear herself, and Eli was willing to own his error. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Hannah asked for grace in Eli's sight, then went her way, ate, and was sad no more. Having committed her case to God in prayer, and received Eli's prayer for her, prayer became heart's ease to her soul and smoothed her countenance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. The family rose early, worshipped before the LORD, and returned to Ramah, where Elkanah knew Hannah his wife. They would not set out on their journey until they had worshipped God together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. In due time Hannah conceived and bore a son, naming him Samuel, because she had asked him of the LORD. The name itself keeps the answer to prayer in view. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Elkanah and all his house went up to offer the yearly sacrifice and his vow, keeping the household's regular worship even with a new child at home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Hannah stayed behind until the child was weaned, intending then to bring him to appear before the LORD. Henry notes God will have mercy and not sacrifice: those detained from public ordinances by nursing little children may take comfort here if they do that duty in a right spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Elkanah agreed she should tarry until the child was weaned, praying only that the LORD would establish his word. So Hannah nursed Samuel at home in preparation for giving him up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. When Samuel was weaned, Hannah brought him to the house of the LORD in Shiloh with three bullocks, an ephah of flour, and a bottle of wine — a generous offering to accompany the gift of the child himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. They slew a bullock and brought the child to Eli, presenting Samuel in person to the priest who had once misjudged his mother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Hannah reminded Eli that she was the woman who had stood there praying to the LORD, identifying herself by that answered prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. For this child I prayed, she said, and the LORD has given me my petition. Her grateful acknowledgment names the gift as an answer to prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Hannah lent Samuel to the LORD for as long as he lived, and he worshipped the LORD there. Whatever we give to God, Henry observes, is what we have first asked and received from him; and the child early showed true piety, a pattern for teaching little children to worship God young. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
