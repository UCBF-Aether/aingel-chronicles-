The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The burden of the valley of vision. What aileth thee now, that thou art wholly gone up to the housetops?

2 Thou that art full of stirs, a tumultuous city, joyous city: thy slain men are not slain with the sword, nor dead in battle.

3 All thy rulers are fled together, they are bound by the archers: all that are found in thee are bound together, which have fled from far.

4 Therefore said I, Look away from me; I will weep bitterly, labour not to comfort me, because of the spoiling of the daughter of my people.

5 For it is a day of trouble, and of treading down, and of perplexity by the Lord GOD of hosts in the valley of vision, breaking down the walls, and of crying to the mountains.

6 And Elam bare the quiver with chariots of men and horsemen, and Kir uncovered the shield.

7 And it shall come to pass, that thy choicest valleys shall be full of chariots, and the horsemen shall set themselves in array at the gate.

8 And he discovered the covering of Judah, and thou didst look in that day to the armour of the house of the forest.

9 Ye have seen also the breaches of the city of David, that they are many: and ye gathered together the waters of the lower pool.

10 And ye have numbered the houses of Jerusalem, and the houses have ye broken down to fortify the wall.

11 Ye made also a ditch between the two walls for the water of the old pool: but ye have not looked unto the maker thereof, neither had respect unto him that fashioned it long ago.

12 And in that day did the Lord GOD of hosts call to weeping, and to mourning, and to baldness, and to girding with sackcloth:

13 And behold joy and gladness, slaying oxen, and killing sheep, eating flesh, and drinking wine: let us eat and drink; for to morrow we shall die.

14 And it was revealed in mine ears by the LORD of hosts, Surely this iniquity shall not be purged from you till ye die, saith the Lord GOD of hosts.

15 Thus saith the Lord GOD of hosts, Go, get thee unto this treasurer, even unto Shebna, which is over the house, and say,

16 What hast thou here? and whom hast thou here, that thou hast hewed thee out a sepulchre here, as he that heweth him out a sepulchre on high, and that graveth an habitation for himself in a rock?

17 Behold, the LORD will carry thee away with a mighty captivity, and will surely cover thee.

18 He will surely violently turn and toss thee like a ball into a large country: there shalt thou die, and there the chariots of thy glory shall be the shame of thy lord’s house.

19 And I will drive thee from thy station, and from thy state shall he pull thee down.

20 And it shall come to pass in that day, that I will call my servant Eliakim the son of Hilkiah:

21 And I will clothe him with thy robe, and strengthen him with thy girdle, and I will commit thy government into his hand: and he shall be a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and to the house of Judah.

22 And the key of the house of David will I lay upon his shoulder; so he shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open.

23 And I will fasten him as a nail in a sure place; and he shall be for a glorious throne to his father’s house.

24 And they shall hang upon him all the glory of his father’s house, the offspring and the issue, all vessels of small quantity, from the vessels of cups, even to all the vessels of flagons.

25 In that day, saith the LORD of hosts, shall the nail that is fastened in the sure place be removed, and be cut down, and fall; and the burden that was upon it shall be cut off: for the LORD hath spoken it.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jerusalem, the valley of vision, is caught feasting on the housetops while the enemy is at the gates — rejoicing instead of repenting. The chapter denounces Shebna, the steward who carved himself a grand tomb, and installs Eliakim in his place as a nail in a sure place — only for that nail, too, to be cut down and fall with all that hung on it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The burden of the valley of vision: What ails you now, that you are wholly gone up to the housetops? The valley of visions is Jerusalem, seat of divine revelations and visions and nursery of prophets, lying in a valley surrounded by hills higher than Zion and Moriah; panic-struck, the people personified go up on the flat balustraded roofs to look whether the enemy is near and partly to defend themselves from the roofs. The prophecy concerns Sennacherib's attack in Hezekiah's fourteenth year, though its language on infidelity and ruin also foreshadows Nebuchadnezzar's destruction under Zedekiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. You that are full of stirs, a tumultuous city, a joyous city: your slain men are not slain with the sword, nor dead in battle. The city was rather once joyous, for it cannot now be called joyous; their joy may have come because Sennacherib accepted Hezekiah's offer to renew tribute and they were glad for peace on any humiliating terms, or because of the Egyptian alliance, and their slain are not slain in open honourable battle but by the famine and pestilence of the coming siege, or slain in flight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. All your rulers are fled together, they are bound by the archers, and all found in you are bound together, who have fled from far. The rulers are rather generals, bound — rather taken — by the bow, for bowmen were light troops who skirmished in front and pursued fugitives; all found in the city, not merely generals, are taken, including those who fled from distant parts to Jerusalem as a place of safety, or fled afar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Therefore Isaiah said: Look away from me, I will weep bitterly, labour not to comfort me, because of the spoiling of the daughter of my people. Deep grief seeks to be alone, and while others feast joyously, Isaiah mourns in prospect of the disaster coming on Jerusalem, the daughter of his people as in chapter 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For it is a day of trouble, and of treading down, and of perplexity by the Lord God of hosts in the valley of vision, breaking down the walls, and of crying to the mountains. The day is sent by the Lord, a day of breaking the walls of the city, and the mournful cry of the townsmen reaches to the mountains and is echoed back, as Josephus describes the assault under Titus, to which the prophecy probably refers ultimately. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Elam bore the quiver with chariots of men and horsemen, and Kir uncovered the shield. Elam, the country east of the Lower Tigris answering to later Persia, was subject to Assyria in Sennacherib's time and furnished a contingent famed for the bow, used both in chariots and on horseback; Kir, another people subject to Assyria about the river Kur, uncovered the shield by taking off the leather covering that protected its embossed figures on the march, the quiver and shield expressing light and heavy armed troops. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Your choicest valleys will be full of chariots, and the horsemen will set themselves in array at the gate. The valleys east, north, and south of Jerusalem — Hinnom on the south being the richest — fill with chariots, and horsemen array themselves at the gate, where Rabshakeh stood at the upper pool close to the city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He discovered the covering of Judah, and you looked in that day to the armour of the house of the forest. The veil of Judah will be taken off, figuratively exposing her to shame as a captive, for Sennacherib dismantled all the defenced cities of Judah; and instead of looking to Jehovah, the people looked to their own strength in the house of armour built of cedar from Lebanon's forest by Solomon on Ophel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. You have seen also the breaches of the city of David, that they are many, and you gathered together the waters of the lower pool. The city of David is the upper city on Zion, surrounded by a wall of its own, yet even in it there will be breaches, matching Hezekiah's preparations; and the waters of the lower pool will be brought into the city by subterranean passages cut in Zion's rock, while Hezekiah stopped the outflowing waters to debar the foe from them and turned them into the city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. You have numbered the houses of Jerusalem, and the houses you have broken down to fortify the wall. They will number the houses to see which may be pulled down with least loss to the city and most advantage for repairing the walls and rearing towers, as Hezekiah did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. You made also a ditch between the two walls for the water of the old pool, but you have not looked unto the maker thereof, neither had respect unto him that fashioned it long ago. Hezekiah surrounded Siloah, from which the old or king's or upper pool took its rise, with a wall joined to Zion's wall on both sides, and between the two walls made a new pool receiving the former's waters, cutting the foe from his supply; yet the people did not look to the Maker of these defences, God, by whose command and aid they were made and who gave the fountain long ago, nor respect him who prepared this danger from afar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. In that day the Lord God of hosts called to weeping, and to mourning, and to baldness, and to girding with sackcloth. Usually priests gave the summons to national mourning, but now Jehovah himself gives it, the call consisting in the presence of a terrible foe, and baldness is the emblem of grief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. And behold, joy and gladness, slaying oxen and killing sheep, eating flesh and drinking wine: let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we will die. Notwithstanding Jehovah's call to mourning, many make the desperate state of affairs a reason for reckless revelry, drowning their sorrows as to their country in feasting, as in chapters 5 and 56. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Thus says the Lord God of hosts: Go, get yourself unto this treasurer, even unto Shebna, who is over the house, and say. This prophecy that Shebna will be deposed as prefect of the palace and Eliakim promoted is fulfilled when Shebna appears as a scribe and Eliakim over the household under Hezekiah; Shebna is singled out, the only individual so prophesied against in Isaiah, as one of the irreligious faction that set at naught the prophet's warnings, perhaps advising Hezekiah's temporary ignominious submission to Sennacherib. The treasurer is rather the king's friend or principal officer of the court, and "this" is prefixed contemptuously. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. What have you here, and whom have you here, that you have hewed yourself out a sepulchre here, as he that hews himself out a sepulchre on high, and that graves a habitation for himself in a rock? The prophet accosts Shebna at the very place where he was building a grand sepulchre for himself and his family, asking what business he has there and whom of his family is likely to be buried there, seeing he is soon to be deposed and carried into captivity; sepulchres were made in the highest rocks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Behold, the Lord will carry you away with a mighty captivity, and will surely cover you. The Lord will cast him away with a mighty throw, of a man, and cover him with shame where he is rearing a monument to perpetuate his fame, rolling him on continually as a ball to be tossed away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He will surely violently turn and toss you like a ball into a large country, and there you will die, and there the chariots of your glory will be the shame of your lord's house. He will whirl him round and round and cast him away, as a stone in a sling is whirled repeatedly before the string is let go, into a large country, perhaps Assyria, where his magnificent chariots will be there only as the disgrace of his lord's house, borne thither in ignominy instead of in glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. God will drive you from your station, and from your state he will pull you down. The state is the office itself, and God, by a change of persons, will pull Shebna from his station and office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. In that day God will call his servant Eliakim son of Hilkiah. Eliakim son of Hilkiah is supposed by Kimchi to be the same as Azariah son of Hilkiah, who perhaps had two names and was over the household in Hezekiah's time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. God will clothe him with your robe, strengthen him with your girdle, commit your government into his hand, and he will be a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem and to the house of Judah. The robe is the robe of office, the girdle the one in which the purse was carried and to which the sword was attached, often adorned with gold and jewels, and a father means a counsellor and friend to the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The key of the house of David God will lay upon his shoulder, so he will open and none will shut, and he will shut and none will open. The key is the emblem of his office over the house, since access rested with him, and keys are sometimes carried hanging from the kerchief on the shoulder, though the phrase is rather figurative for sustaining the government on one's shoulders. Eliakim, as his name implies, is plainly a type of the God-man Christ, son of David, of whom chapter 9 uses the same language, and Revelation 3 uses the latter clause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. God will fasten him as a nail in a sure place, and he will be for a glorious throne to his father's house. Large nails or pegs stood in ancient houses on which the ornaments of the family were suspended, so all that is valuable to the nation will rest securely on him; the throne is a resting-place to his family as applied to Eliakim, but a throne in the strict sense as applied to Messiah the antitype. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. They will hang upon him all the glory of his father's house, the offspring and the issue, all vessels of small quantity, from the vessels of cups even to all the vessels of flagons. It was customary to hang a house's valuables on nails, and the offspring and issue are the offshoots of the family, high and low, whom Eliakim will honour; the vessels of cups, of small capacity, answer to the low and humble offshoots, and the vessels of flagons, larger, to the high. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. In that day, says the Lord of hosts, the nail that is fastened in the sure place will be removed, and be cut down and fall, and the burden that was upon it will be cut off, for the Lord has spoken it. The nail fastened is Shebna, supposed firmly fixed in his post, and when the peg is suddenly cut down, all dependent on him — his emoluments, rank, and family — fall with it, for sin reaches in its effects even to the family of the guilty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms
