The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then all the children of Israel went out, and the congregation was gathered together as one man, from Dan even to Beersheba, with the land of Gilead, unto the LORD in Mizpeh.

2 And the chief of all the people, even of all the tribes of Israel, presented themselves in the assembly of the people of God, four hundred thousand footmen that drew sword.

3 (Now the children of Benjamin heard that the children of Israel were gone up to Mizpeh.) Then said the children of Israel, Tell us, how was this wickedness?

4 And the Levite, the husband of the woman that was slain, answered and said, I came into Gibeah that belongeth to Benjamin, I and my concubine, to lodge.

5 And the men of Gibeah rose against me, and beset the house round about upon me by night, and thought to have slain me: and my concubine have they forced, that she is dead.

6 And I took my concubine, and cut her in pieces, and sent her throughout all the country of the inheritance of Israel: for they have committed lewdness and folly in Israel.

7 Behold, ye are all children of Israel; give here your advice and counsel.

8 And all the people arose as one man, saying, We will not any of us go to his tent, neither will we any of us turn into his house.

9 But now this shall be the thing which we will do to Gibeah; we will go up by lot against it;

10 And we will take ten men of an hundred throughout all the tribes of Israel, and an hundred of a thousand, and a thousand out of ten thousand, to fetch victual for the people, that they may do, when they come to Gibeah of Benjamin, according to all the folly that they have wrought in Israel.

11 So all the men of Israel were gathered against the city, knit together as one man.

12 And the tribes of Israel sent men through all the tribe of Benjamin, saying, What wickedness is this that is done among you?

13 Now therefore deliver us the men, the children of Belial, which are in Gibeah, that we may put them to death, and put away evil from Israel. But the children of Benjamin would not hearken to the voice of their brethren the children of Israel.

14 But the children of Benjamin gathered themselves together out of the cities unto Gibeah, to go out to battle against the children of Israel.

15 And the children of Benjamin were numbered at that time out of the cities twenty and six thousand men that drew sword, beside the inhabitants of Gibeah, which were numbered seven hundred chosen men.

16 Among all this people there were seven hundred chosen men lefthanded; every one could sling stones at an hair breadth, and not miss.

17 And the men of Israel, beside Benjamin, were numbered four hundred thousand men that drew sword: all these were men of war.

18 And the children of Israel arose, and went up to the house of God, and asked counsel of God, and said, Which of us shall go up first to the battle against the children of Benjamin? And the LORD said, Judah shall go up first.

19 And the children of Israel rose up in the morning, and encamped against Gibeah.

20 And the men of Israel went out to battle against Benjamin; and the men of Israel put themselves in array to fight against them at Gibeah.

21 And the children of Benjamin came forth out of Gibeah, and destroyed down to the ground of the Israelites that day twenty and two thousand men.

22 And the people the men of Israel encouraged themselves, and set their battle again in array in the place where they put themselves in array the first day.

23 (And the children of Israel went up and wept before the LORD until even, and asked counsel of the LORD, saying, Shall I go up again to battle against the children of Benjamin my brother? And the LORD said, Go up against him.)

24 And the children of Israel came near against the children of Benjamin the second day.

25 And Benjamin went forth against them out of Gibeah the second day, and destroyed down to the ground of the children of Israel again eighteen thousand men; all these drew the sword.

26 Then all the children of Israel, and all the people, went up, and came unto the house of God, and wept, and sat there before the LORD, and fasted that day until even, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the LORD.

27 And the children of Israel enquired of the LORD, (for the ark of the covenant of God was there in those days,

28 And Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron, stood before it in those days,) saying, Shall I yet again go out to battle against the children of Benjamin my brother, or shall I cease? And the LORD said, Go up; for to morrow I will deliver them into thine hand.

29 And Israel set liers in wait round about Gibeah.

30 And the children of Israel went up against the children of Benjamin on the third day, and put themselves in array against Gibeah, as at other times.

31 And the children of Benjamin went out against the people, and were drawn away from the city; and they began to smite of the people, and kill, as at other times, in the highways, of which one goeth up to the house of God, and the other to Gibeah in the field, about thirty men of Israel.

32 And the children of Benjamin said, They are smitten down before us, as at the first. But the children of Israel said, Let us flee, and draw them from the city unto the highways.

33 And all the men of Israel rose up out of their place, and put themselves in array at Baaltamar: and the liers in wait of Israel came forth out of their places, even out of the meadows of Gibeah.

34 And there came against Gibeah ten thousand chosen men out of all Israel, and the battle was sore: but they knew not that evil was near them.

35 And the LORD smote Benjamin before Israel: and the children of Israel destroyed of the Benjamites that day twenty and five thousand and an hundred men: all these drew the sword.

36 So the children of Benjamin saw that they were smitten: for the men of Israel gave place to the Benjamites, because they trusted unto the liers in wait which they had set beside Gibeah.

37 And the liers in wait hasted, and rushed upon Gibeah; and the liers in wait drew themselves along, and smote all the city with the edge of the sword.

38 Now there was an appointed sign between the men of Israel and the liers in wait, that they should make a great flame with smoke rise up out of the city.

39 And when the men of Israel retired in the battle, Benjamin began to smite and kill of the men of Israel about thirty persons: for they said, Surely they are smitten down before us, as in the first battle.

40 But when the flame began to arise up out of the city with a pillar of smoke, the Benjamites looked behind them, and, behold, the flame of the city ascended up to heaven.

41 And when the men of Israel turned again, the men of Benjamin were amazed: for they saw that evil was come upon them.

42 Therefore they turned their backs before the men of Israel unto the way of the wilderness; but the battle overtook them; and them which came out of the cities they destroyed in the midst of them.

43 Thus they inclosed the Benjamites round about, and chased them, and trode them down with ease over against Gibeah toward the sunrising.

44 And there fell of Benjamin eighteen thousand men; all these were men of valour.

45 And they turned and fled toward the wilderness unto the rock of Rimmon: and they gleaned of them in the highways five thousand men; and pursued hard after them unto Gidom, and slew two thousand men of them.

46 So that all which fell that day of Benjamin were twenty and five thousand men that drew the sword; all these were men of valour.

47 But six hundred men turned and fled to the wilderness unto the rock Rimmon, and abode in the rock Rimmon four months.

48 And the men of Israel turned again upon the children of Benjamin, and smote them with the edge of the sword, as well the men of every city, as the beast, and all that came to hand: also they set on fire all the cities that they came to.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

All Israel gathers as one man at Mizpeh over the Gibeah outrage, hears the Levite's recital, and demands the men of Belial from Benjamin; Benjamin refuses and gathers twenty-six thousand swordsmen to Gibeah against four hundred thousand. Twice Israel is beaten, losing twenty-two thousand and then eighteen thousand men, before weeping, fasting and offering before the LORD at the house of God, where Phinehas stands before the ark, and receiving the promise that tomorrow the LORD will deliver Benjamin into their hand. On the third day an ambush takes Gibeah and burns it, Benjamin is encircled and cut down, and only six hundred men escape to the rock of Rimmon, while Israel burns Benjamin's cities. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. All Israel was gathered as one man from Dan to Beersheba with Gilead unto the Lord in Mizpeh, and JFB notes the immense sensation of Gibeah's tragedy convened a national assembly of chiefs from all parts, including the eastern tribes, at Mizpeh on Judah and Benjamin's confines, near Shiloh, where unto the Lord likely denotes consultation of the oracle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The chief of all the people, four hundred thousand footmen that drew sword, presented themselves in the assembly of the people of God, a muster that showed the whole nation, not a faction, had taken up the cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Benjamin heard Israel had gone up to Mizpeh, and Israel demanded how this wickedness was done, and JFB notes Benjamin had probably received a formal summons like other tribes but treated it with indifference or haughty disdain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The Levite, husband of the slain woman, answered with a brief and unvarnished recital of the outrage, and JFB notes force was used which he could not resist, his testimony corroborated by servant and old Ephraimite, and the facts alone produced one common sentiment of detestation and vengeance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He told how he came to Gibeah of Benjamin to lodge, and the men of Gibeah rose against him by night and beset the house, thinking to slay him, and instead humbled his concubine till she died. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He took his concubine, divided her, and sent her into all the inheritance of Israel, because they had committed lewdness and folly in Israel, the summons that had convened the assembly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Behold, ye are all children of Israel, he said, give here your advice and counsel, turning his recital into a demand for national judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. All the people arose as one man, saying none would go to his tent or house, and JFB notes this extraordinary unanimity showed the people were sound at the core, remembering their covenant and feeling the necessity of wiping out so foul a stain, though their resolutions were conditional on inquiry and a demand for satisfaction first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. This was the thing they would do to Gibeah: go up by lot against it, so that vengeance would proceed by ordered decision, not by mob rush. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They would take ten men of a hundred throughout the tribes to fetch victual for the people, that they might do to Gibeah according to all the folly committed in Israel, provisioning the expedition by a tribal levy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. All the men of Israel gathered against the city, knit together as one man, the unanimity of verse 8 now embodied in a single army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Israel sent men through all the tribe of Benjamin, asking what wickedness this was and demanding the sons of Belial in Gibeah be delivered to be put to death, so that evil might be put away from Israel, and JFB notes this request was just and reasonable, and Benjamin's refusal virtually made the tribe a party to the quarrel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But Benjamin would not hearken to their brethren, and JFB notes the tribe was not insensible to the crime's atrocity, but its patriotism and pride were offended by the other tribes' hostile demonstration, and it incurred awful responsibility by resistance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Benjamin gathered out of the cities unto Gibeah to go out to battle against Israel, and JFB notes nothing but blind passion and unbending obstinacy could impel them to take the field against such disparity of numbers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Benjamin numbered that day twenty-six thousand men that drew sword, besides seven hundred chosen men of Gibeah, the small but formidable force that would twice defeat a host. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Among all these people there were seven hundred chosen men left-handed, who could sling stones at a hair's breadth and not miss, and JFB describes the Hebrew sling, a leather thong with loop and lash, carrying full force two hundred yards in skilled hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
