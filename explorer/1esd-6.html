The Text — 1 Esdras 6 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Now in the second year of the reign of Darius Aggeus and Zacharias the son of Addo, the prophets, prophesied unto the Jews in Jewry and Jerusalem in the name of the Lord God of Israel, which was upon them.

2 Then stood up Zorobabel the son of Salatiel, and Jesus the son of Josedec, and began to build the house of the Lord at Jerusalem, the prophets of the Lord being with them, and helping them.

3 At the same time came unto them Sisinnes the governor of Syria and Phenice, with Sathrabuzanes and his companions, and said unto them,

4 By whose appointment do ye build this house and this roof, and perform all the other things? and who are the workmen that perform these things?

5 Nevertheless the elders of the Jews obtained favour, because the Lord had visited the captivity;

6 And they were not hindered from building, until such time as signification was given unto Darius concerning them, and an answer received.

7 The copy of the letters which Sisinnes, governor of Syria and Phenice, and Sathrabuzanes, with their companions, rulers in Syria and Phenice, wrote and sent unto Darius; To king Darius, greeting:

8 Let all things be known unto our lord the king, that being come into the country of Judea, and entered into the city of Jerusalem we found in the city of Jerusalem the ancients of the Jews that were of the captivity

9 Building an house unto the Lord, great and new, of hewn and costly stones, and the timber already laid upon the walls.

10 And those works are done with great speed, and the work goeth on prosperously in their hands, and with all glory and diligence is it made.

11 Then asked we these elders, saying, By whose commandment build ye this house, and lay the foundations of these works?

12 Therefore to the intent that we might give knowledge unto thee by writing, we demanded of them who were the chief doers, and we required of them the names in writing of their principal men.

13 So they gave us this answer, We are the servants of the Lord which made heaven and earth.

14 And as for this house, it was builded many years ago by a king of Israel great and strong, and was finished.

15 But when our fathers provoked God unto wrath, and sinned against the Lord of Israel which is in heaven, he gave them over into the power of Nabuchodonosor king of Babylon, of the Chaldees;

16 Who pulled down the house, and burned it, and carried away the people captives unto Babylon.

17 But in the first year that king Cyrus reigned over the country of Babylon Cyrus the king wrote to build up this house.

18 And the holy vessels of gold and of silver, that Nabuchodonosor had carried away out of the house at Jerusalem, and had set them in his own temple those Cyrus the king brought forth again out of the temple at Babylon, and they were delivered to Zorobabel and to Sanabassarus the ruler,

19 With commandment that he should carry away the same vessels, and put them in the temple at Jerusalem; and that the temple of the Lord should be built in his place.

20 Then the same Sanabassarus, being come hither, laid the foundations of the house of the Lord at Jerusalem; and from that time to this being still a building, it is not yet fully ended.

21 Now therefore, if it seem good unto the king, let search be made among the records of king Cyrus:

22 And if it be found that the building of the house of the Lord at Jerusalem hath been done with the consent of king Cyrus, and if our lord the king be so minded, let him signify unto us thereof.

23 Then commanded king Darius to seek among the records at Babylon: and so at Ecbatane the palace, which is in the country of Media, there was found a roll wherein these things were recorded.

24 In the first year of the reign of Cyrus king Cyrus commanded that the house of the Lord at Jerusalem should be built again, where they do sacrifice with continual fire:

25 Whose height shall be sixty cubits and the breadth sixty cubits, with three rows of hewn stones, and one row of new wood of that country; and the expences thereof to be given out of the house of king Cyrus:

26 And that the holy vessels of the house of the Lord, both of gold and silver, that Nabuchodonosor took out of the house at Jerusalem, and brought to Babylon, should be restored to the house at Jerusalem, and be set in the place where they were before.

27 And also he commanded that Sisinnes the governor of Syria and Phenice, and Sathrabuzanes, and their companions, and those which were appointed rulers in Syria and Phenice, should be careful not to meddle with the place, but suffer Zorobabel, the servant of the Lord, and governor of Judea, and the elders of the Jews, to build the house of the Lord in that place.

28 I have commanded also to have it built up whole again; and that they look diligently to help those that be of the captivity of the Jews, till the house of the Lord be finished:

29 And out of the tribute of Celosyria and Phenice a portion carefully to be given these men for the sacrifices of the Lord, that is, to Zorobabel the governor, for bullocks, and rams, and lambs;

30 And also corn, salt, wine, and oil, and that continually every year without further question, according as the priests that be in Jerusalem shall signify to be daily spent:

31 That offerings may be made to the most high God for the king and for his children, and that they may pray for their lives.

32 And he commanded that whosoever should transgress, yea, or make light of any thing afore spoken or written, out of his own house should a tree be taken, and he thereon be hanged, and all his goods seized for the king.

33 The Lord therefore, whose name is there called upon, utterly destroy every king and nation, that stretcheth out his hand to hinder or endamage that house of the Lord in Jerusalem.

34 I Darius the king have ordained that according unto these things it be done with diligence.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

In the second year of Darius, the prophets Haggai and Zechariah prophesy to the Jews in Jewry and Jerusalem, and the work resumes. The governor Tatnai writes to Darius asking by what authority they build; Darius has the records searched, the roll of Cyrus's decree is found at Ecbatane, and Darius ordains that the work be done with diligence, at the king's cost, with the vessels restored. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Aggeus and Zacharias. the prophets Haggai and Zechariah, whose prophesying restarts the work (verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Ecbatane. the palace in Media where the roll of Cyrus's decree was found (verse 23). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. JFB notes the difficulties and obstacles had first cooled the Jews' zeal and then led to abandonment of the work under a pretended belief that the time for rebuilding had not yet come, until after fifteen years of suspension Haggai and Zechariah upbraided them for sloth, negligence, and worldly selfishness and promised blessing if they resumed with vigor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. JFB notes the strong appeals and animating exhortations of the prophets gave a new impulse to the building, and that it was in the second year of Darius Hystaspes that the work, after long interruption, was resumed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. JFB notes Tatnai was governor on this side the river, that Syria formed one satrapy including Palestine, Phoenicia, and Cyprus, and that though superior to the native Jewish governors he never interfered with internal government except when order was threatened, so he repaired to Jerusalem to ascertain the real state of matters by personal inspection, probably incited by Samaritan complaints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. JFB notes the governor's inquiries reduced to two heads: by what authority the national temple was undertaken, and the names of the principal promoters and directors of the undertaking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. JFB notes the historian throws in the parenthetic remark that the eye of God was upon the elders, so that the formidable presence and authoritative inquiries of the satrap did not dispirit the Jews or afford triumph to their opponents, and matters were to go on till contrary orders arrived from Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. JFB notes God averted the threatening cloud and procured favor for the elders, so that they were not interrupted in their proceedings till communications with the court should be made and received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. JFB notes Tatnai transmitted all the information in an official report to his imperial master, with a recommendatory suggestion that search be made among the national archives at Babylon for the original decree of Cyrus, so the Jews' statement might be verified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. JFB notes Tatnai prudently resolved to repair to Jerusalem to ascertain the real state of matters by personal inspection and enquiry, in company with another dignified officer and his provincial council. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. JFB notes the house is described as built with great stones, literally stones of rolling, that is, stones of such extraordinary size that they could not be carried but had to be rolled or dragged along the ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. JFB notes the elders were left uninterrupted in their proceedings while the report went to court, so that the work went on prosperously in their hands until contrary orders arrived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. JFB notes the two heads of enquiry were authority for the undertaking and the names of the principal promoters, to which the Jews returned ready and distinct replies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. JFB notes the governor demanded the names of the principal promoters and directors, the second head of his enquiry, and that the Jews returned ready and distinct replies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. JFB notes the Jews were perfectly warranted to proceed with the building in virtue of Cyrus's edict, since a public decree remains in force until revoked, and the laws of the Medes and Persians changed not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. JFB notes Tatnai's report recorded that Cyrus had not only released the exiles and permitted their return for the express purpose of rebuilding the house of God, but by royal grace had restored the sacred vessels Nebuchadnezzar had carried off as trophies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. JFB notes the Jews' reply traced the work to Cyrus's decree, which released them for the express purpose of rebuilding and restored the sacred vessels, with commandment that the temple be built in its place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. JFB notes this was not part of the Jews' answer, since they could not have said the building had continued, knowing it had long ceased, but Tatnai used these expressions in his report, either viewing the stoppage as a temporary interruption or supposing the Jews were always working a little as they had means and opportunities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. JFB notes Tatnai accompanied his report with a recommendatory suggestion that search be made among the national archives at Babylon for the original decree of Cyrus, that the truth of the Jews' statement might be verified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. JFB notes the whole conduct of Tatnai and the general tone of his dispatch were marked by sound discretion and prudent moderation, free from party bias, evincing a desire only to do his duty, in favorable contrast with his predecessor Rehum. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. JFB notes Darius was Darius Hystaspes, on whose reign the decipherment of the cuneiform inscriptions at Behistun has thrown great light, and that the house of the rolls was the Babylonian register house, comparable to the chambers of clay tablets discovered in the palace at Nineveh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. JFB notes the decree granted the privilege of drawing from the provincial treasury of Syria whatever was required for furthering the work and providing sacrifice for the temple service, that the priests might daily pray for the health of the king and the prosperity of the empire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. JFB notes the decree granted drawing from the tribute beyond the river for the sacrifices of the Lord, including bullocks, rams, and lambs, so the priests might daily pray for the king and the empire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. JFB notes the expenses were to be given out of the king's goods, even the tribute beyond the river, for whatever was required for the work and the daily service of the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. JFB notes the sacrifices were provided that the priests might daily pray for the health of the king and the prosperity of the empire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. JFB notes the warning against altering the decree was specially directed against the turbulent and fanatical Samaritans, and that the edict's extremely favorable purport owed something to Darius's admiration for Cyrus and more to deep impressions of the being and providence of the God of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. JFB notes the warning was specially directed against the turbulent and fanatical Samaritans who would hinder the house, and that the edict's favorable purport proceeded from deep impressions made even on idolatrous people as to the being and providence of the God of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. JFB notes a concurrence of favorable events accelerated the restoration and infused new energy into the workmen, who labored with unabating assiduity till the temple was brought to completion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
