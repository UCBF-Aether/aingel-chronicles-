The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 23 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.

2 He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters.

3 He restoreth my soul: he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name’s sake.

4 Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me.

5 Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies: thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over.

6 Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life: and I will dwell in the house of the LORD for ever.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The shepherd psalm: the Lord as shepherd gives rest in green pastures and beside still waters, restores the soul and guides in right paths, walks with the sheep through the valley of the shadow of death, prepares a table before enemies, and goodness and mercy follow all life's days into God's house for ever. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want: one relation — shepherd and sheep — contains the whole provision of the psalm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He makes me lie down in green pastures and leads me beside still waters: rest and refreshment are given, not merely promised, and the waters are still because the Shepherd quiets them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He restores my soul and leads me in paths of righteousness for his name's sake: restoration after wandering and guidance in right ways both serve God's reputation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me; your rod and staff comfort me: presence, not the absence of danger, removes fear, and the shepherd's rod and staff both defend and guide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies, anoint my head with oil, and my cup runs over: honour and abundance are given in full view of enemies, not after they are gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life, and I will dwell in the house of the Lord for ever: goodness and mercy pursue the sheep as enemies once did, and the journey ends in God's house for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
