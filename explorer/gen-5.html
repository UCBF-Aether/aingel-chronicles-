The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 This is the book of the generations of Adam. In the day that God created man, in the likeness of God made he him;

2 Male and female created he them; and blessed them, and called their name Adam, in the day when they were created.

3 And Adam lived an hundred and thirty years, and begat a son in his own likeness, and after his image; and called his name Seth:

4 And the days of Adam after he had begotten Seth were eight hundred years: and he begat sons and daughters:

5 And all the days that Adam lived were nine hundred and thirty years: and he died.

6 And Seth lived an hundred and five years, and begat Enos:

7 And Seth lived after he begat Enos eight hundred and seven years, and begat sons and daughters:

8 And all the days of Seth were nine hundred and twelve years: and he died.

9 And Enos lived ninety years, and begat Cainan:

10 And Enos lived after he begat Cainan eight hundred and fifteen years, and begat sons and daughters:

11 And all the days of Enos were nine hundred and five years: and he died.

12 And Cainan lived seventy years and begat Mahalaleel:

13 And Cainan lived after he begat Mahalaleel eight hundred and forty years, and begat sons and daughters:

14 And all the days of Cainan were nine hundred and ten years: and he died.

15 And Mahalaleel lived sixty and five years, and begat Jared:

16 And Mahalaleel lived after he begat Jared eight hundred and thirty years, and begat sons and daughters:

17 And all the days of Mahalaleel were eight hundred ninety and five years: and he died.

18 And Jared lived an hundred sixty and two years, and he begat Enoch:

19 And Jared lived after he begat Enoch eight hundred years, and begat sons and daughters:

20 And all the days of Jared were nine hundred sixty and two years: and he died.

21 And Enoch lived sixty and five years, and begat Methuselah:

22 And Enoch walked with God after he begat Methuselah three hundred years, and begat sons and daughters:

23 And all the days of Enoch were three hundred sixty and five years:

24 And Enoch walked with God: and he was not; for God took him.

25 And Methuselah lived an hundred eighty and seven years, and begat Lamech.

26 And Methuselah lived after he begat Lamech seven hundred eighty and two years, and begat sons and daughters:

27 And all the days of Methuselah were nine hundred sixty and nine years: and he died.

28 And Lamech lived an hundred eighty and two years, and begat a son:

29 And he called his name Noah, saying, This same shall comfort us concerning our work and toil of our hands, because of the ground which the LORD hath cursed.

30 And Lamech lived after he begat Noah five hundred ninety and five years, and begat sons and daughters:

31 And all the days of Lamech were seven hundred seventy and seven years: and he died.

32 And Noah was five hundred years old: and Noah begat Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This is the genealogy from Adam to Noah: each life is counted in years, sons are named, and each father dies, except Enoch, who walked with God and was taken. It ends with Lamech naming his son Noah in hope of relief from the cursed ground. Verses 7, 8, 10, 11, 13, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20, 26, 27, 30 and 31 are genealogy entries carried in the Text panel, on which the study notes are silent. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. This is the book of the generations of Adam, tracing the line through Seth down to Noah in ten generations. Gill stresses only the direct line is given, not every branch, so the promised line stays traceable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 2. God created humanity male and female, blessed them and called their name Adam on the day they were created. Gill notes the shared name marks their union: one man and one woman joined as man and wife. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 3. Adam lived 130 years and fathered a son in his own likeness, after his image, named Seth. Gill stresses the contrast: Seth bears Adam's fallen likeness, not the unfallen image of God in which Adam was first created. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 4. After Seth, Adam lived 800 years and fathered other sons and daughters. The long life shows the early world filling through large families, not through Seth alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 5. All Adam's days were 930 years, and he died. Gill notes these are solar years, and the verse closes the first life with the sentence from Eden fulfilled: death came to Adam too. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 6. Seth lived 105 years and fathered Enos. The next link in the godly line is named at the age when fatherhood begins the next generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 9. Enos lived 90 years and fathered Cainan. The chain continues without break, each patriarch named as the father of the next in the line to Noah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 12. Cainan lived 70 years and fathered Mahalaleel. The genealogy moves steadily forward, preserving the line through which worship and promise would continue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 15. Mahalaleel lived 65 years and fathered Jared. Again the verse marks the handover from one generation to the next in the Seth line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 18. Jared lived 162 years and fathered Enoch. Jared's son is the Enoch whose walk with God will break the bare pattern of this chapter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 21. Enoch lived 65 years and fathered Methuselah. Enoch's fatherhood begins the generation that will live longest in this list. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 22. Enoch walked with God after fathering Methuselah for 300 years, and fathered other sons and daughters. Henry and JFB stress walking with God means a life of steady communion and obedience, not a single act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 23. All Enoch's days were 365 years. His life is notably shorter than his neighbours', which makes his end, not his age, the point of the story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 24. Enoch walked with God, and he was not, for God took him. JFB and Henry read this as translation: Enoch did not die as the others did, but was taken by God as a reward for his walk and a sign of life beyond death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 25. Methuselah lived 187 years and fathered Lamech. Methuselah, the oldest man in the list, becomes the father of the next link toward Noah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 28. Lamech lived 182 years and fathered a son. Lamech's son will be Noah, so this verse opens the last generation before the flood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 29. Lamech named him Noah, saying this one shall comfort us concerning our work and the toil of our hands because of the ground God cursed. The name is a prayer for relief from the curse of Genesis 3, not yet a prediction of the flood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 32. Noah was 500 years old and fathered Shem, Ham and Japheth. Gill notes they were not triplets: Shem is named first for dignity and for the line of promise, though Japheth appears to have been eldest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill

Connections to other passages

  • Genesis 3 — Lamech names Noah in hope of relief from the curse of the ground, and the study note ties that hope expressly to the curse of Genesis 3, not yet to a prediction of the flood. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 5:29, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
