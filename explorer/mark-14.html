The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 After two days was the feast of the passover, and of unleavened bread: and the chief priests and the scribes sought how they might take him by craft, and put him to death.

2 But they said, Not on the feast day, lest there be an uproar of the people.

3 And being in Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, as he sat at meat, there came a woman having an alabaster box of ointment of spikenard very precious; and she brake the box, and poured it on his head.

4 And there were some that had indignation within themselves, and said, Why was this waste of the ointment made?

5 For it might have been sold for more than three hundred pence, and have been given to the poor. And they murmured against her.

6 And Jesus said, Let her alone; why trouble ye her? she hath wrought a good work on me.

7 For ye have the poor with you always, and whensoever ye will ye may do them good: but me ye have not always.

8 She hath done what she could: she is come aforehand to anoint my body to the burying.

9 Verily I say unto you, Wheresoever this gospel shall be preached throughout the whole world, this also that she hath done shall be spoken of for a memorial of her.

10 And Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve, went unto the chief priests, to betray him unto them.

11 And when they heard it, they were glad, and promised to give him money. And he sought how he might conveniently betray him.

12 And the first day of unleavened bread, when they killed the passover, his disciples said unto him, Where wilt thou that we go and prepare that thou mayest eat the passover?

13 And he sendeth forth two of his disciples, and saith unto them, Go ye into the city, and there shall meet you a man bearing a pitcher of water: follow him.

14 And wheresoever he shall go in, say ye to the goodman of the house, The Master saith, Where is the guestchamber, where I shall eat the passover with my disciples?

15 And he will shew you a large upper room furnished and prepared: there make ready for us.

16 And his disciples went forth, and came into the city, and found as he had said unto them: and they made ready the passover.

17 And in the evening he cometh with the twelve.

18 And as they sat and did eat, Jesus said, Verily I say unto you, One of you which eateth with me shall betray me.

19 And they began to be sorrowful, and to say unto him one by one, Is it I? and another said, Is it I?

20 And he answered and said unto them, It is one of the twelve, that dippeth with me in the dish.

21 The Son of man indeed goeth, as it is written of him: but woe to that man by whom the Son of man is betrayed! good were it for that man if he had never been born.

22 And as they did eat, Jesus took bread, and blessed, and brake it, and gave to them, and said, Take, eat: this is my body.

23 And he took the cup, and when he had given thanks, he gave it to them: and they all drank of it.

24 And he said unto them, This is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many.

25 Verily I say unto you, I will drink no more of the fruit of the vine, until that day that I drink it new in the kingdom of God.

26 And when they had sung an hymn, they went out into the mount of Olives.

27 And Jesus saith unto them, All ye shall be offended because of me this night: for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered.

28 But after that I am risen, I will go before you into Galilee.

29 But Peter said unto him, Although all shall be offended, yet will not I.

30 And Jesus saith unto him, Verily I say unto thee, That this day, even in this night, before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice.

31 But he spake the more vehemently, If I should die with thee, I will not deny thee in any wise. Likewise also said they all.

32 And they came to a place which was named Gethsemane: and he saith to his disciples, Sit ye here, while I shall pray.

33 And he taketh with him Peter and James and John, and began to be sore amazed, and to be very heavy;

34 And saith unto them, My soul is exceeding sorrowful unto death: tarry ye here, and watch.

35 And he went forward a little, and fell on the ground, and prayed that, if it were possible, the hour might pass from him.

36 And he said, Abba, Father, all things are possible unto thee; take away this cup from me: nevertheless not what I will, but what thou wilt.

37 And he cometh, and findeth them sleeping, and saith unto Peter, Simon, sleepest thou? couldest not thou watch one hour?

38 Watch ye and pray, lest ye enter into temptation. The spirit truly is ready, but the flesh is weak.

39 And again he went away, and prayed, and spake the same words.

40 And when he returned, he found them asleep again, (for their eyes were heavy,) neither wist they what to answer him.

41 And he cometh the third time, and saith unto them, Sleep on now, and take your rest: it is enough, the hour is come; behold, the Son of man is betrayed into the hands of sinners.

42 Rise up, let us go; lo, he that betrayeth me is at hand.

43 And immediately, while he yet spake, cometh Judas, one of the twelve, and with him a great multitude with swords and staves, from the chief priests and the scribes and the elders.

44 And he that betrayed him had given them a token, saying, Whomsoever I shall kiss, that same is he; take him, and lead him away safely.

45 And as soon as he was come, he goeth straightway to him, and saith, Master, master; and kissed him.

46 And they laid their hands on him, and took him.

47 And one of them that stood by drew a sword, and smote a servant of the high priest, and cut off his ear.

48 And Jesus answered and said unto them, Are ye come out, as against a thief, with swords and with staves to take me?

49 I was daily with you in the temple teaching, and ye took me not: but the scriptures must be fulfilled.

50 And they all forsook him, and fled.

51 And there followed him a certain young man, having a linen cloth cast about his naked body; and the young men laid hold on him:

52 And he left the linen cloth, and fled from them naked.

53 And they led Jesus away to the high priest: and with him were assembled all the chief priests and the elders and the scribes.

54 And Peter followed him afar off, even into the palace of the high priest: and he sat with the servants, and warmed himself at the fire.

55 And the chief priests and all the council sought for witness against Jesus to put him to death; and found none.

56 For many bare false witness against him, but their witness agreed not together.

57 And there arose certain, and bare false witness against him, saying,

58 We heard him say, I will destroy this temple that is made with hands, and within three days I will build another made without hands.

59 But neither so did their witness agree together.

60 And the high priest stood up in the midst, and asked Jesus, saying, Answerest thou nothing? what is it which these witness against thee?

61 But he held his peace, and answered nothing. Again the high priest asked him, and said unto him, Art thou the Christ, the Son of the Blessed?

62 And Jesus said, I am: and ye shall see the Son of man sitting on the right hand of power, and coming in the clouds of heaven.

63 Then the high priest rent his clothes, and saith, What need we any further witnesses?

64 Ye have heard the blasphemy: what think ye? And they all condemned him to be guilty of death.

65 And some began to spit on him, and to cover his face, and to buffet him, and to say unto him, Prophesy: and the servants did strike him with the palms of their hands.

66 And as Peter was beneath in the palace, there cometh one of the maids of the high priest:

67 And when she saw Peter warming himself, she looked upon him, and said, And thou also wast with Jesus of Nazareth.

68 But he denied, saying, I know not, neither understand I what thou sayest. And he went out into the porch; and the cock crew.

69 And a maid saw him again, and began to say to them that stood by, This is one of them.

70 And he denied it again. And a little after, they that stood by said again to Peter, Surely thou art one of them: for thou art a Galilaean, and thy speech agreeth thereto.

71 But he began to curse and to swear, saying, I know not this man of whom ye speak.

72 And the second time the cock crew. And Peter called to mind the word that Jesus said unto him, Before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice. And when he thought thereon, he wept.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Two days before Passover the rulers plot, a woman anoints Jesus at Bethany, and Judas agrees to betray him for money (vv. 1–11). The Passover is prepared, and at supper Jesus gives bread and cup as his body and blood, predicts betrayal and Peter’s denial, and sings a hymn before going to the Mount of Olives (vv. 12–31). In Gethsemane he prays in agony while the disciples sleep (vv. 32–42); Judas arrives with an armed crowd, Jesus is arrested, tried before the council on conflicting witness, struck, and denied three times by Peter as the cock crows (vv. 43–72). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 14

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After two days was the feast of the Passover and unleavened bread, and the chief priests and scribes sought how they might take him by craft and put him to death. The Passover's nearness sharpened the rulers' plot, for they sought craft, not open arrest, to kill him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. But they said, Not on the feast day, lest there be an uproar of the people. They feared the people's uproar more than God's judgment, and their timing was governed by politics, not by the feast's meaning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Being in Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, as he sat at meat, there came a woman having an alabaster box of ointment of spikenard very precious, and she broke the box and poured it on his head. In a leper's house, a woman broke precious spikenard over his head, anointing him for burial before his enemies could bury him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. There were some that had indignation within themselves and said, Why was this waste of the ointment made? Indignation called devotion waste, for they measured love by price and saw only the cost, not the honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For it might have been sold for more than three hundred pence and have been given to the poor, and they murmured against her. Three hundred pence, a year's wages, might have fed the poor, and their murmuring used the poor as a cover for grudging Christ his honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Jesus said, Let her alone; why trouble her? She has wrought a good work on me. Christ defends her against trouble, and names her act a good work, for love's extravagance to him is never waste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For you have the poor with you always, and whenever you will you may do them good, but me you have not always. The poor remain for daily charity, but Christ's bodily presence was brief, and the hour for honouring him would not return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. She has done what she could: she is come beforehand to anoint my body to the burying. She did what she could, and her anointing anticipated burial, for she understood his death better than his disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Verily, wherever this gospel shall be preached throughout the whole world, this also that she has done shall be spoken of for a memorial of her. Her deed travels with the gospel worldwide, a memorial that outlasts the ointment's fragrance, for God remembers what men called waste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve, went to the chief priests to betray him to them. From the anointing table Judas went to the priests, and one of the twelve became the betrayer, a warning that office does not secure grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. When they heard it, they were glad and promised to give him money, and he sought how he might conveniently betray him. Gladness and money sealed the bargain, and Judas sought convenience, waiting for a quiet hour to deliver his master. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The first day of unleavened bread, when they killed the Passover, his disciples said, Where will you that we go and prepare that you may eat the Passover? The disciples' question about preparation shows they expected to keep the feast, and Christ's answer would direct them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He sent forth two disciples and said, Go into the city, and there shall meet you a man bearing a pitcher of water: follow him. A man bearing a pitcher, unusual work for a man, was the sign, and following him led them without asking, to the appointed house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Wherever he shall go in, say to the goodman of the house, The Master says, Where is the guestchamber, where I shall eat the Passover with my disciples? The Master's question claims the guestchamber by right, and the goodman's obedience shows Christ's authority over men's houses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He will show you a large upper room furnished and prepared: there make ready for us. A large, furnished, prepared upper room awaited them, for Christ's foresight had provided the place before they asked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. His disciples went forth, came into the city, found as he had said, and made ready the Passover. Finding exactly as he said confirmed their faith, and they made ready the feast that would become the Lord's Supper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. In the evening he came with the twelve. Evening brought him with the twelve, and the last supper gathered the whole apostolic company, betrayer included. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. As they sat and ate, Jesus said, Verily, one of you which eats with me shall betray me. The announcement at table, that a fellow-eater would betray him, turned the feast into a searching of hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. They began to be sorrowful and to say to him one by one, Is it I? And another said, Is it I? Sorrow and self-suspicion, not accusation of others, mark the disciples, for each feared himself rather than naming another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He answered, It is one of the twelve, that dips with me in the dish. One of the twelve, dipping in the same dish, is the betrayer, and shared bread makes the treachery fouler, not lesser. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The Son of man indeed goes, as it is written of him, but woe to that man by whom the Son of man is betrayed! Good were it for that man if he had never been born. Scripture's fulfilment does not excuse the betrayer, and woe beyond birth itself falls on him who sells his master. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. As they ate, Jesus took bread, blessed, broke it, gave to them, and said, Take, eat: this is my body. Blessing, breaking, and giving bread makes the supper: the bread is his body given, and eating is receiving him by faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. He took the cup, and when he had given thanks, he gave it to them, and they all drank of it. Thanks over the cup, shared by all, makes the cup common, for all drink the same new covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. He said to them, This is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many. The cup is the new testament's blood, shed for many, replacing the old covenant's sacrifices with his own offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Verily, I will drink no more of the fruit of the vine until that day that I drink it new in the kingdom of God. His abstinence looks to the kingdom, where the vine's fruit will be drunk new, and the supper is a pledge of that banquet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. When they had sung a hymn, they went out into the Mount of Olives. A hymn closed the supper, and they went out singing to the Mount of Olives, where prayer and betrayal awaited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Jesus said to them, All you shall be offended because of me this night, for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered. All would be offended, for the shepherd smitten scatters the sheep, and Scripture foretold the disciples' flight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. But after I am risen, I will go before you into Galilee. After rising, he will go before them into Galilee, and the promise of reunion follows the prediction of scattering, that hope may outlast shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. But Peter said, Although all shall be offended, yet will not I. Peter excepts himself from all, and his confidence, though loving, is the pride that precedes a fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Jesus said to him, Verily, this day, even in this night, before the cock crow twice, you shall deny me thrice. This very night, before the cock's second crowing, Peter would deny him thrice, a precise prediction that leaves no room for self-trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. But he spoke the more vehemently, If I should die with you, I will not deny you in any wise. Likewise also said they all. Vehemence increased with warning, and all joined Peter's vow, so that all shared the confidence that would fail. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. They came to a place named Gethsemane, and he said to his disciples, Sit here, while I shall pray. Gethsemane, the oil-press, is where he would be pressed, and sitting disciples were left while he prayed alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. He took with him Peter, James, and John, and began to be sore amazed and very heavy. The three who saw his glory now saw his heaviness, and amazement and heaviness together show a sorrow beyond words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. He said to them, My soul is exceeding sorrowful unto death: tarry here, and watch. Sorrow unto death is his confession, and tarrying to watch is the disciples' part, for his agony needed witnesses, not sleepers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. He went forward a little, fell on the ground, and prayed that, if it were possible, the hour might pass from him. Falling on the ground, he prayed for the hour to pass, if possible, and his prostration shows the weight of the cup he faced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. He said, Abba, Father, all things are possible to you; take away this cup from me: nevertheless not what I will, but what you will. Abba, Father, joins childlike trust with submission: the cup may be removed if the Father wills, but the Son's will yields to the Father's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. He came and found them sleeping, and said to Peter, Simon, do you sleep? Could you not watch one hour? One hour's watch was too much for Peter, who had vowed to die with him, and sleep exposed the vow's weakness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation: the spirit truly is ready, but the flesh is weak. Watching and praying guard against temptation, for a ready spirit in weak flesh needs both, and this is the rule for all disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. Again he went away and prayed, and spoke the same words. A second prayer repeats the same words, for true submission is not one utterance but a persevering surrender. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. When he returned, he found them asleep again, for their eyes were heavy, neither knew they what to answer him. Heavy eyes and no answer mark a second failure, for sorrow itself had made them drowsy, and shame left them speechless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. He came the third time and said, Sleep on now, and take your rest: it is enough, the hour is come; behold, the Son of man is betrayed into the hands of sinners. The third coming ends watching: sleep now, for the hour is come, and the Son of man is betrayed to sinners, not to saints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. Rise up, let us go; lo, he that betrays me is at hand. Rising to meet the betrayer, Christ goes willingly to his arrest, and his rising contrasts with the disciples' sleeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. Immediately, while he yet spoke, came Judas, one of the twelve, and with him a great multitude with swords and staves, from the chief priests, scribes, and elders. While he spoke, Judas arrived with an armed multitude from the rulers, and the arrest came as a military expedition against an unarmed teacher. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. He that betrayed him had given them a token, saying, Whomever I shall kiss, that same is he; take him, and lead him away safely. The kiss was the agreed token, turning affection's sign into betrayal's mark, and 'lead him safely' shows they feared rescue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. As soon as he came, he went straightway to him and said, Master, master, and kissed him. Straightway, with doubled 'Master', Judas kissed him, and haste and flattery crowned the treachery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. They laid their hands on him and took him. Hands laid on Christ fulfilled the rulers' craft, and taking him began the passion his predictions had foretold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. One of those standing by drew a sword, smote a servant of the high priest, and cut off his ear. A disciple's sword cut off an ear, for zeal without Christ's command wounds rather than defends, and the ear's loss shows the danger of carnal weapons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. Jesus answered and said, Are you come out, as against a thief, with swords and staves to take me? Swords and staves against a teacher are the rulers' confession of fear, for they came as against a thief, not as against a criminal tried by law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. I was daily with you in the temple teaching, and you took me not, but the scriptures must be fulfilled. Daily temple teaching had offered them open arrest, and their night arrest fulfilled Scripture, which ordained both his openness and their secrecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. They all forsook him and fled. All forsook and fled, fulfilling the smitten shepherd's scattering, and the disciples' vows dissolved at the first swords. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

A young man in a linen cloth followed, perhaps roused from sleep, and his following, though brief, shows one soul drawn after the fleeing twelve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. He left the linen cloth and fled from them naked. Leaving the cloth and fleeing naked, he escaped their hands at the cost of shame, a small picture of flight's disgrace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. They led Jesus away to the high priest, and with him were assembled all the chief priests, elders, and scribes. The whole council assembled at night for a trial, for the rulers' haste brooked no delay in condemning him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. Peter followed him afar off, even into the palace of the high priest, and sat with the servants and warmed himself at the fire. Following afar off, Peter sat with servants at a fire, and warming himself among enemies prepared his denial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. The chief priests and all the council sought for witness against Jesus to put him to death, and found none. A council seeking witnesses to kill, and finding none, shows the trial's injustice from its start, for justice seeks truth, not a verdict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 56. For many bore false witness against him, but their witness agreed not together. Many false witnesses contradicted one another, and their disagreement itself witnessed to his innocence, for lies cannot agree. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 57. There arose certain and bore false witness against him, saying. Certain witnesses rose with a new charge, twisted from his own words, to supply what the council lacked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

His saying about his body's temple was turned into a threat against the stone temple, for false witnesses pervert true words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Warming himself, Peter was recognised and charged with being with Jesus of Nazareth, the very association he would soon deny. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Supper and arrest — Mark 14, Luke 22, and John 18

Mark 14:22–26, 43–50 and Luke 22:19–20, 47–54 agree on the supper’s bread and cup as body and blood, and on Judas arriving with a multitude, taking Jesus, and leading him to the high priest’s house. The differences matter: Luke alone adds “this do in remembrance of me” to the bread; John’s supper chapter (John 13) gives footwashing and the new commandment rather than the bread-and-cup words, and John 18’s arrest adds Jesus’ garden dialogue — “Whom seek ye?” and “I am he,” at which the band falls backward — and the leading to Annas first. How these accounts fit together as history is not settled by the sources consulted for this page.
