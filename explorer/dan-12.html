The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Daniel 12 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–12; King James Version, Apocrypha for the additions)

1 And at that time shall Michael stand up, the great prince which standeth for the children of thy people: and there shall be a time of trouble, such as never was since there was a nation even to that same time: and at that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book.

2 And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt.

3 And they that be wise shall shine as the brightness of the firmament; and they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever.

4 But thou, O Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end: many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased.

5 Then I Daniel looked, and, behold, there stood other two, the one on this side of the bank of the river, and the other on that side of the bank of the river.

6 And one said to the man clothed in linen, which was upon the waters of the river, How long shall it be to the end of these wonders?

7 And I heard the man clothed in linen, which was upon the waters of the river, when he held up his right hand and his left hand unto heaven, and sware by him that liveth for ever that it shall be for a time, times, and an half; and when he shall have accomplished to scatter the power of the holy people, all these things shall be finished.

8 And I heard, but I understood not: then said I, O my Lord, what shall be the end of these things?

9 And he said, Go thy way, Daniel: for the words are closed up and sealed till the time of the end.

10 Many shall be purified, and made white, and tried; but the wicked shall do wickedly: and none of the wicked shall understand; but the wise shall understand.

11 And from the time that the daily sacrifice shall be taken away, and the abomination that maketh desolate set up, there shall be a thousand two hundred and ninety days.

12 Blessed is he that waiteth, and cometh to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days.

13 But go thou thy way till the end be: for thou shalt rest, and stand in thy lot at the end of the days.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

At that time Michael, the great prince who stands for Daniel’s people, shall stand up, and there shall be a time of trouble such as never was. Many who sleep in the dust shall awake, some to everlasting life and some to shame and everlasting contempt, and those who turn many to righteousness shall shine as the stars. The words are sealed until the time of the end, and Daniel is told to go his way, rest, and stand in his lot at the end of the days. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And at that time shall Michael stand up, the great prince which stands for the children of thy people, and there shall be a time of trouble, such as never was since there was a nation even to that same time, and at that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book. Michael signifies "Who is like God," Henry says, and his name, with the title of the great Prince, points out the Divine Saviour, who stood for the children of our people in their stead as a sacrifice, and stands for them in pleading at the throne of grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt. When God's gospel is preached, Henry says, many who sleep in the dust, both Jews and Gentiles, shall be awakened out of their heathenism or Judaism, and in the end the multitude that sleep in the dust shall awake, many to life and many to shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. And they that be wise shall shine as the brightness of the firmament, and they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever. There is glory reserved for all the saints in the future state, Henry says, for all that are wise, wise for their souls and eternity; and those who turn many to righteousness will share in the glory of those they have helped to heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. But thou, O Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end; many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased. The book is sealed to the time of the end, Henry's outline shows, when the prophecy's varied events, extending through so many successive ages even to the general resurrection, shall be understood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Then I Daniel looked, and behold, there stood other two, the one on this side of the bank of the river, and the other on that side of the bank of the river. Two others stand on either bank, Henry's account shows, as the final questioning begins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. And one said to the man clothed in linen, which was upon the waters of the river: How long shall it be to the end of these wonders? One of the angels asks how long it should be to the end of these wonders, Henry notes, and a solemn reply is made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. And I heard the man clothed in linen, which was upon the waters of the river, when he held up his right hand and his left hand unto heaven, and swore by him that lives for ever that it shall be for a time, times, and a half, and when he shall have accomplished to scatter the power of the holy people, all these things shall be finished. The period is a time, times, and a half, Henry explains, the period mentioned in chapter 7:25 and in Revelation, signifying 1260 prophetic days or years, beginning from the time when the power of the holy people should be scattered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. And I heard, but I understood not; then said I: O my Lord, what shall be the end of these things? Daniel heard but did not understand, Henry's account shows, and asked what the end of these things shall be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. And he said: Go thy way, Daniel, for the words are closed up and sealed till the time of the end. The words are closed up and sealed till the time of the end, Henry repeats, and Daniel is told to go his way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Many shall be purified, and made white, and tried, but the wicked shall do wickedly, and none of the wicked shall understand, but the wise shall understand. All will end well at last, Henry says: all opposing rule, principality, and power shall be put down, and holiness and love will triumph and be in honour to eternity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. And from the time that the daily sacrifice shall be taken away, and the abomination that makes desolate set up, there shall be a thousand two hundred and ninety days. The days are numbered from the taking away of the daily sacrifice, Henry's outline shows, within the same sealed reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Blessed is he that waits, and comes to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days. Blessed is he that waits and comes to the further number, Henry's reading shows, for the end, this end, shall come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. But go thou thy way till the end be, for thou shalt rest, and stand in thy lot at the end of the days. Daniel must comfort himself with the pleasing prospect of his own happiness in death, in judgment, and to eternity, Henry says; it was a comfort to Daniel, and is a comfort to all the saints, that whatever their lot is in the days of their lives, they shall have a happy lot in the end of the days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

  • Time, times, and a half. The period sworn to in the chapter, until the scattering of the power of the holy people is accomplished; the study note reports Henry’s reading of it as 1260 prophetic days or years, as in chapter 7:25 and Revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Trace (editorial — where the text comes from)

  • Source edition. The text above is King James Version (1611) for chs 1–12; King James Version, Apocrypha for the additions. Documented Sources & Method entry for Daniel
  • Translation and provenance notes. 14 chapters in the Ethiopian form: the Prayer of Azariah and the Song of the Three Children stand within ch. 3 (vv. 24–98 in this edition's numbering); ch. 13 is Susanna; ch. 14 is Bel and the Dragon — all restored from the KJV Apocrypha.
