The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Moab rebelled against Israel after the death of Ahab.

2 And Ahaziah fell down through a lattice in his upper chamber that was in Samaria, and was sick: and he sent messengers, and said unto them, Go, enquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron whether I shall recover of this disease.

3 But the angel of the LORD said to Elijah the Tishbite, Arise, go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and say unto them, Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that ye go to enquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron?

4 Now therefore thus saith the LORD, Thou shalt not come down from that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die. And Elijah departed.

5 And when the messengers turned back unto him, he said unto them, Why are ye now turned back?

6 And they said unto him, There came a man up to meet us, and said unto us, Go, turn again unto the king that sent you, and say unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that thou sendest to enquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron? therefore thou shalt not come down from that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die.

7 And he said unto them, What manner of man was he which came up to meet you, and told you these words?

8 And they answered him, He was an hairy man, and girt with a girdle of leather about his loins. And he said, It is Elijah the Tishbite.

9 Then the king sent unto him a captain of fifty with his fifty. And he went up to him: and, behold, he sat on the top of an hill. And he spake unto him, Thou man of God, the king hath said, Come down.

10 And Elijah answered and said to the captain of fifty, If I be a man of God, then let fire come down from heaven, and consume thee and thy fifty. And there came down fire from heaven, and consumed him and his fifty.

11 Again also he sent unto him another captain of fifty with his fifty. And he answered and said unto him, O man of God, thus hath the king said, Come down quickly.

12 And Elijah answered and said unto them, If I be a man of God, let fire come down from heaven, and consume thee and thy fifty. And the fire of God came down from heaven, and consumed him and his fifty.

13 And he sent again a captain of the third fifty with his fifty. And the third captain of fifty went up, and came and fell on his knees before Elijah, and besought him, and said unto him, O man of God, I pray thee, let my life, and the life of these fifty thy servants, be precious in thy sight.

14 Behold, there came fire down from heaven, and burnt up the two captains of the former fifties with their fifties: therefore let my life now be precious in thy sight.

15 And the angel of the LORD said unto Elijah, Go down with him: be not afraid of him. And he arose, and went down with him unto the king.

16 And he said unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Forasmuch as thou hast sent messengers to enquire of Baalzebub the god of Ekron, is it not because there is no God in Israel to enquire of his word? therefore thou shalt not come down off that bed on which thou art gone up, but shalt surely die.

17 So he died according to the word of the LORD which Elijah had spoken. And Jehoram reigned in his stead in the second year of Jehoram the son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah; because he had no son.

18 Now the rest of the acts of Ahaziah which he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ahaziah, injured in a fall, sends to Baal-zebub of Ekron about his recovery, and Elijah intercepts the messengers with the word that he will die. Two captains and their fifties are consumed by fire for seizing the prophet; the third humbles himself and is spared, and Elijah tells the king himself: he dies, and Jehoram succeeds. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After Ahab's death, Moab rebelled against Israel. Henry notes that when Ahaziah rebelled against the LORD, Moab revolted from him: sin weakens and impoverishes, and man's revolt from God is often punished by the rebellion of those who owe him subjection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Ahaziah fell through a lattice in his upper chamber in Samaria and was sick, sending messengers to inquire of Baalzebub, god of Ekron, whether he would recover. Henry observes that wherever we go there is but a step between us and death, and a man's house, though his castle, cannot secure him against God's judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The angel of the LORD told Elijah to meet the king's messengers and ask whether there was no God in Israel that they went to inquire of Baalzebub. Henry's lesson is that those who will not inquire of God's word for comfort shall hear it to their terror, whether they will or no. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Elijah was to tell Ahaziah that because he had sent to Baalzebub, he would not come down from his bed but would surely die. Henry notes the whole creation, groaning under man's sin, will at length sink and break like this lattice, and he is never safe who has God for his enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The messengers returned to Ahaziah sooner than expected, prompting the king to ask why they had come back. Henry frames their return as the first sign that God's word, not Baalzebub's oracle, would decide the king's fate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The messengers reported that a man had met them and sent them back with the word that Ahaziah would surely die for inquiring of Baalzebub. Henry stresses that the prophet's message confronted the king's idolatry directly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The king asked what manner of man had come up to meet them and spoken these words. Henry's passage shows Ahaziah, thunderstruck by the prophet's words, seeking to identify the man who dared pronounce his doom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. They described a hairy man with a girdle of leather about his loins, and the king said, "It is Elijah the Tishbite." Henry treats Elijah's recognizable figure as the voice of God confronting a king who had turned from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The king sent a captain with fifty men to Elijah, who sat on a hill; the captain ordered the man of God to come down by the king's command. Henry calls these haughty, daring sinners, whose challenge Elijah would answer not to secure himself but to prove his mission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Elijah answered that if he were a man of God, fire would come down from heaven and consume the captain and his fifty, and fire did so. Henry notes Elijah called fire to reveal God's wrath from heaven against ungodliness, acting by a divine impulse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The king sent another captain with fifty, who repeated the command to come down quickly. Henry's theme is obstinacy: the fatal end of the first captain did not teach the second submission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Elijah answered again, and fire from heaven consumed the second captain and his fifty. Henry observes that the LORD judges men's practices by their principles, and his judgment is according to truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The third captain went up, fell on his knees before Elijah, and begged that his life and the lives of his fifty servants might be precious in Elijah's sight. Henry notes this captain humbled himself and cast himself on the mercy of God and Elijah, learning submission from the fatal end of obstinacy in others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The third captain pleaded that fire had consumed the two former captains and their fifties, and begged that his life be precious. Henry draws the general lesson that there is nothing to be got by contending with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The angel of the LORD told Elijah to go down with the captain and not be afraid of him. Henry notes the courage of faith has often struck terror into the proudest sinner, and asks who can harm those whom God shelters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Elijah went down to the king and repeated the sentence: because Ahaziah had sent messengers to inquire of Baalzebub, as if there were no God in Israel, he would not come down from his bed but would surely die. Henry observes Ahaziah was so thunderstruck that neither he nor any about him offered the prophet violence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Ahaziah died according to the word of the LORD spoken by Elijah, and Jehoram reigned in his stead because Ahaziah had no son. Henry's closing warning is that many who think to prosper in sin are called hence, like Ahaziah, when they do not expect it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. The rest of Ahaziah's acts are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel. Henry ends the chapter urging all to seek the LORD while he may be found, before a sudden end like Ahaziah's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
