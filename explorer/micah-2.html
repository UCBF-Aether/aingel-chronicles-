The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Micah 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Woe to them that devise iniquity, and work evil upon their beds! when the morning is light, they practise it, because it is in the power of their hand.

2 And they covet fields, and take them by violence; and houses, and take them away: so they oppress a man and his house, even a man and his heritage.

3 Therefore thus saith the LORD; Behold, against this family do I devise an evil, from which ye shall not remove your necks; neither shall ye go haughtily: for this time is evil.

4 In that day shall one take up a parable against you, and lament with a doleful lamentation, and say, We be utterly spoiled: he hath changed the portion of my people: how hath he removed it from me! turning away he hath divided our fields.

5 Therefore thou shalt have none that shall cast a cord by lot in the congregation of the LORD.

6 Prophesy ye not, say they to them that prophesy: they shall not prophesy to them, that they shall not take shame.

7 O thou that art named the house of Jacob, is the spirit of the LORD straitened? are these his doings? do not my words do good to him that walketh uprightly?

8 Even of late my people is risen up as an enemy: ye pull off the robe with the garment from them that pass by securely as men averse from war.

9 The women of my people have ye cast out from their pleasant houses; from their children have ye taken away my glory for ever.

10 Arise ye, and depart; for this is not your rest: because it is polluted, it shall destroy you, even with a sore destruction.

11 If a man walking in the spirit and falsehood do lie, saying, I will prophesy unto thee of wine and of strong drink; he shall even be the prophet of this people.

12 I will surely assemble, O Jacob, all of thee; I will surely gather the remnant of Israel; I will put them together as the sheep of Bozrah, as the flock in the midst of their fold: they shall make great noise by reason of the multitude of men.

13 The breaker is come up before them: they have broken up, and have passed through the gate, and are gone out by it: and their king shall pass before them, and the LORD on the head of them.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Woe to those who devise iniquity on their beds and practise it at morning light because it is in the power of their hand, coveting fields and houses and oppressing a man and his heritage. The LORD devises evil against this family in return, and the chapter turns from false prophets who promise wine and strong drink to a promise that the remnant of Israel will be gathered like sheep in a fold, with their king breaking through before them. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Woe to those who devise evil by night on their beds and rise early to carry it out. Henry stresses it is bad to do mischief on a sudden thought, and much worse to do it with design and forethought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. They covet fields and houses and take them by violence, oppressing a man, his house and his heritage. Where covetousness reigns in the heart, Henry says, compassion is banished, and violence and fraud occupy the hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Therefore the Lord devises an evil against this family from which they cannot remove their necks, and they will walk haughtily no more. Woe to those from whom God turns away; the haughty and secure in prosperity are commonly most ready to despair in adversity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. In that day a taunting lament will be taken up: utterly spoiled, the people's portion changed, their fields divided to strangers. Such a calamity cuts them off from the congregation of the Lord and its privileges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Therefore none of them will cast a cord by lot in the congregation of the Lord. The sorest part of the calamity is being cut short in the enjoyment of the congregation's privileges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. They say to the prophets, "Prophesy not," hoping to escape shame. Henry answers that God will take them at their word, and their sin will become their punishment: the physician no longer attends the patient who will not be healed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The house of Jacob asks whether the Lord's Spirit is straitened, but his doings are not the problem; his words do good to the upright. Those who silence good ministers and stop the means of grace are enemies not only to God but to their country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. God's own people have risen up as an enemy, stripping the robe from travellers passing securely, as from men averse to war. Henry asks what bonds will hold those who have no reverence for God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The women of God's people are cast out of their pleasant houses, and his glory is taken from their children for ever. Sinners cannot expect to rest in a land they have polluted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Arise and depart, for this is not your rest: the polluted land will destroy you with a sore destruction. Henry applies it beyond Israel: this world is our passage, not our portion, our inn, not our home, and we have here no continuing city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. A man who walks in falsehood, prophesying wine and strong drink, will be exactly the prophet such a people wants. Since they will be deceived, Henry says, let them be deceived; teachers who recommend self-indulgence best suit such sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The Lord will surely assemble all Jacob and gather the remnant of Israel together, like sheep of Bozrah in the fold, a multitude making a great noise. Henry reads this of return from captivity, and beyond it of the conversion of the Jews to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The breaker has gone up before them; they break through the gate and go out, their king passing before them and the Lord at their head. Henry reads the breaker as the Lord Jesus opening the way to God and breaking Satan's fetters, so his people follow through their enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
