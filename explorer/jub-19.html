The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 19 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the first year of the first week in the forty-second jubilee, Abraham returned and dwelt opposite Hebron, that is Kirjath Arba, two weeks of years.

2 And in the first year of the third week of this jubilee the days of the life of Sarah were accomplished, and she died in Hebron.

3 And Abraham went to mourn over her and bury her, and we tried him [to see] if his spirit were patient and he were not indignant in the words of his mouth; and he was found patient in this, and was not disturbed. 4.

4 For in patience of spirit he conversed with the children of Heth, to the intent that they should give him a place in which to bury his dead.

5 And the Lord gave him grace before all who saw him, and he besought in gentleness the sons of Heth, and they gave him the land of the double cave over against Mamre, that is Hebron, for four hundred pieces of silver.

6 And they besought him, saying, " We shall give it to thee for nothing " ; but he would not take it from their hands for nothing, for he gave the price of the place, the money in full, and he bowed down before them twice ; and after this he buried his dead in the double cave.

7 And all the days of the life of Sarah were one hundred and twenty-seven years, that is, two jubilees and four weeks and one year : these are the days of the years of the life of Sarah.

8 This is the tenth trial wherewith Abraham was tried, and he was found faithful, patient in spirit.

9 And he said not a single word regarding the rumour in the land how that God had said that He would give it to him and to his seed after him, and he begged a place there to bury his dead ; for he was found faith-

10 ful, and was recorded on the heavenly tables as the friend of God.

11 And in the fourth year thereof he took a wife for his son Isaac and her name was Rebecca [the daughter of Bethuel, the son of Nahor, the brother of Abraham] the sister of Laban and daughter of Bethuel ; and Bethuel was the son of Melca, who was the wife of Nahor, the brother of Abraham.

12 And Abraham took to himself a third wife, and her name was Keturah, from among the daughters of his household servants, for Hagar had died before Sarah.

13 And she bare him six sons, Zimram, and Jokshan, and Medan, and Midian, and Ishbak, and Shuah, in the two weeks of years.

14 And in the sixth week, in the second year thereof, Rebecca bare to Isaac two sons, Jacob and Esau, and Jacob was a smooth and upright man, and Esau was fierce, a man of the field, and hairy, and Jacob dwelt in tents.

15 And the youths grew, and Jacob learned to write ; but Esau did not learn, for he was a man of the field and a hunter, and he learnt war, and all his deeds were fierce.

16 Abraham loves Jacob and blesses him.

17 And Abraham loved Jacob, but Isaac loved Esau.

18 And Abraham saw the deeds of Esau, and he knew that in Jacob should his name and seed be called ; and he called Rebecca and gave commandment regarding Jacob, for he knew that she (too) loved Jacob much more than Esau.

19 And he said unto her : " My daughter, watch over my son Jacob, For he shall be in my stead on the earth, And for a blessing in the midst of the children of

20 men, And for the glory of the whole seed of Shem.

21 For I know that the Lord will choose him to be a people for possession unto Himself, above all peoples that are upon the face of the earth.

22 And behold, Isaac my son loveth Esau more than Jacob, but I see that thou truly lovest Jacob.

23 Add still further to thy kindness to him, And let thine eyes be upon him in love ;

24 For he will be a blessing unto us on the earth from henceforth unto all generations of the earth.

25 Let thy hands be strong

26 And let thy heart rejoice in thy son Jacob ; For I have loved him far beyond all my sons.

27 He will be blessed for ever,

28 And his seed will fill the whole earth.

29 If a man can number the sand of the earth, His seed also will be numbered.

30 And all the blessings wherewith the Lord hath blessed me and my seed shall belong to Jacob and his seed alway.

31 And in his seed shall my name be blessed, and the name of my fathers, Shem, and

32 Noah, and Enoch, and Mahalalel, and Enos, and Seth, and Adam.

33 And these shall serve

34 To lay the foundations of the heaven,

35 And to strengthen the earth,

36 And to renew all the luminaries which are in the firmament." 26.

37 And he called Jacob before the eyes of Rebecca his mother, and kissed him, and blessed him, and said : " Jacob, my beloved son, whom my soul loveth, may God bless thee from above the firmament, and may He give thee all the blessings wherewith He blessed Adam, and Enoch, and Noah, and Shem ; and all the things of which He told me, and all the things which He promised to give me, may He cause to cleave to thee and to thy seed for ever, according to the days of heaven above the earth.

38 And the spirits of Mastema shall not rule over thee or over thy seed to turn thee from the Lord, who is thy God from henceforth for ever.

39 And may the Lord God be a father to thee and thou the first-born son, and to the people alway.

40 Go in peace, my son."

41 And they both went forth together from Abraham.

42 And Rebecca loved Jacob, with all her heart and with all her soul, very much more than Esau; but Isaac loved Esau much more than Jacob.

43 Abraham's Last Words to his Children and Grandchildren.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the first year of the first week in the forty-second jubilee, Abraham returned and dwelt opposite Hebron, that is Kirjath Arba, two weeks of years. Abraham returns to dwell opposite Hebron, Kirjath Arba, for two weeks of years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 2. In the first year of the third week of this jubilee the days of the life of Sarah were accomplished, and she died in Hebron. Sarah's life is accomplished and she dies in Hebron, in the first year of the third week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 3. Abraham went to mourn over her and bury her, and we tried him to see if his spirit were patient and he were not indignant in the words of his mouth; and he was found patient in this, and was not disturbed. JFB on Genesis 23:2 notes the 'mourning' describes conformity to the customary usage of sitting on the ground for a time, while the 'weeping' indicates the natural outburst of sorrow; here the mourning and burial negotiation itself is a trial of patience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For in patience of spirit he conversed with the children of Heth, to the intent that they should give him a place in which to bury his dead. JFB on Genesis 23:3 notes he stood up and bespoke their kind offices to aid him in obtaining possession of a cave belonging to Ephron, a wealthy neighbor; patience of spirit governs the negotiation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Lord gave him grace before all who saw him, and he besought in gentleness the sons of Heth, and they gave him the land of the double cave over against Mamre, that is Hebron, for four hundred pieces of silver. JFB on Genesis 23:9 notes Machpelah is the 'double cave,' and on 23:15 that the land is worth four hundred shekels; grace before all who saw him and gentleness in beseeching win the place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They besought him, saying, 'We shall give it to thee for nothing,' but he would not take it from their hands for nothing, for he gave the price of the place, the money in full, and he bowed down before them twice; and after this he buried his dead in the double cave. JFB on Genesis 23:11-16 notes the great show of generosity was only a show, Ephron joining field and cave and expecting costly presents in return, while the patriarch wished to make a purchase, asked the terms, and paid the money in presence of assembled witnesses, weighed; here he pays in full, bows twice, then buries his dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. All the days of the life of Sarah were one hundred and twenty-seven years, that is, two jubilees and four weeks and one year: these are the days of the years of the life of Sarah. JFB on Genesis 23:1 notes Sarah is the only woman in Scripture whose age, death, and burial are mentioned, probably to do honor to the venerable mother of the Hebrew people; here her 127 years are reckoned as two jubilees, four weeks and one year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. This is the tenth trial wherewith Abraham was tried, and he was found faithful, patient in spirit. The burial negotiation is the tenth trial, and its verdict is faithfulness and patience in spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. He said not a single word regarding the rumour in the land how that God had said that he would give it to him and to his seed after him, and he begged a place there to bury his dead. JFB on Genesis 23:3 notes Abraham's life of faith, his pilgrim state, had prevented him acquiring even so small a possession; he does not invoke the promise to claim the land, but begs a burial place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. This verse completes the verdict: he was found faithful, and was recorded on the heavenly tables as the friend of God. JFB on Genesis 25:7 calls Abraham 'the father of the faithful,' 'the friend of God'; here the tables record him as the friend of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. In the fourth year thereof he took a wife for his son Isaac, and her name was Rebecca, the daughter of Bethuel, the son of Nahor, the brother of Abraham, the sister of Laban and daughter of Bethuel; and Bethuel was the son of Melca, who was the wife of Nahor, the brother of Abraham. JFB on Genesis 24:1-3 notes Abraham's anxiety to see his son married was natural to a pastoral chief preserving the honor of his tribe, and still more to a patriarch with regard to the divine promise of a numerous posterity, with matrimonial arrangements made by parents within the tribe, from a higher motive: fear lest a Canaanitish marriage lead the son away from the true God; Rebekah's genealogy places her within Abraham's own kin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Abraham took to himself a third wife, and her name was Keturah, from among the daughters of his household servants, for Hagar had died before Sarah. JFB on Genesis 25:1 notes 'Abraham took a wife' is rather 'had taken,' for Keturah is called Abraham's concubine or secondary wife, and it is improbable he married after Sarah's death; Jubilees makes her a third wife from his household servants' daughters, after Hagar's death before Sarah's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. She bare him six sons, Zimram, and Jokshan, and Medan, and Midian, and Ishbak, and Shuah, in the two weeks of years. The six sons of Keturah are named, borne within two weeks of years, matching the six more sons promised in chapter 16. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. In the sixth week, in the second year thereof, Rebecca bare to Isaac two sons, Jacob and Esau, and Jacob was a smooth and upright man, and Esau was fierce, a man of the field, and hairy, and Jacob dwelt in tents. The twins are contrasted from birth: Jacob smooth and upright, dwelling in tents, against Esau fierce, hairy, a man of the field. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. The youths grew, and Jacob learned to write, but Esau did not learn, for he was a man of the field and a hunter, and he learnt war, and all his deeds were fierce. Jacob's learning is writing; Esau's learning is war, and all his deeds are fierce. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: Abraham loves Jacob and blesses him. It divides the twins' growth from Abraham's blessing of Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. Abraham loved Jacob, but Isaac loved Esau. The parental loves divide: Abraham to Jacob, Isaac to Esau. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. Abraham saw the deeds of Esau, and he knew that in Jacob should his name and seed be called; and he called Rebecca and gave commandment regarding Jacob, for he knew that she too loved Jacob much more than Esau. Abraham reads Esau's deeds, knows the calling is in Jacob, and charges Rebekah, who loves Jacob more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. He said unto her: 'My daughter, watch over my son Jacob, for he shall be in my stead on the earth, and for a blessing in the midst of the children of men.' The charge opens: watch over Jacob, who shall stand in Abraham's stead on earth, a blessing among the children of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. This verse completes the charge's opening: 'and for the glory of the whole seed of Shem.' Jacob is a blessing among men and the glory of Shem's whole seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. 'For I know that the Lord will choose him to be a people for possession unto himself, above all peoples that are upon the face of the earth.' The Lord will choose Jacob as a people for his own possession above all peoples on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. 'And behold, Isaac my son loveth Esau more than Jacob, but I see that thou truly lovest Jacob.' Abraham names the divided loves plainly: Isaac loves Esau more, Rebekah truly loves Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. 'Add still further to thy kindness to him, and let thine eyes be upon him in love.' Rebekah is charged to add kindness to Jacob and keep her eyes on him in love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. 'For he will be a blessing unto us on the earth from henceforth unto all generations of the earth.' Jacob will be a blessing on earth henceforth unto all generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. 'Let thy hands be strong.' Rebekah's hands are to be strong in the charge over Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. 'And let thy heart rejoice in thy son Jacob; for I have loved him far beyond all my sons.' Her heart is to rejoice in Jacob, whom Abraham has loved far beyond all his sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. 'He will be blessed for ever.' Jacob's blessing is forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. 'And his seed will fill the whole earth.' Jacob's seed will fill the whole earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. 'If a man can number the sand of the earth, his seed also will be numbered.' The sand of the earth gives the measure: if it can be numbered, so can Jacob's seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. 'And all the blessings wherewith the Lord hath blessed me and my seed shall belong to Jacob and his seed alway.' All Abraham's blessings pass to Jacob and his seed always. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. 'And in his seed shall my name be blessed, and the name of my fathers, Shem, and.' The names blessed in Jacob's seed begin: Abraham's own name, then his fathers, beginning with Shem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. This verse completes the fathers' names: 'Noah, and Enoch, and Mahalalel, and Enos, and Seth, and Adam.' The line runs from Shem through Noah, Enoch, Mahalalel, Enos and Seth back to Adam. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. 'And these shall serve.' The fathers named shall serve, in the work the next verses unfold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. 'To lay the foundations of the heaven.' Their service is to lay the foundations of heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. 'And to strengthen the earth.' Their service is to strengthen the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. 'And to renew all the luminaries which are in the firmament.' Their service is to renew all the luminaries in the firmament. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. He called Jacob before the eyes of Rebecca his mother, and kissed him, and blessed him, and said: 'Jacob, my beloved son, whom my soul loveth, may God bless thee from above the firmament, and may he give thee all the blessings wherewith he blessed Adam, and Enoch, and Noah, and Shem; and all the things of which he told me, and all the things which he promised to give me, may he cause to cleave to thee and to thy seed for ever, according to the days of heaven above the earth.' Before Rebekah's eyes, with a kiss, Abraham blesses Jacob with all the blessings of Adam, Enoch, Noah and Shem, and all God told and promised to Abraham, cleaving to Jacob and his seed forever, according to the days of heaven above earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. 'And the spirits of Mastema shall not rule over thee or over thy seed to turn thee from the Lord, who is thy God from henceforth for ever.' The spirits of Mastema shall not rule over Jacob or his seed to turn them from the Lord, their God henceforth forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. 'And may the Lord God be a father to thee and thou the first-born son, and to the people alway.' The Lord God is to be a father to Jacob, and Jacob the first-born son, to the people always. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. 'Go in peace, my son.' The blessing closes with dismissal in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. And they both went forth together from Abraham. Rebekah and Jacob go forth together from Abraham's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. And Rebecca loved Jacob, with all her heart and with all her soul, very much more than Esau; but Isaac loved Esau much more than Jacob. The divided loves are restated with full force: Rebekah loves Jacob with all heart and soul, Isaac loves Esau much more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: Abraham's last words to his children and grandchildren. It divides the blessing of Jacob from the farewell charge of chapter 20. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 41. And they both went forth together from Abraham. Rebekah and Jacob go forth together from Abraham's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. And Rebecca loved Jacob, with all her heart and with all her soul, very much more than Esau; but Isaac loved Esau much more than Jacob. The divided loves are restated with full force: Rebekah loves Jacob with all heart and soul, Isaac loves Esau much more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: Abraham's last words to his children and grandchildren. It divides the blessing of Jacob from the farewell charge of chapter 20. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 3. Abraham went to mourn over her and bury her, and we tried him to see if his spirit were patient and he were not indignant in the words of his mouth; and he was found patient in this, and was not disturbed. JFB on Genesis 23:2 notes the 'mourning' describes conformity to the customary usage of sitting on the ground for a time, while the 'weeping' indicates the natural outburst of sorrow; here the mourning and burial negotiation itself is a trial of patience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. He said not a single word regarding the rumour in the land how that God had said that he would give it to him and to his seed after him, and he begged a place there to bury his dead. JFB on Genesis 23:3 notes Abraham's life of faith, his pilgrim state, had prevented him acquiring even so small a possession; he does not invoke the promise to claim the land, but begs a burial place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. This verse completes the verdict: he was found faithful, and was recorded on the heavenly tables as the friend of God. JFB on Genesis 25:7 calls Abraham 'the father of the faithful,' 'the friend of God'; here the tables record him as the friend of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 4. For in patience of spirit he conversed with the children of Heth, to the intent that they should give him a place in which to bury his dead. JFB on Genesis 23:3 notes he stood up and bespoke their kind offices to aid him in obtaining possession of a cave belonging to Ephron, a wealthy neighbor; patience of spirit governs the negotiation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Lord gave him grace before all who saw him, and he besought in gentleness the sons of Heth, and they gave him the land of the double cave over against Mamre, that is Hebron, for four hundred pieces of silver. JFB on Genesis 23:9 notes Machpelah is the 'double cave,' and on 23:15 that the land is worth four hundred shekels; grace before all who saw him and gentleness in beseeching win the place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They besought him, saying, 'We shall give it to thee for nothing,' but he would not take it from their hands for nothing, for he gave the price of the place, the money in full, and he bowed down before them twice; and after this he buried his dead in the double cave. JFB on Genesis 23:11-16 notes the great show of generosity was only a show, Ephron joining field and cave and expecting costly presents in return, while the patriarch wished to make a purchase, asked the terms, and paid the money in presence of assembled witnesses, weighed; here he pays in full, bows twice, then buries his dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. All the days of the life of Sarah were one hundred and twenty-seven years, that is, two jubilees and four weeks and one year: these are the days of the years of the life of Sarah. JFB on Genesis 23:1 notes Sarah is the only woman in Scripture whose age, death, and burial are mentioned, probably to do honor to the venerable mother of the Hebrew people; here her 127 years are reckoned as two jubilees, four weeks and one year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. In the fourth year thereof he took a wife for his son Isaac, and her name was Rebecca, the daughter of Bethuel, the son of Nahor, the brother of Abraham, the sister of Laban and daughter of Bethuel; and Bethuel was the son of Melca, who was the wife of Nahor, the brother of Abraham. JFB on Genesis 24:1-3 notes Abraham's anxiety to see his son married was natural to a pastoral chief preserving the honor of his tribe, and still more to a patriarch with regard to the divine promise of a numerous posterity, with matrimonial arrangements made by parents within the tribe, from a higher motive: fear lest a Canaanitish marriage lead the son away from the true God; Rebekah's genealogy places her within Abraham's own kin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Abraham took to himself a third wife, and her name was Keturah, from among the daughters of his household servants, for Hagar had died before Sarah. JFB on Genesis 25:1 notes 'Abraham took a wife' is rather 'had taken,' for Keturah is called Abraham's concubine or secondary wife, and it is improbable he married after Sarah's death; Jubilees makes her a third wife from his household servants' daughters, after Hagar's death before Sarah's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
