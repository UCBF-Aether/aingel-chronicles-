The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And after these things I saw another angel come down from heaven, having great power; and the earth was lightened with his glory.

2 And he cried mightily with a strong voice, saying, Babylon the great is fallen, is fallen, and is become the habitation of devils, and the hold of every foul spirit, and a cage of every unclean and hateful bird.

3 For all nations have drunk of the wine of the wrath of her fornication, and the kings of the earth have committed fornication with her, and the merchants of the earth are waxed rich through the abundance of her delicacies.

4 And I heard another voice from heaven, saying, Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues.

5 For her sins have reached unto heaven, and God hath remembered her iniquities.

6 Reward her even as she rewarded you, and double unto her double according to her works: in the cup which she hath filled fill to her double.

7 How much she hath glorified herself, and lived deliciously, so much torment and sorrow give her: for she saith in her heart, I sit a queen, and am no widow, and shall see no sorrow.

8 Therefore shall her plagues come in one day, death, and mourning, and famine; and she shall be utterly burned with fire: for strong is the Lord God who judgeth her.

9 And the kings of the earth, who have committed fornication and lived deliciously with her, shall bewail her, and lament for her, when they shall see the smoke of her burning,

10 Standing afar off for the fear of her torment, saying, Alas, alas that great city Babylon, that mighty city! for in one hour is thy judgment come.

11 And the merchants of the earth shall weep and mourn over her; for no man buyeth their merchandise any more:

12 The merchandise of gold, and silver, and precious stones, and of pearls, and fine linen, and purple, and silk, and scarlet, and all thyine wood, and all manner vessels of ivory, and all manner vessels of most precious wood, and of brass, and iron, and marble,

13 And cinnamon, and odours, and ointments, and frankincense, and wine, and oil, and fine flour, and wheat, and beasts, and sheep, and horses, and chariots, and slaves, and souls of men.

14 And the fruits that thy soul lusted after are departed from thee, and all things which were dainty and goodly are departed from thee, and thou shalt find them no more at all.

15 The merchants of these things, which were made rich by her, shall stand afar off for the fear of her torment, weeping and wailing,

16 And saying, Alas, alas that great city, that was clothed in fine linen, and purple, and scarlet, and decked with gold, and precious stones, and pearls!

17 For in one hour so great riches is come to nought. And every shipmaster, and all the company in ships, and sailors, and as many as trade by sea, stood afar off,

18 And cried when they saw the smoke of her burning, saying, What city is like unto this great city!

19 And they cast dust on their heads, and cried, weeping and wailing, saying, Alas, alas that great city, wherein were made rich all that had ships in the sea by reason of her costliness! for in one hour is she made desolate.

20 Rejoice over her, thou heaven, and ye holy apostles and prophets; for God hath avenged you on her.

21 And a mighty angel took up a stone like a great millstone, and cast it into the sea, saying, Thus with violence shall that great city Babylon be thrown down, and shall be found no more at all.

22 And the voice of harpers, and musicians, and of pipers, and trumpeters, shall be heard no more at all in thee; and no craftsman, of whatsoever craft he be, shall be found any more in thee; and the sound of a millstone shall be heard no more at all in thee;

23 And the light of a candle shall shine no more at all in thee; and the voice of the bridegroom and of the bride shall be heard no more at all in thee: for thy merchants were the great men of the earth; for by thy sorceries were all nations deceived.

24 And in her was found the blood of prophets, and of saints, and of all that were slain upon the earth.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Babylon is fallen, is fallen, cries a mighty angel, and a voice from heaven calls God's people to come out of her, that they share neither her sins nor her plagues, for her sins have reached to heaven and she shall be rewarded double. Kings who committed fornication with her, merchants grown rich by her delicacies, and shipmasters who traded by sea all stand afar off lamenting her burning in one hour, while heaven, apostles and prophets are told to rejoice over her, and a mighty angel casts a millstone into the sea as the sign that Babylon shall be found no more. JFB's note adds that Babylon here means not literal Rome alone but the whole faithless church of both testaments. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After these things John saw another angel come down from heaven, having great power, and the earth was lightened with his glory. JFB notes the angel's power is authority, and the earth was illumined owing to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He cried mightily with a strong voice, saying, "Babylon the great is fallen, is fallen, and is become the habitation of devils, and the hold of every foul spirit, and a cage of every unclean and hateful bird." JFB notes the phrase is prophetical of her fall, still future, as the call to come out in verse 4 proves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For all nations have drunk of the wine of the wrath of her fornication, and the kings of the earth have committed fornication with her, and the merchants of the earth are waxed rich through the abundance of her delicacies. JFB notes the reference is not to earthly merchandise but to spiritual wares, indulgences, idolatries, and superstitions, wherewith the harlot, the apostate church, has made merchandise of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. John heard another voice from heaven, saying, "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues." JFB notes even in the Romish church God has a people, but their only safety is coming out at once, as Lot was warned out of Sodom and the first Christians out of Jerusalem before judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For her sins have reached unto heaven, and God hath remembered her iniquities. JFB notes her sins stand as a great heap, reaching so far as to come into close contact with heaven itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Reward her even as she rewarded you, and double unto her double according to her works: in the cup which she hath filled fill to her double. JFB notes this is addressed to the executioners of God's wrath, and that the double is of sorrow, contrasted with the double of joy Jerusalem shall receive for past suffering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. How much she hath glorified herself, and lived deliciously, so much torment and sorrow give her: for she saith in her heart, "I sit a queen, and am no widow, and shall see no sorrow." JFB notes her complete unconcerned security as to past, present, and future, since the world power is her husband and supporter, and Rome, like Babylon queen of the East, is queen of the West. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Therefore shall her plagues come in one day, death, and mourning, and famine; and she shall be utterly burned with fire: for strong is the Lord God who judgeth her. JFB notes death instead of her security, mourning instead of feasting, famine instead of luxurious delicacies, and fire, literal or mystical, symbolizing utter destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The kings of the earth, who have committed fornication and lived deliciously with her, shall bewail her and lament for her when they see the smoke of her burning. JFB contrasts this with the world's rejoicing over the two witnesses: men relish a religion that gives an opiate to conscience while leaving license to indulge lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Standing afar off for the fear of her torment, saying, "Alas, alas, that great city Babylon, that mighty city! for in one hour is thy judgment come." JFB notes God's judgments inspire fear even in the worldly, but it is short-lived, for the kings soon attach themselves to the beast in its last and worst shape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The merchants of the earth shall weep and mourn over her, for no man buyeth their merchandise any more. JFB notes the merchandise must be spiritual, since Rome was not a commercial city: the harlot did not witness against carnal luxury, the source of the merchants' gains, but conformed to it, caring for the wool, not the sheep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The merchandise of gold, silver, precious stones, pearls, fine linen, purple, silk, scarlet, thyine wood, ivory vessels, precious wood, brass, iron, and marble. JFB notes the fine linen manufacture was Egypt's, the type of the apostate church, in contrast to the fine linen God puts on Israel and on the New Testament church, the Bride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Cinnamon, odours, ointments, frankincense, wine, oil, fine flour, wheat, beasts, sheep, horses, chariots, slaves, and souls of men. JFB notes cinnamon was designed for the holy anointing oil but desecrated to vile uses, and that the harlot, especially Rome, has enslaved both bodies and souls of men, deriving her greatest gains from masses and indulgences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The fruits that thy soul lusted after are departed from thee, and all things which were dainty and goodly are departed from thee, and thou shalt find them no more at all. JFB notes this is a direct address to Babylon, whose autumn-ripe fruits of the soul's eager desire have perished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The merchants of these things, which were made rich by her, shall stand afar off for the fear of her torment, weeping and wailing. JFB notes they derived their riches from her, and stand afar off as the kings did, for fear of sharing her torment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. They say, "Alas, alas, that great city, that was clothed in fine linen, and purple, and scarlet, and decked with gold, and precious stones, and pearls! For in one hour so great riches is come to nought." JFB notes the merchants' lament echoes the harlot's array in chapter 17, now desolated in a single hour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Every shipmaster, and all the company in ships, and sailors, and as many as trade by sea, stood afar off. JFB notes vessels were freighted with pilgrims to various shrines, a source of gain to the papal see and to shipmasters and pilots, but mainly the passage refers in the mystical sense to all who share in the spiritual traffic of apostate Christendom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They cried when they saw the smoke of her burning, saying, "What city is like unto this great city!" JFB notes the praise closely resembles that given to the beast in chapter 13, whereas such praise is due to God alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. They cast dust on their heads, and cried, weeping and wailing, saying, "Alas, alas, that great city, wherein were made rich all that had ships in the sea by reason of her costliness! for in one hour is she made desolate." JFB notes the costly treasures, abstract for concrete, enriched all who traded by sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Rejoice over her, thou heaven, and ye holy apostles and prophets; for God hath avenged you on her. JFB notes there is more joy in heaven at the harlot's downfall than at that of the two beasts, since the most heinous sin is that of those who know God's word of grace and keep it not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. A mighty angel took up a stone like a great millstone, and cast it into the sea, saying, "Thus with violence shall that great city Babylon be thrown down, and shall be found no more at all." JFB compares the judgment on Egypt's hosts at the Red Sea and the foretold doom of Babylon in Jeremiah, noting the verse shows the prophecy is still to be fulfilled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The voice of harpers, musicians, pipers, and trumpeters shall be heard no more at all in thee, and no craftsman of whatsoever craft shall be found any more in thee, and the sound of a millstone shall be heard no more at all in thee. JFB notes musicians, painters, and sculptors desecrated their art to lend fascination to the sensuous worship of corrupt Christendom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The light of a candle shall shine no more at all in thee, and the voice of the bridegroom and of the bride shall be heard no more at all in thee: for thy merchants were the great men of the earth; for by thy sorceries were all nations deceived. JFB contrasts this with the city of God, where they need no candle because the Lord God gives them light, and the Bridegroom and Bride are present at the marriage supper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. In her was found the blood of prophets, and of saints, and of all that were slain upon the earth. JFB notes this proves Babylon means not merely literal Rome but the whole faithless church of both testaments, and gives three reasons for her downfall: the worldly greatness of her merchants, her sorceries, and her persecution of prophets and saints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
