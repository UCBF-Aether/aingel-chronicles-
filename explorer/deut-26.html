The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Deuteronomy 26 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it shall be, when thou art come in unto the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee for an inheritance, and possessest it, and dwellest therein;

2 That thou shalt take of the first of all the fruit of the earth, which thou shalt bring of thy land that the LORD thy God giveth thee, and shalt put it in a basket, and shalt go unto the place which the LORD thy God shall choose to place his name there.

3 And thou shalt go unto the priest that shall be in those days, and say unto him, I profess this day unto the LORD thy God, that I am come unto the country which the LORD sware unto our fathers for to give us.

4 And the priest shall take the basket out of thine hand, and set it down before the altar of the LORD thy God.

5 And thou shalt speak and say before the LORD thy God, A Syrian ready to perish was my father, and he went down into Egypt, and sojourned there with a few, and became there a nation, great, mighty, and populous:

6 And the Egyptians evil entreated us, and afflicted us, and laid upon us hard bondage:

7 And when we cried unto the LORD God of our fathers, the LORD heard our voice, and looked on our affliction, and our labour, and our oppression:

8 And the LORD brought us forth out of Egypt with a mighty hand, and with an outstretched arm, and with great terribleness, and with signs, and with wonders:

9 And he hath brought us into this place, and hath given us this land, even a land that floweth with milk and honey.

10 And now, behold, I have brought the firstfruits of the land, which thou, O LORD, hast given me. And thou shalt set it before the LORD thy God, and worship before the LORD thy God:

11 And thou shalt rejoice in every good thing which the LORD thy God hath given unto thee, and unto thine house, thou, and the Levite, and the stranger that is among you.

12 When thou hast made an end of tithing all the tithes of thine increase the third year, which is the year of tithing, and hast given it unto the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless, and the widow, that they may eat within thy gates, and be filled;

13 Then thou shalt say before the LORD thy God, I have brought away the hallowed things out of mine house, and also have given them unto the Levite, and unto the stranger, to the fatherless, and to the widow, according to all thy commandments which thou hast commanded me: I have not transgressed thy commandments, neither have I forgotten them.

14 I have not eaten thereof in my mourning, neither have I taken away ought thereof for any unclean use, nor given ought thereof for the dead: but I have hearkened to the voice of the LORD my God, and have done according to all that thou hast commanded me.

15 Look down from thy holy habitation, from heaven, and bless thy people Israel, and the land which thou hast given us, as thou swarest unto our fathers, a land that floweth with milk and honey.

16 This day the LORD thy God hath commanded thee to do these statutes and judgments: thou shalt therefore keep and do them with all thine heart, and with all thy soul.

17 Thou hast avouched the LORD this day to be thy God, and to walk in his ways, and to keep his statutes, and his commandments, and his judgments, and to hearken unto his voice:

18 And the LORD hath avouched thee this day to be his peculiar people, as he hath promised thee, and that thou shouldest keep all his commandments;

19 And to make thee high above all nations which he hath made, in praise, and in name, and in honour; and that thou mayest be an holy people unto the LORD thy God, as he hath spoken.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The first basket of the land's fruit is brought to the priest with a spoken confession retelling the whole story — a wandering Aramean was my father, Egypt, affliction, deliverance, and now this land flowing with milk and honey. Every third year the tithe stays at home for the Levite, stranger, fatherless and widow. The chapter closes with Israel avouching the Lord and the Lord avouching Israel. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The land is inheritance before it is harvest: firstfruits are owed because possession itself is gift, and the offering that opens the chapter is the acknowledgement of that gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. That you shall take of the first of all the fruit of the earth, which you shall bring of your land given you, and put it in a basket, and go to the place which the Lord shall choose to place his name there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The offerer professes, not merely presents: he tells the priest that the promised land has arrived, turning a basket of fruit into a spoken confession of God's fulfilled oath to the fathers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The priest receives the basket and sets it before the altar: the gift passes out of the offerer's hands into God's presence, so firstfruits are worship, not a fee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And you shall speak and say before the Lord, A Syrian ready to perish was my father, and he went down into Egypt, and sojourned there with a few, and became there a nation, great, mighty, and populous; the offerer must own the mean origin of his nation, Jacob called a Syrian, and a people poor and oppressed in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. In the first-fruits confession Israel must own its mean origin: the nation sojourned in Egypt as strangers, served there as slaves, and was poor, despised and oppressed, so that, now rich and great, it has no reason to be proud or forgetful of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The confession moves from affliction to audience: Israel cried, God heard and looked, so deliverance begins not with Israel's strength but with God's attention to its labour and oppression. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Exodus is told as God's hand, not Israel's escape: mighty hand, outstretched arm, terror, signs and wonders together say the deliverance was wholly God's work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The gift completes the journey: from Egypt's bondage to a land flowing with milk and honey, the same God who brought them out is the God who brings them in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And now, behold, I have brought the firstfruits of the land, which you, O Lord, have given me, and you shall set it before the Lord, and worship before the Lord; creature comforts are doubly sweet when seen flowing from the fountain of promise, and thankfulness for present mercies should join remembrance of former ones. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And you shall rejoice in every good thing which the Lord has given to you, and to your house, you, the Levite, and the stranger among you, tracing the streams to the Fountain of all consolation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. When you have made an end of tithing all the tithes of your increase the third year, which is the year of tithing, and have given it to the Levite, stranger, fatherless, and widow, that they may eat within your gates, and be filled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Then you shall say before the Lord, I have brought away the hallowed things out of my house, and also have given them to the Levite, stranger, fatherless, and widow, according to all your commandments, and I have not transgressed your commandments, neither have I forgotten them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The tithe confession is a clearing of conscience: nothing eaten in mourning, nothing put to unclean use, nothing given to the dead, so obedience is proved by what the offerer has refused to do with holy things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Look down from your holy habitation, from heaven, and bless your people Israel, and the land which you have given us, as you swore to our fathers, a land flowing with milk and honey; without God's blessing, what comfort can we take in the increase of the earth? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. This day the Lord your God has commanded you to do these statutes and judgments; you shall therefore keep and do them with all your heart, and with all your soul, not carelessly or hypocritically, but with your whole heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Avouching is mutual covenant language: Israel takes the Lord as its God and binds itself to walk in his ways and hearken to his voice, in the same breath as God takes Israel as his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. God's avouching answers Israel's: a peculiar people, kept by promise, whose distinctness is measured by keeping all his commandments, not by privilege alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And to make you high above all nations which he has made, in praise, and in name, and in honour, and that you may be a holy people to the Lord your God, as he has spoken; holiness is true honour, and the only way to everlasting honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

Glosses below are for words a first-time reader may stumble on in this chapter; they explain the chapter's own usage and add nothing to it.

  • Firstfruits (verses 1–4). The first of the land's produce, brought in a basket to the priest at the place God chooses, with a spoken confession of the whole story from Egypt to the land — an offering made because possession itself is gift, not only harvest.
