The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Corinthians 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And I, brethren, when I came to you, came not with excellency of speech or of wisdom, declaring unto you the testimony of God.

2 For I determined not to know any thing among you, save Jesus Christ, and him crucified.

3 And I was with you in weakness, and in fear, and in much trembling.

4 And my speech and my preaching was not with enticing words of man’s wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power:

5 That your faith should not stand in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God.

6 Howbeit we speak wisdom among them that are perfect: yet not the wisdom of this world, nor of the princes of this world, that come to nought:

7 But we speak the wisdom of God in a mystery, even the hidden wisdom, which God ordained before the world unto our glory:

8 Which none of the princes of this world knew: for had they known it, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory.

9 But as it is written, Eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither have entered into the heart of man, the things which God hath prepared for them that love him.

10 But God hath revealed them unto us by his Spirit: for the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God.

11 For what man knoweth the things of a man, save the spirit of man which is in him? even so the things of God knoweth no man, but the Spirit of God.

12 Now we have received, not the spirit of the world, but the spirit which is of God; that we might know the things that are freely given to us of God.

13 Which things also we speak, not in the words which man’s wisdom teacheth, but which the Holy Ghost teacheth; comparing spiritual things with spiritual.

14 But the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God: for they are foolishness unto him: neither can he know them, because they are spiritually discerned.

15 But he that is spiritual judgeth all things, yet he himself is judged of no man.

16 For who hath known the mind of the Lord, that he may instruct him? But we have the mind of Christ.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul reminds Corinth that he came not with excellency of speech or human wisdom but in weakness, fear, and demonstration of the Spirit, preaching Jesus Christ and him crucified. The deep things of God are revealed by the Spirit, whom believers have received, so the spiritual person discerns all things, having the mind of Christ, while the natural person cannot receive them. Interpreted Overview of this chapter's text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul came to Corinth as one of the foolish, weak, and despised instruments God uses, glorying in the Lord rather than in human wisdom. Though trained at Tarsus in secular learning, he declared the testimony of God without excellency of speech — the testimony of Christ, who is therefore God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Paul made it his one definite business among the Corinthians to know Jesus Christ and him crucified — his person and his office, the sum of the gospel. The seekers after human wisdom had played down the crucifixion to avoid offending learned hearers; Paul put it at the center. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Paul was with them in bodily weakness and in trembling — not personal fear, but an anxious conscientiousness to perform his duty aright. His trembling was the opposite of eye-service: a careful desire to be faithful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. His private speech and public preaching did not rest on persuasive words of human wisdom. Persuasion is man's means of moving men; God's means is demonstration by the Spirit, working outwardly by miracles then and inwardly on the heart now, leaving no doubt and inspiring implicit faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Corinthians' faith was meant to rest on God's power, not to owe its origin or continuance to human wisdom. A faith built on eloquence would stand or fall with the speaker. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Yet the gospel is no enemy of true wisdom — it is a wisdom infinitely higher than that of this age, spoken among the mature in Christian experience and knowledge. The rulers of this age, great and learned as they seem, are being brought to nothing, transient rather than immortal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The wisdom Paul speaks is God's wisdom in a mystery — not a secret to be kept, but what was once hidden in God's counsels and is now announced to all who will obey the truth. Foreordained before the ages, from eternity, it was appointed for our glory, now and hereafter, from the Lord of glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. None of this age's rulers knew this wisdom, and that is the strongest proof of the natural man's lack of heavenly wisdom. Had they known it, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory — the one who held glory in his own right before the world was, crucified in his humanity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. What eye has not seen nor heart imagined, God prepared for those who love him — Paul gives an inspired exposition of Isaiah, not a verbatim quote. Isaiah spoke to those waiting for Messiah as future; Paul speaks to those who love him as already come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. God revealed these things to the mature by his Spirit, who searches the deep things of God — his nature, attributes, and counsels. The Spirit delights to explore the infinite depths of his own divine mind and reveal them as believers are capable of receiving them, which proves the Spirit's personality and Godhead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. As no fellow man knows the hidden things of a man except that man's own spirit, so none knows the things of God except the Spirit of God. Any knowledge of God, therefore, must come by his Spirit alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Believers have received not the spirit of the world — the evil spirit at work in the disobedient — but the Spirit who comes from God, received only as his gift. The purpose is present experimental knowledge, to their comfort, of the things freely given by God, including the future good he has prepared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The apostles not only knew these things by the Spirit but also spoke them in words the Spirit taught. Spiritual things are expounded by comparing spiritual things with spiritual — Old Testament Scripture with the gospel, and gospel mysteries with Old Testament types — and spiritual truths can only be joined to spiritual words, not to words of worldly wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The natural man, governed by his animal soul with his spirit overridden and without the Spirit of God, does not receive the things of the Spirit, though they are offered to him. They seem foolishness to him, and he not only does not know them but cannot know them, so he has no wish to receive them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The spiritual man, in whom the Spirit rules, discerns all things by their true standard and has practical insight into gospel truth, though he is not infallible on every point. He himself is discerned by no ordinary man, for judging him would require knowing the mind of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Who has known the mind of the Lord so as to instruct him as his counselor? Natural men who judge spiritual men are virtually trying to set God right. Believers, in their measure, have the mind of Christ — and since Isaiah's words about Jehovah are applied here to Christ, Christ is Jehovah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • excellency of speech. Polished rhetoric; Paul says he deliberately did not come to Corinth with it. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • natural man. The person without the Spirit, who does not receive the things of God because they are spiritually discerned. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • mind of Christ. The Spirit-given outlook of believers, contrasted with the mind that would instruct the Lord. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
