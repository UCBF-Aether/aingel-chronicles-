The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word which came to Jeremiah from the LORD, saying,

2 Arise, and go down to the potter’s house, and there I will cause thee to hear my words.

3 Then I went down to the potter’s house, and, behold, he wrought a work on the wheels.

4 And the vessel that he made of clay was marred in the hand of the potter: so he made it again another vessel, as seemed good to the potter to make it.

5 Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying,

6 O house of Israel, cannot I do with you as this potter? saith the LORD. Behold, as the clay is in the potter’s hand, so are ye in mine hand, O house of Israel.

7 At what instant I shall speak concerning a nation, and concerning a kingdom, to pluck up, and to pull down, and to destroy it;

8 If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil that I thought to do unto them.

9 And at what instant I shall speak concerning a nation, and concerning a kingdom, to build and to plant it;

10 If it do evil in my sight, that it obey not my voice, then I will repent of the good, wherewith I said I would benefit them.

11 Now therefore go to, speak to the men of Judah, and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, saying, Thus saith the LORD; Behold, I frame evil against you, and devise a device against you: return ye now every one from his evil way, and make your ways and your doings good.

12 And they said, There is no hope: but we will walk after our own devices, and we will every one do the imagination of his evil heart.

13 Therefore thus saith the LORD; Ask ye now among the heathen, who hath heard such things: the virgin of Israel hath done a very horrible thing.

14 Will a man leave the snow of Lebanon which cometh from the rock of the field? or shall the cold flowing waters that come from another place be forsaken?

15 Because my people hath forgotten me, they have burned incense to vanity, and they have caused them to stumble in their ways from the ancient paths, to walk in paths, in a way not cast up;

16 To make their land desolate, and a perpetual hissing; every one that passeth thereby shall be astonished, and wag his head.

17 I will scatter them as with an east wind before the enemy; I will shew them the back, and not the face, in the day of their calamity.

18 Then said they, Come and let us devise devices against Jeremiah; for the law shall not perish from the priest, nor counsel from the wise, nor the word from the prophet. Come, and let us smite him with the tongue, and let us not give heed to any of his words.

19 Give heed to me, O LORD, and hearken to the voice of them that contend with me.

20 Shall evil be recompensed for good? for they have digged a pit for my soul. Remember that I stood before thee to speak good for them, and to turn away thy wrath from them.

21 Therefore deliver up their children to the famine, and pour out their blood by the force of the sword; and let their wives be bereaved of their children, and be widows; and let their men be put to death; let their young men be slain by the sword in battle.

22 Let a cry be heard from their houses, when thou shalt bring a troop suddenly upon them: for they have digged a pit to take me, and hid snares for my feet.

23 Yet, LORD, thou knowest all their counsel against me to slay me: forgive not their iniquity, neither blot out their sin from thy sight, but let them be overthrown before thee; deal thus with them in the time of thine anger.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

At the potter's house Jeremiah watches clay spoiled in the potter's hand and remade into another vessel, and hears that God deals likewise with nations: a nation threatened with plucking up may be spared if it turns, and a nation promised blessing may be broken if it does evil. The chapter ends with the plot against Jeremiah and his fierce prayer against his persecutors. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The word which came to Jeremiah from the Lord, saying. Henry's word comes to Jeremiah as he looks upon the potter's work: God darts into his mind two great truths, authority over nations and fixed rules of justice and goodness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Arise, and go down to the potter's house, and there I will cause thee to hear my words. Henry's prophet is sent to the potter's house to see before he hears: the work on the wheels is the sermon God will speak. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Then I went down to the potter's house, and, behold, he wrought a work on the wheels. Henry notes while Jeremiah looks upon the potter's work, God darts into his mind the truth of his authority: the potter works the wheels as he pleases, and so does God with kingdoms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. And the vessel that he made of clay was marred in the hand of the potter: so he made it again another vessel, as seemed good to the potter to make it. Henry's marred vessel remade as seemed good to the potter is the image of God's disposal: he may dispose of us as he thinks fit, and it would be as absurd for us to dispute this as for the clay to quarrel with the potter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Then the word of the Lord came to me, saying. Henry's word follows the sight: the potter's work seen, the Lord's word speaks, and the two great truths are declared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. O house of Israel, cannot I do with you as this potter? Says the Lord. Behold, as the clay is in the potter's hand, so are ye in mine hand, O house of Israel. Henry's first great truth is authority and power: God can form and fashion kingdoms and nations as he pleases, and Israel is clay in his hand as the vessel is in the potter's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. At what instant I shall speak concerning a nation, and concerning a kingdom, to pluck up, and to pull down, and to destroy it. Henry's second great truth tempers the first: he always goes by fixed rules of justice and goodness, so plucking up, pulling down, and destroying are spoken at an instant, yet never without rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. If that nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil that I thought to do unto them. Henry notes sincere conversion from the evil of sin will prevent the evil of punishment, as to persons, and to families, and nations: the pronounced evil is repented of when the nation turns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. And at what instant I shall speak concerning a nation, and concerning a kingdom, to build and to plant it. Henry's fixed rules work both ways: as plucking up follows sin, so building and planting are spoken at an instant to a nation, under the same justice and goodness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. If it do evil in my sight, that it obey not my voice, then I will repent of the good, wherewith I said I would benefit them. Henry warns that when God is coming against us in judgements, we may be sure it is for our sins: the nation that does evil and obeys not his voice loses the good promised to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Now therefore go to, speak to the men of Judah, and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, saying, Thus says the Lord: Behold, I frame evil against you, and devise a device against you: return ye now every one from his evil way, and make your ways and your doings good. Henry exhorts Judah and Jerusalem to repentance: return every one from his evil way, and make ways and doings good, before the framed evil falls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. And they said: There is no hope: but we will walk after our own devices, and we will every one do the imagination of his evil heart. Henry notes sinners call it liberty to live at large, whereas for a man to be a slave to his lusts is the very worst slavery: no hope, they say, and walk after their own devices, every one after his evil heart's imagination. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Therefore thus says the Lord: Ask ye now among the heathen, who has heard such things: the virgin of Israel has done a very horrible thing. Henry's horrible thing is Israel's alone: ask among the heathen, and none has heard such a thing as the virgin of Israel has done, forsaking God for idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Will a man leave the snow of Lebanon which comes from the rock of the field? Or shall the cold flowing waters that come from another place be forsaken? Henry notes when men are parched with heat, and meet with cooling, refreshing streams, they use them: in these things men will not leave a certainty for an uncertainty, yet Israel left the snow of Lebanon and the cold flowing waters for idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Because my people has forgotten me, they have burned incense to vanity, and they have caused them to stumble in their ways from the ancient paths, to walk in paths, in a way not cast up. Henry notes Israel left the ancient paths appointed by the divine law: they walked not in the highway, in which they might travel safely, but in a way in which they must stumble, such was the way of idolatry, and such is the way of iniquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. To make their land desolate, and a perpetual hissing; every one that passes thereby shall be astonished, and wag his head. Henry notes this made their land desolate, and themselves miserable: a perpetual hissing, with every passer-by astonished and wagging his head, is the stumbling way's end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. I will scatter them as with an east wind before the enemy; I will shew them the back, and not the face, in the day of their calamity. Henry warns calamities may be borne, if God smile upon us when under them; but if he is displeased, and refuses his help, we are undone: scattered as with an east wind, shown the back and not the face in calamity, is being undone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Then said they: Come, and let us devise devices against Jeremiah; for the law shall not perish from the priest, nor counsel from the wise, nor the word from the prophet. Let us smite him with the tongue, and let us not give heed to any of his words. Henry notes when the prophet called to repentance, instead of obeying the call, the people devised devices against him: smite him with the tongue, and give heed to none of his words, is the answer to the call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Give heed to me, O Lord, and hearken to the voice of them that contend with me. Henry's prophet appeals to God: give heed to me, and hearken to the contenders' voice, for the appeal is the prophet's refuge when devices are devised against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Shall evil be recompensed for good? For they have digged a pit for my soul. Remember that I stood before thee to speak good for them, and to turn away thy wrath from them. Henry notes thus do sinners deal with the great Intercessor, crucifying him afresh, and speaking against him on earth, while his blood is speaking for them in heaven: evil recompensed for good, a pit dug for the soul that stood before God to speak good for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Therefore deliver up their children to the famine, and pour out their blood by the force of the sword; and let their wives be bereaved of their children, and be widows; and let their men be put to death; let their young men be slain by the sword in battle. Henry's appeal asks the judgement the devices deserve: children to famine, blood by sword, wives widows, men put to death, young men slain in battle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Let a cry be heard from their houses, when thou shalt bring a troop suddenly upon them: for they have digged a pit to take me, and hid snares for my feet. Henry's cry from their houses, when a troop comes suddenly, answers the pit dug and snares hidden for the prophet's feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Yet, Lord, thou knowest all their counsel against me to slay me: forgive not their iniquity, neither blot out their sin from thy sight, but let them be overthrown before thee; deal thus with them in the time of thine anger. Henry closes that the prophet had done his duty to them, and the same will be our rejoicing in a day of evil: the Lord knows all their counsel to slay him, and the prophet commits the unforgiven iniquity to God's anger, having done his duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
