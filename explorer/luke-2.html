The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass in those days, that there went out a decree from Caesar Augustus that all the world should be taxed.

2 (And this taxing was first made when Cyrenius was governor of Syria.)

3 And all went to be taxed, every one into his own city.

4 And Joseph also went up from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth, into Judaea, unto the city of David, which is called Bethlehem; (because he was of the house and lineage of David:)

5 To be taxed with Mary his espoused wife, being great with child.

6 And so it was, that, while they were there, the days were accomplished that she should be delivered.

7 And she brought forth her firstborn son, and wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger; because there was no room for them in the inn.

8 And there were in the same country shepherds abiding in the field, keeping watch over their flock by night.

9 And, lo, the angel of the Lord came upon them, and the glory of the Lord shone round about them: and they were sore afraid.

10 And the angel said unto them, Fear not: for, behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy, which shall be to all people.

11 For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord.

12 And this shall be a sign unto you; Ye shall find the babe wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger.

13 And suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host praising God, and saying,

14 Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men.

15 And it came to pass, as the angels were gone away from them into heaven, the shepherds said one to another, Let us now go even unto Bethlehem, and see this thing which is come to pass, which the Lord hath made known unto us.

16 And they came with haste, and found Mary, and Joseph, and the babe lying in a manger.

17 And when they had seen it, they made known abroad the saying which was told them concerning this child.

18 And all they that heard it wondered at those things which were told them by the shepherds.

19 But Mary kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart.

20 And the shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for all the things that they had heard and seen, as it was told unto them.

21 And when eight days were accomplished for the circumcising of the child, his name was called JESUS, which was so named of the angel before he was conceived in the womb.

22 And when the days of her purification according to the law of Moses were accomplished, they brought him to Jerusalem, to present him to the Lord;

23 (As it is written in the law of the LORD, Every male that openeth the womb shall be called holy to the Lord;)

24 And to offer a sacrifice according to that which is said in the law of the Lord, A pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons.

25 And, behold, there was a man in Jerusalem, whose name was Simeon; and the same man was just and devout, waiting for the consolation of Israel: and the Holy Ghost was upon him.

26 And it was revealed unto him by the Holy Ghost, that he should not see death, before he had seen the Lord’s Christ.

27 And he came by the Spirit into the temple: and when the parents brought in the child Jesus, to do for him after the custom of the law,

28 Then took he him up in his arms, and blessed God, and said,

29 Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace, according to thy word:

30 For mine eyes have seen thy salvation,

31 Which thou hast prepared before the face of all people;

32 A light to lighten the Gentiles, and the glory of thy people Israel.

33 And Joseph and his mother marvelled at those things which were spoken of him.

34 And Simeon blessed them, and said unto Mary his mother, Behold, this child is set for the fall and rising again of many in Israel; and for a sign which shall be spoken against;

35 (Yea, a sword shall pierce through thy own soul also,) that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed.

36 And there was one Anna, a prophetess, the daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of Aser: she was of a great age, and had lived with an husband seven years from her virginity;

37 And she was a widow of about fourscore and four years, which departed not from the temple, but served God with fastings and prayers night and day.

38 And she coming in that instant gave thanks likewise unto the Lord, and spake of him to all them that looked for redemption in Jerusalem.

39 And when they had performed all things according to the law of the Lord, they returned into Galilee, to their own city Nazareth.

40 And the child grew, and waxed strong in spirit, filled with wisdom: and the grace of God was upon him.

41 Now his parents went to Jerusalem every year at the feast of the passover.

42 And when he was twelve years old, they went up to Jerusalem after the custom of the feast.

43 And when they had fulfilled the days, as they returned, the child Jesus tarried behind in Jerusalem; and Joseph and his mother knew not of it.

44 But they, supposing him to have been in the company, went a day’s journey; and they sought him among their kinsfolk and acquaintance.

45 And when they found him not, they turned back again to Jerusalem, seeking him.

46 And it came to pass, that after three days they found him in the temple, sitting in the midst of the doctors, both hearing them, and asking them questions.

47 And all that heard him were astonished at his understanding and answers.

48 And when they saw him, they were amazed: and his mother said unto him, Son, why hast thou thus dealt with us? behold, thy father and I have sought thee sorrowing.

49 And he said unto them, How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about my Father’s business?

50 And they understood not the saying which he spake unto them.

51 And he went down with them, and came to Nazareth, and was subject unto them: but his mother kept all these sayings in her heart.

52 And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

By Caesar’s decree Joseph and Mary travel to Bethlehem, where Jesus is born, laid in a manger, and announced to shepherds keeping watch by night, who find him exactly as the angel said (vv. 1–20). At Jerusalem he is circumcised, presented in the temple, and recognized by Simeon and Anna as the Lord’s salvation and a light to the Gentiles (vv. 21–40). The chapter closes twelve years later, with the boy Jesus found in the temple among the doctors, listening and asking questions, before returning to Nazareth in obedience and growing in wisdom, stature, and favour (vv. 41–52). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 2

What the chapter says, verse by verse

  • Verse 1. A decree from Caesar Augustus ordered all the world to be taxed. Henry notes the fulness of time was now come: God sends forth his Son, made of a woman and under the law, and even an emperor's decree serves that timing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. This taxing was first made when Cyrenius was governor of Syria. The historical detail anchors the birth in real imperial administration, not in legend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. All went to be taxed, each to his own city. The universal movement sets the stage for Joseph's journey to the city of his lineage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Joseph went up from Nazareth to Bethlehem, the city of David, because he was of David's house and lineage. The census thus brings the promised King's heir to David's city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Joseph travelled to be taxed with Mary, his espoused wife, who was great with child. The journey itself is part of the lowliness of the birth that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. While they were in Bethlehem, the days were accomplished for Mary to be delivered. Henry stresses the circumstances were very mean, yet this is the appointed hour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Mary brought forth her firstborn son, wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger, because there was no room in the inn. Henry notes Christ came to sojourn as at an inn, and the manger checks our vanity, ambition, and envy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Shepherds were abiding in the field, keeping watch over their flock by night. Henry observes the angels were sent not to the great but to poor, humble, pious, industrious shepherds in the business of their calling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The angel of the Lord came upon them, and the glory of the Lord shone round about them, and they were sore afraid. Divine glory breaks into ordinary night work, and fear is the first honest response. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Fear not: the angel brings good tidings of great joy, which shall be to all people. The message is joy before it is doctrine, and its scope is all peoples, not one class. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord. Henry notes it is still proclaimed in our ears that to us is born a Saviour, Christ the Lord, and these should be glad tidings to all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The sign is a babe wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger. The sign matches the message: the Saviour is found in lowliness, not in splendour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Suddenly a multitude of the heavenly host joined the angel, praising God. One herald becomes a choir, because the birth deserves heaven's own praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men. Henry explains that God's good-will in sending the Messiah redounds to his glory in the highest and brings peace, here put for all the good flowing from Christ taking our nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. When the angels departed, the shepherds said, let us go to Bethlehem and see this thing which the Lord has made known. Henry notes they lost no time, but came with haste; we are not out of the way of divine visits when employed in an honest calling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. They came with haste and found Mary, Joseph, and the babe lying in a manger. What was told them matches exactly what they find, confirming the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Having seen, they made known abroad the saying told them concerning this child. Henry notes they were satisfied, and spread word that he was the Saviour, even Christ the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. All who heard wondered at the things told them by the shepherds. Wonder is the right first response of hearers, even before full understanding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. But Mary kept all these things and pondered them in her heart. Henry urges the same pondering: we should be more delivered from errors in judgment and practice if we more fully ponder these things in our hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for all they had heard and seen, as it was told them. Their return to the flock is itself worship, because the word proved true. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. At eight days the child was circumcised, and his name called Jesus, as the angel had named him before conception. Henry notes he did not need circumcision's mortification of a corrupt nature, but it pledged his future perfect obedience to the whole law, even unto death for us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. When the days of purification were accomplished, they brought him to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord. Mary goes up to offer the appointed sacrifices, and Joseph presents the firstborn son according to the law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. As written in the law, every male that opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord. The presentation owns the child as the Lord's before he is ever the parents'. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. They offered a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons, according to the law. Henry's application is to present our children to the Lord who gave them, beseeching him to redeem them from sin and death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Simeon in Jerusalem was just and devout, waiting for the consolation of Israel, and the Holy Ghost was upon him. Henry notes the same Spirit that supported Simeon's hope also provided for his joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. It was revealed to Simeon by the Holy Ghost that he should not see death before he had seen the Lord's Christ. His long waiting had a promise attached to it, and the promise held him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Simeon came by the Spirit into the temple, just as the parents brought the child to do for him after the custom of the law. Henry notes those who would see Christ must go to his temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Then Simeon took the child up in his arms and blessed God. The old man's arms hold the salvation he had waited for, and blessing is his first act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. For mine eyes have seen thy salvation. Simeon confesses this child in his arms is the Saviour, the salvation itself, of God's appointing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Which thou hast prepared before the face of all people. Salvation is prepared openly, not as a secret for one nation alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. A light to lighten the Gentiles, and the glory of thy people Israel. The same child is light to outsiders and glory to Israel, holding both promises together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Joseph and Mary marvelled at the things spoken of him. Even parents who knew the angels' words afresh marvel at what Simeon sees in their child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry notes Jesus, his doctrine, and his people are still spoken against, and his preached word remains the touchstone of men's characters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. A sword shall pierce through Mary's own soul also, that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed.   • Verse 36. Anna, a prophetess of the tribe of Aser, was of great age, having lived seven years with her husband from her virginity. She is introduced as a second aged witness, matching Simeon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Widowed about fourscore and four years, Anna departed not from the temple, but served God with fastings and prayers night and day. Henry notes she was always in a praying spirit, and God left himself not without witness even when much evil was in the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Coming in that instant, Anna gave thanks to the Lord and spoke of him to all who looked for redemption in Jerusalem. Henry's lesson is that those to whom Christ is made known have great reason to thank the Lord and to teach others concerning him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. When they had performed all things according to the law, they returned to Galilee, to Nazareth. Obedience to the law frames the whole infancy: nothing is skipped, even by the law's own Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. The child grew, waxed strong in spirit, filled with wisdom, and the grace of God was upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. His parents went to Jerusalem every year at the feast of the passover. Yearly worship was the family's habit, and the boy is raised inside it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. When he was twelve years old, they went up after the custom of the feast. Henry notes it is for the honour of Christ that children attend public worship, and his parents stayed all seven days of the feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. When the days were fulfilled, as they returned, the child Jesus tarried behind in Jerusalem, unknown to Joseph and Mary. The loss is real, and it happens at the end of an ordinance faithfully kept. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Supposing him in the company, they went a day's journey and sought him among kinsfolk and acquaintance. Henry's counsel applies: those who have lost their comforts in Christ must bethink themselves where, when, and how they lost them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Not finding him, they turned back to Jerusalem, seeking him. Recovery requires returning, not pressing on and hoping he will catch up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. After three days they found him in the temple, sitting among the doctors, hearing them and asking questions. Henry notes they found him where the doctors kept their schools, hearkening, proposing questions, and answering with wisdom that delighted his hearers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. All who heard him were astonished at his understanding and answers. The astonishment is at a twelve-year-old's grasp, not at display: he listens and asks as well as answers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. When they saw him they were amazed, and his mother asked why he had thus dealt with them, since she and Joseph had sought him sorrowing. Henry notes those who seek Christ in sorrow shall find him with the greater joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. How is it that ye sought me? Wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business? Henry quotes the saying in full: I ought to be in my Father's house, at my Father's work, and children of God must make all other concerns give way to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. They understood not the saying he spoke to them. Henry adds that we must not think God's sayings obscure merely because at first they are dark; what is dark may afterwards become plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. He went down with them to Nazareth and was subject to them, but his mother kept all these sayings in her heart. Henry draws the example of true greatness: to know one's own place and office, even for the Son of God, who obeyed earthly parents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man. The hidden years end as they began: real human growth, in wisdom and favour alike, under God's grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry notes they found him where the doctors kept their schools, hearkening, proposing questions, and answering with wisdom that delighted his hearers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. All who heard him were astonished at his understanding and answers. The astonishment is at a twelve-year-old's grasp, not at display: he listens and asks as well as answers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. When they saw him they were amazed, and his mother asked why he had thus dealt with them, since she and Joseph had sought him sorrowing. Henry notes those who seek Christ in sorrow shall find him with the greater joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. How is it that ye sought me? Wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business? Henry quotes the saying in full: I ought to be in my Father's house, at my Father's work, and children of God must make all other concerns give way to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. They understood not the saying he spoke to them. Henry adds that we must not think God's sayings obscure merely because at first they are dark; what is dark may afterwards become plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. He went down with them to Nazareth and was subject to them, but his mother kept all these sayings in her heart. Henry draws the example of true greatness: to know one's own place and office, even for the Son of God, who obeyed earthly parents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man. The hidden years end as they began: real human growth, in wisdom and favour alike, under God's grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
