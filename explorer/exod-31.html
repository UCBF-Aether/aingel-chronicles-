The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 31 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 See, I have called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah:

3 And I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship,

4 To devise cunning works, to work in gold, and in silver, and in brass,

5 And in cutting of stones, to set them, and in carving of timber, to work in all manner of workmanship.

6 And I, behold, I have given with him Aholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan: and in the hearts of all that are wise hearted I have put wisdom, that they may make all that I have commanded thee;

7 The tabernacle of the congregation, and the ark of the testimony, and the mercy seat that is thereupon, and all the furniture of the tabernacle,

8 And the table and his furniture, and the pure candlestick with all his furniture, and the altar of incense,

9 And the altar of burnt offering with all his furniture, and the laver and his foot,

10 And the cloths of service, and the holy garments for Aaron the priest, and the garments of his sons, to minister in the priest’s office,

11 And the anointing oil, and sweet incense for the holy place: according to all that I have commanded thee shall they do.

12 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

13 Speak thou also unto the children of Israel, saying, Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep: for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations; that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you.

14 Ye shall keep the sabbath therefore; for it is holy unto you: every one that defileth it shall surely be put to death: for whosoever doeth any work therein, that soul shall be cut off from among his people.

15 Six days may work be done; but in the seventh is the sabbath of rest, holy to the LORD: whosoever doeth any work in the sabbath day, he shall surely be put to death.

16 Wherefore the children of Israel shall keep the sabbath, to observe the sabbath throughout their generations, for a perpetual covenant.

17 It is a sign between me and the children of Israel for ever: for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed.

18 And he gave unto Moses, when he had made an end of communing with him upon mount Sinai, two tables of testimony, tables of stone, written with the finger of God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God calls Bezaleel of Judah and Aholiab of Dan by name, filling them with wisdom, understanding, and knowledge for every craft needed for the tabernacle. Then the Sabbath is given again as a perpetual sign between God and Israel. At the end, on Mount Sinai, God gives Moses the two tables of testimony, written with the finger of God. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And the LORD spake unto Moses, giving the workmen for the work he had commanded. Henry notes the honour which comes from God is always attended with a work to be done, and to be employed for God is high honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. See, I have called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah. Henry observes the call was by name: God chose a particular man from Judah, and named him, for the work of his house was personal, not anonymous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship. Henry notes the Israelites, who had been masons and bricklayers in Egypt, were not qualified for curious workmanship, but the Spirit who gave the apostles utterance miraculously gave Bezaleel the skill that was wanting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. To devise cunning works, to work in gold, and in silver, and in brass. Henry observes the Spirit's gifts covered design as well as execution: to devise cunning works, and then to work them in gold, silver, and brass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And in cutting of stones, to set them, and in carving of timber, to work in all manner of workmanship. Henry notes the skill extended to stone and timber alike: the craftsman was fitted for every material the tabernacle required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And I, behold, I have given with him Aholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan, and in the hearts of all that are wise hearted I have put wisdom, that they may make all that I have commanded thee. Henry observes those whom God calls to any service, he will find or make fit for it, and the Lord gives different gifts to different persons, so let each mind his proper work, remembering that whatever wisdom any possesses, the Lord put it in the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The tabernacle of the congregation, and the ark of the testimony, and the mercy seat that is thereupon, and all the furniture of the tabernacle. Henry notes the work list began with the house and its throne: tabernacle, ark, and mercy seat, with all their furniture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And the table and his furniture, and the pure candlestick with all his furniture, and the altar of incense. Henry observes the holy place furniture followed: table, candlestick, and incense altar, the bread, the light, and the prayer of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And the altar of burnt offering with all his furniture, and the laver and his foot. Henry notes the court furniture followed: the altar of sacrifice and the laver of washing, the outer service of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And the cloths of service, and the holy garments for Aaron the priest, and the garments of his sons, to minister in the priest's office. Henry observes the garments were part of the same commission: the priests' clothing was made by the same Spirit-filled hands as the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And the anointing oil, and sweet incense for the holy place, according to all that I have commanded thee shall they do. Henry notes the oil and incense completed the list, and the rule governed all: according to all that God had commanded, nothing added and nothing left out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And the LORD spake unto Moses, giving the sabbath law after the tabernacle law. Henry notes orders were now given that a tabernacle should be set up, but they must not think the nature of the work, or the haste required, would justify working at it on sabbath days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Speak thou also unto the children of Israel, saying, Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep, for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations, that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you. Henry observes the sabbath was a sign between God and Israel, and the Hebrew word shabath signifies rest, or ceasing from labour, and the rest signified is that rest in glory which remains for the people of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Ye shall keep the sabbath therefore, for it is holy unto you, and every one that defileth it shall surely be put to death, and whosoever doeth any work therein, that soul shall be cut off from among his people. Henry notes the penalty matched the holiness: defiling the sabbath was capital, and working on it cut the worker off from the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Six days may work be done, but in the seventh is the sabbath of rest, holy to the LORD, and whosoever doeth any work in the sabbath day, he shall surely be put to death. Henry observes the six days of work were granted, but the seventh was holy to the LORD, and no work, not even tabernacle work, might invade it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Wherefore the children of Israel shall keep the sabbath, to observe the sabbath throughout their generations, for a perpetual covenant. Henry notes the sabbath was a perpetual covenant, observed throughout generations, for the moral obligation of the sabbath must continue till time is swallowed up in eternity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. It is a sign between me and the children of Israel forever, for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed. Henry observes the sign rested on creation itself: six days of making, and a seventh of rest, a pattern older than Sinai. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And he gave unto Moses, when he had made an end of communing with him upon mount Sinai, two tables of testimony, tables of stone, written with the finger of God. Henry notes the law was written in tables of stone to show how lasting it is, and to denote the hardness of our hearts, for one might more easily write on stone than write anything good on corrupt natural hearts, and it was written with the finger of God, by his will and power, for God only can write his law in the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Sabbath as a sign. Henry observes the Sabbath was a sign between God and Israel throughout their generations, marking that the LORD sanctifies them. Interpreted Study note on verse 13, summarizing Matthew Henry
