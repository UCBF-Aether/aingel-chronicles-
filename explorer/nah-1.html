The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Nahum 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The burden of Nineveh. The book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite.

2 God is jealous, and the LORD revengeth; the LORD revengeth, and is furious; the LORD will take vengeance on his adversaries, and he reserveth wrath for his enemies.

3 The LORD is slow to anger, and great in power, and will not at all acquit the wicked: the LORD hath his way in the whirlwind and in the storm, and the clouds are the dust of his feet.

4 He rebuketh the sea, and maketh it dry, and drieth up all the rivers: Bashan languisheth, and Carmel, and the flower of Lebanon languisheth.

5 The mountains quake at him, and the hills melt, and the earth is burned at his presence, yea, the world, and all that dwell therein.

6 Who can stand before his indignation? and who can abide in the fierceness of his anger? his fury is poured out like fire, and the rocks are thrown down by him.

7 The LORD is good, a strong hold in the day of trouble; and he knoweth them that trust in him.

8 But with an overrunning flood he will make an utter end of the place thereof, and darkness shall pursue his enemies.

9 What do ye imagine against the LORD? he will make an utter end: affliction shall not rise up the second time.

10 For while they be folden together as thorns, and while they are drunken as drunkards, they shall be devoured as stubble fully dry.

11 There is one come out of thee, that imagineth evil against the LORD, a wicked counsellor.

12 Thus saith the LORD; Though they be quiet, and likewise many, yet thus shall they be cut down, when he shall pass through. Though I have afflicted thee, I will afflict thee no more.

13 For now will I break his yoke from off thee, and will burst thy bonds in sunder.

14 And the LORD hath given a commandment concerning thee, that no more of thy name be sown: out of the house of thy gods will I cut off the graven image and the molten image: I will make thy grave; for thou art vile.

15 Behold upon the mountains the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace! O Judah, keep thy solemn feasts, perform thy vows: for the wicked shall no more pass through thee; he is utterly cut off.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The burden of Nineveh opens with God himself: jealous, avenging, furious against his adversaries, yet slow to anger and great in power, with his way in the whirlwind and the storm, and at the same time good, a stronghold in the day of trouble, knowing those who trust in him. With an overrunning flood he will make an utter end of Nineveh, break Assyria's yoke from Judah, and cut off the name of its gods, so that good tidings of peace are published on the mountains and Judah keeps its feasts again. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The burden of Nineveh, the book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite, is the prophetic doom of Nineveh, prophesied a hundred and fifty years after Jonah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. God is jealous, and the LORD revengeth, the LORD revengeth and is furious, the LORD will take vengeance on adversaries and reserve wrath for enemies, jealousy here being stern yet tender affection, since God will not bear a rival in His claims on those He loves, nor admit His friends being oppressed while enemies flourish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The LORD is slow to anger and great in power, and will not at all acquit the wicked, the LORD having His way in whirlwind and storm, and clouds being the dust of His feet, His long-suffering not being want of power, and His vengeance sweeping the Assyrian foe like a whirlwind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He rebukes the sea and makes it dry, and dries up all rivers, Bashan and Carmel languish, and the flower of Lebanon languishes, Bashan famed for pastures, Carmel for corn and vineyards, Lebanon for forests, and nothing so blooming that God cannot change it when wroth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The mountains quake at Him, and the hills melt, and the earth is burned up at His presence, yea, the world and all that dwell therein, the earth heaving before Him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Who can stand before His indignation, and who can abide in the fierceness of His anger, His fury being poured out like fire, and rocks thrown down by Him, like liquid fire from volcanoes, breaking rocks asunder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The LORD is good, a strong hold in the day of trouble, and He knows them that trust in Him, Nahum here entering his special subject, assuring his people of safety under Sennacherib's impending attack, He knowing His own and so caring for and guarding them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. But with an overrunning flood He will make an utter end of the place thereof, and darkness shall pursue His enemies, the flood being irresistible might overrunning barriers, perhaps alluding to Nineveh's capture through a river flood breaking the wall, and Nineveh's place itself being utterly destroyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. What do you imagine against the LORD, He will make an utter end, affliction shall not rise up the second time, an abrupt address to the Assyrians, mad to resist so powerful a God, Sennacherib's overthrow being an earnest of Nineveh's utter end, and Judah's affliction never rising again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For while they are folded together as thorns, and while drunken as drunkards, they shall be devoured as stubble fully dry, thorns entangled and thrown in a mass into fire picturing speedy destruction, and drunkards, bold yet weak and easily thrown down, picturing self-confident Assyrians rushing of their own accord into ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. There is one come out of thee, that imagines evil against the LORD, a wicked counsellor, the cause of Nineveh's overthrow being Sennacherib's plots against Judah, from Nineveh itself arising the source of its own ruin, Belial meaning worthless and bad. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Thus says the LORD, Though they be quiet and likewise many, yet thus shall they be cut down when he passes through, though I have afflicted thee, I will afflict thee no more, the quiet and many Assyrians being shorn as hair by a razor, retribution in kind, while Judah's affliction, temporary and corrective, ends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. For now will I break his yoke from off thee, and burst thy bonds asunder, the Assyrian yoke being the tribute imposed by Sennacherib on Hezekiah, broken from off Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The LORD has given a commandment concerning thee, that no more of thy name be sown, out of the house of thy gods will I cut off graven and molten images, I will make thy grave, for thou art vile, the Assyrian king's dynasty becoming extinct, his gods cut off by the idol-hating Medes, and Nisroch's house becoming Sennacherib's grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Behold upon the mountains the feet of him that brings good tidings, that publishes peace, O Judah, keep thy solemn feasts, perform thy vows, for the wicked shall no more pass through thee, he is utterly cut off, the messenger announcing Sennacherib's overthrow and Jerusalem's deliverance on the mountains round Jerusalem, a type of the Gospel heralding of spiritual deliverance by Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Nineveh. the great Assyrian city, three days' journey across, to which Jonah is sent and whose repentance spares it in Jonah 3, and whose fall Nahum announces. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
