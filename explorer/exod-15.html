The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the LORD, and spake, saying, I will sing unto the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously: the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea.

2 The LORD is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation: he is my God, and I will prepare him an habitation; my father’s God, and I will exalt him.

3 The LORD is a man of war: the LORD is his name.

4 Pharaoh’s chariots and his host hath he cast into the sea: his chosen captains also are drowned in the Red sea.

5 The depths have covered them: they sank into the bottom as a stone.

6 Thy right hand, O LORD, is become glorious in power: thy right hand, O LORD, hath dashed in pieces the enemy.

7 And in the greatness of thine excellency thou hast overthrown them that rose up against thee: thou sentest forth thy wrath, which consumed them as stubble.

8 And with the blast of thy nostrils the waters were gathered together, the floods stood upright as an heap, and the depths were congealed in the heart of the sea.

9 The enemy said, I will pursue, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil; my lust shall be satisfied upon them; I will draw my sword, my hand shall destroy them.

10 Thou didst blow with thy wind, the sea covered them: they sank as lead in the mighty waters.

11 Who is like unto thee, O LORD, among the gods? who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?

12 Thou stretchedst out thy right hand, the earth swallowed them.

13 Thou in thy mercy hast led forth the people which thou hast redeemed: thou hast guided them in thy strength unto thy holy habitation.

14 The people shall hear, and be afraid: sorrow shall take hold on the inhabitants of Palestina.

15 Then the dukes of Edom shall be amazed; the mighty men of Moab, trembling shall take hold upon them; all the inhabitants of Canaan shall melt away.

16 Fear and dread shall fall upon them; by the greatness of thine arm they shall be as still as a stone; till thy people pass over, O LORD, till the people pass over, which thou hast purchased.

17 Thou shalt bring them in, and plant them in the mountain of thine inheritance, in the place, O LORD, which thou hast made for thee to dwell in, in the Sanctuary, O LORD, which thy hands have established.

18 The LORD shall reign for ever and ever.

19 For the horse of Pharaoh went in with his chariots and with his horsemen into the sea, and the LORD brought again the waters of the sea upon them; but the children of Israel went on dry land in the midst of the sea.

20 And Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a timbrel in her hand; and all the women went out after her with timbrels and with dances.

21 And Miriam answered them, Sing ye to the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea.

22 So Moses brought Israel from the Red sea, and they went out into the wilderness of Shur; and they went three days in the wilderness, and found no water.

23 And when they came to Marah, they could not drink of the waters of Marah, for they were bitter: therefore the name of it was called Marah.

24 And the people murmured against Moses, saying, What shall we drink?

25 And he cried unto the LORD; and the LORD shewed him a tree, which when he had cast into the waters, the waters were made sweet: there he made for them a statute and an ordinance, and there he proved them,

26 And said, If thou wilt diligently hearken to the voice of the LORD thy God, and wilt do that which is right in his sight, and wilt give ear to his commandments, and keep all his statutes, I will put none of these diseases upon thee, which I have brought upon the Egyptians: for I am the LORD that healeth thee.

27 And they came to Elim, where were twelve wells of water, and threescore and ten palm trees: and they encamped there by the waters.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Moses and Israel sing of the LORD's triumph at the sea, horse and rider thrown down, and Miriam leads the women with timbrels in answer. Three days into the wilderness the people find only bitter water at Marah, which is made sweet when Moses casts in a tree, and then they come to Elim, with twelve wells and seventy palm trees. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then sang Moses and Israel this song unto the LORD: I will sing unto the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously, the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea. Henry notes this most ancient song is holy throughout, honouring God only and magnifying no man, and it is typical of the final destruction of the church's enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The LORD is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation; he is my God, and I will prepare him a habitation, my father's God, and I will exalt him. Henry observes the song's theology: God's people are weak in themselves, but his grace is their strength, and in sorrow he is their song. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The LORD is a man of war; the LORD is his name. Henry notes the title is not of cruelty but of almighty power: woe to those that strive with their Maker, for he fights for his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Pharaoh's chariots and his host hath he cast into the sea, and his chosen captains are drowned in the Red Sea. Henry observes the song names the best of Egypt's army, that none might say only the weak perished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The depths have covered them; they sank into the bottom as a stone. Henry notes the image of weight: the army that trusted in iron sank like stone, while unarmed Israel walked on dry ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Thy right hand, O LORD, is become glorious in power; thy right hand hath dashed in pieces the enemy. Henry observes the song ascribes all to God's hand, not to Israel's sword, for Israel had not lifted one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. In the greatness of thine excellency thou hast overthrown them that rose up against thee, and sent forth thy wrath, which consumed them as stubble. Henry notes God's holiness appears in his wrath against obstinate sinners, as surely as in his deliverance of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. With the blast of thy nostrils the waters were gathered together, the floods stood upright as a heap, and the depths were congealed in the heart of the sea. Henry observes the wonder was wrought by God's breath alone: the sea stood as a wall at his command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The enemy said, I will pursue, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil, my lust shall be satisfied, I will draw my sword, my hand shall destroy them. Henry notes the song quotes Pharaoh's boast in full, that his pride might be measured against his fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Thou didst blow with thy wind, the sea covered them, and they sank as lead in the mighty waters. Henry observes the same wind that parted the sea for Israel closed it upon Egypt: one breath saved and judged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Who is like unto thee, O LORD, among the gods? Who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders? Henry notes the heart of the song: God is glorious in holiness, and his holiness is his glory, fearful to enemies and matter of praise to his servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Thou stretchedst out thy right hand, and the earth swallowed them. Henry observes the song compresses the whole deliverance into one gesture: God's hand stretched out, and Egypt was gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Thou in thy mercy hast led forth the people which thou hast redeemed, and guided them in thy strength unto thy holy habitation. Henry notes the song turns from judgment to guidance: mercy that redeemed will also lead, and the journey's end is God's own dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The people shall hear and be afraid, and sorrow shall take hold on the inhabitants of Palestina. Henry observes the song prophesies: the news of the sea would go before Israel, and fear would prepare their way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Then the dukes of Edom shall be amazed, the mighty men of Moab shall tremble, and all the inhabitants of Canaan shall melt away. Henry notes the neighbouring powers are named in order, each undone by report before Israel ever met them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Fear and dread shall fall upon them, and by the greatness of thine arm they shall be as still as a stone, till thy people pass over, the people which thou hast purchased. Henry observes the stillness was God's doing: terror held Israel's enemies motionless until the purchased people had passed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Thou shalt bring them in and plant them in the mountain of thine inheritance, in the place thou hast made for thee to dwell in, the sanctuary which thy hands have established. Henry notes the song ends in settlement: a wandering people planted, and a sanctuary established by God's own hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The LORD shall reign for ever and ever. Henry observes the song's final line outlasts every kingdom named in it: Pharaoh's reign ended in the sea, and the LORD's has no end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. For the horse of Pharaoh went in with his chariots and horsemen into the sea, and the LORD brought the waters back upon them, but Israel went on dry land in the midst of the sea. Henry notes the verse restates the history plainly after the poetry, that the song might never float free from the fact. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a timbrel in her hand, and all the women went out after her with timbrels and dances. Henry observes the women answered the men's song with their own, for deliverance belonged to the whole people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Miriam answered them, Sing ye to the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea. Henry notes the refrain returned: the women's chorus repeated the song's first line, that all Israel might carry it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Moses brought Israel from the Red Sea into the wilderness of Shur, and they went three days in the wilderness and found no water. Henry observes the song ended and the thirst began: three days from the sea, the people who had sung were already in want. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. When they came to Marah, they could not drink of the waters of Marah, for they were bitter, and therefore the name of it was called Marah. Henry notes God can make bitter to us that from which we promise ourselves most, that disappointment in the creature may drive us to the Creator. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And the people murmured against Moses, saying, What shall we drink? Henry observes even true believers, in sharp trial, are tempted to fret and murmur, and hypocrites who showed high affections fall away when temptation comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Moses cried unto the LORD, and the LORD showed him a tree, which when he had cast into the waters, the waters were made sweet, and there God made a statute and an ordinance, and proved them. Henry notes Moses did what the people had neglected: he cried to the Lord, and God provided graciously, and some make the tree typical of the cross, which sweetens affliction's bitter waters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. God said, If thou wilt diligently hearken to the voice of the LORD thy God, and do that which is right, and give ear to his commandments, and keep his statutes, I will put none of the diseases of Egypt upon thee, for I am the LORD that healeth thee. Henry notes the promise is expressed and the threatening only implied: God is the great Physician, and if we are kept well, it is he that keeps us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. They came to Elim, where were twelve wells of water and threescore and ten palm trees, and they encamped there by the waters. Henry observes that though God may order his people to encamp by bitter Marah for a time, that shall not always be their lot, and Elim's wells were enough, and to spare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Marah. The place-name means bitter: Israel could not drink its waters, for they were bitter. Interpreted Study note on verse 23, summarizing Matthew Henry
