The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now Sarai Abram’s wife bare him no children: and she had an handmaid, an Egyptian, whose name was Hagar.

2 And Sarai said unto Abram, Behold now, the LORD hath restrained me from bearing: I pray thee, go in unto my maid; it may be that I may obtain children by her. And Abram hearkened to the voice of Sarai.

3 And Sarai Abram’s wife took Hagar her maid the Egyptian, after Abram had dwelt ten years in the land of Canaan, and gave her to her husband Abram to be his wife.

4 And he went in unto Hagar, and she conceived: and when she saw that she had conceived, her mistress was despised in her eyes.

5 And Sarai said unto Abram, My wrong be upon thee: I have given my maid into thy bosom; and when she saw that she had conceived, I was despised in her eyes: the LORD judge between me and thee.

6 But Abram said unto Sarai, Behold, thy maid is in thine hand; do to her as it pleaseth thee. And when Sarai dealt hardly with her, she fled from her face.

7 And the angel of the LORD found her by a fountain of water in the wilderness, by the fountain in the way to Shur.

8 And he said, Hagar, Sarai’s maid, whence camest thou? and whither wilt thou go? And she said, I flee from the face of my mistress Sarai.

9 And the angel of the LORD said unto her, Return to thy mistress, and submit thyself under her hands.

10 And the angel of the LORD said unto her, I will multiply thy seed exceedingly, that it shall not be numbered for multitude.

11 And the angel of the LORD said unto her, Behold, thou art with child and shalt bear a son, and shalt call his name Ishmael; because the LORD hath heard thy affliction.

12 And he will be a wild man; his hand will be against every man, and every man’s hand against him; and he shall dwell in the presence of all his brethren.

13 And she called the name of the LORD that spake unto her, Thou God seest me: for she said, Have I also here looked after him that seeth me?

14 Wherefore the well was called Beerlahairoi; behold, it is between Kadesh and Bered.

15 And Hagar bare Abram a son: and Abram called his son’s name, which Hagar bare, Ishmael.

16 And Abram was fourscore and six years old, when Hagar bare Ishmael to Abram.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Childless Sarai gives her Egyptian handmaid Hagar to Abram, and Hagar conceives and, despised and harshly dealt with, flees. The angel of the LORD meets her, promises a multitude of descendants through her son Ishmael, and she names the well; Abram is eighty-six when Ishmael is born. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Sarai was still childless after many years, and she had an Egyptian maid named Hagar, likely one of the servants gained in Egypt, who now becomes central to a plan born of impatience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 2. Sarai told Abram that God had kept her from bearing, and urged him to take Hagar, hoping to gain children through her, since a maid's children counted as her mistress's. Gill notes both acted from eager desire for the promised seed, without consulting God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 3. After ten years in Canaan, when Abram was eighty-five, Sarai herself gave Hagar to him as a secondary wife, a step Gill says nothing could excuse except their longing for the promised child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 4. Hagar conceived at once, and then despised Sarai in her eyes, reproaching her mistress for barrenness, an ungrateful turn after Sarai herself had proposed the arrangement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 5. Sarai, stung by Hagar's contempt, appealed to Abram and blamed him for the wrong, asking God to judge between them, as people often blame others for troubles their own plans have made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Abram, seeking peace, left Hagar in Sarai's hand, and Sarai dealt harshly with her until she fled. Gill notes Abram gave too large a commission and should have judged between them himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 7. The angel of the Lord found Hagar by a spring on the road to Shur, toward Egypt, meeting a runaway servant in the wilderness with a question rather than a rebuke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 8. The angel called her by name and station, Hagar, Sarai's maid, and asked where she had come from and where she was going, reminding her of her duty before giving her any promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 9. She was told to return to her mistress and submit, a hard command that required humility and trust that God could deal with her situation better than flight could. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 10. Along with the command to return came a promise that her offspring would be multiplied beyond numbering, so obedience was not left without hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. She was told she would bear a son and call him Ishmael, because God had heard her affliction, so the child's very name would remember that God listens to the afflicted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Ishmael would be a wild man, at odds with others and others with him, yet living in the presence of his brethren, a picture of a free, restless people that would not be easily ruled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Hagar named God as the God who sees her, amazed that the all-seeing God had looked on so lowly a runaway with love and care, and that she had lived after seeing him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 14. The well was therefore called Beer-lahai-roi, the well of the Living One who sees, between Kadesh and Bered, keeping the memory of this meeting in the very name of the place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Hagar bore Abram a son, so the household's first child came not through Sarai but through her maid, setting up the tensions that would follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Abram named the boy Ishmael, as the angel had directed, accepting the name God had given and with it the promise and the trouble bound up in this son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
