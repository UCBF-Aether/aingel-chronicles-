The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 42 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O God.

2 My soul thirsteth for God, for the living God: when shall I come and appear before God?

3 My tears have been my meat day and night, while they continually say unto me, Where is thy God?

4 When I remember these things, I pour out my soul in me: for I had gone with the multitude, I went with them to the house of God, with the voice of joy and praise, with a multitude that kept holyday.

5 Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him for the help of his countenance.

6 O my God, my soul is cast down within me: therefore will I remember thee from the land of Jordan, and of the Hermonites, from the hill Mizar.

7 Deep calleth unto deep at the noise of thy waterspouts: all thy waves and thy billows are gone over me.

8 Yet the LORD will command his lovingkindness in the day time, and in the night his song shall be with me, and my prayer unto the God of my life.

9 I will say unto God my rock, Why hast thou forgotten me? why go I mourning because of the oppression of the enemy?

10 As with a sword in my bones, mine enemies reproach me; while they say daily unto me, Where is thy God?

11 Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Thirst for the living God: as the hart pants for water, tears are meat day and night under the taunt, 'Where is your God?', with remembered worship among the multitude, and deep calling to deep under waves and billows. Yet the refrain returns twice: why cast down, O my soul? Hope in God, for I shall yet praise him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. As the hart pants after the water brooks, so pants my soul after you, O God: thirst after water pictures thirst after God — a bodily need for a spiritual one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. My soul thirsts for God, for the living God; when shall I come and appear before God? Thirst is for the living God, and the question is when he may appear before him in worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. My tears have been my meat day and night, while they continually say to me, Where is your God? Tears replace food, day and night, and the taunt 'Where is your God?' is the salt in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. When I remember these things, I pour out my soul in me, for I had gone with the multitude, I went with them to the house of God, with joy and praise, with a multitude keeping holyday: memory of festal processions to God's house pours out his soul in present exile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why disquieted in me? Hope in God, for I shall yet praise him for the help of his countenance: the soul is preached to — cast down and disquieted, yet bidden to hope and to future praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. O my God, my soul is cast down within me; therefore I will remember you from the land of Jordan, and of the Hermonites, from the hill Mizar: from exile's far places — Jordan, Hermon, little Mizar — he remembers God, and remembrance steadies the cast-down soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Deep calls to deep at the noise of your waterspouts; all your waves and billows are gone over me: waterspout answers waterspout, wave upon wave — God's own waters overwhelm him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Yet the Lord will command his lovingkindness in the daytime, and in the night his song shall be with me, and my prayer to the God of my life: daytime lovingkindness commanded, night song given, prayer to the God of his life — the pattern holds through the flood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. I will say to God my rock, Why have you forgotten me? Why go I mourning because of the enemy's oppression? The rock is questioned — forgotten, mourning, oppressed — yet still called rock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. As with a sword in my bones, my enemies reproach me, while they say daily to me, Where is your God? The taunt cuts like a sword in the bones, daily repeated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why disquieted within me? Hope in God, for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God: the refrain returns, now with God as the health of his countenance — the face that was downcast shall be healed by praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
