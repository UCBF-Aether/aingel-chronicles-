The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Amos 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Thus hath the Lord GOD shewed unto me: and behold a basket of summer fruit.

2 And he said, Amos, what seest thou? And I said, A basket of summer fruit. Then said the LORD unto me, The end is come upon my people of Israel; I will not again pass by them any more.

3 And the songs of the temple shall be howlings in that day, saith the Lord GOD: there shall be many dead bodies in every place; they shall cast them forth with silence.

4 Hear this, O ye that swallow up the needy, even to make the poor of the land to fail,

5 Saying, When will the new moon be gone, that we may sell corn? and the sabbath, that we may set forth wheat, making the ephah small, and the shekel great, and falsifying the balances by deceit?

6 That we may buy the poor for silver, and the needy for a pair of shoes; yea, and sell the refuse of the wheat?

7 The LORD hath sworn by the excellency of Jacob, Surely I will never forget any of their works.

8 Shall not the land tremble for this, and every one mourn that dwelleth therein? and it shall rise up wholly as a flood; and it shall be cast out and drowned, as by the flood of Egypt.

9 And it shall come to pass in that day, saith the Lord GOD, that I will cause the sun to go down at noon, and I will darken the earth in the clear day:

10 And I will turn your feasts into mourning, and all your songs into lamentation; and I will bring up sackcloth upon all loins, and baldness upon every head; and I will make it as the mourning of an only son, and the end thereof as a bitter day.

11 Behold, the days come, saith the Lord GOD, that I will send a famine in the land, not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of the LORD:

12 And they shall wander from sea to sea, and from the north even to the east, they shall run to and fro to seek the word of the LORD, and shall not find it.

13 In that day shall the fair virgins and young men faint for thirst.

14 They that swear by the sin of Samaria, and say, Thy god, O Dan, liveth; and, The manner of Beersheba liveth; even they shall fall, and never rise up again.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A basket of summer fruit is shown to Amos, and the wordplay carries the sentence: summer fruit does not keep, and the end is come upon Israel. Songs of the temple will become howlings, dead bodies will lie in every place, and a worse famine is promised than bread or water: a famine of hearing the words of the LORD, as people wander from sea to sea seeking the word and not finding it. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Thus the Lord God showed Amos: behold, a basket of summer fruit, fruit gathered and ready to be eaten, signifying the people ripe for destruction, the year of God's patience drawing to conclusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And God said, Amos, what seest thou, and he said, a basket of summer fruit, then the Lord said, the end is come upon my people Israel, I will not again pass by them any more, summer fruits not keeping till winter but needing use at once, so judgment can no longer be deferred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And the temple's songs shall be howlings in that day, says the Lord God, with many dead bodies in every place, cast forth with silence, worship turned to wailing and the dead too many to lament. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Hear this, you who swallow up the needy, even to make the poor of the land fail, the rich and powerful being most guilty of oppression as well as foremost in idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Saying, when will the new moon be gone, that we may sell corn, and the sabbath, that we may set forth wheat, making the ephah small, the shekel great, and falsifying balances by deceit, weary of holy restraints because no common work might be done therein, thinking of marketings even during worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. That we may buy the poor for silver and the needy for a pair of shoes, yea, and sell the refuse of the wheat, the poor themselves becoming merchandise and even spoiled grain sold at full price. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The Lord has sworn by Jacob's excellency, surely I will never forget any of their works, God remembering sin against unjust men, though sinners put off repentance thinking the Lord delays judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Shall not the land tremble for this, and everyone mourning that dwells therein, rising wholly as a flood, cast out and drowned as by Egypt's flood, terror and desolation coming when little thought of, creature comforts proving uncertain, in the midst of life we are in death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And in that day, says the Lord God, I will cause the sun to go down at noon and darken the earth in the clear day, judgment as a noonday eclipse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And I will turn their feasts into mourning, all songs into lamentation, bring sackcloth on all loins and baldness on every head, making it as mourning for an only son, the end as a bitter day, wailing following sinful and sensual pleasures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Behold, days come, says the Lord God, when I will send a famine in the land, not of bread nor thirst for water, but of hearing the Lord's words, a token of God's highest displeasure and the heaviest judgment, especially in trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And they shall wander from sea to sea, and from north even to east, running to and fro to seek the Lord's word, and shall not find it, their own plans for salvation standing them in no stead when God visits a backsliding church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. In that day fair virgins and young men shall faint for thirst, even the most amiable and zealous perishing for want of the water of life which Christ only can bestow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. They who swear by Samaria's sin, saying, thy god, O Dan, lives, and, the manner of Beersheba lives, even they shall fall and never rise again, idolatrous oaths ending in final fall, a warning to value advantages, profit by them, and fear sinning them away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Samaria. the capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, standing in the prophets for the ten tribes as Jerusalem stands for Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Basket of summer fruit. ripe fruit that does not keep till winter; in Amos 8 the image carries the sentence that the end is come and judgment can no longer be deferred. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
