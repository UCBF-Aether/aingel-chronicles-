The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when Rehoboam was come to Jerusalem, he gathered of the house of Judah and Benjamin an hundred and fourscore thousand chosen men, which were warriors, to fight against Israel, that he might bring the kingdom again to Rehoboam.

2 But the word of the LORD came to Shemaiah the man of God, saying,

3 Speak unto Rehoboam the son of Solomon, king of Judah, and to all Israel in Judah and Benjamin, saying,

4 Thus saith the LORD, Ye shall not go up, nor fight against your brethren: return every man to his house: for this thing is done of me. And they obeyed the words of the LORD, and returned from going against Jeroboam.

5 And Rehoboam dwelt in Jerusalem, and built cities for defence in Judah.

6 He built even Bethlehem, and Etam, and Tekoa,

7 And Bethzur, and Shoco, and Adullam,

8 And Gath, and Mareshah, and Ziph,

9 And Adoraim, and Lachish, and Azekah,

10 And Zorah, and Aijalon, and Hebron, which are in Judah and in Benjamin fenced cities.

11 And he fortified the strong holds, and put captains in them, and store of victual, and of oil and wine.

12 And in every several city he put shields and spears, and made them exceeding strong, having Judah and Benjamin on his side.

13 And the priests and the Levites that were in all Israel resorted to him out of all their coasts.

14 For the Levites left their suburbs and their possession, and came to Judah and Jerusalem: for Jeroboam and his sons had cast them off from executing the priest’s office unto the LORD:

15 And he ordained him priests for the high places, and for the devils, and for the calves which he had made.

16 And after them out of all the tribes of Israel such as set their hearts to seek the LORD God of Israel came to Jerusalem, to sacrifice unto the LORD God of their fathers.

17 So they strengthened the kingdom of Judah, and made Rehoboam the son of Solomon strong, three years: for three years they walked in the way of David and Solomon.

18 And Rehoboam took him Mahalath the daughter of Jerimoth the son of David to wife, and Abihail the daughter of Eliab the son of Jesse;

19 Which bare him children; Jeush, and Shamariah, and Zaham.

20 And after her he took Maachah the daughter of Absalom; which bare him Abijah, and Attai, and Ziza, and Shelomith.

21 And Rehoboam loved Maachah the daughter of Absalom above all his wives and his concubines: (for he took eighteen wives, and threescore concubines; and begat twenty and eight sons, and threescore daughters.)

22 And Rehoboam made Abijah the son of Maachah the chief, to be ruler among his brethren: for he thought to make him king.

23 And he dealt wisely, and dispersed of all his children throughout all the countries of Judah and Benjamin, unto every fenced city: and he gave them victual in abundance. And he desired many wives.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Rehoboam's forbidden war against Israel, his fortification of Judah's cities, the priests and Levites who came to him from the north, and his wives and children. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And when Rehoboam was come to Jerusalem, he gathered of Judah and Benjamin a hundred and fourscore thousand chosen warriors, to fight against Israel, that he might bring the kingdom again to Rehoboam. Henry notes Rehoboam at length, like a bold man, raised an army to reduce the revolters, with Judah and Benjamin resolved to continue allegiance and ready to assist. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. But the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah the man of God, saying. Henry notes Rehoboam, like a conscientious man, let his design fall when God forbade it, reverencing divine authority and knowing he should not prosper contrary to God's command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Speak unto Rehoboam son of Solomon, king of Judah, and to all Israel in Judah and Benjamin, saying. Henry notes God called him Rehoboam son of Solomon, to intimate the division was determined for Solomon's sin, and opposing a decree gone forth would be to no purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Thus saith the Lord, Ye shall not go up, nor fight against your brethren: return every man to his house: for this thing is done of me. And they obeyed the words of the Lord, and returned from going against Jeroboam. Henry notes they obeyed though it looked mean and would turn to reproach among neighbours, because God would have it so, and it is dangerous to undertake anything, especially war, contrary to God's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And Rehoboam dwelt in Jerusalem, and built cities for defence in Judah. Henry notes that, like a discreet man, he fortified his own country, making the best of what remained instead of chasing what was lost, and submitting to God's will concerning the lost tribes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He built even Bethlehem, and Etam, and Tekoa. Henry notes he fortified his frontiers and many principal cities of his kingdom, which in Solomon's peaceable reign had not been cared for in defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And Bethzur, and Shoco, and Adullam. Henry's fortification account applies to each of these frontier cities, built for defence in Judah as Rehoboam made the best of the kingdom that remained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And Gath, and Mareshah, and Ziph. Henry notes the same discreet course: fortifying principal cities, since a few good words might have prevented defection, but now all his forces could not bring the revolters back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And Adoraim, and Lachish, and Azekah. Henry's fortification point covers these also: Rehoboam's wisdom was to keep what he had, fortifying the frontiers of Judah and Benjamin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And Zorah, and Aijalon, and Hebron, which are in Judah and Benjamin fenced cities. Henry notes these were fenced cities in Judah and Benjamin, the fortified strongholds by which Rehoboam preserved the two tribes that remained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And he fortified the strong holds, and put captains in them, and store of victual, and of oil and wine. Henry notes he furnished the cities with good stores of victuals and arms, and because God forbade him to fight, he did not sit down sullenly but prudently provided against attack, for those who may not be conquerors may yet be builders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And in every several city he put shields and spears, and made them exceeding strong, having Judah and Benjamin on his side. Henry notes the furnishing with arms made each city exceeding strong, preserving Judah and Benjamin on Rehoboam's side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And the priests and Levites that were in all Israel resorted to him out of all their coasts. Henry notes Rehoboam was strengthened by the accession of priests and Levites, and all devout Israelites true to God and religion, resorting to where God's altar was. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. For the Levites left their suburbs and their possession, and came to Judah and Jerusalem, for Jeroboam and his sons had cast them off from executing the priest's office unto the Lord. Henry notes Jeroboam set up worship they could not in conscience comply with, and would not let them go up to Jerusalem, so they left comfortable provision, loving their work better than maintenance, since the Lord was their inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And he ordained him priests for the high places, and for the devils, and for the calves which he had made. Henry notes Jeroboam was willing they should turn out, to make room for mean and scandalous persons he ordained for high places, for he who cast off God cast off his ministers, who would not serve his gods or worship his golden image. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And after them out of all tribes of Israel such as set their hearts to seek the Lord God of Israel came to Jerusalem, to sacrifice unto the Lord God of their fathers. Henry notes the devout of every tribe followed the priests, leaving fathers' inheritances for houses in Jerusalem, because what is best for our souls must take place of outward conveniences, and where God's faithful priests are, his faithful people should be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. So they strengthened the kingdom of Judah, and made Rehoboam strong, three years, for three years they walked in the way of David and Solomon. Henry notes they strengthened Judah not only by added persons but by piety and prayers procuring blessing, and strength lasted while they cleaved to God and duty, and no longer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And Rehoboam took him Mahalath daughter of Jerimoth son of David to wife, and Abihail daughter of Eliab son of Jesse. Henry notes Rehoboam was more wise than his father in this, that his wives mentioned were not strange wives but daughters of Israel, of David's family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Which bare him children: Jeush, and Shamariah, and Zaham. Henry notes Rehoboam was more happy than his father in having many sons and daughters named as men of note, whereas only one son of Solomon is read of as worth mentioning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And after her he took Maachah daughter of Absalom, which bare him Abijah, and Attai, and Ziza, and Shelomith. Henry notes Maachah was probably Absalom's daughter, of David's line, and her sons are named among Rehoboam's notable children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And Rehoboam loved Maachah daughter of Absalom above all his wives and concubines, for he took eighteen wives and threescore concubines, and begat twenty-eight sons and threescore daughters. Henry notes Rehoboam was weakened by indulging himself in pleasures and desiring many wives, as his father did, though his wives were of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And Rehoboam made Abijah son of Maachah the chief, to be ruler among his brethren, for he thought to make him king. Henry notes Rehoboam designed Abijah for successor, and dispersed his other sons so they might not rival Abijah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And he dealt wisely, and dispersed all his children throughout Judah and Benjamin, unto every fenced city, and gave them victual in abundance. And he desired many wives. Henry notes this dispersal was wisdom bought dearly: sons trusted with fenced cities, well victualled, to preserve public peace, and after-wisdom is better than none, though bought with the loss of a kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
