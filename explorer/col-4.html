The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Colossians 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Masters, give unto your servants that which is just and equal; knowing that ye also have a Master in heaven.

2 Continue in prayer, and watch in the same with thanksgiving;

3 Withal praying also for us, that God would open unto us a door of utterance, to speak the mystery of Christ, for which I am also in bonds:

4 That I may make it manifest, as I ought to speak.

5 Walk in wisdom toward them that are without, redeeming the time.

6 Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man.

7 All my state shall Tychicus declare unto you, who is a beloved brother, and a faithful minister and fellowservant in the Lord:

8 Whom I have sent unto you for the same purpose, that he might know your estate, and comfort your hearts;

9 With Onesimus, a faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you. They shall make known unto you all things which are done here.

10 Aristarchus my fellowprisoner saluteth you, and Marcus, sister’s son to Barnabas, (touching whom ye received commandments: if he come unto you, receive him;)

11 And Jesus, which is called Justus, who are of the circumcision. These only are my fellowworkers unto the kingdom of God, which have been a comfort unto me.

12 Epaphras, who is one of you, a servant of Christ, saluteth you, always labouring fervently for you in prayers, that ye may stand perfect and complete in all the will of God.

13 For I bear him record, that he hath a great zeal for you, and them that are in Laodicea, and them in Hierapolis.

14 Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas, greet you.

15 Salute the brethren which are in Laodicea, and Nymphas, and the church which is in his house.

16 And when this epistle is read among you, cause that it be read also in the church of the Laodiceans; and that ye likewise read the epistle from Laodicea.

17 And say to Archippus, Take heed to the ministry which thou hast received in the Lord, that thou fulfil it.

18 The salutation by the hand of me Paul. Remember my bonds. Grace be with you. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Masters are told to give servants what is just and fair, and all are urged to continue in prayer and speak with grace, seasoned with salt, toward outsiders. Tychicus and Onesimus will report Paul's circumstances; greetings follow, including Luke and Demas, a word for the Laodiceans and for Nymphas and the church in his house, and a charge to Archippus to fulfil his ministry. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Masters are to give their servants what is just and equal, knowing they also have a Master in heaven, and Henry notes not only strict justice but equity and kindness is required, dealing with servants as they expect God to deal with themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Believers are to continue in prayer and watch in the same with thanksgiving, since no duties can be done aright unless they persevere in fervent prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They are to pray also for Paul and his companions, that God would open a door of utterance to speak the mystery of Christ, for which Paul was in bonds, since the people are to pray particularly for their ministers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Paul desired to make the mystery manifest as he ought to speak, and Henry notes believers must be careful in all converse to do good and recommend religion by all fit means. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They are to walk in wisdom toward those without, redeeming the time, since diligence in redeeming time commends religion to others, while even carelessness may cause a lasting prejudice against the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Their speech is to be always with grace, seasoned with salt, that they may know how to answer every man, since discourse need not always be of grace but must always be with grace, discreet and seasonable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Tychicus, a beloved brother and faithful minister and fellowservant in the Lord, would declare all Paul's state to them, and Henry notes ministers are servants to Christ and fellow-servants to one another under one Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Tychicus was sent for this purpose, that he might know their estate and comfort their hearts, and it is a great comfort under life's troubles to have fellow Christians caring for us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. With him came Onesimus, a faithful and beloved brother, one of themselves, who would make known all things at Rome, showing what amazing changes divine grace makes, since faithless servants become faithful and beloved brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Aristarchus the fellowprisoner and Marcus, sister's son to Barnabas, salute them, and Henry notes Paul had once differed with Barnabas over Mark, yet was now reconciled and recommended him, an example of a truly Christian and forgiving spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Jesus called Justus, of the circumcision, was among Paul's fellowworkers unto the kingdom of God, and such fellowship was a comfort to him in his imprisonment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Epaphras, one of themselves and a servant of Christ, saluted them, always labouring fervently for them in prayers that they might stand perfect and complete in all the will of God, and Henry notes the effectual, fervent prayer is the prevailing prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Paul bore record that Epaphras had a great zeal for them and for Laodicea and Hierapolis, showing the communion of saints and ministers that comforted the apostle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Luke the beloved physician and Demas greeted them, fellow-workers in the same work of promoting the salvation of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Paul asked them to salute the brethren in Laodicea and Nymphas and the church in his house, extending the same fellowship beyond Colosse itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. When the epistle was read among them, it was also to be read in the church of the Laodiceans, and they were likewise to read the epistle from Laodicea, sharing the apostolic teaching between the churches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Archippus was to take heed to the ministry he had received in the Lord, that he fulfil it, since Henry warns that the smiles or frowns of the world and self-love lead many to fall far short of fulfilling their ministry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The salutation was by Paul's own hand, with the charge to remember his bonds and the closing blessing of grace, and those who preach Paul's doctrine and follow his example may expect the divine favour and blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
