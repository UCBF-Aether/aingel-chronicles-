The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And in that day thou shalt say, O LORD, I will praise thee: though thou wast angry with me, thine anger is turned away, and thou comfortedst me.

2 Behold, God is my salvation; I will trust, and not be afraid: for the LORD JEHOVAH is my strength and my song; he also is become my salvation.

3 Therefore with joy shall ye draw water out of the wells of salvation.

4 And in that day shall ye say, Praise the LORD, call upon his name, declare his doings among the people, make mention that his name is exalted.

5 Sing unto the LORD; for he hath done excellent things: this is known in all the earth.

6 Cry out and shout, thou inhabitant of Zion: for great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of thee.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This short chapter is a song of thanksgiving for the restored people pictured in chapter 11. Anger has turned to comfort, God has become salvation, and the inhabitant of Zion is told to cry out and shout, because the Holy One of Israel is great in her midst. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In that day the restored people will say: O Lord, I will praise you, for though you were angry with me, your anger is turned away and you comforted me. This thanksgiving hymn of the restored and converted Jews answers to Miriam's ode after the Red Sea deliverance, which chapter 11's second exodus recalls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Behold, God is my salvation; I will trust and not be afraid, for the Lord Jehovah is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation. The repeated name, Jah Jehovah, denotes emphasis and the unchangeableness of God's character, and the words are drawn from Exodus 15 and Psalm 118, with salvation peculiarly associated with the feast of tabernacles and its Hosanna cry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Therefore with joy you will draw water out of the wells of salvation, an expressive image in a hot country. The wells are not mere streams that may run dry but ever-flowing fountains of living water, recalling the water poured from Siloam's fountain on the last day of tabernacles and Jesus' words on that feast day, with a latter outpouring of the Spirit like the former at Pentecost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. In that day you will say: Praise the Lord, call upon his name, declare his doings among the people, and make mention that his name is exalted. To make mention is in Hebrew to cause his name to be remembered, proclaiming his deeds among the peoples so that his exalted name is kept in memory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Sing unto the Lord, for he has done excellent things, and this is known in all the earth, alluding to Miriam's song in Exodus 15. The hymn's praise is to be sung because God's excellent deeds deserve worldwide renown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Cry out and shout, you inhabitant of Zion — in Hebrew the inhabitress, daughter of Zion, that is, Zion and its people — for great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of you. The Holy One dwells in Jerusalem literally, as Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and Zephaniah promise, and his presence in their midst is the ground of their shouting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
