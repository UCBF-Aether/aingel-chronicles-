The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Esdras 1 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 And Josias held the feast of the passover in Jerusalem unto his Lord, and offered the passover the fourteenth day of the first month;

2 Having set the priests according to their daily courses, being arrayed in long garments, in the temple of the Lord.

3 And he spake unto the Levites, the holy ministers of Israel, that they should hallow themselves unto the Lord, to set the holy ark of the Lord in the house that king Solomon the son of David had built:

4 And said, Ye shall no more bear the ark upon your shoulders: now therefore serve the Lord your God, and minister unto his people Israel, and prepare you after your families and kindreds,

5 According as David the king of Israel prescribed, and according to the magnificence of Solomon his son: and standing in the temple according to the several dignity of the families of you the Levites, who minister in the presence of your brethren the children of Israel,

6 Offer the passover in order, and make ready the sacrifices for your brethren, and keep the passover according to the commandment of the Lord, which was given unto Moses.

7 And unto the people that was found there Josias gave thirty thousand lambs and kids, and three thousand calves: these things were given of the king's allowance, according as he promised, to the people, to the priests, and to the Levites.

8 And Helkias, Zacharias, and Syelus, the governors of the temple, gave to the priests for the passover two thousand and six hundred sheep, and three hundred calves.

9 And Jeconias, and Samaias, and Nathanael his brother, and Assabias, and Ochiel, and Joram, captains over thousands, gave to the Levites for the passover five thousand sheep, and seven hundred calves.

10 And when these things were done, the priests and Levites, having the unleavened bread, stood in very comely order according to the kindreds,

11 And according to the several dignities of the fathers, before the people, to offer to the Lord, as it is written in the book of Moses: and thus did they in the morning.

12 And they roasted the passover with fire, as appertaineth: as for the sacrifices, they sod them in brass pots and pans with a good savour,

13 And set them before all the people: and afterward they prepared for themselves, and for the priests their brethren, the sons of Aaron.

14 For the priests offered the fat until night: and the Levites prepared for themselves, and the priests their brethren, the sons of Aaron.

15 The holy singers also, the sons of Asaph, were in their order, according to the appointment of David, to wit, Asaph, Zacharias, and Jeduthun, who was of the king's retinue.

16 Moreover the porters were at every gate; it was not lawful for any to go from his ordinary service: for their brethren the Levites prepared for them.

17 Thus were the things that belonged to the sacrifices of the Lord accomplished in that day, that they might hold the passover,

18 And offer sacrifices upon the altar of the Lord, according to the commandment of king Josias.

19 So the children of Israel which were present held the passover at that time, and the feast of sweet bread seven days.

20 And such a passover was not kept in Israel since the time of the prophet Samuel.

21 Yea, all the kings of Israel held not such a passover as Josias, and the priests, and the Levites, and the Jews, held with all Israel that were found dwelling at Jerusalem.

22 In the eighteenth year of the reign of Josias was this passover kept.

23 And the works or Josias were upright before his Lord with an heart full of godliness.

24 As for the things that came to pass in his time, they were written in former times, concerning those that sinned, and did wickedly against the Lord above all people and kingdoms, and how they grieved him exceedingly, so that the words of the Lord rose up against Israel.

25 Now after all these acts of Josias it came to pass, that Pharaoh the king of Egypt came to raise war at Carchamis upon Euphrates: and Josias went out against him.

26 But the king of Egypt sent to him, saying, What have I to do with thee, O king of Judea?

27 I am not sent out from the Lord God against thee; for my war is upon Euphrates: and now the Lord is with me, yea, the Lord is with me hasting me forward: depart from me, and be not against the Lord.

28 Howbeit Josias did not turn back his chariot from him, but undertook to fight with him, not regarding the words of the prophet Jeremy spoken by the mouth of the Lord:

29 But joined battle with him in the plain of Magiddo, and the princes came against king Josias.

30 Then said the king unto his servants, Carry me away out of the battle; for I am very weak. And immediately his servants took him away out of the battle.

31 Then gat he up upon his second chariot; and being brought back to Jerusalem died, and was buried in his father's sepulchre.

32 And in all Jewry they mourned for Josias, yea, Jeremy the prophet lamented for Josias, and the chief men with the women made lamentation for him unto this day: and this was given out for an ordinance to be done continually in all the nation of Israel.

33 These things are written in the book of the stories of the kings of Judah, and every one of the acts that Josias did, and his glory, and his understanding in the law of the Lord, and the things that he had done before, and the things now recited, are reported in the book of the kings of Israel and Judea.

34 And the people took Joachaz the son of Josias, and made him king instead of Josias his father, when he was twenty and three years old.

35 And he reigned in Judea and in Jerusalem three months: and then the king of Egypt deposed him from reigning in Jerusalem.

36 And he set a tax upon the land of an hundred talents of silver and one talent of gold.

37 The king of Egypt also made king Joacim his brother king of Judea and Jerusalem.

38 And he bound Joacim and the nobles: but Zaraces his brother he apprehended, and brought him out of Egypt.

39 Five and twenty years old was Joacim when he was made king in the land of Judea and Jerusalem; and he did evil before the Lord.

40 Wherefore against him Nabuchodonosor the king of Babylon came up, and bound him with a chain of brass, and carried him into Babylon.

41 Nabuchodonosor also took of the holy vessels of the Lord, and carried them away, and set them in his own temple at Babylon.

42 But those things that are recorded of him, and of his uncleaness and impiety, are written in the chronicles of the kings.

43 And Joacim his son reigned in his stead: he was made king being eighteen years old;

44 And reigned but three months and ten days in Jerusalem; and did evil before the Lord.

45 So after a year Nabuchodonosor sent and caused him to be brought into Babylon with the holy vessels of the Lord;

46 And made Zedechias king of Judea and Jerusalem, when he was one and twenty years old; and he reigned eleven years:

47 And he did evil also in the sight of the Lord, and cared not for the words that were spoken unto him by the prophet Jeremy from the mouth of the Lord.

48 And after that king Nabuchodonosor had made him to swear by the name of the Lord, he forswore himself, and rebelled; and hardening his neck, his heart, he transgressed the laws of the Lord God of Israel.

49 The governors also of the people and of the priests did many things against the laws, and passed all the pollutions of all nations, and defiled the temple of the Lord, which was sanctified in Jerusalem.

50 Nevertheless the God of their fathers sent by his messenger to call them back, because he spared them and his tabernacle also.

51 But they had his messengers in derision; and, look, when the Lord spake unto them, they made a sport of his prophets:

52 So far forth, that he, being wroth with his people for their great ungodliness, commanded the kings of the Chaldees to come up against them;

53 Who slew their young men with the sword, yea, even within the compass of their holy temple, and spared neither young man nor maid, old man nor child, among them; for he delivered all into their hands.

54 And they took all the holy vessels of the Lord, both great and small, with the vessels of the ark of God, and the king's treasures, and carried them away into Babylon.

55 As for the house of the Lord, they burnt it, and brake down the walls of Jerusalem, and set fire upon her towers:

56 And as for her glorious things, they never ceased till they had consumed and brought them all to nought: and the people that were not slain with the sword he carried unto Babylon:

57 Who became servants to him and his children, till the Persians reigned, to fulfil the word of the Lord spoken by the mouth of Jeremy:

58 Until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths, the whole time of her desolation shall she rest, until the full term of seventy years.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Josiah keeps the passover in Jerusalem on the fourteenth day of the first month, priests and Levites set in their courses, with offerings so generous that the text says such a passover had not been kept since Samuel. Josiah is then mortally wounded fighting Pharaoh at Megiddo; his successors Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim, and Zedekiah do evil, the temple vessels are carried to Babylon, and the land, desolate, enjoys her sabbaths for seventy years. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Passover. the feast kept on the fourteenth day of the first month, remembering the deliverance from Egypt (verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Courses. the ordered divisions of priests and Levites for temple service (verses 2-4). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. JFB notes the day appointed by the law was kept on this occasion, with the priests ranged in their courses and exhorted to be ready in the manner legal purity required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. JFB explains the priests were set in their courses for the feast, and Bissell notes their long garments were their official costume. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, E. C. Bissell
  • Verse 3. JFB explains the Levites, as ministers and instructors of the people in divine worship, were commanded to restore the holy ark to Solomon's house, whether it had been put away by an idolatrous king or removed during temple repairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. JFB notes each course was composed of those belonging to the same fathers' house, and that since worship was now fixed in a permanent temple the Levites were no longer required to bear the ark on their shoulders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. JFB explains the Levites stood in the court of the priests where the victims were killed, with the people admitted by families in companies, while the Levites handed the blood and fat from one to another to the officiating priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. JFB notes the minute directions were meant to facilitate distribution of the paschal lambs, especially providing for the many reduced to poverty by the Assyrian devastations, so the lambs could be delivered in order to each family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. JFB notes the lambs and kids were in all probability destined for the poor, since a lamb or a kid might be used at convenience, while the bullocks were offered after the lambs on each successive day of the feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. JFB identifies these temple governors as ecclesiastical princes, including Hilkiah the high priest, and notes their gifts to the priests followed the pattern of Hezekiah's princes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. JFB notes that as the Levitical tribes were not yet sufficiently provided, eminent Levites distinguished in Hezekiah's time gave a large additional contribution for the use of the Levites exclusively. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. JFB notes the Levites took a prominent part in killing and stripping the sacrifices, properly priestly duties, because the priests could not overtake the extraordinary amount of work and the Levites had been duly sanctified for the service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. JFB notes that once all preparations were complete and the appointed time arrived, the solemnity was celebrated with the Levites enlisted for the time in priestly employment because of the multitudes engaged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. JFB explains the small cattle designed for burnt offerings were put apart from the paschal lambs, which were carefully selected and intended to be sacramentally eaten, and that the passover was roasted with fire according to the ordinance while other offerings were cooked in pots and pans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. JFB notes the portions were divided speedily among the people, either because of the multiplicity of the priests' business or to preserve the heat and flavor of the viands, and that the meal included the meat of the thank offerings as well as the paschal lambs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. JFB notes the Levites rendered this aid to the priests solely because the priests were so engrossed the entire day that they had no leisure to provide refreshments for themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. JFB notes the choir sang Psalms 113 to 118, once, twice, and even a third time during each company of offerers, and since they could not leave their posts the Levites prepared food for them as the bands relieved each other in turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. JFB notes what distinguished this passover above all preceding solemnities was not grandeur or concourse but the ardent devotion of king and people and unusually strict adherence, even in the smallest minutiae, to the forms prescribed in the book of the law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. JFB notes the reference to Samuel means the same as the reference in Kings to the days of the judges, since Samuel concluded the era of the judges, and that though most of the northern kingdom was in exile, some remaining inhabitants journeyed to Jerusalem for the feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. JFB notes that if the chronology rests on an historical basis, all the events from the temple repairs to this passover happened in about five and a half months, and that Josiah's eighteenth year was reckoned from the autumn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. JFB explains Josiah likely calculated that restored worship and revived religion would bring settled peace and prosperity, but the bright interval was brief, not from unfaithfulness in the divine promise but from the national apostasy drawing down long-threatened judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. JFB notes Necho informed Josiah he had no intention of molesting the Jews, since his expedition was directed solely against his old Assyrian enemy, and that commentators disagree whether his claimed divine commission came through Jeremiah or was merely an appeal to authority Josiah would respect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. JFB notes that as Josiah could not know the truth of Necho's declaration, he did not sin in opposing him, or if he sinned at all it was a sin of ignorance, and that he disregarded the warning and joined battle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. JFB notes Josiah, as a faithful vassal of Assyria, resolved to oppose Necho's march across the northern country, and that the armies met in the valley of Megiddo, the plain of Esdraelon, where Josiah could most conveniently intercept the Egyptian passage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. JFB notes the engagement took place and Josiah was mortally wounded, with the Egyptian army striking across the country past Megiddo. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. JFB notes the wounded king was taken out of his war chariot and put into his second chariot, the carriage for ordinary use, which would be more comfortable for the royal sufferer than the war chariot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. JFB notes the second chariot carried the suffering king back toward Jerusalem, and that the death of this good king was the subject of universal and lasting regret. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. JFB notes Jeremiah's elegy for Josiah has not reached us, but it seems to have been long preserved and chanted on public occasions, and the lamentation at Hadad-rimmon in the valley of Megiddo passed into a proverbial phrase for any great and extraordinary sorrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. JFB notes the prophet's lament was preserved among the people and sung by professional singers, probably from a collection of funeral odes composed on the death of good and great men of the nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. JFB notes the people raised Shallum, afterwards called Jehoahaz, to the throne in preference to his older brother Eliakim, and that the royal anointing, not usually necessary in regular succession, was resorted to in order to impart greater validity to the popular election. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. JFB notes Jehoahaz's power lasted only three months, since Necho, deeming it expedient to have a king of his own nomination on the throne, deposed the popularly elected monarch and placed his brother Eliakim, or Jehoiakim, there as an obsequious vassal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. JFB notes the tribute of a hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold amounted to about a hundred and ninety-five thousand dollars by their reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. JFB notes Necho laid a tribute on the country, raised Eliakim, or Jehoiakim, as his vassal to the throne, and on his departure brought Jehoahaz captive with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. JFB notes Jehoiakim followed the course of his idolatrous predecessors, and that as the deputy of the king of Egypt he departed further than his predecessor from Josiah's principles, grinding exactions from his subjects to meet his master's cupidity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. JFB explains this refers to Nebuchadnezzar's first expedition, when as joint sovereign with his infirm father he defeated the Egyptians at Carchemish, reduced the provinces west of the Euphrates, and made Jehoiakim a vassal who was bound in chains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. JFB notes Nebuchadnezzar took Jerusalem, carried away a portion of the sacred vessels, perhaps in lieu of unpaid tribute, and deposited them in the temple of his god Belus at Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. JFB notes Jehoiachin, also called Jeconiah or Coniah, was eighteen rather than eight years old, as appears from Kings and from the full development of his ungodly principles, and that his appointment by the people gave umbrage to Nebuchadnezzar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. JFB notes Jehoiachin's short reign cannot be considered at variance with the prophetic denunciation against his father, and that his ungodly principles were already fully developed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. JFB notes that when the year was expired, in the spring when campaigns usually began, Nebuchadnezzar came in person against Jerusalem, captured the city, sent Jehoiachin in chains to Babylon, and pillaged the remaining treasures of temple and palace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. JFB notes Nebuchadnezzar appointed Zedekiah, whose name was originally Mattaniah and was changed according to the custom of Oriental conquerors, and that though son of Josiah he is called Jehoiachin's brother in the latitude of Hebrew style for a kinsman. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. JFB notes Zedekiah's own pride and obdurate impiety, the incurable idolatry of the nation, and their reckless disregard of prophetic warnings brought down the long-threatened judgments of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. JFB notes Zedekiah received his crown on the express condition of a solemn oath of fealty to Babylon, so his revolt by joining a league with Pharaoh-hophra involved the crime of perjury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. JFB notes the incurable idolatry of the nation and reckless disregard of prophetic warnings brought down judgment, with Nebuchadnezzar as the executioner of divine vengeance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. JFB notes Nebuchadnezzar commenced a third siege of Jerusalem, which after holding out for a year and a half was taken in the eleventh year of Zedekiah, resulting in the burning of the temple and the overthrow of the kingdom of Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. JFB notes the siege ended in the burning of the temple, with most probably the ark, and the overthrow of the kingdom of Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 57. JFB notes the land was to enjoy her sabbaths after the divine institution of the sabbatic year had been neglected, and that Jeremiah's prophecy told it would remain desolate seventy years in divine retribution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 58. JFB notes the sabbatic rest of the land, neglected for so long, was now enforced in desolation for seventy years, as Moses and Jeremiah had foretold, and that leaving Palestine uncolonized, contrary to Assyrian custom, must be ascribed to the overruling providence of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a question investigated, not concluded)

Josiah's passover, told here and in 2 Chronicles 35

This section investigates a parallel; it does not conclude that one passage depends on the other, shares its authorship, or records the same event. Parallels and differences are both stated, and the question is left open where the evidence runs out. Interpreted Editorial framing for this section

Parallel. Both 1 Esdras 1 and 2 Chronicles 35 tell of Josiah keeping the passover in Jerusalem, priests and Levites set in their courses, generous offerings, and a passover unmatched in living memory, followed by Josiah's death in battle against the king of Egypt. Interpreted 1 Esdras 1; 2 Chronicles 35 (Standard corpus)

Difference. The two tellings differ in order, detail, and spelling of names, and 1 Esdras carries the story on through Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim, and Zedekiah to the seventy years of desolation, a span 2 Chronicles 35 does not cover in the same passage. Interpreted 1 Esdras 1; 2 Chronicles 35 (Standard corpus)

Unresolved. Whether these passages are related beyond the parallel itself is not established by the sources consulted for this page, and no dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty is claimed here. Unresolved No source consulted for this page establishes the relationship

JFB notes Josiah, as a faithful vassal of Assyria, resolved to oppose Necho's march across the northern country, and that the armies met in the valley of Megiddo, the plain of Esdraelon, where Josiah could most conveniently intercept the Egyptian passage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. JFB notes the engagement took place and Josiah was mortally wounded, with the Egyptian army striking across the country past Megiddo. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. JFB notes the wounded king was taken out of his war chariot and put into his second chariot, the carriage for ordinary use, which would be more comfortable for the royal sufferer than the war chariot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. JFB notes the second chariot carried the suffering king back toward Jerusalem, and that the death of this good king was the subject of universal and lasting regret. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. JFB notes Jeremiah's elegy for Josiah has not reached us, but it seems to have been long preserved and chanted on public occasions, and the lamentation at Hadad-rimmon in the valley of Megiddo passed into a proverbial phrase for any great and extraordinary sorrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. JFB notes the prophet's lament was preserved among the people and sung by professional singers, probably from a collection of funeral odes composed on the death of good and great men of the nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. JFB notes the people raised Shallum, afterwards called Jehoahaz, to the throne in preference to his older brother Eliakim, and that the royal anointing, not usually necessary in regular succession, was resorted to in order to impart greater validity to the popular election. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. JFB notes Jehoahaz's power lasted only three months, since Necho, deeming it expedient to have a king of his own nomination on the throne, deposed the popularly elected monarch and placed his brother Eliakim, or Jehoiakim, there as an obsequious vassal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. JFB notes the tribute of a hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold amounted to about a hundred and ninety-five thousand dollars by their reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. JFB notes Necho laid a tribute on the country, raised Eliakim, or Jehoiakim, as his vassal to the throne, and on his departure brought Jehoahaz captive with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. JFB notes Jehoiakim followed the course of his idolatrous predecessors, and that as the deputy of the king of Egypt he departed further than his predecessor from Josiah's principles, grinding exactions from his subjects to meet his master's cupidity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. JFB explains this refers to Nebuchadnezzar's first expedition, when as joint sovereign with his infirm father he defeated the Egyptians at Carchemish, reduced the provinces west of the Euphrates, and made Jehoiakim a vassal who was bound in chains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. JFB notes Nebuchadnezzar took Jerusalem, carried away a portion of the sacred vessels, perhaps in lieu of unpaid tribute, and deposited them in the temple of his god Belus at Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. JFB notes Jehoiachin, also called Jeconiah or Coniah, was eighteen rather than eight years old, as appears from Kings and from the full development of his ungodly principles, and that his appointment by the people gave umbrage to Nebuchadnezzar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. JFB notes Jehoiachin's short reign cannot be considered at variance with the prophetic denunciation against his father, and that his ungodly principles were already fully developed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. JFB notes that when the year was expired, in the spring when campaigns usually began, Nebuchadnezzar came in person against Jerusalem, captured the city, sent Jehoiachin in chains to Babylon, and pillaged the remaining treasures of temple and palace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. JFB notes Nebuchadnezzar appointed Zedekiah, whose name was originally Mattaniah and was changed according to the custom of Oriental conquerors, and that though son of Josiah he is called Jehoiachin's brother in the latitude of Hebrew style for a kinsman. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. JFB notes Zedekiah's own pride and obdurate impiety, the incurable idolatry of the nation, and their reckless disregard of prophetic warnings brought down the long-threatened judgments of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. JFB notes Zedekiah received his crown on the express condition of a solemn oath of fealty to Babylon, so his revolt by joining a league with Pharaoh-hophra involved the crime of perjury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. JFB notes the incurable idolatry of the nation and reckless disregard of prophetic warnings brought down judgment, with Nebuchadnezzar as the executioner of divine vengeance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. JFB notes Nebuchadnezzar commenced a third siege of Jerusalem, which after holding out for a year and a half was taken in the eleventh year of Zedekiah, resulting in the burning of the temple and the overthrow of the kingdom of Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. JFB notes the siege ended in the burning of the temple, with most probably the ark, and the overthrow of the kingdom of Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 57. JFB notes the land was to enjoy her sabbaths after the divine institution of the sabbatic year had been neglected, and that Jeremiah's prophecy told it would remain desolate seventy years in divine retribution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 58. JFB notes the sabbatic rest of the land, neglected for so long, was now enforced in desolation for seventy years, as Moses and Jeremiah had foretold, and that leaving Palestine uncolonized, contrary to Assyrian custom, must be ascribed to the overruling providence of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, E. C. Bissell

  • Verse 2. JFB explains the priests were set in their courses for the feast, and Bissell notes their long garments were their official costume. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, E. C. Bissell
