The Text — Jubilees 6 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And on the new moon of the third month he went forth from the ark, and built an altar on that mountain.

2 And he made atonement for the earth and took a kid and made atonement by its blood for

3 all the guilt of the earth ; for everything that had been on it had been destroyed, save those that were in the ark with Noah.

4 And he placed the fat thereof on the altar, and he took an ox, and a goat, and a sheep and kids, and salt, and a turtle-dove, and the young of a dove, and placed a burnt sacrifice on the altar, and poured thereon an offering mingled with oil, and sprinkled wine and strewed frankincense over everything, and caused a goodly savour to arise, acceptable before the Lord.

5 And the Lord smelt the goodly savour, and He made a covenant with him that there should not be any more a flood to destroy the earth ; that all the days of the earth seed-time and harvest should never cease ; cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night should not change their order, nor cease for ever.

6 "And you, increase ye and multiply upon the earth, and become many upon it, and be a blessing upon it.

7 The fear of you and the dread of you I shall inspire in everything that is on earth and in the sea.

8 And behold I have given unto you all beasts, and all winged things, and everything that moveth on the earth, and the fish in the waters, and all things for food ; as the green herbs, I have given you all things to eat.

9 But flesh, with the life thereof, with the blood, ye shall not eat ; for the life of all flesh is in the blood, lest your blood of your lives be required. At the hand of every man, at the hand of every (beast), shall I require the blood of man.

10 Whoso sheddeth man's blood by man shall his blood be shed; for in the image of God made He man.

11 And you, increase ye, and multiply on the earth." 10.

12 And Noah and his sons swore that they would not eat

13 any blood that was in any flesh, and he made a covenant before the Lord God for ever throughout all the generations of the earth in this month.

14 On this account He spake to thee that thou shouldst make a covenant with the children of Israel in this month upon the mountain with an oath, and that thou shouldst sprinkle blood upon them because of all the words of the covenant, which the Lord made with them for ever.

15 And this testimony is written concerning you that you should observe it continually, so that you should not eat on any day any blood of beasts or birds or cattle during all the days of the earth, and the man who eateth the blood of beast or of cattle or of birds during all the days of the earth, he and his seed shall be rooted out of the land.

16 And do thou command the children of Israel to eat no blood, so that their names and their seed may be before the Lord our God continually.

17 And for this law there is no limit of days, for it is for ever.

18 They shall observe it throughout their generations, so that they may continue supplicating on your behalf with blood before the altar ; every day and at the time of morning and evening they shall seek forgiveness on your behalf perpetually before the Lord that they may keep it and not be rooted out.

19 And He gave to Noah and his sons a sign that there should not again be a flood on the earth.

20 He set His bow in the cloud for a sign of the eternal covenant that there should not again be a flood on the earth to destroy it all the days of the earth.

21 For this reason it is ordained and written on the heavenly tables, that they should celebrate the feast

22 of weeks in this month once a year, to renew the covenant every year.

23 And this whole festival was celebrated in heaven from the day of creation till the days of Noah — twenty-six jubilees and five weeks of years : and Noah and his sons observed it for seven jubilees and one week of years, till the day of Noah's death, and from the day of Noah's death his sons did away with (it) until the days of Abraham, and they ate blood.

24 But Abraham observed it, and Isaac and Jacob and his children observed it up to thy days, and in thy days the children of Israel forgot it until ye celebrated it anew on this mountain.

25 And do thou command the children of Israel to observe this festival in all their generations for a commandment unto them : one day in the year in this month they shall celebrate the festival.

26 For it is the feast of weeks and the feast of firstfruits : this feast is twofold and of a double nature : according to what is written and engraven concerning it celebrate it.

27 For I have written in the book of the first law, in that which I have written for thee, that thou shouldst celebrate it in its season, one day 7 in the year, and I explained to thee its sacrifices that the children of Israel should remember and should celebrate it throughout their generations in this month, one day in every year.

28 And on the new moon of the first month, and on the new moon of the fourth

29 i.e. the Pentateuch.

30 month, and on the new moon of the seventh month, and on the new moon of the tenth month are the days of remembrance, and the days of the seasons in the four divisions of the year.

31 These are written and ordained as a testimony for ever.

32 And Noah ordained them for himself as feasts for the generations for ever, so that they have become thereby a memorial unto him.

33 And on the new moon of the first month he was bidden to make for himself an ark, and on that (day) the earth became dry and he opened (the ark) and saw the earth, 26.

34 And on the new moon of the fourth month the mouths of the depths of the abysses beneath were closed.

35 And on the new moon of the seventh month all the mouths of the abysses of the earth were opened, and the waters began to descend into them.

36 And on the new moon of the tenth month the tops of the mountains were seen, and, Noah was glad.

37 And on this account he ordained them for himself as feasts for a memorial for ever, and thus are they ordained.

38 And they placed them on the heavenly tables, each had thirteen weeks ; from one to another (passed) their memorial, from the first to the second, and from the second to the third, and from the third to the fourth.

39 And all the days of the commandment will be two and fifty weeks of days, and (these will make) the entire year complete.

40 Thus it is engraven and ordained on the heavenly tables.

41 And

42 there is no neglecting (this commandment) for a single year or from year to year.

43 And command thou the children of Israel that they observe the years according to this reckoning — three hundred and sixty-four days, and (these) will constitute a complete year, and they will not disturb its time from its days and from its feasts ; for everything will fall out in them according to their testimony, and they will not leave out any day nor disturb any feasts.

44 But if they do neglect and do not observe them according to His commandment, then they will disturb all their seasons, and the years will be dislodged from this (order), [and they will disturb the seasons and the years will be dislodged] and they will neglect their ordinances.

45 And all the children of Israel will forget, and will not find the path of the years, and will forget the new moons, and seasons, and sabbaths, and they will go wrong as to all the order of the years.

46 For I know and from henceforth shall I declare it unto thee, and it is not of my own devising ; for the book (lieth) written before me, and on the heavenly tables the division of days is ordained, lest they forget the feasts of the covenant and walk according to the feasts of the Gentiles after their error and after their ignorance.

47 For there will be those who will assuredly make observations of the moon — how (it) disturbeth the seasons and cometh in from year to year ten days too soon. 37.

48 For this reason the years will come upon them when they will disturb (the order),

49 ' A lunar year consists of 354 days.

50 Our author wages a polemic against the use of the moon for determining the seasons and feasts.

51 But a lunar year was accepted by the Pharisees.

52 and make an abominable (day) the day of testimony, and an unclean day a feast day, and they will confound all the days, the holy with the unclean, and the unclean day with the holy; for they will go wrong as to the months and sabbaths and feasts and jubilees.

53 For this reason I command and testify to thee that thou mayest testify to them ; for after thy death thy children will disturb (them), so that they will not make the year three hundred and sixty-four days only, and for this reason they will go wrong as to the new moons and seasons and sabbaths and festivals, and they will eat all kinds of blood with all kinds of flesh.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: On the new moon of the third month Noah went forth from the ark and built an altar on that mountain. For this reason God commands and testifies to Moses that he may testify to them, for after his death his children will disturb them, so that they will not make the year three hundred and sixty-four days only, and for this reason they will go wrong as to new moons, seasons, sabbaths and festivals, and will eat all kinds of blood with all kinds of flesh. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. On the new moon of the third month Noah went forth from the ark and built an altar on that mountain. JFB on Genesis 8:20 notes the altar was literally a high place, probably a mound of earth, and Noah's first care was to return thanks for the signal mercy he and his family had experienced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He made atonement for the earth and took a kid and made atonement by its blood for the guilt of the earth. The first act after the flood is atonement, not merely thanksgiving: the earth itself needs cleansing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 3. This verse completes the atonement: it was for all the guilt of the earth, for everything on it had been destroyed save those in the ark with Noah. The survivors alone are left to make atonement for a drowned world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 4. He placed the fat on the altar, and took an ox, a goat, a sheep and kids, salt, a turtle-dove and the young of a dove, placing a burnt sacrifice on the altar, pouring an offering mingled with oil, sprinkling wine and strewing frankincense over everything, causing a goodly savour acceptable before the Lord. JFB on Genesis 8:20-21 notes Noah took of every clean beast and fowl for so unparalleled a deliverance, and the sacrifice of a righteous man in faith was acceptable as the most fragrant incense; Jubilees gives the full offering system, salt, oil, wine and frankincense, before Sinai. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Lord smelt the goodly savour and made a covenant that there should not be any more a flood to destroy the earth, that seed-time and harvest should never cease, and cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night should not change their order nor cease forever. JFB on Genesis 8:21-22 reads 'the Lord said in his heart' as a sworn promise that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth, holding good while the earth remains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. 'And you, increase ye and multiply upon the earth, and become many upon it, and be a blessing upon it.' JFB on Genesis 9:1 notes this republishes the law of nature announced to Adam, the original blessing reannounced in the very same words as at first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. 'The fear of you and the dread of you I shall inspire in everything that is on earth and in the sea.' JFB on Genesis 9:2 explains dominion is re-established, now founded not in love and kindness as at first but in terror, a dread that keeps beasts from man's haunts except those employed in his service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. 'Behold I have given unto you all beasts, and all winged things, and everything that moveth on the earth, and the fish in the waters, and all things for food; as the green herbs, I have given you all things to eat.' JFB on Genesis 9:3 notes man was for the first time allowed animal food, but the grant came with one restriction, given in the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. 'But flesh, with the life thereof, with the blood, ye shall not eat; for the life of all flesh is in the blood, lest your blood of your lives be required. At the hand of every man, at the hand of every beast, shall I require the blood of man.' JFB on Genesis 9:4-5 explains the prohibition as preventing the ferocity of eating flesh from living animals, and the requirement of blood as a new power for protecting life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. 'Whoso sheddeth man's blood by man shall his blood be shed; for in the image of God made He man.' JFB on Genesis 9:6 notes the image has been injured by the fall but is not lost, so a high value attaches to the life of every man, even the poorest, and an awful criminality lies in destroying it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. 'And you, increase ye, and multiply on the earth.' The covenant blessing is repeated to close the quoted words, framing the blood law between two blessings of fruitfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. This verse opens the oath: Noah and his sons swore that they would not eat any blood in any flesh. The sentence is completed by the covenant of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. Noah made a covenant before the Lord God forever, throughout all the generations of the earth, in this month. The blood oath is sealed as an everlasting covenant in the third month, the month of the exodus from the ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. On this account God spoke to Moses to make a covenant with Israel in this month upon the mountain with an oath, and to sprinkle blood upon them because of all the words of the covenant the Lord made with them forever. Noah's third-month blood covenant is the pattern for Sinai's third-month blood sprinkling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. This testimony is written concerning you, that you should observe it continually, never eating on any day any blood of beasts or birds or cattle during all the days of the earth, and the man who eats such blood during all the days of the earth, he and his seed shall be rooted out of the land. The prohibition is perpetual and its penalty reaches the seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. Moses is to command Israel to eat no blood, so that their names and their seed may be before the Lord continually. Abstinence from blood keeps their names standing before God without break. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. For this law there is no limit of days, for it is forever. Unlike the seasonal feasts, the blood law is not bounded by a calendar: it binds every day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. They shall observe it throughout their generations, so that they may continue supplicating on your behalf with blood before the altar, and every day, morning and evening, they shall seek forgiveness on your behalf perpetually before the Lord, that they may keep it and not be rooted out. Daily morning and evening altar blood becomes perpetual supplication and forgiveness for the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. God gave Noah and his sons a sign that there should not again be a flood on the earth. The sign follows the covenant, sealing the promise that the seasons will not cease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. He set his bow in the cloud for a sign of the eternal covenant that there should not again be a flood on the earth to destroy it all the days of the earth. JFB on Genesis 9:13 explains 'set' as constituted or appointed: a common, familiar phenomenon made the pledge of peace, welcomed with joy whenever showers begin to fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. This verse introduces the ordinance: for this reason it is ordained and written on the heavenly tables that they should celebrate the feast of weeks. The sentence is completed by the next verse's annual renewal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. The feast of weeks is to be celebrated in this month once a year, to renew the covenant every year. The annual feast renews Noah's covenant, as the bow renews its sign in the cloud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. This whole festival was celebrated in heaven from the day of creation until the days of Noah, twenty-six jubilees and five weeks of years, and Noah and his sons observed it for seven jubilees and one week of years until Noah's death, but from his death his sons did away with it until the days of Abraham, and they ate blood. Charles notes the feast of weeks was first observed on earth in connection with Noah's covenant, designed to celebrate that covenant's institution, whereas later Judaism held it celebrated the Sinai legislation; here its observance lapses after Noah, and blood-eating returns with the lapse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. But Abraham observed it, and Isaac and Jacob and his children observed it up to Moses' days, and in Moses' days the children of Israel forgot it until they celebrated it anew on this mountain. The patriarchal chain preserves the feast, Israel forgets it, and Sinai restores it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. Moses is to command Israel to observe this festival in all their generations as a commandment: one day in the year, in this month, they shall celebrate the festival. The feast is fixed to a single annual day in the third month. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. For it is the feast of weeks and the feast of firstfruits: this feast is twofold and of a double nature, and according to what is written and engraven concerning it they are to celebrate it. Two names mark one feast: weeks for the counting, firstfruits for the harvest it celebrates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. For it is written in the book of the first law, in that which was written for Moses, that he should celebrate it in its season, one day in the year, and its sacrifices were explained to him so that Israel should remember and celebrate it throughout their generations in this month, one day in every year. Charles's note explains 'the book of the first law' as the Pentateuch, in which the feast's season and sacrifices stand written. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. This verse opens the season-days list: on the new moon of the first month, and on the new moon of the fourth month. The sentence is completed by the seventh and tenth months in verse 30. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. On the new moon of the seventh month and on the new moon of the tenth month are the days of remembrance, and the days of the seasons in the four divisions of the year. Four new moons quarter the year, each marking a season's turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. These are written and ordained as a testimony forever. The four season days stand as everlasting testimony, like the Sabbath and the blood law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. Noah ordained them for himself as feasts for the generations forever, so that they have become thereby a memorial unto him. Noah's personal ordinance becomes a generational memorial, tied to his own flood dates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. On the new moon of the first month Noah was bidden to make for himself an ark, and on that day the earth became dry and he opened the ark and saw the earth. The first month's new moon is doubly memorial: the ark's command and the earth's drying both fall on it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. On the new moon of the fourth month the mouths of the depths of the abysses beneath were closed. The fourth month's new moon recalls the shutting of the deep's fountains in chapter 5. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. On the new moon of the seventh month all the mouths of the abysses of the earth were opened, and the waters began to descend into them. The seventh month's new moon recalls the waters' descent into the deep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. On the new moon of the tenth month the tops of the mountains were seen, and Noah was glad. The tenth month's new moon recalls the first sight of the mountains' tops and Noah's gladness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. On this account he ordained them for himself as feasts for a memorial forever, and thus are they ordained. The flood's dated turning points become feasts, so the calendar itself remembers the deluge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. They placed them on the heavenly tables, each season having thirteen weeks, and from one to another passed their memorial, from the first to the second, from the second to the third, and from the third to the fourth. Four seasons of thirteen weeks each carry the memorial in unbroken succession through the year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. All the days of the commandment will be two and fifty weeks of days, and these will make the entire year complete. Fifty-two weeks complete the year with nothing left over and nothing lacking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. Thus it is engraven and ordained on the heavenly tables. The 52-week year is engraved, not devised: the calendar, like the laws, comes from the tables. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. This verse is the hinge to the no-neglect rule: there is no neglecting this commandment. The sentence is completed by the next verse's ban on gap years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. There is no neglecting this commandment for a single year or from year to year. Calendar observance admits no gap year; one missed year breaks the reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. Moses is to command Israel to observe the years according to this reckoning, three hundred and sixty-four days, constituting a complete year, not disturbing its time from its days and feasts, for everything will fall out according to their testimony, leaving out no day and disturbing no feast. The 364-day year keeps every feast on its appointed day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. But if they neglect and do not observe them according to his commandment, they will disturb all their seasons, and the years will be dislodged from this order, disturbing the seasons and dislodging the years, and neglecting their ordinances. Neglect unseats seasons and years together, and ordinances fall with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. All the children of Israel will forget, and will not find the path of the years, and will forget the new moons, seasons and sabbaths, and will go wrong as to all the order of the years. Forgetting the calendar means losing the year's path itself, the apostasy foretold in chapter 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. For God knows and will declare it to Moses, and it is not of his own devising, for the book lies written before him, and on the heavenly tables the division of days is ordained, lest they forget the feasts of the covenant and walk according to the feasts of the Gentiles after their error and ignorance. The calendar is revealed, not invented, and its purpose is to keep Israel on covenant feasts rather than Gentile ones. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. For there will be those who will assuredly make observations of the moon, how it disturbs the seasons and comes in from year to year ten days too soon. Charles's note explains the arithmetic: a lunar year of 354 days falls ten days short of the 364-day year, so moon reckoning shifts the seasons year by year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 48. This verse opens the consequence: for this reason the years will come upon them when they will disturb the order. The sentence is completed by verse 52's confounding of holy and unclean days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 52. They will make an abominable day the day of testimony, and an unclean day a feast day, and will confound all the days, the holy with the unclean and the unclean with the holy, for they will go wrong as to the months, sabbaths, feasts and jubilees. Moon reckoning ends in inverted holiness: abominable days kept as testimony, unclean days kept as feasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 53. For this reason God commands and testifies to Moses that he may testify to them, for after his death his children will disturb them, so that they will not make the year three hundred and sixty-four days only, and for this reason they will go wrong as to new moons, seasons, sabbaths and festivals, and will eat all kinds of blood with all kinds of flesh. Moses' testimony anticipates the post-Mosaic disturbance, and the chapter's two laws fall together: calendar error ends in blood-eating. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

Noah's third-month blood covenant is the pattern for Sinai's third-month blood sprinkling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. This testimony is written concerning you, that you should observe it continually, never eating on any day any blood of beasts or birds or cattle during all the days of the earth, and the man who eats such blood during all the days of the earth, he and his seed shall be rooted out of the land. The prohibition is perpetual and its penalty reaches the seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. Moses is to command Israel to eat no blood, so that their names and their seed may be before the Lord continually. Abstinence from blood keeps their names standing before God without break. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. For this law there is no limit of days, for it is forever. Unlike the seasonal feasts, the blood law is not bounded by a calendar: it binds every day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. They shall observe it throughout their generations, so that they may continue supplicating on your behalf with blood before the altar, and every day, morning and evening, they shall seek forgiveness on your behalf perpetually before the Lord, that they may keep it and not be rooted out. Daily morning and evening altar blood becomes perpetual supplication and forgiveness for the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. God gave Noah and his sons a sign that there should not again be a flood on the earth. The sign follows the covenant, sealing the promise that the seasons will not cease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. This verse introduces the ordinance: for this reason it is ordained and written on the heavenly tables that they should celebrate the feast of weeks. The sentence is completed by the next verse's annual renewal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. The feast of weeks is to be celebrated in this month once a year, to renew the covenant every year. The annual feast renews Noah's covenant, as the bow renews its sign in the cloud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. This whole festival was celebrated in heaven from the day of creation until the days of Noah, twenty-six jubilees and five weeks of years, and Noah and his sons observed it for seven jubilees and one week of years until Noah's death, but from his death his sons did away with it until the days of Abraham, and they ate blood. Charles notes the feast of weeks was first observed on earth in connection with Noah's covenant, designed to celebrate that covenant's institution, whereas later Judaism held it celebrated the Sinai legislation; here its observance lapses after Noah, and blood-eating returns with the lapse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. But Abraham observed it, and Isaac and Jacob and his children observed it up to Moses' days, and in Moses' days the children of Israel forgot it until they celebrated it anew on this mountain. The patriarchal chain preserves the feast, Israel forgets it, and Sinai restores it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. Moses is to command Israel to observe this festival in all their generations as a commandment: one day in the year, in this month, they shall celebrate the festival. The feast is fixed to a single annual day in the third month. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. For it is the feast of weeks and the feast of firstfruits: this feast is twofold and of a double nature, and according to what is written and engraven concerning it they are to celebrate it. Two names mark one feast: weeks for the counting, firstfruits for the harvest it celebrates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. For it is written in the book of the first law, in that which was written for Moses, that he should celebrate it in its season, one day in the year, and its sacrifices were explained to him so that Israel should remember and celebrate it throughout their generations in this month, one day in every year. Charles's note explains 'the book of the first law' as the Pentateuch, in which the feast's season and sacrifices stand written. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. This verse opens the season-days list: on the new moon of the first month, and on the new moon of the fourth month. The sentence is completed by the seventh and tenth months in verse 30. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. On the new moon of the seventh month and on the new moon of the tenth month are the days of remembrance, and the days of the seasons in the four divisions of the year. Four new moons quarter the year, each marking a season's turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. These are written and ordained as a testimony forever. The four season days stand as everlasting testimony, like the Sabbath and the blood law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. Noah ordained them for himself as feasts for the generations forever, so that they have become thereby a memorial unto him. Noah's personal ordinance becomes a generational memorial, tied to his own flood dates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. On the new moon of the first month Noah was bidden to make for himself an ark, and on that day the earth became dry and he opened the ark and saw the earth. The first month's new moon is doubly memorial: the ark's command and the earth's drying both fall on it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. On the new moon of the fourth month the mouths of the depths of the abysses beneath were closed. The fourth month's new moon recalls the shutting of the deep's fountains in chapter 5. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. On the new moon of the seventh month all the mouths of the abysses of the earth were opened, and the waters began to descend into them. The seventh month's new moon recalls the waters' descent into the deep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. On the new moon of the tenth month the tops of the mountains were seen, and Noah was glad. The tenth month's new moon recalls the first sight of the mountains' tops and Noah's gladness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. On this account he ordained them for himself as feasts for a memorial forever, and thus are they ordained. The flood's dated turning points become feasts, so the calendar itself remembers the deluge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. They placed them on the heavenly tables, each season having thirteen weeks, and from one to another passed their memorial, from the first to the second, from the second to the third, and from the third to the fourth. Four seasons of thirteen weeks each carry the memorial in unbroken succession through the year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. All the days of the commandment will be two and fifty weeks of days, and these will make the entire year complete. Fifty-two weeks complete the year with nothing left over and nothing lacking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. Thus it is engraven and ordained on the heavenly tables. The 52-week year is engraved, not devised: the calendar, like the laws, comes from the tables. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. This verse is the hinge to the no-neglect rule: there is no neglecting this commandment. The sentence is completed by the next verse's ban on gap years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. There is no neglecting this commandment for a single year or from year to year. Calendar observance admits no gap year; one missed year breaks the reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. Moses is to command Israel to observe the years according to this reckoning, three hundred and sixty-four days, constituting a complete year, not disturbing its time from its days and feasts, for everything will fall out according to their testimony, leaving out no day and disturbing no feast. The 364-day year keeps every feast on its appointed day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. But if they neglect and do not observe them according to his commandment, they will disturb all their seasons, and the years will be dislodged from this order, disturbing the seasons and dislodging the years, and neglecting their ordinances. Neglect unseats seasons and years together, and ordinances fall with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. All the children of Israel will forget, and will not find the path of the years, and will forget the new moons, seasons and sabbaths, and will go wrong as to all the order of the years. Forgetting the calendar means losing the year's path itself, the apostasy foretold in chapter 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. For God knows and will declare it to Moses, and it is not of his own devising, for the book lies written before him, and on the heavenly tables the division of days is ordained, lest they forget the feasts of the covenant and walk according to the feasts of the Gentiles after their error and ignorance. The calendar is revealed, not invented, and its purpose is to keep Israel on covenant feasts rather than Gentile ones. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. For there will be those who will assuredly make observations of the moon, how it disturbs the seasons and comes in from year to year ten days too soon. Charles's note explains the arithmetic: a lunar year of 354 days falls ten days short of the 364-day year, so moon reckoning shifts the seasons year by year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 48. This verse opens the consequence: for this reason the years will come upon them when they will disturb the order. The sentence is completed by verse 52's confounding of holy and unclean days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 52. They will make an abominable day the day of testimony, and an unclean day a feast day, and will confound all the days, the holy with the unclean and the unclean with the holy, for they will go wrong as to the months, sabbaths, feasts and jubilees. Moon reckoning ends in inverted holiness: abominable days kept as testimony, unclean days kept as feasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 53. For this reason God commands and testifies to Moses that he may testify to them, for after his death his children will disturb them, so that they will not make the year three hundred and sixty-four days only, and for this reason they will go wrong as to new moons, seasons, sabbaths and festivals, and will eat all kinds of blood with all kinds of flesh. Moses' testimony anticipates the post-Mosaic disturbance, and the chapter's two laws fall together: calendar error ends in blood-eating. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 5. The Lord smelt the goodly savour and made a covenant that there should not be any more a flood to destroy the earth, that seed-time and harvest should never cease, and cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night should not change their order nor cease forever. JFB on Genesis 8:21-22 reads 'the Lord said in his heart' as a sworn promise that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth, holding good while the earth remains. JFB on Genesis 9:1 notes this republishes the law of nature announced to Adam, the original blessing reannounced in the very same words as at first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

JFB on Genesis 9:2 explains dominion is re-established, now founded not in love and kindness as at first but in terror, a dread that keeps beasts from man's haunts except those employed in his service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. 'Behold I have given unto you all beasts, and all winged things, and everything that moveth on the earth, and the fish in the waters, and all things for food; as the green herbs, I have given you all things to eat.' JFB on Genesis 9:3 notes man was for the first time allowed animal food, but the grant came with one restriction, given in the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. 'But flesh, with the life thereof, with the blood, ye shall not eat; for the life of all flesh is in the blood, lest your blood of your lives be required. At the hand of every man, at the hand of every beast, shall I require the blood of man.' JFB on Genesis 9:4-5 explains the prohibition as preventing the ferocity of eating flesh from living animals, and the requirement of blood as a new power for protecting life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. 'Whoso sheddeth man's blood by man shall his blood be shed; for in the image of God made He man.' JFB on Genesis 9:6 notes the image has been injured by the fall but is not lost, so a high value attaches to the life of every man, even the poorest, and an awful criminality lies in destroying it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. 'And you, increase ye, and multiply on the earth.' The covenant blessing is repeated to close the quoted words, framing the blood law between two blessings of fruitfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He set his bow in the cloud for a sign of the eternal covenant that there should not again be a flood on the earth to destroy it all the days of the earth. JFB on Genesis 9:13 explains 'set' as constituted or appointed: a common, familiar phenomenon made the pledge of peace, welcomed with joy whenever showers begin to fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
