The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Timothy 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the commandment of God our Saviour, and Lord Jesus Christ, which is our hope;

2 Unto Timothy, my own son in the faith: Grace, mercy, and peace, from God our Father and Jesus Christ our Lord.

3 As I besought thee to abide still at Ephesus, when I went into Macedonia, that thou mightest charge some that they teach no other doctrine,

4 Neither give heed to fables and endless genealogies, which minister questions, rather than godly edifying which is in faith: so do.

5 Now the end of the commandment is charity out of a pure heart, and of a good conscience, and of faith unfeigned:

6 From which some having swerved have turned aside unto vain jangling;

7 Desiring to be teachers of the law; understanding neither what they say, nor whereof they affirm.

8 But we know that the law is good, if a man use it lawfully;

9 Knowing this, that the law is not made for a righteous man, but for the lawless and disobedient, for the ungodly and for sinners, for unholy and profane, for murderers of fathers and murderers of mothers, for manslayers,

10 For whoremongers, for them that defile themselves with mankind, for menstealers, for liars, for perjured persons, and if there be any other thing that is contrary to sound doctrine;

11 According to the glorious gospel of the blessed God, which was committed to my trust.

12 And I thank Christ Jesus our Lord, who hath enabled me, for that he counted me faithful, putting me into the ministry;

13 Who was before a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and injurious: but I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief.

14 And the grace of our Lord was exceeding abundant with faith and love which is in Christ Jesus.

15 This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.

16 Howbeit for this cause I obtained mercy, that in me first Jesus Christ might shew forth all longsuffering, for a pattern to them which should hereafter believe on him to life everlasting.

17 Now unto the King eternal, immortal, invisible, the only wise God, be honour and glory for ever and ever. Amen.

18 This charge I commit unto thee, son Timothy, according to the prophecies which went before on thee, that thou by them mightest war a good warfare;

19 Holding faith, and a good conscience; which some having put away concerning faith have made shipwreck:

20 Of whom is Hymenaeus and Alexander; whom I have delivered unto Satan, that they may learn not to blaspheme.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul charges Timothy, left at Ephesus, to stop teachers of fables and endless genealogies, whose speculations miss the goal of the commandment, which is love from a pure heart, good conscience and sincere faith. The law is good when used lawfully, and Paul presents himself, once blasphemer and persecutor, as the foremost example of mercy, that Christ's patience might be seen in him. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul writes as an apostle of Jesus Christ by the commandment of God our Saviour, and Henry notes his credentials were unquestionable, since he held not only a commission but a commandment from God and from Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Timothy is addressed as Paul's own son in the faith, with grace, mercy, and peace wished him, and Henry notes Paul had been the means of Timothy's conversion and Timothy had served with him as a dutiful son with a loving father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Paul had besought Timothy to abide at Ephesus when he went to Macedonia, charging some to teach no other doctrine, since the epistle's design is to guard the church against false teachers who corrupt the gospel by subtle distinctions and endless disputes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Timothy must oppose fables and endless genealogies, which raise questions rather than edifying, and Henry warns that what gives occasion for doubtful disputes pulls the church down rather than building it up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The end of the commandment is charity out of a pure heart, a good conscience, and faith unfeigned, and Henry notes that whatever weakens love to God or to brethren defeats the commandment's very end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Some, having swerved from that end, turned aside to vain jangling, substituting empty talk for the love the commandment was meant to produce. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. These men desired to be teachers of the law yet understood neither what they said nor what they affirmed, claiming authority over a law whose meaning they had not grasped. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The law is good if used lawfully, and Henry explains that for believers made righteous in God's appointed way the law is not against them, since its design agrees with the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The law is made not for a righteous man but for the lawless and disobedient, for the ungodly and sinners, so that its proper work is to expose and restrain sin rather than to condemn those already righteous by faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The catalogue continues through the unholy and profane, murderers, whoremongers, men-stealers, liars, and perjurers, all contrary to sound doctrine, showing the law's reach over every form of ungodliness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. All of this stands according to the glorious gospel of the blessed God, and Henry notes that unless we are made righteous by faith in Christ, really repenting and forsaking sin, we remain under the curse of the law even according to that gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Paul thanks Christ who enabled him, counting him faithful and putting him into the ministry, and Henry observes that Paul knew he would justly have perished had the Lord been extreme to mark what was amiss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Paul had been a blasphemer, persecutor, and injurious man, yet he obtained mercy because he did it ignorantly in unbelief, and Henry traces his whole turn to grace abundant when he was dead in sin, working faith and love in his heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The grace of the Lord was exceeding abundant with faith and love in Christ Jesus, so that the chief persecutor became the chief exhibit of grace's power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. This is a faithful saying worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom Paul is chief, and Henry insists no man with Paul's example before him can question Christ's love and power to save him if he truly trusts him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Paul obtained mercy so that in him first Jesus Christ might show all longsuffering, as a pattern to those who should afterward believe to life everlasting, making his own case the template of hope for later sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. To the King eternal, immortal, invisible, the only wise God, Paul ascribes honour and glory for ever, and Henry turns the doxology into a call to admire and praise the grace of God our Saviour and ascribe glory to Father, Son, and Holy Ghost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Paul renews his charge to Timothy according to the prophecies that went before, that he might war a good warfare, and Henry notes the ministry is a warfare against sin and Satan under Christ, the Captain of salvation, and that others' good hopes of us should stir us to duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Timothy must hold faith and a good conscience, which some put away and made shipwreck of their faith, and Henry warns that putting away conscience is the way to make shipwreck of faith also. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Hymenaeus and Alexander are named as those delivered to Satan that they might learn not to blaspheme, and Henry explains the highest censure's design: to prevent further sin and reclaim the sinner, not merely to punish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
