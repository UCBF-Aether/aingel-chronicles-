The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Abram went up out of Egypt, he, and his wife, and all that he had, and Lot with him, into the south.

2 And Abram was very rich in cattle, in silver, and in gold.

3 And he went on his journeys from the south even to Bethel, unto the place where his tent had been at the beginning, between Bethel and Hai;

4 Unto the place of the altar, which he had made there at the first: and there Abram called on the name of the LORD.

5 And Lot also, which went with Abram, had flocks, and herds, and tents.

6 And the land was not able to bear them, that they might dwell together: for their substance was great, so that they could not dwell together.

7 And there was a strife between the herdmen of Abram’s cattle and the herdmen of Lot’s cattle: and the Canaanite and the Perizzite dwelled then in the land.

8 And Abram said unto Lot, Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee, and between my herdmen and thy herdmen; for we be brethren.

9 Is not the whole land before thee? separate thyself, I pray thee, from me: if thou wilt take the left hand, then I will go to the right; or if thou depart to the right hand, then I will go to the left.

10 And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where, before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah, even as the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt, as thou comest unto Zoar.

11 Then Lot chose him all the plain of Jordan; and Lot journeyed east: and they separated themselves the one from the other.

12 Abram dwelled in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom.

13 But the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly.

14 And the LORD said unto Abram, after that Lot was separated from him, Lift up now thine eyes, and look from the place where thou art northward, and southward, and eastward, and westward:

15 For all the land which thou seest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed for ever.

16 And I will make thy seed as the dust of the earth: so that if a man can number the dust of the earth, then shall thy seed also be numbered.

17 Arise, walk through the land in the length of it and in the breadth of it; for I will give it unto thee.

18 Then Abram removed his tent, and came and dwelt in the plain of Mamre, which is in Hebron, and built there an altar unto the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Back from Egypt, Abram and Lot grow too rich in flocks to dwell together, and their herdsmen quarrel. Lot chooses the well-watered plain of Jordan toward Sodom, Abram remains in Canaan, God renews the promise of land and offspring, and Abram settles by Mamre at Hebron and builds an altar. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Abram returned from Egypt to the south of Canaan, rich in livestock, silver, and gold, but his wealth was now held by a man humbled by his Egyptian failure and restored to the land of promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Abram's great wealth in cattle and precious metals is noted because it soon becomes the occasion of strife, showing that prosperity itself can test a household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Abram journeyed back stage by stage to Bethel, retracing his steps to the place where he had first worshipped in Canaan, as a man returning to his first faithfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. At Bethel Abram returned to the altar he had built at first and called on God's name again, a picture of a believer going back to the worship he had left behind in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Lot also had flocks, herds, and tents, so the two households had both grown large, and their shared prosperity, not poverty, is what made living together difficult. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The land could not support both households together with their herds, so what had been a blessing of increase became a practical problem that had to be faced honestly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Quarrels broke out between Abram's herdsmen and Lot's herdsmen, and the Canaanites and Perizzites living nearby could see it, which made the strife a poor witness for Abram's household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Abram took the lead in making peace, reminding Lot that they were kinsmen and that strife between brothers was shameful, especially before watching neighbours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Abram offered Lot the first choice of land, yielding his own right as elder and host. Henry praises this as the peacemaker's wisdom: giving way in lesser matters to keep love intact. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Lot looked out over the well-watered plain of Jordan, green like a garden, and chose by sight, judging the land by its present richness rather than by its people or its future. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Lot chose the whole Jordan plain for himself and moved east, so the two kinsmen parted, with Abram left in Canaan and Lot heading toward the cities of the plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Abram stayed in Canaan while Lot pitched his tent toward Sodom, a first step of nearness to a wicked city that would later cost him dearly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The men of Sodom are described as exceedingly wicked, a warning note set beside Lot's choice: the fairest land can hold the worst neighbours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. After Lot's departure, God spoke to Abram again, as if to show that losing Lot's company was no loss to the promise, which rested on God alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Abram was told to look in every direction, because the whole land he could see was given to him and his offspring, a promise far wider than the portion Lot had chosen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. His offspring would be as countless as dust, a promise that stretched Abram's faith, since he was still childless, and pointed to a multitude no man could number. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Abram was told to walk through the land in its length and breadth, taking possession by faith and survey, since God had given it to him even before his feet owned it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Abram moved his tent to the oaks of Mamre near Hebron and built an altar there, settling into the promised land in worship and in peace after the quarrel was ended. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
