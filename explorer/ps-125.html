The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 125 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 They that trust in the LORD shall be as mount Zion, which cannot be removed, but abideth for ever.

2 As the mountains are round about Jerusalem, so the LORD is round about his people from henceforth even for ever.

3 For the rod of the wicked shall not rest upon the lot of the righteous; lest the righteous put forth their hands unto iniquity.

4 Do good, O LORD, unto those that be good, and to them that are upright in their hearts.

5 As for such as turn aside unto their crooked ways, the LORD shall lead them forth with the workers of iniquity: but peace shall be upon Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A psalm of stability: those who trust in the LORD shall be as Mount Zion, which cannot be removed but abides for ever, and as the mountains are round about Jerusalem, so the LORD is round about his people from henceforth even for ever. The rod of the wicked shall not rest upon the lot of the righteous, lest the righteous put forth their hands to iniquity. The prayer is for good to those who are good and upright in heart, while those who turn aside to crooked ways shall be led forth with workers of iniquity — and peace shall be upon Israel. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry sums this psalm Isaiah 3:10-11: well with righteous, ill with wicked, blessing and curse set before us; trusters are Mount Zion — church built on a rock gates of hell shall not prevail, firm by providence much more by promise, not removed from integrity or confidence, abiding for ever in grace earnest of glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry shelters Jerusalem by round mountains breaking winds and barring enemies, such a defence is God's providence: Lord round about his people on every side, no gap in hedge where seeking enemy can enter (Job 1:10), henceforth even for ever — mountains may moulder, covenant cannot break (Isa 54:10), full accomplishment in heaven as pillars going no more out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry allows rod of wicked power and anger may fall on persons, estates, liberties, families, names — anything of lot, only not souls — but promises it shall not rest: God cuts work short in righteousness, makes way of escape with temptation, lest over-long trial tempt righteous to put hands to iniquity, grow weary, distrust promise; God proportions trials to strength and strength to trials, for elect's sake days shortened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry prays for sincere and constant: Do good, O Lord, to those that are good — supplication for all saints in faith, those who do well shall be well dealt with, as they should be shall be as they would be provided upright in heart, really as good as they seem; with upright God shows upright; not perfect sinless spotless, but sincere honest; promises should quicken prayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry predicts, not prays, ruin of hypocrites and deserters turning aside for fear of rod to crooked indirect ways or growing worse and more daring: God sends away, casts out, leads forth with workers of iniquity — portion with worst sinners, doom same as lifelong walkers and more grievous, led as prisoners to execution, former righteousness not mentioned; but peace upon Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
