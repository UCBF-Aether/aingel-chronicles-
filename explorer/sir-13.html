The Text — Sirach 13 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 He that toucheth pitch shall be defiled therewith; and he that hath fellowship with a proud man shall be like unto him.

2 Burden not thyself above thy power while thou livest; and have no fellowship with one that is mightier and richer than thyself: for how agree the kettle and the earthen pot together? for if the one be smitten against the other, it shall be broken.

3 The rich man hath done wrong, and yet he threateneth withal: the poor is wronged, and he must intreat also.

4 If thou be for his profit, he will use thee: but if thou have nothing, he will forsake thee.

5 If thou have any thing, he will live with thee: yea, he will make thee bare, and will not be sorry for it.

6 If he have need of thee, he will deceive thee, and smile upon thee, and put thee in hope; he will speak thee fair, and say, What wantest thou?

7 And he will shame thee by his meats, until he have drawn thee dry twice or thrice, and at the last he will laugh thee to scorn afterward, when he seeth thee, he will forsake thee, and shake his head at thee.

8 Beware that thou be not deceived and brought down in thy jollity.

9 If thou be invited of a mighty man, withdraw thyself, and so much the more will he invite thee.

10 Press thou not upon him, lest thou be put back; stand not far off, lest thou be forgotten.

11 Affect not to be made equal unto him in talk, and believe not his many words: for with much communication will he tempt thee, and smiling upon thee will get out thy secrets:

12 But cruelly he will lay up thy words, and will not spare to do thee hurt, and to put thee in prison.

13 Observe, and take good heed, for thou walkest in peril of thy overthrowing: when thou hearest these things, awake in thy sleep.

14 Love the Lord all thy life, and call upon him for thy salvation.

15 Every beast loveth his like, and every man loveth his neighbor.

16 All flesh consorteth according to kind, and a man will cleave to his like.

17 What fellowship hath the wolf with the lamb? so the sinner with the godly.

18 What agreement is there between the hyena and a dog? and what peace between the rich and the poor?

19 As the wild ass is the lion's prey in the wilderness: so the rich eat up the poor.

20 As the proud hate humility: so doth the rich abhor the poor.

21 A rich man beginning to fall is held up of his friends: but a poor man being down is thrust away by his friends.

22 When a rich man is fallen, he hath many helpers: he speaketh things not to be spoken, and yet men justify him: the poor man slipped, and yet they rebuked him too; he spake wisely, and could have no place.

23 When a rich man speaketh, every man holdeth his tongue, and, look, what he saith, they extol it to the clouds: but if the poor man speak, they say, What fellow is this? and if he stumble, they will help to overthrow him.

24 Riches are good unto him that hath no sin, and poverty is evil in the mouth of the ungodly.

25 The heart of a man changeth his countenance, whether it be for good or evil: and a merry heart maketh a cheerful countenance.

26 A cheerful countenance is a token of a heart that is in prosperity; and the finding out of parables is a wearisome labour of the mind.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Contact defiles, like pitch on the hand, so fellowship with the proud makes a man like them, and the chapter traces the snares of rich and mighty company. Against all of them the steady answer is to love the Lord all one's life and call on him for salvation, ending on a cheerful countenance as the token of a prosperous heart. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He who touches pitch will be defiled with it, and he who has fellowship with a proud man will be like him. Contact defiles: pitch stains the toucher, and fellowship with the proud makes a man proud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Do not burden yourself above your power while you live, and have no fellowship with one mightier and richer than yourself, for how do the kettle and the earthen pot agree together? If one is smitten against the other, it will be broken. Unequal fellowship with the mightier and richer breaks the weaker vessel, as an earthen pot breaks against a kettle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. The rich man has done wrong, and yet he threatens as well; the poor man is wronged, and must entreat also. Wrongdoing by the rich is answered with threats, while the wronged poor man must still beg. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. If you are for his profit, he will use you, but if you have nothing, he will forsake you. The rich man's friendship lasts only while you profit him; emptiness ends it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. If you have anything, he will live with you; indeed he will make you bare, and will not be sorry for it. While you have something, the rich man lives off you until you are bare, without sorrow for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. If he has need of you, he will deceive you, smile upon you, put you in hope, speak you fair, and say, what do you want? Need makes the rich man a smiling deceiver, raising hopes with fair speech to get what he wants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. He will shame you by his meats until he has drawn you dry twice or thrice, and at last he will laugh you to scorn; afterward, when he sees you, he will forsake you and shake his head at you. Feasts at his table shame and drain you repeatedly, ending in scorn, forsaking, and a shaken head. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Beware that you are not deceived and brought down in your jollity. Jollity itself is a danger: deception in merriment brings a man down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. If you are invited by a mighty man, withdraw yourself, and so much the more will he invite you. Modest withdrawal from a mighty man's invitation draws a warmer second invitation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Do not press upon him, lest you be put back; do not stand far off, lest you be forgotten. Court manners require a middle course: pressing earns rebuff, distance earns forgetfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. Do not affect to be made equal to him in talk, and do not believe his many words, for with much communication he will tempt you, and smiling upon you will get out your secrets.
