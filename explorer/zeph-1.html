The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Zephaniah 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word of the LORD which came unto Zephaniah the son of Cushi, the son of Gedaliah, the son of Amariah, the son of Hizkiah, in the days of Josiah the son of Amon, king of Judah.

2 I will utterly consume all things from off the land, saith the LORD.

3 I will consume man and beast; I will consume the fowls of the heaven, and the fishes of the sea, and the stumblingblocks with the wicked: and I will cut off man from off the land, saith the LORD.

4 I will also stretch out mine hand upon Judah, and upon all the inhabitants of Jerusalem; and I will cut off the remnant of Baal from this place, and the name of the Chemarims with the priests;

5 And them that worship the host of heaven upon the housetops; and them that worship and that swear by the LORD, and that swear by Malcham;

6 And them that are turned back from the LORD; and those that have not sought the LORD, nor enquired for him.

7 Hold thy peace at the presence of the Lord GOD: for the day of the LORD is at hand: for the LORD hath prepared a sacrifice, he hath bid his guests.

8 And it shall come to pass in the day of the LORD’s sacrifice, that I will punish the princes, and the king’s children, and all such as are clothed with strange apparel.

9 In the same day also will I punish all those that leap on the threshold, which fill their masters’ houses with violence and deceit.

10 And it shall come to pass in that day, saith the LORD, that there shall be the noise of a cry from the fish gate, and an howling from the second, and a great crashing from the hills.

11 Howl, ye inhabitants of Maktesh, for all the merchant people are cut down; all they that bear silver are cut off.

12 And it shall come to pass at that time, that I will search Jerusalem with candles, and punish the men that are settled on their lees: that say in their heart, The LORD will not do good, neither will he do evil.

13 Therefore their goods shall become a booty, and their houses a desolation: they shall also build houses, but not inhabit them; and they shall plant vineyards, but not drink the wine thereof.

14 The great day of the LORD is near, it is near, and hasteth greatly, even the voice of the day of the LORD: the mighty man shall cry there bitterly.

15 That day is a day of wrath, a day of trouble and distress, a day of wasteness and desolation, a day of darkness and gloominess, a day of clouds and thick darkness,

16 A day of the trumpet and alarm against the fenced cities, and against the high towers.

17 And I will bring distress upon men, that they shall walk like blind men, because they have sinned against the LORD: and their blood shall be poured out as dust, and their flesh as the dung.

18 Neither their silver nor their gold shall be able to deliver them in the day of the LORD’s wrath; but the whole land shall be devoured by the fire of his jealousy: for he shall make even a speedy riddance of all them that dwell in the land.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The word of the LORD comes to Zephaniah in the days of Josiah, and it opens with total consumption: man and beast, fowl and fish, stumblingblocks with the wicked, cut off from the land. The hand stretches first over Judah and Jerusalem, against the remnant of Baal, those who worship the host of heaven on housetops, those who swear by the LORD and by Malcham, and those settled on their lees who say the LORD will do neither good nor evil. The great day of the LORD is near and hastens greatly, a day of wrath, trouble, darkness, and trumpet alarm, when neither silver nor gold will deliver anyone. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. This is the title-page of the book, and its authority is from heaven, not from men: it is the word of the Lord. Zephaniah's pedigree is traced back four generations to Hizkiah, likely Hezekiah king of Judah, so that a prophet of royal descent could with the better grace reprove the folly of the king's children, and he prophesied in the days of Josiah, who reigned well and carried on a work of reformation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Ruin is coming, utter ruin, and the expressions are figurative, speaking desolation everywhere. The Hebrew word for utterly consume means to sweep or scrape off utterly, and the land to be swept is Judah, left without inhabitants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. This verse spells out in detail the all things of verse 2: man and beast, birds and fish, are all to be consumed. The stumblingblocks are the idols which cause Judah to stumble, and the idols and their worshippers shall be involved in a common destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. God will stretch out his hand upon Judah, including Benjamin, and upon Jerusalem, the fountainhead of the evil, for God begins with his sanctuary. The remnant of Baal is the remains of Baal worship which Josiah had not yet been able utterly to eradicate in remote places, and the Chemarims, the idol priests, shall have their very name forgotten, along with the Aaronic priests who secretly abetted idolatry instead of eradicating it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Those to be cut off include those who worship the host of heaven upon the flat housetops, and those who swear to the Lord and yet swear by Malcham, that is, their king, the same as Molech. Those who think to divide their affections between God and idols will come short of acceptance with God, for if the Lord has but half, he will have none. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. This verse describes more comprehensively those guilty of defection from the Lord in any way: those turned back from following him, and those who have not sought him nor inquired for him. Neglect of God shows impiety and contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Hold thy peace at the presence of the Lord: let the earth be silent at his approach, and let murmurers cease their self-justifications and repent in time. The day of the Lord is at hand, and the sacrifice the Lord has prepared is the slaughter of the guilty Jews, with the Chaldeans bidden as guests to execute it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. In the day of the Lord's sacrifice he will punish the princes, who ought to have been an example of good but were ringleaders in evil, and the king's children, a word fulfilled on Zedekiah's children and earlier on Jehoahaz and Eliakim. Those clothed with strange apparel are the courtiers in costly foreign garments, adopted partly for luxury and partly to ingratiate themselves with foreign nations, whose costume as well as their idolatries they imitated, whereas God would have his people separate from the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Those that leap on the threshold are invaders of their neighbours' rights, seizing their possessions, and they fill their masters' houses with goods obtained by violence and deceit. God's day is at hand, and such presumptuous sinners are reckoned with for their pride and vanity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. This verse describes Jerusalem when besieged by Nebuchadnezzar, through the fish gate, named for the fish market near it, that the foe first entered. The cry is heard first at the fish gate, then in the second or lower city, and lastly with a great crashing from the hills within the walls, Zion, Moriah, and Ophel, as the foe advances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Maktesh, the mortar, is the valley where the merchants dwelt, and Jerusalem itself, lying among hills, is doomed to be the scene of its people being destroyed as corn is pounded in a mortar. The merchant people are called Canaanites in irony, for they are Canaanites in greed for gain and in idolatries, and those laden with silver carry what will prove only a burden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. At that time God will search Jerusalem with lamps, so that no dark corner is left wherein sin can escape punishment, the Chaldeans being his instruments. The men settled on their lees are hardened like the crust on wine long left undisturbed, made stupidly secure by wealthy, undisturbed ease, and they deny that God regards human affairs or renders good to the good and evil to the evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Therefore their goods shall become a booty and their houses a desolation, fulfilling the prophecy in Deuteronomy that they should build houses but not inhabit them and plant vineyards but not drink the wine. The trading people and the rich merchants are called to account for their secure and careless presumption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The great day of the Lord is near and hastens quickly, and it is a day of God's wrath to the utmost, in which he will show himself by taking vengeance. The mighty men shall cry bitterly in hopeless despair, for the might on which Jerusalem prides itself shall then fail utterly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. That day is a day of wrath, trouble, and distress, devastation and desolation, darkness and gloominess, clouds and thick darkness. The Hebrew terms, similar in sound, express the dreary monotony of desolation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. A day of the trumpet and alarm against the fortified cities and high towers: the trumpet is that of the besieging enemy, and the alarm is the war shout. The towers stood at the angles of the walls, built so that besiegers advancing might be assailed from the sides as well as in front. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. God will bring distress upon men, and they shall walk like blind men, unable to see where to turn to find escape from existing evils, because they have sinned against the Lord. Their blood shall be poured out like dust, and their flesh like refuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Neither their silver nor their gold shall be able to deliver them in the day of the Lord's wrath, for riches profit not in the day of wrath. The whole land shall be devoured by the fire of his jealousy, his wrath jealous for his honour consuming the guilty, and he will make a complete and sudden end of all who dwell in the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Baal and Baalim. the Canaanite god, and the plural form for the Baals, whose worship the prophets charge God's people with, crediting idols with gifts God himself gave. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • The day of the LORD. the day of God's visitation and judgment, pictured in the prophets as darkness, trumpet alarm, and distress, and also as deliverance for those who call on his name. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Malcham. explained in the study note for Zephaniah 1 as their king, the same as Molech, the idol with whom some in Jerusalem divided the worship owed to the LORD alone. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Maktesh. the mortar, the valley quarter of Jerusalem where the merchants dwelt, doomed in Zephaniah 1 to be pounded like corn in a mortar. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
