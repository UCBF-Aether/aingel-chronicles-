The Text — Judith 7 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 The next day Holofernes commanded all his army, and all his people which were come to take his part, that they should remove their camp against Bethulia, to take aforehand the ascents of the hill country, and to make war against the children of Israel.

2 Then their strong men removed their camps in that day, and the army of the men of war was an hundred and seventy thousand footmen, and twelve thousand horsemen, beside the baggage, and other men that were afoot among them, a very great multitude.

3 And they camped in the valley near unto Bethulia, by the fountain, and they spread themselves in breadth over Dothaim even to Belmaim, and in length from Bethulia unto Cynamon, which is over against Esdraelon.

4 Now the children of Israel, when they saw the multitude of them, were greatly troubled, and said every one to his neighbour, Now will these men lick up the face of the earth; for neither the high mountains, nor the valleys, nor the hills, are able to bear their weight.

5 Then every man took up his weapons of war, and when they had kindled fires upon their towers, they remained and watched all that night.

6 But in the second day Holofernes brought forth all his horsemen in the sight of the children of Israel which were in Bethulia,

7 And viewed the passages up to the city, and came to the fountains of their waters, and took them, and set garrisons of men of war over them, and he himself removed toward his people.

8 Then came unto him all the chief of the children of Esau, and all the governors of the people of Moab, and the captains of the sea coast, and said,

9 Let our lord now hear a word, that there be not an overthrow in thine army.

10 For this people of the children of Israel do not trust in their spears, but in the height of the mountains wherein they dwell, because it is not easy to come up to the tops of their mountains.

11 Now therefore, my lord, fight not against them in battle array, and there shall not so much as one man of thy people perish.

12 Remain in thy camp, and keep all the men of thine army, and let thy servants get into their hands the fountain of water, which issueth forth of the foot of the mountain:

13 For all the inhabitants of Bethulia have their water thence; so shall thirst kill them, and they shall give up their city, and we and our people shall go up to the tops of the mountains that are near, and will camp upon them, to watch that none go out of the city.

14 So they and their wives and their children shall be consumed with fire, and before the sword come against them, they shall be overthrown in the streets where they dwell.

15 Thus shalt thou render them an evil reward; because they rebelled, and met not thy person peaceably.

16 And these words pleased Holofernes and all his servants, and he appointed to do as they had spoken.

17 So the camp of the children of Ammon departed, and with them five thousand of the Assyrians, and they pitched in the valley, and took the waters, and the fountains of the waters of the children of Israel.

18 Then the children of Esau went up with the children of Ammon, and camped in the hill country over against Dothaim: and they sent some of them toward the south, and toward the east over against Ekrebel, which is near unto Chusi, that is upon the brook Mochmur; and the rest of the army of the Assyrians camped in the plain, and covered the face of the whole land; and their tents and carriages were pitched to a very great multitude.

19 Then the children of Israel cried unto the Lord their God, because their heart failed, for all their enemies had compassed them round about, and there was no way to escape out from among them.

20 Thus all the company of Assur remained about them, both their footmen, chariots, and horsemen, four and thirty days, so that all their vessels of water failed all the inhibitants of Bethulia.

21 And the cisterns were emptied, and they had not water to drink their fill for one day; for they gave them drink by measure.

22 Therefore their young children were out of heart, and their women and young men fainted for thirst, and fell down in the streets of the city, and by the passages of the gates, and there was no longer any strength in them.

23 Then all the people assembled to Ozias, and to the chief of the city, both young men, and women, and children, and cried with a loud voice, and said before all the elders,

24 God be judge between us and you: for ye have done us great injury, in that ye have not required peace of the children of Assur.

25 For now we have no helper: but God hath sold us into their hands, that we should be thrown down before them with thirst and great destruction.

26 Now therefore call them unto you, and deliver the whole city for a spoil to the people of Holofernes, and to all his army.

27 For it is better for us to be made a spoil unto them, than to die for thirst: for we will be his servants, that our souls may live, and not see the death of our infants before our eyes, nor our wives nor our children to die.

28 We take to witness against you the heaven and the earth, and our God and Lord of our fathers, which punisheth us according to our sins and the sins of our fathers, that he do not according as we have said this day.

29 Then there was great weeping with one consent in the midst of the assembly; and they cried unto the Lord God with a loud voice.

30 Then said Ozias to them, Brethren, be of good courage, let us yet endure five days, in the which space the Lord our God may turn his mercy toward us; for he will not forsake us utterly.

31 And if these days pass, and there come no help unto us, I will do according to your word.

32 And he dispersed the people, every one to their own charge; and they went unto the walls and towers of their city, and sent the women and children into their houses: and they were very low brought in the city.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Holofernes moves his camp against Bethulia, seizes the ascents of the hill country, and, on the counsel of the Edomites and Ammonites, takes the fountains of water rather than storming the walls. Thirst breaks the city: children faint, women and young men fall in the streets, and the people demand surrender. Ozias grants five days: if God does not help within them, they will deliver the city, and he disperses the people, every one to his charge, upon the walls and towers. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Fountains. the springs and water sources seized to force Bethulia by thirst rather than by battle (verse 12 in the chapter's account). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Five days. the term Ozias grants before surrender, which Judith will challenge in the next chapter (verse 30). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. Then their strong men removed their camps, and the army of men of war was an hundred and seventy thousand footmen and twelve thousand horsemen; the numbers vary by version — Greek seventy (thousand footmen?), Syriac sixty-two, and for horsemen Greek twelve as in chapter 2:7, Syriac twenty-two — and some texts must be inaccurate, though Holofernes' cavalry might have increased since he began the war. "Taken" is in Syriac "servants, and those who had joined them in great numbers." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. And they camped in the valley near unto Bethulia, by the fountain, spreading in breadth over Dothaim even to Belmaim; the hill side lay on the coast of the Mediterranean, leaving the mountains of Ephraim, which were so strait, on the left, as well as Jerusalem, as he intended to go into Egypt, and Bethulia alone opposed his progress (chapter 6:7). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. Now the children of Israel, when they saw the multitude, were greatly troubled, and said every one to his neighbour, "Now will these men lick up the face of the earth"; the Greek adds, "were greatly afraid, and each one said to his neighbour: Now these will consume (Complutensian, shut up) the face of the earth, and neither the high mountains, nor the vales, nor the hills, will bear their weight." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. Then every man took up his weapons of war, and when they had kindled fires upon their towers, they remained and watched all that night; the Greek continues, "Then taking their arms, and lighting fires upon their towers, they kept guard all that night" — the servants of God first humble themselves, and then take arms, confiding in God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. But in the second day Holofernes brought forth all his horsemen in the sight of the children of Israel in Bethulia; the Greek continues, "but the second day, Holofernes brought out all his cavalry against the Israelites, in Bethulia." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. And viewed the passages up to the city, and came to the fountains of their waters, and took them, and set garrisons of men of war over them; the Greek continues that he "reconnoitred the ascent of their city, and came to the fountains of their waters, and took them; and leaving a guard of soldiers, he returned to his men." An aqueduct is implied, so the fountain must have been on higher ground than the city, though the city was on an eminence — yet water may be forced to ascend by pipes. The Syriac is silent about the aqueduct and the small springs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. Then came unto him all the chief of the children of Esau, and all the governors of the people of Moab, and the captains of the sea coast; the Greek and Syriac read "the chiefs of the sons of Esau," as Idumea had been conquered (chapter 3:14), omitting Ammon, and after Moab subjoining "the generals of the maritime countries," Tyrians and Philistines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. So the camp of the children of Ammon departed, and with them five thousand of the Assyrians, and they pitched in the valley, and took the waters, and the fountains of the waters of the children of Israel; the Greek and Syriac intimate that the Ammonites and Moabites, with 5,000 Assyrian infantry, guarded the springs, while the rest of the army was stationed in the plain, for it was agreed only to blockade the place, that no men might be lost and the army might be ready to march against the more distant and powerful nations of Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. Then the children of Esau went up with the children of Ammon, and camped in the hill country over against Dothaim; the Greek and Syriac specify over-against Dothaim, which seems too remote, though such an immense army might cover a great part of the country, and another detachment was "southward, at Ecrebel (Syriac Ekarbat, probably Akrabim, the ascent of the scorpions) near Cush (or Arabia), which is above the torrent Mochmur (Syriac Peor, or Bezor), and the rest of the army of the Assyrians encamped in the plain, and covered the face of the earth." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. Thus all the company of Assur remained about them, both footmen, chariots, and horsemen, four and thirty days, so that all their vessels of water failed; the Greek reads "thirty-four days," the Old Vulgate "twenty-four days," and the Syriac "two months and four days." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. And the cisterns were emptied, and they had not water to drink their fill for one day, for they gave them drink by measure; "measure" marks the rationing under the blockade. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. Therefore their young children were out of heart, and their women and young men fainted for thirst, and fell down in the streets of the city; the Greek adds, "the infants, women, and young men fainted, and fell down dead." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 26. Now therefore call them unto you, and deliver the whole city for a spoil to the people of Holofernes and to all his army; the Greek reads, "call them, and give up all the city to plunder." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 28. We take to witness against you the heaven and the earth, and our God and Lord of our fathers, which punisheth us according to our sins and the sins of our parents; the Greek reads, "and the sins of our parents, that he may not treat us thus to-day," and deliver us up to death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 30. Then said Ozias to them, "Brethren, be of good courage, let us yet endure five days, in the which space the Lord our God may turn his mercy toward us"; the Greek reads, "And Ozias said to them: Take courage, brethren." On "five," Sulpitius reads fifteen, but instead of decim we should read demum. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 32. And he dispersed the people, every one to their own charge, and they went unto the walls and towers of their city, and sent the women and children into their houses; the Greek and Syriac add, "and he dispersed the people to their tents, and they went upon the walls and towers of the city, and sent their wives and children home. And they were under great humiliation in the city." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock

Where the notes differ

  • The size of the army. The study note on verse 2 records that the numbers vary by version: the Greek gives seventy (the note queries "thousand") footmen and twelve thousand horsemen, the Syriac sixty-two thousand footmen and twenty-two thousand horsemen, against the chapter's own figure of one hundred and seventy thousand footmen — the horsemen do not agree either. The versions are reported side by side, not harmonised. Interpreted Study note on verse 2, summarizing George Leo Haydock
