The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 35 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And God said unto Jacob, Arise, go up to Bethel, and dwell there: and make there an altar unto God, that appeared unto thee when thou fleddest from the face of Esau thy brother.

2 Then Jacob said unto his household, and to all that were with him, Put away the strange gods that are among you, and be clean, and change your garments:

3 And let us arise, and go up to Bethel; and I will make there an altar unto God, who answered me in the day of my distress, and was with me in the way which I went.

4 And they gave unto Jacob all the strange gods which were in their hand, and all their earrings which were in their ears; and Jacob hid them under the oak which was by Shechem.

5 And they journeyed: and the terror of God was upon the cities that were round about them, and they did not pursue after the sons of Jacob.

6 So Jacob came to Luz, which is in the land of Canaan, that is, Bethel, he and all the people that were with him.

7 And he built there an altar, and called the place Elbethel: because there God appeared unto him, when he fled from the face of his brother.

8 But Deborah Rebekah’s nurse died, and she was buried beneath Bethel under an oak: and the name of it was called Allonbachuth.

9 And God appeared unto Jacob again, when he came out of Padanaram, and blessed him.

10 And God said unto him, Thy name is Jacob: thy name shall not be called any more Jacob, but Israel shall be thy name: and he called his name Israel.

11 And God said unto him, I am God Almighty: be fruitful and multiply; a nation and a company of nations shall be of thee, and kings shall come out of thy loins;

12 And the land which I gave Abraham and Isaac, to thee I will give it, and to thy seed after thee will I give the land.

13 And God went up from him in the place where he talked with him.

14 And Jacob set up a pillar in the place where he talked with him, even a pillar of stone: and he poured a drink offering thereon, and he poured oil thereon.

15 And Jacob called the name of the place where God spake with him, Bethel.

16 And they journeyed from Bethel; and there was but a little way to come to Ephrath: and Rachel travailed, and she had hard labour.

17 And it came to pass, when she was in hard labour, that the midwife said unto her, Fear not; thou shalt have this son also.

18 And it came to pass, as her soul was in departing, (for she died) that she called his name Benoni: but his father called him Benjamin.

19 And Rachel died, and was buried in the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem.

20 And Jacob set a pillar upon her grave: that is the pillar of Rachel’s grave unto this day.

21 And Israel journeyed, and spread his tent beyond the tower of Edar.

22 And it came to pass, when Israel dwelt in that land, that Reuben went and lay with Bilhah his father’s concubine: and Israel heard it. Now the sons of Jacob were twelve:

23 The sons of Leah; Reuben, Jacob’s firstborn, and Simeon, and Levi, and Judah, and Issachar, and Zebulun:

24 The sons of Rachel; Joseph, and Benjamin:

25 And the sons of Bilhah, Rachel’s handmaid; Dan, and Naphtali:

26 And the sons of Zilpah, Leah’s handmaid: Gad, and Asher: these are the sons of Jacob, which were born to him in Padanaram.

27 And Jacob came unto Isaac his father unto Mamre, unto the city of Arbah, which is Hebron, where Abraham and Isaac sojourned.

28 And the days of Isaac were an hundred and fourscore years.

29 And Isaac gave up the ghost, and died, and was gathered unto his people, being old and full of days: and his sons Esau and Jacob buried him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God sends Jacob back to Bethel, where the household puts away foreign gods, Deborah dies, and God renews the covenant and the name Israel. Rachel dies bearing Benjamin on the road to Ephrath, Reuben sins with Bilhah, and Isaac dies old and full of days and is buried by Esau and Jacob. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God told Jacob to arise, go up to Bethel, dwell there, and build an altar to the God who appeared to him when he fled from Esau. JFB notes the command came seasonably, when Jacob longed to leave Shechem, and tenderly, as a reminder of a neglected vow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Jacob told his household to put away strange gods, be clean, and change garments. Henry reads the outward cleansing as a sign of inward purifying: clean clothes without a clean heart mean nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They were to arise and go to Bethel, where Jacob would build an altar to the God who answered him in distress and was with him on the way. The journey itself was to be an act of worship, not mere relocation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They gave Jacob all the strange gods and earrings, and he hid them under the oak by Shechem. JFB explains the earrings were linked to incantation and idolatry, and burial under a consecrated tree put them where no bold hand would disturb them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. As they journeyed, the terror of God fell on the surrounding cities, and no one pursued Jacob's sons. Henry's lesson is that the way of duty is the way of safety: when we are about God's work, we are under special protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. So Jacob came to Luz, which is Bethel, in Canaan, with all his people. JFB notes Luz was the Canaanite town name, from its almond trees, while Bethel was Jacob's family name for the place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. There Jacob built an altar and called the place El-bethel, because God had appeared to him there when he fled from his brother. Henry notes the comfort lies not in Bethel the house of God, but in El-bethel, the God of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, died and was buried beneath Bethel under an oak, called Allon-bachuth. JFB notes old nurses were honoured and loved as mothers, and her death was greatly lamented. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. God appeared to Jacob again after his return from Padan-aram and blessed him. The appearance after the solemn rites showed God's acceptance and renewed the covenant promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. God said Jacob's name would no longer be Jacob only, but Israel. As at Peniel, the new name marked a confirmed standing, not a fresh bargain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. God declared himself God Almighty and commanded fruitfulness: a nation and a company of nations, and kings, would come from Jacob. Henry notes God all-sufficient is able to make good the promise in due time and to support Jacob meanwhile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The land given to Abraham and Isaac was promised to Jacob and to his seed after him. The promise tied Jacob's personal blessing to the same land and lineage as his fathers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. God went up from Jacob in the place where he had talked with him. JFB notes God's presence had been shown in visible form, with acceptance shown by fire consuming the sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Jacob set up a stone pillar where God had spoken, poured a drink offering and oil on it. The rite repeated his earlier consecration of the place, now with fuller worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Jacob called the place where God spoke with him Bethel. The name, house of God, fixed the memory of the meeting in the landscape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. They journeyed from Bethel, and a little way from Ephrath Rachel went into hard labour. JFB notes Jacob left Bethel a better and more devoted servant of God, only to be plunged into mourning on the road. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. In hard labour the midwife told Rachel not to fear, for she would have this son also. The reassurance came in the midst of danger, not after it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. As her soul departed, Rachel called the child Ben-oni, son of my sorrow, but his father called him Benjamin. JFB explains Benjamin as son of the right hand, particularly dear and precious, while Henry adds Jacob would not renew the sorrow of his wife's death every time he named his son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Rachel died and was buried on the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem. JFB notes Ephrath was the old name and Bethlehem the later name, meaning house of bread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Jacob set a pillar on Rachel's grave, which remained the pillar of Rachel's grave. JFB notes the spot still marked in his day agreed with the record, about a mile from Bethlehem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Israel journeyed on and spread his tent beyond the tower of Edar. The journey continued through grief, without a pause in the narrative for comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. While Israel dwelt in that land, Reuben lay with Bilhah his father's concubine, and Israel heard of it; the verse then opens the reckoning that Jacob's sons were twelve. Henry notes only that Israel heard it, and that is enough: secret sin is generally disappointed in its secrecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Leah's sons are listed: Reuben the firstborn, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun. The grouping by mother preserves the family structure behind later tribal history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Rachel's sons are listed: Joseph and Benjamin. The short list carries the weight of the chapters that follow, since Joseph's story dominates the rest of Genesis. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Bilhah's sons are listed: Dan and Naphtali. They are named as Rachel's handmaid's sons, keeping the household's divisions visible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Zilpah's sons are listed: Gad and Asher, said to have been born to Jacob in Padan-aram. JFB's caution applies here too: the statement is true of the company in general, with Benjamin as the known exception. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Jacob came to Isaac his father at Mamre, the city of Arbah, which is Hebron, where Abraham and Isaac had sojourned. The return to his father's home closed the long journey from Padan-aram. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Isaac's days were a hundred and fourscore years. The long life is recorded simply, as the measure of a patriarch's span. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Isaac died, old and full of days, and was gathered to his people, and Esau and Jacob buried him. Henry notes the brothers' agreement at their father's funeral shows how wonderfully God had changed Esau's mind, and JFB adds the death is recorded here by anticipation, since Isaac lived years longer in the story's chronology. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
