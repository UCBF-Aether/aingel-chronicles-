The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And David spake unto the LORD the words of this song in the day that the LORD had delivered him out of the hand of all his enemies, and out of the hand of Saul:

2 And he said, The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer;

3 The God of my rock; in him will I trust: he is my shield, and the horn of my salvation, my high tower, and my refuge, my saviour; thou savest me from violence.

4 I will call on the LORD, who is worthy to be praised: so shall I be saved from mine enemies.

5 When the waves of death compassed me, the floods of ungodly men made me afraid;

6 The sorrows of hell compassed me about; the snares of death prevented me;

7 In my distress I called upon the LORD, and cried to my God: and he did hear my voice out of his temple, and my cry did enter into his ears.

8 Then the earth shook and trembled; the foundations of heaven moved and shook, because he was wroth.

9 There went up a smoke out of his nostrils, and fire out of his mouth devoured: coals were kindled by it.

10 He bowed the heavens also, and came down; and darkness was under his feet.

11 And he rode upon a cherub, and did fly: and he was seen upon the wings of the wind.

12 And he made darkness pavilions round about him, dark waters, and thick clouds of the skies.

13 Through the brightness before him were coals of fire kindled.

14 The LORD thundered from heaven, and the most High uttered his voice.

15 And he sent out arrows, and scattered them; lightning, and discomfited them.

16 And the channels of the sea appeared, the foundations of the world were discovered, at the rebuking of the LORD, at the blast of the breath of his nostrils.

17 He sent from above, he took me; he drew me out of many waters;

18 He delivered me from my strong enemy, and from them that hated me: for they were too strong for me.

19 They prevented me in the day of my calamity: but the LORD was my stay.

20 He brought me forth also into a large place: he delivered me, because he delighted in me.

21 The LORD rewarded me according to my righteousness: according to the cleanness of my hands hath he recompensed me.

22 For I have kept the ways of the LORD, and have not wickedly departed from my God.

23 For all his judgments were before me: and as for his statutes, I did not depart from them.

24 I was also upright before him, and have kept myself from mine iniquity.

25 Therefore the LORD hath recompensed me according to my righteousness; according to my cleanness in his eye sight.

26 With the merciful thou wilt shew thyself merciful, and with the upright man thou wilt shew thyself upright.

27 With the pure thou wilt shew thyself pure; and with the froward thou wilt shew thyself unsavoury.

28 And the afflicted people thou wilt save: but thine eyes are upon the haughty, that thou mayest bring them down.

29 For thou art my lamp, O LORD: and the LORD will lighten my darkness.

30 For by thee I have run through a troop: by my God have I leaped over a wall.

31 As for God, his way is perfect; the word of the LORD is tried: he is a buckler to all them that trust in him.

32 For who is God, save the LORD? and who is a rock, save our God?

33 God is my strength and power: and he maketh my way perfect.

34 He maketh my feet like hinds’ feet: and setteth me upon my high places.

35 He teacheth my hands to war; so that a bow of steel is broken by mine arms.

36 Thou hast also given me the shield of thy salvation: and thy gentleness hath made me great.

37 Thou hast enlarged my steps under me; so that my feet did not slip.

38 I have pursued mine enemies, and destroyed them; and turned not again until I had consumed them.

39 And I have consumed them, and wounded them, that they could not arise: yea, they are fallen under my feet.

40 For thou hast girded me with strength to battle: them that rose up against me hast thou subdued under me.

41 Thou hast also given me the necks of mine enemies, that I might destroy them that hate me.

42 They looked, but there was none to save; even unto the LORD, but he answered them not.

43 Then did I beat them as small as the dust of the earth, I did stamp them as the mire of the street, and did spread them abroad.

44 Thou also hast delivered me from the strivings of my people, thou hast kept me to be head of the heathen: a people which I knew not shall serve me.

45 Strangers shall submit themselves unto me: as soon as they hear, they shall be obedient unto me.

46 Strangers shall fade away, and they shall be afraid out of their close places.

47 The LORD liveth; and blessed be my rock; and exalted be the God of the rock of my salvation.

48 It is God that avengeth me, and that bringeth down the people under me.

49 And that bringeth me forth from mine enemies: thou also hast lifted me up on high above them that rose up against me: thou hast delivered me from the violent man.

50 Therefore I will give thanks unto thee, O LORD, among the heathen, and I will sing praises unto thy name.

51 He is the tower of salvation for his king: and sheweth mercy to his anointed, unto David, and to his seed for evermore.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

David sings a long song of deliverance, praising the LORD who rescued him from Saul and all his enemies, in storm and battle imagery. It moves from personal rescue to a covenant promise: God shows mercy to his anointed, to David and his seed for evermore. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David speaks the words of this song to the Lord in the day the Lord delivered him from all his enemies and from Saul. It has often been the lot of God's people to have many enemies, Henry notes, and David, a man after God's heart but not after men's, was hated by many; yet those who trust God in duty find him a present help in their greatest dangers, and every new mercy should put a new song in the mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The Lord is my rock, my fortress, and my deliverer. If God is my God, Henry says, then he is my rock, my strength and power, the rock under which I take shelter and on which I build my hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The God of my rock, in whom I trust, is shield, horn of salvation, high tower, refuge, and saviour who saves from violence. David often hid in a rock, Henry observes, but God was his chief hiding-place, and the horn of salvation both protects strongly and pushes enemies strongly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. I will call on the Lord, worthy to be praised, and so shall I be saved from my enemies. What we find in God worthy of praise, Henry notes, should engage us to pray to him and give him glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The waves of death compass him, and the floods of ungodly men make him afraid. The danger was very great, Henry says: surrounded with death on every side, threatened to be overwhelmed, seeing no way of escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The sorrows of hell compass him, and the snares of death prevent him. The floods of Belial and his instruments made him afraid, as if earth, death, and hell were all in arms against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. In distress he calls on the Lord and cries to his God, and God hears his voice out of his temple, and his cry enters his ears. His deliverance was an answer to prayer, Henry notes, and he leaves the example of crying to God with importunity, as frightened children cry to their parents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Then the earth shakes and trembles, and heaven's foundations move because God is wroth. The expressions are borrowed from Sinai, Henry says, to set forth God's glory manifested in the deliverance and his displeasure against his enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Smoke goes up from his nostrils, fire from his mouth devours, and coals are kindled by it. God's anger is set forth in these metaphors, and who knows the power and terror of his wrath? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. He bows the heavens, comes down, and darkness is under his feet. God, who seemed at a distance, comes near in judgment and rescue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He rides upon a cherub and flies, seen upon the wings of the wind. God hastens to his succour with seasonable relief, Henry notes, though he had seemed far off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. He makes darkness pavilions round about him, dark waters and thick clouds of the skies. God hides himself in darkness for the amazement of his enemies and the protection of his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Through the brightness before him coals of fire are kindled. Light and fire together mark the divine appearing: glory to the delivered, terror to the opposer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The Lord thunders from heaven, and the Most High utters his voice. The voice from above answers the cry from below, and the battle turns on a word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. He sends out arrows and scatters them, lightning, and discomfits them. The extraordinary confusion of his enemies is as if the earth trembled and the world's foundations were discovered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The channels of the sea appear and the world's foundations are discovered at the Lord's rebuking, at the blast of his nostrils. Who can stand before God when he is angry, Henry asks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. He sends from above, takes David, and draws him out of many waters. Rescue reaches down from heaven into the flood that was drowning him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. He delivers David from his strong enemy and from haters too strong for him. The strong enemy is Saul, Henry says, who would have been too hard for David had God not succoured him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. They prevent him in the day of his calamity, but the Lord is his stay. Burdened and ready to sink, he is supported by the Lord, who holds him up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. He brings David into a large place and delivers him because he delights in him. The deliverance comes not from common providence but covenant love, Henry notes, and David is herein a type of Christ, whom God upheld because he delighted in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The Lord rewards David according to his righteousness, according to the cleanness of his hands. God recompenses him according to his righteousness, Henry explains, not as if righteousness merited anything, but as what God is well pleased with and has an eye to. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. He has kept the ways of the Lord and has not wickedly departed from his God. His conscience witnesses that he kept to God's word as his rule, taking his religion wherever he went. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. All God's judgments are before him, and as for his statutes, he does not depart from them. Wherever he was, God's judgments were his guide, and he did not depart from the statutes even when exiled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. He is upright before God and keeps himself from his iniquity. Careful abstaining from our own iniquity, Henry says, is one of the best evidences of integrity, and David reflected with more comfort on victories over his own iniquity than on Goliath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Therefore the Lord recompenses him according to his righteousness, according to his cleanness in God's eyesight. The witness of his own heart to uprightness is sweeter, Henry notes, than the song that David slew his ten thousands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. With the merciful thou wilt show thyself merciful, and with the upright thou wilt show thyself upright. As we are found toward God, Henry says, he will be found toward us, and even the merciful need mercy and shall obtain it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. With the pure thou wilt show thyself pure, and with the froward thou wilt show thyself unsavoury. God's purity is the joy of the pure, Henry notes, but with the froward he wrestles, and those with whom God wrestles are sure to be foiled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The afflicted people thou wilt save, but thine eyes are on the haughty to bring them down. The afflicted are saved out of or by their afflictions, while the haughty are marked out, for God resists the proud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Thou art my lamp, O Lord, and the Lord will lighten my darkness. In the dark, benighted and at a loss, David finds God his lamp to show the way and dispel darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. By thee he runs through a troop, and by his God leaps over a wall. Military swiftness and boldness are both credited to God, not to David's own force. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. As for God, his way is perfect; the word of the Lord is tried, a buckler to all who trust in him. Men begin in kindness but end not, promise but perform not, Henry observes, but God finishes his work, and his word is tried and trustworthy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Who is God save the Lord, and who is a rock save our God? All others adored as deities are counterfeits, Henry says; none is to be relied on but he, for they disappoint worshippers when most needed, but the Lord lives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. God is his strength and power, and makes his way perfect. Called to fight God's battles, David was qualified for the service, and God gave him success in all his undertakings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. He makes David's feet like hinds' feet and sets him on his high places. Swiftness in charging and retreating, Henry notes, is of great advantage, and the high places denote both safety and dignity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. He teaches David's hands to war, so that a bow of steel is broken by his arms. Though bred to the crook, David was instructed in war, and his ingenuity was as good as strength, breaking steel by dexterity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. God gives the shield of salvation, and his gentleness makes David great. God's grace and tender mercy, Henry says, gave him wealth, authority, and a name like the great men of the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. God enlarges David's steps under him, so his feet do not slip. Brought into a large place, he has room to thrive and stir, no longer straitened and confined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. He pursues his enemies, destroys them, and turns not again until he has consumed them. The victories are speedy, Henry notes, and complete: the enemies are wounded, destroyed, consumed, and fall under his feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. He consumes and wounds them so they cannot arise, fallen under his feet. Disabled to rise, they lie at his mercy, trampled and spent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. God girds him with strength for battle and subdues those who rise against him. David gives God the glory of all his courage and ability for service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. God gives him the necks of his enemies, that he may destroy those who hate him. Victory is described as necks laid at the victor's mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. They look, but there is none to save, even to the Lord, but he answers them not. They cry to earth and heaven in vain, Henry says, for they are not on God's side and cry only at the last extremity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. He beats them as small as the dust of the earth, stamps them as mire in the street, and spreads them abroad. Abandoned, they become an easy prey, scattered like dust by wind and trodden by every foot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. God delivers him from the strivings of his people and keeps him to be head of the heathen; a people he knew not shall serve him. His preservations show he was reserved for something great: to rule all Israel despite the people's strivings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Strangers submit to him; as soon as they hear, they obey him. The sons of the stranger, hearing the report of his victories and God's presence with him, are possessed with fear and forced to submit, though feignedly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Strangers fade away and are afraid out of their close places. Past successes are earnests of more, Henry notes, and thus the Son of David goes on conquering and to conquer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. The Lord lives, blessed be his rock, and exalted be the God of the rock of his salvation. The living God stands against dead counterfeits that disappoint their worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. It is God who avenges David and brings down the people under him. Vengeance is God's work, not David's private revenge, and the subduing of peoples is credited to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. God brings David forth from his enemies, lifts him above those who rise against him, and delivers him from the violent man. After all his dangers, Henry says, he gained his point and was lifted as high as the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Therefore David will give thanks to the Lord among the heathen and sing praises to his name. Among the heathen he will neither be afraid nor ashamed to own his obligations to the God of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. He is the tower of salvation for his king, and shows mercy to his anointed, to David and his seed for evermore. The tower of salvation can never be scaled, battered, or undermined, Henry says, and David's hopes terminate in Christ, who alone is his seed for evermore, whose throne still continues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 45. Strangers submit to him; as soon as they hear, they obey him. The sons of the stranger, hearing the report of his victories and God's presence with him, are possessed with fear and forced to submit, though feignedly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Strangers fade away and are afraid out of their close places. Past successes are earnests of more, Henry notes, and thus the Son of David goes on conquering and to conquer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. The Lord lives, blessed be his rock, and exalted be the God of the rock of his salvation. The living God stands against dead counterfeits that disappoint their worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. It is God who avenges David and brings down the people under him. Vengeance is God's work, not David's private revenge, and the subduing of peoples is credited to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. God brings David forth from his enemies, lifts him above those who rise against him, and delivers him from the violent man. After all his dangers, Henry says, he gained his point and was lifted as high as the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Therefore David will give thanks to the Lord among the heathen and sing praises to his name. Among the heathen he will neither be afraid nor ashamed to own his obligations to the God of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. He is the tower of salvation for his king, and shows mercy to his anointed, to David and his seed for evermore. The tower of salvation can never be scaled, battered, or undermined, Henry says, and David's hopes terminate in Christ, who alone is his seed for evermore, whose throne still continues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
