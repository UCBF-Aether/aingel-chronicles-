The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, when men began to multiply on the face of the earth, and daughters were born unto them,

2 That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose.

3 And the LORD said, My spirit shall not always strive with man, for that he also is flesh: yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years.

4 There were giants in the earth in those days; and also after that, when the sons of God came in unto the daughters of men, and they bare children to them, the same became mighty men which were of old, men of renown.

5 And God saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually.

6 And it repented the LORD that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him at his heart.

7 And the LORD said, I will destroy man whom I have created from the face of the earth; both man, and beast, and the creeping thing, and the fowls of the air; for it repenteth me that I have made them.

8 But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.

9 These are the generations of Noah: Noah was a just man and perfect in his generations, and Noah walked with God.

10 And Noah begat three sons, Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

11 The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence.

12 And God looked upon the earth, and, behold, it was corrupt; for all flesh had corrupted his way upon the earth.

13 And God said unto Noah, The end of all flesh is come before me; for the earth is filled with violence through them; and, behold, I will destroy them with the earth.

14 Make thee an ark of gopher wood; rooms shalt thou make in the ark, and shalt pitch it within and without with pitch.

15 And this is the fashion which thou shalt make it of: The length of the ark shall be three hundred cubits, the breadth of it fifty cubits, and the height of it thirty cubits.

16 A window shalt thou make to the ark, and in a cubit shalt thou finish it above; and the door of the ark shalt thou set in the side thereof; with lower, second, and third stories shalt thou make it.

17 And, behold, I, even I, do bring a flood of waters upon the earth, to destroy all flesh, wherein is the breath of life, from under heaven; and every thing that is in the earth shall die.

18 But with thee will I establish my covenant; and thou shalt come into the ark, thou, and thy sons, and thy wife, and thy sons’ wives with thee.

19 And of every living thing of all flesh, two of every sort shalt thou bring into the ark, to keep them alive with thee; they shall be male and female.

20 Of fowls after their kind, and of cattle after their kind, of every creeping thing of the earth after his kind, two of every sort shall come unto thee, to keep them alive.

21 And take thou unto thee of all food that is eaten, and thou shalt gather it to thee; and it shall be for food for thee, and for them.

22 Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

As humanity multiplies, wickedness fills the earth and God grieves over what he has made. Noah alone is described as righteous and faithful, and God tells him to build an ark, giving its size and pattern, and to take his household and pairs of every living kind into it. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When men began to multiply on the earth, daughters were born to them. The chapter opens with a growing, spreading humanity, setting the scene for the corruption that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The sons of God saw the daughters of men, that they were fair, and took wives of all they chose. JFB reads the sons of God as the professedly religious line of Seth, marrying into apostate Cain's line, so that mixed marriages spread corruption through households. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God said his Spirit would not always strive with man, for he was flesh, yet his days would be 120 years. JFB reads this as a long respite: God had preached repentance through prophets like Enoch and Noah, but the people were incorrigible, and judgment was given a fixed delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Giants, men of renown, were on the earth in those days, born of these unions and present also afterward. JFB notes the Hebrew points less to great stature than to reckless, violent, impious characters who spread devastation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God saw the wickedness of man was great, and every imagination of his heart only evil continually. Henry stresses the corruption reached the heart itself, the citadel of human life, with no exception and no pause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The LORD repented that he had made man, and it grieved him at his heart. JFB explains this in human language: God does not change, but his visible dealings change from long-suffering mercy to judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God said he would destroy man, beast, creeping thing and fowl from the earth, for he repented he had made them. The judgment reaches the whole order man was set over, because man's corruption had filled the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD. JFB stresses the awful contrast: in a world of professed religion, only one family of piety remained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Noah was a just man and perfect in his generations, and walked with God. JFB clarifies perfect does not mean sinless, which no fallen man is, but sincere: living by faith and desiring to do God's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Noah fathered Shem, Ham and Japheth. The three sons are named now because the ark and the covenant will preserve this one household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The earth was corrupt before God and filled with violence. Without well-ordered government or fear of God, JFB notes, men did what was right in their own eyes and misery followed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. God looked on the earth and saw it corrupt, for all flesh had corrupted its way. The verdict is universal: corruption was not a few bad actors but the way of the whole world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. God told Noah the end of all flesh had come before him, for the earth was filled with violence, and he would destroy them with the earth. Henry notes how startling this was: nature and public opinion gave no sign of flood, yet Noah believed God's word over appearances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. God commanded an ark of gopher wood, with rooms, pitched inside and outside. JFB explains gopher as durable cypress, rooms as small cabins, and pitch as bitumen that would make the vessel watertight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The ark's measure was 300 cubits long, 50 wide and 30 high. JFB notes it was not a ship built to sail, but an immense floating house, designed only to float and hold its cargo. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Noah was to make a window, finish it to a cubit above, set a door in the side, and build lower, second and third decks. JFB reads the window as a skylight and the raised roof as a slope to shed water. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. God announced he would bring a flood of waters to destroy all flesh with the breath of life, and everything on earth would die. JFB notes the repeated I, even I, stresses certainty, and the flood is presented as God's moral judgment, not mere natural accident. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. But with Noah God would establish his covenant, and Noah, his sons, his wife and his sons' wives would enter the ark. JFB calls this a special promise of deliverance, given to make Noah confident in it before any flood appeared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Of every living thing, two of every sort, male and female, were to be brought into the ark to keep them alive. Preservation, not just Noah's family, is part of the covenant plan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Of birds, cattle and creeping things after their kind, two of every kind would come to Noah. The animals come by God's direction, sorted by kind, so the ark's company is ordered rather than random. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Noah was to take all food that is eaten and gather it, for food for himself and for them. God employs ordinary means: a long voyage needs stored provision, planned in advance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Noah did according to all God commanded him. JFB stresses he began without delay and followed the directions faithfully at every step, which is why his obedience is held up as faith in action. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • giants (Nephilim). Men of renown born of these unions; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown notes the Hebrew points less to great stature than to reckless, violent, impious characters who spread devastation. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 6:4, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
