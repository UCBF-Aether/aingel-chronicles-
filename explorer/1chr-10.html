The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the Philistines fought against Israel; and the men of Israel fled from before the Philistines, and fell down slain in mount Gilboa.

2 And the Philistines followed hard after Saul, and after his sons; and the Philistines slew Jonathan, and Abinadab, and Malchishua, the sons of Saul.

3 And the battle went sore against Saul, and the archers hit him, and he was wounded of the archers.

4 Then said Saul to his armourbearer, Draw thy sword, and thrust me through therewith; lest these uncircumcised come and abuse me. But his armourbearer would not; for he was sore afraid. So Saul took a sword, and fell upon it.

5 And when his armourbearer saw that Saul was dead, he fell likewise on the sword, and died.

6 So Saul died, and his three sons, and all his house died together.

7 And when all the men of Israel that were in the valley saw that they fled, and that Saul and his sons were dead, then they forsook their cities, and fled: and the Philistines came and dwelt in them.

8 And it came to pass on the morrow, when the Philistines came to strip the slain, that they found Saul and his sons fallen in mount Gilboa.

9 And when they had stripped him, they took his head, and his armour, and sent into the land of the Philistines round about, to carry tidings unto their idols, and to the people.

10 And they put his armour in the house of their gods, and fastened his head in the temple of Dagon.

11 And when all Jabeshgilead heard all that the Philistines had done to Saul,

12 They arose, all the valiant men, and took away the body of Saul, and the bodies of his sons, and brought them to Jabesh, and buried their bones under the oak in Jabesh, and fasted seven days.

13 So Saul died for his transgression which he committed against the LORD, even against the word of the LORD, which he kept not, and also for asking counsel of one that had a familiar spirit, to enquire of it;

14 And enquired not of the LORD: therefore he slew him, and turned the kingdom unto David the son of Jesse.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Saul's death: defeated by the Philistines on Mount Gilboa, Saul and his sons fall, his armour is displayed in Philistia, and the kingdom is turned over to David. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Philistines fought against Israel, and Israel fled and fell slain on Mount Gilboa. Henry observes princes sin and the people suffer: Israel quitted its cities and the Philistines dwelt in them, chiefly for Saul's sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The Philistines followed hard after Saul and his sons, and slew Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchi-shua. Henry notes parents sin and children suffer: Jonathan, gracious and generous, fell with his father as part of the condemned house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The battle went sore against Saul, and the archers hit and wounded him. Henry sees a fitting answer to his sin: he had thrown a javelin at David and missed, but the archers hit him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Saul told his armour-bearer to thrust him through, lest the uncircumcised abuse him, but the bearer refused in fear, so Saul fell on his own sword. Henry notes he who commanded Doeg to slay the priests is now left to be his own murderer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. When his armour-bearer saw Saul dead, he likewise fell on his sword and died. Henry treats this as the close of Saul's household falling together, sentence long reprieved at last executed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. So Saul died, and his three sons, and all his house died together. Henry stresses the visitation on the house: the measure of Saul's iniquity was full, and his day to fall had come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. When Israel in the valley saw the flight and Saul's death, they forsook their cities and fled, and the Philistines dwelt in them. Henry notes the people suffered for their prince's sin, even though Samuel had reformed them from idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. On the morrow the Philistines came to strip the slain and found Saul and his sons fallen on Gilboa. Henry draws the lesson that greater dignity brings greater danger of disgrace: Saul's body, because he was king, was abused more than any other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. They stripped him, took his head and armour, and sent tidings to their idols and people throughout Philistia. Henry mocks the idols that knew nothing till tidings were brought, and warns that if we give God no glory in success, even Philistines will rise in judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They put his armour in the house of their gods and fastened his head in Dagon's temple. Henry asks whether Dagon should have so honourable a share in their triumph while the true God is forgotten in ours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. When all Jabesh-gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul, they acted. Henry introduces their rescue as the respect due to the remains of the deceased, especially of princes, whose bodies were once united to immortal souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. All the valiant men arose, took Saul's body and his sons' bodies, brought them to Jabesh, buried their bones under the oak, and fasted seven days. Henry honours this rescue and burial as due respect, leaving the eternal state to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Saul died for his transgression against the Lord's word, which he kept not, and for asking counsel of one with a familiar spirit. Henry lists the causes: disobedience in not destroying the Amalekites, and consulting a witch, a sin that fills the measure of iniquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He enquired not of the Lord, therefore the Lord slew him and turned the kingdom to David son of Jesse. Henry reconciles this with Saul's late enquiry: he did not enquire till the last extremity, too late, and what wicked hands did was yet by God's determinate counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
