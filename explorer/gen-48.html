Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 48 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass after these things, that one told Joseph, Behold, thy father is sick: and he took with him his two sons, Manasseh and Ephraim.

2 And one told Jacob, and said, Behold, thy son Joseph cometh unto thee: and Israel strengthened himself, and sat upon the bed.

3 And Jacob said unto Joseph, God Almighty appeared unto me at Luz in the land of Canaan, and blessed me,

4 And said unto me, Behold, I will make thee fruitful, and multiply thee, and I will make of thee a multitude of people; and will give this land to thy seed after thee for an everlasting possession.

5 And now thy two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, which were born unto thee in the land of Egypt before I came unto thee into Egypt, are mine; as Reuben and Simeon, they shall be mine.

6 And thy issue, which thou begettest after them, shall be thine, and shall be called after the name of their brethren in their inheritance.

7 And as for me, when I came from Padan, Rachel died by me in the land of Canaan in the way, when yet there was but a little way to come unto Ephrath: and I buried her there in the way of Ephrath; the same is Bethlehem.

8 And Israel beheld Joseph’s sons, and said, Who are these?

9 And Joseph said unto his father, They are my sons, whom God hath given me in this place. And he said, Bring them, I pray thee, unto me, and I will bless them.

10 Now the eyes of Israel were dim for age, so that he could not see. And he brought them near unto him; and he kissed them, and embraced them.

11 And Israel said unto Joseph, I had not thought to see thy face: and, lo, God hath shewed me also thy seed.

12 And Joseph brought them out from between his knees, and he bowed himself with his face to the earth.

13 And Joseph took them both, Ephraim in his right hand toward Israel’s left hand, and Manasseh in his left hand toward Israel’s right hand, and brought them near unto him.

14 And Israel stretched out his right hand, and laid it upon Ephraim’s head, who was the younger, and his left hand upon Manasseh’s head, guiding his hands wittingly; for Manasseh was the firstborn.

15 And he blessed Joseph, and said, God, before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac did walk, the God which fed me all my life long unto this day,

16 The Angel which redeemed me from all evil, bless the lads; and let my name be named on them, and the name of my fathers Abraham and Isaac; and let them grow into a multitude in the midst of the earth.

17 And when Joseph saw that his father laid his right hand upon the head of Ephraim, it displeased him: and he held up his father’s hand, to remove it from Ephraim’s head unto Manasseh’s head.

18 And Joseph said unto his father, Not so, my father: for this is the firstborn; put thy right hand upon his head.

19 And his father refused, and said, I know it, my son, I know it: he also shall become a people, and he also shall be great: but truly his younger brother shall be greater than he, and his seed shall become a multitude of nations.

20 And he blessed them that day, saying, In thee shall Israel bless, saying, God make thee as Ephraim and as Manasseh: and he set Ephraim before Manasseh.

21 And Israel said unto Joseph, Behold, I die: but God shall be with you, and bring you again unto the land of your fathers.

22 Moreover I have given to thee one portion above thy brethren, which I took out of the hand of the Amorite with my sword and with my bow.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

When Jacob is sick, Joseph brings Ephraim and Manasseh, and Jacob adopts them as his own sons. Blessing them with crossed hands, he deliberately sets the younger Ephraim before the elder Manasseh, promises that God will bring the family back to Canaan, and gives Joseph one portion above his brothers. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Joseph is told his father is sick and takes his two sons Manasseh and Ephraim with him, so they may receive their dying grandfather's blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Jacob is told Joseph is coming, and Israel strengthens himself and sits up on the bed, gathering himself for a solemn act of blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Jacob recalls that God Almighty appeared to him at Luz in Canaan and blessed him, returning to a landmark of his history to engage Joseph's interest in the people of God rather than Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He repeats the covenant promise, to be made fruitful and multiplied into a multitude, with the land given to his seed as an everlasting possession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Joseph's two sons, born in Egypt before Jacob came, are now claimed as Jacob's own, as Reuben and Simeon, so Joseph receives a double portion by adoption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Later children of Joseph will be his own and called by their brothers' names in their inheritance, a rule that settles future genealogies and explains why only certain names appear in the lists. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Jacob recalls Rachel's death and roadside burial at Ephrath as he adopts Joseph's sons. Henry notes the dying patriarch calls his losses to mind to make death and the grave familiar, and that strong affection in enjoyment brings long affliction in loss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Israel sees Joseph's sons and asks who they are, a question from dim eyes and from a father formally presenting the boys for blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Joseph answers that they are his sons, whom God has given him in Egypt, and Jacob asks that they be brought near so he may bless them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Israel's eyes are dim with age so he cannot see clearly, yet Joseph brings the boys near, and Jacob kisses and embraces them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Israel says he had not thought to see Joseph's face again, and God has shown him also Joseph's offspring, a double restoration beyond his hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Joseph brings the boys from beside his knees and bows with his face to the earth, in reverence before the blessing his father is about to give. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Joseph places Ephraim toward Israel's left hand and Manasseh toward his right, arranging them so the firstborn would receive the right-hand blessing by custom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Israel stretches out his right hand to Ephraim the younger and his left to Manasseh, guiding his hands wittingly, literally making his hands wise, and crossing them on purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He blesses Joseph, invoking the God before whom Abraham and Isaac walked, the God who has fed Jacob all his life long, so the blessing rests on a lifetime of provision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He calls on the Angel who redeemed him from all evil to bless the lads, an Angel Jacob himself identifies with God Almighty, whose name is repeated three times in the blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Joseph is displeased when he sees the right hand on Ephraim, and tries to lift his father's hand to Manasseh's head, thinking dim eyes have caused a mistake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Joseph explains to his father that Manasseh is the firstborn and asks him to put his right hand on his head, appealing to birth order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Jacob refuses, saying he knows it, Manasseh also shall become a people and be great, but his younger brother shall be greater, and his seed a multitude of nations, a prediction later verified in Ephraim's might. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He blesses them that day with the formula, God make thee as Ephraim and as Manasseh, so their prosperity becomes the proverbial wish for others, and he sets Ephraim before Manasseh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Israel tells Joseph he is dying, but God will be with them and bring them back to the land of their fathers, speaking of death with composure and of restoration with certainty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. He gives Joseph one portion above his brothers, a ridge of land near Shechem, which JFB thinks Jacob recovered from the Amorites by sword and bow and later became Joseph's burial place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
