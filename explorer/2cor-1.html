The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Corinthians 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, and Timothy our brother, unto the church of God which is at Corinth, with all the saints which are in all Achaia:

2 Grace be to you and peace from God our Father, and from the Lord Jesus Christ.

3 Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort;

4 Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

5 For as the sufferings of Christ abound in us, so our consolation also aboundeth by Christ.

6 And whether we be afflicted, it is for your consolation and salvation, which is effectual in the enduring of the same sufferings which we also suffer: or whether we be comforted, it is for your consolation and salvation.

7 And our hope of you is stedfast, knowing, that as ye are partakers of the sufferings, so shall ye be also of the consolation.

8 For we would not, brethren, have you ignorant of our trouble which came to us in Asia, that we were pressed out of measure, above strength, insomuch that we despaired even of life:

9 But we had the sentence of death in ourselves, that we should not trust in ourselves, but in God which raiseth the dead:

10 Who delivered us from so great a death, and doth deliver: in whom we trust that he will yet deliver us;

11 Ye also helping together by prayer for us, that for the gift bestowed upon us by the means of many persons thanks may be given by many on our behalf.

12 For our rejoicing is this, the testimony of our conscience, that in simplicity and godly sincerity, not with fleshly wisdom, but by the grace of God, we have had our conversation in the world, and more abundantly to you-ward.

13 For we write none other things unto you, than what ye read or acknowledge; and I trust ye shall acknowledge even to the end;

14 As also ye have acknowledged us in part, that we are your rejoicing, even as ye also are ours in the day of the Lord Jesus.

15 And in this confidence I was minded to come unto you before, that ye might have a second benefit;

16 And to pass by you into Macedonia, and to come again out of Macedonia unto you, and of you to be brought on my way toward Judaea.

17 When I therefore was thus minded, did I use lightness? or the things that I purpose, do I purpose according to the flesh, that with me there should be yea yea, and nay nay?

18 But as God is true, our word toward you was not yea and nay.

19 For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, who was preached among you by us, even by me and Silvanus and Timotheus, was not yea and nay, but in him was yea.

20 For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us.

21 Now he which stablisheth us with you in Christ, and hath anointed us, is God;

22 Who hath also sealed us, and given the earnest of the Spirit in our hearts.

23 Moreover I call God for a record upon my soul, that to spare you I came not as yet unto Corinth.

24 Not for that we have dominion over your faith, but are helpers of your joy: for by faith ye stand.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul blesses the God of all comfort, who comforts believers in tribulation so they can comfort others, recalling his crushing trouble in Asia, when he despaired even of life and learned to trust God who raises the dead. He defends his change of travel plans as honest, not fickle, grounded in God's faithful Yes in Christ, and says he spared Corinth a painful visit, acting as a helper of their joy rather than having dominion over their faith. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, and Timothy our brother, write to the church of God at Corinth, with all the saints in all Achaia. Henry notes the authority is not Paul's own choosing but God's will, and the address widens from one church to a whole province. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Grace be to you and peace from God our Father, and from the Lord Jesus Christ. JFB notes the greeting joins the two gifts the whole epistle will unfold: grace as the source, peace as the fruit, from Father and Son together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort. Henry notes Paul blesses God before he mentions his trouble, and the titles name God as the fountain of both mercy and comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Who comforts us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God. JFB notes comfort is not kept: it is received in order to be passed on, and experience of it qualifies the comforter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For as the sufferings of Christ abound in us, so our consolation also abounds by Christ. Henry notes the proportion: suffering and consolation rise together, and both come through Christ, so neither is borne alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And whether we be afflicted, it is for your consolation and salvation, which is effectual in enduring the same sufferings which we also suffer: or whether we be comforted, it is for your consolation and salvation. JFB notes both Paul's affliction and his comfort serve Corinth: his endurance teaches theirs, and his comfort encourages theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And our hope of you is steadfast, knowing, that as ye are partakers of the sufferings, so shall ye be also of the consolation. Henry notes the hope rests on a partnership: sharers in suffering will be sharers in comfort, by the same rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For we would not have you ignorant of our trouble in Asia, that we were pressed out of measure, above strength, insomuch that we despaired even of life. JFB notes Paul tells Corinth of a trouble that passed human strength, so that the deliverance that follows is plainly God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. But we had the sentence of death in ourselves, that we should not trust in ourselves, but in God which raises the dead. Henry notes the sentence of death was the cure for self-trust: despair of life drove Paul to the God who raises the dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Who delivered us from so great a death, and does deliver: in whom we trust that he will yet deliver us. JFB notes three tenses of deliverance: past, present and future, all resting on the same God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Ye also helping together by prayer for us, that for the gift bestowed upon us by the means of many persons thanks may be given by many on our behalf. Henry notes Corinth's prayers are part of the deliverance, and many prayers will mean many thanksgivings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. For our rejoicing is this, the testimony of our conscience, that in simplicity and godly sincerity, not with fleshly wisdom, but by the grace of God, we have had our conversation in the world, and more abundantly to you-ward. JFB notes Paul's boast is his conscience: a life lived in sincerity by grace, not by worldly craft, especially toward Corinth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. For we write none other things unto you, than what ye read or acknowledge; and I trust ye shall acknowledge even to the end. Henry notes Paul writes plainly: there is no hidden meaning behind his words for Corinth to suspect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. As also ye have acknowledged us in part, that we are your rejoicing, even as ye also are ours in the day of the Lord Jesus. JFB notes the mutual rejoicing is deferred to the Lord's day, when minister and people will rejoice in each other before Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And in this confidence I was minded to come unto you before, that ye might have a second benefit. Henry notes the planned first visit was meant as a double blessing: coming and going through Corinth on the way to Macedonia and back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. And to pass by you into Macedonia, and to come again out of Macedonia unto you, and of you to be brought on my way toward Judaea. JFB notes the itinerary shows Paul intended Corinth as his base twice over, not as a place to avoid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. When I therefore was thus minded, did I use lightness? or the things that I purpose, do I purpose according to the flesh, that with me there should be yea yea, and nay nay? Henry notes Paul meets the charge of fickleness head-on: changing a plan is not the same as breaking a promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. But as God is true, our word toward you was not yea and nay. JFB notes Paul appeals to God's truthfulness for his own: his preaching, like God's character, does not contradict itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, who was preached among you by us, even by me and Silvanus and Timotheus, was not yea and nay, but in him was yea. Henry notes the preached Christ is the proof: a gospel that is 'yea' in Christ cannot come from a double-tongued apostle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us. JFB notes every promise finds its yes in Christ and its amen in the church's assent, to God's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Now he which establishes us with you in Christ, and has anointed us, is God. Henry notes establishment and anointing are God's acts, covering both apostle and people in the same Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Who has also sealed us, and given the earnest of the Spirit in our hearts. JFB notes seal and earnest together: the Spirit marks believers as God's own and gives a first instalment of the inheritance to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Moreover I call God for a record upon my soul, that to spare you I came not as yet unto Corinth. Henry notes the real reason for the delayed visit is mercy: Paul stayed away to spare Corinth a painful visit of discipline. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Not for that we have dominion over your faith, but are helpers of your joy: for by faith ye stand. JFB notes apostolic authority is bounded: Paul helps joy and serves faith, he does not lord over it, for faith stands by itself before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • Asia. The Roman province where Paul's trouble was so heavy he despaired of life itself. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • sentence of death. Paul's phrase for his conviction in Asia that death was certain, driving him to trust God who raises the dead. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • earnest of the Spirit. The Spirit given as a down payment or pledge, sealing believers for what is to come. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
