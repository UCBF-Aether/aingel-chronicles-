The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ezekiel 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Thou also, son of man, take thee a tile, and lay it before thee, and pourtray upon it the city, even Jerusalem:

2 And lay siege against it, and build a fort against it, and cast a mount against it; set the camp also against it, and set battering rams against it round about.

3 Moreover take thou unto thee an iron pan, and set it for a wall of iron between thee and the city: and set thy face against it, and it shall be besieged, and thou shalt lay siege against it. This shall be a sign to the house of Israel.

4 Lie thou also upon thy left side, and lay the iniquity of the house of Israel upon it: according to the number of the days that thou shalt lie upon it thou shalt bear their iniquity.

5 For I have laid upon thee the years of their iniquity, according to the number of the days, three hundred and ninety days: so shalt thou bear the iniquity of the house of Israel.

6 And when thou hast accomplished them, lie again on thy right side, and thou shalt bear the iniquity of the house of Judah forty days: I have appointed thee each day for a year.

7 Therefore thou shalt set thy face toward the siege of Jerusalem, and thine arm shall be uncovered, and thou shalt prophesy against it.

8 And, behold, I will lay bands upon thee, and thou shalt not turn thee from one side to another, till thou hast ended the days of thy siege.

9 Take thou also unto thee wheat, and barley, and beans, and lentiles, and millet, and fitches, and put them in one vessel, and make thee bread thereof, according to the number of the days that thou shalt lie upon thy side, three hundred and ninety days shalt thou eat thereof.

10 And thy meat which thou shalt eat shall be by weight, twenty shekels a day: from time to time shalt thou eat it.

11 Thou shalt drink also water by measure, the sixth part of an hin: from time to time shalt thou drink.

12 And thou shalt eat it as barley cakes, and thou shalt bake it with dung that cometh out of man, in their sight.

13 And the LORD said, Even thus shall the children of Israel eat their defiled bread among the Gentiles, whither I will drive them.

14 Then said I, Ah Lord GOD! behold, my soul hath not been polluted: for from my youth up even till now have I not eaten of that which dieth of itself, or is torn in pieces; neither came there abominable flesh into my mouth.

15 Then he said unto me, Lo, I have given thee cow’s dung for man’s dung, and thou shalt prepare thy bread therewith.

16 Moreover he said unto me, Son of man, behold, I will break the staff of bread in Jerusalem: and they shall eat bread by weight, and with care; and they shall drink water by measure, and with astonishment:

17 That they may want bread and water, and be astonied one with another, and consume away for their iniquity.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ezekiel acts out the coming siege of Jerusalem in signs: he draws the city on a tile and lays siege works around it, sets an iron pan as a wall between himself and the city, lies on his left side for Israel’s years of iniquity and on his right side for Judah’s, and eats rationed, defiled bread. Each acted sign pictures famine, siege, and exile before they happen. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 4. Lying on his left side, he bears the iniquity of the house of Israel, the sign-act embodying their guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Three hundred and ninety days answer the years of Israel's iniquity, a day for a year of idolatrous rebellion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Forty days on his right side then bear Judah's iniquity, each day appointed for a year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. With face set toward besieged Jerusalem and arm uncovered, he prophesies against it openly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Bands hold him from turning side to side until the days of his siege are ended, so the sign cannot be softened or cut short. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Bread of wheat, barley, beans, lentils, millet, and fitches mixed in one vessel figures coarse scarcity fare for 390 days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Twenty shekels of food a day, eaten from time to time, pictures rationed eating in a besieged city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Water by measure, a sixth of a hin, pictures rationed drinking alongside rationed bread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Barley cakes baked with human dung in their sight picture the defilement awaiting Israel in exile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. So shall Israel eat defiled bread among the Gentiles where God will drive them, their plenty abused to luxury punished by want. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Ezekiel pleads not delicacy but conscience: from youth he has never eaten what dies of itself, is torn, or is abominable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. God relents in the sign itself, giving cow's dung for man's dung, so the prophet's obedience need not break the law he kept. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. God will break the staff of bread in Jerusalem, so bread is eaten by weight with care and water by measure with astonishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Wanting bread and water, astonied at one another, they consume away for their iniquity, a woeful work sin makes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
