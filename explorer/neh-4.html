Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Nehemiah 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 But it came to pass, that when Sanballat heard that we builded the wall, he was wroth, and took great indignation, and mocked the Jews.

2 And he spake before his brethren and the army of Samaria, and said, What do these feeble Jews? will they fortify themselves? will they sacrifice? will they make an end in a day? will they revive the stones out of the heaps of the rubbish which are burned?

3 Now Tobiah the Ammonite was by him, and he said, Even that which they build, if a fox go up, he shall even break down their stone wall.

4 Hear, O our God; for we are despised: and turn their reproach upon their own head, and give them for a prey in the land of captivity:

5 And cover not their iniquity, and let not their sin be blotted out from before thee: for they have provoked thee to anger before the builders.

6 So built we the wall; and all the wall was joined together unto the half thereof: for the people had a mind to work.

7 But it came to pass, that when Sanballat, and Tobiah, and the Arabians, and the Ammonites, and the Ashdodites, heard that the walls of Jerusalem were made up, and that the breaches began to be stopped, then they were very wroth,

8 And conspired all of them together to come and to fight against Jerusalem, and to hinder it.

9 Nevertheless we made our prayer unto our God, and set a watch against them day and night, because of them.

10 And Judah said, The strength of the bearers of burdens is decayed, and there is much rubbish; so that we are not able to build the wall.

11 And our adversaries said, They shall not know, neither see, till we come in the midst among them, and slay them, and cause the work to cease.

12 And it came to pass, that when the Jews which dwelt by them came, they said unto us ten times, From all places whence ye shall return unto us they will be upon you.

13 Therefore set I in the lower places behind the wall, and on the higher places, I even set the people after their families with their swords, their spears, and their bows.

14 And I looked, and rose up, and said unto the nobles, and to the rulers, and to the rest of the people, Be not ye afraid of them: remember the LORD, which is great and terrible, and fight for your brethren, your sons, and your daughters, your wives, and your houses.

15 And it came to pass, when our enemies heard that it was known unto us, and God had brought their counsel to nought, that we returned all of us to the wall, every one unto his work.

16 And it came to pass from that time forth, that the half of my servants wrought in the work, and the other half of them held both the spears, the shields, and the bows, and the habergeons; and the rulers were behind all the house of Judah.

17 They which builded on the wall, and they that bare burdens, with those that laded, every one with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other hand held a weapon.

18 For the builders, every one had his sword girded by his side, and so builded. And he that sounded the trumpet was by me.

19 And I said unto the nobles, and to the rulers, and to the rest of the people, The work is great and large, and we are separated upon the wall, one far from another.

20 In what place therefore ye hear the sound of the trumpet, resort ye thither unto us: our God shall fight for us.

21 So we laboured in the work: and half of them held the spears from the rising of the morning till the stars appeared.

22 Likewise at the same time said I unto the people, Let every one with his servant lodge within Jerusalem, that in the night they may be a guard to us, and labour on the day.

23 So neither I, nor my brethren, nor my servants, nor the men of the guard which followed me, none of us put off our clothes, saving that every one put them off for washing.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Sanballat's mockery turns to conspiracy: the enemies plot to fight Jerusalem and hinder the work. Nehemiah answers with prayer and a guard, then arms the builders themselves: half work while half hold spears, shields, bows, and coats of mail, a trumpet ready to rally them, and the people stay on the walls day and night, not even putting off their clothes except for washing. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Habergeon. a coat of mail, part of the armour carried by half the builders while the other half worked (verse 16). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite, Geshem the Arabian. the named opponents of the rebuilding, from neighbouring peoples around Judah. Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When Sanballat heard the wall was being built, he was very angry and greatly indignant, mocking the Jews. Henry notes the church's strength and safety are the grief of its enemies, who had hoped an unwalled Jerusalem could be swallowed up at pleasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Sanballat scorned the workmen before his brethren and Samaria's army: "these feeble Jews" — would they revive stones from rubbish heaps, offer sacrifice, and finish in a day? Henry observes the mockery backfired, since enemies who despised the work as foolish did not obstruct it until it was too late. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Tobiah added his jest: even if a fox went up on their stone wall, he would break it down. Henry notes profane scoffers sharpen one another, and many a good work has been looked on with contempt by proud scorners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Instead of answering folly with folly, Nehemiah prayed: "Hear, O our God; for we are despised," asking God to turn their reproach on their own heads and give them as a prey in captivity. Henry notes God's people may appeal to a God who hears every slight put upon them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He further prayed that their iniquity not be covered nor their sin blotted out, since they had provoked God before the builders. Henry cautions that this was spoken by a spirit of prophecy rather than as a pattern for us, who are taught by Christ to pray for persecutors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Despite the scoffs, the wall was joined to half its height, because the people had a mind to work. Henry's lesson: good work goes on well when people have a mind to it, and enemies' reproaches should quicken us to duty rather than drive us from it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. When Sanballat, Tobiah, the Arabians, Ammonites, and Ashdodites heard the breaches were being stopped, they were very wroth. Henry notes they were angry both at the Jews for pushing the work and at themselves for being slow to oppose it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. They conspired together to come and fight against Jerusalem and hinder the work. Henry stresses their unanimity, secrecy, and cruelty: men of different interests became one in opposing God's work, planning murder to make the building cease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Nehemiah's answer was prayer joined with watchfulness: "we made our prayer unto our God, and set a watch against them day and night." Henry's rule is watch and pray together: prayer without watchfulness is slothful, watchfulness without prayer is proud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Judah said the bearers' strength was decayed, there was much rubbish, and they were not able to build the wall. Henry notes that active leaders often have as much trouble grappling with their friends' fears as with their enemies' terrors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The adversaries plotted in secret: the builders would neither know nor see until their enemies came among them, slew them, and caused the work to cease. Henry observes they promised themselves success only through surprise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Jews dwelling near the enemies came and warned Nehemiah ten times over, from every side, that attack was coming. Henry reads the repeated, breathless report as God's way of bringing hidden devices to light, even through feeble neighbours who served as spies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Nehemiah therefore set the people in the lower places behind the wall and in higher places upon it, arranged by families, with swords, spears, and bows. Henry notes the family order: mutual relation would engage them to mutual assistance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Seeing the nobles and people in consternation, he urged them not to be afraid but to remember the Lord, great and terrible, and to fight for their brethren, sons, daughters, wives, and houses. Henry's point: the reigning fear of God is the best antidote to the ensnaring fear of man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. When the enemies heard their design was known, God brought their counsel to nought, and everyone returned to his work. Henry notes they returned with more cheerfulness, seeing plainly that God owned them in the doing of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Still, half the servants worked while the other half held spears, shields, bows, and coats of mail, with rulers behind the house of Judah. Henry notes they kept their arms ready because the enemy was restless, and one failed design would be followed by another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The builders worked with one hand and held a weapon with the other, and the burden-bearers likewise laboured laden. Henry explains this cannot be literal, since work needs both hands; it means they were equally employed in building and in readiness to fight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Every builder had his sword girded by his side as he built, and the trumpeter stood beside Nehemiah. Henry compares the sword to the word of God, the sword of the Spirit, which Christians should keep always at hand in labour and in conflict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Nehemiah told the nobles, rulers, and people that the work was great and large, with builders separated far apart upon the wall. Henry notes that as workmen they had to be dispersed, but as soldiers they had to come together in close order at the alarm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Wherever the trumpet sounded, they were to resort to Nehemiah there, for their God would fight for them. Henry stresses the assurance given with the alarm: unity against a common foe, under a God who fights for his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. So they laboured from morning until the stars appeared, half of them holding spears. Henry notes the spears were held up day and night, from twilight to twilight, as a terror to the enemy and a sign of constant guard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Nehemiah ordered the people and their servants to lodge within Jerusalem, so that at night they could guard and by day labour. Henry's maxim: a city's strength lies more in its hands than in its walls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Neither Nehemiah, his brethren, his servants, nor the guard put off their clothes, except for washing. Henry reads this as total intentness: they could not find time to dress and undress, resolving to be at all times ready for service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
