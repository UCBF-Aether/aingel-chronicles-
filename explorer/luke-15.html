The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then drew near unto him all the publicans and sinners for to hear him.

2 And the Pharisees and scribes murmured, saying, This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them.

3 And he spake this parable unto them, saying,

4 What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it?

5 And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing.

6 And when he cometh home, he calleth together his friends and neighbours, saying unto them, Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost.

7 I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.

8 Either what woman having ten pieces of silver, if she lose one piece, doth not light a candle, and sweep the house, and seek diligently till she find it?

9 And when she hath found it, she calleth her friends and her neighbours together, saying, Rejoice with me; for I have found the piece which I had lost.

10 Likewise, I say unto you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner that repenteth.

11 And he said, A certain man had two sons:

12 And the younger of them said to his father, Father, give me the portion of goods that falleth to me. And he divided unto them his living.

13 And not many days after the younger son gathered all together, and took his journey into a far country, and there wasted his substance with riotous living.

14 And when he had spent all, there arose a mighty famine in that land; and he began to be in want.

15 And he went and joined himself to a citizen of that country; and he sent him into his fields to feed swine.

16 And he would fain have filled his belly with the husks that the swine did eat: and no man gave unto him.

17 And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father’s have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger!

18 I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee,

19 And am no more worthy to be called thy son: make me as one of thy hired servants.

20 And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.

21 And the son said unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and in thy sight, and am no more worthy to be called thy son.

22 But the father said to his servants, Bring forth the best robe, and put it on him; and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet:

23 And bring hither the fatted calf, and kill it; and let us eat, and be merry:

24 For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found. And they began to be merry.

25 Now his elder son was in the field: and as he came and drew nigh to the house, he heard musick and dancing.

26 And he called one of the servants, and asked what these things meant.

27 And he said unto him, Thy brother is come; and thy father hath killed the fatted calf, because he hath received him safe and sound.

28 And he was angry, and would not go in: therefore came his father out, and intreated him.

29 And he answering said to his father, Lo, these many years do I serve thee, neither transgressed I at any time thy commandment: and yet thou never gavest me a kid, that I might make merry with my friends:

30 But as soon as this thy son was come, which hath devoured thy living with harlots, thou hast killed for him the fatted calf.

31 And he said unto him, Son, thou art ever with me, and all that I have is thine.

32 It was meet that we should make merry, and be glad: for this thy brother was dead, and is alive again; and was lost, and is found.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Because Jesus receives publicans and sinners and eats with them, Pharisees and scribes murmur, and he answers with three parables of lost things found (vv. 1–2). A shepherd leaves ninety-nine sheep to find one; a woman sweeps her house for one lost coin; and a father runs to a returning prodigal son, kills the fatted calf, and pleads with the elder brother who will not come in (vv. 3–32). In each case heaven’s joy over one sinner who repents is set against the grumbling of those who never left home. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 15

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then drew near unto him all the publicans and sinners for to hear him. The audience itself is the occasion: outcasts draw near to hear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The Pharisees and scribes murmured, This man receives sinners, and eats with them. Murmuring at reception becomes the target of three parables. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And he spoke this parable unto them, saying. The parables answer the murmur directly, not in general. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. What man of you, having a hundred sheep, if he lose one, does not leave the ninety-nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it? Henry notes the lost sheep represents the sinner departed from God, exposed to certain ruin if not brought back, yet not desirous to return, and Christ is earnest in bringing sinners home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And when he has found it, he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing. Finding is not scolding; it is carrying, rejoicing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. When he comes home, he calls friends and neighbours, saying, Rejoice with me, for I have found my sheep which was lost. Joy is shared, not private. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repents, more than over ninety-nine just persons which need no repentance. Heaven's joy over one repentant outweighs complacency over the ninety-nine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Either what woman having ten pieces of silver, if she lose one, does not light a candle, sweep the house, and seek diligently till she find it? Henry notes the lost piece is of small value compared with the rest, yet the woman seeks diligently, representing the various means God uses to bring lost souls home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And when she has found it, she calls her friends and neighbours, saying, Rejoice with me, for I have found the piece which I had lost. The second parable repeats the pattern: diligence, finding, shared rejoicing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Likewise, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner that repents. Henry urges care that our repentance be unto salvation, in view of the Saviour's joy on return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And he said, A certain man had two sons. The third parable has two sons, because the murmurers are the second son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The younger said, Father, give me the portion of goods that falls to me. And he divided unto them his living. Henry notes it is bad, and the beginning of worse, when men look on God's gifts as debts due to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Not many days after, the younger gathered all, took his journey into a far country, and there wasted his substance with riotous living. Henry notes a sinful state is departure and distance from God, and a spending state: wilful sinners misemploy thoughts, powers, time, and opportunities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. When he had spent all, there arose a mighty famine in that land, and he began to be in want. Spending ends in want, and famine arrives after the money is gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. He went and joined himself to a citizen of that country, who sent him into his fields to feed swine. Henry notes a sinful state is vile and slavish: the devil's servants make provision for the flesh, no better than feeding swine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He would fain have filled his belly with the husks the swine did eat, and no man gave unto him. Henry notes a sinful state is wanting: sinners want necessaries for souls, neither food nor raiment nor provision hereafter, and in vain cry to world and flesh, which poison but cannot feed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. When he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father's have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! Henry notes recovery begins by coming to himself, the turning point of conversion, when the Lord opens eyes and convinces of sin, and the meanest servant of God appears happier than he. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. I will arise and go to my father, and will say, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee. The resolve names sin against heaven first, then before the father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And am no more worthy to be called thy son: make me as one of thy hired servants. Unworthiness is confessed, and servanthood, not sonship, is requested. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. He arose and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, had compassion, ran, fell on his neck, and kissed him. Henry notes the Lord meets him with unexpected tokens of forgiving love, before the speech is finished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And the son said, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and in thy sight, and am no more worthy to be called thy son. The prepared speech is spoken, but the hired-servant request is now unnecessary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. But the father said to his servants, Bring forth the best robe, and put it on him, and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet. Henry notes the humbled sinner is clothed in the robe of the Redeemer's righteousness, made partaker of the Spirit of adoption, prepared by peace and gospel grace to walk in holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And bring hither the fatted calf, and kill it, and let us eat, and be merry. Feasting replaces husks, and merriment replaces want. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found. And they began to be merry. Henry notes the parable fully sets forth gospel grace, of unspeakable use to poor sinners to direct and encourage them in repenting and returning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Now his elder son was in the field, and as he came near the house, he heard music and dancing. The elder son, who stayed, hears the celebration from outside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. He called one of the servants, and asked what these things meant. Inquiry, not entry, is his first response. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And he said, Thy brother is come, and thy father has killed the fatted calf, because he has received him safe and sound. The servant's plain report states the reason: safe and sound return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And he was angry, and would not go in; therefore came his father out, and entreated him. Henry notes the latter part sets forth the Pharisees' character, and the Lord's kindness, and the proud manner in which gracious kindness is often received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Lo, these many years do I serve thee, neither transgressed I at any time thy commandment, and yet thou never gavest me a kid, that I might make merry with my friends. Years of service are tallied against a kid never given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. But as soon as this thy son was come, which has devoured thy living with harlots, thou hast killed for him the fatted calf. The elder son renames his brother 'this thy son,' distancing himself from both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And he said, Son, thou art ever with me, and all that I have is thine. The father's answer corrects the accounting: presence and possession were never withheld. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. It was meet that we should make merry, and be glad, for this thy brother was dead, and is alive again, and was lost, and is found. Henry notes God's mercy shines almost as bright in tender bearing with peevish saints as in receiving prodigals, and it is the children's happiness, keeping close to the Father's house, to be ever with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
