The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And God spake all these words, saying,

2 I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.

3 Thou shalt have no other gods before me.

4 Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth.

5 Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them: for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me;

6 And shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments.

7 Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain.

8 Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy.

9 Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work:

10 But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates:

11 For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it.

12 Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee.

13 Thou shalt not kill.

14 Thou shalt not commit adultery.

15 Thou shalt not steal.

16 Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour.

17 Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour’s house, thou shalt not covet thy neighbour’s wife, nor his manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is thy neighbour’s.

18 And all the people saw the thunderings, and the lightnings, and the noise of the trumpet, and the mountain smoking: and when the people saw it, they removed, and stood afar off.

19 And they said unto Moses, Speak thou with us, and we will hear: but let not God speak with us, lest we die.

20 And Moses said unto the people, Fear not: for God is come to prove you, and that his fear may be before your faces, that ye sin not.

21 And the people stood afar off, and Moses drew near unto the thick darkness where God was.

22 And the LORD said unto Moses, Thus thou shalt say unto the children of Israel, Ye have seen that I have talked with you from heaven.

23 Ye shall not make with me gods of silver, neither shall ye make unto you gods of gold.

24 An altar of earth thou shalt make unto me, and shalt sacrifice thereon thy burnt offerings, and thy peace offerings, thy sheep, and thine oxen: in all places where I record my name I will come unto thee, and I will bless thee.

25 And if thou wilt make me an altar of stone, thou shalt not build it of hewn stone: for if thou lift up thy tool upon it, thou hast polluted it.

26 Neither shalt thou go up by steps unto mine altar, that thy nakedness be not discovered thereon.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

From the mountain God speaks the Ten Commandments: no other gods, no graven images, no taking his name in vain, keep the Sabbath, honour father and mother, and no killing, adultery, stealing, false witness, or coveting. The people tremble at the thunder and ask Moses to speak to them instead, and the chapter closes with the law of the altar of earth or unhewn stone. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And God spake all these words, saying. Henry observes God speaks many ways to men, by conscience, by providences, by his voice, but he never spake at any time as he spake the Ten Commandments, for this law had been written in man's heart and sin had defaced it, so that its knowledge must be revived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage. Henry notes the preface grounds the commands in deliverance: the God who redeemed claims obedience, and his law is spiritual, taking knowledge of secret thoughts and desires. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Thou shalt have no other gods before me. Henry notes the first commandment concerns the object of worship, Jehovah and him only, and whatever comes short of perfect love, gratitude, reverence, or worship breaks it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of anything in heaven above, or in the earth beneath, or in the water under the earth. Henry notes it is forbidden to make any image or picture of the Deity in any form or for any purpose, or to worship any creature, image, or picture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them, for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me. Henry observes the spiritual import extends further: all superstition and all mere human inventions in worship are forbidden, and the jealousy of God guards his sole honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And showing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments. Henry notes the mercy outruns the judgment: iniquity is visited to the third and fourth generation, but mercy runs to thousands who love God and keep his commands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain, for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain. Henry notes the third commandment concerns the manner of worship, with all possible reverence: all false oaths, all light appealing to God, and all profane cursing are a horrid breach of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy. Henry notes the form, Remember, shows the command was not now first given but was known before, and one day in seven is to be kept holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work. Henry observes six days are allotted to worldly business, but not so as to neglect the service of God and the care of our souls, and on those days we must do all our work and leave none for the sabbath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God, in which thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates. Henry notes Christ allowed works of necessity, charity, and piety, for the sabbath was made for man, but all works of luxury, vanity, or self-indulgence are forbidden, and sloth is a carnal, not a holy, rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day, wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day and hallowed it. Henry observes the day is blessed and men are blessed by it and in it, and the blessing and direction to keep holy are spoken of the sabbath day itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Honour thy father and thy mother, that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee. Henry notes the fifth commandment concerns duties to relations: esteem shown in conduct, obedience to lawful commands, submission to counsels and corrections, and care to comfort parents and make their old age easy, and observers have noted a peculiar blessing in temporal things on obedient children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Thou shalt not kill. Henry notes the sixth commandment requires regarding the life and safety of others as our own, and much which is not deemed murder by the laws of man is such before God, including envy, malice, hatred, and provoking language, while it requires a spirit of kindness, longsuffering, and forgiveness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Thou shalt not commit adultery. Henry notes the seventh commandment concerns chastity, and we should be as much afraid of that which defiles the body as of that which destroys it, for whatever pollutes the imagination or raises the passions falls under this law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Thou shalt not steal. Henry notes the eighth commandment is the law of love as it respects property, requiring industry, frugality, and content, and forbidding fraud, evasion of just debts, and squeezing the poor in their wages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour. Henry notes the ninth commandment forbids speaking falsely on any matter, lying, equivocating, slandering, backbiting, and tale-bearing, and endeavouring to raise our reputation upon the ruin of our neighbour's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house, wife, manservant, maidservant, ox, ass, nor anything that is thy neighbour's. Henry notes the tenth commandment strikes at the root: the others forbid the desire of doing injury, and this forbids all wrong desire of having what will gratify ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. All the people saw the thunderings, and the lightnings, and the noise of the trumpet, and the mountain smoking, and when they saw it, they removed and stood afar off. Henry observes this law is so extensive we cannot measure it, so spiritual we cannot evade it, and so reasonable we cannot find fault with it, and it will be the rule of the future judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. They said unto Moses, Speak thou with us, and we will hear, but let not God speak with us, lest we die. Henry notes the people's fear was well placed: with this holy law and an awful judgment before us, who can despise the gospel of Christ, and the knowledge of the law shows our need of repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Moses said unto the people, Fear not, for God is come to prove you, and that his fear may be before your faces, that ye sin not. Henry observes the terror had a purpose: to prove them and to plant God's fear before them as a guard against sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And the people stood afar off, and Moses drew near unto the thick darkness where God was. Henry notes the contrast: the people stood afar, while the mediator drew near into the darkness, the pattern of all mediation between God and sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The LORD said unto Moses, Thus thou shalt say unto the children of Israel, Ye have seen that I have talked with you from heaven. Henry notes Moses, having entered the thick darkness, heard there all that follows to the end of chapter 23, mostly an exposition of the Ten Commandments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Ye shall not make with me gods of silver, neither shall ye make unto you gods of gold. Henry notes idolatry is forbidden again, in its costliest forms: silver and gold gods are no better than gods of wood and stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. An altar of earth thou shalt make unto me, and shalt sacrifice thereon thy burnt offerings and thy peace offerings, thy sheep and thine oxen, and in all places where I record my name I will come unto thee and bless thee. Henry notes the Israelites are assured of God's gracious acceptance of their devotions, and under the gospel men are encouraged to pray everywhere, for wherever God's people meet in his name, he will be in the midst of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And if thou wilt make me an altar of stone, thou shalt not build it of hewn stone, for if thou lift up thy tool upon it, thou hast polluted it. Henry observes the altar must be unhewn: human art upon it would pollute it, for God's worship must not be dressed by man's invention. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Neither shalt thou go up by steps unto mine altar, that thy nakedness be not discovered thereon. Henry notes even the approach to the altar was guarded, that nothing unseemly might appear in worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • covet. To wrongly desire what belongs to another; Henry notes the tenth commandment forbids all wrong desire of having what will gratify ourselves, not only the act of taking. Interpreted Study note on verse 17, summarizing Matthew Henry
