The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Corinthians 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 But I determined this with myself, that I would not come again to you in heaviness.

2 For if I make you sorry, who is he then that maketh me glad, but the same which is made sorry by me?

3 And I wrote this same unto you, lest, when I came, I should have sorrow from them of whom I ought to rejoice; having confidence in you all, that my joy is the joy of you all.

4 For out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote unto you with many tears; not that ye should be grieved, but that ye might know the love which I have more abundantly unto you.

5 But if any have caused grief, he hath not grieved me, but in part: that I may not overcharge you all.

6 Sufficient to such a man is this punishment, which was inflicted of many.

7 So that contrariwise ye ought rather to forgive him, and comfort him, lest perhaps such a one should be swallowed up with overmuch sorrow.

8 Wherefore I beseech you that ye would confirm your love toward him.

9 For to this end also did I write, that I might know the proof of you, whether ye be obedient in all things.

10 To whom ye forgive any thing, I forgive also: for if I forgave any thing, to whom I forgave it, for your sakes forgave I it in the person of Christ;

11 Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices.

12 Furthermore, when I came to Troas to preach Christ’s gospel, and a door was opened unto me of the Lord,

13 I had no rest in my spirit, because I found not Titus my brother: but taking my leave of them, I went from thence into Macedonia.

14 Now thanks be unto God, which always causeth us to triumph in Christ, and maketh manifest the savour of his knowledge by us in every place.

15 For we are unto God a sweet savour of Christ, in them that are saved, and in them that perish:

16 To the one we are the savour of death unto death; and to the other the savour of life unto life. And who is sufficient for these things?

17 For we are not as many, which corrupt the word of God: but as of sincerity, but as of God, in the sight of God speak we in Christ.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul explains that he wrote his painful letter instead of coming again in heaviness, and now urges the Corinthians to forgive and comfort the offender, lest he be swallowed up with overmuch sorrow, confirming their love toward him. He tells of finding no rest at Troas for lack of Titus, then turns to triumph: God always causes his people to triumph in Christ, and they are the fragrance of Christ to God in every place. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. But I determined this with myself, that I would not come again to you in heaviness. Henry notes the determination is pastoral: a second visit in sorrow would have wounded both Paul and Corinth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. For if I make you sorry, who is he then that makes me glad, but the same which is made sorry by me? JFB notes the circle: Paul's gladness depends on the very people his discipline would grieve, so he writes instead of visiting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And I wrote this same unto you, lest, when I came, I should have sorrow from them of whom I ought to rejoice; having confidence in you all, that my joy is the joy of you all. Henry notes the letter is meant to clear the sorrow beforehand, so that the visit can be joy, shared because his joy is theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote unto you with many tears; not that ye should be grieved, but that ye might know the love which I have more abundantly unto you. JFB notes the severe letter was written in tears: its severity is the measure of its love, not of its anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. But if any have caused grief, he has not grieved me, but in part: that I may not overcharge you all. Henry notes Paul softens the charge: the offender grieved the church in part, and Paul will not load the whole church with one man's fault. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Sufficient to such a man is this punishment, which was inflicted of many. JFB notes the church's censure, inflicted by the many, is now declared sufficient: discipline has done its work and must stop. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. So that contrariwise ye ought rather to forgive him, and comfort him, lest perhaps such a one should be swallowed up with overmuch sorrow. Henry notes the turn from censure to comfort: too much sorrow swallows a penitent, where measured sorrow restores him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Wherefore I beseech you that ye would confirm your love toward him. JFB notes forgiveness must be confirmed publicly, by a renewed declaration of love, not left as private feeling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. For to this end also did I write, that I might know the proof of you, whether ye be obedient in all things. Henry notes the severe letter was also a test of obedience, and Corinth's discipline of the offender proved it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. To whom ye forgive any thing, I forgive also: for if I forgave any thing, to whom I forgave it, for your sakes forgave I it in the person of Christ. JFB notes Paul joins his forgiveness to theirs, forgiving in Christ's presence and for their sake, so that no gap remains between apostle and church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices. Henry notes the danger in both directions: unpunished sin emboldens Satan, and unforgiven penitence gives him the swallowed man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Furthermore, when I came to Troas to preach Christ's gospel, and a door was opened unto me of the Lord. JFB notes Troas offered an open door for preaching, the very opportunity a minister prays for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. I had no rest in my spirit, because I found not Titus my brother: but taking my leave of them, I went from thence into Macedonia. Henry notes Paul left an open door because anxiety for Corinth and for Titus's news outweighed opportunity: love for one church interrupted work in another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Now thanks be unto God, which always causes us to triumph in Christ, and makes manifest the savour of his knowledge by us in every place. JFB notes the triumph is God's procession, and the apostles are the incense of Christ's knowledge, spread wherever they go. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. For we are unto God a sweet savour of Christ, in them that are saved, and in them that perish. Henry notes the savour is sweet to God in both cases, because faithfulness, not success, is what God smells. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. To the one we are the savour of death unto death; and to the other the savour of life unto life. And who is sufficient for these things? JFB notes the same gospel smells of death to the unbeliever, as the announcement of a dead Christ ending in death, and of life to the believer, as a risen Saviour ending in life, and the question 'who is sufficient?' prepares the answer in chapter 3. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For we are not as many, which corrupt the word of God: but as of sincerity, but as of God, in the sight of God speak we in Christ. JFB notes the false teachers adulterate the word like hucksters watering wine for gain, while Paul speaks from sincerity, from God, in God's sight, in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • savour. Fragrance or aroma; Paul says believers are the savour of Christ, of life to some and of death to others. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • corrupt the word. To adulterate or peddle God's word for profit, which Paul says he and his companions do not do. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • Troas. The city where Paul found an open door but no rest in his spirit, because Titus had not arrived. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
