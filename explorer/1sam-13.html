The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Saul reigned one year; and when he had reigned two years over Israel,

2 Saul chose him three thousand men of Israel; whereof two thousand were with Saul in Michmash and in mount Bethel, and a thousand were with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin: and the rest of the people he sent every man to his tent.

3 And Jonathan smote the garrison of the Philistines that was in Geba, and the Philistines heard of it. And Saul blew the trumpet throughout all the land, saying, Let the Hebrews hear.

4 And all Israel heard say that Saul had smitten a garrison of the Philistines, and that Israel also was had in abomination with the Philistines. And the people were called together after Saul to Gilgal.

5 And the Philistines gathered themselves together to fight with Israel, thirty thousand chariots, and six thousand horsemen, and people as the sand which is on the sea shore in multitude: and they came up, and pitched in Michmash, eastward from Bethaven.

6 When the men of Israel saw that they were in a strait, (for the people were distressed,) then the people did hide themselves in caves, and in thickets, and in rocks, and in high places, and in pits.

7 And some of the Hebrews went over Jordan to the land of Gad and Gilead. As for Saul, he was yet in Gilgal, and all the people followed him trembling.

8 And he tarried seven days, according to the set time that Samuel had appointed: but Samuel came not to Gilgal; and the people were scattered from him.

9 And Saul said, Bring hither a burnt offering to me, and peace offerings. And he offered the burnt offering.

10 And it came to pass, that as soon as he had made an end of offering the burnt offering, behold, Samuel came; and Saul went out to meet him, that he might salute him.

11 And Samuel said, What hast thou done? And Saul said, Because I saw that the people were scattered from me, and that thou camest not within the days appointed, and that the Philistines gathered themselves together at Michmash;

12 Therefore said I, The Philistines will come down now upon me to Gilgal, and I have not made supplication unto the LORD: I forced myself therefore, and offered a burnt offering.

13 And Samuel said to Saul, Thou hast done foolishly: thou hast not kept the commandment of the LORD thy God, which he commanded thee: for now would the LORD have established thy kingdom upon Israel for ever.

14 But now thy kingdom shall not continue: the LORD hath sought him a man after his own heart, and the LORD hath commanded him to be captain over his people, because thou hast not kept that which the LORD commanded thee.

15 And Samuel arose, and gat him up from Gilgal unto Gibeah of Benjamin. And Saul numbered the people that were present with him, about six hundred men.

16 And Saul, and Jonathan his son, and the people that were present with them, abode in Gibeah of Benjamin: but the Philistines encamped in Michmash.

17 And the spoilers came out of the camp of the Philistines in three companies: one company turned unto the way that leadeth to Ophrah, unto the land of Shual:

18 And another company turned the way to Bethhoron: and another company turned to the way of the border that looketh to the valley of Zeboim toward the wilderness.

19 Now there was no smith found throughout all the land of Israel: for the Philistines said, Lest the Hebrews make them swords or spears:

20 But all the Israelites went down to the Philistines, to sharpen every man his share, and his coulter, and his axe, and his mattock.

21 Yet they had a file for the mattocks, and for the coulters, and for the forks, and for the axes, and to sharpen the goads.

22 So it came to pass in the day of battle, that there was neither sword nor spear found in the hand of any of the people that were with Saul and Jonathan: but with Saul and with Jonathan his son was there found.

23 And the garrison of the Philistines went out to the passage of Michmash.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Saul, having reigned one year and now two years over Israel, chooses three thousand men, two thousand with him at Michmash and a thousand with Jonathan at Gibeah, and Jonathan smites the Philistine garrison at Geba, so that Saul blows the trumpet through the land. The Philistines answer with thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen and people as the sand on the seashore, and Israel, in a strait, hides in caves, thickets, rocks and pits, some crossing Jordan to Gad and Gilead. At Gilgal Saul tarries seven days for Samuel, the people scatter, and Saul offers the burnt offering himself; Samuel arrives as he finishes and charges him, thou hast done foolishly, thou hast not kept the commandment of the LORD, for now would the LORD have established thy kingdom for ever, but now thy kingdom shall not continue, for the LORD has sought him a man after his own heart. The chapter closes on Israel's disarmament: no smith is found in all Israel, lest the Hebrews make swords or spears, and in the day of battle no sword or spear is found in any hand except Saul's and Jonathan's, while the Philistine garrison holds the passage of Michmash. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Saul reigned one year, and when he had reigned two years over Israel, the events of this chapter took place. For above a year, Henry notes, he gave the Philistines time to prepare for war and to weaken and disarm Israel; when men are lifted up in self-sufficiency, they are often led into folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Saul chose three thousand men of Israel: two thousand with him in Michmash and Mount Bethel, a thousand with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin, and the rest he sent home. The small standing force was the army of a king who had let a year slip by. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Jonathan smote the Philistine garrison in Geba, and Saul blew the trumpet throughout the land, saying, Let the Hebrews hear. The first blow against the occupiers was struck by the son, and proclaimed by the father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. All Israel heard that Saul had smitten a garrison and that Israel was now had in abomination with the Philistines, and the people were called together after Saul to Gilgal. The chief advantages of the church's enemies, Henry observes, are derived from the misconduct of its professed friends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The Philistines gathered to fight Israel: thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and people as the sand on the seashore, pitching in Michmash eastward from Bethaven. The response dwarfed anything Israel could field. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. When Israel saw they were in a strait and distressed, the people hid themselves in caves, thickets, rocks, high places, and pits. Terror scattered the nation before a sword was drawn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Some Hebrews went over Jordan to Gad and Gilead, while Saul remained in Gilgal with the people following him trembling. When Saul at length sounded the alarm, Henry notes, the people, dissatisfied with his management or terrified by the enemy, did not come to him or speedily deserted him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. He tarried seven days, according to the set time Samuel had appointed, but Samuel came not to Gilgal, and the people were scattered from him. The appointed wait ran out while the army melted away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Saul said, Bring hither a burnt offering to me, and peace offerings, and he offered the burnt offering. Saul broke the order expressly given by Samuel, offering sacrifice himself though he was neither priest nor prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. As soon as he had made an end of offering, behold, Samuel came, and Saul went out to meet and salute him. The prophet arrived the moment the disobedience was complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Samuel said, What hast thou done? And Saul said, Because I saw the people scattered, and thou camest not within the appointed days, and the Philistines gathered at Michmash. When charged with disobedience, Henry observes, Saul justified himself and gave no sign of repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Therefore said I, the Philistines will come down upon me to Gilgal, and I have not made supplication unto the LORD: I forced myself therefore, and offered a burnt offering. He would have this act of disobedience pass for prudence and piety, Henry says, but men destitute of inward piety often lay great stress on outward performances of religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Samuel said, Thou hast done foolishly; thou hast not kept the commandment of the LORD thy God, which he commanded thee, for now would the LORD have established thy kingdom upon Israel for ever. Samuel charged Saul with being an enemy to himself: those who disobey God's commandments do foolishly for themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. But now thy kingdom shall not continue: the LORD hath sought him a man after his own heart, and commanded him to be captain over his people, because thou hast not kept what the LORD commanded thee. Men see only Saul's outward act, which seems small, Henry notes, but God saw unbelief and distrust of his providence, contempt of his authority, and rebellion against conscience; our disposition to obey or disobey is often proved by behaviour in things that appear small. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Samuel arose and went up from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin, and Saul numbered the people present with him, about six hundred men. The prophet left; the army had shrunk to six hundred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Saul, Jonathan, and the people with them abode in Gibeah of Benjamin, while the Philistines encamped in Michmash. The two camps faced each other across the pass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Spoilers came out of the Philistine camp in three companies, one turning toward Ophrah in the land of Shual. The raiders spread to strip the country while Israel watched, powerless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Another company turned the way to Bethhoron, and another to the border overlooking the valley of Zeboim toward the wilderness. Three roads, three raids, one defenceless land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Now there was no smith found throughout all Israel, for the Philistines said, Lest the Hebrews make them swords or spears. See how politic the Philistines were, Henry observes: they not only prevented Israel from making weapons but obliged them to depend on their enemies even for husbandry tools. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. All the Israelites went down to the Philistines to sharpen every man his share, coulter, axe, and mattock. The farmer's tools had to be carried to the enemy's forge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Yet they had a file for the mattocks, coulters, forks, axes, and to sharpen the goads. Even the sharpening was grudging and costly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. So in the day of battle there was neither sword nor spear found in the hand of any of the people with Saul and Jonathan, except with Saul and Jonathan themselves. Miserable is a guilty, defenceless nation, Henry says — much more those destitute of the whole armour of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. The garrison of the Philistines went out to the passage of Michmash. The enemy held the pass, and the stage was set for Jonathan's exploit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
