The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD said unto Samuel, How long wilt thou mourn for Saul, seeing I have rejected him from reigning over Israel? fill thine horn with oil, and go, I will send thee to Jesse the Bethlehemite: for I have provided me a king among his sons.

2 And Samuel said, How can I go? if Saul hear it, he will kill me. And the LORD said, Take an heifer with thee, and say, I am come to sacrifice to the LORD.

3 And call Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will shew thee what thou shalt do: and thou shalt anoint unto me him whom I name unto thee.

4 And Samuel did that which the LORD spake, and came to Bethlehem. And the elders of the town trembled at his coming, and said, Comest thou peaceably?

5 And he said, Peaceably: I am come to sacrifice unto the LORD: sanctify yourselves, and come with me to the sacrifice. And he sanctified Jesse and his sons, and called them to the sacrifice.

6 And it came to pass, when they were come, that he looked on Eliab, and said, Surely the LORD’s anointed is before him.

7 But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.

8 Then Jesse called Abinadab, and made him pass before Samuel. And he said, Neither hath the LORD chosen this.

9 Then Jesse made Shammah to pass by. And he said, Neither hath the LORD chosen this.

10 Again, Jesse made seven of his sons to pass before Samuel. And Samuel said unto Jesse, The LORD hath not chosen these.

11 And Samuel said unto Jesse, Are here all thy children? And he said, There remaineth yet the youngest, and, behold, he keepeth the sheep. And Samuel said unto Jesse, Send and fetch him: for we will not sit down till he come hither.

12 And he sent, and brought him in. Now he was ruddy, and withal of a beautiful countenance, and goodly to look to. And the LORD said, Arise, anoint him: for this is he.

13 Then Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the midst of his brethren: and the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward. So Samuel rose up, and went to Ramah.

14 But the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the LORD troubled him.

15 And Saul’s servants said unto him, Behold now, an evil spirit from God troubleth thee.

16 Let our lord now command thy servants, which are before thee, to seek out a man, who is a cunning player on an harp: and it shall come to pass, when the evil spirit from God is upon thee, that he shall play with his hand, and thou shalt be well.

17 And Saul said unto his servants, Provide me now a man that can play well, and bring him to me.

18 Then answered one of the servants, and said, Behold, I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite, that is cunning in playing, and a mighty valiant man, and a man of war, and prudent in matters, and a comely person, and the LORD is with him.

19 Wherefore Saul sent messengers unto Jesse, and said, Send me David thy son, which is with the sheep.

20 And Jesse took an ass laden with bread, and a bottle of wine, and a kid, and sent them by David his son unto Saul.

21 And David came to Saul, and stood before him: and he loved him greatly; and he became his armourbearer.

22 And Saul sent to Jesse, saying, Let David, I pray thee, stand before me; for he hath found favour in my sight.

23 And it came to pass, when the evil spirit from God was upon Saul, that David took an harp, and played with his hand: so Saul was refreshed, and was well, and the evil spirit departed from him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The LORD tells Samuel to mourn Saul no longer and sends him to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for he has provided a king among his sons, with a heifer for sacrifice as his true errand, since Saul would kill him if he heard of the anointing. At the sacrifice Samuel looks on Eliab and judges by countenance and stature, as he had with Saul, but the LORD answers, Look not on his countenance or the height of his stature, for the LORD seeth not as man seeth: man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart. Seven sons pass by and are refused; the youngest, David, keeping the sheep, is fetched, ruddy and goodly to look to, and Samuel anoints him in the midst of his brethren, and the Spirit of the LORD comes on David from that day forward. Meanwhile the Spirit departs from Saul and an evil spirit troubles him, his servants propose a cunning harp player, and David, described as cunning in playing, a mighty valiant man, a man of war, prudent in matters, comely, and with the LORD with him, is brought to court, becomes Saul's armourbearer, and refreshes the king with his harp when the evil spirit is upon him. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD said to Samuel, How long wilt thou mourn for Saul, seeing I have rejected him from reigning over Israel? Fill thine horn with oil, and go; I will send thee to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for I have provided me a king among his sons. Mourning for the rejected king was to give way to anointing his successor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Samuel feared that if Saul heard of the errand he would kill him, so the LORD told him to take a heifer and say he had come to sacrifice. Saul was grown very wicked, Henry notes, and God gave his prophet a true errand that concealed the anointing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Call Jesse to the sacrifice, God said, and I will show thee what thou shalt do, and thou shalt anoint unto me him whom I name unto thee. The choice would be revealed at the sacrifice, not before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Samuel did as the LORD spoke and came to Bethlehem, and the elders trembled at his coming and asked, Comest thou peaceably? It becomes us, Henry says, to stand in awe of God's messengers and to tremble at his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. He said, Peaceably: I am come to sacrifice unto the LORD; sanctify yourselves and come with me. And he sanctified Jesse and his sons and called them to the sacrifice. When our Lord Jesus came, Henry reflects, though men had reason to fear his errand was condemnation, he gave full assurance he came peaceably, for he came to sacrifice — and brought his offering with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. When they were come, Samuel looked on Eliab and said, Surely the LORD's anointed is before him. It was strange, Henry notes, that Samuel, so disappointed in Saul, whose countenance and stature had recommended him, should judge another man by that rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. But the LORD said, Look not on his countenance or the height of his stature, because I have refused him; for the LORD seeth not as man seeth: man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart. We can tell how men look, Henry says, but God can tell what they are; he judges by the heart, valuing the faith, fear, and love planted there beyond human discernment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Jesse called Abinadab and made him pass before Samuel, and he said, Neither hath the LORD chosen this. The second son was refused as the first had been. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Jesse made Shammah to pass by, and Samuel said, Neither hath the LORD chosen this. Stature and birth order were not God's measure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Jesse made seven of his sons to pass before Samuel, and Samuel said, The LORD hath not chosen these. All seven of the sons present were passed over. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Samuel asked, Are here all thy children? And Jesse said, There remaineth yet the youngest, and behold, he keepeth the sheep, and Samuel said, Send and fetch him, for we will not sit down till he come. God does not favour children according to fond partiality, Henry observes, but often most honours and blesses those least regarded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. He sent and brought him in: ruddy, of beautiful countenance, goodly to look to, and the LORD said, Arise, anoint him, for this is he. David was the youngest of Jesse's sons, Henry notes; his name signifies Beloved, and he was a type of God's beloved Son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brethren, and the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward, and Samuel rose up and went to Ramah. His anointing was not an empty ceremony, Henry says: a Divine power went with the instituted sign, and David found himself advanced in wisdom and courage with all the qualifications of a prince, though not advanced in outward circumstances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. But the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the LORD troubled him. Saul was made a terror to himself: if God and his grace do not rule us, Henry warns, sin and Satan will have possession of us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Saul's servants said, Behold now, an evil spirit from God troubleth thee. The court named the trouble plainly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Let our lord command thy servants to seek a man cunning in playing the harp, that when the evil spirit is upon thee, he shall play, and thou shalt be well. Music was proposed as the remedy for the troubled king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Saul said, Provide me now a man that can play well, and bring him to me. The king accepted the court's counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. One servant answered, I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite, cunning in playing, a mighty valiant man, a man of war, prudent in matters, a comely person, and the LORD is with him. The description already joined the harpist and the warrior in one young man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Saul sent messengers to Jesse, saying, Send me David thy son, which is with the sheep. The shepherd was summoned from the flock to the court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Jesse took an ass laden with bread, a bottle of wine, and a kid, and sent them by David his son unto Saul. The father's gift accompanied the son's first coming to court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. David came to Saul and stood before him, and Saul loved him greatly, and he became his armourbearer. The anointed shepherd now bore the rejected king's armour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Saul sent to Jesse, saying, Let David stand before me, for he hath found favour in my sight. The king kept at court the very youth God had chosen to replace him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. When the evil spirit from God was upon Saul, David took a harp and played, so Saul was refreshed and well, and the evil spirit departed from him. It is a pity, Henry reflects, that music, which may serve the good temper of the mind, should ever be abused to support vanity; and music, diversions, company, or business may quiet a wounded conscience for a time, but nothing effects a real cure but the blood of Christ applied in faith and the sanctifying Spirit sealing the pardon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
