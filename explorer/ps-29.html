The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 29 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Give unto the LORD, O ye mighty, give unto the LORD glory and strength.

2 Give unto the LORD the glory due unto his name; worship the LORD in the beauty of holiness.

3 The voice of the LORD is upon the waters: the God of glory thundereth: the LORD is upon many waters.

4 The voice of the LORD is powerful; the voice of the LORD is full of majesty.

5 The voice of the LORD breaketh the cedars; yea, the LORD breaketh the cedars of Lebanon.

6 He maketh them also to skip like a calf; Lebanon and Sirion like a young unicorn.

7 The voice of the LORD divideth the flames of fire.

8 The voice of the LORD shaketh the wilderness; the LORD shaketh the wilderness of Kadesh.

9 The voice of the LORD maketh the hinds to calve, and discovereth the forests: and in his temple doth every one speak of his glory.

10 The LORD sitteth upon the flood; yea, the LORD sitteth King for ever.

11 The LORD will give strength unto his people; the LORD will bless his people with peace.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The storm psalm: the mighty are bidden to give glory to God, and his voice, thunder over waters, breaks Lebanon's cedars, makes mountains skip, divides lightning, shakes the wilderness, and strips forests, while in his temple all speak glory. Above the flood the Lord sits King for ever, giving his people strength and peace. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Give unto the Lord, O you mighty, give unto the Lord glory and strength: the mighty — whether angels or princes — are bidden to ascribe glory and strength to God rather than claim it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Give unto the Lord the glory due to his name; worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness: glory is due, not optional, and worship's beauty is holiness itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The voice of the Lord is upon the waters; the God of glory thunders; the Lord is upon many waters: the storm begins over the sea, and God's voice is the thunder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The voice of the Lord is powerful; the voice of the Lord is full of majesty: power and majesty are the two qualities of that voice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The voice of the Lord breaks the cedars, yea, breaks the cedars of Lebanon: the mightiest trees fall before a voice — strength is no match for God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He makes them skip like a calf, Lebanon and Sirion like a young unicorn: mountains themselves leap under the storm's force. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The voice of the Lord divides the flames of fire: lightning is forked by God's voice, as if the fire obeyed his division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The voice of the Lord shakes the wilderness; the Lord shakes the wilderness of Kadesh: the storm moves inland, shaking even the barren south. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The voice of the Lord makes the hinds calve and discovers the forests, and in his temple everyone speaks of his glory: the storm's terror in nature becomes glory spoken in the temple — fear turns to worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The Lord sits upon the flood; yea, the Lord sits King for ever: above the flood-waters, the symbol of chaos, God sits enthroned as everlasting King. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The Lord will give strength to his people; the Lord will bless his people with peace: the God of the storm gives his own people strength, and his last word to them is peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
