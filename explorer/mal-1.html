The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Malachi 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The burden of the word of the LORD to Israel by Malachi.

2 I have loved you, saith the LORD. Yet ye say, Wherein hast thou loved us? Was not Esau Jacob’s brother? saith the LORD: yet I loved Jacob,

3 And I hated Esau, and laid his mountains and his heritage waste for the dragons of the wilderness.

4 Whereas Edom saith, We are impoverished, but we will return and build the desolate places; thus saith the LORD of hosts, They shall build, but I will throw down; and they shall call them, The border of wickedness, and, The people against whom the LORD hath indignation for ever.

5 And your eyes shall see, and ye shall say, The LORD will be magnified from the border of Israel.

6 A son honoureth his father, and a servant his master: if then I be a father, where is mine honour? and if I be a master, where is my fear? saith the LORD of hosts unto you, O priests, that despise my name. And ye say, Wherein have we despised thy name?

7 Ye offer polluted bread upon mine altar; and ye say, Wherein have we polluted thee? In that ye say, The table of the LORD is contemptible.

8 And if ye offer the blind for sacrifice, is it not evil? and if ye offer the lame and sick, is it not evil? offer it now unto thy governor; will he be pleased with thee, or accept thy person? saith the LORD of hosts.

9 And now, I pray you, beseech God that he will be gracious unto us: this hath been by your means: will he regard your persons? saith the LORD of hosts.

10 Who is there even among you that would shut the doors for nought? neither do ye kindle fire on mine altar for nought. I have no pleasure in you, saith the LORD of hosts, neither will I accept an offering at your hand.

11 For from the rising of the sun even unto the going down of the same my name shall be great among the Gentiles; and in every place incense shall be offered unto my name, and a pure offering: for my name shall be great among the heathen, saith the LORD of hosts.

12 But ye have profaned it, in that ye say, The table of the LORD is polluted; and the fruit thereof, even his meat, is contemptible.

13 Ye said also, Behold, what a weariness is it! and ye have snuffed at it, saith the LORD of hosts; and ye brought that which was torn, and the lame, and the sick; thus ye brought an offering: should I accept this of your hand? saith the LORD.

14 But cursed be the deceiver, which hath in his flock a male, and voweth, and sacrificeth unto the LORD a corrupt thing: for I am a great King, saith the LORD of hosts, and my name is dreadful among the heathen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The burden of the word of the LORD to Israel by Malachi opens with love answered by argument: I have loved you, yet you say, wherein have you loved us? Jacob was loved and Esau's mountains laid waste, while Edom's vow to rebuild its desolate places is answered with, they shall build, but I will throw down. The priests are charged with despising God's name, offering polluted bread and the blind, lame, and sick on the altar, gifts they would not dare offer their governor, while from the rising of the sun to its going down God's name will be great among the Gentiles with a pure offering in every place. Cursed is the deceiver who vows a male from his flock and sacrifices a corrupt thing, for God is a great King and his name dreadful among the nations. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The burden of the word of the LORD to Israel by Malachi, for Malachi was the last of the prophets, reproving priests and people for their evil practices and inviting them to repentance, with promises of blessings at the coming of the Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. I have loved you, saith the LORD, yet ye say, Wherein hast thou loved us, for all advantages, outward or spiritual, come from the free love of God, yet we are all prone to undervalue his mercies and excuse our own offences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Was not Esau Jacob's brother, saith the LORD, yet I loved Jacob, and I hated Esau, and laid his mountains and his heritage waste for the dragons of the wilderness, for God chose his people that they might be holy, and if we love him, it is because he has first loved us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Whereas Edom saith, We are impoverished, but we will return and build the desolate places, thus saith the LORD of hosts, They shall build, but I will throw down, and they shall call them, The border of wickedness, and, The people against whom the LORD hath indignation for ever, for all the evils sinners feel and fear are the just recompense of their crimes, while all their hopes and comforts are from unmerited mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And your eyes shall see, and ye shall say, The LORD will be magnified from the border of Israel, for God's judgments on Edom and mercies to Israel would both display his greatness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. A son honoureth his father, and a servant his master, he said; if then I be a father, where is mine honour, and if I be a master, where is my fear, saith the LORD of hosts unto you, O priests, that despise my name, and ye say, Wherein have we despised thy name, for our relation to God as Father and Master strongly obliges us to fear and honour him, and we may each charge upon ourselves what is here charged upon the priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Ye offer polluted bread upon mine altar, he said, and ye say, Wherein have we polluted thee, in that ye say, The table of the LORD is contemptible, for those who live in careless neglect of holy ordinances, attending without reverence and going away unconcerned, in effect say the table of the Lord is contemptible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And if ye offer the blind for sacrifice, is it not evil, he asked, and if ye offer the lame and sick, is it not evil, for if we worship ignorantly we bring the blind, if carelessly and coldly we bring the sick, if resting in bodily exercise without heart-work we bring the lame, and if we suffer vain thoughts to lodge within us we bring the torn, and is not this evil, a great affront to God and injury to our own souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And now, I pray you, beseech God that he will be gracious unto us, he said in irony; this hath been by your means, shall he regard your persons, saith the LORD of hosts, for sinners ruin themselves by trying to baffle their convictions, and a worship offered in contempt cannot expect acceptance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Who is there even among you that would shut the doors for nought, he asked, neither do ye kindle fire on mine altar for nought; I have no pleasure in you, saith the LORD of hosts, neither will I accept an offering at your hand, for they grudged the expense, thinking all thrown away which did not turn to their profit, and understood not the sacrifices as shadowing forth the unblemished Lamb of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. For from the rising of the sun even unto the going down of the same my name shall be great among the Gentiles, he said, and in every place incense shall be offered unto my name, and a pure offering, for a spiritual worship shall be established, incense signifying prayer and praise, and when true worshippers worshipped the Father in spirit and in truth, then this pure offering was offered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. But ye have profaned it, he said, in that ye say, The table of the LORD is polluted, and the fruit thereof, even his meat, is contemptible, for they despised God's name in what they did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Ye said also, Behold, what a weariness is it, he said, and ye have snuffed at it, saith the LORD of hosts, and ye brought that which was torn, and the lame, and the sick, thus ye brought an offering; should I accept this of your hand, saith the LORD, for in order to acceptance, it is not enough to do what is good in matter, but we must do it from a right principle, in a right manner, and for a right end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. But cursed be the deceiver, he said, which hath in his flock a male, and voweth, and sacrificeth unto the Lord a corrupt thing, for the LORD is a great King, and my name is dreadful among the heathen, for if there be a willing mind it will be accepted though defective, but if any be a deceiver, devoting his best to Satan and his lusts, he is under a curse, and our constant mercies make worse our slothfulness and niggardliness in returns of duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
