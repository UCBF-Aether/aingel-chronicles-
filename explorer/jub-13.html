The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 13 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And Abram journeyed from Haran, and he took Sarai, his wife, and Lot, his brother Haran's son, to the land of Canaan, and he came into Asshur, and proceeded to Shechem, and dwelt near a lofty oak.

2 And he saw, and, behold, the land was very pleasant from the entering of Hamath to the lofty. oak.

3 And the Lord said to him : " To thee and to thy seed will I give this land." 4.

4 And he built an altar there, and he offered thereon a burnt sacrifice to the Lord, who had appeared to him.

5 And he removed from thence unto the mountain . . . Bethel on the west and Ai on the east, and pitched his tent there.

6 And he saw. and behold, the land was very wide and good, and everything grew thereon — vines and figs and pomegranates, oaks and ilexes, and terebinths and oil trees, and cedars and cypresses and date trees, and all trees of the field, and there was water on the mountains.

7 And he blessed the Lord who had led him out of Ur of the Chaldees, and had brought him to this land.

8 And it came to pass in the first year, in the seventh week, on the new moon of the first month, that he built an altar on this mountain, and called on the name of the Lord ; " Thou, the eternal God, art my God." 9.

9 And he offered on the altar a burnt sacrifice unto the Lord

10 that He should be with him and not forsake him all the days of his life.

11 And he removed from thence and went towards the south and he came to Hebron, and Hebron was built at that time, and he dwelt there two years, and he went (thence) into the land of the south, to Bealoth and there was a famine in the land.

12 And Abram went into Egypt in the third year of the week, and he dwelt in Egypt five years before his wife was torn away from him.

13 Now Tanais in Egypt was at that time built — seven years after Hebron.

14 And it came to pass when Pharaoh seized Sarai, the wife of Abram, that the Lord plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai, Abram's wife.

15 And Abram was very glorious by reason of possessions in sheep, and cattle, and asses, and horses, and camels, and menservants, and maidservants, and in silver and gold exceedingly.

16 And Lot also, his brother's son, was wealthy.

17 And Pharaoh gave back Sarai, the wife of Abram, and he sent him out of the land of Egypt and he journeyed to the place where he had pitched his tent at the beginning, to the place of the altar, with Ai on the east, and Bethel on the west, and he blessed the Lord his God who had brought him back in peace.

18 And it came to pass in the forty-first jubilee, in the third year of the first week, that he returned to this place and offered thereon a burnt sacrifice, and called on the name of the Lord, and said : " Thou, the most high God, art my God for ever and ever." 17.

19 And in the fourth year this week Lot parted from him, and Lot dwelt in Sodom, and the men of Sodom were sinners exceedingly.

20 And it grieved him in his heart that his brother's son had parted from him ; for he had no

21 children.

22 In that year when Lot was taken captive, the Lord said unto Abram, after that Lot had parted from him, in the fourth year of this week : ' Lift up thine eyes from the place where thou art dwelling, northward and southward, and westward and eastward.

23 For all the land which thou seest I shall give to thee and to thy seed for ever, and I shall make thy seed as the sand of the sea : though a man may number the dust of the earth, yet thy seed shall not be numbered.

24 Arise, walk (through the land) in the length of it and the breadth of it, and see it all; for to thy seed shall I give it."

25 And Abram went to Hebron, and dwelt there.

26 The Campaign of Chedorlaomer.

27 And in this year came Chedorlaomer, king of Elam, and Amraphel, king of Shinar, and Arioch, king of Sellasar and Tergal, king of nations, and slew the king of Gomorrah, and the king of Sodom fled, and many fell through wounds in the vale of Siddim, by the Salt Sea.

28 And they took captive Sodom and Adam and Zeboim, and they took captive Lot also, the son of Abram's brother, and all his possessions, and they went to Dan.

29 And one who had escaped came and told Abram that his brother's son had been taken captive and (Abram) armed his household servants . . .

30 . . . for Abram, and for his seed, a

31 " So that if a man can number . . . then shall thy seed also be numbered " (Gen. xiii. 16).

32 i. e.

33 Admah.

34 "led forth"; the rendering "armed" has the support of the Targum Onkelos.

35 tenth of the first-fruits to the Lord and the Lord ordained it as an ordinance for ever that they should give it to the priests who served before Him, that they should possess it for ever.

36 And to this law there is no limit of days ; for He hath ordained it for the generations for ever that they should give to the Lord the tenth of everything, of the seed and of the wine and of the oil and of the cattle and of the sheep.

37 And He gave (it) unto His priests to eat and to drink with joy before Him.

38 And the king of Sodom came to him and bowed himself before him, and said : " Our Lord Abram, give unto us the souls which thou hast rescued, but let the booty be thine." 29.

39 And Abram said unto him : " I lift up my hands to the Most High God, that from a thread to a shoe-latchet I shall not take aught that is thine, lest thou shouldst say I have made Abram rich; save only what the young men have eaten, and the portion of the men who went with me — Aner, Eschol, and Mamre.

40 These will take their portion."

41 God's Covenant with Abram.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: Abram journeyed from Haran, taking Sarai his wife and Lot his brother Haran's son to the land of Canaan, and he came into Asshur and proceeded to Shechem, dwelling near a lofty oak. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. Abram journeyed from Haran, taking Sarai his wife and Lot his brother Haran's son to the land of Canaan, and he came into Asshur and proceeded to Shechem, dwelling near a lofty oak. JFB on Genesis 12:5-6 notes Abram reached his destination in safety with his wife and orphan nephew, so the first promise was made good, and Shechem was a pastoral valley then unoccupied, with the terebinth grove of Moreh offering shade for encampment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He saw, and behold, the land was very pleasant from the entering of Hamath to the lofty oak. The land's pleasantness stretches from Hamath's entering to the oak of Shechem, the whole scope of the first view. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 3. The Lord said to him: 'To thee and to thy seed will I give this land.' JFB on Genesis 12:7 notes God was dealing with Abram not in a private capacity merely but with a view to high interests in future ages, the land his posterity would inhabit as a peculiar people, chosen in divine wisdom as the fittest cradle of a revelation designed for the whole world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He built an altar there and offered thereon a burnt sacrifice to the Lord who had appeared to him. JFB notes that by this solemn act Abram made an open profession of his religion, established the worship of the true God, and declared his faith in the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He removed from thence unto the mountain, Bethel on the west and Ai on the east, and pitched his tent there. The encampment moves to the mountain between Bethel and Ai, the altar place to which he will return in verse 17. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 6. He saw, and behold, the land was very wide and good, and everything grew thereon: vines, figs, pomegranates, oaks, ilexes, terebinths, oil trees, cedars, cypresses, date trees, all trees of the field, and there was water on the mountains. The catalogue of fruitfulness, crowned by water on the mountains, shows why the land is called very wide and good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. He blessed the Lord who had led him out of Ur of the Chaldees and brought him to this land. Blessing answers guidance: the God who led out of Ur has brought him into Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. In the first year, in the seventh week, on the new moon of the first month, he built an altar on this mountain and called on the name of the Lord: 'Thou, the eternal God, art my God.' The dated altar joins the book's new-moon reckoning to Abram's confession of the eternal God as his God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. He offered on the altar a burnt sacrifice unto the Lord. The offering on the new altar begins the sacrifice whose purpose the next verse states. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. The sacrifice was that God should be with him and not forsake him all the days of his life. Abram's burnt sacrifice seeks abiding presence: God with him, never forsaking, for life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. He removed from thence and went towards the south, coming to Hebron, which was built at that time, and he dwelt there two years, and went thence into the land of the south, to Bealoth, and there was a famine in the land. The southward march rests two years at newly built Hebron, then reaches Bealoth, where famine drives the next move. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. Abram went into Egypt in the third year of the week, and dwelt in Egypt five years before his wife was torn away from him. JFB on Genesis 12:10 notes he did not go back to his nativity, regretting his pilgrimage and despising the promised land, but withdrew for a while into a neighbouring country; Jubilees dates the Egyptian stay at five years before Sarai's seizure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Now Tanais in Egypt was at that time built, seven years after Hebron. The chronological aside dates Tanais, Zoan, seven years after Hebron's building, anchoring Abram's journey in the book's reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. When Pharaoh seized Sarai, Abram's wife, the Lord plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai. JFB on Genesis 12:18-20 notes God's interference rebuked Abram through enemies, reminding him this world is not his rest; here the plagues fall on Pharaoh's house for Sarai's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Abram was very glorious by reason of possessions in sheep, cattle, asses, horses, camels, menservants, maidservants, and in silver and gold exceedingly. JFB on Genesis 13:2 notes Abram was very rich compared with the pastoral tribes, and his gold and silver probably arose from the sale of his produce in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Lot also, his brother's son, was wealthy. Lot shares the prosperity, the wealth that will soon strain their common pasturage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. Pharaoh gave back Sarai, Abram's wife, and sent him out of Egypt, and he journeyed to the place where he had pitched his tent at the beginning, to the place of the altar, with Ai on the east and Bethel on the west, and he blessed the Lord his God who had brought him back in peace. The return goes straight to the first altar place, and the blessing thanks God for a peaceful bringing back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. In the forty-first jubilee, in the third year of the first week, he returned to this place and offered thereon a burnt sacrifice, and called on the name of the Lord, saying: 'Thou, the most high God, art my God forever and ever.' JFB on Genesis 13:4 notes Abram desired to reanimate his faith and piety on the scene of his former worship, whether in humility and penitence for his Egypt misconduct or thankfulness for deliverance, embracing the first opportunity to renew allegiance and offer the sacrifices pointing to the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. In the fourth year of this week Lot parted from him, and Lot dwelt in Sodom, and the men of Sodom were sinners exceedingly. JFB on Genesis 13:7, 10-11 notes Abraham's amiable character: waiving his right to dictate, he gave Lot freedom of choice, disinterested and generous, while Lot's choice of the plain was excellent worldly but inexpedient spiritually, a selfish and covetous spirit imperilling his soul for worldly advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. This verse opens Abram's grief: it grieved him in his heart that his brother's son had parted from him, for he had no children. The sentence is completed by the next verse's childlessness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. He had no children. Childlessness sharpens the parting: Lot was the nearest figure to an heir, and his departure leaves Abram alone with the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. In that year when Lot was taken captive, the Lord said unto Abram, after Lot had parted from him, in the fourth year of this week: 'Lift up thine eyes from the place where thou art dwelling, northward and southward, and westward and eastward.' After parting and captivity, the survey command comes: lift up the eyes in all four directions from where he dwells. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. 'For all the land which thou seest I shall give to thee and to thy seed forever, and I shall make thy seed as the sand of the sea: though a man may number the dust of the earth, yet thy seed shall not be numbered.' JFB on Genesis 13:14-15 notes so extensive a survey in all directions can be obtained from no other point in the neighbourhood, and those plains and hills, then desolate before the solitary patriarch, were to be peopled with a mighty nation like the dust of the earth in number. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. 'Arise, walk through the land in the length of it and the breadth of it, and see it all; for to thy seed shall I give it.' Walking the land's length and breadth is taking possession by survey, seeing all that the seed will receive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. Abram went to Hebron and dwelt there. After the promise and the survey, Abram settles at Hebron, the place of his earlier two-year stay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the campaign of Chedorlaomer. It divides Abram's settlement at Hebron from the war that will take Lot captive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. In this year came Chedorlaomer, king of Elam, and Amraphel, king of Shinar, and Arioch, king of Sellasar, and Tergal, king of nations, and they slew the king of Gomorrah, and the king of Sodom fled, and many fell through wounds in the vale of Siddim by the Salt Sea. JFB on Genesis 14:1 explains the occasion: Sodom and the adjoining cities, tributaries for twelve years to Elam, combined to throw off the yoke, and Chedorlaomer with three allies invaded, defeated them in a pitched battle where the ground favoured his army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. They took captive Sodom and Admah and Zeboim, and they took captive Lot, the son of Abram's brother, and all his possessions, and they went to Dan. JFB on Genesis 14:12 notes how Lot's conscience would now upbraid him for selfish folly and ingratitude in withdrawing from his kind and pious relative, since out of the path of duty one is put away from God's protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. One who had escaped came and told Abram that his brother's son had been taken captive, and Abram armed his household servants. JFB on Genesis 14:13-14 notes Abram might have excused himself from concern for an undeserving nephew, but far from rendering evil for evil, he resolved on immediate rescue, arming his trained household servants, domestic slaves treated as family members, whose sparing of 318 implies a large establishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Abram gave a tenth of the first-fruits to the Lord, and the Lord ordained it as an ordinance forever that they should give it to the priests who served before him, that they should possess it forever. JFB on Genesis 14:20 notes Abram gave tithes of all as evidence of piety as well as valour, to a priest and mediator between God and him, a tenth of the spoil as a token of gratitude and in honour of a divine ordinance; Jubilees makes the tithe an everlasting ordinance to the serving priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. To this law there is no limit of days, for God has ordained it for the generations forever that they should give to the Lord the tenth of everything, of the seed and of the wine and of the oil and of the cattle and of the sheep. The tithe covers every kind of increase, seed, wine, oil, cattle and sheep, without any limit of days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. He gave it unto his priests to eat and to drink with joy before him. The tithe feeds the priests with joy before God, turning ordinance into joyful provision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. The king of Sodom came to him and bowed himself before him, saying: 'Our Lord Abram, give unto us the souls which thou hast rescued, but let the booty be thine.' JFB on Genesis 14:21 notes that by war custom Abram might have retained the recovered goods, and his right was acknowledged by the king of Sodom himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. Abram said to him: 'I lift up my hands to the Most High God, that from a thread to a shoe-latchet I shall not take aught that is thine, lest thou shouldst say I have made Abram rich; save only what the young men have eaten, and the portion of the men who went with me, Aner, Eschol, and Mamre.' JFB on Genesis 14:22-23 notes Abram's honest pride and generosity, unknown in that part of the world: sworn to take not even a thread or sandal-thong, lest Sodom claim to have enriched him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. 'These will take their portion.' Aner, Eschol and Mamre take their portion: Abram's oath excepts his allies' shares from his own refusal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: God's covenant with Abram. It divides the war and tithe from the covenant dream that opens chapter 14. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 25. Abram went to Hebron and dwelt there. After the promise and the survey, Abram settles at Hebron, the place of his earlier two-year stay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the campaign of Chedorlaomer. It divides Abram's settlement at Hebron from the war that will take Lot captive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. To this law there is no limit of days, for God has ordained it for the generations forever that they should give to the Lord the tenth of everything, of the seed and of the wine and of the oil and of the cattle and of the sheep. The tithe covers every kind of increase, seed, wine, oil, cattle and sheep, without any limit of days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. He gave it unto his priests to eat and to drink with joy before him. The tithe feeds the priests with joy before God, turning ordinance into joyful provision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: God's covenant with Abram. It divides the war and tithe from the covenant dream that opens chapter 14. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 3. The Lord said to him: 'To thee and to thy seed will I give this land.' JFB on Genesis 12:7 notes God was dealing with Abram not in a private capacity merely but with a view to high interests in future ages, the land his posterity would inhabit as a peculiar people, chosen in divine wisdom as the fittest cradle of a revelation designed for the whole world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He built an altar there and offered thereon a burnt sacrifice to the Lord who had appeared to him. JFB notes that by this solemn act Abram made an open profession of his religion, established the worship of the true God, and declared his faith in the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. When Pharaoh seized Sarai, Abram's wife, the Lord plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai. JFB on Genesis 12:18-20 notes God's interference rebuked Abram through enemies, reminding him this world is not his rest; here the plagues fall on Pharaoh's house for Sarai's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Abram was very glorious by reason of possessions in sheep, cattle, asses, horses, camels, menservants, maidservants, and in silver and gold exceedingly. JFB on Genesis 13:2 notes Abram was very rich compared with the pastoral tribes, and his gold and silver probably arose from the sale of his produce in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. In the fourth year of this week Lot parted from him, and Lot dwelt in Sodom, and the men of Sodom were sinners exceedingly. JFB on Genesis 13:7, 10-11 notes Abraham's amiable character: waiving his right to dictate, he gave Lot freedom of choice, disinterested and generous, while Lot's choice of the plain was excellent worldly but inexpedient spiritually, a selfish and covetous spirit imperilling his soul for worldly advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. 'For all the land which thou seest I shall give to thee and to thy seed forever, and I shall make thy seed as the sand of the sea: though a man may number the dust of the earth, yet thy seed shall not be numbered.' JFB on Genesis 13:14-15 notes so extensive a survey in all directions can be obtained from no other point in the neighbourhood, and those plains and hills, then desolate before the solitary patriarch, were to be peopled with a mighty nation like the dust of the earth in number. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. In this year came Chedorlaomer, king of Elam, and Amraphel, king of Shinar, and Arioch, king of Sellasar, and Tergal, king of nations, and they slew the king of Gomorrah, and the king of Sodom fled, and many fell through wounds in the vale of Siddim by the Salt Sea. JFB on Genesis 14:1 explains the occasion: Sodom and the adjoining cities, tributaries for twelve years to Elam, combined to throw off the yoke, and Chedorlaomer with three allies invaded, defeated them in a pitched battle where the ground favoured his army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. They took captive Sodom and Admah and Zeboim, and they took captive Lot, the son of Abram's brother, and all his possessions, and they went to Dan. JFB on Genesis 14:12 notes how Lot's conscience would now upbraid him for selfish folly and ingratitude in withdrawing from his kind and pious relative, since out of the path of duty one is put away from God's protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. One who had escaped came and told Abram that his brother's son had been taken captive, and Abram armed his household servants. JFB on Genesis 14:13-14 notes Abram might have excused himself from concern for an undeserving nephew, but far from rendering evil for evil, he resolved on immediate rescue, arming his trained household servants, domestic slaves treated as family members, whose sparing of 318 implies a large establishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. The king of Sodom came to him and bowed himself before him, saying: 'Our Lord Abram, give unto us the souls which thou hast rescued, but let the booty be thine.' JFB on Genesis 14:21 notes that by war custom Abram might have retained the recovered goods, and his right was acknowledged by the king of Sodom himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. Abram said to him: 'I lift up my hands to the Most High God, that from a thread to a shoe-latchet I shall not take aught that is thine, lest thou shouldst say I have made Abram rich; save only what the young men have eaten, and the portion of the men who went with me, Aner, Eschol, and Mamre.' JFB on Genesis 14:22-23 notes Abram's honest pride and generosity, unknown in that part of the world: sworn to take not even a thread or sandal-thong, lest Sodom claim to have enriched him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. 'These will take their portion.' Aner, Eschol and Mamre take their portion: Abram's oath excepts his allies' shares from his own refusal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
