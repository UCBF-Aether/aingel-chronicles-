The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 32 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And he abode that night at Bethel, and Levi dreamed that they had ordained and made him the priest of the Most High God him and his sons for ever ; and he awoke from his sleep and blessed the Lord.

2 And Jacob rose early in the morning, on the fourteenth of this month, and he gave a tithe of all that came with him, both of men and cattle, both of gold and every vessel and garment, yea, he gave tithes of all.

3 And in those days Rachel became pregnant with her son Benjamin.

4 And Jacob counted his sons from him upwards and Levi fell to the portion of the Lord and his father clothed him in the garments of the priesthood and filled his hands.

5 And on the fifteenth of this month, he brought to the altar fourteen oxen from amongst the cattle, and twenty-eight rams, and forty-nine sheep, and seven lambs, and twenty-one kids of the goats as a burntoffering on the altar of sacrifice, well pleasing for a sweet savour before God.

6 This was his offering,

7 in consequence of the vow which he had vowed that he would give a tenth with their fruit-offerings and their drink-offerings.

8 And when the fire had consumed it, he burnt incense on the fire over the fire, and for a thank-offering two oxen and four rams and four sheep, four he-goats, and two sheep of a year old, and two kids of the goats ; and thus he did daily for seven days.

9 And he and all his sons and his men were eating (this) with joy there during seven days and blessing and thanking the Lord, who had delivered him out of all his tribulation and had given him his vow.

10 And he tithed all the clean animals, and made a burnt sacrifice, but the unclean animals he gave (not) to Levi his son, and he gave him all the souls of the men.

11 And Levi discharged the priestly office at Bethel before Jacob his father in preference to his ten brothers, and he was a priest there, and Jacob gave his vow : thus he tithed again the tithe to the Lord and sanctified it, and it became holy unto Him.

12 And for this reason it is ordained on the heavenly tables as a law for the tithing again the tithe to eat before the Lord from year to year in the place where it is chosen that His name should dwell, and to this law there is no limit of days for ever.

13 This ordinance is written that it may be fulfilled from year to year in eating the second tithe before the Lord in the place where it hath been chosen, and nothing shall remain over from it from this year to the year following.

14 For in its year shall the seed be eaten till the days of the gathering of the seed of the year, and the wine till the days of the wine, and the oil till the days of its season.

15 And all that is left thereof and becometh old, let it be regarded as polluted : let it be burnt with fire, for it is unclean.

16 And thus let them eat it together in the

17 sanctuary, and let them not suffer it to become old.

18 And all the tithes of the oxen and sheep shall be holy unto the Lord and shall belong to His priests, which they will eat before Him from year to year ; for thus is it ordained and engraven regarding the tithe on the heavenly tables.

19 And on the following night, on the twenty-second day of this month, Jacob resolved to build that place, and to surround the court with a wall, and to sanctify, it and make it holy for ever, for himself and his children after him.

20 And the Lord appeared to him by night and blessed him and said unto him : ' " Thy name shall not be called Jacob, but Israel shall they name thy name." 18.

21 And He said unto him again : "I am the Lord who created the heaven and the earth, and I shall increase thee and multiply thee exceedingly, and kings will come forth from thee, and they will judge everywhere wherever the foot of the sons of men hath trodden.

22 And I shall give to thy seed all the earth which is under heaven, and they will judge all the nations according to their desires, and after that they will get possession of the whole earth and inherit it for ever."

23 And He finished speaking with him, and He went up from him, and Jacob looked till He had ascended into heaven.

24 And he saw in a vision of the night, and behold an angel descended from heaven with seven tablets in his hands, and he gave them to Jacob, and he read them and knew all that was written therein which would befall him and his sons through-

25 out all the ages.

26 And he showed him all that was written on the tablets, and said unto him : " Do not build this place, and do not make it an eternal sanctuary and do not dwell here; for this is not the place.

27 Go to the house of Abraham thy father and dwell with Isaac thy father until the day of the death of thy father.

28 For in Egypt thou wilt die in peace, and in this land thou wilt be buried with honour in the sepulchre of thy fathers, with Abraham and Isaac. Fear not, for as thou hast seen and read it, 'thus will it all be; and do thou write down everything as thou hast seen and read." 25.

29 And Jacob said : " Lord, how can I remember all that I have read and seen?

30 " And he said unto him : " I will bring all things to thy remembrance." 26.

31 And he went up from him, and he awoke from his sleep, and he remembered everything which he had read and seen, and he wrote down all the words which he had read and seen.

32 And he celebrated there yet another day and he sacrificed thereon according to all that he sacrificed on the former days, and called its name " Addition," for this day was added, and the former days he called " The Feast." 28.

33 And thus it was manifested that it should be, and it is written on the heavenly tables : wherefore it was revealed to him that he should celebrate it, and add it to the seven days of the feast. And its name

34 was called " Addition," because that it was recorded amongst the days of the feast days, according to the number of the days of the year.

35 And in the night, on the twenty-third of this month, Deborah Rebecca's nurse died, and they buried her beneath the city under the oak of the river, and he called the name of this place, " The river of Deborah," and the oak, " The oak of the mourning of Deborah."1 31.

36 And Rebecca went and returned to her house to his father Isaac, and Jacob sent by her hand rams and sheep and he-goats that she should prepare a meal for his father such as he desired.

37 And he went after his mother till he came to the land of Kabratan and he dwelt there.

38 And Rachel bare a son in the night, and called his name " Son of my sorrow " ; for she suffered in giving him birth : but his father called his name Benjamin, on the eleventh of the eighth month in the first of the sixth week of this jubilee.

39 And Rachel died there and she was buried in the land of Ephrath, the same is Bethlehem, and Jacob built a pillar on the grave of Rachel, on the road above her grave.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. Levi dreamed at Bethel that they had ordained and made him priest of the Most High God, him and his sons for ever, and he awoke and blessed the Lord. The dream confirms at Bethel what Isaac's blessing had spoken: Levi's line would serve before God continually. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jacob rose early on the fourteenth of the month and gave a tithe of all that came with him, men, cattle, gold, vessels, and garments, tithes of all. Henry notes that the tenth is a fit proportion to devote to God, and that Jacob's vow at Bethel is now paid in full. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. In those days Rachel became pregnant with her son Benjamin. The pregnancy comes late in the journey, after the Bethel vow, and its end will bring both joy and grief to the household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Jacob counted his sons from the youngest upwards, and Levi fell to the portion of the Lord, so his father clothed him in the garments of the priesthood and filled his hands. The counting is a lot cast for God's portion, and Levi's investiture makes the dream and the blessing visible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. On the fifteenth of the month Jacob brought to the altar fourteen oxen, twenty-eight rams, forty-nine sheep, seven lambs, and twenty-one kids as a burnt offering, well pleasing for a sweet savour before God. The scale of the offering matches the scale of the deliverance it thanked God for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. This was Jacob's offering in consequence of the vow he had vowed, to give a tenth with fruit-offerings and drink-offerings. Henry stresses that a vow must not be deferred, and Jacob's offering is the payment of the Bethel vow made when he fled from Esau. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The offering was given in consequence of the vow, tying the sacrifice to the promise made at Bethel. Vow and offering belong together: the promise made in trouble is paid in safety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When the fire had consumed the burnt offering, Jacob burnt incense and offered a thank-offering of oxen, rams, sheep, he-goats, and yearling sheep and kids, daily for seven days. The seven days of thanksgiving turn the vow-payment into a feast, not a bare transaction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Jacob, his sons, and his men ate with joy for seven days, blessing and thanking the Lord who had delivered him out of all his tribulation and given him his vow. Henry notes that the comfort of holy ordinances lies in meeting God in them, and the feast is the household's joy in that meeting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jacob tithed all the clean animals and made a burnt sacrifice, but the unclean animals he did not give to Levi, and he gave him all the souls of the men. The distinction keeps the priest's portion holy, clean animals for sacrifice and men for service, while the unclean are excluded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Levi discharged the priestly office at Bethel before his father, in preference to his ten brothers, and Jacob gave his vow, tithing the tithe again and sanctifying it. Henry's reading of Levi's blessing fits: the tribe set apart to God as priests began its service here, before Jacob, at Bethel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. On the following night, on the twenty-second day of the month, Jacob resolved to build that place, surround the court with a wall, and sanctify it for ever for himself and his children. The resolve shows Jacob's desire to make the place of his vision a permanent sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The Lord appeared to Jacob by night, blessed him, and said his name would no longer be Jacob but Israel. Henry notes that God appeared to Jacob at Bethel, renewed the covenant, and changed his name: Jacob the supplanter became Israel, a prince with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. God said he was the Lord who created heaven and earth, and would increase and multiply Jacob exceedingly, with kings coming forth from him. Henry notes the two promises renewed at Bethel: a great nation and a good land, with a spiritual sense, Christ the promised Seed and heaven the promised land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. God promised Jacob's seed all the earth under heaven, judging the nations and at last possessing the whole earth and inheriting it for ever. The promise widens the land of Canaan into a universal inheritance, the full scope of the covenant with Abraham. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. God finished speaking and went up from Jacob, who looked until he had ascended into heaven. The ascending God closes the Bethel theophany as the ladder had opened it: heaven and earth meet, and then part, leaving Jacob with the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The angel commanded Jacob to go to the house of Abraham his father and dwell with Isaac his father until the day of his father's death. Henry's account of Genesis 35 records the fulfilment: Jacob came to Isaac at Hebron, and Isaac's death and burial by his two sons closed the patriarch's life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. On the night of the twenty-third of the month Deborah, Rebecca's nurse, died, and they buried her beneath the city under the oak of the river, calling the place the river of Deborah and the oak the oak of Deborah's mourning. Henry notes that Deborah died much lamented, and that old servants in a family who have been faithful and useful ought to be respected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Rebecca went and returned to her house to Isaac, and Jacob sent by her hand rams, sheep, and he-goats for a meal for his father such as he desired. The provision honours the aged father, the same care Henry finds in Jacob's household throughout his return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Rachel bore a son in the night and called him son of my sorrow, for she suffered in giving him birth, but his father called him Benjamin, on the eleventh of the eighth month in the first of the sixth week of this jubilee. Henry explains the two names: Ben-oni, son of sorrow, from the mother's dying lips, changed by Jacob to Benjamin, son of the right hand, so that sorrow would not be renewed every time the boy was called. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Rachel died there and was buried in Ephrath, that is Bethlehem, and Jacob built a pillar on her grave on the road above it. Henry notes that Rachel, who had passionately said give me children or else I die, now died having children, and that God alone knows what is best for his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
