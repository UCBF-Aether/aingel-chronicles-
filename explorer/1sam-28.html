The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 28 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass in those days, that the Philistines gathered their armies together for warfare, to fight with Israel. And Achish said unto David, Know thou assuredly, that thou shalt go out with me to battle, thou and thy men.

2 And David said to Achish, Surely thou shalt know what thy servant can do. And Achish said to David, Therefore will I make thee keeper of mine head for ever.

3 Now Samuel was dead, and all Israel had lamented him, and buried him in Ramah, even in his own city. And Saul had put away those that had familiar spirits, and the wizards, out of the land.

4 And the Philistines gathered themselves together, and came and pitched in Shunem: and Saul gathered all Israel together, and they pitched in Gilboa.

5 And when Saul saw the host of the Philistines, he was afraid, and his heart greatly trembled.

6 And when Saul enquired of the LORD, the LORD answered him not, neither by dreams, nor by Urim, nor by prophets.

7 Then said Saul unto his servants, Seek me a woman that hath a familiar spirit, that I may go to her, and enquire of her. And his servants said to him, Behold, there is a woman that hath a familiar spirit at Endor.

8 And Saul disguised himself, and put on other raiment, and he went, and two men with him, and they came to the woman by night: and he said, I pray thee, divine unto me by the familiar spirit, and bring me him up, whom I shall name unto thee.

9 And the woman said unto him, Behold, thou knowest what Saul hath done, how he hath cut off those that have familiar spirits, and the wizards, out of the land: wherefore then layest thou a snare for my life, to cause me to die?

10 And Saul sware to her by the LORD, saying, As the LORD liveth, there shall no punishment happen to thee for this thing.

11 Then said the woman, Whom shall I bring up unto thee? And he said, Bring me up Samuel.

12 And when the woman saw Samuel, she cried with a loud voice: and the woman spake to Saul, saying, Why hast thou deceived me? for thou art Saul.

13 And the king said unto her, Be not afraid: for what sawest thou? And the woman said unto Saul, I saw gods ascending out of the earth.

14 And he said unto her, What form is he of? And she said, An old man cometh up; and he is covered with a mantle. And Saul perceived that it was Samuel, and he stooped with his face to the ground, and bowed himself.

15 And Samuel said to Saul, Why hast thou disquieted me, to bring me up? And Saul answered, I am sore distressed; for the Philistines make war against me, and God is departed from me, and answereth me no more, neither by prophets, nor by dreams: therefore I have called thee, that thou mayest make known unto me what I shall do.

16 Then said Samuel, Wherefore then dost thou ask of me, seeing the LORD is departed from thee, and is become thine enemy?

17 And the LORD hath done to him, as he spake by me: for the LORD hath rent the kingdom out of thine hand, and given it to thy neighbour, even to David:

18 Because thou obeyedst not the voice of the LORD, nor executedst his fierce wrath upon Amalek, therefore hath the LORD done this thing unto thee this day.

19 Moreover the LORD will also deliver Israel with thee into the hand of the Philistines: and to morrow shalt thou and thy sons be with me: the LORD also shall deliver the host of Israel into the hand of the Philistines.

20 Then Saul fell straightway all along on the earth, and was sore afraid, because of the words of Samuel: and there was no strength in him; for he had eaten no bread all the day, nor all the night.

21 And the woman came unto Saul, and saw that he was sore troubled, and said unto him, Behold, thine handmaid hath obeyed thy voice, and I have put my life in my hand, and have hearkened unto thy words which thou spakest unto me.

22 Now therefore, I pray thee, hearken thou also unto the voice of thine handmaid, and let me set a morsel of bread before thee; and eat, that thou mayest have strength, when thou goest on thy way.

23 But he refused, and said, I will not eat. But his servants, together with the woman, compelled him; and he hearkened unto their voice. So he arose from the earth, and sat upon the bed.

24 And the woman had a fat calf in the house; and she hasted, and killed it, and took flour, and kneaded it, and did bake unleavened bread thereof:

25 And she brought it before Saul, and before his servants; and they did eat. Then they rose up, and went away that night.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The Philistines gather for war and Achish tells David he shall go out with him to battle, thou and thy men, making David keeper of his head for ever on his evasive answer. Samuel is dead and buried at Ramah, and Saul has put away those with familiar spirits and wizards out of the land; now the armies pitch at Shunem and Gilboa, Saul is afraid and his heart greatly trembles, and when he enquires of the LORD, the LORD answers him not, neither by dreams, nor by Urim, nor by prophets. Saul disguises himself, goes by night with two men to the woman at Endor who has a familiar spirit, swears by the LORD that no punishment shall happen to her for it, and says, Bring me up Samuel. At the sight of Samuel the woman cries out and knows her visitor is Saul; Samuel asks why he has disquieted him, seeing the LORD is departed from him and become his enemy, confirms that the kingdom is rent from his hand and given to David, names his disobedience over Amalek as the cause, and says, tomorrow shalt thou and thy sons be with me. Saul falls straightway on the earth, sore afraid and without strength, having eaten no bread all day nor all night, and is compelled by the woman and his servants to eat of the calf and unleavened bread she hastily prepares, then rises and goes away that night in sullen despair. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In those days the Philistines gathered their armies for warfare against Israel, and Achish said to David, Know thou assuredly, thou shalt go out with me to battle, thou and thy men. David could not refuse Achish without danger, Henry notes, and his position seemed impossible to escape with a clear conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. David said to Achish, Surely thou shalt know what thy servant can do, and Achish said, Therefore will I make thee keeper of mine head for ever. His evasive answer, intended to gain time, was not consistent with the character of an Israelite indeed, Henry observes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Now Samuel was dead, and all Israel had lamented him and buried him in Ramah, and Saul had put away those with familiar spirits and wizards out of the land. The prophet's death left Saul without his counsellor, and the king's own law had cleared the land of mediums — the very people he would soon seek. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The Philistines gathered and pitched in Shunem, and Saul gathered all Israel and pitched in Gilboa. The two hosts faced each other for the final campaign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. When Saul saw the Philistine host, he was afraid, and his heart greatly trembled. Troubles are terrors to the children of disobedience, Henry says. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. When Saul enquired of the LORD, the LORD answered him not, neither by dreams, nor by Urim, nor by prophets. In his distress Saul enquired, Henry notes, but he did not seek in faith — he sought with a double, unstable mind, and heaven was silent by every channel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Then said Saul to his servants, Seek me a woman that hath a familiar spirit, that I may go to her and enquire of her, and his servants said, Behold, there is a woman at Endor. Many seem zealous against sin when they are hurt by it, Henry observes, who have no concern for God's glory nor dislike of sin as sin; Saul would drive the devil out of his kingdom yet harbour him in his heart by envy and malice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Saul disguised himself, put on other raiment, and went with two men by night to the woman, saying, Divine unto me by the familiar spirit, and bring me up him whom I shall name. How foolish, Henry asks, to consult those whom, according to God's law, he had endeavoured to root out! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The woman said, Thou knowest what Saul hath done, how he hath cut off those with familiar spirits and wizards out of the land; wherefore then layest thou a snare for my life, to cause me to die? The medium feared the king's own law was a trap. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Saul sware to her by the LORD, As the LORD liveth, there shall no punishment happen to thee for this thing. The king swore by the LORD to protect a practice the LORD had forbidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Then said the woman, Whom shall I bring up unto thee? And he said, Bring me up Samuel. The name he wanted was the prophet he had despised in life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. When the woman saw Samuel, she cried with a loud voice, and said to Saul, Why hast thou deceived me? For thou art Saul. The woman's surprise and terror, Henry notes, proved this was an unusual and unexpected appearance, and no human fraud: though the woman could not cause Samuel's being sent, Saul's inquiry might be the occasion of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The king said, Be not afraid; for what sawest thou? And the woman said, I saw gods ascending out of the earth. The apparition rose as something more than a spirit of the dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. He asked, What form is he of? And she said, An old man cometh up, covered with a mantle, and Saul perceived it was Samuel, and stooped with his face to the ground and bowed himself. The mantle identified the prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Samuel asked why Saul had disquieted him to bring him up, and Saul answered that he was sore distressed, with the Philistines at war against him and God departed and answering no more by prophets or dreams. While Samuel lived, Henry observes, we never read of Saul going to advise with him in difficulties, yet now he sought the dead prophet's counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Then said Samuel, Wherefore then dost thou ask of me, seeing the LORD is departed from thee and is become thine enemy? The question exposed the folly: counsel was sought from the prophet of the God who had become his enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The LORD hath done to him as he spake by me, for the LORD hath rent the kingdom out of thine hand and given it to thy neighbour, even to David. The sentence pronounced in life was confirmed from beyond the grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Because thou obeyedst not the voice of the LORD, nor executedst his fierce wrath upon Amalek, therefore hath the LORD done this thing unto thee this day. Amalek, the old disobedience, was named as the cause of the present doom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Moreover the LORD will also deliver Israel with thee into the hand of the Philistines, and tomorrow shalt thou and thy sons be with me, and the LORD shall deliver Israel's host into Philistine hands. The expression, Thou and thy sons shall be with me, Henry explains, means no more than that they shall be in the eternal world; and there appears much solemnity in God's permitting a departed prophet to come as a witness from heaven to confirm the word he spoke on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Then Saul fell straightway all along on the earth, and was sore afraid because of Samuel's words, and there was no strength in him, for he had eaten no bread all day nor all night. Those who expect good counsel or comfort otherwise than from God, Henry warns, will be as wretchedly disappointed as Saul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. The woman came unto Saul, saw he was sore troubled, and said, Behold, thine handmaid hath obeyed thy voice, and I have put my life in my hand, and have hearkened unto thy words. The medium, who had risked her life, now became the king's nurse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Now therefore, I pray thee, hearken also unto the voice of thine handmaid, and let me set a morsel of bread before thee, and eat, that thou mayest have strength when thou goest on thy way. Bread was pressed on the man who would not eat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. But he refused, saying, I will not eat, but his servants together with the woman compelled him, and he hearkened, arose from the earth, and sat upon the bed. Though terrified even to despair, Henry notes, he was not humbled: he confessed not his sins, offered no sacrifices, and presented no supplications. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. The woman had a fat calf in the house, and she hasted and killed it, took flour, kneaded it, and baked unleavened bread thereof. The last meal of the doomed king was prepared in haste by the medium's hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. She brought it before Saul and his servants, and they did eat, then rose up and went away that night. He does not seem to have cared about his sons or his people, Henry observes, nor attempted any escape, but in sullen despair rushed upon his doom; God sets up a few such beacons to warn men not to stifle convictions or despise his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words in this chapter that a modern reader may stumble on. They explain the chapter text; they are not Scripture.

  • familiar spirit. a spirit consulted through a medium, forbidden by the LORD's law and by Saul's own edict Interpreted Editorial gloss, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes
  • Urim. one of the channels by which the LORD was enquired of, named alongside dreams and prophets, by which Saul now receives no answer Interpreted Editorial gloss, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes
