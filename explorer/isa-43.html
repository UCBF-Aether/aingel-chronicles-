The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 43 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 But now thus saith the LORD that created thee, O Jacob, and he that formed thee, O Israel, Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.

2 When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow thee: when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned; neither shall the flame kindle upon thee.

3 For I am the LORD thy God, the Holy One of Israel, thy Saviour: I gave Egypt for thy ransom, Ethiopia and Seba for thee.

4 Since thou wast precious in my sight, thou hast been honourable, and I have loved thee: therefore will I give men for thee, and people for thy life.

5 Fear not: for I am with thee: I will bring thy seed from the east, and gather thee from the west;

6 I will say to the north, Give up; and to the south, Keep not back: bring my sons from far, and my daughters from the ends of the earth;

7 Even every one that is called by my name: for I have created him for my glory, I have formed him; yea, I have made him.

8 Bring forth the blind people that have eyes, and the deaf that have ears.

9 Let all the nations be gathered together, and let the people be assembled: who among them can declare this, and shew us former things? let them bring forth their witnesses, that they may be justified: or let them hear, and say, It is truth.

10 Ye are my witnesses, saith the LORD, and my servant whom I have chosen: that ye may know and believe me, and understand that I am he: before me there was no God formed, neither shall there be after me.

11 I, even I, am the LORD; and beside me there is no saviour.

12 I have declared, and have saved, and I have shewed, when there was no strange god among you: therefore ye are my witnesses, saith the LORD, that I am God.

13 Yea, before the day was I am he; and there is none that can deliver out of my hand: I will work, and who shall let it?

14 Thus saith the LORD, your redeemer, the Holy One of Israel; For your sake I have sent to Babylon, and have brought down all their nobles, and the Chaldeans, whose cry is in the ships.

15 I am the LORD, your Holy One, the creator of Israel, your King.

16 Thus saith the LORD, which maketh a way in the sea, and a path in the mighty waters;

17 Which bringeth forth the chariot and horse, the army and the power; they shall lie down together, they shall not rise: they are extinct, they are quenched as tow.

18 Remember ye not the former things, neither consider the things of old.

19 Behold, I will do a new thing; now it shall spring forth; shall ye not know it? I will even make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert.

20 The beast of the field shall honour me, the dragons and the owls: because I give waters in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert, to give drink to my people, my chosen.

21 This people have I formed for myself; they shall shew forth my praise.

22 But thou hast not called upon me, O Jacob; but thou hast been weary of me, O Israel.

23 Thou hast not brought me the small cattle of thy burnt offerings; neither hast thou honoured me with thy sacrifices. I have not caused thee to serve with an offering, nor wearied thee with incense.

24 Thou hast bought me no sweet cane with money, neither hast thou filled me with the fat of thy sacrifices: but thou hast made me to serve with thy sins, thou hast wearied me with thine iniquities.

25 I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins.

26 Put me in remembrance: let us plead together: declare thou, that thou mayest be justified.

27 Thy first father hath sinned, and thy teachers have transgressed against me.

28 Therefore I have profaned the princes of the sanctuary, and have given Jacob to the curse, and Israel to reproaches.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Fear not, for I have redeemed you: through waters and fire God will be with his people, because they are his. Israel is called as God's witness against the idols, which can show nothing and foretell nothing, and the chapter ends with a promise to blot out transgressions for God's own sake and to make a new way in the wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. But now thus says the Lord that created you, O Jacob, and he that formed you, O Israel: Fear not, for I have redeemed you, I have called you by your name; you are mine. This chapter is a succession of arguments wherein Israel may be assured that, notwithstanding their perversity towards God in chapter 42 verse 25, He will deliver and restore them. But now means notwithstanding God's past just judgments for Israel's sins. Created means not only in the general sense, but specially created as a peculiar people unto Himself, as in verses 7, 15, and 21, and chapters 44. So believers, created in Christ Jesus, a peculiar people. Redeemed is a second argument why they should trust Him besides creation; the Hebrew means to ransom by a price paid in lieu of the captives, as in verse 3. Babylon was to be the ransom in this case, that is, was to be destroyed, in order that they might be delivered; so Christ became a curse, doomed to death, that we might be redeemed. Called by name means not merely called in general, as in chapters 42, 48, and 51, but designated as His own peculiar people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When you pass through the waters, I will be with you, and through the rivers, they will not overflow you; when you walk through the fire, you will not be burned, neither will the flame kindle upon you. Not overflow thee is so in passing Jordan, though at its overflow, when its swellings were especially dangerous. Waters and fire are a proverbial phrase for the extremest perils; literally fulfilled at the Red Sea, and in the case of the three youths cast into the fiery furnace for conscience' sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For I am the Lord your God, the Holy One of Israel, your Saviour; I gave Egypt for your ransom, Ethiopia and Seba for you. Egypt for thy ransom means either Egypt or Israel must perish; God chose that Egypt, though so much more mighty, should be destroyed, in order that His people might be delivered; thus Egypt stood, instead of Israel, as a kind of ransom. The Hebrew, kopher, means properly that with which anything is overlaid, as the pitch with which the ark was overlaid, hence that which covers over sins, an atonement. Nebuchadnezzar had subdued Egypt, Ethiopia, Hebrew Cush, and Saba, descended from Cush, probably Meroe of Ethiopia, a great island formed by the Astaboras and the Nile, conquered by Cambyses, successor of Cyrus. Cyrus received these from God with the rest of the Babylonian dominions, in consideration of his being about to deliver Israel. However, the reference may be to the three years' war in which Sargon overcame these countries, and so had his attention diverted from Israel, as in chapter 20. But the reference is probably more general, namely, to all the instances in which Jehovah sacrificed mighty heathen nations, when the safety of Israel required it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Since you were precious in my sight, you have been honourable, and I have loved you; therefore will I give men for you, and people for your life. Since means all along from the beginning, for there was never a time when Israel was not Jehovah's people; the apodosis should be at, I will give: Since ever thou wast precious in My sight, honourable, and that I loved thee, I will give, etc. Gesenius, as the English version, takes Since to mean, Inasmuch as. If the apodosis be as in the English version, Since thou wast precious will refer to the time when God called His people out of Egypt, manifesting then first the love which He had from everlasting towards them; honourable and loved refer to outward marks of honour and love from God. Men and people mean other nations for thee, as in verse 3. Thy life means thy person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Fear not, for I am with you; I will bring your seed from the east, and gather you from the west. This recalls Deuteronomy 30. Seed means descendants scattered in all lands; some understand it of the spiritual seed of the church produced by mystical regeneration, for the expression is, bring, not bring back. This sense is perhaps included, but not to the exclusion of the literal Israel's restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. I will say to the north: Give up, and to the south: Keep not back; bring my sons from far, and my daughters from the ends of the earth. Give up means namely, My people. Sons and daughters means the feminine joined to the masculine expresses the complete totality of anything. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Even every one that is called by my name, for I have created him for my glory, I have formed him, yes, I have made him. Called by my name means belong to Israel, whose people, as sons of God, bear the name of their Father. For my glory recalls verses 21 and chapter 29. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Bring forth the blind people that have eyes, and the deaf that have ears. This is a solemn challenge given by God to the nations to argue with Him the question of His superiority to their idols, and His power to deliver Israel, as in chapter 41 verse 1. Blind people are the Gentiles, who also, like Israel in chapter 42 verse 19, are blind spiritually, though having eyes, that is, natural faculties, whereby they might know God; or else, the Jews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Let all the nations be gathered together, and let the people be assembled; who among them can declare this, and show us former things? Let them bring forth their witnesses, that they may be justified, or let them hear, and say: It is truth. Can declare this means who among the idolatrous soothsayers hath predicted this, that is, as to Cyrus being the deliverer of Israel? Former means predictions, as in chapter 42 verse 9, or things that shall first come to pass, as in chapter 41. Let them bring forth their witnesses means as I do mine in verse 10. Justified means declared veracious in their pretended prophecies. Or means rather, and; let men hear their prediction and say, from the event, It is verified, as in chapter 41 verse 26. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. You are my witnesses, says the Lord, and my servant whom I have chosen, that you may know and believe me, and understand that I am he; before me there was no God formed, neither will there be after me. Ye are the Jews, to whom I have given predictions, verified by the event, and in delivering whom I have so often manifested My power, as in verses 3 and 4 and chapter 44. And my servant means that is, the whole Jewish people, as in chapter 41 verse 8. Believe means trust in. Formed means before I existed none of the false gods were formed; formed applies to the idols, not to God. Revelation uses the same language to prove the Godhead of Jesus, as Isaiah here to prove the Godhead of Jehovah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. I, even I, am the Lord, and beside me there is no saviour. Lord is Jehovah. Saviour is temporally, from Babylon, and eternally, from sin and hell; the same titles as are applied to God are applied to Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. I have declared, and have saved, and I have showed, when there was no strange god among you; therefore you are my witnesses, says the Lord, that I am God. Declared means predicted the future, as in chapter 41. Saved means the nation, in past times of danger. Showed means namely, that I was God. When no strange god, etc., means to whom the predictions uttered by Me could be assigned; strange means foreign, introduced from abroad. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Yes, before the day was I am he, and there is none that can deliver out of my hand; I will work, and who will let it? Before means literally, from the time of the first existence of day. Let is Old English for hinder, as in chapter 14; rather, translate, undo it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Thus says the Lord, your redeemer, the Holy One of Israel: For your sake I have sent to Babylon, and have brought down all their nobles, and the Chaldeans, whose cry is in the ships. Sent means namely, the Medes and Persians. Brought down means made to go down to the sea, as in chapter 42 verse 10, in order to escape the impending destruction of Babylon. Nobles are rather fugitives, namely, the foreigners who sojourned in populous Babylon, distinct from the Chaldeans. Whose cry is in the ships means exulting in their ships with the joyous sailors, cry, boastingly; their joy heretofore in their ships contrasts sadly with their present panic in fleeing to them. Babylon was on the Euphrates, which was joined to the Tigris by a canal, and flowed into the Persian Gulf; thus it was famed for ships and commerce until the Persian monarchs, to prevent revolt or invasion, obstructed navigation by dams across the Tigris and Euphrates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. I am the Lord, your Holy One, the creator of Israel, your King. Creator of Israel recalls verse 1. Your means proved to be specially yours by delivering you. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Thus says the Lord, which makes a way in the sea, and a path in the mighty waters. This, with verse 17, alludes to the deliverance of Israel and overthrow of Pharaoh in the Red Sea, the standing illustration of God's unchanging character towards His people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Which brings forth the chariot and horse, the army and the power; they will lie down together, they will not rise; they are extinct, they are quenched as tow. This, with verse 16, alludes to the deliverance of Israel and overthrow of Pharaoh in the Red Sea, the standing illustration of God's unchanging character towards His people. The power means the might of the enemies' host, every mighty warrior. They shall lie down together means as Pharaoh's army sank together in a watery grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Remember not the former things, neither consider the things of old. So wonderful shall be God's future interpositions in your behalf, that all past ones shall be forgotten in comparison; plainly the future restoration of Israel is the event ultimately meant. Thus the former things are such events as the destruction of Sennacherib and the return from Babylon. Things of old are events still more ancient, the deliverance from Egypt and at the Red Sea, and entry into Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Behold, I will do a new thing; now it will spring forth; will you not know it? I will even make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert. New means unprecedented in its wonderful character, as in chapter 42 verse 9. Spring forth means as a germinating herb: a beautiful image of the silent but certain gradual growth of events in God's providence. Way in the wilderness means just as Israel in the wilderness, between the Red Sea and Canaan, was guided, and supplied with water by Jehovah; but the new deliverance shall be attended with manifestations of God's power and love, eclipsing the old, as in chapter 41. I will open a way, not merely in the Red Sea, but in the wilderness of the whole world; and not merely one river shall gush out of the rock, but many, which shall refresh, not the bodies as formerly, but the souls of the thirsty, so that the prophecy shall be fulfilled: With joy shall ye draw water out of the wells of salvation. A way often stands for the true religion. Rivers express the influences of the Holy Spirit. Israel's literal restoration hereafter is included, as appears by comparing chapters 11. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The beast of the field will honour me, the dragons and the owls, because I give waters in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert, to give drink to my people, my chosen. Beast is an image of idolaters, defiled with blood and pollutions, dwelling like dragons, etc., in the wastes of Gentile ignorance: even they shall be converted. Or else, literally, such copious floods of water shall be given by God in the desert, that the very beasts shall, in poetic language, praise the Lord. Dragons are serpents, or else jackals, as in chapter 13. Owls are rather ostriches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. This people have I formed for myself; they will show forth my praise. This people means namely, the same as My people, My chosen, as in verses 1 and 7. My praise means on account of the many and great benefits conferred on them, especially their restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. But you have not called upon me, O Jacob, but you have been weary of me, O Israel. But means Israel, however, is not to think that these divine favours are due to their own piety towards God; so the believer. But is rather, for. Weary of me recalls Amos and Malachi, though I have not wearied thee in verse 23, yet thou hast been weary of Me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. You have not brought me the small cattle of your burnt offerings, neither have you honoured me with your sacrifices. I have not caused you to serve with an offering, nor wearied you with incense. Small cattle are rather the lamb or kid, required by the law to be daily offered to God. Sacrifices means offered any way, whereas the Hebrew for holocaust, or burnt offering, denotes that which ascends as an offering consumed by fire. I have not caused thee to serve means that is, to render the service of a slave. Offering is bloodless. Wearied is antithetical to verse 22, Thou hast been weary of Me. Though God in the law required such offerings, yet not so as to weary the worshipper, or to exact them in cases where, as in the Babylonish captivity, they were physically unable to render them; God did not require them, save in subordination to the higher moral duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. You have bought me no sweet cane with money, neither have you filled me with the fat of your sacrifices, but you have made me to serve with your sins, you have wearied me with your iniquities. Bought means for sweet cane, aromatic calamus, was not indigenous to Palestine, but had to be bought from foreign countries; it was used among the Hebrews to make the sacred ointment. It is often offered as a mark of hospitality. Filled means satiated; God deigns to use human language to adapt Himself to human modes of thought. Made me to serve means though I have not caused thee to serve in verse 23; our sin made the Son of God to become a servant. He served to save us from servile bondage. Wearied me means though I have not wearied thee, as in verse 23 and chapter 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. I, even I, am he that blots out your transgressions for my own sake, and will not remember your sins. I, even I means the God against whom your sin is committed, and who alone can and will pardon, as in chapter 44. For mine own sake recalls chapter 48. How abominable a thing sin is, since it is against such a God of grace! Blotted out is an image from an account-book, in which, when a debt is paid, the charge is cancelled or blotted out. Not remember sins means, as in Jeremiah, when God forgives, He forgets; that is, treats the sinner as if He had forgotten his sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Put me in remembrance; let us plead together; declare you, that you may be justified. Put me in remembrance means remind Me of every plea which thou hast to urge before Me in thy defence; image from a trial, as in chapters 1 and 41. Our strongest plea is to remind God of His own promises, so Jacob did at Mahanaim and Peniel. God, then, instead of pleading against us with His great power, will put His strength in us; we thus become the Lord's remembrancers. Declare God's righteousness vindicated in Jesus Christ that thou mayest be justified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Your first father has sinned, and your teachers have transgressed against me. First father is collectively for most ancient ancestors, as the parallelism, teachers, proves; or, thy chief religious ministers or priests. Adam, the common father of all nations, can hardly be meant here, as it would have been irrelevant to mention his sin in an address to the Jews specially; Abraham is equally out of place here, as he is everywhere cited as an example of faithfulness, not of sin. However, taking the passage in its ultimate application to the church at large, Adam may be meant. Teachers are literally interpreters between God and man, the priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Therefore I have profaned the princes of the sanctuary, and have given Jacob to the curse, and Israel to reproaches. Profaned the princes recalls Psalm 89 and Lamentations: I have esteemed, or treated, them as persons not sacred. I have left them to suffer the same treatment as the common people, stripped of their holy office and in captivity. Princes of the sanctuary are governors of it, directing its holy services, priests. Curse is Hebrew, cherim, a solemn anathema, or excommunication. Reproaches recalls Psalm 123. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Before means literally, from the time of the first existence of day. Let is Old English for hinder, as in chapter 14; rather, translate, undo it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Thus says the Lord, your redeemer, the Holy One of Israel: For your sake I have sent to Babylon, and have brought down all their nobles, and the Chaldeans, whose cry is in the ships. Sent means namely, the Medes and Persians. Brought down means made to go down to the sea, as in chapter 42 verse 10, in order to escape the impending destruction of Babylon. Nobles are rather fugitives, namely, the foreigners who sojourned in populous Babylon, distinct from the Chaldeans. Whose cry is in the ships means exulting in their ships with the joyous sailors, cry, boastingly; their joy heretofore in their ships contrasts sadly with their present panic in fleeing to them. Babylon was on the Euphrates, which was joined to the Tigris by a canal, and flowed into the Persian Gulf; thus it was famed for ships and commerce until the Persian monarchs, to prevent revolt or invasion, obstructed navigation by dams across the Tigris and Euphrates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. I am the Lord, your Holy One, the creator of Israel, your King. Creator of Israel recalls verse 1. Your means proved to be specially yours by delivering you. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Thus says the Lord, which makes a way in the sea, and a path in the mighty waters. This, with verse 17, alludes to the deliverance of Israel and overthrow of Pharaoh in the Red Sea, the standing illustration of God's unchanging character towards His people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Which brings forth the chariot and horse, the army and the power; they will lie down together, they will not rise; they are extinct, they are quenched as tow. This, with verse 16, alludes to the deliverance of Israel and overthrow of Pharaoh in the Red Sea, the standing illustration of God's unchanging character towards His people. The power means the might of the enemies' host, every mighty warrior. They shall lie down together means as Pharaoh's army sank together in a watery grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Remember not the former things, neither consider the things of old. So wonderful shall be God's future interpositions in your behalf, that all past ones shall be forgotten in comparison; plainly the future restoration of Israel is the event ultimately meant. Thus the former things are such events as the destruction of Sennacherib and the return from Babylon. Things of old are events still more ancient, the deliverance from Egypt and at the Red Sea, and entry into Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Behold, I will do a new thing; now it will spring forth; will you not know it? I will even make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert. New means unprecedented in its wonderful character, as in chapter 42 verse 9. Spring forth means as a germinating herb: a beautiful image of the silent but certain gradual growth of events in God's providence. Way in the wilderness means just as Israel in the wilderness, between the Red Sea and Canaan, was guided, and supplied with water by Jehovah; but the new deliverance shall be attended with manifestations of God's power and love, eclipsing the old, as in chapter 41. I will open a way, not merely in the Red Sea, but in the wilderness of the whole world; and not merely one river shall gush out of the rock, but many, which shall refresh, not the bodies as formerly, but the souls of the thirsty, so that the prophecy shall be fulfilled: With joy shall ye draw water out of the wells of salvation. A way often stands for the true religion. Rivers express the influences of the Holy Spirit. Israel's literal restoration hereafter is included, as appears by comparing chapters 11. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The beast of the field will honour me, the dragons and the owls, because I give waters in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert, to give drink to my people, my chosen. Beast is an image of idolaters, defiled with blood and pollutions, dwelling like dragons, etc., in the wastes of Gentile ignorance: even they shall be converted. Or else, literally, such copious floods of water shall be given by God in the desert, that the very beasts shall, in poetic language, praise the Lord. Dragons are serpents, or else jackals, as in chapter 13. Owls are rather ostriches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. This people have I formed for myself; they will show forth my praise. This people means namely, the same as My people, My chosen, as in verses 1 and 7. My praise means on account of the many and great benefits conferred on them, especially their restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. But you have not called upon me, O Jacob, but you have been weary of me, O Israel. But means Israel, however, is not to think that these divine favours are due to their own piety towards God; so the believer. But is rather, for. Weary of me recalls Amos and Malachi, though I have not wearied thee in verse 23, yet thou hast been weary of Me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. You have not brought me the small cattle of your burnt offerings, neither have you honoured me with your sacrifices. I have not caused you to serve with an offering, nor wearied you with incense. Small cattle are rather the lamb or kid, required by the law to be daily offered to God. Sacrifices means offered any way, whereas the Hebrew for holocaust, or burnt offering, denotes that which ascends as an offering consumed by fire. I have not caused thee to serve means that is, to render the service of a slave. Offering is bloodless. Wearied is antithetical to verse 22, Thou hast been weary of Me. Though God in the law required such offerings, yet not so as to weary the worshipper, or to exact them in cases where, as in the Babylonish captivity, they were physically unable to render them; God did not require them, save in subordination to the higher moral duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. You have bought me no sweet cane with money, neither have you filled me with the fat of your sacrifices, but you have made me to serve with your sins, you have wearied me with your iniquities. Bought means for sweet cane, aromatic calamus, was not indigenous to Palestine, but had to be bought from foreign countries; it was used among the Hebrews to make the sacred ointment. It is often offered as a mark of hospitality. Filled means satiated; God deigns to use human language to adapt Himself to human modes of thought. Made me to serve means though I have not caused thee to serve in verse 23; our sin made the Son of God to become a servant. He served to save us from servile bondage. Wearied me means though I have not wearied thee, as in verse 23 and chapter 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. I, even I, am he that blots out your transgressions for my own sake, and will not remember your sins. I, even I means the God against whom your sin is committed, and who alone can and will pardon, as in chapter 44. For mine own sake recalls chapter 48. How abominable a thing sin is, since it is against such a God of grace! Blotted out is an image from an account-book, in which, when a debt is paid, the charge is cancelled or blotted out. Not remember sins means, as in Jeremiah, when God forgives, He forgets; that is, treats the sinner as if He had forgotten his sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Put me in remembrance; let us plead together; declare you, that you may be justified. Put me in remembrance means remind Me of every plea which thou hast to urge before Me in thy defence; image from a trial, as in chapters 1 and 41. Our strongest plea is to remind God of His own promises, so Jacob did at Mahanaim and Peniel. God, then, instead of pleading against us with His great power, will put His strength in us; we thus become the Lord's remembrancers. Declare God's righteousness vindicated in Jesus Christ that thou mayest be justified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Your first father has sinned, and your teachers have transgressed against me. First father is collectively for most ancient ancestors, as the parallelism, teachers, proves; or, thy chief religious ministers or priests. Adam, the common father of all nations, can hardly be meant here, as it would have been irrelevant to mention his sin in an address to the Jews specially; Abraham is equally out of place here, as he is everywhere cited as an example of faithfulness, not of sin. However, taking the passage in its ultimate application to the church at large, Adam may be meant. Teachers are literally interpreters between God and man, the priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Therefore I have profaned the princes of the sanctuary, and have given Jacob to the curse, and Israel to reproaches. Profaned the princes recalls Psalm 89 and Lamentations: I have esteemed, or treated, them as persons not sacred. I have left them to suffer the same treatment as the common people, stripped of their holy office and in captivity. Princes of the sanctuary are governors of it, directing its holy services, priests. Curse is Hebrew, cherim, a solemn anathema, or excommunication. Reproaches recalls Psalm 123. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
