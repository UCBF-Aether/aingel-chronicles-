The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And after Abimelech there arose to defend Israel Tola the son of Puah, the son of Dodo, a man of Issachar; and he dwelt in Shamir in mount Ephraim.

2 And he judged Israel twenty and three years, and died, and was buried in Shamir.

3 And after him arose Jair, a Gileadite, and judged Israel twenty and two years.

4 And he had thirty sons that rode on thirty ass colts, and they had thirty cities, which are called Havothjair unto this day, which are in the land of Gilead.

5 And Jair died, and was buried in Camon.

6 And the children of Israel did evil again in the sight of the LORD, and served Baalim, and Ashtaroth, and the gods of Syria, and the gods of Zidon, and the gods of Moab, and the gods of the children of Ammon, and the gods of the Philistines, and forsook the LORD, and served not him.

7 And the anger of the LORD was hot against Israel, and he sold them into the hands of the Philistines, and into the hands of the children of Ammon.

8 And that year they vexed and oppressed the children of Israel: eighteen years, all the children of Israel that were on the other side Jordan in the land of the Amorites, which is in Gilead.

9 Moreover the children of Ammon passed over Jordan to fight also against Judah, and against Benjamin, and against the house of Ephraim; so that Israel was sore distressed.

10 And the children of Israel cried unto the LORD, saying, We have sinned against thee, both because we have forsaken our God, and also served Baalim.

11 And the LORD said unto the children of Israel, Did not I deliver you from the Egyptians, and from the Amorites, from the children of Ammon, and from the Philistines?

12 The Zidonians also, and the Amalekites, and the Maonites, did oppress you; and ye cried to me, and I delivered you out of their hand.

13 Yet ye have forsaken me, and served other gods: wherefore I will deliver you no more.

14 Go and cry unto the gods which ye have chosen; let them deliver you in the time of your tribulation.

15 And the children of Israel said unto the LORD, We have sinned: do thou unto us whatsoever seemeth good unto thee; deliver us only, we pray thee, this day.

16 And they put away the strange gods from among them, and served the LORD: and his soul was grieved for the misery of Israel.

17 Then the children of Ammon were gathered together, and encamped in Gilead. And the children of Israel assembled themselves together, and encamped in Mizpeh.

18 And the people and princes of Gilead said one to another, What man is he that will begin to fight against the children of Ammon? he shall be head over all the inhabitants of Gilead.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After Abimelech, Tola judges twenty-three years and Jair twenty-two, the latter remembered for thirty sons riding thirty ass colts over thirty cities called Havoth-jair. Then Israel's apostasy grows wider than before, serving the gods of Syria, Zidon, Moab, Ammon and Philistia, and Philistine and Ammonite oppression lasts eighteen years. Israel confesses, is told to cry to the gods it chose, confesses again, puts away the strange gods, and gathers at Mizpeh asking who will begin the fight against Ammon and become head over Gilead. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After Abimelech there arose Tola son of Puah to defend Israel, and JFB notes deliverance was necessary from intestine usurpation as well as foreign aggression, and that Tola, whose name means to save, was Gideon's brother by the father's side though of Issachar while Gideon was of Manasseh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Tola judged Israel twenty-three years, died, and was buried in Shamir, having made that central place in Mount Ephraim the seat of government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. After him arose Jair the Gileadite, and JFB distinguishes this judge from the earlier conqueror and founder of Havoth-jair, Jair's villages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Jair had thirty sons who rode on thirty ass colts and had thirty cities called Havoth-jair, and JFB notes this as a trait of Eastern manners and a proof of his extensive possessions, not evidence of many wives at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Jair judged Israel twenty-two years, died, and was buried in Camon, closing a quiet judgeship between two storms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Israel again did evil and served Baalim, Ashtaroth, and the gods of Syria, Zidon, Moab, Ammon, and Philistia, forsaking the Lord, and JFB notes this apostasy exceeded every former one in grossness and universality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The Lord's anger was hot, and he sold them into the Philistines and Ammon, whose incursions fell naturally on the lands contiguous to them, while Ammon, animated by conquest, crossed the Jordan and desolated the central and southern provinces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. That year they vexed and oppressed Israel eighteen years, all Israel beyond Jordan in the land of the Amorites in Gilead, a long oppression matching the wide apostasy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The Ammonites also passed over Jordan to fight Judah, Benjamin, and Ephraim, so that Israel was sore distressed on both sides of the river. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Israel cried to the Lord, saying, We have sinned against thee, because we have forsaken our God and served Baalim, and JFB notes the first step of repentance is confession, proved sincere when the sinner mourns the evil against God, not only its painful consequences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The Lord answered by rehearsing deliverances from Egyptians, Amorites, Ammonites, Philistines, Zidonians, Amalekites, and Maonites, and JFB notes these circumstances were probably not declared through the high priest, and that Maonites means Midianites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. When Israel cried in those oppressions, God delivered them, and the rehearsal makes the present complaint answerable by the whole history of rescue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Yet ye have forsaken me and served other gods, the Lord said, so that he would deliver them no more, and the refusal is the just answer to repeated forsaking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Go and cry to the gods ye have chosen, the Lord said, let them deliver you in your tribulation, sending Israel to the gods whose service had brought the tribulation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Israel answered, We have sinned, do thou unto us whatsoever seemeth good unto thee, deliver us only this day, a confession that leaves the punishment to God while begging present deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. They put away the strange gods and served the Lord, and his soul was grieved for Israel's misery, and JFB notes that on abandoning idolatry and returning to pure worship, God graciously abridged the affliction and restored peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The Ammonites gathered at Gilead while Israel assembled at Mizpeh, and JFB notes Ammon had moved from guerrilla warfare to a continued campaign aiming to wrest all trans-Jordanic territory, meeting a general tribal gathering at Mizpeh in eastern Manasseh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The people and princes of Gilead asked who would begin the fight against Ammon and become head over all Gilead, so that the crisis forced Israel to choose a captain before it could field an army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
