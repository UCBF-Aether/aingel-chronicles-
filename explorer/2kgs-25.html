The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 25 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, in the tenth day of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came, he, and all his host, against Jerusalem, and pitched against it; and they built forts against it round about.

2 And the city was besieged unto the eleventh year of king Zedekiah.

3 And on the ninth day of the fourth month the famine prevailed in the city, and there was no bread for the people of the land.

4 And the city was broken up, and all the men of war fled by night by the way of the gate between two walls, which is by the king’s garden: (now the Chaldees were against the city round about:) and the king went the way toward the plain.

5 And the army of the Chaldees pursued after the king, and overtook him in the plains of Jericho: and all his army were scattered from him.

6 So they took the king, and brought him up to the king of Babylon to Riblah; and they gave judgment upon him.

7 And they slew the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes, and put out the eyes of Zedekiah, and bound him with fetters of brass, and carried him to Babylon.

8 And in the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month, which is the nineteenth year of king Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, came Nebuzaradan, captain of the guard, a servant of the king of Babylon, unto Jerusalem:

9 And he burnt the house of the LORD, and the king’s house, and all the houses of Jerusalem, and every great man’s house burnt he with fire.

10 And all the army of the Chaldees, that were with the captain of the guard, brake down the walls of Jerusalem round about.

11 Now the rest of the people that were left in the city, and the fugitives that fell away to the king of Babylon, with the remnant of the multitude, did Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard carry away.

12 But the captain of the guard left of the poor of the land to be vinedressers and husbandmen.

13 And the pillars of brass that were in the house of the LORD, and the bases, and the brasen sea that was in the house of the LORD, did the Chaldees break in pieces, and carried the brass of them to Babylon.

14 And the pots, and the shovels, and the snuffers, and the spoons, and all the vessels of brass wherewith they ministered, took they away.

15 And the firepans, and the bowls, and such things as were of gold, in gold, and of silver, in silver, the captain of the guard took away.

16 The two pillars, one sea, and the bases which Solomon had made for the house of the LORD; the brass of all these vessels was without weight.

17 The height of the one pillar was eighteen cubits, and the chapiter upon it was brass: and the height of the chapiter three cubits; and the wreathen work, and pomegranates upon the chapiter round about, all of brass: and like unto these had the second pillar with wreathen work.

18 And the captain of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, and Zephaniah the second priest, and the three keepers of the door:

19 And out of the city he took an officer that was set over the men of war, and five men of them that were in the king’s presence, which were found in the city, and the principal scribe of the host, which mustered the people of the land, and threescore men of the people of the land that were found in the city:

20 And Nebuzaradan captain of the guard took these, and brought them to the king of Babylon to Riblah:

21 And the king of Babylon smote them, and slew them at Riblah in the land of Hamath. So Judah was carried away out of their land.

22 And as for the people that remained in the land of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had left, even over them he made Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, the son of Shaphan, ruler.

23 And when all the captains of the armies, they and their men, heard that the king of Babylon had made Gedaliah governor, there came to Gedaliah to Mizpah, even Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, and Johanan the son of Careah, and Seraiah the son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite, and Jaazaniah the son of a Maachathite, they and their men.

24 And Gedaliah sware to them, and to their men, and said unto them, Fear not to be the servants of the Chaldees: dwell in the land, and serve the king of Babylon; and it shall be well with you.

25 But it came to pass in the seventh month, that Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, the son of Elishama, of the seed royal, came, and ten men with him, and smote Gedaliah, that he died, and the Jews and the Chaldees that were with him at Mizpah.

26 And all the people, both small and great, and the captains of the armies, arose, and came to Egypt: for they were afraid of the Chaldees.

27 And it came to pass in the seven and thirtieth year of the captivity of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the seven and twentieth day of the month, that Evilmerodach king of Babylon in the year that he began to reign did lift up the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah out of prison;

28 And he spake kindly to him, and set his throne above the throne of the kings that were with him in Babylon;

29 And changed his prison garments: and he did eat bread continually before him all the days of his life.

30 And his allowance was a continual allowance given him of the king, a daily rate for every day, all the days of his life.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jerusalem falls: after a siege of famine the city is breached, Zedekiah flees, is blinded and bound, and Nebuzaradan burns the temple, the palace, and the houses, and carries the people away. The book ends in exile, except for a last, quiet mercy: in Babylon, Evil-merodach lifts Jehoiachin from prison and seats him at the king's table. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the ninth year of Zedekiah's reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth day, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came with all his host against Jerusalem, pitched against it, and built forts round about. JFB explains that, incensed by Zedekiah's revolt, the despot determined to end Judah's perfidious monarchy in his third and last invasion, conducted in person with an immense army, having taken almost all the fenced cities before marching direct to invest Jerusalem, and building lines of circumvallation with a ditch and engines for throwing missiles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The city was besieged until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah. JFB notes that, with a brief interruption when Nebuchadnezzar marched against relieving Egyptians who retreated without fighting, the siege lasted a year and a half, the long resistance owing not to Jewish skill or valour but to the city's fortifications, on which the king too confidently relied. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. On the ninth day of the fourth month the famine prevailed in the city, with no bread for the land's people. JFB explains that under the close, protracted blockade the inhabitants were reduced to dreadful extremities, perpetrating inhuman atrocities under hunger's maddening influence, fulfilling prophetic denunciations against the chosen people's apostasy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The city was broken up, and all the men of war fled by night by the gate between two walls by the king's garden, while the Chaldees were round about the city, and the king went toward the plain. JFB explains a breach was effected in the lower city's wall, and the king's garden lay at Siloam's pool at the Tyropoeon valley's mouth, where the besiegers had probably overlooked this pass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Chaldees' army pursued the king and overtook him in Jericho's plains, and all his army was scattered from him. JFB's section on Zedekiah's capture covers this pursuit and scattering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They took the king and brought him up to Babylon's king at Riblah, where they gave judgment upon him. JFB explains Nebuchadnezzar, having left the siege to oppose Pharaohhophra, stationed himself at Riblah in Hamath, where his council condemned Zedekiah as a seditious vassal for violating his oath and neglecting Jeremiah's announcements of God's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They slew Zedekiah's sons before his eyes, put out his eyes, bound him with fetters of brass, and carried him to Babylon. JFB notes his sons and the nobles who joined his flight were slain before his eyes, and, in Eastern ideas deeming a blind man incapable of ruling, his eyes were put out and he was chained to perpetual imprisonment in Babylon, which, as Ezekiel foretold, he came to but did not see. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. In the fifth month, on the seventh day, in Nebuchadnezzar's nineteenth year, Nebuzaradan, captain of the guard and servant of Babylon's king, came to Jerusalem. JFB explains Nebuzaradan set out on the seventh but may not have arrived or executed orders until the tenth, and his office as captain of the guard called him to execute justice, so he was dispatched to rase the city, plunder the temple, lay both in ruins, demolish fortifications, and transport the inhabitants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Nebuzaradan burnt the LORD's house, the king's house, and all Jerusalem's houses, burning every great man's house with fire. JFB notes he was dispatched to rase the city and plunder the temple, laying both in ruins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. All the Chaldees' army with the captain of the guard broke down Jerusalem's walls round about. JFB includes demolishing the fortifications among Nebuzaradan's orders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Nebuzaradan carried away the rest of the people left in the city, the fugitives who had fallen to Babylon's king, and the remnant of the multitude. JFB notes he was dispatched to transport the inhabitants to Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. But the captain of the guard left some of the poor of the land to be vinedressers and husbandmen. JFB notes those permitted to remain were only the peasantry who could till the land and dress the vineyards, besides the king's daughters and a few attendants too insignificant to remove. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The captain of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest, and the three keepers of the door. JFB notes the most eminent captives were taken to the king at Riblah and executed as instigators of rebellion, among them Seraiah the high priest, grandfather of Ezra, and his deputy, while the three door-keepers were not mere porters but officers of high trust among the Levites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Out of the city he took an officer over the men of war, five men of the king's presence, the principal scribe of the host who mustered the land's people, and threescore men of the land's people found in the city. JFB explains the five men of the king's presence belonged to the royal retinue, probably five at first with two more found afterwards. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Nebuzaradan took these and brought them to Babylon's king at Riblah. JFB notes the most eminent captives were taken to the king at Riblah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Babylon's king smote them and slew them at Riblah in Hamath, and so Judah was carried away out of their land. JFB notes they were executed as instigators and abettors of the rebellion or otherwise obnoxious to the Assyrian government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. As for the people remaining in Judah's land, whom Nebuchadnezzar had left, he made Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, ruler over them. JFB notes Gedaliah was Jeremiah's friend, who, by the prophet's counsel, probably fled the city as abandoned of God, surrendered to the conqueror, and, promoted to Judea's government, fixed his court at Mizpeh, well qualified to rule at such a crisis. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. When all the captains of the armies and their men heard Babylon's king had made Gedaliah governor, they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah, with Ishmael, Johanan, Seraiah, and Jaazaniah. JFB notes many fugitive Jews and Zedekiah's soldiers left their retreats and flocked around the governor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Gedaliah swore to them and their men, saying, "Fear not to be the servants of the Chaldees: dwell in the land, and serve Babylon's king, and it shall be well with you." JFB notes Gedaliah counselled submission, promising on oath that, complying, they would retain their possessions and enjoy their land's produce. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. In the seventh month Ishmael son of Nethaniah, of the seed royal, came with ten men and smote Gedaliah, so that he died, along with the Jews and Chaldees who were with him at Mizpah. JFB explains Ishmael had found refuge with Baalis king of the Ammonites, returning with a bad design, either from envy of a governor not descended from David or bribed by Baalis, and the generous governor, though warned, refused to credit the report or sanction cutting Ishmael off, so he was murdered while entertaining Ishmael in his own house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. All the people, small and great, with the captains of the armies, arose and came to Egypt, for fear of the Chaldees. JFB notes they settled in various Egyptian cities despite Jeremiah's dissuasions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. In the thirty-seventh year of Jehoiachin's captivity, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-seventh day, Evilmerodach king of Babylon, in the year he began to reign, lifted up Jehoiachin king of Judah's head out of prison. JFB notes this year corresponded with Nebuchadnezzar's death and Evilmerodach's accession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Evilmerodach spoke kindly to Jehoiachin and set his throne above the throne of the kings with him in Babylon. JFB explains he gave him liberty upon parole, a kindly feeling said to have originated in prison acquaintance, since Evilmerodach had lain in prison till his father's death, but doubtless traced to the overruling providence and grace of God, who still cherished purposes of love to David's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Jehoiachin changed his prison garments and ate bread continually before the king all the days of his life. JFB notes that by ancient Eastern court usage he had a seat at the royal table on great days and a stated provision for his exiled court's maintenance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. His allowance was a continual allowance given him by the king, a daily rate for every day, all the days of his life. JFB's note on his continual eating before the king covers this stated daily provision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
