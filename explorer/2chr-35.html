The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 35 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover Josiah kept a passover unto the LORD in Jerusalem: and they killed the passover on the fourteenth day of the first month.

2 And he set the priests in their charges, and encouraged them to the service of the house of the LORD,

3 And said unto the Levites that taught all Israel, which were holy unto the LORD, Put the holy ark in the house which Solomon the son of David king of Israel did build; it shall not be a burden upon your shoulders: serve now the LORD your God, and his people Israel,

4 And prepare yourselves by the houses of your fathers, after your courses, according to the writing of David king of Israel, and according to the writing of Solomon his son.

5 And stand in the holy place according to the divisions of the families of the fathers of your brethren the people, and after the division of the families of the Levites.

6 So kill the passover, and sanctify yourselves, and prepare your brethren, that they may do according to the word of the LORD by the hand of Moses.

7 And Josiah gave to the people, of the flock, lambs and kids, all for the passover offerings, for all that were present, to the number of thirty thousand, and three thousand bullocks: these were of the king’s substance.

8 And his princes gave willingly unto the people, to the priests, and to the Levites: Hilkiah and Zechariah and Jehiel, rulers of the house of God, gave unto the priests for the passover offerings two thousand and six hundred small cattle and three hundred oxen.

9 Conaniah also, and Shemaiah and Nethaneel, his brethren, and Hashabiah and Jeiel and Jozabad, chief of the Levites, gave unto the Levites for passover offerings five thousand small cattle, and five hundred oxen.

10 So the service was prepared, and the priests stood in their place, and the Levites in their courses, according to the king’s commandment.

11 And they killed the passover, and the priests sprinkled the blood from their hands, and the Levites flayed them.

12 And they removed the burnt offerings, that they might give according to the divisions of the families of the people, to offer unto the LORD, as it is written in the book of Moses. And so did they with the oxen.

13 And they roasted the passover with fire according to the ordinance: but the other holy offerings sod they in pots, and in caldrons, and in pans, and divided them speedily among all the people.

14 And afterward they made ready for themselves, and for the priests: because the priests the sons of Aaron were busied in offering of burnt offerings and the fat until night; therefore the Levites prepared for themselves, and for the priests the sons of Aaron.

15 And the singers the sons of Asaph were in their place, according to the commandment of David, and Asaph, and Heman, and Jeduthun the king’s seer; and the porters waited at every gate; they might not depart from their service; for their brethren the Levites prepared for them.

16 So all the service of the LORD was prepared the same day, to keep the passover, and to offer burnt offerings upon the altar of the LORD, according to the commandment of king Josiah.

17 And the children of Israel that were present kept the passover at that time, and the feast of unleavened bread seven days.

18 And there was no passover like to that kept in Israel from the days of Samuel the prophet; neither did all the kings of Israel keep such a passover as Josiah kept, and the priests, and the Levites, and all Judah and Israel that were present, and the inhabitants of Jerusalem.

19 In the eighteenth year of the reign of Josiah was this passover kept.

20 After all this, when Josiah had prepared the temple, Necho king of Egypt came up to fight against Charchemish by Euphrates: and Josiah went out against him.

21 But he sent ambassadors to him, saying, What have I to do with thee, thou king of Judah? I come not against thee this day, but against the house wherewith I have war: for God commanded me to make haste: forbear thee from meddling with God, who is with me, that he destroy thee not.

22 Nevertheless Josiah would not turn his face from him, but disguised himself, that he might fight with him, and hearkened not unto the words of Necho from the mouth of God, and came to fight in the valley of Megiddo.

23 And the archers shot at king Josiah; and the king said to his servants, Have me away; for I am sore wounded.

24 His servants therefore took him out of that chariot, and put him in the second chariot that he had; and they brought him to Jerusalem, and he died, and was buried in one of the sepulchres of his fathers. And all Judah and Jerusalem mourned for Josiah.

25 And Jeremiah lamented for Josiah: and all the singing men and the singing women spake of Josiah in their lamentations to this day, and made them an ordinance in Israel: and, behold, they are written in the lamentations.

26 Now the rest of the acts of Josiah, and his goodness, according to that which was written in the law of the LORD,

27 And his deeds, first and last, behold, they are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Josiah's great passover, kept with exactness by priests and Levites, and his death at Megiddo at Pharaoh-necho's hand, lamented by Jeremiah and the singers. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Moreover Josiah kept a passover unto the Lord in Jerusalem, and they killed the passover on the fourteenth day of the first month. Henry notes Josiah's solemnizing the passover, touched upon in Kings, is very particularly related here, for the passover was the chief feast, beginning them all in Egypt's exodus night and concluding them in Christ's betrayal night, and in its celebration Hezekiah and Josiah, two great reformers, revived religion in their day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And he set the priests in their charges, and encouraged them to the service of the house of the Lord. Henry notes the king exhorted and directed, quickened and encouraged priests and Levites to do their office, perhaps seeing them remiss and indifferent, and those whom we charge we should encourage, for most people will be wrought upon by encouragements more than threats. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And said unto the Levites that taught all Israel, which were holy unto the Lord, Put the holy ark in the house which Solomon son of David king of Israel did build; it shall not be a burden upon your shoulders: serve now the Lord your God, and his people Israel. Henry notes he ordered the ark put in its place, displaced of late either by wicked kings to make room for idols or by Hezekiah for workmen, and charged Levites to serve God and his people Israel, for ministers must look on themselves as servants both to Christ and to his church, for God's glory and his people's welfare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And prepare yourselves by the houses of your fathers, after your courses, according to the writing of David king of Israel, and according to the writing of Solomon his son. Henry notes he reduced them to the office appointed by Moses' law and the order put by David and Solomon, not cutting out new work or method but calling them back to institution, with courses settled in writing to marshal themselves by families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And stand in the holy place according to the divisions of the families of the fathers of your brethren the people, and after the division of the families of the Levites. Henry notes their courses were settled in writing, and they were to marshal themselves according to divisions of their families, for our rule is settled in the written word, and magistrates should take care ministers walk according to that rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. So kill the passover, and sanctify yourselves, and prepare your brethren, that they may do according to the word of the Lord by the hand of Moses. Henry notes he charged them to sanctify themselves and prepare their brethren, for ministers' work must begin at home, purifying from sin and devoting to God, but must not end there, preparing brethren by admonishing, instructing, exhorting and comforting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And Josiah gave to the people, of the flock, lambs and kids, all for the passover offerings, for all that were present, to the number of thirty thousand, and three thousand bullocks: these were of the king's substance. Henry notes Josiah at his own cost furnished the congregation with paschal lambs and other sacrifices for the seven feast days, 30,000 lambs and 3,000 bullocks, for those serious in religion, when persuading others to good, should make it as cheap and easy as may be, and where God sows plentifully he expects to reap accordingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And his princes gave willingly unto the people, to the priests, and to the Levites: Hilkiah and Zechariah and Jehiel, rulers of the house of God, gave unto the priests for the passover offerings two thousand and six hundred small cattle and three hundred oxen. Henry notes the chief priests, men of great estates, contributed towards priests' charges as Josiah did towards the people's, the princes of the holy tribe, rulers of God's house, bearing priests' charges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Conaniah also, and Shemaiah and Nethaneel, his brethren, and Hashabiah and Jeiel and Jozabad, chief of the Levites, gave unto the Levites for passover offerings five thousand small cattle, and five hundred oxen. Henry notes some rich and great Levites furnished their brethren also with cattle great and small for offerings, for Providence sometimes raises up friends to bear sincere duty-seekers out beyond expectation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. So the service was prepared, and the priests stood in their place, and the Levites in their courses, according to the king's commandment. Henry notes priests and Levites performed their office very readily, standing in place and courses according to the king's commandment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And they killed the passover, and the priests sprinkled the blood from their hands, and the Levites flayed them. Henry notes they killed paschal lambs in the temple court, priests sprinkled blood upon the altar, Levites flayed them, and gave flesh to the people according to families, not fewer than ten nor more than twenty to a lamb. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And they removed the burnt offerings, that they might give according to the divisions of the families of the people, to offer unto the Lord, as it is written in the book of Moses. And so did they with the oxen. Henry notes they gave flesh to the people according to families to offer to the Lord as written in Moses' book, and so did they with the oxen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And they roasted the passover with fire according to the ordinance, but the other holy offerings sod they in pots, and in caldrons, and in pans, and divided them speedily among all the people. Henry notes they took lambs to apartments, roasted and ate according to the ordinance, while other eucharistical sacrifices were boiled according to peace-offerings' law and divided speedily among the people, that they might feast as token of joy in atonement and reconciliation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And afterward they made ready for themselves, and for the priests, because the priests Aaron's sons were busied in offering of burnt offerings and the fat until night, therefore the Levites prepared for themselves, and for the priests Aaron's sons. Henry notes priests and Levites took care to honour God by eating of the passover themselves, for care for others' souls will not excuse neglect of their own, and Levites made ready for priests wholly taken up all day in altar service, that they might not dress their lamb when they should eat it, so ministers should help one another as brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And the singers Asaph's sons were in their place, according to the commandment of David, and Asaph, and Heman, and Jeduthun the king's seer, and the porters waited at every gate, they might not depart from their service, for their brethren the Levites prepared for them. Henry notes singers with sacred songs and music expressed and excited the congregation's joy and made service pleasant, and porters at gates took care nothing defiling or disquieting broke in nor anyone stole away till service was done, while their brethren prepared paschal lambs for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. So all the service of the Lord was prepared the same day, to keep the passover, and to offer burnt offerings upon the altar of the Lord, according to the commandment of king Josiah. Henry notes the whole solemnity was performed with great exactness according to the law, and upon that account there was none like it since Samuel's time, for Hezekiah's passover had several irregularities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And Israel's children present kept the passover at that time, and the feast of unleavened bread seven days. Henry notes it was more in compliance with the king that they all kept the passover than from any great inclination of their own, taking some pride in the form of godliness but little pleasure in its power, though magistrates and ministers did their part for due solemnity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And there was no passover like to that kept in Israel from the days of Samuel the prophet, neither did all Israel's kings keep such a passover as Josiah kept, and the priests, and the Levites, and all Judah and Israel present, and Jerusalem's inhabitants. Henry notes there was none like it since Samuel's time, and it exceeded preceding kings' passovers in that though Josiah was by no means so rich as David, Solomon and Jehoshaphat, yet he furnished the whole congregation with beasts for sacrifice at his own cost, more than any king ever did before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. In the eighteenth year of the reign of Josiah was this passover kept. Henry notes this passover was kept in Josiah's eighteenth year, thirteen years before his death, after which the people, for all this, were not turned from love of sins nor God from fierceness of anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. After all this, when Josiah had prepared the temple, Necho king of Egypt came up to fight against Charchemish by Euphrates, and Josiah went out against him. Henry notes the next news after thirteen silent years is that Josiah is cut off in mid-days and usefulness, before full forty, for he went out against Egypt's king coming up to fight Carchemish by Euphrates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. But he sent ambassadors to him, saying, What have I to do with thee, thou king of Judah? I come not against thee this day, but against the house wherewith I have war, for God commanded me to make haste: forbear thee from meddling with God, who is with me, that he destroy thee not. Henry notes Egypt's king argued from justice, that he had no desire to hurt Judah and it was unfair for Josiah to take arms against him, from religion, that God was with him and commanded him to make haste, and from policy, that it was at Josiah's peril to engage one with a better army, better cause and God on his side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Nevertheless Josiah would not turn his face from him, but disguised himself, that he might fight with him, and hearkened not unto the words of Necho from the mouth of God, and came to fight in the valley of Megiddo. Henry notes it was not in wrath to Josiah, whose heart was upright, but in wrath to a hypocritical nation unworthy of so good a king, that he was infatuated not to hearken to fair reasonings, and he would not turn his face but fought at Megiddo, though upon Necho's pleading a command from God he ought to have consulted God's oracles before going out, and in this he walked not in David's ways, for David would have enquired of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And the archers shot at king Josiah, and the king said to his servants, Have me away, for I am sore wounded. Henry notes Josiah's rashness in engaging Egypt's king cost him dearly: archers shot him and he cried to his servants to have him away, for he was sore wounded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. His servants therefore took him out of that chariot, and put him in the second chariot that he had, and they brought him to Jerusalem, and he died, and was buried in one of the sepulchres of his fathers. And all Judah and Jerusalem mourned for Josiah. Henry notes his servants brought him to Jerusalem, where he died and was buried in his fathers' sepulchres, and all Judah and Jerusalem mourned for him, contriving mourning by singing men and women spread through the kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And Jeremiah lamented for Josiah, and all the singing men and the singing women spake of Josiah in their lamentations to this day, and made them an ordinance in Israel, and behold, they are written in the lamentations. Henry notes Jeremiah lamented him, the weeping prophet foreseeing utter ruin following this good king's death, and singing men and women made lamentations an ordinance in Israel, written in the Lamentations, showing the people had some respect for their good prince and some sense of danger now he was gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Now the rest of the acts of Josiah, and his goodness, according to that which was written in the law of the Lord. Henry notes the rest of Josiah's acts and goodness, according to what was written in the Lord's law, are referred to the kings' book, closing the account of a king whose pious endeavours tried Judah once more before ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And his deeds, first and last, behold, they are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah. Henry notes his deeds, first and last, are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah, and so we must take our leave of Josiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry notes the rest of Josiah's acts and goodness, according to what was written in the Lord's law, are referred to the kings' book, closing the account of a king whose pious endeavours tried Judah once more before ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And his deeds, first and last, behold, they are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah. Henry notes his deeds, first and last, are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah, and so we must take our leave of Josiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
