The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 121 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help.

2 My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth.

3 He will not suffer thy foot to be moved: he that keepeth thee will not slumber.

4 Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep.

5 The LORD is thy keeper: the LORD is thy shade upon thy right hand.

6 The sun shall not smite thee by day, nor the moon by night.

7 The LORD shall preserve thee from all evil: he shall preserve thy soul.

8 The LORD shall preserve thy going out and thy coming in from this time forth, and even for evermore.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A psalm of keeping: "I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help" — and the answer is immediate, help comes from the LORD who made heaven and earth. He will not suffer the traveller's foot to be moved; he who keeps Israel neither slumbers nor sleeps. The LORD is keeper and shade upon the right hand, so sun shall not smite by day nor moon by night, and the LORD shall preserve from all evil, preserve the soul, and preserve going out and coming in from this time forth and even for evermore. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry lifts eyes to hills, or above hills: shall help come from hills' strength, princes like hills filling earth? No — in vain is salvation hoped for from hills — or lift eyes beyond instruments to God who makes them what they are, and to special presence on Moriah and Zion, temple, ark and oracle, whence help comes from word, prayer and secret of tabernacle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry lays all help in God: help desired is what he sends, expected in his own way and time — if he does not help no creature can, if he does none can hinder — and encourages confidence because he made heaven and earth from nothing by a word, sovereign of both hosts, able to use or restrain them for his people's succour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry promises feet kept: God will not suffer foot moved, providing saints not tempted above ability, not falling into sin though near it, nor into trouble though undermined by fraud or overthrown by force, nor frightened as when slipping — for he keeps feet of saints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry makes Israel's keeper every believer's keeper: wakeful, watchful, never weary, not so much as slumbering — the flock's shepherd is each sheep's shepherd, not one little one perishing, same wisdom, power and promises for church in general and particular believer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry calls God keeper and shade: infinite wisdom contriving and infinite power working safety, God himself guardian though angels are charged — and shade close as shadow to body, sheltering from scorching heat like a great rock in weary land, under which they sit with delight and assurance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry shields day and night: sun shall not smite with heat by day nor moon with cold and moisture by night, as wilderness pillar of cloud screened heat and fire diffused warmth against Jacob's drought by day and frost by night — figuratively, neither open assaults visible as sunbeams nor secret treacheries insinuating as night cold shall hurt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry preserves from all evil and especially the soul: evil of sin and trouble, preventing feared evil, sanctifying, removing or lightening felt evil, keeping from doing evil so affliction has no evil in it — and soul peculiarly cared for, not defiled by sin, disturbed by affliction, or perishing eternally, for soul is the man and all souls are his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry preserves going out and coming in for evermore: in all journeys outward or homeward, affairs at home and abroad, beginning and conclusion, life and death — going out to labour in morning of days and coming home to rest when evening of old age calls — a protection for life never out of date, guide even unto death, then hidden in grave and heaven, Spirit abiding for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
