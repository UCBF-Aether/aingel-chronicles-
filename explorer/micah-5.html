The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Micah 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now gather thyself in troops, O daughter of troops: he hath laid siege against us: they shall smite the judge of Israel with a rod upon the cheek.

2 But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.

3 Therefore will he give them up, until the time that she which travaileth hath brought forth: then the remnant of his brethren shall return unto the children of Israel.

4 And he shall stand and feed in the strength of the LORD, in the majesty of the name of the LORD his God; and they shall abide: for now shall he be great unto the ends of the earth.

5 And this man shall be the peace, when the Assyrian shall come into our land: and when he shall tread in our palaces, then shall we raise against him seven shepherds, and eight principal men.

6 And they shall waste the land of Assyria with the sword, and the land of Nimrod in the entrances thereof: thus shall he deliver us from the Assyrian, when he cometh into our land, and when he treadeth within our borders.

7 And the remnant of Jacob shall be in the midst of many people as a dew from the LORD, as the showers upon the grass, that tarrieth not for man, nor waiteth for the sons of men.

8 And the remnant of Jacob shall be among the Gentiles in the midst of many people as a lion among the beasts of the forest, as a young lion among the flocks of sheep: who, if he go through, both treadeth down, and teareth in pieces, and none can deliver.

9 Thine hand shall be lifted up upon thine adversaries, and all thine enemies shall be cut off.

10 And it shall come to pass in that day, saith the LORD, that I will cut off thy horses out of the midst of thee, and I will destroy thy chariots:

11 And I will cut off the cities of thy land, and throw down all thy strong holds:

12 And I will cut off witchcrafts out of thine hand; and thou shalt have no more soothsayers:

13 Thy graven images also will I cut off, and thy standing images out of the midst of thee; and thou shalt no more worship the work of thine hands.

14 And I will pluck up thy groves out of the midst of thee: so will I destroy thy cities.

15 And I will execute vengeance in anger and fury upon the heathen, such as they have not heard.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Under siege, with the judge of Israel struck on the cheek, the chapter names Bethlehem Ephratah, little among the thousands of Judah, as the place from which the ruler of Israel will come forth, whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting. He will stand and feed in the strength of the LORD, the remnant of Jacob will be among the nations like dew and like a lion, and idols, witchcrafts, and graven images will be cut off. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The daughter of troops gathers herself in troops because siege is laid against her, and the judge of Israel is smitten with a rod on the cheek. Henry sets this low point of the house of David as the dark ground against which the Messiah's prediction shines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. But Bethlehem Ephratah, little among the thousands of Judah, will give forth the ruler of Israel, whose goings forth are from of old, from everlasting. Henry calls this perhaps the most important single prophecy in the Old Testament: it distinguishes his human birth at Bethlehem from his existence from eternity, and was universally known among the Jews as his birthplace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Therefore God will give them up until she who travails has brought forth, and then the remnant of his brethren will return to the children of Israel. Henry reads here the rejection of Israel for a season and their final restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. He will stand and feed his flock in the strength of the Lord and the majesty of his God's name, and they will abide, for he will be great to the ends of the earth. Henry notes Christ's government is very happy for his subjects: they will be safe and easy, and we may trust our Shepherd's care and power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. This man will be the peace when the Assyrian comes into the land and treads in the palaces, and seven shepherds and eight principal men will be raised against him. Henry explains Christ is our Peace as Priest, making atonement and reconciling us to God, and as King, conquering our enemies, so our souls may dwell at ease in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. They will waste the land of Assyria with the sword, and the land of Nimrod at its entrances, and he will deliver his people from the Assyrian within their borders. Under the shadow of protection from the Assyrians lies a promise of protection for the gospel church from the powers of darkness, and those who threaten ruin to God's church soon bring ruin on themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The remnant of Jacob among many peoples will be like dew from the Lord and showers on the grass, which do not wait for man. Henry reads the remnant converted to Christ in the earliest times as drops of dew, made instruments in calling a large increase of spiritual worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The same remnant among the Gentiles will be like a lion among forest beasts and a young lion among flocks, treading down and tearing in pieces, with none to deliver. To those who neglected or opposed this salvation, Henry says, they would cause terror, their doctrine condemning them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Your hand will be lifted up upon your adversaries, and all your enemies will be cut off. Victory in personal conflicts comes, Henry says, as we simply depend upon the Lord our salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. In that day the Lord will cut off their horses and destroy their chariots. This is part of the purification Henry describes: God strips away the instruments of warlike self-trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. He will cut off the cities of the land and throw down all its strongholds. Human fortifications are removed along with the horses and chariots, so that trust rests in the Lord alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. He will cut off witchcrafts from their hand, and they will have no more soothsayers. The purification of the church includes ending all resort to forbidden arts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Graven images and standing images will be cut off from their midst, and they will no more worship the work of their hands. Reformation means idols gone, not merely reformed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. He will pluck up their groves from their midst and destroy their cities. The idol groves fall with the idolatrous cities they served. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. He will execute vengeance in anger and fury on the heathen, such as they have not heard. Henry's warning stands behind this: those who threaten ruin to the church of God soon bring ruin on themselves, in the ruin of all antichristian powers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
