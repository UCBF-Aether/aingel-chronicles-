The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 20 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 The LORD hear thee in the day of trouble; the name of the God of Jacob defend thee;

2 Send thee help from the sanctuary, and strengthen thee out of Zion;

3 Remember all thy offerings, and accept thy burnt sacrifice; Selah.

4 Grant thee according to thine own heart, and fulfil all thy counsel.

5 We will rejoice in thy salvation, and in the name of our God we will set up our banners: the LORD fulfil all thy petitions.

6 Now know I that the LORD saveth his anointed; he will hear him from his holy heaven with the saving strength of his right hand.

7 Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.

8 They are brought down and fallen: but we are risen, and stand upright.

9 Save, LORD: let the king hear us when we call.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The people's prayer for their king before battle: hearing in trouble, help from sanctuary and Zion, remembered offerings, plans fulfilled, banners under God's name. Assurance follows, the Lord saves his anointed, set against trust in chariots and horses, which falls, while trust in God's name stands. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Lord hear you in the day of trouble; the name of the God of Jacob defend you: the people's prayer for their king asks God's hearing and his name as defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Send help from the sanctuary and strengthen you out of Zion: help comes from God's dwelling-place, the centre of worship and of covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Remember all your offerings and accept your burnt sacrifice: the king's worship is remembered as the ground of acceptance in battle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Grant you according to your own heart, and fulfil all your counsel: the people's wish is that the king's godly plans succeed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. We will rejoice in your salvation, and in our God's name set up our banners; the Lord fulfil all your petitions: victory will be celebrated under God's banner, not the king's alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Now I know the Lord saves his anointed, hearing him from his holy heaven with the saving strength of his right hand: assurance replaces petition — the anointed's cause is heaven's cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Some trust in chariots and some in horses, but we will remember the name of the Lord our God: military power is contrasted with covenant remembrance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They are brought down and fallen, but we are risen and stand upright: trust in horses ends in a fall; trust in God's name ends standing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Save, Lord; let the king hear us when we call: the psalm closes by joining the people's cry to the king's hearing, under God's saving rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
