The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Kings 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Ahab told Jezebel all that Elijah had done, and withal how he had slain all the prophets with the sword.

2 Then Jezebel sent a messenger unto Elijah, saying, So let the gods do to me, and more also, if I make not thy life as the life of one of them by to morrow about this time.

3 And when he saw that, he arose, and went for his life, and came to Beersheba, which belongeth to Judah, and left his servant there.

4 But he himself went a day’s journey into the wilderness, and came and sat down under a juniper tree: and he requested for himself that he might die; and said, It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life; for I am not better than my fathers.

5 And as he lay and slept under a juniper tree, behold, then an angel touched him, and said unto him, Arise and eat.

6 And he looked, and, behold, there was a cake baken on the coals, and a cruse of water at his head. And he did eat and drink, and laid him down again.

7 And the angel of the LORD came again the second time, and touched him, and said, Arise and eat; because the journey is too great for thee.

8 And he arose, and did eat and drink, and went in the strength of that meat forty days and forty nights unto Horeb the mount of God.

9 And he came thither unto a cave, and lodged there; and, behold, the word of the LORD came to him, and he said unto him, What doest thou here, Elijah?

10 And he said, I have been very jealous for the LORD God of hosts: for the children of Israel have forsaken thy covenant, thrown down thine altars, and slain thy prophets with the sword; and I, even I only, am left; and they seek my life, to take it away.

11 And he said, Go forth, and stand upon the mount before the LORD. And, behold, the LORD passed by, and a great and strong wind rent the mountains, and brake in pieces the rocks before the LORD; but the LORD was not in the wind: and after the wind an earthquake; but the LORD was not in the earthquake:

12 And after the earthquake a fire; but the LORD was not in the fire: and after the fire a still small voice.

13 And it was so, when Elijah heard it, that he wrapped his face in his mantle, and went out, and stood in the entering in of the cave. And, behold, there came a voice unto him, and said, What doest thou here, Elijah?

14 And he said, I have been very jealous for the LORD God of hosts: because the children of Israel have forsaken thy covenant, thrown down thine altars, and slain thy prophets with the sword; and I, even I only, am left; and they seek my life, to take it away.

15 And the LORD said unto him, Go, return on thy way to the wilderness of Damascus: and when thou comest, anoint Hazael to be king over Syria:

16 And Jehu the son of Nimshi shalt thou anoint to be king over Israel: and Elisha the son of Shaphat of Abelmeholah shalt thou anoint to be prophet in thy room.

17 And it shall come to pass, that him that escapeth the sword of Hazael shall Jehu slay: and him that escapeth from the sword of Jehu shall Elisha slay.

18 Yet I have left me seven thousand in Israel, all the knees which have not bowed unto Baal, and every mouth which hath not kissed him.

19 So he departed thence, and found Elisha the son of Shaphat, who was plowing with twelve yoke of oxen before him, and he with the twelfth: and Elijah passed by him, and cast his mantle upon him.

20 And he left the oxen, and ran after Elijah, and said, Let me, I pray thee, kiss my father and my mother, and then I will follow thee. And he said unto him, Go back again: for what have I done to thee?

21 And he returned back from him, and took a yoke of oxen, and slew them, and boiled their flesh with the instruments of the oxen, and gave unto the people, and they did eat. Then he arose, and went after Elijah, and ministered unto him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jezebel threatens Elijah's life, and he flees to the wilderness, asking to die, but is fed by an angel and walks forty days to Horeb. There, not in wind, earthquake or fire but in a still small voice, the LORD gives him his next tasks: anoint Hazael, Jehu, and Elisha, and Elisha, called from the plough, burns his gear and follows. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Ahab told Jezebel all Elijah had done, and how he had slain all the prophets with the sword, the victory at Carmel reported to its fiercest enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jezebel sent a messenger to Elijah, swearing by her gods that by tomorrow she would make his life as the life of one of them, a queen's oath of murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. When he saw that, he arose and went for his life, coming to Beersheba in Judah, and leaving his servant there, the prophet who faced Ahab now fleeing a woman's threat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Alone under the juniper he asked that he might die, saying it is enough, for he was no better than his fathers — Henry distinguishes the passionate wish of corruption from Paul's deliberate desire to depart and be with Christ: God left him to himself to show his boldness had been in the Lord's power, not his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. As he lay and slept under the juniper, an angel touched him and said, Arise and eat, God feeding the prophet who had asked to die. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He looked and found cake baked on coals and water at his head, ate and drank and lay down again — Henry notes he was not only provided for but attended by an angel who guarded him sleeping and twice called him to his food, so that he fared better than the grove prophets at Jezebel's table. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The angel came a second time and bade him eat, because the journey was too great for him — Henry's note is that God, who appoints the voyage, victuals the ship accordingly: he knows what service and trials he designs us for and furnishes grace sufficient for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. In the strength of that food he travelled forty days and nights to Horeb — Henry notes he neither needed food nor desired it, traversing the desert a day for a year of Israel's wanderings, to show that man lives not by bread alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. He came to a cave and lodged there, and the Lord's word came to him, What doest thou here, Elijah, a question that searches the fugitive's heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Elijah pleaded he had been very jealous for the Lord, but Israel had forsaken the covenant, thrown down the altars and slain the prophets, and he alone was left — Henry reads it as despair of success, not want of zeal: he had laboured in vain, and despair hinders many a good enterprise, forgetting that those with God are not alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. God said, Go forth and stand on the mount before the Lord, and behold, the Lord passed by, with a great strong wind rending mountains and breaking rocks, but the Lord was not in the wind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. After wind, earthquake and fire came a still small voice, and the Lord was not in the fire — Henry's point is that God chose to speak in whispers soft: wind and fire awaken awe, but gracious souls are more affected by tender mercies than terrors, and faith comes by hearing the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. When Elijah heard it, he wrapped his face in his mantle, went out, and stood in the cave's entrance, reverence before a God who speaks softly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The voice asked again, What doest thou here, Elijah, and he answered again with the same complaint, showing his heart had not yet been changed by the vision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The Lord said, Go, return on thy way to the wilderness of Damascus, and when thou comest, anoint Hazael to be king over Syria, work given to a prophet who thought his work was done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Jehu son of Nimshi he was to anoint king over Israel, and Elisha son of Shaphat of Abel-meholah to be prophet in his room, a succession of instruments against Baal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Him that escaped Hazael's sword Jehu would slay, and him that escaped Jehu's sword Elisha would slay, judgment by three hands, none escaping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Yet God said, I have left me seven thousand in Israel, all knees that have not bowed to Baal, and every mouth that has not kissed him, so Elijah was not alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Elijah found Elisha ploughing with twelve yoke, himself with the twelfth, and cast his mantle upon him — Henry stresses the unexpected call: not in the schools of the prophets but in the field, for an honest calling in the world puts no man out of the way of his heavenly calling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Elisha left the oxen and ran after Elijah, asking first to kiss his father and mother — Henry notes the call was powerful: an invisible hand touched his heart at the mere casting of the mantle, and he left the oxen to go as they would. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Elisha returned, took a yoke of oxen, slew them, boiled their flesh with the instruments of the oxen, gave to the people, and they ate, then he arose, went after Elijah, and ministered to him, burning his old livelihood behind him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
