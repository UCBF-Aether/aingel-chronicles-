The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 21 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, that after we were gotten from them, and had launched, we came with a straight course unto Coos, and the day following unto Rhodes, and from thence unto Patara:

2 And finding a ship sailing over unto Phenicia, we went aboard, and set forth.

3 Now when we had discovered Cyprus, we left it on the left hand, and sailed into Syria, and landed at Tyre: for there the ship was to unlade her burden.

4 And finding disciples, we tarried there seven days: who said to Paul through the Spirit, that he should not go up to Jerusalem.

5 And when we had accomplished those days, we departed and went our way; and they all brought us on our way, with wives and children, till we were out of the city: and we kneeled down on the shore, and prayed.

6 And when we had taken our leave one of another, we took ship; and they returned home again.

7 And when we had finished our course from Tyre, we came to Ptolemais, and saluted the brethren, and abode with them one day.

8 And the next day we that were of Paul’s company departed, and came unto Caesarea: and we entered into the house of Philip the evangelist, which was one of the seven; and abode with him.

9 And the same man had four daughters, virgins, which did prophesy.

10 And as we tarried there many days, there came down from Judaea a certain prophet, named Agabus.

11 And when he was come unto us, he took Paul’s girdle, and bound his own hands and feet, and said, Thus saith the Holy Ghost, So shall the Jews at Jerusalem bind the man that owneth this girdle, and shall deliver him into the hands of the Gentiles.

12 And when we heard these things, both we, and they of that place, besought him not to go up to Jerusalem.

13 Then Paul answered, What mean ye to weep and to break mine heart? for I am ready not to be bound only, but also to die at Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus.

14 And when he would not be persuaded, we ceased, saying, The will of the Lord be done.

15 And after those days we took up our carriages, and went up to Jerusalem.

16 There went with us also certain of the disciples of Caesarea, and brought with them one Mnason of Cyprus, an old disciple, with whom we should lodge.

17 And when we were come to Jerusalem, the brethren received us gladly.

18 And the day following Paul went in with us unto James; and all the elders were present.

19 And when he had saluted them, he declared particularly what things God had wrought among the Gentiles by his ministry.

20 And when they heard it, they glorified the Lord, and said unto him, Thou seest, brother, how many thousands of Jews there are which believe; and they are all zealous of the law:

21 And they are informed of thee, that thou teachest all the Jews which are among the Gentiles to forsake Moses, saying that they ought not to circumcise their children, neither to walk after the customs.

22 What is it therefore? the multitude must needs come together: for they will hear that thou art come.

23 Do therefore this that we say to thee: We have four men which have a vow on them;

24 Them take, and purify thyself with them, and be at charges with them, that they may shave their heads: and all may know that those things, whereof they were informed concerning thee, are nothing; but that thou thyself also walkest orderly, and keepest the law.

25 As touching the Gentiles which believe, we have written and concluded that they observe no such thing, save only that they keep themselves from things offered to idols, and from blood, and from strangled, and from fornication.

26 Then Paul took the men, and the next day purifying himself with them entered into the temple, to signify the accomplishment of the days of purification, until that an offering should be offered for every one of them.

27 And when the seven days were almost ended, the Jews which were of Asia, when they saw him in the temple, stirred up all the people, and laid hands on him,

28 Crying out, Men of Israel, help: This is the man, that teacheth all men every where against the people, and the law, and this place: and further brought Greeks also into the temple, and hath polluted this holy place.

29 (For they had seen before with him in the city Trophimus an Ephesian, whom they supposed that Paul had brought into the temple.)

30 And all the city was moved, and the people ran together: and they took Paul, and drew him out of the temple: and forthwith the doors were shut.

31 And as they went about to kill him, tidings came unto the chief captain of the band, that all Jerusalem was in an uproar.

32 Who immediately took soldiers and centurions, and ran down unto them: and when they saw the chief captain and the soldiers, they left beating of Paul.

33 Then the chief captain came near, and took him, and commanded him to be bound with two chains; and demanded who he was, and what he had done.

34 And some cried one thing, some another, among the multitude: and when he could not know the certainty for the tumult, he commanded him to be carried into the castle.

35 And when he came upon the stairs, so it was, that he was borne of the soldiers for the violence of the people.

36 For the multitude of the people followed after, crying, Away with him.

37 And as Paul was to be led into the castle, he said unto the chief captain, May I speak unto thee? Who said, Canst thou speak Greek?

38 Art not thou that Egyptian, which before these days madest an uproar, and leddest out into the wilderness four thousand men that were murderers?

39 But Paul said, I am a man which am a Jew of Tarsus, a city in Cilicia, a citizen of no mean city: and, I beseech thee, suffer me to speak unto the people.

40 And when he had given him licence, Paul stood on the stairs, and beckoned with the hand unto the people. And when there was made a great silence, he spake unto them in the Hebrew tongue, saying,

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Warned repeatedly on the way, Paul reaches Jerusalem, is welcomed by James and the elders, and, to show he keeps the law, joins four men in a purification vow in the temple. Seized by a crowd charging that he teaches against the law and defiles the temple, he is rescued by the Roman chief captain and, standing on the stairs, asks leave to address the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. They are informed of thee, that thou teachest all the Jews which are among the Gentiles to forsake Moses, saying that they ought not to circumcise their children, neither to walk after the customs. Henry says the weakness and evil of the human heart strongly appear, when we consider how many, even of the disciples of Christ, had not due regard to the most eminent minister that even lived, for not the excellence of his character, nor the success with which God blessed his labours, could gain their esteem and affection, seeing that he did not render the same respect as themselves to mere ceremonial observances, and how watchful should we be against prejudices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry's prudence plan begins with the multitude's coming: James and the elders of the church at Jerusalem asked Paul to gratify the believing Jews by some compliance with the ceremonial law, thinking it prudent in him to conform thus far. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Do therefore this that we say to thee: We have four men which have a vow on them. Henry's compliance plan is the four men with a vow: James and the elders ask Paul to gratify the believing Jews by compliance with the ceremonial law, thinking it prudent to conform thus far. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Them take, and purify thyself with them, and be at charges with them, that they may shave their heads: and all may know that those things, whereof they were informed concerning thee, are nothing; but that thou thyself also walkest orderly, and keepest the law. Henry says it was great weakness to be so fond of the shadows, when the substance was come, for the religion Paul preached tended not to destroy the law, but to fulfil it, since he preached Christ, the end of the law for righteousness, and repentance and faith, in which we are to make great use of the law, yet the elders thought it prudent for Paul to conform thus far and walk orderly, keeping the law, to answer the report. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. As touching the Gentiles which believe, we have written and concluded that they observe no such thing, save only that they keep themselves from things offered to idols, and from blood, and from strangled, and from fornication. Henry's distinction is preserved in the restatement: the Gentiles observe no such ceremonial thing, save the necessary things of the council's letter, while Paul's compliance is asked for the sake of the believing Jews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Then Paul took the men, and the next day purifying himself with them entered into the temple, to signify the accomplishment of the days of purification, until that an offering should be offered for every one of them. Henry is frank about the compliance: the apostles were not free from blame in all they did, and it would be hard to defend Paul from the charge of giving way too much in this matter, for it is vain to attempt to court the favour of zealots, or bigots to a party, and this compliance did not answer, for the very thing by which he hoped to pacify the Jews provoked them, and brought him into trouble, but the all-wise God overruled both their advice and Paul's compliance with it, to serve a better purpose than was intended, and integrity and uprightness will be more likely to preserve us than insincere compliances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. When the seven days were almost ended, the Jews which were of Asia, when they saw him in the temple, stirred up all the people, and laid hands on him. Henry says in the temple, where Paul should have been protected as in a place of safety, he was violently set upon, and they falsely charged him with ill doctrine and ill practice against the Mosaic ceremonies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Crying out, Men of Israel, help: This is the man, that teacheth all men every where against the people, and the law, and this place: and further brought Greeks also into the temple, and hath polluted this holy place. Henry says it is no new thing for those who mean honestly and act regularly, to have things laid to their charge which they know not and never thought of, and it is common for the wise and good to have that charged against them by malicious people, with which they thought to have obliged them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For they had seen before with him in the city Trophimus an Ephesian, whom they supposed that Paul had brought into the temple. Henry says see what false, mistaken notions of good people and good ministers, many run away with, and the supposition about Trophimus, seen before in the city, is that mistaken notion made into a charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. All the city was moved, and the people ran together: and they took Paul, and drew him out of the temple: and forthwith the doors were shut. Henry's temple safety fails: in the temple, where Paul should have been protected as in a place of safety, he was violently set upon, and the city moved, the people ran together, took Paul, and drew him out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. As they went about to kill him, tidings came unto the chief captain of the band, that all Jerusalem was in an uproar. Henry says God often makes those a protection to his people, who have no affection to them, but only have compassion for sufferers, and regard to the public peace, and the tidings coming to the chief captain is that protection beginning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Who immediately took soldiers and centurions, and ran down unto them: and when they saw the chief captain and the soldiers, they left beating of Paul. Henry says God seasonably interposes for the safety of his servants, from wicked and unreasonable men, and the captain immediately taking soldiers and running down, so that they left beating Paul, is that interposition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Then the chief captain came near, and took him, and commanded him to be bound with two chains; and demanded who he was, and what he had done. Henry's protection that binds is the captain's taking: God makes a protector of one with no affection but regard to the public peace, and the captain takes Paul, binds him with two chains, and demands who he is, which both protects him and leads to his opportunity to speak. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry says see what false, mistaken notions of good people and good ministers, many run away with, and the captain's Egyptian is that mistaken notion, taking Paul for a murderous uproar-maker. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. But Paul said, I am a man which am a Jew of Tarsus, a city in Cilicia, a citizen of no mean city: and, I beseech thee, suffer me to speak unto the people. Henry's correction is Paul's answer: a Jew of Tarsus, a citizen of no mean city, correcting the false, mistaken notion, and beseeching leave to speak unto the people, for God gives his servants opportunities to speak for themselves and plead for the Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. When he had given him licence, Paul stood on the stairs, and beckoned with the hand unto the people. And when there was made a great silence, he spake unto them in the Hebrew tongue, saying. Henry closes on the opportunity given: God seasonably interposes for the safety of his servants, from wicked and unreasonable men, and gives them opportunities to speak for themselves, to plead for the Redeemer, and to spread abroad his glorious gospel, and the licence, the beckoning, the great silence, and the Hebrew tongue are that opportunity. Henry says it was great weakness to be so fond of the shadows, when the substance was come, for the religion Paul preached tended not to destroy the law, but to fulfil it, since he preached Christ, the end of the law for righteousness, and repentance and faith, in which we are to make great use of the law, yet the elders thought it prudent for Paul to conform thus far and walk orderly, keeping the law, to answer the report. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. As touching the Gentiles which believe, we have written and concluded that they observe no such thing, save only that they keep themselves from things offered to idols, and from blood, and from strangled, and from fornication. Henry's distinction is preserved in the restatement: the Gentiles observe no such ceremonial thing, save the necessary things of the council's letter, while Paul's compliance is asked for the sake of the believing Jews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Then Paul took the men, and the next day purifying himself with them entered into the temple, to signify the accomplishment of the days of purification, until that an offering should be offered for every one of them. Henry is frank about the compliance: the apostles were not free from blame in all they did, and it would be hard to defend Paul from the charge of giving way too much in this matter, for it is vain to attempt to court the favour of zealots, or bigots to a party, and this compliance did not answer, for the very thing by which he hoped to pacify the Jews provoked them, and brought him into trouble, but the all-wise God overruled both their advice and Paul's compliance with it, to serve a better purpose than was intended, and integrity and uprightness will be more likely to preserve us than insincere compliances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. When the seven days were almost ended, the Jews which were of Asia, when they saw him in the temple, stirred up all the people, and laid hands on him. Henry says in the temple, where Paul should have been protected as in a place of safety, he was violently set upon, and they falsely charged him with ill doctrine and ill practice against the Mosaic ceremonies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Crying out, Men of Israel, help: This is the man, that teacheth all men every where against the people, and the law, and this place: and further brought Greeks also into the temple, and hath polluted this holy place. Henry says it is no new thing for those who mean honestly and act regularly, to have things laid to their charge which they know not and never thought of, and it is common for the wise and good to have that charged against them by malicious people, with which they thought to have obliged them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For they had seen before with him in the city Trophimus an Ephesian, whom they supposed that Paul had brought into the temple. Henry says see what false, mistaken notions of good people and good ministers, many run away with, and the supposition about Trophimus, seen before in the city, is that mistaken notion made into a charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. All the city was moved, and the people ran together: and they took Paul, and drew him out of the temple: and forthwith the doors were shut. Henry's temple safety fails: in the temple, where Paul should have been protected as in a place of safety, he was violently set upon, and the city moved, the people ran together, took Paul, and drew him out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. As they went about to kill him, tidings came unto the chief captain of the band, that all Jerusalem was in an uproar. Henry says God often makes those a protection to his people, who have no affection to them, but only have compassion for sufferers, and regard to the public peace, and the tidings coming to the chief captain is that protection beginning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Who immediately took soldiers and centurions, and ran down unto them: and when they saw the chief captain and the soldiers, they left beating of Paul. Henry says God seasonably interposes for the safety of his servants, from wicked and unreasonable men, and the captain immediately taking soldiers and running down, so that they left beating Paul, is that interposition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Then the chief captain came near, and took him, and commanded him to be bound with two chains; and demanded who he was, and what he had done. Henry's protection that binds is the captain's taking: God makes a protector of one with no affection but regard to the public peace, and the captain takes Paul, binds him with two chains, and demands who he is, which both protects him and leads to his opportunity to speak. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. As Paul was to be led into the castle, he said unto the chief captain, May I speak unto thee? Henry's opportunity to speak begins with the request: God seasonably interposes for the safety of his servants, and gives them opportunities to speak for themselves, to plead for the Redeemer, and to spread abroad his glorious gospel, and Paul's May I speak unto thee is that opportunity sought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Art not thou that Egyptian, which before these days madest an uproar, and leddest out into the wilderness four thousand men that were murderers? Henry says see what false, mistaken notions of good people and good ministers, many run away with, and the captain's Egyptian is that mistaken notion, taking Paul for a murderous uproar-maker. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. But Paul said, I am a man which am a Jew of Tarsus, a city in Cilicia, a citizen of no mean city: and, I beseech thee, suffer me to speak unto the people. Henry's correction is Paul's answer: a Jew of Tarsus, a citizen of no mean city, correcting the false, mistaken notion, and beseeching leave to speak unto the people, for God gives his servants opportunities to speak for themselves and plead for the Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. When he had given him licence, Paul stood on the stairs, and beckoned with the hand unto the people. And when there was made a great silence, he spake unto them in the Hebrew tongue, saying. Henry closes on the opportunity given: God seasonably interposes for the safety of his servants, from wicked and unreasonable men, and gives them opportunities to speak for themselves, to plead for the Redeemer, and to spread abroad his glorious gospel, and the licence, the beckoning, the great silence, and the Hebrew tongue are that opportunity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
