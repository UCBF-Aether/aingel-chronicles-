Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 136 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 O give thanks unto the LORD; for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever.

2 O give thanks unto the God of gods: for his mercy endureth for ever.

3 O give thanks to the Lord of lords: for his mercy endureth for ever.

4 To him who alone doeth great wonders: for his mercy endureth for ever.

5 To him that by wisdom made the heavens: for his mercy endureth for ever.

6 To him that stretched out the earth above the waters: for his mercy endureth for ever.

7 To him that made great lights: for his mercy endureth for ever:

8 The sun to rule by day: for his mercy endureth for ever:

9 The moon and stars to rule by night: for his mercy endureth for ever.

10 To him that smote Egypt in their firstborn: for his mercy endureth for ever:

11 And brought out Israel from among them: for his mercy endureth for ever:

12 With a strong hand, and with a stretched out arm: for his mercy endureth for ever.

13 To him which divided the Red sea into parts: for his mercy endureth for ever:

14 And made Israel to pass through the midst of it: for his mercy endureth for ever:

15 But overthrew Pharaoh and his host in the Red sea: for his mercy endureth for ever.

16 To him which led his people through the wilderness: for his mercy endureth for ever.

17 To him which smote great kings: for his mercy endureth for ever:

18 And slew famous kings: for his mercy endureth for ever:

19 Sihon king of the Amorites: for his mercy endureth for ever:

20 And Og the king of Bashan: for his mercy endureth for ever:

21 And gave their land for an heritage: for his mercy endureth for ever:

22 Even an heritage unto Israel his servant: for his mercy endureth for ever.

23 Who remembered us in our low estate: for his mercy endureth for ever:

24 And hath redeemed us from our enemies: for his mercy endureth for ever.

25 Who giveth food to all flesh: for his mercy endureth for ever.

26 O give thanks unto the God of heaven: for his mercy endureth for ever.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A litany in which every verse ends with the same refrain: "for his mercy endureth for ever." Thanks is given to the LORD, the God of gods and Lord of lords, who alone does great wonders: by wisdom he made the heavens, stretched out the earth above the waters, made great lights — sun to rule by day, moon and stars by night. The litany then walks through the Exodus story — Egypt smitten in its firstborn, Israel brought out with strong hand and stretched-out arm, the Red Sea divided and Pharaoh overthrown, the people led through the wilderness, great and famous kings smitten, Sihon and Og named, their land given as heritage to Israel — and ends where daily life is: God remembered us in our low estate, redeemed us from enemies, and gives food to all flesh. Thanks to the God of heaven closes the song. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry calls thanks again and again — sacrifice of praise continually, fruit of lips giving thanks to his name (Heb 13:15), never so earnestly called to pray and repent as give thanks, will of God abound most in pleasant exercises, work of heaven; give thanks to Lord Jehovah Israel's God, not only because he does good but because he is good, streams traced to fountain, not only merciful to us but mercy endures for ever drawn out to those after us, not only mercy handed out here on earth but that shall endure for ever in glories joys of heaven; singular composition latter half each verse same repeated yet no vain repetition, burdens keepings add beauty moving affecting, weightiest matter worthy repeated, twenty-six repetitions intimate mercies repeated drawn continuando beginning to end progress advance in infinitum, every particular favour notice mercy enduring still same now as been and for ever same always, everlasting continuance God's honour glory and saints' comfort glory, hearts full greatly affected most frequent mention instead of cloying raise more, subject of praise to all eternity, owned in Solomon's time (2 Chr 5:13 house filled cloud) and Jehoshaphat's (2 Chr 20:21-22 victory). Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry thanks God of gods — God whom angels adore, from whom magistrates derive power, by whom all pretended deities are and shall be conquered; adorations must eye God's excellency transcendent, power dominion incontestably uncontrollably supreme; not Pharisee thanksgivings terminating own praise, directing all to God's glory; mercy disposition to save whom sin rendered miserable vile, provision redemption sinners by Jesus Christ, counsels from everlasting effects endure for ever to interested, Lord equally ready show mercy all seek, source of all hope comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry thanks Lord of lords — Sovereign of all sovereigns, stay supporter of all supports; supreme dominion over every lordship, same enduring mercy as fountain for thanks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry alone does great wonders: contrivance wonderful design laid infinite wisdom, performance wonderful execution infinite power; alone does marvellous things, none besides can do such, does them without assistance advice of any other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry made heavens by wisdom, stretched them out: therein see wisdom power, taste mercy in benign influences; as long as heavens endure mercy of God endures in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry stretched earth above waters: raised earth out of waters caused dry land appear fit habitation for man, therein mercy to man still endures, earth given children of men and all its products. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry made great lights: settled correspondence between heaven and earth notwithstanding distance, sun moon stars placed firmament shed light influences upon earth; called great lights because appear so to us, astronomers tell moon less than many stars but nearer earth seems much greater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry sun rules by day: lights said to rule not only govern seasons year but useful world, benefactors best rulers (Luke 22:25); empire divided one rules day other night, yet all subject God's direction disposal; rulers Gentiles idolized are world's servants God's subjects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry moon and stars rule by night: night empire of moon and stars likewise subject to God, serving earth by light and seasons, not deities but servants under his direction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry smote Egypt in firstborn: great things God did for Israel first forming people setting kingdom among them, instances power and particular kindness; brought out Egypt mercy endured long, redemption by Christ typified endures for ever eternal redemption; of all plagues none mentioned but death first-born because conquering plague, by that God who in all plagues distinguished Israelites from Egyptians brought them at last from among them not by wile but strong hand arm stretched reach far do great things; miracles of mercy proved Moses commission give law, laid Israel lasting obligations obey law (Exod 20:2). Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry brought Israel out from among Egyptians: same conquering deliverance as v10 — not by wile but by distinction and strong hand, bringing a formed people out from among oppressors into covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry strong hand stretched arm: arm reaching far doing great things, power over Egypt and over hearts, miracles of mercy proving commission and laying obligation, strong hand not human contrivance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry divided Red Sea into parts: forced way through sea obstructing first setting out, power control common course nature divided sea two parts opened path, made Israel pass between parts now entering covenant (Jer 34:18); not only divided but gave people courage go through when divided — power over hearts as former over waters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry made Israel pass through midst: courage to walk between divided waters, covenant entry, path opened and people emboldened to take it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry overthrew Pharaoh and host in Red Sea: miracle justice as well as mercy — same sea lane to Israelites grave to pursuers, there shook off Pharaoh and his host. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry led people through wilderness: conducted vast howling wilderness, led and fed them, camp victualled fortified constant series miracles forty years, though loitered wandered not lost; mercy and constancy more observable because often provoked him wilderness grieved desert. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry slew great kings: destroyed kings before them make room, not deposed banished but smote slew, appeared wrath against them but mercy never-failing to Israel; magnified they were great kings yet God subdued as easily as least weakest meanest children of men, wicked kings grandeur lustre would not secure from justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry slew famous kings: famous as well as great — more great famous more God's mercy to Israel appeared giving such kings for them, fame no shield against justice, mercy magnified by stature of fallen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry Sihon king of Amorites: particularly mentioned because first two conquered other side Jordan, detail of favours not gross view, each instance observe own mercy endureth for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry Og king of Bashan: second of first two conquered beyond Jordan, paired with Sihon as opening conquests making room for Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry gave their land heritage: he whose earth fulness world dwellers took land one people gave another as pleased him, iniquity Amorites now full therefore taken; Israel his servant though provoking wilderness yet intended some service out of them, to them pertained service of God; as said Egyptians Let my people go so Canaanites Let my people in that they may serve me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry heritage unto Israel his servant: land given not to strangers but servant people, service purpose, mercy endureth for ever because figure of heavenly Canaan, mercy of Lord Jesus Christ unto eternal life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry remembered us in low estate: many redemptions Jewish church out of oppressors, years servitude estate very low God remembered raised saviours judges and David at length gave rest all enemies; especially great redemption universal church types — remembered children of men low estate lost estate, sent Son redeem from sin death hell all spiritual enemies, sent redeem us not angels sinned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry redeemed us from enemies: church's redemption completed — saviours judges David rest from enemies as types, Christ's redemption from sin death hell spiritual enemies as substance, mercy enduring in deliverance itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry gives food to all flesh: instance mercy of providence wherever given life gives food agreeable sufficient, good housekeeper provides so large family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry thanks God of heaven: denotes glorious God, glory of mercy taken notice in praises, riches of glory displayed vessels of mercy (Rom 9:23); denotes great benefactor every good perfect gift from above Father of lights God of heaven, trace every stream fountain; this and that particular mercy may endure but while, mercy in God endures for ever inexhaustible fountain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

Connections to other passages
