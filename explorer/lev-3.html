The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And if his oblation be a sacrifice of peace offering, if he offer it of the herd; whether it be a male or female, he shall offer it without blemish before the LORD.

2 And he shall lay his hand upon the head of his offering, and kill it at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation: and Aaron’s sons the priests shall sprinkle the blood upon the altar round about.

3 And he shall offer of the sacrifice of the peace offering an offering made by fire unto the LORD; the fat that covereth the inwards, and all the fat that is upon the inwards,

4 And the two kidneys, and the fat that is on them, which is by the flanks, and the caul above the liver, with the kidneys, it shall he take away.

5 And Aaron’s sons shall burn it on the altar upon the burnt sacrifice, which is upon the wood that is on the fire: it is an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.

6 And if his offering for a sacrifice of peace offering unto the LORD be of the flock; male or female, he shall offer it without blemish.

7 If he offer a lamb for his offering, then shall he offer it before the LORD.

8 And he shall lay his hand upon the head of his offering, and kill it before the tabernacle of the congregation: and Aaron’s sons shall sprinkle the blood thereof round about upon the altar.

9 And he shall offer of the sacrifice of the peace offering an offering made by fire unto the LORD; the fat thereof, and the whole rump, it shall he take off hard by the backbone; and the fat that covereth the inwards, and all the fat that is upon the inwards,

10 And the two kidneys, and the fat that is upon them, which is by the flanks, and the caul above the liver, with the kidneys, it shall he take away.

11 And the priest shall burn it upon the altar: it is the food of the offering made by fire unto the LORD.

12 And if his offering be a goat, then he shall offer it before the LORD.

13 And he shall lay his hand upon the head of it, and kill it before the tabernacle of the congregation: and the sons of Aaron shall sprinkle the blood thereof upon the altar round about.

14 And he shall offer thereof his offering, even an offering made by fire unto the LORD; the fat that covereth the inwards, and all the fat that is upon the inwards,

15 And the two kidneys, and the fat that is upon them, which is by the flanks, and the caul above the liver, with the kidneys, it shall he take away.

16 And the priest shall burn them upon the altar: it is the food of the offering made by fire for a sweet savour: all the fat is the LORD’s.

17 It shall be a perpetual statute for your generations throughout all your dwellings, that ye eat neither fat nor blood.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the law of the peace offering, a voluntary offering of gratitude for benefits received or prayed for. Unlike the burnt offering, only the fat is burnt on the altar; the rest is shared as a sacred feast by the offerer and his friends, with portions for the priests. The same pattern is given for herd, flock, and goat, and the chapter closes with the standing statute that the fat and the blood are reserved to the LORD and must not be eaten. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Peace offering. A voluntary offering of gratitude or vow, festive in character, whose flesh is shared as a sacred feast after the fat is burnt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • The fat. The inward fat, kidneys, and caul reserved for the altar; the chapter's statute forbids eating it, because all the fat is the LORD's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. A peace offering is a voluntary tribute of gratitude, eucharistic for benefits already received or votive for benefits prayed for, and because it is festive either a male or a female without blemish may serve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The offerer lays a hand on the victim's head, kills it at the tabernacle door, and the priests sprinkle its blood round about the altar, following the same significant act as in the burnt offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Unlike the burnt offering, which is wholly consumed, only the fat of the peace offering is burnt, with a small part for the priests and the rest for the offerer and his friends, forming a sacred feast shared by the LORD, His priests, and His people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The kidneys with their fat and the caul above the liver are taken away with the inward fat, as the choicest parts reserved for burning on the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The priests burn these fat portions on the altar upon the burnt sacrifice, so that the peace offering's sweet savour rises together with the whole burnt offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. A flock peace offering follows the herd pattern, male or female without blemish being equally acceptable, since both are good for food and the offering is festive in character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. A lamb brought as a peace offering is presented before the LORD under the same rule, the kind of animal varying with the offerer's circumstances rather than with the meaning of the rite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The lamb is killed before the tabernacle with the hand laid on its head, and Aaron's sons sprinkle its blood round about the altar, as with the herd offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. From the sheep the whole rump is taken off hard by the backbone together with the inward fat, a provision explained by the broad-tailed Eastern sheep, whose tails were large, fat-rich, and prized. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The kidneys with their fat and the caul above the liver are removed with the rest of the inward fat, the same choice portions being reserved for the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The priest burns these portions on the altar as the food of the offering made by fire, while the remainder becomes the sacred feast of offerer and friends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. A goat may equally be a peace offering, and whichever of the three animals is chosen, the same general directions govern the ceremony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The goat is killed before the tabernacle with the hand laid on its head, and the sons of Aaron sprinkle its blood round about the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. From the goat, as from herd and flock, the fat covering and lying upon the inwards is offered by fire, the fat alone being the LORD's portion from the sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The kidneys with their fat and the caul above the liver are taken away with the inward fat, completing the altar portion of the goat offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The priest burns these portions on the altar for a sweet savour, a reminder that all the fat is the LORD's, even though the rest of the animal is shared as food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The standing statute forbidding fat and blood rests on this definition of the reserved fat, while the blood prohibition guards reverence for the Messiah who would shed His blood as an atoning sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
