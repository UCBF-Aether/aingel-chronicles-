The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Better is a dry morsel, and quietness therewith, than an house full of sacrifices with strife.

2 A wise servant shall have rule over a son that causeth shame, and shall have part of the inheritance among the brethren.

3 The fining pot is for silver, and the furnace for gold: but the LORD trieth the hearts.

4 A wicked doer giveth heed to false lips; and a liar giveth ear to a naughty tongue.

5 Whoso mocketh the poor reproacheth his Maker: and he that is glad at calamities shall not be unpunished.

6 Children’s children are the crown of old men; and the glory of children are their fathers.

7 Excellent speech becometh not a fool: much less do lying lips a prince.

8 A gift is as a precious stone in the eyes of him that hath it: whithersoever it turneth, it prospereth.

9 He that covereth a transgression seeketh love; but he that repeateth a matter separateth very friends.

10 A reproof entereth more into a wise man than an hundred stripes into a fool.

11 An evil man seeketh only rebellion: therefore a cruel messenger shall be sent against him.

12 Let a bear robbed of her whelps meet a man, rather than a fool in his folly.

13 Whoso rewardeth evil for good, evil shall not depart from his house.

14 The beginning of strife is as when one letteth out water: therefore leave off contention, before it be meddled with.

15 He that justifieth the wicked, and he that condemneth the just, even they both are abomination to the LORD.

16 Wherefore is there a price in the hand of a fool to get wisdom, seeing he hath no heart to it?

17 A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity.

18 A man void of understanding striketh hands, and becometh surety in the presence of his friend.

19 He loveth transgression that loveth strife: and he that exalteth his gate seeketh destruction.

20 He that hath a froward heart findeth no good: and he that hath a perverse tongue falleth into mischief.

21 He that begetteth a fool doeth it to his sorrow: and the father of a fool hath no joy.

22 A merry heart doeth good like a medicine: but a broken spirit drieth the bones.

23 A wicked man taketh a gift out of the bosom to pervert the ways of judgment.

24 Wisdom is before him that hath understanding; but the eyes of a fool are in the ends of the earth.

25 A foolish son is a grief to his father, and bitterness to her that bare him.

26 Also to punish the just is not good, nor to strike princes for equity.

27 He that hath knowledge spareth his words: and a man of understanding is of an excellent spirit.

28 Even a fool, when he holdeth his peace, is counted wise: and he that shutteth his lips is esteemed a man of understanding.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Better a dry morsel with quietness than a house full of sacrifices with strife, and the chapter tests friendship, speech and judgment: a friend loves at all times, a brother is born for adversity, while a fool's wrath is heavier than stone and sand. Covering a transgression seeks love, repeating a matter separates chief friends, and even a fool holding his peace is counted wise. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Better a dry morsel with quietness than a house full of sacrifices with strife, for these words recommend family love and peace as needful for the comfort of human life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. A wise servant shall have rule over a son causing shame, and part of the inheritance among brethren, for the wise servant is more deserving and more likely to appear one of the family than a profligate son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The fining pot is for silver and the furnace for gold, but the LORD tries the hearts, for God tries the heart by affliction, often showing the sin remaining in the believer's heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. A wicked doer gives heed to false lips, and a liar gives ear to a naughty tongue, for flatterers, especially false teachers, are welcome to those living in sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Whoso mocks the poor reproaches his Maker, and he glad at calamities shall not be unpunished, for those laughing at poverty treat God's providence and precepts with contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Children's children are the crown of old men, and the glory of children are their fathers, for it is an honour to children to have wise and godly parents continued to them even after they are grown and settled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Excellent speech becomes not a fool, much less lying lips a prince, for a fool in Solomon's Proverbs signifies a wicked man, whom excellent speech does not become because his conversation contradicts it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. A gift is as a precious stone in the eyes of him who has it, whithersoever it turns it prospers, for those setting their hearts on money will do anything for it, and we should consider what influence God's gifts should have on our hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. He who covers a transgression seeks love, but he who repeats a matter separates very friends, for the way to preserve peace is to make the best of everything and not notice what has been said or done against ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. A reproof enters more into a wise man than a hundred stripes into a fool, for a gentle reproof enters not only the head but the heart of a wise man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. An evil man seeks only rebellion, therefore a cruel messenger shall be sent against him, for Satan and Satan's messengers shall be let loose upon an evil man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Let a bear robbed of her whelps meet a man, rather than a fool in his folly, for we must watch over our own passions and avoid the company of furious men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Whoso rewards evil for good, evil shall not depart from his house, for rendering evil for good is devilish, and he who does so brings a curse upon his family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The beginning of strife is as when one lets out water, therefore leave off contention before it is meddled with, for what danger there is in the beginning of strife: resist its earliest display, and leave it off, if possible, before you begin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. He who justifies the wicked and he who condemns the just are both abomination to the LORD, for it is an offence to God to acquit the guilty or condemn those not guilty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Wherefore is there a price in the hand of a fool to get wisdom, seeing he has no heart to it, for man's neglect of God's favour and his own interest is very absurd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity, for no change of outward circumstances should abate affection for friends or relatives, though no friend except Christ deserves unlimited confidence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. A man void of understanding strikes hands and becomes surety in his friend's presence, for let none wrong their families, yet Christ becoming Surety for men was a glorious display of Divine wisdom, since he could discharge the bond. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. He loves transgression who loves strife, and he who exalts his gate seeks destruction, for to keep a clear conscience and quiet mind we must shun all excitements to anger, and a man living above his means goes the way to ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. He who has a froward heart finds no good, and he with a perverse tongue falls into mischief, for there is nothing got by ill designs, and many have paid dear for an unbridled tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. He who begets a fool does it to his sorrow, and the father of a fool has no joy, speaking plainly what many wise and good men feel strongly: how grievous it is to have a foolish, wicked child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. A merry heart does good like a medicine, but a broken spirit dries the bones, for it is great mercy that God gives us leave to be cheerful and cause to be cheerful, if by his grace he gives hearts to be cheerful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. A wicked man takes a gift out of the bosom to pervert the ways of judgment, for the wicked are ready to part with money, though loved, that they may not suffer for their crimes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Wisdom is before him who has understanding, but the eyes of a fool are in the ends of the earth, for the prudent man keeps God's word continually in view, while the foolish man cannot fix his thoughts nor pursue any purpose steadily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. A foolish son is a grief to his father and bitterness to her who bore him, for wicked children despise the authority of their father and the tenderness of their mother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Also to punish the just is not good, nor to strike princes for equity, for it is very wrong to find fault for doing what is duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. He who has knowledge spares his words, and a man of understanding is of an excellent spirit, for a man may show himself wise by good temper of mind and good government of his tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Even a fool, when he holds his peace, is counted wise, and he who shuts his lips is esteemed a man of understanding, for a wise man is careful when he speaks to speak to the purpose, and God knows his heart and cannot be deceived as men may be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
