The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — James 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 From whence come wars and fightings among you? come they not hence, even of your lusts that war in your members?

2 Ye lust, and have not: ye kill, and desire to have, and cannot obtain: ye fight and war, yet ye have not, because ye ask not.

3 Ye ask, and receive not, because ye ask amiss, that ye may consume it upon your lusts.

4 Ye adulterers and adulteresses, know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God.

5 Do ye think that the scripture saith in vain, The spirit that dwelleth in us lusteth to envy?

6 But he giveth more grace. Wherefore he saith, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.

7 Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

8 Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.

9 Be afflicted, and mourn, and weep: let your laughter be turned to mourning, and your joy to heaviness.

10 Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.

11 Speak not evil one of another, brethren. He that speaketh evil of his brother, and judgeth his brother, speaketh evil of the law, and judgeth the law: but if thou judge the law, thou art not a doer of the law, but a judge.

12 There is one lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy: who art thou that judgest another?

13 Go to now, ye that say, To day or to morrow we will go into such a city, and continue there a year, and buy and sell, and get gain:

14 Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour, that appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away.

15 For that ye ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that.

16 But now ye rejoice in your boastings: all such rejoicing is evil.

17 Therefore to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Quarrels and fights come from desires at war within, James argues: people lust, envy and kill, ask wrongly to spend on pleasures, and friendship with the world is enmity with God. The remedy is submission to God, resistance of the devil, drawing near to God, cleansing hands and hearts, and humility before the Lord, who alone is lawgiver and judge. Plans for tomorrow's trading must say, if the Lord wills, since life is a vapour, and knowing good and not doing it is sin. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Wars and fightings come from lusts that war in our members. Since all wars and fightings come from the corruptions of our own hearts, Henry says, it is right to mortify those lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. We lust and have not, kill and desire to have and cannot obtain, fight and war, yet have not because we ask not. Worldly and fleshly lusts are distempers, Henry notes, which will not allow content or satisfaction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. We ask and receive not because we ask amiss, to consume it upon our lusts. When men ask prosperity with wrong aims and intentions, it is just in God to deny them; prayers that are the language of lusts rather than graces will return empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Adulterers and adulteresses, friendship with the world is enmity with God. Here is a decided warning against criminal friendships with this world: an enemy may be reconciled, but enmity never can be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Do ye think the scripture speaks in vain? The spirit that dwells in us lusts to envy. Natural corruption shows itself by envying, Henry says, while the Holy Spirit teaches us to be willing to do good to all about us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. God gives more grace: he resists the proud but gives grace unto the humble. The proud resist God in understanding, will, and passions, so no wonder God resists the proud; the humble see their need of grace, pray for it, and are thankful for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Submit yourselves therefore to God; resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Submit understanding to the truth of God and will to the will of his precept and providence; if we yield to temptations the devil will follow us, but if we stand out against him he will leave us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you; cleanse your hands, ye sinners, and purify your hearts, ye double minded. Sinners are to submit to God and seek his grace and favour, resisting the devil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Be afflicted, and mourn, and weep; let laughter be turned to mourning and joy to heaviness. All sin must be wept over, Henry says, here in godly sorrow, or hereafter in eternal misery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up. The Lord will not refuse to comfort one who really mourns for sin, or to exalt one who humbles himself before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Speak not evil one of another, brethren; he that speaks evil of his brother and judges his brother speaks evil of the law and judges the law. Our lips must be governed by the law of kindness as well as truth and justice, Henry says. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. There is one lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy; who art thou that judgest another? We have the law of God as a rule to all, and must not set up our own notions as a rule to those about us, lest we be condemned of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Go to now, ye that say, today or tomorrow we will go into such a city, continue a year, buy and sell, and get gain. "Go to now" is a call to consider such conduct as wrong, Henry notes, and how apt contriving men are to leave God out of their plans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow; what is your life? It is even a vapour that appears for a little time and then vanishes away. The frailty, shortness, and uncertainty of life ought to check the vanity of all projects for futurity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. For that ye ought to say, if the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that. We are always to depend on the will of God; our times are not in our own hands but at the disposal of God, and all we design and do should be with submissive dependence on him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. But now ye rejoice in your boastings, and all such rejoicing is evil. It is foolish and hurtful to boast of worldly things and aspiring projects; it will bring great disappointment and prove destruction in the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. To him that knows to do good and does it not, to him it is sin. Omissions are sins which will be brought into judgment as well as commissions, Henry stresses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

  • adulterers and adulteresses. used figuratively here for unfaithfulness to God through friendship with the world (verse 4) Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
