The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Philippians 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Therefore, my brethren dearly beloved and longed for, my joy and crown, so stand fast in the Lord, my dearly beloved.

2 I beseech Euodias, and beseech Syntyche, that they be of the same mind in the Lord.

3 And I intreat thee also, true yokefellow, help those women which laboured with me in the gospel, with Clement also, and with other my fellowlabourers, whose names are in the book of life.

4 Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice.

5 Let your moderation be known unto all men. The Lord is at hand.

6 Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.

7 And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.

8 Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things.

9 Those things, which ye have both learned, and received, and heard, and seen in me, do: and the God of peace shall be with you.

10 But I rejoiced in the Lord greatly, that now at the last your care of me hath flourished again; wherein ye were also careful, but ye lacked opportunity.

11 Not that I speak in respect of want: for I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content.

12 I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound: every where and in all things I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need.

13 I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.

14 Notwithstanding ye have well done, that ye did communicate with my affliction.

15 Now ye Philippians know also, that in the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no church communicated with me as concerning giving and receiving, but ye only.

16 For even in Thessalonica ye sent once and again unto my necessity.

17 Not because I desire a gift: but I desire fruit that may abound to your account.

18 But I have all, and abound: I am full, having received of Epaphroditus the things which were sent from you, an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable, wellpleasing to God.

19 But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.

20 Now unto God and our Father be glory for ever and ever. Amen.

21 Salute every saint in Christ Jesus. The brethren which are with me greet you.

22 All the saints salute you, chiefly they that are of Caesar’s household.

23 The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul pleads with Euodia and Syntyche to agree in the Lord, then gives the letter's best-known encouragements: rejoice always, let gentleness be known, be anxious for nothing but pray about everything, and think on whatever is true, honourable, just and lovely. Content in plenty and in need through Christ who strengthens him, he thanks the Philippians for gifts sent again and again. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Therefore, my brethren dearly beloved and longed for, my joy and crown, so stand fast in the Lord, my dearly beloved. Dearly beloved and longed for, his joy and crown, they are to stand fast in the Lord, for a minister's crown is a standing church, and love's terms urge steadfastness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. I beseech Euodia, and beseech Syntyche, that they be of the same mind in the Lord. Euodia and Syntyche, each besought by name, are to be of the same mind in the Lord, for two honoured women at variance endanger the church's unity, and Paul pleads, not commands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. I entreat you also, true yokefellow, help those women which laboured with me in the gospel, with Clement also, and with other fellow labourers, whose names are in the book of life. The true yokefellow is entreated to help these labouring women, with Clement and fellow labourers whose names are in life's book, for past labour deserves present help, and heaven's book remembers what men forget. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Rejoice in the Lord always, and again I say, Rejoice. Rejoicing in the Lord always, repeated as again rejoice, is the standing command, for joy in the Lord, not in circumstances, can be always. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Let your moderation be known unto all men. The Lord is at hand. Moderation known to all men, with the Lord at hand, is the church's witness, for gentleness before all, under a near Lord, restrains both passion and fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Be careful for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. Careful for nothing, but in everything by prayer, supplication, and thanksgiving, requests are made known to God, for prayer replaces anxiety, and thanksgiving seasons every petition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The peace of God, which passes all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus. God's peace, passing all understanding, shall keep hearts and minds through Christ, for peace beyond reason guards what reason cannot, like a garrison around the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Finally, brethren, whatever things are true, whatever things are honest, whatever things are just, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are of good report, if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things. True, honest, just, pure, lovely, and of good report, with any virtue and praise, are to fill thought, for the mind's furniture decides the life's course. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Those things, which you have both learned, and received, and heard, and seen in me, do, and the God of peace shall be with you. Learned, received, heard, and seen in Paul, these things done bring the God of peace with them, for practice, not only precept, secures God's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. But I rejoiced in the Lord greatly, that now at the last your care of me has flourished again, wherein you were also careful, but you lacked opportunity. He rejoiced greatly that their care flourished again, for they were careful but lacked opportunity, and their revived gift is like a plant flowering after winter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Not that I speak in respect of want, for I have learned, in whatever state I am, therewith to be content. Not speaking of want, he has learned contentment in whatever state, for contentment is learned, not natural, and the lesson holds in every condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound: everywhere and in all things I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. Abased and abounding, full and hungry, abounding and in need, he is instructed in all, for contentment's school has two lessons, poverty and plenty, and he has learned both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. I can do all things through Christ which strengthens me. All things through Christ who strengthens him is the secret of that contentment, for strength is not his own but Christ's, and through it every state is bearable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Notwithstanding you have well done, that you did communicate with my affliction. They did well to communicate with his affliction, for sharing a prisoner's suffering by gift is fellowship in deed, and Paul commends what he did not need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Now you Philippians know also, that in the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no church communicated with me as concerning giving and receiving, but you only. In the gospel's beginning, on leaving Macedonia, no church but Philippi shared giving and receiving with him, for their generosity was singular from the start. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. For even in Thessalonica you sent once and again unto my necessity. Even in Thessalonica they sent once and again to his necessity, for their giving was repeated, not single, and distance did not cool it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Not because I desire a gift, but I desire fruit that may abound to your account. Not desiring a gift, he desires fruit abounding to their account, for the giver's profit, not the receiver's, is the apostle's aim, and generosity is credited to heaven's ledger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. But I have all, and abound: I am full, having received of Epaphroditus the things which were sent from you, an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable, well pleasing to God. Having all and abounding, full by Epaphroditus' delivery, he counts their gift a sweet-smelling sacrifice, acceptable and pleasing to God, for charity to a minister is worship to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus. His God shall supply all their need according to riches in glory by Christ, for the God who received their gift will return supply from his own abundance, measured by glory, not by their gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Now unto God and our Father be glory for ever and ever. Amen. To God and Father be glory for ever and ever, Amen, for supply, sacrifice, and all grace end in doxology, and eternity is the only fit measure for God's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Salute every saint in Christ Jesus. The brethren which are with me greet you. Every saint is to be saluted, and the brethren with him greet them, for greetings bind the churches, and no saint is too small for a salutation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. All the saints salute you, chiefly they that are of Caesar's household. All saints salute them, chiefly Caesar's household, for the gospel had entered the palace itself, and Paul's bonds had won saints in the emperor's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen. Grace of the Lord Jesus Christ with them all, Amen, closes the letter where it began, for grace is both its greeting and its farewell, and all the church's blessings are summed in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
