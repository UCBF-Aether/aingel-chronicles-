The Text — 2 Samuel 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass after this, that David enquired of the LORD, saying, Shall I go up into any of the cities of Judah? And the LORD said unto him, Go up. And David said, Whither shall I go up? And he said, Unto Hebron.

2 So David went up thither, and his two wives also, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess, and Abigail Nabal’s wife the Carmelite.

3 And his men that were with him did David bring up, every man with his household: and they dwelt in the cities of Hebron.

4 And the men of Judah came, and there they anointed David king over the house of Judah. And they told David, saying, That the men of Jabeshgilead were they that buried Saul.

5 And David sent messengers unto the men of Jabeshgilead, and said unto them, Blessed be ye of the LORD, that ye have shewed this kindness unto your lord, even unto Saul, and have buried him.

6 And now the LORD shew kindness and truth unto you: and I also will requite you this kindness, because ye have done this thing.

7 Therefore now let your hands be strengthened, and be ye valiant: for your master Saul is dead, and also the house of Judah have anointed me king over them.

8 But Abner the son of Ner, captain of Saul’s host, took Ishbosheth the son of Saul, and brought him over to Mahanaim;

9 And made him king over Gilead, and over the Ashurites, and over Jezreel, and over Ephraim, and over Benjamin, and over all Israel.

10 Ishbosheth Saul’s son was forty years old when he began to reign over Israel, and reigned two years. But the house of Judah followed David.

11 And the time that David was king in Hebron over the house of Judah was seven years and six months.

12 And Abner the son of Ner, and the servants of Ishbosheth the son of Saul, went out from Mahanaim to Gibeon.

13 And Joab the son of Zeruiah, and the servants of David, went out, and met together by the pool of Gibeon: and they sat down, the one on the one side of the pool, and the other on the other side of the pool.

14 And Abner said to Joab, Let the young men now arise, and play before us. And Joab said, Let them arise.

15 Then there arose and went over by number twelve of Benjamin, which pertained to Ishbosheth the son of Saul, and twelve of the servants of David.

16 And they caught every one his fellow by the head, and thrust his sword in his fellow’s side; so they fell down together: wherefore that place was called Helkathhazzurim, which is in Gibeon.

17 And there was a very sore battle that day; and Abner was beaten, and the men of Israel, before the servants of David.

18 And there were three sons of Zeruiah there, Joab, and Abishai, and Asahel: and Asahel was as light of foot as a wild roe.

19 And Asahel pursued after Abner; and in going he turned not to the right hand nor to the left from following Abner.

20 Then Abner looked behind him, and said, Art thou Asahel? And he answered, I am.

21 And Abner said to him, Turn thee aside to thy right hand or to thy left, and lay thee hold on one of the young men, and take thee his armour. But Asahel would not turn aside from following of him.

22 And Abner said again to Asahel, Turn thee aside from following me: wherefore should I smite thee to the ground? how then should I hold up my face to Joab thy brother?

23 Howbeit he refused to turn aside: wherefore Abner with the hinder end of the spear smote him under the fifth rib, that the spear came out behind him; and he fell down there, and died in the same place: and it came to pass, that as many as came to the place where Asahel fell down and died stood still.

24 Joab also and Abishai pursued after Abner: and the sun went down when they were come to the hill of Ammah, that lieth before Giah by the way of the wilderness of Gibeon.

25 And the children of Benjamin gathered themselves together after Abner, and became one troop, and stood on the top of an hill.

26 Then Abner called to Joab, and said, Shall the sword devour for ever? knowest thou not that it will be bitterness in the latter end? how long shall it be then, ere thou bid the people return from following their brethren?

27 And Joab said, As God liveth, unless thou hadst spoken, surely then in the morning the people had gone up every one from following his brother.

28 So Joab blew a trumpet, and all the people stood still, and pursued after Israel no more, neither fought they any more.

29 And Abner and his men walked all that night through the plain, and passed over Jordan, and went through all Bithron, and they came to Mahanaim.

30 And Joab returned from following Abner: and when he had gathered all the people together, there lacked of David’s servants nineteen men and Asahel.

31 But the servants of David had smitten of Benjamin, and of Abner’s men, so that three hundred and threescore men died.

32 And they took up Asahel, and buried him in the sepulchre of his father, which was in Bethlehem. And Joab and his men went all night, and they came to Hebron at break of day.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

David is anointed king over Judah at Hebron, while Abner sets up Saul's son Ish-bosheth over the rest of Israel, so the kingdom splits in two. A battle at the pool of Gibeon turns bloody, and Abner kills Asahel, Joab's brother, as he flees, a killing that will poison everything that follows. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Even with God's promise secure, David still enquires of the Lord before moving, asking whether and where to go up. Henry draws the lesson that assurance of hope should quicken pious endeavour, not replace it, and God directs him to Hebron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. David goes up with his two wives, Ahinoam and Abigail. The move is a household migration under God's direction, not a solitary military advance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. His men go up with their households and settle in the cities of Hebron. In all our journeys, Henry notes, it is comfortable to see God going before us when by faith and prayer we set him before us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The men of Judah anoint David king over the house of Judah, and tell him the men of Jabeshgilead buried Saul. David rises gradually, Henry observes, as the kingdom of the Messiah, the Son of David, is set up by degrees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. David blesses the men of Jabeshgilead for showing kindness to their lord Saul in burying him. His first royal act honours his predecessor rather than celebrating his own rise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. David prays the Lord will show them kindness and truth, and promises to requite their kindness himself. Gratitude toward those who honoured Saul becomes part of how David secures loyalty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. David urges them to be strong and valiant, telling them Judah has anointed him king. He appeals to them as a king already chosen, yet still courting the allegiance of Saul's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Abner, Saul's captain, takes Ishbosheth across to Mahanaim and sets him up as a rival king. The nation in general refused David, Henry notes, and by this the Lord trained his servant for future honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Ishbosheth is made king over Gilead, Jezreel, Ephraim, Benjamin, and all Israel. The division is a rival claim to the whole kingdom, not merely a tribal remnant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Ishbosheth, forty years old, reigns two years, while Judah follows David. The short, contested reign measures how much of Israel still clung to Saul's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. David reigns in Hebron over Judah seven years and six months. His long, partial reign is the slow road by which God brings him to the whole kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Abner's men march from Mahanaim to Gibeon, and Joab's men meet them at the pool. Two armies face each other across the water, with civil war already arranged before a word is spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The two forces sit on opposite sides of the pool of Gibeon, poised for battle. The standoff shows a nation divided against itself, Judah against Israel, with no enemy but each other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 16. Each man seizes his fellow and thrusts his sword into his side, so all fall together, and the place is named Helkathhazzurim. The mutual slaughter exposes the folly of treating war as a game. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The duel ignites a sore battle, and Abner and Israel are beaten before David's servants. David is herein a type of Christ, Henry notes, whom Israel would not submit to though anointed by the Father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Henry notes distinctions are made between the dust of some and others, but in the resurrection the only lasting difference is between the godly and the ungodly.
