The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 24 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Be not thou envious against evil men, neither desire to be with them.

2 For their heart studieth destruction, and their lips talk of mischief.

3 Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established:

4 And by knowledge shall the chambers be filled with all precious and pleasant riches.

5 A wise man is strong; yea, a man of knowledge increaseth strength.

6 For by wise counsel thou shalt make thy war: and in multitude of counsellors there is safety.

7 Wisdom is too high for a fool: he openeth not his mouth in the gate.

8 He that deviseth to do evil shall be called a mischievous person.

9 The thought of foolishness is sin: and the scorner is an abomination to men.

10 If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small.

11 If thou forbear to deliver them that are drawn unto death, and those that are ready to be slain;

12 If thou sayest, Behold, we knew it not; doth not he that pondereth the heart consider it? and he that keepeth thy soul, doth not he know it? and shall not he render to every man according to his works?

13 My son, eat thou honey, because it is good; and the honeycomb, which is sweet to thy taste:

14 So shall the knowledge of wisdom be unto thy soul: when thou hast found it, then there shall be a reward, and thy expectation shall not be cut off.

15 Lay not wait, O wicked man, against the dwelling of the righteous; spoil not his resting place:

16 For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.

17 Rejoice not when thine enemy falleth, and let not thine heart be glad when he stumbleth:

18 Lest the LORD see it, and it displease him, and he turn away his wrath from him.

19 Fret not thyself because of evil men, neither be thou envious at the wicked:

20 For there shall be no reward to the evil man; the candle of the wicked shall be put out.

21 My son, fear thou the LORD and the king: and meddle not with them that are given to change:

22 For their calamity shall rise suddenly; and who knoweth the ruin of them both?

23 These things also belong to the wise. It is not good to have respect of persons in judgment.

24 He that saith unto the wicked, Thou are righteous; him shall the people curse, nations shall abhor him:

25 But to them that rebuke him shall be delight, and a good blessing shall come upon them.

26 Every man shall kiss his lips that giveth a right answer.

27 Prepare thy work without, and make it fit for thyself in the field; and afterwards build thine house.

28 Be not a witness against thy neighbour without cause; and deceive not with thy lips.

29 Say not, I will do so to him as he hath done to me: I will render to the man according to his work.

30 I went by the field of the slothful, and by the vineyard of the man void of understanding;

31 And, lo, it was all grown over with thorns, and nettles had covered the face thereof, and the stone wall thereof was broken down.

32 Then I saw, and considered it well: I looked upon it, and received instruction.

33 Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep:

34 So shall thy poverty come as one that travelleth; and thy want as an armed man.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Do not envy evil men or desire their company, but build a house through wisdom, fill its chambers by knowledge, and make war by wise counsel. The just man falls seven times and rises again, the sluggard's field overgrown with thorns teaches the same lesson as the ant, and the chapter closes its wise sayings with impartial judgment, honest answers, work prepared before the house is built, and no revenge in kind. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 8. He who devises to do evil shall be called a mischievous person, for it is bad to do evil, but worse to devise it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The thought of foolishness is sin, and the scorner is an abomination to men, for even the first risings of sin in the heart are sin and must be repented of, and those striving to make others hateful make themselves so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small, for under troubles we are apt to despair of relief, but be of good courage, and God shall strengthen thy heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. If thou forbear to deliver them drawn unto death, and those ready to be slain, remember: if a neighbour is in danger by unjust proceeding, a man is bound to do all in his power to deliver him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. If thou sayest, Behold, we knew it not, does not he who ponders the heart consider it, and shall not he render to every man according to his works, for what is it to suffer immortal souls to perish when our persuasions and example may prevent it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. My son, eat thou honey, because it is good, and the honeycomb sweet to thy taste, for we are quickened to wisdom's study by considering both its pleasure and profit, as all men relish what is sweet to the palate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. So shall the knowledge of wisdom be unto thy soul, and when found, there shall be a reward, and thy expectation shall not be cut off, for many have no relish for things sweet to the purified soul that make us wise unto salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Lay not wait, O wicked man, against the dwelling of the righteous, and spoil not his resting place, for the sincere soul falls as a traveller stumbling at a stone, but gets up and goes on with more care and speed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. For a just man falls seven times and rises up again, but the wicked shall fall into mischief, for the sincere soul falls as a traveller may, but gets up and goes on, rather falls into affliction than into actual sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 19. Fret not thyself because of evil men, neither be thou envious at the wicked, for envy not the wicked their prosperity, and be sure there is no true happiness in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. For there shall be no reward to the evil man, and the candle of the wicked shall be put out, for envy not the wicked their prosperity, since there is no true happiness in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. My son, fear thou the LORD and the king, and meddle not with them given to change, for the godly in the land will be quiet in the land, and though there may be cause to change for the better, have nothing to do with them given to change. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. For their calamity shall rise suddenly, and who knows the ruin of them both, for the godly will be quiet in the land, and those given to change face sudden calamity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. These things also belong to the wise, and it is not good to have respect of persons in judgment, for the wisdom God gives renders a man fit for his station. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. He who says to the wicked, Thou art righteous, him shall the people curse, and nations abhor him, for the wisdom God gives renders a man fit for his station, and just judgment without partiality is its mark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. But to them that rebuke him shall be delight, and a good blessing shall come upon them, for the wisdom God gives renders a man fit for his station. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Every man shall kiss his lips that gives a right answer, for every one who finds the benefit of the right answer will be attached to him who gave it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Prepare thy work without, and make it fit for thyself in the field, and afterwards build thine house, for we must prefer necessaries before conveniences, and not go in debt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Be not a witness against thy neighbour without cause, and deceive not with thy lips, for there are three defaults in a witness pointed out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Say not, I will do so to him as he has done to me, I will render to the man according to his work, for there are three defaults in a witness pointed out, including retaliation by false witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. I went by the field of the slothful, and by the vineyard of the man void of understanding, for see what a blessing the husbandman's calling is, and what a wilderness earth would be without it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. And, lo, it was all grown over with thorns, and nettles had covered its face, and its stone wall was broken down, for fields overgrown with thorns and fences broken down are an emblem of many souls' deplorable state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. Then I saw, and considered it well, I looked upon it, and received instruction, for sloth and self-indulgence are the bane of all good, and we should show wisdom by doubling diligence in every good thing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep, for sloth and self-indulgence are the bane of all good, and every vile affection grows in men's hearts while they compose themselves to sleep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. So shall thy poverty come as one that travels, and thy want as an armed man, for sloth and self-indulgence are the bane of all good, and diligence in every good thing is wisdom's answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
