The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ezra 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, that the word of the LORD by the mouth of Jeremiah might be fulfilled, the LORD stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, that he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom, and put it also in writing, saying,

2 Thus saith Cyrus king of Persia, The LORD God of heaven hath given me all the kingdoms of the earth; and he hath charged me to build him an house at Jerusalem, which is in Judah.

3 Who is there among you of all his people? his God be with him, and let him go up to Jerusalem, which is in Judah, and build the house of the LORD God of Israel, (he is the God,) which is in Jerusalem.

4 And whosoever remaineth in any place where he sojourneth, let the men of his place help him with silver, and with gold, and with goods, and with beasts, beside the freewill offering for the house of God that is in Jerusalem.

5 Then rose up the chief of the fathers of Judah and Benjamin, and the priests, and the Levites, with all them whose spirit God had raised, to go up to build the house of the LORD which is in Jerusalem.

6 And all they that were about them strengthened their hands with vessels of silver, with gold, with goods, and with beasts, and with precious things, beside all that was willingly offered.

7 Also Cyrus the king brought forth the vessels of the house of the LORD, which Nebuchadnezzar had brought forth out of Jerusalem, and had put them in the house of his gods;

8 Even those did Cyrus king of Persia bring forth by the hand of Mithredath the treasurer, and numbered them unto Sheshbazzar, the prince of Judah.

9 And this is the number of them: thirty chargers of gold, a thousand chargers of silver, nine and twenty knives,

10 Thirty basons of gold, silver basons of a second sort four hundred and ten, and other vessels a thousand.

11 All the vessels of gold and of silver were five thousand and four hundred. All these did Sheshbazzar bring up with them of the captivity that were brought up from Babylon unto Jerusalem.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In Cyrus’s first year, the word of the LORD by Jeremiah is fulfilled: God stirs Cyrus king of Persia to proclaim throughout his kingdom, in writing, that the God of heaven has charged him to build a house at Jerusalem. Whoever of God’s people will may go up, neighbours are to help with silver, gold, goods, and beasts, and Cyrus himself brings out the temple vessels Nebuchadnezzar had taken, counts them by Mithredath to Sheshbazzar, prince of Judah — five thousand and four hundred vessels of gold and silver in all — and Sheshbazzar brings them up from Babylon to Jerusalem. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text in the Text panel above

Unfamiliar terms

  • Chargers and basons. Interpreted Vessels in the temple inventory counted out to Sheshbazzar (verses 9–10). Ezra 1:9–10, Text panel
  • Sheshbazzar. Documented The prince of Judah to whom the vessels were numbered (verse 8). Ezra 1:8, Text panel

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Cyrus acts in his very first year, so that Jeremiah's word about seventy years is fulfilled at once. Henry stresses that God stirred a heathen king's spirit, showing that even empires serve God's purposes for his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Cyrus confesses that the God of heaven gave him all the kingdoms of the earth and charged him to build a house at Jerusalem. JFB notes this language shows God using a conqueror who at least acknowledged Israel's God as supreme. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The proclamation is an open invitation, not a forced march: whoever is of God's people may go up. Henry reads the added 'his God be with him' as both permission and blessing on the return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Those who stayed behind were to help the returners with silver, gold, goods and beasts, besides freewill offerings. JFB treats this as neighbours bearing the cost of worship, as Egypt once enriched Israel at the Exodus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The heads of Judah and Benjamin, the priests and Levites rose up, with all whose spirit God had raised. Henry notes the double movement: a royal decree without, and God's stirring within, without which no one would have left Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Neighbours strengthened their hands with silver, gold, goods, beasts and precious things. The gifts match the needs of a journey and a building, and Henry sees God's providence supplying both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Cyrus brought out the temple vessels Nebuchadnezzar had carried off and lodged in the house of his gods. Their return reverses the plunder of Jerusalem and restores what idolatry had seized. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The vessels were handed over by Mithredath the treasurer and counted out to Sheshbazzar, prince of Judah. JFB identifies Sheshbazzar with Zerubbabel, the hereditary prince recognised among the exiles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The inventory begins with thirty gold chargers, a thousand silver chargers and twenty-nine knives. JFB notes these are the larger vessels only, the detailed count proving the restoration was careful, not careless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Thirty gold basins, four hundred and ten silver basins of a second sort, and a thousand other vessels complete the list. Each class is counted, so that nothing sacred is lost in the transfer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. All the gold and silver vessels numbered five thousand and four hundred, and Sheshbazzar brought them all up from Babylon to Jerusalem. JFB explains the gross sum exceeds the itemised list because small vessels are folded into the total. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
