The Text — 2 Kings 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the second year of Joash son of Jehoahaz king of Israel reigned Amaziah the son of Joash king of Judah.

2 He was twenty and five years old when he began to reign, and reigned twenty and nine years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Jehoaddan of Jerusalem.

3 And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, yet not like David his father: he did according to all things as Joash his father did.

4 Howbeit the high places were not taken away: as yet the people did sacrifice and burnt incense on the high places.

5 And it came to pass, as soon as the kingdom was confirmed in his hand, that he slew his servants which had slain the king his father.

6 But the children of the murderers he slew not: according unto that which is written in the book of the law of Moses, wherein the LORD commanded, saying, The fathers shall not be put to death for the children, nor the children be put to death for the fathers; but every man shall be put to death for his own sin.

7 He slew of Edom in the valley of salt ten thousand, and took Selah by war, and called the name of it Joktheel unto this day.

8 Then Amaziah sent messengers to Jehoash, the son of Jehoahaz son of Jehu, king of Israel, saying, Come, let us look one another in the face.

9 And Jehoash the king of Israel sent to Amaziah king of Judah, saying, The thistle that was in Lebanon sent to the cedar that was in Lebanon, saying, Give thy daughter to my son to wife: and there passed by a wild beast that was in Lebanon, and trode down the thistle.

10 Thou hast indeed smitten Edom, and thine heart hath lifted thee up: glory of this, and tarry at home: for why shouldest thou meddle to thy hurt, that thou shouldest fall, even thou, and Judah with thee?

11 But Amaziah would not hear. Therefore Jehoash king of Israel went up; and he and Amaziah king of Judah looked one another in the face at Bethshemesh, which belongeth to Judah.

12 And Judah was put to the worse before Israel; and they fled every man to their tents.

13 And Jehoash king of Israel took Amaziah king of Judah, the son of Jehoash the son of Ahaziah, at Bethshemesh, and came to Jerusalem, and brake down the wall of Jerusalem from the gate of Ephraim unto the corner gate, four hundred cubits.

14 And he took all the gold and silver, and all the vessels that were found in the house of the LORD, and in the treasures of the king’s house, and hostages, and returned to Samaria.

15 Now the rest of the acts of Jehoash which he did, and his might, and how he fought with Amaziah king of Judah, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

16 And Jehoash slept with his fathers, and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel; and Jeroboam his son reigned in his stead.

17 And Amaziah the son of Joash king of Judah lived after the death of Jehoash son of Jehoahaz king of Israel fifteen years.

18 And the rest of the acts of Amaziah, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

19 Now they made a conspiracy against him in Jerusalem: and he fled to Lachish; but they sent after him to Lachish, and slew him there.

20 And they brought him on horses: and he was buried at Jerusalem with his fathers in the city of David.

21 And all the people of Judah took Azariah, which was sixteen years old, and made him king instead of his father Amaziah.

22 He built Elath, and restored it to Judah, after that the king slept with his fathers.

23 In the fifteenth year of Amaziah the son of Joash king of Judah Jeroboam the son of Joash king of Israel began to reign in Samaria, and reigned forty and one years.

24 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD: he departed not from all the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin.

25 He restored the coast of Israel from the entering of Hamath unto the sea of the plain, according to the word of the LORD God of Israel, which he spake by the hand of his servant Jonah, the son of Amittai, the prophet, which was of Gathhepher.

26 For the LORD saw the affliction of Israel, that it was very bitter: for there was not any shut up, nor any left, nor any helper for Israel.

27 And the LORD said not that he would blot out the name of Israel from under heaven: but he saved them by the hand of Jeroboam the son of Joash.

28 Now the rest of the acts of Jeroboam, and all that he did, and his might, how he warred, and how he recovered Damascus, and Hamath, which belonged to Judah, for Israel, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

29 And Jeroboam slept with his fathers, even with the kings of Israel; and Zachariah his son reigned in his stead.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Amaziah of Judah defeats Edom and challenges Joash of Israel, who answers with the parable of thistle and cedar and crushes Judah at Beth-shemesh, plundering Jerusalem. In Israel Jeroboam the second restores the border, and the chapter ends with both kings' deaths and successions. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the second year of Joash son of Jehoahaz king of Israel, Amaziah son of Joash king of Judah began to reign. JFB's section "Amaziah's good reign over Judah" opens with this accession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Amaziah was twenty-five when he began to reign and reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem, his mother Jehoaddan of Jerusalem. JFB's section frames the beginning of his reign as excellent, acting as a constitutional king according to God's law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Amaziah did right in the LORD's sight, yet not like David his father, doing according to all his father Joash did. JFB notes his reign began excellently yet not with perfect sincerity of heart, and his early promise was belied by his later devious course and the public irregularities he tolerated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Howbeit the high places were not taken away, and the people still sacrificed and burnt incense on them. JFB cites these tolerated irregularities as part of the failure of Amaziah's later course. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. As soon as the kingdom was confirmed in his hand, Amaziah slew his servants who had slain his father the king. JFB calls this an act of justice and filial piety, noting the assassins must have had considerable weight, since the king dared not proceed against them until his power was fully consolidated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But the children of the murderers he slew not, according to the law of Moses that fathers shall not be put to death for children, nor children for fathers. JFB praises this moderation, inspired by the Mosaic law, as directly contrary to ancient custom, under which all connected with criminals were doomed to unsparing destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Amaziah slew ten thousand Edomites in the valley of salt, took Selah by war, and called it Joktheel to this day. JFB explains the Edomites had revolted under Joram, and Amaziah, determined to reduce them, routed their army and took their capital; the valley of salt was the salt plain south of the Dead Sea, Selah was "the rock," generally thought to be Petra, and Joktheel meant "given or conquered by God." Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Amaziah sent messengers to Jehoash king of Israel, saying, "Come, let us look one another in the face." JFB calls this bold, haughty challenge most probably stimulated by desire for satisfaction over discharged Israelite auxiliaries' outrages and revenge for Jehu's massacre of his ancestors, springing from pride inspired by the Edomite victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Jehoash answered with the fable of the thistle in Lebanon that sent to the cedar seeking marriage for his son, until a wild beast passed by and trode down the thistle. JFB explains Eastern speakers often used parables for unwelcome truths: the thistle represented Amaziah, a petty prince, the cedar the powerful king of Israel, and the wild beast the overwhelming army that could desolate Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Jehoash warned that Amaziah's heart was lifted up by smiting Edom, and he should glory in that and tarry at home rather than meddle to his hurt and Judah's fall. JFB notes the moral of the fable is contained here: pride towering beyond its natural sphere is sure to fall with a sudden, ruinous crash. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. But Amaziah would not hear, so Jehoash went up, and the two kings faced each other at Bethshemesh in Judah. JFB explains the sarcastic reply incited Amaziah the more, for in judicial blindness he was immovably determined on war, but Joash's superior energy surprised him before his preparations were complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Judah was put to the worse before Israel, and every man fled to his tent. JFB records Joash routing Amaziah's army in pitched battle and taking him prisoner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Jehoash took Amaziah at Bethshemesh, came to Jerusalem, broke down its wall from the Ephraim gate to the corner gate, four hundred cubits. JFB notes Joash marched to Jerusalem, demolished part of its walls, and plundered palace and temple treasures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Jehoash took all the gold, silver, and vessels found in the LORD's house and the king's house, with hostages, and returned to Samaria. JFB notes he took hostages to prevent further molestation from Judah, then returned with all speed, his forces being needed against Syrian incursions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Amaziah son of Joash king of Judah lived fifteen years after the death of Jehoash son of Jehoahaz king of Israel. JFB's section "He is slain by a conspiracy" opens with this interval before Amaziah's fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. A conspiracy was made against Amaziah in Jerusalem, and he fled to Lachish, but they sent after him to Lachish and slew him there. JFB explains his apostasy was followed by maladministration, and the ruin of Jerusalem, temple plunder, and hostage children lost him the respect of grandees and subjects alike, so he fled in terror to the Philistine frontier town, where he was traced and murdered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. They brought Amaziah on horses and buried him at Jerusalem with his fathers in the city of David. JFB notes his friends had his corpse brought without pomp in a chariot to Jerusalem, where he was interred among his royal ancestors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. All the people of Judah took Azariah, sixteen years old, and made him king instead of his father Amaziah. JFB notes Azariah, or Uzziah, succeeded because popular opposition was directed against Amaziah personally, not extended to his family or heir. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Azariah built Elath and restored it to Judah after his father slept with his fathers. JFB explains Elath was a fortified seaport that had revolted with Edom but was now recovered, completing the Edomite conquest his father had left unfinished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. In the fifteenth year of Amaziah, Jeroboam son of Joash king of Israel began to reign in Samaria for forty-one years. JFB identifies him as Jeroboam II, who, on regaining lost territory, raised the kingdom to great political power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Jeroboam did evil in the LORD's sight, departing not from all the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat. JFB notes he adhered to the favourite religious policy of Israel's sovereigns even while God granted national prosperity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Jeroboam restored Israel's coast from the entering of Hamath to the sea of the plain, according to the LORD's word by Jonah son of Amittai. JFB notes this regaining of lost territory raised the kingdom to great political power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The LORD saw Israel's affliction was very bitter, with none shut up or left, and no helper for Israel. JFB notes the reason for God's granting such prosperity is expressly stated in these verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The LORD had not said he would blot out Israel's name from under heaven, but saved them by Jeroboam son of Joash. JFB explains the divine covenant's purposes forbade as yet the overthrow of the ten tribes' kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms
