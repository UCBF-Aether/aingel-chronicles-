The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 27 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the beginning of the reign of Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah came this word unto Jeremiah from the LORD, saying,

2 Thus saith the LORD to me; Make thee bonds and yokes, and put them upon thy neck,

3 And send them to the king of Edom, and to the king of Moab, and to the king of the Ammonites, and to the king of Tyrus, and to the king of Zidon, by the hand of the messengers which come to Jerusalem unto Zedekiah king of Judah;

4 And command them to say unto their masters, Thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Thus shall ye say unto your masters;

5 I have made the earth, the man and the beast that are upon the ground, by my great power and by my outstretched arm, and have given it unto whom it seemed meet unto me.

6 And now have I given all these lands into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, my servant; and the beasts of the field have I given him also to serve him.

7 And all nations shall serve him, and his son, and his son’s son, until the very time of his land come: and then many nations and great kings shall serve themselves of him.

8 And it shall come to pass, that the nation and kingdom which will not serve the same Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, and that will not put their neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon, that nation will I punish, saith the LORD, with the sword, and with the famine, and with the pestilence, until I have consumed them by his hand.

9 Therefore hearken not ye to your prophets, nor to your diviners, nor to your dreamers, nor to your enchanters, nor to your sorcerers, which speak unto you, saying, Ye shall not serve the king of Babylon:

10 For they prophesy a lie unto you, to remove you far from your land; and that I should drive you out, and ye should perish.

11 But the nations that bring their neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon, and serve him, those will I let remain still in their own land, saith the LORD; and they shall till it, and dwell therein.

12 I spake also to Zedekiah king of Judah according to all these words, saying, Bring your necks under the yoke of the king of Babylon, and serve him and his people, and live.

13 Why will ye die, thou and thy people, by the sword, by the famine, and by the pestilence, as the LORD hath spoken against the nation that will not serve the king of Babylon?

14 Therefore hearken not unto the words of the prophets that speak unto you, saying, Ye shall not serve the king of Babylon: for they prophesy a lie unto you.

15 For I have not sent them, saith the LORD, yet they prophesy a lie in my name; that I might drive you out, and that ye might perish, ye, and the prophets that prophesy unto you.

16 Also I spake to the priests and to all this people, saying, Thus saith the LORD; Hearken not to the words of your prophets that prophesy unto you, saying, Behold, the vessels of the LORD’s house shall now shortly be brought again from Babylon: for they prophesy a lie unto you.

17 Hearken not unto them; serve the king of Babylon, and live: wherefore should this city be laid waste?

18 But if they be prophets, and if the word of the LORD be with them, let them now make intercession to the LORD of hosts, that the vessels which are left in the house of the LORD, and in the house of the king of Judah, and at Jerusalem, go not to Babylon.

19 For thus saith the LORD of hosts concerning the pillars, and concerning the sea, and concerning the bases, and concerning the residue of the vessels that remain in this city.

20 Which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon took not, when he carried away captive Jeconiah the son of Jehoiakim king of Judah from Jerusalem to Babylon, and all the nobles of Judah and Jerusalem;

21 Yea, thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, concerning the vessels that remain in the house of the LORD, and in the house of the king of Judah and of Jerusalem;

22 They shall be carried to Babylon, and there shall they be until the day that I visit them, saith the LORD; then will I bring them up, and restore them to this place.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jeremiah is told to make bonds and yokes and send them to the neighbouring kings with the message to submit to Nebuchadnezzar's yoke, for God has given the lands into his hand for a time. The vessels of the house, the chapter insists, will also be carried to Babylon and remain there until the day of visitation — against the false prophets who promise their swift return. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the beginning of the reign of Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah came this word unto Jeremiah from the Lord, saying. Henry notes Jeremiah is to prepare a sign that all the neighbouring countries would be made subject to the king of Babylon: the word in Jehoiakim's beginning prepares the yoke-sign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Thus says the Lord to me: Make thee bonds and yokes, and put them upon thy neck. Henry's sign is bonds and yokes put upon his own neck: the prophet wears the subjection he preaches, that all neighbouring countries may see it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. And send them to the king of Edom, and to the king of Moab, and to the king of the Ammonites, and to the king of Tyrus, and to the king of Zidon, by the hand of the messengers which come to Jerusalem unto Zedekiah king of Judah. Henry's yokes are sent to Edom, Moab, Ammon, Tyre, and Zidon by messengers come to Zedekiah: the sign goes to every neighbouring king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. And command them to say unto their masters: Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Thus shall ye say unto your masters. Henry's command to say unto masters is God's asserted right: the Lord of hosts, God of Israel, speaks to every master through the yoke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. I have made the earth, the man and the beast that are upon the ground, by my great power and by my outstretched arm, and have given it unto whom it seemed meet unto me. Henry notes God asserts his right to dispose of kingdoms as he pleases: whatever any have of the good things of this world, it is what God sees fit to give, and we should therefore be content, for the Maker of earth, man, and beast gives it to whom it seems meet to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. And now have I given all these lands into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, my servant; and the beasts of the field have I given him also to serve him. Henry notes the things of this world are not the best things, for the Lord often gives the largest share to bad men, and dominion is not founded in grace: all lands, and beasts of the field, are given to Nebuchadnezzar, God's servant for this work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. And all nations shall serve him, and his son, and his son's son, until the very time of his land come: and then many nations and great kings shall serve themselves of him. Henry's service lasts son and son's son, until his land's time comes, when many nations and great kings serve themselves of him: dominion given is dominion limited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. And it shall come to pass, that the nation and kingdom which will not serve the same Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, and that will not put their neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon, that nation will I punish, says the Lord, with the sword, and with the famine, and with the pestilence, until I have consumed them by his hand. Henry notes those who will not serve the God who made them shall justly be made to serve their enemies that seek to ruin them: the unyoked nation is punished with sword, famine, and pestilence until consumed by Babylon's hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Therefore hearken not ye to your prophets, nor to your diviners, nor to your dreamers, nor to your enchanters, nor to your sorcerers, which speak unto you, saying, Ye shall not serve the king of Babylon. Henry urges them to prevent their destruction by submission: hearken not to prophets, diviners, dreamers, enchanters, and sorcerers who promise no service to Babylon, for their promise is destruction's road. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. For they prophesy a lie unto you, to remove you far from your land; and that I should drive you out, and ye should perish. Henry's lying prophets prophesy removal far from land, driving out, and perishing: the lie that promises liberty delivers exile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. But the nations that bring their neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon, and serve him, those will I let remain still in their own land, says the Lord; and they shall till it, and dwell therein. Henry notes a meek spirit, by quiet submission to the hardest turns of providence, makes the best of what is bad: many persons may escape destroying providences by submitting to humbling providences, and the yoked nations remain, till, and dwell in their own land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. I spoke also to Zedekiah king of Judah according to all these words, saying: Bring your necks under the yoke of the king of Babylon, and serve him and his people, and live. Henry notes Jeremiah persuades the king of Judah to surrender to the king of Babylon: bring necks under the yoke, serve him and his people, and live, for it is better to take up a light cross in our way than to pull a heavier on our own heads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Why will ye die, thou and thy people, by the sword, by the famine, and by the pestilence, as the Lord has spoken against the nation that will not serve the king of Babylon? Henry asks: is it their wisdom to submit to the heavy iron yoke of a cruel tyrant, that they may secure their lives; and is it not much more our wisdom to submit to the pleasant and easy yoke of our Lord and Master, Jesus Christ, that we may secure our souls? Why die by sword, famine, and pestilence, when submission is life? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Therefore hearken not unto the words of the prophets that speak unto you, saying, Ye shall not serve the king of Babylon: for they prophesy a lie unto you. Henry's hearken-not is repeated for Zedekiah: the prophets promising no service prophesy a lie, and the lie is death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. For I have not sent them, says the Lord, yet they prophesy a lie in my name; that I might drive you out, and that ye might perish, ye, and the prophets that prophesy unto you. Henry warns those who encourage sinners to go on in sinful ways will perish with them: unsent prophets prophesying lies in God's name drive king and prophets alike to perishing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Also I spoke to the priests and to all this people, saying: Thus says the Lord; Hearken not to the words of your prophets that prophesy unto you, saying, Behold, the vessels of the Lord's house shall now shortly be brought again from Babylon: for they prophesy a lie unto you. Henry's priests and people hear the same hearken-not: the vessels shortly brought again from Babylon is a lie prophesied to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Hearken not unto them; serve the king of Babylon, and live: wherefore should this city be laid waste? Henry asks why should they die the second death, infinitely worse than that by sword and famine, when they may submit and live? Hearken not, serve Babylon, and live, for wherefore should this city be laid waste? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. But if they be prophets, and if the word of the Lord be with them, let them now make intercession to the Lord of hosts, that the vessels which are left in the house of the Lord, and in the house of the king of Judah, and at Jerusalem, go not to Babylon. Henry's test is intercession: if they be prophets with the Lord's word, let them intercede that the left vessels go not to Babylon, for true prophets pray, and liars only promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. For thus says the Lord of hosts concerning the pillars, and concerning the sea, and concerning the bases, and concerning the residue of the vessels that remain in this city. Henry notes Jeremiah assures them that the brazen vessels should go after the golden ones: pillars, sea, bases, and residue vessels remaining in the city are named for Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon took not, when he carried away captive Jeconiah the son of Jehoiakim king of Judah, from Jerusalem to Babylon, and all the nobles of Judah and Jerusalem. Henry's vessels not taken at Jeconiah's captivity, with all Judah and Jerusalem's nobles, are the residue now named: what Babylon left then, Babylon takes now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Yea, thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, concerning the vessels that remain in the house of the Lord, and in the house of the king of Judah and of Jerusalem. Henry's all shall be carried to Babylon: vessels remaining in the Lord's house, the king's house, and Jerusalem are all for Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. They shall be carried to Babylon, and there shall they be until the day that I visit them, says the Lord; then will I bring them up, and restore them to this place. Henry concludes with a gracious promise, that the time would come when they should be brought back: though the return of the prosperity of the church does not come in our time, we must not despair, for it will come in God's time, and the vessels carried to Babylon shall be visited, brought up, and restored to this place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

Glosses below rest on the chapter's own study notes, paraphrased above; they explain words, not Scripture itself.

  • Bonds and yokes (v. 2). The wooden harness of a draught animal, made and worn as a sign: submission to Nebuchadnezzar's yoke is the Lord's command for Judah and its neighbours for a time. Interpreted Study note for this chapter, as paraphrased in the verse-by-verse list above
