The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Esther 6 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–10; King James Version, Apocrypha (the Rest of Esther) for the Greek additions)

1 On that night could not the king sleep, and he commanded to bring the book of records of the chronicles; and they were read before the king.

2 And it was found written, that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king’s chamberlains, the keepers of the door, who sought to lay hand on the king Ahasuerus.

3 And the king said, What honour and dignity hath been done to Mordecai for this? Then said the king’s servants that ministered unto him, There is nothing done for him.

4 And the king said, Who is in the court? Now Haman was come into the outward court of the king’s house, to speak unto the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows that he had prepared for him.

5 And the king’s servants said unto him, Behold, Haman standeth in the court. And the king said, Let him come in.

6 So Haman came in. And the king said unto him, What shall be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour? Now Haman thought in his heart, To whom would the king delight to do honour more than to myself?

7 And Haman answered the king, For the man whom the king delighteth to honour,

8 Let the royal apparel be brought which the king useth to wear, and the horse that the king rideth upon, and the crown royal which is set upon his head:

9 And let this apparel and horse be delivered to the hand of one of the king’s most noble princes, that they may array the man withal whom the king delighteth to honour, and bring him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaim before him, Thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delighteth to honour.

10 Then the king said to Haman, Make haste, and take the apparel and the horse, as thou hast said, and do even so to Mordecai the Jew, that sitteth at the king’s gate: let nothing fail of all that thou hast spoken.

11 Then took Haman the apparel and the horse, and arrayed Mordecai, and brought him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaimed before him, Thus shall it be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour.

12 And Mordecai came again to the king’s gate. But Haman hasted to his house mourning, and having his head covered.

13 And Haman told Zeresh his wife and all his friends every thing that had befallen him. Then said his wise men and Zeresh his wife unto him, If Mordecai be of the seed of the Jews, before whom thou hast begun to fall, thou shalt not prevail against him, but shalt surely fall before him.

14 And while they were yet talking with him, came the king’s chamberlains, and hasted to bring Haman unto the banquet that Esther had prepared.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

On a sleepless night the king has the book of records of the chronicles read, and discovers that Mordecai's exposure of the plot against him was never rewarded. Haman, arriving to ask for Mordecai's hanging, is instead ordered to parade Mordecai through the city in royal apparel and crown, on the king's horse, proclaiming him the man the king delights to honour; Haman returns home mourning with his head covered, to his wife's warning that if Mordecai is of the seed of the Jews, Haman shall surely fall before him. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. On that night could not the king sleep, and he commanded to bring the book of records of the chronicles; and they were read before the king. Henry notes the sleepless night is providence, not accident: the king who cannot sleep reaches for the book that will undo Haman. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And it was found written, that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king's chamberlains, keepers of the door, who sought to lay hand on king Ahasuerus. JFB notes the record is read at exactly the passage Haman would have wished forgotten, on the very night before he asks for Mordecai's death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And the king said, What honour and dignity has been done to Mordecai for this? Then said the king's servants that ministered unto him, There is nothing done for him. Henry notes the forgotten service is discovered by the king himself, not by Mordecai's complaint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. And the king said, Who is in the court? Now Haman was come into the outward court, to speak unto the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows he had prepared. JFB notes the timing is exact: Haman arrives to ask a hanging at the moment the king asks about honouring. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. And the king's servants said unto him, Behold, Haman stands in the court. And the king said, Let him come in. Haman is admitted at once, as a favourite, to a question he will answer against himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Henry notes Haman's heart answers the king's question before his mouth does, assuming every honour must be meant for himself, which is why his prescription will be turned against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And Haman answered the king, For the man whom the king delights to honour. Haman begins his prescription without asking whose honour it is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Let the royal apparel be brought which the king uses to wear, and the horse that the king rides upon, and the crown royal which is set upon his head. JFB notes Haman asks for the king's own clothes, horse and crown, honours that border on royalty itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And let this apparel and horse be delivered to one of the king's most noble princes, that they may array the man, and bring him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaim before him, Thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delights to honour. Henry notes the procession is designed for envy: a public proclamation through the streets, where all can see. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Then the king said to Haman, Make haste, and take the apparel and the horse, as thou hast said, and do even so to Mordecai the Jew, that sits at the king's gate: let nothing fail of all that thou hast spoken. JFB notes the reversal is spoken in Haman's own words, and 'Mordecai the Jew' is named to make the irony complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Then took Haman the apparel and the horse, and arrayed Mordecai, and brought him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaimed before him, Thus shall it be done unto the man whom the king delights to honour. Henry notes Haman becomes the herald of his enemy's honour, proclaiming with his own mouth what he had plotted to prevent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And Mordecai came again to the king's gate. But Haman hasted to his house mourning, and having his head covered. JFB notes Mordecai returns quietly to his post, while Haman flees home in mourning, his head covered as a man shamed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. And Haman told Zeresh his wife and all his friends everything that had befallen him. Then said his wise men and Zeresh his wife unto him, If Mordecai be of the seed of the Jews, before whom thou hast begun to fall, thou shalt not prevail against him, but shalt surely fall before him. Henry notes even Haman's own household reads the omen: a fall before a Jew is the beginning of a total fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. And while they were yet talking with him, came the king's chamberlains, and hasted to bring Haman unto the banquet that Esther had prepared. JFB notes the summons cuts off the counsel: Haman is hurried from his own house of mourning to the queen's banquet, where his fall will be completed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Book of records of the chronicles. The court record read before the king on his sleepless night, in which Mordecai's exposure of the plot against the king is found written. Documented Chapter text, verses 1-2
  • Royal apparel, horse and crown. The honours Haman himself devises for the man the king delights to honour, which the king then orders him to bestow on Mordecai. Documented Chapter text, verses 8-10
