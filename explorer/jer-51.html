The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 51 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Thus saith the LORD; Behold, I will raise up against Babylon, and against them that dwell in the midst of them that rise up against me, a destroying wind;

2 And will send unto Babylon fanners, that shall fan her, and shall empty her land: for in the day of trouble they shall be against her round about.

3 Against him that bendeth let the archer bend his bow, and against him that lifteth himself up in his brigandine: and spare ye not her young men; destroy ye utterly all her host.

4 Thus the slain shall fall in the land of the Chaldeans, and they that are thrust through in her streets.

5 For Israel hath not been forsaken, nor Judah of his God, of the LORD of hosts; though their land was filled with sin against the Holy One of Israel.

6 Flee out of the midst of Babylon, and deliver every man his soul: be not cut off in her iniquity; for this is the time of the LORD’s vengeance; he will render unto her a recompence.

7 Babylon hath been a golden cup in the LORD’s hand, that made all the earth drunken: the nations have drunken of her wine; therefore the nations are mad.

8 Babylon is suddenly fallen and destroyed: howl for her; take balm for her pain, if so be she may be healed.

9 We would have healed Babylon, but she is not healed: forsake her, and let us go every one into his own country: for her judgment reacheth unto heaven, and is lifted up even to the skies.

10 The LORD hath brought forth our righteousness: come, and let us declare in Zion the work of the LORD our God.

11 Make bright the arrows; gather the shields: the LORD hath raised up the spirit of the kings of the Medes: for his device is against Babylon, to destroy it; because it is the vengeance of the LORD, the vengeance of his temple.

12 Set up the standard upon the walls of Babylon, make the watch strong, set up the watchmen, prepare the ambushes: for the LORD hath both devised and done that which he spake against the inhabitants of Babylon.

13 O thou that dwellest upon many waters, abundant in treasures, thine end is come, and the measure of thy covetousness.

14 The LORD of hosts hath sworn by himself, saying, Surely I will fill thee with men, as with caterpillers; and they shall lift up a shout against thee.

15 He hath made the earth by his power, he hath established the world by his wisdom, and hath stretched out the heaven by his understanding.

16 When he uttereth his voice, there is a multitude of waters in the heavens; and he causeth the vapours to ascend from the ends of the earth: he maketh lightnings with rain, and bringeth forth the wind out of his treasures.

17 Every man is brutish by his knowledge; every founder is confounded by the graven image: for his molten image is falsehood, and there is no breath in them.

18 They are vanity, the work of errors: in the time of their visitation they shall perish.

19 The portion of Jacob is not like them; for he is the former of all things: and Israel is the rod of his inheritance: the LORD of hosts is his name.

20 Thou art my battle axe and weapons of war: for with thee will I break in pieces the nations, and with thee will I destroy kingdoms;

21 And with thee will I break in pieces the horse and his rider; and with thee will I break in pieces the chariot and his rider;

22 With thee also will I break in pieces man and woman; and with thee will I break in pieces old and young; and with thee will I break in pieces the young man and the maid;

23 I will also break in pieces with thee the shepherd and his flock; and with thee will I break in pieces the husbandman and his yoke of oxen; and with thee will I break in pieces captains and rulers.

24 And I will render unto Babylon and to all the inhabitants of Chaldea all their evil that they have done in Zion in your sight, saith the LORD.

25 Behold, I am against thee, O destroying mountain, saith the LORD, which destroyest all the earth: and I will stretch out mine hand upon thee, and roll thee down from the rocks, and will make thee a burnt mountain.

26 And they shall not take of thee a stone for a corner, nor a stone for foundations; but thou shalt be desolate for ever, saith the LORD.

27 Set ye up a standard in the land, blow the trumpet among the nations, prepare the nations against her, call together against her the kingdoms of Ararat, Minni, and Ashchenaz; appoint a captain against her; cause the horses to come up as the rough caterpillers.

28 Prepare against her the nations with the kings of the Medes, the captains thereof, and all the rulers thereof, and all the land of his dominion.

29 And the land shall tremble and sorrow: for every purpose of the LORD shall be performed against Babylon, to make the land of Babylon a desolation without an inhabitant.

30 The mighty men of Babylon have forborn to fight, they have remained in their holds: their might hath failed; they became as women: they have burned her dwellingplaces; her bars are broken.

31 One post shall run to meet another, and one messenger to meet another, to shew the king of Babylon that his city is taken at one end,

32 And that the passages are stopped, and the reeds they have burned with fire, and the men of war are affrighted.

33 For thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; The daughter of Babylon is like a threshingfloor, it is time to thresh her: yet a little while, and the time of her harvest shall come.

34 Nebuchadrezzar the king of Babylon hath devoured me, he hath crushed me, he hath made me an empty vessel, he hath swallowed me up like a dragon, he hath filled his belly with my delicates, he hath cast me out.

35 The violence done to me and to my flesh be upon Babylon, shall the inhabitant of Zion say; and my blood upon the inhabitants of Chaldea, shall Jerusalem say.

36 Therefore thus saith the LORD; Behold, I will plead thy cause, and take vengeance for thee; and I will dry up her sea, and make her springs dry.

37 And Babylon shall become heaps, a dwellingplace for dragons, an astonishment, and an hissing, without an inhabitant.

38 They shall roar together like lions: they shall yell as lions’ whelps.

39 In their heat I will make their feasts, and I will make them drunken, that they may rejoice, and sleep a perpetual sleep, and not wake, saith the LORD.

40 I will bring them down like lambs to the slaughter, like rams with he goats.

41 How is Sheshach taken! and how is the praise of the whole earth surprised! how is Babylon become an astonishment among the nations!

42 The sea is come up upon Babylon: she is covered with the multitude of the waves thereof.

43 Her cities are a desolation, a dry land, and a wilderness, a land wherein no man dwelleth, neither doth any son of man pass thereby.

44 And I will punish Bel in Babylon, and I will bring forth out of his mouth that which he hath swallowed up: and the nations shall not flow together any more unto him: yea, the wall of Babylon shall fall.

45 My people, go ye out of the midst of her, and deliver ye every man his soul from the fierce anger of the LORD.

46 And lest your heart faint, and ye fear for the rumour that shall be heard in the land; a rumour shall both come one year, and after that in another year shall come a rumour, and violence in the land, ruler against ruler.

47 Therefore, behold, the days come, that I will do judgment upon the graven images of Babylon: and her whole land shall be confounded, and all her slain shall fall in the midst of her.

48 Then the heaven and the earth, and all that is therein, shall sing for Babylon: for the spoilers shall come unto her from the north, saith the LORD.

49 As Babylon hath caused the slain of Israel to fall, so at Babylon shall fall the slain of all the earth.

50 Ye that have escaped the sword, go away, stand not still: remember the LORD afar off, and let Jerusalem come into your mind.

51 We are confounded, because we have heard reproach: shame hath covered our faces: for strangers are come into the sanctuaries of the LORD’s house.

52 Wherefore, behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will do judgment upon her graven images: and through all her land the wounded shall groan.

53 Though Babylon should mount up to heaven, and though she should fortify the height of her strength, yet from me shall spoilers come unto her, saith the LORD.

54 A sound of a cry cometh from Babylon, and great destruction from the land of the Chaldeans:

55 Because the LORD hath spoiled Babylon, and destroyed out of her the great voice; when her waves do roar like great waters, a noise of their voice is uttered:

56 Because the spoiler is come upon her, even upon Babylon, and her mighty men are taken, every one of their bows is broken: for the LORD God of recompences shall surely requite.

57 And I will make drunk her princes, and her wise men, her captains, and her rulers, and her mighty men: and they shall sleep a perpetual sleep, and not wake, saith the King, whose name is the LORD of hosts.

58 Thus saith the LORD of hosts; The broad walls of Babylon shall be utterly broken, and her high gates shall be burned with fire; and the people shall labour in vain, and the folk in the fire, and they shall be weary.

59 The word which Jeremiah the prophet commanded Seraiah the son of Neriah, the son of Maaseiah, when he went with Zedekiah the king of Judah into Babylon in the fourth year of his reign. And this Seraiah was a quiet prince.

60 So Jeremiah wrote in a book all the evil that should come upon Babylon, even all these words that are written against Babylon.

61 And Jeremiah said to Seraiah, When thou comest to Babylon, and shalt see, and shalt read all these words;

62 Then shalt thou say, O LORD, thou hast spoken against this place, to cut it off, that none shall remain in it, neither man nor beast, but that it shall be desolate for ever.

63 And it shall be, when thou hast made an end of reading this book, that thou shalt bind a stone to it, and cast it into the midst of Euphrates:

64 And thou shalt say, Thus shall Babylon sink, and shall not rise from the evil that I will bring upon her: and they shall be weary. Thus far are the words of Jeremiah.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Against Babylon a destroying wind is raised, and the chapter piles up the instruments of her fall — fanners, archers, kings of the Medes — while commanding God's people to flee out of her midst and not be cut off in her iniquity. It ends with Seraiah carrying the written words to Babylon, reading them, binding the book to a stone, and casting it into the Euphrates: thus shall Babylon sink and not rise. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry compares Babylon's enemies to a destroying wind, brought out of God's treasuries, raised against those dwelling in the midst of the Chaldeans and incorporated with them. The Chaldeans rise up against God by falling down before idols, he notes, and God will raise up destroyers who will be too hard for those who contend with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry compares the invaders to fanners who shall drive Babylon away as chaff is driven by the fan and empty her land. The Chaldeans had themselves been fanners to winnow God's people and empty them, he observes, and now they shall in like manner be despoiled and dispersed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry reads this as the ample commission to destroy: let the archers bend their bows against Babylon's archers, spare not her young men, and utterly destroy all her host. This may animate God's instruments, he says, because what he has devised shall surely prosper, for he himself will do it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry counts this among the marks of a universal destruction: the slain shall fall in great abundance throughout the land of the Chaldeans, with multitudes thrust through in her streets. God will make thorough work of it, performing what he has purposed and perfecting what he has begun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry stresses that though Israel's land was filled with sin against the Holy One of Israel, and God justly delivered them to their enemies, Israel is not forsaken and not cast off, though cast out. It does not follow, he notes, that a people who have broken God's laws are thrown out of covenant, for God's care and love will flourish again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry calls flight out of Babylon wisdom when ruin approaches: quit the city and retire into a remote corner, that you may save your lives and not be cut off in her iniquity. There is a fixed time for vengeance, he adds, and though it cannot be expected sooner than that time, then it will come, for the avenging of Israel is the vengeance of the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry acknowledges Babylon's great pomp: a golden cup in the LORD's hand, a rich and glorious empire, a golden city and a head of gold, filled with all good things as a cup with wine. He had made the earth drunk with this cup, he explains, some intoxicated with her pleasures and debauched, others intoxicated with her terrors and destroyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry calls Babylon's fall a certain and surprising destruction: she is as sure to fall as if fallen already, though when Jeremiah prophesied she was in the height of her power. It came suddenly, when they did not think of it, and was perfected in a little time, yet taking balm for her pain is vain, for she who would not be healed by God's word shall not be healed by his providence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry records two readings of the complaint: God's captive people endeavoured, as instructed, to convince the Babylonians of the folly of idolatry but could not, since they still doted on graven images, and so resolved to quit them; or hired auxiliaries declared they had done their best to save her to no purpose. Either way, her judgment reaches unto heaven, and it is vain to withstand or avert it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry says Zion's children shall triumph in particular: the LORD has brought forth their righteousness, appearing on their behalf against unjust dealers and giving redress, and showing himself reconciled to them as a righteous nation. Let it therefore be declared in Zion to his praise, he says, that others may join in praising him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry says God has raised up the spirit of the kings of the Medes, Darius and Cyrus, who come against Babylon by a divine instinct, for God's device is against Babylon to destroy it, and this is the vengeance of his temple. The call to make bright the arrows and gather the shields is ironic, he notes: in a long time of peace and security the arrows have grown rusty and the shields scattered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry reads the call to set up the standard, strengthen the watch and prepare ambushes as upbraiding irony: Babylon would be found very secure and remiss, needing to be thus quickened, and was in the midst of her revels when taken. Yet the methods God has devised shall prosper, for the LORD has both devised and done what he spoke against Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry says Babylon seems well fenced, dwelling upon many waters that embarrass an enemy's march, and abundant in treasures, yet neither waters nor wealth shall secure her. Her end is come as the measure of her covetousness, he explains: it sets bounds to ambition and avarice that otherwise would be boundless, as God says to proud waves, Hitherto shall you come, and no further. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry says it is the LORD of hosts who has sworn it, swearing by himself since he could swear by no greater, that he will fill Babylon with vast and incredible numbers of the enemy's forces, as with caterpillars. They need only lift up a shout against it, he adds, for that shout shall dispirit all the inhabitants and make them an easy prey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry says no one need doubt that he who determined to destroy Babylon can make his words good, for he is the God who made the world by his power. Nothing is too hard for him, he notes, and it is in his name that our help stands and on him our hope is built. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry says God's providence is a continued creation, with wind and rain at his disposal: at his voice there is a multitude of waters in the heavens, fed by vapours ascending from the earth, and lightnings and rain, seeming contraries like fire and water, are produced together, while the wind is brought out of his treasuries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry says the idols opposing God's word are a mere sham and their worshippers brutish people: the molten image is falsehood, with no breath in it, and those who make such images are like unto them. When idols come to be visited, examined and enquired into, their reputation sinks and they appear to be nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry calls the idols vanity, the work of errors, which in the time of their visitation shall perish. Their reputation sinks on examination, he says, and they are shown to be nothing at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry insists there is no comparison between the God of Israel and the gods of the heathen: the portion of Jacob is the former of all things and the LORD of hosts. There is a near relation between him and his people, he notes, for he is their portion and they are his, the lot of his inheritance, so he will do what is best for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry says Babylon had been God's battle-axe and his weapons of war, tools in his hand with which he broke in pieces nations and kingdoms. Such havoc the Chaldeans made as instruments of his wrath, he observes, yet now Babylon itself must fall, for the rod will itself be thrown into the fire at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry lists the instruments of kingdoms that Babylon broke: horses and chariots, which are so much the strength of kingdoms. With Babylon as his battle-axe, God broke these in pieces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry continues the catalogue: with Babylon God broke in pieces man and woman, old and young, the young man and the maid, with which kingdoms are replenished. None of these orders of society escaped the havoc of God's instrument. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry completes the catalogue: the shepherd and his flock, the husbandman and his yoke of oxen, and captains and rulers, with which kingdoms are maintained and supplied. Yet, he stresses, being instrumental in executing God's judgments on others will not exempt Babylon from God's judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry says the LORD God of recompences, to whom vengeance belongs, will surely requite Babylon, rendering unto her all the evil she has done in Zion, and returning it in the sight of his people. They shall have the satisfaction, he notes, of seeing their cause pleaded with jealousy and plainly seeing the punishment as the recompense for wrong done to Zion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry calls Babylon a destroying mountain, very lofty and bulky, destroying all the earth as stones tumbled from high mountains spoil the grounds about them. God declares he is against her, will stretch out his hand, roll her down from her rocks, and leave her a burnt mountain, a heap of ashes, for those cannot stand long whom God is against. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry says the finality of Babylon's ruin appears in this: no use shall be made even of her ruins, so execrable shall they be, for none shall take from her a stone for a corner or for foundations. This denotes, he adds, that nothing shall be left on which to ground any hopes of raising her into a kingdom again, for she shall be desolate forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry reads the call to set up a standard and blow the trumpet as enlisting soldiers and animating them for Cyrus's expedition, with the nations preparing recruits and the kingdoms of Ararat, Minni and Ashkenaz sending in their quota of men. General officers are to be appointed and the cavalry to advance in great numbers, like caterpillars laying the country waste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry says the kings and captains must prepare nations against Babylon, for the service is great and there is occasion for many hands. The kings of the Medes, their captains, all their rulers and all the land of his dominion are called together for this work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry says the whole land shall tremble and sorrow in universal consternation, seeing both the irresistible arm and the irreversible counsel and decree of God against Babylon. Every purpose of the LORD shall be performed, making the land a desolation without an inhabitant, for it is his purpose, which shall be performed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 30. Henry says God has taken away the Babylonians' strength and spirit, so that their mighty men forbear to fight and remain in their holds, not daring so much as to peep forth. Their might fails, they become as timorous as women, and the enemy without resistance burns their dwelling-places and breaks their bars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 31. Henry explains the running posts: the king of Babylon, at a distance from where the attack was made, had notice only late, so couriers from several quarters met at court, like Job's messengers one upon the heels of another, to tell him his city was taken at one end, with nothing to obstruct the conquerors from quickly reaching the other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 32. Henry says the messengers must tell the king that the enemy has seized the passes, the forts upon the river, and, having got over the river, has burned the reeds on the river side to alarm and terrify the city. The men of war are affrighted and throw down their arms, surrendering at discretion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 33. Henry compares Babylon to a threshing-floor: God's people were long threshed there as sheaves in the floor, but now the time has come for her herself to be threshed, with her princes, great men and all her inhabitants beaten in their own land. By sin she is made meet to be a seat of war, and her people, like corn in harvest, are ripe for destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 34. Henry records Zion's complaint against Nebuchadnezzar: he devoured her, crushed her, emptied her of all that was valuable, swallowed her up as a dragon or whale swallows little fish by shoals, filled his belly and his treasures with her delicates, and cast her out as a vessel in which there is no pleasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 35. Henry says Zion and Jerusalem shall appeal to God that the violence done to them and their flesh, and the blood of their people shed like water, be upon Babylon and the inhabitants of Chaldea, with the guilt required at their hands. Ruin, he notes, is not far off from those who lie under the guilt of wrong done to God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 36. Henry says the righteous Judge answers the appeal: I will plead thy cause and take vengeance for thee, and every drop of Jerusalem's blood shall be accounted for with interest. Drying up her sea and springs points to the waters that secured and enriched Babylon being taken away, as Cyrus turned the Euphrates aside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 37. Henry says Babylon shall become heaps, a dwelling-place for dragons, an astonishment and a hissing, without an inhabitant. Jerome, he notes, said that in his time, though the ruins of Babylon's walls were to be seen, the ground enclosed by them was a forest of wild beasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 38. Henry says the Babylonians in their revels shall roar together like lions and yell as lions' whelps, drinking confusion to Cyrus and his army with loud huzzas. They call it singing, he remarks, but in scripture language and in the language of sober men it is called yelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 39. Henry says that in their heat, when their heads are hot with hard drinking, God will make their feasts and give them the cup of the LORD's right hand, a cup of fury that makes them drunk to revel and sleep a perpetual sleep, never to wake. On that very night, in the midst of the jollity, Belshazzar was slain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 40. Henry says the Babylonians shall be brought down like lambs to the slaughter, like rams with he-goats, in such great numbers and so easily that the enemies make no more of killing them than the butcher does of killing lambs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 41. Henry records the lament over Sheshach: how is the praise of the whole earth surprised, and how is Babylon become an astonishment among the nations! That which was universally cried up, he observes, may fall into general contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 42. Henry compares the invading strength to an irruption and inundation of waters: the sea has come up upon Babylon, and once it has broken through its bounds there is no fence against it, so she is covered with the multitude of its waves and overpowered by a numerous army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 43. Henry says Babylon's cities shall become a desolation, a dry land and a wilderness, an uninhabited and uncultivated desert, wherein no man dwells and no son of man passes by. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 44. Henry says Bel, the principal idol the Babylonians worshipped, is by name marked for punishment: that great devourer, to whom abundance of sacrifices and rich spoils were dedicated, shall disgorge what he greedily regaled himself with, and God will bring forth out of his temple all the wealth laid up there. His altars shall be forsaken and none shall regard him any more, so the idol thought to be a wall to Babylon shall fall and fail them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 45. Henry repeats the call to God's people to go out of the midst of Babylon and deliver every man his soul from the fierce anger of the LORD. It is their wisdom, he says, to get out of the midst of Babylon lest they be involved, if not in her ruins, yet in her fears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 46. Henry explains the rumours as a gradual destruction foreseen in stages: a rumour shall come one year that Cyrus is making vast preparations for war, and after that, in another year, a rumour that his design is upon Babylon. The people are warned lest their hearts faint at these alarms, for they might have sought conditions of peace but were too proud and secure, and their hearts were hardened to their destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 47. Henry says God will do judgment upon Babylon's graven images, in token that the whole land shall be confounded and all her slain shall fall, with the wounded groaning throughout the country. Though the invaders are themselves idolaters, he notes, they shall destroy the images and temples of Babylon's gods, as an earnest of the abolishing of all counterfeit deities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 48. Henry says some shall rejoice in Babylon's fall, not as it is the misery of fellow-creatures, but as the manifestation of God's righteous judgment and as opening the way for the release of his captives. On these accounts, he says, heaven and earth and all in both shall sing for Babylon, for Babylon's ruin is Zion's praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 49. Henry says Cyrus shall measure to the Chaldeans the same that they measured to the Jews, so that every observer may discern God recompensing them for what they did against his people. Just as Babylon caused the slain of Israel to fall, slaying all without distinction, so at Babylon shall fall the slain of all the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 50. Henry says it is not only wisdom to quit the city when ruin approaches, but duty to quit the country too when ruin is accomplished and the captives are set at liberty by the pulling down of the prison over their heads. Those who escaped the sword must go away and stand not still, hastening to their own country, however comfortably seated in Babylon, for this is not their rest, but Canaan is, and wherever they are they must remember the LORD and let Jerusalem come into their minds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 51. Henry takes notice of the discouragement of the returning captives: reminded of Jerusalem, they cry that they are confounded, shame covers their faces, for strangers have come into the sanctuaries of the LORD's house. They cannot bear the thought of the sanctuary profaned and ruined by strangers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 52. Henry answers that discouragement with God's triumph over Babylon's gods: God will do judgment upon her graven images, and through all her land the wounded shall groan, so that the reproach of Jerusalem will be forever rolled away. The believing prospect of Jerusalem's recovery, he notes, will keep us from being ashamed of Jerusalem's ruins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 53. Henry says Babylon, thinking herself very safe and very great, will be deceived: though she should mount up to heaven and fortify the height of her strength, yet God will send spoilers against her who shall break through her strength and bring down her height. All her height and fortification will not do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 54. Henry says there is a sound of a cry coming from Babylon, a great outcry lamenting the great destruction from the land of the Chaldeans. This is the voice of mourning because the LORD has destroyed the great voice of mirth that used to be heard in Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 55. Henry says the LORD has spoiled Babylon and destroyed out of her the great voice, the voice of the multitude and of mirth, so that when her waves roar like great waters, the noise of their voice is uttered only in lamentation. The cry from Babylon replaces the city's former great voice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 56. Henry says when the spoiler comes upon Babylon her mighty men are immediately taken, their weapons fail them, and every one of their bows is broken and stands them in no stead. Their politics fail them too, he adds, for their princes and captains in council are made drunk, but the ground of it all is that the LORD God of recompences will surely requite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 57. Henry says Babylon's princes, wise men, captains, rulers and mighty men shall be made drunk and sleep a perpetual sleep, never waking. Their counsellors, he explains, are as men intoxicated through stupidity or despair, forming no right notions, staggering in their counsels and falling out among themselves, until the wine of God's wrath lays them into a fatal lethargy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 58. Henry says Babylon's broad walls shall be utterly broken and her high gates burned with fire, and the people labouring in the defence shall labour in vain in the fire. The walls, he notes, were some 200 cubits high and 50 broad, with stones laid in pitch scarcely separable, yet they shall be utterly broken and the defenders shall tire themselves to no good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 59. Henry says this prophecy was sent to Babylon by the hand of Seraiah, who went with or on behalf of Zedekiah into Babylon in the fourth year of his reign. Seraiah was a quiet prince, a prince of rest, Henry observes: in honour and power, yet calm of temper, studying peace, keeping his master from rebelling, and no persecutor of God's prophets, so Jeremiah might safely entrust him with the errand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 60. Henry says a copy of this prophecy was taken, it seems by Jeremiah himself, since Baruch his scribe is not mentioned here, and Jeremiah wrote in a book all the evil that should come upon Babylon. It is of great advantage, he notes, both to propagating and to perpetuating the word of God to have it written and copies taken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 61. Henry says Seraiah is desired to read the words when he comes to Babylon and sees what a magnificent, large, strong, rich and well-fortified place it is, and is tempted to think it will stand forever. Then he shall read all these words to himself and his particular friends, that with an eye of faith they may see to the end of these threatening powers and comfort themselves in captivity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 62. Henry says Seraiah must make a solemn protestation of the divine authority and unquestionable certainty of what he has read: O LORD, thou hast spoken against this place, to cut it off, so that none shall remain in it, neither man nor beast, but it shall be desolate forever. Though he sees Babylon flourishing, he must foresee it falling, believing that God who knows everything can do everything, and therefore neither envy its pomp nor fear its power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 63. Henry says Seraiah must then tie a stone to the book and cast it into the midst of the Euphrates, as a confirming sign of the things contained in it. In the sign it was the stone that sunk the book, which otherwise would have swum, he notes, but in the thing signified it was rather the book that sunk the stone: the divine sentence passed upon Babylon sunk that city, which seemed as firm as a stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 64. Henry says Seraiah must declare, Thus shall Babylon sink and shall not rise from the evil God will bring upon her, and they shall be weary, succumbing under a load they shall never shake off. Those who sink under the weight of God's wrath and curse, he notes, sink irrecoverably, and the chapter's last words seal the vision: thus far are the words of Jeremiah, recorded last because this prophecy was to be last accomplished among his prophecies against the Gentiles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

Perspectives (editorial — views side by side, not adjudicated)

Readings below are grouped by the commentator voice each study note summarizes. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations.

Matthew Henry (Complete)

  • Verse 1. Henry compares Babylon's enemies to a destroying wind, brought out of God's treasuries, raised against those dwelling in the midst of the Chaldeans and incorporated with them. The Chaldeans rise up against God by falling down before idols, he notes, and God will raise up destroyers who will be too hard for those who contend with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry compares the invaders to fanners who shall drive Babylon away as chaff is driven by the fan and empty her land. The Chaldeans had themselves been fanners to winnow God's people and empty them, he observes, and now they shall in like manner be despoiled and dispersed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry reads this as the ample commission to destroy: let the archers bend their bows against Babylon's archers, spare not her young men, and utterly destroy all her host. This may animate God's instruments, he says, because what he has devised shall surely prosper, for he himself will do it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry counts this among the marks of a universal destruction: the slain shall fall in great abundance throughout the land of the Chaldeans, with multitudes thrust through in her streets. God will make thorough work of it, performing what he has purposed and perfecting what he has begun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry stresses that though Israel's land was filled with sin against the Holy One of Israel, and God justly delivered them to their enemies, Israel is not forsaken and not cast off, though cast out. It does not follow, he notes, that a people who have broken God's laws are thrown out of covenant, for God's care and love will flourish again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry calls flight out of Babylon wisdom when ruin approaches: quit the city and retire into a remote corner, that you may save your lives and not be cut off in her iniquity. There is a fixed time for vengeance, he adds, and though it cannot be expected sooner than that time, then it will come, for the avenging of Israel is the vengeance of the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry acknowledges Babylon's great pomp: a golden cup in the LORD's hand, a rich and glorious empire, a golden city and a head of gold, filled with all good things as a cup with wine. He had made the earth drunk with this cup, he explains, some intoxicated with her pleasures and debauched, others intoxicated with her terrors and destroyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry calls Babylon's fall a certain and surprising destruction: she is as sure to fall as if fallen already, though when Jeremiah prophesied she was in the height of her power. It came suddenly, when they did not think of it, and was perfected in a little time, yet taking balm for her pain is vain, for she who would not be healed by God's word shall not be healed by his providence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry records two readings of the complaint: God's captive people endeavoured, as instructed, to convince the Babylonians of the folly of idolatry but could not, since they still doted on graven images, and so resolved to quit them; or hired auxiliaries declared they had done their best to save her to no purpose. Either way, her judgment reaches unto heaven, and it is vain to withstand or avert it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry says Zion's children shall triumph in particular: the LORD has brought forth their righteousness, appearing on their behalf against unjust dealers and giving redress, and showing himself reconciled to them as a righteous nation. Let it therefore be declared in Zion to his praise, he says, that others may join in praising him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry says God has raised up the spirit of the kings of the Medes, Darius and Cyrus, who come against Babylon by a divine instinct, for God's device is against Babylon to destroy it, and this is the vengeance of his temple. The call to make bright the arrows and gather the shields is ironic, he notes: in a long time of peace and security the arrows have grown rusty and the shields scattered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry reads the call to set up the standard, strengthen the watch and prepare ambushes as upbraiding irony: Babylon would be found very secure and remiss, needing to be thus quickened, and was in the midst of her revels when taken. Yet the methods God has devised shall prosper, for the LORD has both devised and done what he spoke against Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry says Babylon seems well fenced, dwelling upon many waters that embarrass an enemy's march, and abundant in treasures, yet neither waters nor wealth shall secure her. Her end is come as the measure of her covetousness, he explains: it sets bounds to ambition and avarice that otherwise would be boundless, as God says to proud waves, Hitherto shall you come, and no further. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry says it is the LORD of hosts who has sworn it, swearing by himself since he could swear by no greater, that he will fill Babylon with vast and incredible numbers of the enemy's forces, as with caterpillars. They need only lift up a shout against it, he adds, for that shout shall dispirit all the inhabitants and make them an easy prey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry says no one need doubt that he who determined to destroy Babylon can make his words good, for he is the God who made the world by his power. Nothing is too hard for him, he notes, and it is in his name that our help stands and on him our hope is built. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry says God's providence is a continued creation, with wind and rain at his disposal: at his voice there is a multitude of waters in the heavens, fed by vapours ascending from the earth, and lightnings and rain, seeming contraries like fire and water, are produced together, while the wind is brought out of his treasuries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry says the idols opposing God's word are a mere sham and their worshippers brutish people: the molten image is falsehood, with no breath in it, and those who make such images are like unto them. When idols come to be visited, examined and enquired into, their reputation sinks and they appear to be nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry calls the idols vanity, the work of errors, which in the time of their visitation shall perish. Their reputation sinks on examination, he says, and they are shown to be nothing at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry insists there is no comparison between the God of Israel and the gods of the heathen: the portion of Jacob is the former of all things and the LORD of hosts. There is a near relation between him and his people, he notes, for he is their portion and they are his, the lot of his inheritance, so he will do what is best for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry says Babylon had been God's battle-axe and his weapons of war, tools in his hand with which he broke in pieces nations and kingdoms. Such havoc the Chaldeans made as instruments of his wrath, he observes, yet now Babylon itself must fall, for the rod will itself be thrown into the fire at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry lists the instruments of kingdoms that Babylon broke: horses and chariots, which are so much the strength of kingdoms. With Babylon as his battle-axe, God broke these in pieces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry continues the catalogue: with Babylon God broke in pieces man and woman, old and young, the young man and the maid, with which kingdoms are replenished. None of these orders of society escaped the havoc of God's instrument. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry completes the catalogue: the shepherd and his flock, the husbandman and his yoke of oxen, and captains and rulers, with which kingdoms are maintained and supplied. Yet, he stresses, being instrumental in executing God's judgments on others will not exempt Babylon from God's judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry says the LORD God of recompences, to whom vengeance belongs, will surely requite Babylon, rendering unto her all the evil she has done in Zion, and returning it in the sight of his people. They shall have the satisfaction, he notes, of seeing their cause pleaded with jealousy and plainly seeing the punishment as the recompense for wrong done to Zion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry calls Babylon a destroying mountain, very lofty and bulky, destroying all the earth as stones tumbled from high mountains spoil the grounds about them. God declares he is against her, will stretch out his hand, roll her down from her rocks, and leave her a burnt mountain, a heap of ashes, for those cannot stand long whom God is against. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry says the finality of Babylon's ruin appears in this: no use shall be made even of her ruins, so execrable shall they be, for none shall take from her a stone for a corner or for foundations. This denotes, he adds, that nothing shall be left on which to ground any hopes of raising her into a kingdom again, for she shall be desolate forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry reads the call to set up a standard and blow the trumpet as enlisting soldiers and animating them for Cyrus's expedition, with the nations preparing recruits and the kingdoms of Ararat, Minni and Ashkenaz sending in their quota of men. General officers are to be appointed and the cavalry to advance in great numbers, like caterpillars laying the country waste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry says the kings and captains must prepare nations against Babylon, for the service is great and there is occasion for many hands. The kings of the Medes, their captains, all their rulers and all the land of his dominion are called together for this work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry says the whole land shall tremble and sorrow in universal consternation, seeing both the irresistible arm and the irreversible counsel and decree of God against Babylon. Every purpose of the LORD shall be performed, making the land a desolation without an inhabitant, for it is his purpose, which shall be performed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 30. Henry says God has taken away the Babylonians' strength and spirit, so that their mighty men forbear to fight and remain in their holds, not daring so much as to peep forth. Their might fails, they become as timorous as women, and the enemy without resistance burns their dwelling-places and breaks their bars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 31. Henry explains the running posts: the king of Babylon, at a distance from where the attack was made, had notice only late, so couriers from several quarters met at court, like Job's messengers one upon the heels of another, to tell him his city was taken at one end, with nothing to obstruct the conquerors from quickly reaching the other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 32. Henry says the messengers must tell the king that the enemy has seized the passes, the forts upon the river, and, having got over the river, has burned the reeds on the river side to alarm and terrify the city. The men of war are affrighted and throw down their arms, surrendering at discretion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 33. Henry compares Babylon to a threshing-floor: God's people were long threshed there as sheaves in the floor, but now the time has come for her herself to be threshed, with her princes, great men and all her inhabitants beaten in their own land. By sin she is made meet to be a seat of war, and her people, like corn in harvest, are ripe for destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 34. Henry records Zion's complaint against Nebuchadnezzar: he devoured her, crushed her, emptied her of all that was valuable, swallowed her up as a dragon or whale swallows little fish by shoals, filled his belly and his treasures with her delicates, and cast her out as a vessel in which there is no pleasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 35. Henry says Zion and Jerusalem shall appeal to God that the violence done to them and their flesh, and the blood of their people shed like water, be upon Babylon and the inhabitants of Chaldea, with the guilt required at their hands. Ruin, he notes, is not far off from those who lie under the guilt of wrong done to God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 36. Henry says the righteous Judge answers the appeal: I will plead thy cause and take vengeance for thee, and every drop of Jerusalem's blood shall be accounted for with interest. Drying up her sea and springs points to the waters that secured and enriched Babylon being taken away, as Cyrus turned the Euphrates aside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 37. Henry says Babylon shall become heaps, a dwelling-place for dragons, an astonishment and a hissing, without an inhabitant. Jerome, he notes, said that in his time, though the ruins of Babylon's walls were to be seen, the ground enclosed by them was a forest of wild beasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 38. Henry says the Babylonians in their revels shall roar together like lions and yell as lions' whelps, drinking confusion to Cyrus and his army with loud huzzas. They call it singing, he remarks, but in scripture language and in the language of sober men it is called yelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 39. Henry says that in their heat, when their heads are hot with hard drinking, God will make their feasts and give them the cup of the LORD's right hand, a cup of fury that makes them drunk to revel and sleep a perpetual sleep, never to wake. On that very night, in the midst of the jollity, Belshazzar was slain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 40. Henry says the Babylonians shall be brought down like lambs to the slaughter, like rams with he-goats, in such great numbers and so easily that the enemies make no more of killing them than the butcher does of killing lambs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 41. Henry records the lament over Sheshach: how is the praise of the whole earth surprised, and how is Babylon become an astonishment among the nations! That which was universally cried up, he observes, may fall into general contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 42. Henry compares the invading strength to an irruption and inundation of waters: the sea has come up upon Babylon, and once it has broken through its bounds there is no fence against it, so she is covered with the multitude of its waves and overpowered by a numerous army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 43. Henry says Babylon's cities shall become a desolation, a dry land and a wilderness, an uninhabited and uncultivated desert, wherein no man dwells and no son of man passes by. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 44. Henry says Bel, the principal idol the Babylonians worshipped, is by name marked for punishment: that great devourer, to whom abundance of sacrifices and rich spoils were dedicated, shall disgorge what he greedily regaled himself with, and God will bring forth out of his temple all the wealth laid up there. His altars shall be forsaken and none shall regard him any more, so the idol thought to be a wall to Babylon shall fall and fail them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 45. Henry repeats the call to God's people to go out of the midst of Babylon and deliver every man his soul from the fierce anger of the LORD. It is their wisdom, he says, to get out of the midst of Babylon lest they be involved, if not in her ruins, yet in her fears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 46. Henry explains the rumours as a gradual destruction foreseen in stages: a rumour shall come one year that Cyrus is making vast preparations for war, and after that, in another year, a rumour that his design is upon Babylon. The people are warned lest their hearts faint at these alarms, for they might have sought conditions of peace but were too proud and secure, and their hearts were hardened to their destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 47. Henry says God will do judgment upon Babylon's graven images, in token that the whole land shall be confounded and all her slain shall fall, with the wounded groaning throughout the country. Though the invaders are themselves idolaters, he notes, they shall destroy the images and temples of Babylon's gods, as an earnest of the abolishing of all counterfeit deities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 48. Henry says some shall rejoice in Babylon's fall, not as it is the misery of fellow-creatures, but as the manifestation of God's righteous judgment and as opening the way for the release of his captives. On these accounts, he says, heaven and earth and all in both shall sing for Babylon, for Babylon's ruin is Zion's praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 49. Henry says Cyrus shall measure to the Chaldeans the same that they measured to the Jews, so that every observer may discern God recompensing them for what they did against his people. Just as Babylon caused the slain of Israel to fall, slaying all without distinction, so at Babylon shall fall the slain of all the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 50. Henry says it is not only wisdom to quit the city when ruin approaches, but duty to quit the country too when ruin is accomplished and the captives are set at liberty by the pulling down of the prison over their heads. Those who escaped the sword must go away and stand not still, hastening to their own country, however comfortably seated in Babylon, for this is not their rest, but Canaan is, and wherever they are they must remember the LORD and let Jerusalem come into their minds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 51. Henry takes notice of the discouragement of the returning captives: reminded of Jerusalem, they cry that they are confounded, shame covers their faces, for strangers have come into the sanctuaries of the LORD's house. They cannot bear the thought of the sanctuary profaned and ruined by strangers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 52. Henry answers that discouragement with God's triumph over Babylon's gods: God will do judgment upon her graven images, and through all her land the wounded shall groan, so that the reproach of Jerusalem will be forever rolled away. The believing prospect of Jerusalem's recovery, he notes, will keep us from being ashamed of Jerusalem's ruins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 53. Henry says Babylon, thinking herself very safe and very great, will be deceived: though she should mount up to heaven and fortify the height of her strength, yet God will send spoilers against her who shall break through her strength and bring down her height. All her height and fortification will not do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 54. Henry says there is a sound of a cry coming from Babylon, a great outcry lamenting the great destruction from the land of the Chaldeans. This is the voice of mourning because the LORD has destroyed the great voice of mirth that used to be heard in Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 55. Henry says the LORD has spoiled Babylon and destroyed out of her the great voice, the voice of the multitude and of mirth, so that when her waves roar like great waters, the noise of their voice is uttered only in lamentation. The cry from Babylon replaces the city's former great voice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 56. Henry says when the spoiler comes upon Babylon her mighty men are immediately taken, their weapons fail them, and every one of their bows is broken and stands them in no stead. Their politics fail them too, he adds, for their princes and captains in council are made drunk, but the ground of it all is that the LORD God of recompences will surely requite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 57. Henry says Babylon's princes, wise men, captains, rulers and mighty men shall be made drunk and sleep a perpetual sleep, never waking. Their counsellors, he explains, are as men intoxicated through stupidity or despair, forming no right notions, staggering in their counsels and falling out among themselves, until the wine of God's wrath lays them into a fatal lethargy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 58. Henry says Babylon's broad walls shall be utterly broken and her high gates burned with fire, and the people labouring in the defence shall labour in vain in the fire. The walls, he notes, were some 200 cubits high and 50 broad, with stones laid in pitch scarcely separable, yet they shall be utterly broken and the defenders shall tire themselves to no good.   • Verse 60. Henry says a copy of this prophecy was taken, it seems by Jeremiah himself, since Baruch his scribe is not mentioned here, and Jeremiah wrote in a book all the evil that should come upon Babylon. It is of great advantage, he notes, both to propagating and to perpetuating the word of God to have it written and copies taken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
