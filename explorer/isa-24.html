The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 24 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Behold, the LORD maketh the earth empty, and maketh it waste, and turneth it upside down, and scattereth abroad the inhabitants thereof.

2 And it shall be, as with the people, so with the priest; as with the servant, so with his master; as with the maid, so with her mistress; as with the buyer, so with the seller; as with the lender, so with the borrower; as with the taker of usury, so with the giver of usury to him.

3 The land shall be utterly emptied, and utterly spoiled: for the LORD hath spoken this word.

4 The earth mourneth and fadeth away, the world languisheth and fadeth away, the haughty people of the earth do languish.

5 The earth also is defiled under the inhabitants thereof; because they have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, broken the everlasting covenant.

6 Therefore hath the curse devoured the earth, and they that dwell therein are desolate: therefore the inhabitants of the earth are burned, and few men left.

7 The new wine mourneth, the vine languisheth, all the merryhearted do sigh.

8 The mirth of tabrets ceaseth, the noise of them that rejoice endeth, the joy of the harp ceaseth.

9 They shall not drink wine with a song; strong drink shall be bitter to them that drink it.

10 The city of confusion is broken down: every house is shut up, that no man may come in.

11 There is a crying for wine in the streets; all joy is darkened, the mirth of the land is gone.

12 In the city is left desolation, and the gate is smitten with destruction.

13 When thus it shall be in the midst of the land among the people, there shall be as the shaking of an olive tree, and as the gleaning grapes when the vintage is done.

14 They shall lift up their voice, they shall sing for the majesty of the LORD, they shall cry aloud from the sea.

15 Wherefore glorify ye the LORD in the fires, even the name of the LORD God of Israel in the isles of the sea.

16 From the uttermost part of the earth have we heard songs, even glory to the righteous. But I said, My leanness, my leanness, woe unto me! the treacherous dealers have dealt treacherously; yea, the treacherous dealers have dealt very treacherously.

17 Fear, and the pit, and the snare, are upon thee, O inhabitant of the earth.

18 And it shall come to pass, that he who fleeth from the noise of the fear shall fall into the pit; and he that cometh up out of the midst of the pit shall be taken in the snare: for the windows from on high are open, and the foundations of the earth do shake.

19 The earth is utterly broken down, the earth is clean dissolved, the earth is moved exceedingly.

20 The earth shall reel to and fro like a drunkard, and shall be removed like a cottage; and the transgression thereof shall be heavy upon it; and it shall fall, and not rise again.

21 And it shall come to pass in that day, that the LORD shall punish the host of the high ones that are on high, and the kings of the earth upon the earth.

22 And they shall be gathered together, as prisoners are gathered in the pit, and shall be shut up in the prison, and after many days shall they be visited.

23 Then the moon shall be confounded, and the sun ashamed, when the LORD of hosts shall reign in mount Zion, and in Jerusalem, and before his ancients gloriously.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The first chapter of a continuous prophecy running through chapter 27: the earth is emptied and turned upside down, its inhabitants scattered, its proud city broken, and its rulers shut up as prisoners. It closes with the Lord of hosts reigning gloriously on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem, before his ancients, in a light that puts sun and moon to shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Behold, the Lord makes the earth empty, and makes it waste, and turns it upside down, and scatters abroad its inhabitants. Chapters 24–27 form one continuous poetical prophecy: the dispersion and successive calamities of the Jews, the gospel preached by the first Hebrew converts, judgments on the church's adversaries and its final triumph, thanksgiving for the apostate faction's overthrow, the righteous established in peace, and leviathan judged. Having treated the several nations in particular, Isaiah passes to the last times of the world at large and of Judah, representative and future head of the churches. The earth here is rather the land of Judah, and the desolation under Nebuchadnezzar prefigured that under Titus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. It will be as with the people, so with the priest; as with the servant, so with his master; as with the maid, so with her mistress; as with the buyer, so with the seller; as with the lender, so with the borrower; as with the taker of usury, so with the giver of usury to him. All alike will share the same calamity, and no favoured class will escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The earth mourns and fades away, the world languishes and fades away, and the haughty people of the earth do languish. The world is the kingdom of Israel, as in chapter 13 Babylon is, and the haughty are literally the height of the people, the high people, so even the nobles share the general distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The earth also is defiled under its inhabitants, because they have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, and broken the everlasting covenant. The land is defiled with innocent blood, and the laws, ordinance, and everlasting covenant are the moral laws, positive statutes, and national covenant designed to be forever between God and them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Therefore the curse has devoured the earth, and those who dwell in it are desolate; therefore the inhabitants of the earth are burned, and few men are left. The land is burned with the consuming wrath of heaven, whether internally or externally, as the people are consumed with the withering dryness of their doomed land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The new wine mourns, and the vine languishes, and all the merryhearted do sigh. The new wine mourns because there are none to drink it, or rather it has become vapid, and the vine languishes because there are none to cultivate it now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The mirth of tabrets ceases, the noise of those who rejoice ends, and the joy of the harp ceases. The mirth of tabrets and the joy of the harp end as in Revelation's picture of fallen Babylon, with all festive music silenced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They will not drink wine with a song, and strong drink will be bitter to them that drink it. Wine will no longer be drunk with a song, the usual accompaniment of feasts, and strong drink — date wine — will be bitter in consequence of the national calamities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The city of confusion is broken down, and every house is shut up, that no man may come in. The city of confusion is rather desolation, what Jerusalem would become, called so by anticipation, and the houses are shut up through fear, or rather choked up by ruins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. There is a crying for wine in the streets, and all joy is darkened, and the mirth of the land is gone. The crying is for wine to drown sorrows in drink, as in chapter 16, and Joel, written about the same time, resembles this. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. In the city is left desolation, and the gate is smitten with destruction. The gate is smitten with a crash, as with a great tumult the gate is battered down, leaving only desolation in the city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. When thus it will be in the midst of the land among the people, it will be as the shaking of an olive tree, and as the gleaning grapes when the vintage is done. The land is Judea, and among the people a remnant will be left, as the shaking or after-picking of an olive tree, a few remaining on the highest boughs as in chapter 17. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. They will lift up their voice, and sing for the majesty of the Lord, and cry aloud from the sea. They are those who are left, the remnant, singing a thanksgiving for the goodness of the Lord who has so mercifully preserved them, crying from the distant lands beyond the sea whither they have escaped. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Therefore glorify the Lord in the fires, even the name of the Lord God of Israel in the isles of the sea. The fires may be translated caves, or the fires of affliction that loose carnal bonds without injuring a hair, as with Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, or in the regions of morning light, the east, in antithesis to the isles of the sea, the west: wherever the scattered are, east or west, they are still to glorify the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. From the uttermost part of the earth we have heard songs, even glory to the righteous. But Isaiah says: My leanness, my leanness, woe unto me! The treacherous dealers have dealt treacherously, yes, the treacherous dealers have dealt very treacherously. Songs to God come together to Palestine from distant lands as a grand chorus, with glory to the righteous as their burden; amidst exile, loss of temple, and all dear to man, their confidence in God is unshaken. These songs recall the joy of other times and draw from Jerusalem in her calamities the cry "My leanness" — or glory to the Just One, with leanness expressing man's corruption that led the Jews to crucify the Just One and the prophet's own deficiency of righteousness. The treacherous dealers are the foreign nations oppressing Jerusalem and overcoming it by stratagem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Fear, and the pit, and the snare, are upon you, O inhabitant of the earth. This verse explains the wretchedness of verse 16, and Jeremiah uses the same proverbial words: the inhabitants are nowhere safe, for if they escape one danger they fall into another and worse on the opposite side, and fear is the term for the cords with coloured feathers which, fluttered in the air, scare beasts into the pitfall or birds into the snare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He who flees from the noise of the fear will fall into the pit, and he who comes up out of the midst of the pit will be taken in the snare, for the windows from on high are open, and the foundations of the earth do shake. Fear here is the shout designed to rouse game and drive it into the pitfall, and the windows from on high are taken from the deluge account, the flood-gates, so the final judgments of fire on the apostate world are compared to the deluge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The earth is utterly broken down, the earth is clean dissolved, and the earth is moved exceedingly. The land is pictured by the image of an earthquake, utterly broken, dissolved, and moved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The earth will reel to and fro like a drunkard, and will be removed like a cottage, and the transgression thereof will be heavy upon it, and it will fall, and not rise again. The earth will wave to and fro like a hammock, a hanging couch suspended from trees by cords as Arab land-keepers use, swung about by the wind, and its transgression will lie heavy upon it like an overwhelming burden; it will not rise again in those convulsions, though not meaning it would never rise, as verse 23 shows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. In that day the Lord will punish the host of the high ones that are on high, and the kings of the earth upon the earth. The high ones are the heavenly host, either the visible host of heaven, the present economy of nature affected by sun, moon, and stars, objects of idolatry, being abolished, or rather the invisible rulers of the darkness of this world, as the antithesis to kings of the earth shows, for angels preside over kingdoms of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. They will be gathered together, as prisoners are gathered in the pit, and will be shut up in the prison, and after many days they will be visited. The image is of captives thrust together into a dungeon, and the shutting up of the Jews in Jerusalem under Nebuchadnezzar and again under Titus was to be followed by a visitation of mercy after many days — seventy years in the former case, the time not yet elapsed in the latter — though visited may also be taken in wrath, and probably a double visitation is intended, deliverance to the elect and wrath to hardened unbelievers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Then the moon will be confounded, and the sun ashamed, when the Lord of hosts will reign in Mount Zion, and in Jerusalem, and before his ancients gloriously. Still future, of which Jesus' triumphal entry into Jerusalem amidst hosannas was a pledge, Jehovah's kingdom will be set up with a splendour exceeding the light of sun and moon under the previous order, and his ancients are the elders of his people, or his ancient people the Jews, before whom he reigns gloriously after the overthrow of world kingdoms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
