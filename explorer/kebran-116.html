The Text — Kebra Nagast 116 (E. A. Wallis Budge, The Queen of Sheba and Her Only Son Menyelek (1922), public domain, for chs 1–118; Ge'ez text for ch. 119)

*116. CONCERNING THE CHARIOT OF ETHIOPIA*

1 And the Archbishops answered and said unto Gregory, the Worker of Wonders, "Behold now, thou hast told us concerning the vanquisher of the enemy of Rômê, and now [tell us] of the chariot of Ethiopia and whether it shall remain henceforward, to the Coming of Christ, as thou hast told us concerning Zion, and concerning the faith of the people of Ethiopia, and likewise if their chariot shall remain." And Gregory said unto them, "It shall assuredly not disappear. And again, hearken ye unto me and I will declare this unto you: A few Jews shall lift up their heads against our faith in Nâgrân and in Armenia in the days after this, and this God will do by His Will so that He may destroy them, for Armenia is a territory of Rômê and Nâgrân is a territory of Ethiopia."

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The archbishops ask Gregory the Worker of Wonders a further question: he has told them concerning the vanquisher of the enemy of Rome, and now they ask concerning the chariot of Ethiopia — whether it shall remain henceforward to the coming of Christ, as he has told them concerning Zion and concerning the faith of the people of Ethiopia. Gregory answers that it shall assuredly not disappear, and goes on to speak of a few Jews lifting up their heads against the faith in Nagran and in Armenia, Nagran being a territory of Ethiopia and Armenia a territory of Rome. Unfamiliar terms

  • Zion. In this book, “Zion” is the name given to the Tabernacle of the Law of God — the Ark and its Law — not only the hill in Jerusalem. The book itself calls her “our Lady Zion, the holy and heavenly Tabernacle of the Law of God.” Interpreted Chapter text above
  • Rômê. The book's name for the Roman kingdom; chapter 72 heads its account “Concerning the King of Rome (Constantinople).” Interpreted Chapter text above
