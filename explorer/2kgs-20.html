The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In those days was Hezekiah sick unto death. And the prophet Isaiah the son of Amoz came to him, and said unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Set thine house in order; for thou shalt die, and not live.

2 Then he turned his face to the wall, and prayed unto the LORD, saying,

3 I beseech thee, O LORD, remember now how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart, and have done that which is good in thy sight. And Hezekiah wept sore.

4 And it came to pass, afore Isaiah was gone out into the middle court, that the word of the LORD came to him, saying,

5 Turn again, and tell Hezekiah the captain of my people, Thus saith the LORD, the God of David thy father, I have heard thy prayer, I have seen thy tears: behold, I will heal thee: on the third day thou shalt go up unto the house of the LORD.

6 And I will add unto thy days fifteen years; and I will deliver thee and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria; and I will defend this city for mine own sake, and for my servant David’s sake.

7 And Isaiah said, Take a lump of figs. And they took and laid it on the boil, and he recovered.

8 And Hezekiah said unto Isaiah, What shall be the sign that the LORD will heal me, and that I shall go up into the house of the LORD the third day?

9 And Isaiah said, This sign shalt thou have of the LORD, that the LORD will do the thing that he hath spoken: shall the shadow go forward ten degrees, or go back ten degrees?

10 And Hezekiah answered, It is a light thing for the shadow to go down ten degrees: nay, but let the shadow return backward ten degrees.

11 And Isaiah the prophet cried unto the LORD: and he brought the shadow ten degrees backward, by which it had gone down in the dial of Ahaz.

12 At that time Berodachbaladan, the son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a present unto Hezekiah: for he had heard that Hezekiah had been sick.

13 And Hezekiah hearkened unto them, and shewed them all the house of his precious things, the silver, and the gold, and the spices, and the precious ointment, and all the house of his armour, and all that was found in his treasures: there was nothing in his house, nor in all his dominion, that Hezekiah shewed them not.

14 Then came Isaiah the prophet unto king Hezekiah, and said unto him, What said these men? and from whence came they unto thee? And Hezekiah said, They are come from a far country, even from Babylon.

15 And he said, What have they seen in thine house? And Hezekiah answered, All the things that are in mine house have they seen: there is nothing among my treasures that I have not shewed them.

16 And Isaiah said unto Hezekiah, Hear the word of the LORD.

17 Behold, the days come, that all that is in thine house, and that which thy fathers have laid up in store unto this day, shall be carried into Babylon: nothing shall be left, saith the LORD.

18 And of thy sons that shall issue from thee, which thou shalt beget, shall they take away; and they shall be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.

19 Then said Hezekiah unto Isaiah, Good is the word of the LORD which thou hast spoken. And he said, Is it not good, if peace and truth be in my days?

20 And the rest of the acts of Hezekiah, and all his might, and how he made a pool, and a conduit, and brought water into the city, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

21 And Hezekiah slept with his fathers: and Manasseh his son reigned in his stead.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Hezekiah, sick unto death, prays and weeps, and Isaiah returns with fifteen more years and a sign: the shadow goes back ten degrees on the dial of Ahaz. Hezekiah then shows Babylon's envoys all his treasures, and Isaiah foretells that all of it will one day be carried to Babylon. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In those days Hezekiah was sick unto death, and Isaiah son of Amoz came saying, "Thus saith the LORD, Set thine house in order; for thou shalt die, and not live." JFB notes this sudden severe illness occurred in the very year of the Assyrian invasion, and setting his house in order referred chiefly to the kingdom, since he had as yet no son, while the disease was malignant and mortal unless God miraculously interposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed unto the LORD. JFB notes this was not like Ahab's fretful discontent, but to secure a better opportunity for prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Hezekiah besought the LORD to remember how he had walked before him in truth and with a perfect heart, doing what was good in his sight, and he wept sore. JFB explains his thoughts turned to David's promise, which he had kept as faithfully as human infirmity admitted, free from great crimes that cut life short, grieving partly from love of life, partly from the Mosaic dispensation's obscurity about immortality, and partly from his reformation plans being frustrated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Before Isaiah had gone out into the middle court, the LORD's word came to him. JFB identifies the middle court as that of the royal castle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Isaiah was told to turn again and tell Hezekiah, captain of God's people, that the LORD, the God of David his father, had heard his prayer and seen his tears, and would heal him, so that on the third day he would go up to the LORD's house. JFB calls this an immediate answer assuring the LORD was mindful of his promise to David, to be accomplished both by prolonging Hezekiah's life and delivering him from Assyria, with perfect recovery in three days showing the cure's miraculous character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The LORD would add fifteen years to Hezekiah's days and deliver him and Jerusalem from Assyria's king, defending the city for his own sake and David's sake. JFB notes the promise joined prolongation of life with deliverance from the Assyrians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Isaiah said to take a lump of figs, and they laid it on the boil, and Hezekiah recovered. JFB notes figs are used by Arab physicians to ripen and soothe inflammatory ulcers, and the text gives no hint plague was raging in Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Hezekiah asked Isaiah what sign the LORD would heal him and he would go up to the LORD's house on the third day. JFB explains his recovery in nature's course was so unlooked for that he asked for a token to justify reliance on the prophet's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Isaiah said the sign would be that the LORD would do the thing he had spoken: the shadow would go forward ten degrees or go back ten degrees. JFB explains the dial's original word meant degrees or steps, and many supposed it a stair whose shadows marked the sun's course, though more probably it was a proper instrument imported from Babylon by Ahaz. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Hezekiah answered it was a light thing for the shadow to go down ten degrees, but let it return backward ten degrees. JFB notes the king specified this sign, and it was granted to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Isaiah cried unto the LORD, and he brought the shadow ten degrees backward, by which it had gone down in Ahaz's dial. JFB notes the retrogression was miraculously accomplished by God's omnipotent power, temporary, local, and intended only for Hezekiah and his court's satisfaction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. At that time Berodachbaladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a present to Hezekiah, having heard he had been sick. JFB identifies Berodachbaladan as the first king of Babylon mentioned in sacred history, formerly Assyria's viceroys having ruled Babylon, who threw off the yoke and resisted Assyria, and whose congratulations likely accompanied proposals for a defensive alliance against their common enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Hezekiah hearkened to them and showed them all his precious things, silver, gold, spices, precious ointment, armour, and all found in his treasures, hiding nothing. JFB notes that though he had paid Sennacherib so much tribute as to exhaust his treasury, presents had since come from various quarters, and possibly the Assyrian camp's spoils restored what he had paid; yet the vain display offended his divine liege lord, who sent Isaiah to reprove him, and it baited the rapacious foreigners' cupidity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Isaiah came to Hezekiah asking what these men had said and whence they came, and Hezekiah answered they had come from far, from Babylon. JFB presents Isaiah's visit as the reproof sent for the vain display. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Isaiah asked what they had seen in Hezekiah's house, and Hezekiah answered there was nothing among his treasures he had not showed them. JFB notes the king's answer showed how elated he was by the compliment of their visit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Hear the word of the LORD." JFB's section on the Babylonian embassy turns here to the prophetic sentence on the display. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Isaiah declared days would come when all in Hezekiah's house and all his fathers had laid up would be carried to Babylon, with nothing left. JFB notes these rapacious foreigners would at no distant period return, pillage the country, and transfer all the displayed possessions to Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Of Hezekiah's sons issuing from him, some would be taken away to be eunuchs in Babylon's king's palace. JFB notes his posterity would be carried as court attendants in Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "Good is the word of the LORD which thou hast spoken," adding there would be peace and truth in his days. JFB notes this indicated humble, pious resignation, and the concluding part, uttered after a pause as an ejaculation to himself, expressed thankfulness that judgment would be suspended during his own lifetime. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Hezekiah slept with his fathers, and Manasseh his son reigned in his stead. JFB's chapter closes with this succession after Hezekiah's lengthened life and deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
