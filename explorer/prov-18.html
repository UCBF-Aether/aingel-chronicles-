The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Through desire a man, having separated himself, seeketh and intermeddleth with all wisdom.

2 A fool hath no delight in understanding, but that his heart may discover itself.

3 When the wicked cometh, then cometh also contempt, and with ignominy reproach.

4 The words of a man’s mouth are as deep waters, and the wellspring of wisdom as a flowing brook.

5 It is not good to accept the person of the wicked, to overthrow the righteous in judgment.

6 A fool’s lips enter into contention, and his mouth calleth for strokes.

7 A fool’s mouth is his destruction, and his lips are the snare of his soul.

8 The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly.

9 He also that is slothful in his work is brother to him that is a great waster.

10 The name of the LORD is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe.

11 The rich man’s wealth is his strong city, and as an high wall in his own conceit.

12 Before destruction the heart of man is haughty, and before honour is humility.

13 He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.

14 The spirit of a man will sustain his infirmity; but a wounded spirit who can bear?

15 The heart of the prudent getteth knowledge; and the ear of the wise seeketh knowledge.

16 A man’s gift maketh room for him, and bringeth him before great men.

17 He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him.

18 The lot causeth contentions to cease, and parteth between the mighty.

19 A brother offended is harder to be won than a strong city: and their contentions are like the bars of a castle.

20 A man’s belly shall be satisfied with the fruit of his mouth; and with the increase of his lips shall he be filled.

21 Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof.

22 Whoso findeth a wife findeth a good thing, and obtaineth favour of the LORD.

23 The poor useth intreaties; but the rich answereth roughly.

24 A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly: and there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Separation, the fool's mouth and the talebearer's wounds open a chapter on speech and standing: the name of the LORD is a strong tower the righteous run into, while the rich man's wealth is only a strong city in his own conceit. Death and life are in the power of the tongue, a brother offended is harder to win than a strong city, and a friend sticks closer than a brother. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Through desire a man, having separated himself, seeks and intermeddles with all wisdom, for if we would get knowledge and grace, we must try all methods of improving ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. A fool has no delight in understanding, but that his heart may discover itself, for those who make nothing to purpose of learning or religion have only the design of making a show. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. When the wicked comes, then comes also contempt, and with ignominy reproach, for as soon as sin entered, shame followed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The words of a man's mouth are as deep waters, and the wellspring of wisdom as a flowing brook, for the wellspring of wisdom in a believer's heart continually supplies words of wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. It is not good to accept the person of the wicked, to overthrow the righteous in judgment, for the merits of a cause must be looked to, not the person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. A fool's lips enter into contention, and his mouth calls for strokes, for what mischief bad men do to themselves by their ungoverned tongues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. A fool's mouth is his destruction, and his lips are the snare of his soul, for what mischief bad men do to themselves by their ungoverned tongues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The words of a talebearer are as wounds, going down into the innermost parts of the belly, for how base are those who sow contention, and what fatal effects may be expected from small beginnings of jealousy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. He slothful in his work is brother to him who is a great waster, for omissions of duty, and in duty, are fatal to the soul, as well as commissions of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The name of the LORD is a strong tower, and the righteous run into it and are safe, for Divine power made known in and through our Lord Jesus Christ forms a strong tower for the believer who relies on the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The rich man's wealth is his strong city, and as a high wall in his own conceit, yet how deceitful that defence is: it will fail when most in need, exposing him to the wrath of the Judge he despised as Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Before destruction the heart of man is haughty, and before honour is humility, for after the heart is lifted up with pride a fall comes, but honour shall be humility's reward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. He who answers a matter before he hears it, it is folly and shame to him, for eagerness with self-conceit will expose to shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The spirit of a man will sustain his infirmity, but a wounded spirit who can bear, for firmness of mind supports under many pains, but when conscience is tortured with remorse no human fortitude can bear the misery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The heart of the prudent gets knowledge, and the ear of the wise seeks knowledge, for we must get knowledge not only into our heads but into our hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. A man's gift makes room for him and brings him before great men, for blessed be the Lord who makes us welcome to his throne without money and without price, and may his gifts make room for him in our souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. He first in his own cause seems just, but his neighbour comes and searches him, for it is well to listen to our enemies, that we may form a better judgment of ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. The lot causes contentions to cease and parts between the mighty, for it was customary sometimes to refer matters to God by casting lots with solemn prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. A brother offended is harder to be won than a strong city, and their contentions are like bars of a castle, for great care must be taken to prevent quarrels among relations and those under obligations to each other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. A man's belly shall be satisfied with the fruit of his mouth, and with the increase of his lips shall he be filled, for the belly is here put for the heart, and what it is filled with, our satisfaction and inward peace will be accordingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Death and life are in the power of the tongue, and they who love it shall eat its fruit, for many a one has caused his own death, or others', by a false or injurious tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Whoso finds a wife finds a good thing and obtains favour of the LORD, for a good wife is a great blessing to a man and a token of Divine favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. The poor uses entreaties, but the rich answers roughly, for poverty tells men they must not order or demand, and at God's throne of grace we are all poor and must use entreaties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. A man who has friends must show himself friendly, and there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother, for Christ Jesus never will forsake those who trust in and love him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
