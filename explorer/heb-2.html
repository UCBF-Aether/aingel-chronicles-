The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hebrews 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Therefore we ought to give the more earnest heed to the things which we have heard, lest at any time we should let them slip.

2 For if the word spoken by angels was stedfast, and every transgression and disobedience received a just recompence of reward;

3 How shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation; which at the first began to be spoken by the Lord, and was confirmed unto us by them that heard him;

4 God also bearing them witness, both with signs and wonders, and with divers miracles, and gifts of the Holy Ghost, according to his own will?

5 For unto the angels hath he not put in subjection the world to come, whereof we speak.

6 But one in a certain place testified, saying, What is man, that thou art mindful of him? or the son of man that thou visitest him?

7 Thou madest him a little lower than the angels; thou crownedst him with glory and honour, and didst set him over the works of thy hands:

8 Thou hast put all things in subjection under his feet. For in that he put all in subjection under him, he left nothing that is not put under him. But now we see not yet all things put under him.

9 But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels for the suffering of death, crowned with glory and honour; that he by the grace of God should taste death for every man.

10 For it became him, for whom are all things, and by whom are all things, in bringing many sons unto glory, to make the captain of their salvation perfect through sufferings.

11 For both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one: for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren,

12 Saying, I will declare thy name unto my brethren, in the midst of the church will I sing praise unto thee.

13 And again, I will put my trust in him. And again, Behold I and the children which God hath given me.

14 Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil;

15 And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage.

16 For verily he took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Abraham.

17 Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren, that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make reconciliation for the sins of the people.

18 For in that he himself hath suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Pay closer attention, the writer warns, lest readers drift from so great a salvation, first spoken by the Lord and confirmed by signs, wonders and gifts of the Spirit. Jesus was made a little lower than the angels so that, by tasting death for everyone, he might bring many sons to glory, becoming their brother and a merciful, faithful high priest who can help those who are tempted. Interpreted Editorial overview of the chapter text; verse-by-verse study-note summaries follow

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. We ought to give the more earnest heed to the things heard, lest at any time we let them slip. Henry compares minds and memories to a leaky vessel, which does not retain what is poured in without much care, through corruption, temptation, and worldly cares. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. If the word spoken by angels was steadfast, and every transgression received a just recompense, the gospel word stands firmer still. Henry warns that even partial neglects will not escape rebukes, and often bring darkness on souls they do not finally ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. How shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation, first spoken by the Lord and confirmed by those who heard him? Henry defines the sin as contempt of saving grace: making light of it, not caring for it, and not regarding either the worth of gospel grace or our undone state without it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. God bore witness with signs, wonders, divers miracles, and gifts of the Holy Ghost, according to his own will. Henry notes the gospel was confirmed by witnesses of what Christ began to do and teach, and by gifts qualifying them for their work, so that we have sure ground for faith and a strong foundation for hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The world to come, of which the epistle speaks, is not put in subjection to angels. Henry observes that neither the church's present state nor its more completely restored state, when the prince of this world is cast out, is left to the government of angels: Christ will take his great power and reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. One testified, 'What is man, that thou art mindful of him, or the son of man, that thou visitest him?' Henry asks what the moving cause of all God's kindness in giving Christ is, and answers: the grace of God, shown to creatures so lowly as this question confesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Man was made a little lower than the angels, crowned with glory and honour, and set over the works of God's hands. Henry reads the crowning as the reward of Christ's humiliation in suffering death, by which the ancient scripture is fulfilled in him alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. All things are put in subjection under his feet, leaving nothing not put under him, though we do not yet see all things put under him. Henry notes that as reward for suffering death, Christ has unlimited dominion over all things, even where its full display is still to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. We see Jesus, made a little lower than the angels for the suffering of death, crowned with glory and honour, that by the grace of God he should taste death for every man. Henry stresses that grace is the moving cause: God has done wonderful things for us in creation and providence, yet we have made the basest returns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. It became him, for whom are all things and by whom are all things, in bringing many sons to glory, to make the captain of their salvation perfect through sufferings. Henry sees peculiar glory in the cross here: his way to the crown was by the cross, and so must his people's be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Both he who sanctifies and those who are sanctified are all of one, so he is not ashamed to call them brethren. Henry explains that Christ sanctifies, having purchased and sent the sanctifying Spirit, and believers are set apart to high and holy uses, brought into relation with Christ as children of one heavenly Father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. 'I will declare thy name unto my brethren, in the midst of the church will I sing praise unto thee.' Henry cites this as the first of three scriptures showing Christ is not ashamed to call believers brethren, a condescension expressing his high superiority to our nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. 'I will put my trust in him,' and again, 'Behold I and the children which God hath given me.' Henry points to these further texts, from Psalm 18 and Isaiah 8, as proof of the same brotherhood: Christ trusts as man trusts, and presents the children God gave him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Since the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he himself likewise took part of the same, that through death he might destroy him who had the power of death, that is, the devil. Henry notes the price paid was enough for all and suitable to all precisely because it was paid in our nature, making way for deliverance from Satan's bondage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. He delivers those who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage. Henry urges those who dread death not to outbrave or stifle their terrors, nor grow careless through despair, but to seek pardon, peace, grace, and a lively hope of heaven by faith in him who died and rose again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. He took not on him the nature of angels, but the seed of Abraham. Henry explains that the angels fell and remained without hope or help, and Christ never designed to be the Saviour of fallen angels; moreover, the nature of angels could not be an atoning sacrifice for the sin of man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. In all things it behoved him to be made like his brethren, that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest, to make reconciliation for the sins of the people. Henry marvels at the love shown here: Christ knew what he must suffer in our nature and how he must die in it, yet he readily took it upon him, opening pardon through faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. In that he himself suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted. Henry notes the remembrance of his own sorrows and temptations makes Christ mindful of his people's trials and ready to help: he passed through the same temptations himself, yet continued perfectly free from sin, so no tempted soul crying to the Lord in faith ever perished under temptation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

  • captain of their salvation. author and leader of salvation, made perfect through sufferings in bringing many sons to glory (verse 10) Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Connections to other passages

  • Hebrews 2:13 — Psalm 18 and Isaiah 8. Henry points to these further texts as the source of the quotations in verse 13, “I will put my trust in him” and “Behold I and the children which God hath given me,” as proof of the same brotherhood: Christ trusts as man trusts, and presents the children given to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise), Hebrews 2:13
