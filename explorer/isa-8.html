The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover the LORD said unto me, Take thee a great roll, and write in it with a man’s pen concerning Mahershalalhashbaz.

2 And I took unto me faithful witnesses to record, Uriah the priest, and Zechariah the son of Jeberechiah.

3 And I went unto the prophetess; and she conceived, and bare a son. Then said the LORD to me, Call his name Mahershalalhashbaz.

4 For before the child shall have knowledge to cry, My father, and my mother, the riches of Damascus and the spoil of Samaria shall be taken away before the king of Assyria.

5 The LORD spake also unto me again, saying,

6 Forasmuch as this people refuseth the waters of Shiloah that go softly, and rejoice in Rezin and Remaliah’s son;

7 Now therefore, behold, the Lord bringeth up upon them the waters of the river, strong and many, even the king of Assyria, and all his glory: and he shall come up over all his channels, and go over all his banks:

8 And he shall pass through Judah; he shall overflow and go over, he shall reach even to the neck; and the stretching out of his wings shall fill the breadth of thy land, O Immanuel.

9 Associate yourselves, O ye people, and ye shall be broken in pieces; and give ear, all ye of far countries: gird yourselves, and ye shall be broken in pieces; gird yourselves, and ye shall be broken in pieces.

10 Take counsel together, and it shall come to nought; speak the word, and it shall not stand: for God is with us.

11 For the LORD spake thus to me with a strong hand, and instructed me that I should not walk in the way of this people, saying,

12 Say ye not, A confederacy, to all them to whom this people shall say, A confederacy; neither fear ye their fear, nor be afraid.

13 Sanctify the LORD of hosts himself; and let him be your fear, and let him be your dread.

14 And he shall be for a sanctuary; but for a stone of stumbling and for a rock of offence to both the houses of Israel, for a gin and for a snare to the inhabitants of Jerusalem.

15 And many among them shall stumble, and fall, and be broken, and be snared, and be taken.

16 Bind up the testimony, seal the law among my disciples.

17 And I will wait upon the LORD, that hideth his face from the house of Jacob, and I will look for him.

18 Behold, I and the children whom the LORD hath given me are for signs and for wonders in Israel from the LORD of hosts, which dwelleth in mount Zion.

19 And when they shall say unto you, Seek unto them that have familiar spirits, and unto wizards that peep, and that mutter: should not a people seek unto their God? for the living to the dead?

20 To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them.

21 And they shall pass through it, hardly bestead and hungry: and it shall come to pass, that when they shall be hungry, they shall fret themselves, and curse their king and their God, and look upward.

22 And they shall look unto the earth; and behold trouble and darkness, dimness of anguish; and they shall be driven to darkness.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God tells Isaiah to write a child's name, Maher-shalal-hash-baz, in large plain letters for all to see: before the child can speak, Assyria will plunder Damascus and Samaria. The same Assyrian flood will reach up to Judah's neck, yet the chapter ends with a warning to trust the Lord's word rather than mediums and wizards, lest those who reject it be driven into darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Lord told Isaiah to take a great roll — a tablet of wood, metal, or stone — and write on it with a man's pen, in ordinary characters the humblest can read, concerning Maher-shalal-hash-baz. The large, plain letters ensured that after the event all might see it had been predicted, and the name means that the Assyrians hasten to the spoil and speed to the prey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Isaiah was to take faithful witnesses, Uriah the priest and Zechariah son of Jeberechiah, to attest the tablet. Uriah was an accomplice of Ahaz in idolatry and therefore a witness not likely to help the prophet fabricate a prophecy after the event; the witnesses existed so that when the event came they could testify the tablet was inscribed beforehand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Isaiah went to the prophetess — perhaps the same as the virgin of chapter 7, now married as his second wife — and she conceived and bore a son, whose name the Lord gave as Maher-shalal-hash-baz. Immanuel remains distinct from this child even in the temporary sense, with about three years in all from the chapter 7 prophecy to this child crying "Father," agreeing with chapters 7:15–16. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For before the child knows how to cry, My father and my mother, the riches of Damascus and the spoil of Samaria will be taken away before the king of Assyria. JFB notes this is within a year, a shorter interval than in chapter 7, since the accomplishment is now nearer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Lord spoke again to Isaiah, saying: Forasmuch as this people refuses the waters of Shiloah that go softly, and rejoice in Rezin and Remaliah's son. Shiloah, meaning "sent," its water sent through an aqueduct, figures the mild though now weak sway of the house of David, and in the highest sense the benignant sway of Jehovah in the theocracy administered through David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. This people — both Israel, which preferred alliance with Rezin to the kings of Judah, and Judah, a party of which favoured the son of Tabeal and sought an Assyrian alliance — has refused Shiloah and rejoiced in Rezin and Remaliah's son. Both nations alike rejected the divine Shiloah, and God pointedly calls them "this people," not "my people" as when he expresses favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Therefore the Lord will bring upon them the waters of the river, strong and many, even the king of Assyria and all his glory, and it will come up over all its channels and go over all its banks. The Assyrian flood, first to overflow Syria and Samaria, will rise high enough to reach rebel Judah also, like the Euphrates swollen in spring by melting Armenian snow, with Eastern kings travelling in gorgeous retinue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The flood will pass through Judah, reaching even to the neck, and the stretching out of its wings will fill the breadth of thy land, O Immanuel. When waters reach the neck a man is near drowning, yet the head is not overflowed: Jerusalem on her hills is the head, and though danger will come near at Sennacherib's invasion, she will be spared — a pledge that Immanuel's land, however overwhelmed, will be saved at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Associate yourselves, O peoples — rather, raise tumults, do your worst — and you will be thrown into consternation; gird yourselves, and you will be thrown into consternation. The name Immanuel suggests the ultimate safety of Immanuel's land from its present invaders and even from the Assyrians, since the succession of David's house cannot be set aside, for Messiah is to be born in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Take counsel together and it will come to nothing; speak the word and it will not stand, for God is with us. The word of command for the assault of Jerusalem fails because "Immanuel" implies God is with his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. For the Lord spoke to Isaiah with a strong hand — the felt impulse of inspiration grasping him — instructing him not to walk in the way of this people. Their way was distrust of Jehovah and the panic that led them and Ahaz to seek Assyrian aid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Do not say, A confederacy, to all those to whom this people says, A confederacy; neither fear their fear, nor be afraid. The confederacy is rather a conspiracy — the unnatural combination of Israel with Syrian foreigners against Judah and the theocracy — and the fear meant is the hostile conspiracy as an object of fear, which believers must not share or spread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Sanctify the Lord of hosts himself, and let him be your fear and your dread. To sanctify him is to honour his holy name by regarding him as the only hope of safety, fearing lest you provoke his wrath by fear of man and distrust of him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He will be for a sanctuary — an inviolable asylum like the altar of the temple — to those who fear and trust in him, but for a stone of stumbling and a rock of offence to both houses of Israel, and for a gin and a snare to the inhabitants of Jerusalem. The very stone Immanuel, a sanctuary on belief, becomes a fatal stumbling-block through unbelief, as Jesus himself applies it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Many among them will stumble and fall, and be broken, snared, and taken, images drawn from the means used in taking wild animals. The gin of verse 14 closes on those who reject the sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Bind up the testimony and seal the law among my disciples. What Isaiah had briefly inscribed as Maher-shalal-hash-baz he later wrote out more fully and now seals, implying it relates to distant events and is a sealed testimony understood only in part among God's disciples, those who sanctify the Lord by obedient trust, until later revelations clear it up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Whatever the rest of the nation does, Isaiah says, I will wait upon the Lord, who hides his face from the house of Jacob, and I will look for him. Though God seems to withdraw his countenance from Judah, the prophet will wait and trust in him though he cannot see him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Behold, I and the children whom the Lord has given me are for signs and wonders in Israel from the Lord of hosts, who dwells in Mount Zion. Isaiah's own name means salvation of Jehovah, and his children's names were signs of coming and final deliverance; Hebrews quotes this to prove the manhood of Messiah, whose believers are his children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. When people say, Seek unto those with familiar spirits and wizards that peep and mutter, the answer is: should not a people seek unto their God, and for the living seek to the dead? Necromancers and spirit-charmers, chirping faintly like young birds as if from the grave, followed in the wake of idolatry under Ahaz, though the law forbids consulting them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them. The revelation of God by his prophet is the standard to which necromancy's advisers must be referred, and those in the dark night of trial without a dawn of hope will themselves say: seek not to necromancy but to the law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. They will pass through the land hardly bestead and hungry, and when they are hungry they will fret themselves and curse their king and their God, and look upward. This describes the despair of those who sought necromancy instead of God: given over to despair, they curse Jehovah their King and God, foreshadowing those under the beast's kingdom who curse God under his plagues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. They will look unto the earth, and behold trouble and darkness, dimness of anguish, and they will be driven to darkness. Whether they look up to heaven or down to the land of Judea, nothing but despair presents itself, and they are driven onward as by a sweeping storm into thick darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Maher-shalal-hash-baz. The name Isaiah writes in large plain letters means, the note on verse 1 says, that the Assyrians hasten to the spoil and speed to the prey — a public, readable warning that Damascus and Samaria will fall quickly. Interpreted Study note on verse v1, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
