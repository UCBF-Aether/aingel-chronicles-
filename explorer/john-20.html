The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — John 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The first day of the week cometh Mary Magdalene early, when it was yet dark, unto the sepulchre, and seeth the stone taken away from the sepulchre.

2 Then she runneth, and cometh to Simon Peter, and to the other disciple, whom Jesus loved, and saith unto them, They have taken away the LORD out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him.

3 Peter therefore went forth, and that other disciple, and came to the sepulchre.

4 So they ran both together: and the other disciple did outrun Peter, and came first to the sepulchre.

5 And he stooping down, and looking in, saw the linen clothes lying; yet went he not in.

6 Then cometh Simon Peter following him, and went into the sepulchre, and seeth the linen clothes lie,

7 And the napkin, that was about his head, not lying with the linen clothes, but wrapped together in a place by itself.

8 Then went in also that other disciple, which came first to the sepulchre, and he saw, and believed.

9 For as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead.

10 Then the disciples went away again unto their own home.

11 But Mary stood without at the sepulchre weeping: and as she wept, she stooped down, and looked into the sepulchre,

12 And seeth two angels in white sitting, the one at the head, and the other at the feet, where the body of Jesus had lain.

13 And they say unto her, Woman, why weepest thou? She saith unto them, Because they have taken away my LORD, and I know not where they have laid him.

14 And when she had thus said, she turned herself back, and saw Jesus standing, and knew not that it was Jesus.

15 Jesus saith unto her, Woman, why weepest thou? whom seekest thou? She, supposing him to be the gardener, saith unto him, Sir, if thou have borne him hence, tell me where thou hast laid him, and I will take him away.

16 Jesus saith unto her, Mary. She turned herself, and saith unto him, Rabboni; which is to say, Master.

17 Jesus saith unto her, Touch me not; for I am not yet ascended to my Father: but go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and your Father; and to my God, and your God.

18 Mary Magdalene came and told the disciples that she had seen the LORD, and that he had spoken these things unto her.

19 Then the same day at evening, being the first day of the week, when the doors were shut where the disciples were assembled for fear of the Jews, came Jesus and stood in the midst, and saith unto them, Peace be unto you.

20 And when he had so said, he shewed unto them his hands and his side. Then were the disciples glad, when they saw the LORD.

21 Then said Jesus to them again, Peace be unto you: as my Father hath sent me, even so send I you.

22 And when he had said this, he breathed on them, and saith unto them, Receive ye the Holy Ghost:

23 Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them; and whose soever sins ye retain, they are retained.

24 But Thomas, one of the twelve, called Didymus, was not with them when Jesus came.

25 The other disciples therefore said unto him, We have seen the LORD. But he said unto them, Except I shall see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger into the print of the nails, and thrust my hand into his side, I will not believe.

26 And after eight days again his disciples were within, and Thomas with them: then came Jesus, the doors being shut, and stood in the midst, and said, Peace be unto you.

27 Then saith he to Thomas, Reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands; and reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side: and be not faithless, but believing.

28 And Thomas answered and said unto him, My LORD and my God.

29 Jesus saith unto him, Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed: blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed.

30 And many other signs truly did Jesus in the presence of his disciples, which are not written in this book:

31 But these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Early on the first day, while it is yet dark, Mary Magdalene finds the stone taken away and runs to Peter and the beloved disciple, who race to the tomb and find only linen clothes (vv. 1–10). Mary, weeping outside, turns and hears her name — “Mary” — and answers “Rabboni”; she is sent to tell the brethren he ascends to his Father and theirs (vv. 11–18). That evening he stands among the shut-in disciples with “Peace be unto you,” shows his hands and side, and breathes the Holy Ghost on them; a week later Thomas, who would not believe without touching, confesses “My Lord and my God” (vv. 19–29). John closes by saying these signs are written that readers may believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life through his name (vv. 30–31). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, John 20

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The first day of the week Mary Magdalene came early, when it was yet dark, unto the sepulchre, and saw the stone taken away from the sepulchre, for if Christ gave his life a ransom and had not taken it again, it would not have appeared that his giving was accepted as satisfaction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Then she ran, and came to Simon Peter, and to the other disciple, whom Jesus loved, and said, They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him, for it was a great trial to Mary that the body was gone, and weak believers often make that a complaint which is really ground of hope and joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Peter therefore went forth, and that other disciple, and came to the sepulchre, for it is well when those most honoured with disciples' privileges are most active in disciples' duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. So they ran both together, and the other disciple outran Peter, and came first to the sepulchre, for the disciple whom Jesus loved in a special manner, and who therefore in a special manner loved Jesus, was foremost, and the love of Christ makes us abound in every duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And he, stooping down and looking in, saw the linen clothes lying, yet went he not in, pausing at the threshold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Then Simon Peter, following him, went into the sepulchre, and saw the linen clothes lie, for he who was behind was Peter, who had denied Christ, and a sense of guilt hinders us in the service of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And the napkin, which was about his head, was not lying with the linen clothes, but wrapped together in a place by itself, an orderly sign that the body had not been stolen in haste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Then went in also that other disciple, who came first to the sepulchre, and he saw, and believed, for sight of the empty tomb, with the clothes in order, kindled faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. For as yet they knew not the Scripture, that he must rise again from the dead, for their faith ran ahead of their understanding of prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Then the disciples went away again unto their own home, leaving Mary still seeking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. But Mary stood without at the sepulchre weeping, and as she wept, she stooped down and looked into the sepulchre, for we are likely to seek and find when we seek with affection and seek in tears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And she saw two angels in white sitting, the one at the head, and the other at the feet, where the body of Jesus had lain, yet a sight of angels and their smiles will not suffice without a sight of Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And they said unto her, Woman, why weepest thou, and she said, Because they have taken away my Lord, and I know not where they have laid him, for many believers complain of clouds and darkness which are methods of grace for humbling their souls and endearing Christ to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And when she had said this, she turned herself back, and saw Jesus standing, and knew not that it was Jesus, for Christ, in manifesting himself to those who seek him, often outdoes their expectations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Jesus said unto her, Woman, why weepest thou, whom seekest thou, and she, supposing him to be the gardener, said, Sir, if thou have borne him hence, tell me where thou hast laid him, and I will take him away, for her heart was in earnest to find Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Jesus said unto her, Mary, and she turned herself, and said unto him, Rabboni, which is to say, Master, for Christ's way of making himself known to his people is by his word, applied to their souls, speaking to them in particular. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Jesus said unto her, Touch me not, for I am not yet ascended to my Father, but go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and your Father, and to my God, and your God, forbidding her to expect his bodily presence to continue as before, and teaching the relation to God from union with Christ, for Christ's Father is our Father, and our God is his God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Mary Magdalene came and told the disciples that she had seen the Lord, and that he had spoken these things unto her, for those who know the word of Christ should endeavour that others get good from their knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Then the same day at evening, being the first day of the week, when the doors were shut where the disciples were assembled for fear of the Jews, came Jesus and stood in the midst, and said unto them, Peace be unto you, for this day was evidently set apart as the Christian Sabbath, in remembrance of his resurrection, and no doors can shut out Christ's presence from his disciples' private assemblies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And when he had said this, he showed unto them his hands and his side, and then were the disciples glad, when they saw the Lord, for a sight of Christ will gladden the heart of a disciple at any time, and the more we see of Jesus, the more we shall rejoice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Then Jesus said to them again, Peace be unto you; as my Father has sent me, even so send I you, giving them their mission in his peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And when he had said this, he breathed on them, and said, Receive ye the Holy Ghost, showing that their spiritual life and all their ability for their work would be derived from him and depend upon him, for every word of Christ received by faith comes accompanied by this divine breathing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them, and whose soever sins ye retain, they are retained, for Christ directed the apostles to declare the only method by which sin would be forgiven, not as a power to give judgment, but to declare the character of those whom God would accept or reject. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. But Thomas, one of the twelve, called Didymus, was not with them when Jesus came, and absence from the assembly cost him the sight of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The other disciples therefore said unto him, We have seen the Lord, but he said, Except I shall see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger into the print of the nails, and thrust my hand into his side, I will not believe, for Thomas limited the Holy One of Israel, when he would be convinced by his own method or not at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And after eight days again his disciples were within, and Thomas with them, and then came Jesus, the doors being shut, and stood in the midst, and said, Peace be unto you, for that one day in seven should be religiously observed was pointed out by Christ's meeting his disciples on that day once and again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Then he said to Thomas, Reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands, and reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side, and be not faithless, but believing, for there is not an unbelieving word in our tongues, nor thought in our minds, but it is known to the Lord Jesus, and he was pleased to accommodate himself even to Thomas, rather than leave him in unbelief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And Thomas answered and said unto him, My Lord and my God, speaking with affection, as one who took hold of Christ with all his might, and ashamed of his unbelief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Jesus said unto him, Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed; blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed, for if we are faithless, we are Christless and graceless, hopeless and joyless, but sound and sincere believers, though slow and weak, shall be graciously accepted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And many other signs truly did Jesus in the presence of his disciples, which are not written in this book, for only a part of his works was recorded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. But these are written, that you might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you might have life through his name, for these were committed to writing that all might believe he was the promised Messiah, the Saviour of sinners, and the Son of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Investigation (editorial — a parallel examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a possible parallel. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

Mary at the tomb — John 20, Mark 16, and Luke 24

John 20:1–29, Mark 16:1–14, and Luke 24:1–12, 36–49 all report an empty tomb on the first day and appearances that convince slowly. John’s account is the most personal: Mary Magdalene comes alone in the dark, the beloved disciple outruns Peter and believes at the linen clothes, and Mary, mistaking Jesus for the gardener, knows him when he says her name and answers “Rabboni.” Mark’s women flee in fear and say nothing; Luke’s women report at once and are disbelieved, and Peter alone runs to see. The sources consulted for this page do not harmonize these first-morning reports, and this page does not claim a single order of events.
