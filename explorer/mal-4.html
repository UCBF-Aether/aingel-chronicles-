The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Malachi 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 For, behold, the day cometh, that shall burn as an oven; and all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly, shall be stubble: and the day that cometh shall burn them up, saith the LORD of hosts, that it shall leave them neither root nor branch.

2 But unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his wings; and ye shall go forth, and grow up as calves of the stall.

3 And ye shall tread down the wicked; for they shall be ashes under the soles of your feet in the day that I shall do this, saith the LORD of hosts.

4 Remember ye the law of Moses my servant, which I commanded unto him in Horeb for all Israel, with the statutes and judgments.

5 Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD:

6 And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

For behold, the day comes that will burn as an oven, and all the proud and all who do wickedly will be stubble, root and branch consumed, but to those who fear God's name the Sun of righteousness will arise with healing in his wings, and they will go forth and grow up as calves of the stall, treading down the wicked as ashes under their feet. The book of the prophets closes with two commands: remember the law of Moses my servant, commanded in Horeb for all Israel with statutes and judgments, and behold, Elijah the prophet will be sent before the great and dreadful day of the LORD, to turn the heart of the fathers to the children and the children to their fathers, lest God come and smite the earth with a curse. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. For, behold, the day cometh, that shall burn as an oven, he said, and all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly, shall be stubble, and the day that cometh shall burn them up, saith the LORD of hosts, that it shall leave them neither root nor branch, for this refers to the first and second coming of Christ, and God has fixed the day of both, and it is certainly to be applied to the day of judgment, when Christ shall be revealed in flaming fire to execute judgment on the proud and all that do wickedly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. But unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his wings, he said, and ye shall go forth, and grow up as calves of the stall, for by the Sun of Righteousness we understand Jesus Christ, through whom believers are justified and sanctified and brought to see light, and his influences render the sinner holy, joyful, and fruitful, and that day which to the wicked will burn as an oven, will to the righteous be bright as the morning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And ye shall tread down the wicked, he said, for they shall be ashes under the soles of your feet in the day that I shall do this, saith the LORD of hosts, for the saints' triumphs are all owing to God's victories, it is not they that do this, but God who does it for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Remember ye the law of Moses my servant, he said, which I commanded unto him in Horeb for all Israel, with the statutes and judgments, for this is a solemn conclusion, not only of this prophecy, but of the Old Testament, and conscience bids us remember the law, and though we have not prophets, yet as long as we have Bibles, we may keep up communion with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD, he said, for they must keep up a believing expectation of the gospel of Christ, and look for the beginning of it, and John the Baptist preached repentance and reformation, as Elijah had done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, he said, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse, for the turning of souls to God and their duty is the best preparation for the great and dreadful day, and John shall preach a doctrine that reaches men's hearts and works a change in them, preparing the way for the kingdom of heaven, while those who turn not to him who smites them with a rod must expect to be smitten with a curse, and none can escape the curse of God's broken law unless their hearts are turned from sin and the world, to Christ and holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • The day of the LORD. the day of God's visitation and judgment, pictured in the prophets as darkness, trumpet alarm, and distress, and also as deliverance for those who call on his name. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Sun of righteousness. Malachi's closing image for those who fear God's name: a rising sun with healing in its wings, set against a day burning as an oven for the proud. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Elijah. the prophet Malachi promises God will send before the great and dreadful day of the LORD, to turn the hearts of fathers to children and children to fathers. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
