Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Canon 30, the book's closing order of the priesthood, argues from the distinctness of orders: bishops, presbyters and deacons are appointed by prayer and laying on of hand, each with his own function, so that deacons may not offer, baptize or give the eulogia, and a presbyter may not ordain, for God is not the author of confusion. Christ is named the first priest and chief of the priesthood, Moses is recalled as appointing chief priests, priests and Levites, the apostles are said to have appointed Clement and James, and Stephen the first martyr, Philip the deacon and Ananias are held up as men who kept their order. The rest of the chapter is a long book of supplications, each introduced the same way and each ending in the same doxology, for the morning, the sick, travellers, rains, the fruit of the earth, the river waters, the king Iyasu, those bringing the entrance-offering, the catechumens, the departed — including the bishop Abba Egale, named in prayer — peace, the congregation, and the bishop himself.
