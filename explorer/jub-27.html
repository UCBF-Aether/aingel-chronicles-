The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 27 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And the words of Esau, her elder son, were told to Rebecca in a dream, and Rebecca sent and called Jacob her younger son, and said unto him : " Behold Esau thy brother will take vengeance on thee so as to kill thee.

2 Now, therefore, my son, obey my voice, and arise and flee thou to Laban, my brother, to Haran, and tarry with him a few days until thy brother's anger turneth away, and he remove his anger from thee, and forget all that thou hast done ; then I will send and fetch thee from thence." 4.

3 And Jacob said: " I am not afraid; if he wisheth to kill me, I will kill him." 5.

4 But she said unto him : " Let me not be bereft of both my sons on one day." 6.

5 And Jacob said to Rebecca his mother : " Behold, thou knowest that my father has become old, and does not see because his eyes are dull, and if I leave him it will be evil in his eyes, because I leave him and go away from you, and my father will be angry, and will curse me. I will not go ; when he sends me, then only will I go." And Rebecca said to Jacob : " I will go in and speak to him, and he will send thee away." And Rebecca went in and said to Isaac : " I loathe my life because of the two daughters of Heth, whom Esau has taken him as wives ; and if Jacob take a wife from among the daughters of the land such as these, for what purpose

6 do I further live ; for the daughters of Canaan are

7 evil."

8 And Isaac called Jacob and blessed him,

9 and admonished him and said unto him : " Do

10 not take thee a wife of any of the daughters of Canaan ;

11 arise and go to Mesopotamia, to the house of Bethuel,

12 thy mother's father, and take thee a wife from thence

13 of the daughters of Laban, thy mother's brother.

14 And God Almighty bless thee and increase and

15 multiply thee that thou mayest become a company

16 of nations, and give thee the blessings of my father

17 Abraham, to thee and to thy seed after thee, that

18 thou mayest inherit the land of thy sojournings and

19 all the land which God gave to Abraham : go, my

20 son, in peace." 12.

21 And Isaac sent Jacob away, and

22 he went to Mesopotamia, to Laban the son of Bethuel

23 the Syrian, the brother of Rebecca, Jacob's mother.

24 And it came to pass after Jacob had arisen to

25 go to Mesopotamia that the spirit of Rebecca was

26 grieved after her son, and she wept.

27 And Isaac

28 said to Rebecca : " My sister weep not on account

29 of Jacob, my son ; for he goeth in peace, and in peace

30 will he return.

31 The Most High God will preserve

32 him from all evil, and will be with him; for He will

33 not forsake him all his days; For I know that

34 his ways will be prospered in all things wherever he

35 goeth, until he return in peace to us, and we see him

36 in peace.

37 Fear not on his account, my sister,

38 for he is on the upright path and he is a perfect man :

39 and he is faithful and will not perish.

40 Weep not."

41 And Isaac comforted Rebecca on account of her

42 son Jacob, and blessed him.

43 And Jacob went

44 from the Well of the Oath to go to Haran on the

45 first year of the second week in the forty-fourth

46 jubilee, and he came to Luz on the mountains, that is,

47 Bethel, on the new moon of the first month of this

48 week, and he came to the place at even and turned from the way to the west of the road that night : and he slept there ; for the sun had set.

49 And he took one of the stones of that place and laid it (at his head) under the tree and he was journeying alone, and he slept.

50 And he dreamt that night, and behold a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven, and behold, the angels of the Lord ascended and descended on it : and behold, the Lord stood upon it.

51 And He spake to Jacob and said : "I am the Lord God of Abraham, thy father, and the God of Isaac ; the land whereon thou art sleeping, to thee shall I give it, and to thy seed after thee.

52 And thy seed will be as the dust of the earth, and thou wilt increase to the west and to the east, to the north and the south, and in thee and in thy seed will all the families of the nations be blessed.

53 And behold, I shall be with thee, and shall keep thee whithersoever thou goest, and I shall bring thee again into this land in peace; for I shall not leave thee until I do everything that I told thee of."

54 And Jacob awoke from his sleep, and said, " Truly this place is the house of the Lord, and I knew it not."

55 And he was afraid and said : " Dreadful is this place which is none other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven." 26.

56 And Jacob arose early in the morning, and took the stone which he had put under his head and set it up as a pillar for a sign, and he poured oil upon the top of it.

57 And he called the name of that place Bethel; but the name of the place was Luz at the first.

58 And Jacob vowed a vow unto the Lord, saying : " If the Lord will be with me, and will keep me in this way that I go, and give me bread to eat and raiment to put on, so that I come again to my father's house in peace, then shall the Lord be my God, and this stone which I have set up as a pillar for a sign in this place, shall be the Lord's house, and of all that thou givest me, I shall give the tenth to thee, my God."

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: Esau's murderous words were told to Rebecca, and she sent for Jacob to warn him. Jacob vowed that if the Lord would be with him, keep him on his way, and give him bread and clothing, so that he returned in peace, then the Lord would be his God, the stone would be God's house, and he would give a tenth of all. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. Esau's murderous words were told to Rebecca, and she sent for Jacob to warn him. Henry notes that Rebekah warned Jacob of his danger and advised him to withdraw for his safety, rather than presuming on his wisdom or resolution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Rebecca told Jacob to flee to her brother Laban in Haran and stay only a few days, until Esau's anger turned away and he forgot what Jacob had done. Henry stresses that care must be taken to keep even hopeful children out of the way of evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Rebecca answered that she must not be bereft of both her sons on one day. The danger was double: Esau might kill Jacob, or Jacob might kill Esau in return, and either loss would leave her childless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Jacob objected that his aged, dim-eyed father would take it badly if he left, fearing his father's anger and curse; Rebecca then said she loathed her life because of the daughters of Heth whom Esau had married. Henry observes that Jacob, though blessed, would still smart for the indirect course taken to obtain the blessing, and that the Heth marriages gave a lawful reason to send him away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Rebecca closed her plea by saying the daughters of Canaan are evil, and that Jacob must not take such a wife either. Henry's rule is plain: those who profess religion should not marry with those that care not for religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Isaac now enters the scene, called in by Rebecca's plea. What follows is his deliberate blessing and charge, which Henry contrasts with the former blessing given unwittingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Isaac called Jacob and blessed him, this time designedly. Henry notes that this blessing is more full than the former, a gospel blessing looking as high as heaven, of which Canaan was a type. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Isaac admonished Jacob with a solemn charge before blessing him. Henry notes that Jacob was dismissed by his father with a solemn charge and a solemn blessing together, discipline and promise in one farewell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jacob was charged not to take a wife from the daughters of Canaan. Henry's rule is plain: those who profess religion should not marry with those that care not for religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Jacob was told to arise and go to Mesopotamia, to the house of Bethuel his mother's father. The journey was both a flight from Esau and an obedient errand to find a wife among his own kin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. He was to take a wife from the house of Bethuel, his mother's father, the kin from which Abraham's servant had once brought Rebecca herself. The errand kept the covenant line within the family of faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The wife was to come from the daughters of Laban, his mother's brother. Laban's household in Haran was the place appointed for Jacob's exile and his marriages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Isaac's blessing began with the name God Almighty, praying that he would bless Jacob and increase him. Henry notes that Jacob had blessings promised both as to this world and that which is to come, yet went out to a hard service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The blessing prayed that Jacob might be multiplied until he became a company of nations. One man was to become a multitude, the covenant promise of Abraham now deliberately laid on Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Isaac prayed that God would give Jacob the blessings of his father Abraham. Henry calls this blessing more full than the former one, because it names the covenant itself, not only earthly plenty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The blessings of Abraham were given to Jacob and to his seed after him, passing the covenant to the next generation. The promise was not for Jacob alone but for the nation that would come from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The blessing was given so that Jacob might inherit the land of his sojournings. The land was still only a promise, but Henry notes it was the better country the patriarchs had in view. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Isaac named the whole land which God gave to Abraham as Jacob's inheritance, and sent him on his way. The gift of the land and the command to go belonged together: the heir must leave home to claim it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Isaac's charge closed with the words, go, my son, in peace. The peace wished for him was the protection of God on a road that would otherwise be full of danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Isaac sent Jacob away, an orderly and blessed dismissal, unlike the secret flight Rebecca had first planned. The father's sending made Jacob's exile an act of obedience, not of mere escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Jacob went to Mesopotamia, to Laban the son of Bethuel. The long road to Haran began here, and with it the hard service that would correct Jacob for the fraud on his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Laban is named as the Syrian, the brother of Rebecca, Jacob's mother. The kinship mattered: Jacob travelled to his mother's family, where he would find both wives and affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Isaac now speaks to comfort Rebecca, turning from the son who has gone to the mother who grieves. His comfort rests on God's promise, which Henry states plainly: God promises to keep Jacob and bring him back again to this land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Isaac told Rebecca not to weep on account of Jacob, calling her his sister in the old familiar style. The comfort began by naming the grief before answering it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Isaac assured her that Jacob went in peace, and in peace he would return. Henry's comment on God's promise to Jacob fits Isaac's confidence: God had promised to keep him wherever he went and to bring him back again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The promise of return turns the farewell from a final parting into a sojourn. Jacob's exile had an appointed end, fixed by God's word rather than by Esau's anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Isaac appealed to the Most High God, who would preserve Jacob. The title stresses the power that guards the traveller, higher than any brother's hatred or any foreign danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. God would preserve Jacob from all evil and be with him, the same promise God himself would give at Bethel. Henry notes that God visits his people with comfort precisely when they are most destitute of other comforts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. God would not forsake Jacob all his days, and Isaac said he knew Jacob's ways would be prospered. Henry stresses that whom God loves, he never leaves, until his last promise is accomplished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Isaac's confidence extended to all Jacob's ways, prospered in all things wherever he went. The blessing was not limited to the road to Haran but covered the whole of Jacob's life abroad. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Jacob would return in peace, and his parents would see him in peace. The return was pictured as a reunion, not merely a safe arrival in the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. The vision of seeing Jacob again in peace closes Isaac's assurance. Peace at departure and peace at return frame the whole exile under God's keeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Isaac told Rebecca not to fear on Jacob's account, addressing her again as his sister. Fear was answered not by denying the danger but by pointing to the God who kept Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Isaac described Jacob as a man on the upright path, a perfect man. The description looks to Jacob's standing before God rather than to his past craft, which Henry says the hard service ahead would correct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Jacob was faithful and would not perish, Isaac said. Faithfulness here is trust in God's promise, the same faith Henry finds in Jacob's later prayer and wrestling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Isaac's comfort closed with the words, weep not. The repetition of the comfort shows a father's care to leave the mother at peace when the son had gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Isaac comforted Rebecca concerning her son Jacob, turning her grief into hope grounded on God's promise. The comfort was not empty cheer but a rehearsal of what God had pledged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Isaac comforted Rebecca and blessed Jacob once more. The second blessing sealed the first: Jacob left not as a fugitive only, but as a son sent with his father's blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Jacob went on his way, the journey itself now beginning after the farewells. From this point the narrative follows the traveller, not the household he left. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Jacob went from the Well of the Oath to go to Haran, leaving the home of his father for his mother's kin. The Well of the Oath was the home he left behind, and Haran the place of his exile and marriages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. The journey is dated to the first year of the second week of the forty-fourth jubilee, in Jubilees' chronology of weeks and jubilees. The careful dating marks Jacob's departure as a fixed point in the book's ordered history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Jacob came to Luz on the mountains, the place later called Bethel. Henry notes that Jacob, obliged to flee and in trouble, looked only to God to make him dwell in safety, and could sleep in the open air with his head on a stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. The place was Bethel, reached on the new moon of the first month of that week. Jubilees dates the arrival precisely, while Henry stresses what happened there: God manifested himself to Jacob when he was asleep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Jacob came to the place at evening, turned from the way, and slept there because the sun had set. Henry observes that any true believer would be willing to take up with Jacob's pillow, provided he might have Jacob's vision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. Jacob took a stone of that place, laid it at his head under the tree, and slept while journeying alone. His destitution was complete, which Henry says is God's time to visit his people with comforts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Jacob dreamt of a ladder set up on the earth reaching to heaven, with the angels of God ascending and descending on it, and the Lord standing upon it. Henry reads the ladder as God's providence keeping constant intercourse between heaven and earth, and as the mediation of Christ, the Way by which all God's favours come to us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. The Lord spoke to Jacob as the God of Abraham and Isaac, giving him the land where he slept, to him and his seed. Henry notes that God spake comfortably to Jacob from the head of the ladder, and that the Messiah should come from Jacob, the great blessing of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Jacob's seed would be as the dust of the earth, spreading west, east, north, and south, and all families of nations would be blessed in him and his seed. Henry stresses that none of any family are shut out from blessedness in Christ except those who shut out themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. God promised to be with Jacob, keep him wherever he went, and bring him back into this land in peace, never leaving him until all was done. Henry observes that Jacob had a long journey to an unknown country, but God promised to keep him and bring him back again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. Jacob awoke and said truly this place was the house of the Lord, though he had not known it. Henry notes that God manifested himself to Jacob while he slept, and that grace, like the dew, tarries not for the sons of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. Jacob was afraid and said the place was dreadful, none other than the house of God and the gate of heaven. Henry remarks that the more we see of God, the more cause we see for holy trembling before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Jacob rose early, took the stone from under his head, set it up as a pillar for a sign, and poured oil on top of it. The pillar marked the place of the vision, so that the spot would be remembered as holy ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 57. Jacob called the place Bethel, though its first name was Luz. The new name, house of God, recorded what had happened there rather than what the town had been called before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 58. Jacob vowed that if the Lord would be with him, keep him on his way, and give him bread and clothing, so that he returned in peace, then the Lord would be his God, the stone would be God's house, and he would give a tenth of all. Henry praises Jacob's moderation in his desires and his piety: he asked not for soft clothing and dainty meat, but for God's presence, and the tenth is a fit proportion to devote to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 45. The journey is dated to the first year of the second week of the forty-fourth jubilee, in Jubilees' chronology of weeks and jubilees. The careful dating marks Jacob's departure as a fixed point in the book's ordered history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Jacob came to Luz on the mountains, the place later called Bethel. Henry notes that Jacob, obliged to flee and in trouble, looked only to God to make him dwell in safety, and could sleep in the open air with his head on a stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. The place was Bethel, reached on the new moon of the first month of that week. Jubilees dates the arrival precisely, while Henry stresses what happened there: God manifested himself to Jacob when he was asleep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Jacob came to the place at evening, turned from the way, and slept there because the sun had set. Henry observes that any true believer would be willing to take up with Jacob's pillow, provided he might have Jacob's vision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. Jacob took a stone of that place, laid it at his head under the tree, and slept while journeying alone. His destitution was complete, which Henry says is God's time to visit his people with comforts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Jacob dreamt of a ladder set up on the earth reaching to heaven, with the angels of God ascending and descending on it, and the Lord standing upon it. Henry reads the ladder as God's providence keeping constant intercourse between heaven and earth, and as the mediation of Christ, the Way by which all God's favours come to us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. The Lord spoke to Jacob as the God of Abraham and Isaac, giving him the land where he slept, to him and his seed. Henry notes that God spake comfortably to Jacob from the head of the ladder, and that the Messiah should come from Jacob, the great blessing of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Jacob's seed would be as the dust of the earth, spreading west, east, north, and south, and all families of nations would be blessed in him and his seed. Henry stresses that none of any family are shut out from blessedness in Christ except those who shut out themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. God promised to be with Jacob, keep him wherever he went, and bring him back into this land in peace, never leaving him until all was done. Henry observes that Jacob had a long journey to an unknown country, but God promised to keep him and bring him back again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. Jacob awoke and said truly this place was the house of the Lord, though he had not known it. Henry notes that God manifested himself to Jacob while he slept, and that grace, like the dew, tarries not for the sons of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. Jacob was afraid and said the place was dreadful, none other than the house of God and the gate of heaven. Henry remarks that the more we see of God, the more cause we see for holy trembling before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Jacob rose early, took the stone from under his head, set it up as a pillar for a sign, and poured oil on top of it. The pillar marked the place of the vision, so that the spot would be remembered as holy ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 57. Jacob called the place Bethel, though its first name was Luz. The new name, house of God, recorded what had happened there rather than what the town had been called before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 58. Jacob vowed that if the Lord would be with him, keep him on his way, and give him bread and clothing, so that he returned in peace, then the Lord would be his God, the stone would be God's house, and he would give a tenth of all. Henry praises Jacob's moderation in his desires and his piety: he asked not for soft clothing and dainty meat, but for God's presence, and the tenth is a fit proportion to devote to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
