The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Daniel 11 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–12; King James Version, Apocrypha for the additions)

1 Also I in the first year of Darius the Mede, even I, stood to confirm and to strengthen him.

2 And now will I shew thee the truth. Behold, there shall stand up yet three kings in Persia; and the fourth shall be far richer than they all: and by his strength through his riches he shall stir up all against the realm of Grecia.

3 And a mighty king shall stand up, that shall rule with great dominion, and do according to his will.

4 And when he shall stand up, his kingdom shall be broken, and shall be divided toward the four winds of heaven; and not to his posterity, nor according to his dominion which he ruled: for his kingdom shall be plucked up, even for others beside those.

5 And the king of the south shall be strong, and one of his princes; and he shall be strong above him, and have dominion; his dominion shall be a great dominion.

6 And in the end of years they shall join themselves together; for the king’s daughter of the south shall come to the king of the north to make an agreement: but she shall not retain the power of the arm; neither shall he stand, nor his arm: but she shall be given up, and they that brought her, and he that begat her, and he that strengthened her in these times.

7 But out of a branch of her roots shall one stand up in his estate, which shall come with an army, and shall enter into the fortress of the king of the north, and shall deal against them, and shall prevail:

8 And shall also carry captives into Egypt their gods, with their princes, and with their precious vessels of silver and of gold; and he shall continue more years than the king of the north.

9 So the king of the south shall come into his kingdom, and shall return into his own land.

10 But his sons shall be stirred up, and shall assemble a multitude of great forces: and one shall certainly come, and overflow, and pass through: then shall he return, and be stirred up, even to his fortress.

11 And the king of the south shall be moved with choler, and shall come forth and fight with him, even with the king of the north: and he shall set forth a great multitude; but the multitude shall be given into his hand.

12 And when he hath taken away the multitude, his heart shall be lifted up; and he shall cast down many ten thousands: but he shall not be strengthened by it.

13 For the king of the north shall return, and shall set forth a multitude greater than the former, and shall certainly come after certain years with a great army and with much riches.

14 And in those times there shall many stand up against the king of the south: also the robbers of thy people shall exalt themselves to establish the vision; but they shall fall.

15 So the king of the north shall come, and cast up a mount, and take the most fenced cities: and the arms of the south shall not withstand, neither his chosen people, neither shall there be any strength to withstand.

16 But he that cometh against him shall do according to his own will, and none shall stand before him: and he shall stand in the glorious land, which by his hand shall be consumed.

17 He shall also set his face to enter with the strength of his whole kingdom, and upright ones with him; thus shall he do: and he shall give him the daughter of women, corrupting her: but she shall not stand on his side, neither be for him.

18 After this shall he turn his face unto the isles, and shall take many: but a prince for his own behalf shall cause the reproach offered by him to cease; without his own reproach he shall cause it to turn upon him.

19 Then he shall turn his face toward the fort of his own land: but he shall stumble and fall, and not be found.

20 Then shall stand up in his estate a raiser of taxes in the glory of the kingdom: but within few days he shall be destroyed, neither in anger, nor in battle.

21 And in his estate shall stand up a vile person, to whom they shall not give the honour of the kingdom: but he shall come in peaceably, and obtain the kingdom by flatteries.

22 And with the arms of a flood shall they be overflown from before him, and shall be broken; yea, also the prince of the covenant.

23 And after the league made with him he shall work deceitfully: for he shall come up, and shall become strong with a small people.

24 He shall enter peaceably even upon the fattest places of the province; and he shall do that which his fathers have not done, nor his fathers’ fathers; he shall scatter among them the prey, and spoil, and riches: yea, and he shall forecast his devices against the strong holds, even for a time.

25 And he shall stir up his power and his courage against the king of the south with a great army; and the king of the south shall be stirred up to battle with a very great and mighty army; but he shall not stand: for they shall forecast devices against him.

26 Yea, they that feed of the portion of his meat shall destroy him, and his army shall overflow: and many shall fall down slain.

27 And both of these kings’ hearts shall be to do mischief, and they shall speak lies at one table; but it shall not prosper: for yet the end shall be at the time appointed.

28 Then shall he return into his land with great riches; and his heart shall be against the holy covenant; and he shall do exploits, and return to his own land.

29 At the time appointed he shall return, and come toward the south; but it shall not be as the former, or as the latter.

30 For the ships of Chittim shall come against him: therefore he shall be grieved, and return, and have indignation against the holy covenant: so shall he do; he shall even return, and have intelligence with them that forsake the holy covenant.

31 And arms shall stand on his part, and they shall pollute the sanctuary of strength, and shall take away the daily sacrifice, and they shall place the abomination that maketh desolate.

32 And such as do wickedly against the covenant shall he corrupt by flatteries: but the people that do know their God shall be strong, and do exploits.

33 And they that understand among the people shall instruct many: yet they shall fall by the sword, and by flame, by captivity, and by spoil, many days.

34 Now when they shall fall, they shall be holpen with a little help: but many shall cleave to them with flatteries.

35 And some of them of understanding shall fall, to try them, and to purge, and to make them white, even to the time of the end: because it is yet for a time appointed.

36 And the king shall do according to his will; and he shall exalt himself, and magnify himself above every god, and shall speak marvellous things against the God of gods, and shall prosper till the indignation be accomplished: for that that is determined shall be done.

37 Neither shall he regard the God of his fathers, nor the desire of women, nor regard any god: for he shall magnify himself above all.

38 But in his estate shall he honour the God of forces: and a god whom his fathers knew not shall he honour with gold, and silver, and with precious stones, and pleasant things.

39 Thus shall he do in the most strong holds with a strange god, whom he shall acknowledge and increase with glory: and he shall cause them to rule over many, and shall divide the land for gain.

40 And at the time of the end shall the king of the south push at him: and the king of the north shall come against him like a whirlwind, with chariots, and with horsemen, and with many ships; and he shall enter into the countries, and shall overflow and pass over.

41 He shall enter also into the glorious land, and many countries shall be overthrown: but these shall escape out of his hand, even Edom, and Moab, and the chief of the children of Ammon.

42 He shall stretch forth his hand also upon the countries: and the land of Egypt shall not escape.

43 But he shall have power over the treasures of gold and of silver, and over all the precious things of Egypt: and the Libyans and the Ethiopians shall be at his steps.

44 But tidings out of the east and out of the north shall trouble him: therefore he shall go forth with great fury to destroy, and utterly to make away many.

45 And he shall plant the tabernacles of his palace between the seas in the glorious holy mountain; yet he shall come to his end, and none shall help him.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The messenger’s long prophecy runs through the kings that follow: three kings of Persia, a mighty king of Grecia whose kingdom is divided to the four winds, and then the long wars of the king of the north and the king of the south, with shifting alliances, betrayals, and desecrations, down to a contemptible king who exalts himself, pollutes the sanctuary, and at the end plants his palace between the seas and the glorious holy mountain — yet comes to his end, and none helps him. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Also I in the first year of Darius the Mede, even I, stood to confirm and to strengthen him. The angel who speaks this vision, Henry's outline shows, is the same messenger who stood to confirm Darius, linking the vision of the Scriptures of truth to God's providential care of kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. And now will I show thee the truth. Behold, there shall stand up yet three kings in Persia, and the fourth shall be far richer than they all, and by his strength through his riches he shall stir up all against the realm of Grecia. The angel shows Daniel the succession of the Persian and Grecian empires, Henry says, and all changes and revolutions of states are plainly and perfectly foreseen by God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. And a mighty king shall stand up, that shall rule with great dominion, and do according to his will. The mighty king of Grecia, Henry's outline shows, follows the Persian succession, and God in his providence sets up one and pulls down another as he pleases. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. And when he shall stand up, his kingdom shall be broken, and shall be divided toward the four winds of heaven, and not to his posterity, nor according to his dominion which he ruled, for his kingdom shall be plucked up, even for others beside those. See what decaying, perishing things worldly pomp and possessions are, Henry says, and the power by which they are gotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. And the king of the south shall be strong, and one of his princes, and he shall be strong above him, and have dominion; his dominion shall be a great dominion. The kings of Egypt and Syria are noticed here, Henry explains, for Judea was between their dominions and affected by their contests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. And in the end of years they shall join themselves together, for the king's daughter of the south shall come to the king of the north to make an agreement, but she shall not retain the power of the arm, neither shall he stand, nor his arm, but she shall be given up, and they that brought her, and he that begat her, and he that strengthened her in these times. This world is full of wars and fightings, Henry says, which come from men's lusts, and alliances made by marriage fail like the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. But out of a branch of her roots shall one stand up in his estate, which shall come with an army, and shall enter into the fortress of the king of the north, and shall deal against them, and shall prevail. The contests of south and north continue, Henry's outline shows, with Judea caught between their dominions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. And shall also carry captives into Egypt their gods, with their princes, and with their precious vessels of silver and of gold, and he shall continue more years than the king of the north. The spoils carried into Egypt, Henry's reading implies, mark the south's turn of victory in the long contest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. So the king of the south shall come into his kingdom, and shall return into his own land. The ebb and flow of these wars, Henry says, shows the potsherds of the earth striving with each other, prevailing and being prevailed against. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. But his sons shall be stirred up, and shall assemble a multitude of great forces, and one shall certainly come, and overflow, and pass through, then shall he return, and be stirred up, even to his fortress. The sons of the north gather great forces, Henry's outline shows, and the contest renews in the next generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. And the king of the south shall be moved with choler, and shall come forth and fight with him, even with the king of the north, and he shall set forth a great multitude, but the multitude shall be given into his hand. No word of God shall fall to the ground, Henry stresses; what he has designed and declared shall infallibly come to pass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. And when he has taken away the multitude, his heart shall be lifted up, and he shall cast down many ten thousands, but he shall not be strengthened by it. Victory lifts the heart, Henry's account warns, yet does not strengthen the victor, for worldly pomp is a decaying thing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. For the king of the north shall return, and shall set forth a multitude greater than the former, and shall certainly come after certain years with a great army and with much riches. The north returns with a greater multitude, Henry's outline shows, and the wars of the kings continue at the times appointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. And in those times there shall many stand up against the king of the south; also the robbers of thy people shall exalt themselves to establish the vision, but they shall fall. Even the robbers of Daniel's people, Henry's reading implies, are caught up in these foreseen revolutions, and fall in their turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. So the king of the north shall come, and cast up a mount, and take the most fenced cities, and the arms of the south shall not withstand, neither his chosen people, neither shall there be any strength to withstand. The south's arms fail before the north, Henry's outline shows, as God foresees every turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. But he that comes against him shall do according to his own will, and none shall stand before him, and he shall stand in the glorious land, which by his hand shall be consumed. The northern conqueror stands in the glorious land, Henry notes, and Judea, between the dominions, is affected by the contest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. He shall also set his face to enter with the strength of his whole kingdom, and upright ones with him; thus shall he do, and he shall give him the daughter of women, corrupting her, but she shall not stand on his side, neither be for him. The northern king's designs, Henry's reading shows, include a corrupting marriage alliance that fails, as the potsherds deceive and are deceived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. After this shall he turn his face unto the isles, and shall take many, but a prince for his own behalf shall cause the reproach offered by him to cease, without his own reproach he shall cause it to turn upon him. The turn to the isles meets a prince who turns the reproach back, Henry's outline shows, and the conqueror's advance is checked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Then he shall turn his face toward the fort of his own land, but he shall stumble and fall, and not be found. The conqueror stumbles and falls and is not found, Henry's account shows: God sets up one and pulls down another as he pleases. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Then shall stand up in his estate a raiser of taxes in the glory of the kingdom, but within few days he shall be destroyed, neither in anger, nor in battle. The tax-raiser's brief reign and quiet destruction, Henry's outline shows, are one more foreseen turn of the succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. And in his estate shall stand up a vile person, to whom they shall not give the honour of the kingdom, but he shall come in peaceably, and obtain the kingdom by flatteries. From this verse, Henry says, the passage is generally considered to relate to Antiochus Epiphanes, a cruel and violent persecutor of the Jews, who comes in peaceably and obtains the kingdom by flatteries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. And with the arms of a flood shall they be overflown from before him, and shall be broken, yea, also the prince of the covenant. The vile person's flood overwhelms opposition, Henry's reading shows, and even the prince of the covenant is broken before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. And after the league made with him he shall work deceitfully, for he shall come up, and shall become strong with a small people. After the league he works deceitfully, Henry notes, becoming strong with a small people, the way of the flattering usurper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. He shall enter peaceably even upon the fattest places of the province, and he shall do that which his fathers have not done, nor his fathers' fathers; he shall scatter among them the prey, and spoil, and riches, yea, and he shall forecast his devices against the strong holds, even for a time. His scattering of spoil, Henry's account shows, buys the loyalty his fathers never won. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. And he shall stir up his power and his courage against the king of the south with a great army, and the king of the south shall be stirred up to battle with a very great and mighty army, but he shall not stand, for they shall forecast devices against him. The war against the south, Henry's outline shows, renews the old Egypt-Syria contest under the persecutor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Yea, they that feed of the portion of his meat shall destroy him, and his army shall overflow, and many shall fall down slain. The southern king is destroyed by his own household, Henry's reading shows, as deceit answers deceit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. And both of these kings' hearts shall be to do mischief, and they shall speak lies at one table, but it shall not prosper, for yet the end shall be at the time appointed. Both kings speak lies at one table, Henry notes, but it shall not prosper, for the end is at the time appointed, not at man's plotting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Then shall he return into his land with great riches, and his heart shall be against the holy covenant, and he shall do exploits, and return to his own land. His heart against the holy covenant, Henry's reading shows, turns the persecutor from foreign war to war on God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. At the time appointed he shall return, and come toward the south, but it shall not be as the former, or as the latter. The return at the time appointed differs from the former campaigns, Henry's outline shows, and the appointed time governs all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. For the ships of Chittim shall come against him, therefore he shall be grieved, and return, and have indignation against the holy covenant; so shall he do, he shall even return, and have intelligence with them that forsake the holy covenant. Checked by the ships of Chittim, Henry's reading shows, he turns his indignation against the holy covenant and leagues with those who forsake it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. And arms shall stand on his part, and they shall pollute the sanctuary of strength, and shall take away the daily sacrifice, and they shall place the abomination that makes desolate. The remainder of this prophecy is very difficult, Henry cautions, and commentators differ much respecting it; from Antiochus the account seems to pass to antichrist, with reference to the Roman empire in its pagan, early Christian, and papal states. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. And such as do wickedly against the covenant shall he corrupt by flatteries, but the people that do know their God shall be strong, and do exploits. Those who know God will trust in him, Henry says, and he will enable them to stand their ground, bear their cross, and maintain their conflict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. And they that understand among the people shall instruct many, yet they shall fall by the sword, and by flame, by captivity, and by spoil, many days. The instructing faithful fall by sword, flame, captivity, and spoil, Henry's reading shows, yet their instruction of many stands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. Now when they shall fall, they shall be helped with a little help, but many shall cleave to them with flatteries. The little help in falling, Henry implies, is God's measured aid, while flatterers cleave for their own ends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. And some of them of understanding shall fall, to try them, and to purge, and to make them white, even to the time of the end, because it is yet for a time appointed. The falling of the understanding tries, purges, and makes them white, Henry's reading shows, until the time of the end, which is yet for a time appointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. And the king shall do according to his will, and he shall exalt himself, and magnify himself above every god, and shall speak marvellous things against the God of gods, and shall prosper till the indignation be accomplished, for that that is determined shall be done. The self-exalting king prospers only till the indignation is accomplished, Henry notes, for what is determined shall be done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. Neither shall he regard the God of his fathers, nor the desire of women, nor regard any god, for he shall magnify himself above all. He regards no god, Henry's reading shows, magnifying himself above all, the mark of the antichristian power to which the account seems to pass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. But in his estate shall he honour the God of forces, and a god whom his fathers knew not shall he honour with gold, and silver, and with precious stones, and pleasant things. The god of forces, honoured with gold and precious stones, Henry's reading shows, is the strange god of the self-exalting power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. Thus shall he do in the most strong holds with a strange god, whom he shall acknowledge and increase with glory, and he shall cause them to rule over many, and shall divide the land for gain. The strong holds held with a strange god, Henry notes, divide the land for gain, and the end of the Lord's patience towards his enemies approaches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. And at the time of the end shall the king of the south push at him, and the king of the north shall come against him like a whirlwind, with chariots, and with horsemen, and with many ships, and he shall enter into the countries, and shall overflow and pass over. At the time of the end the kings of south and north assail him like a whirlwind, Henry's outline shows, yet the appointed end governs the storm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 41. He shall enter also into the glorious land, and many countries shall be overthrown, but these shall escape out of his hand, even Edom, and Moab, and the chief of the children of Ammon. He enters the glorious land and overthrows many, Henry's reading shows, yet some escape his hand, as God limits the destroyer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 42. He shall stretch forth his hand also upon the countries, and the land of Egypt shall not escape. Egypt itself does not escape his hand, Henry's outline shows, in the final overrunning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 43. But he shall have power over the treasures of gold and of silver, and over all the precious things of Egypt, and the Libyans and the Ethiopians shall be at his steps. The treasures of Egypt and the peoples at his steps, Henry's reading shows, mark the height of his power before its end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 44. But tidings out of the east and out of the north shall trouble him, therefore he shall go forth with great fury to destroy, and utterly to make away many. Tidings from east and north trouble him, Henry notes, and he goes forth in fury to destroy many, hastening his own end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 45. And he shall plant the tabernacles of his palace between the seas in the glorious holy mountain, yet he shall come to his end, and none shall help him. He plants his palace between the seas in the glorious holy mountain, Henry's reading shows, yet he shall come to his end, and none shall help him; if we would escape the ruin of the infidel, the idolater, and the cruel persecutor, let us make the oracles of God our standard of truth and duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 12. And when he has taken away the multitude, his heart shall be lifted up, and he shall cast down many ten thousands, but he shall not be strengthened by it. Victory lifts the heart, Henry's account warns, yet does not strengthen the victor, for worldly pomp is a decaying thing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. For the king of the north shall return, and shall set forth a multitude greater than the former, and shall certainly come after certain years with a great army and with much riches. The north returns with a greater multitude, Henry's outline shows, and the wars of the kings continue at the times appointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. And in those times there shall many stand up against the king of the south; also the robbers of thy people shall exalt themselves to establish the vision, but they shall fall. Even the robbers of Daniel's people, Henry's reading implies, are caught up in these foreseen revolutions, and fall in their turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. So the king of the north shall come, and cast up a mount, and take the most fenced cities, and the arms of the south shall not withstand, neither his chosen people, neither shall there be any strength to withstand. The south's arms fail before the north, Henry's outline shows, as God foresees every turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. But he that comes against him shall do according to his own will, and none shall stand before him, and he shall stand in the glorious land, which by his hand shall be consumed. The northern conqueror stands in the glorious land, Henry notes, and Judea, between the dominions, is affected by the contest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. He shall also set his face to enter with the strength of his whole kingdom, and upright ones with him; thus shall he do, and he shall give him the daughter of women, corrupting her, but she shall not stand on his side, neither be for him. The northern king's designs, Henry's reading shows, include a corrupting marriage alliance that fails, as the potsherds deceive and are deceived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. After this shall he turn his face unto the isles, and shall take many, but a prince for his own behalf shall cause the reproach offered by him to cease, without his own reproach he shall cause it to turn upon him. The turn to the isles meets a prince who turns the reproach back, Henry's outline shows, and the conqueror's advance is checked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Then he shall turn his face toward the fort of his own land, but he shall stumble and fall, and not be found. The conqueror stumbles and falls and is not found, Henry's account shows: God sets up one and pulls down another as he pleases. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Then shall stand up in his estate a raiser of taxes in the glory of the kingdom, but within few days he shall be destroyed, neither in anger, nor in battle. The tax-raiser's brief reign and quiet destruction, Henry's outline shows, are one more foreseen turn of the succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. And in his estate shall stand up a vile person, to whom they shall not give the honour of the kingdom, but he shall come in peaceably, and obtain the kingdom by flatteries. From this verse, Henry says, the passage is generally considered to relate to Antiochus Epiphanes, a cruel and violent persecutor of the Jews, who comes in peaceably and obtains the kingdom by flatteries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. And with the arms of a flood shall they be overflown from before him, and shall be broken, yea, also the prince of the covenant. The vile person's flood overwhelms opposition, Henry's reading shows, and even the prince of the covenant is broken before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. And after the league made with him he shall work deceitfully, for he shall come up, and shall become strong with a small people. After the league he works deceitfully, Henry notes, becoming strong with a small people, the way of the flattering usurper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. He shall enter peaceably even upon the fattest places of the province, and he shall do that which his fathers have not done, nor his fathers' fathers; he shall scatter among them the prey, and spoil, and riches, yea, and he shall forecast his devices against the strong holds, even for a time. His scattering of spoil, Henry's account shows, buys the loyalty his fathers never won. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. And he shall stir up his power and his courage against the king of the south with a great army, and the king of the south shall be stirred up to battle with a very great and mighty army, but he shall not stand, for they shall forecast devices against him. The war against the south, Henry's outline shows, renews the old Egypt-Syria contest under the persecutor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Yea, they that feed of the portion of his meat shall destroy him, and his army shall overflow, and many shall fall down slain. The southern king is destroyed by his own household, Henry's reading shows, as deceit answers deceit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. And both of these kings' hearts shall be to do mischief, and they shall speak lies at one table, but it shall not prosper, for yet the end shall be at the time appointed. Both kings speak lies at one table, Henry notes, but it shall not prosper, for the end is at the time appointed, not at man's plotting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Then shall he return into his land with great riches, and his heart shall be against the holy covenant, and he shall do exploits, and return to his own land. His heart against the holy covenant, Henry's reading shows, turns the persecutor from foreign war to war on God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. At the time appointed he shall return, and come toward the south, but it shall not be as the former, or as the latter. The return at the time appointed differs from the former campaigns, Henry's outline shows, and the appointed time governs all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. For the ships of Chittim shall come against him, therefore he shall be grieved, and return, and have indignation against the holy covenant; so shall he do, he shall even return, and have intelligence with them that forsake the holy covenant. Checked by the ships of Chittim, Henry's reading shows, he turns his indignation against the holy covenant and leagues with those who forsake it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. And arms shall stand on his part, and they shall pollute the sanctuary of strength, and shall take away the daily sacrifice, and they shall place the abomination that makes desolate. The remainder of this prophecy is very difficult, Henry cautions, and commentators differ much respecting it; from Antiochus the account seems to pass to antichrist, with reference to the Roman empire in its pagan, early Christian, and papal states. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. And such as do wickedly against the covenant shall he corrupt by flatteries, but the people that do know their God shall be strong, and do exploits. Those who know God will trust in him, Henry says, and he will enable them to stand their ground, bear their cross, and maintain their conflict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. And they that understand among the people shall instruct many, yet they shall fall by the sword, and by flame, by captivity, and by spoil, many days. The instructing faithful fall by sword, flame, captivity, and spoil, Henry's reading shows, yet their instruction of many stands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. Now when they shall fall, they shall be helped with a little help, but many shall cleave to them with flatteries. The little help in falling, Henry implies, is God's measured aid, while flatterers cleave for their own ends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. And some of them of understanding shall fall, to try them, and to purge, and to make them white, even to the time of the end, because it is yet for a time appointed. The falling of the understanding tries, purges, and makes them white, Henry's reading shows, until the time of the end, which is yet for a time appointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. And the king shall do according to his will, and he shall exalt himself, and magnify himself above every god, and shall speak marvellous things against the God of gods, and shall prosper till the indignation be accomplished, for that that is determined shall be done. The self-exalting king prospers only till the indignation is accomplished, Henry notes, for what is determined shall be done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. Neither shall he regard the God of his fathers, nor the desire of women, nor regard any god, for he shall magnify himself above all. He regards no god, Henry's reading shows, magnifying himself above all, the mark of the antichristian power to which the account seems to pass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. But in his estate shall he honour the God of forces, and a god whom his fathers knew not shall he honour with gold, and silver, and with precious stones, and pleasant things. The god of forces, honoured with gold and precious stones, Henry's reading shows, is the strange god of the self-exalting power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. Thus shall he do in the most strong holds with a strange god, whom he shall acknowledge and increase with glory, and he shall cause them to rule over many, and shall divide the land for gain. The strong holds held with a strange god, Henry notes, divide the land for gain, and the end of the Lord's patience towards his enemies approaches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. And at the time of the end shall the king of the south push at him, and the king of the north shall come against him like a whirlwind, with chariots, and with horsemen, and with many ships, and he shall enter into the countries, and shall overflow and pass over. At the time of the end the kings of south and north assail him like a whirlwind, Henry's outline shows, yet the appointed end governs the storm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 41. He shall enter also into the glorious land, and many countries shall be overthrown, but these shall escape out of his hand, even Edom, and Moab, and the chief of the children of Ammon. He enters the glorious land and overthrows many, Henry's reading shows, yet some escape his hand, as God limits the destroyer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 42. He shall stretch forth his hand also upon the countries, and the land of Egypt shall not escape. Egypt itself does not escape his hand, Henry's outline shows, in the final overrunning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 43. But he shall have power over the treasures of gold and of silver, and over all the precious things of Egypt, and the Libyans and the Ethiopians shall be at his steps. The treasures of Egypt and the peoples at his steps, Henry's reading shows, mark the height of his power before its end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 44. But tidings out of the east and out of the north shall trouble him, therefore he shall go forth with great fury to destroy, and utterly to make away many. Tidings from east and north trouble him, Henry notes, and he goes forth in fury to destroy many, hastening his own end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 45. And he shall plant the tabernacles of his palace between the seas in the glorious holy mountain, yet he shall come to his end, and none shall help him. He plants his palace between the seas in the glorious holy mountain, Henry's reading shows, yet he shall come to his end, and none shall help him; if we would escape the ruin of the infidel, the idolater, and the cruel persecutor, let us make the oracles of God our standard of truth and duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
