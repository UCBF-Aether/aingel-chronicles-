The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ephesians 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right.

2 Honour thy father and mother; which is the first commandment with promise;

3 That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth.

4 And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.

5 Servants, be obedient to them that are your masters according to the flesh, with fear and trembling, in singleness of your heart, as unto Christ;

6 Not with eyeservice, as menpleasers; but as the servants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart;

7 With good will doing service, as to the Lord, and not to men:

8 Knowing that whatsoever good thing any man doeth, the same shall he receive of the Lord, whether he be bond or free.

9 And, ye masters, do the same things unto them, forbearing threatening: knowing that your Master also is in heaven; neither is there respect of persons with him.

10 Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might.

11 Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.

12 For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.

13 Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand.

14 Stand therefore, having your loins girt about with truth, and having on the breastplate of righteousness;

15 And your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace;

16 Above all, taking the shield of faith, wherewith ye shall be able to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked.

17 And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God:

18 Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, and watching thereunto with all perseverance and supplication for all saints;

19 And for me, that utterance may be given unto me, that I may open my mouth boldly, to make known the mystery of the gospel,

20 For which I am an ambassador in bonds: that therein I may speak boldly, as I ought to speak.

21 But that ye also may know my affairs, and how I do, Tychicus, a beloved brother and faithful minister in the Lord, shall make known to you all things:

22 Whom I have sent unto you for the same purpose, that ye might know our affairs, and that he might comfort your hearts.

23 Peace be to the brethren, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

24 Grace be with all them that love our Lord Jesus Christ in sincerity. Amen.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Children are told to obey and honour parents, fathers not to provoke their children, servants to serve wholeheartedly and masters to act likewise, knowing all have one Master in heaven. Then comes the armour of God, truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation and the Spirit's word, for a struggle not against flesh and blood but against spiritual opposition, fought in constant prayer. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Children are to obey their parents in the Lord, for this is right, an obedience including inward reverence as well as outward acts, since parents hold authority from God and nature in subservience to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Honor father and mother is the first commandment with promise, honor implying reverence, obedience, and relief and maintenance when needed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The promise is that it may be well with them and they may live long on the earth, outward prosperity ordinarily attending obedient children, though where it does not, it is made up with something better. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Fathers must not provoke their children to wrath, but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord, governing with tenderness, convincing their judgments, and giving them a religious education rather than merely providing for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Servants are to obey their earthly masters with fear and trembling, in singleness of heart, as unto Christ, reverencing those over them and serving faithfully, since civil service is not inconsistent with Christian liberty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. That service must not be eye-service, as men-pleasers, but as servants of Christ doing God's will from the heart, conscientious when the master's eye is absent because their Master in heaven beholds them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. It is to be done with good will, as to the Lord and not to men, since a steady regard to Christ makes service easy, pleasing, and acceptable to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Faithful servants may trust God for their wages, knowing whatever good any man does he shall receive from the Lord, whether bond or free, since God rewards even the meanest drudgery done from duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Masters are to do the same things to their servants, forbearing threatening, knowing their own Master is in heaven and there is no respect of persons with him, so masters and servants are fellow servants under Christ's impartial judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Finally, believers are to be strong in the Lord and in the power of his might, since spiritual strength and courage are necessary for warfare and all sufficiency is of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. They are to put on the whole armor of God, that they may stand against the wiles of the devil, aiming at all grace, since God both prepares and bestows this armor but believers must put it on by prayer and exercise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The wrestling is not against flesh and blood but against principalities, powers, rulers of this world's darkness, and spiritual wickedness in high places, a subtle, powerful, unseen enemy who assaults souls before they are aware. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Therefore they are to take the whole armor of God, to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand, resolving by God's grace not to yield, since resisting the devil makes him flee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Standing, they are to have loins girt with truth and the breastplate of righteousness, sincerity girding on all other graces, and Christ's righteousness, imputed and implanted, sheltering the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Their feet are to be shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace, a prepared and resolved frame of heart to adhere to the gospel, bringing peace with God, with oneself, and with others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Above all they take the shield of faith, with which they can quench all the fiery darts of the wicked, faith being all in all in temptation, receiving Christ and deriving grace from him as a universal defense. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. They take the helmet of salvation and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God, good hope of salvation guarding the head against despair, and Scripture, the Spirit's own sword, repelling temptation as Christ himself answered with what is written. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Prayer buckles on all the armor: praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, watching with all perseverance and supplication for all saints, keeping constant times of prayer and a constant disposition to pray. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Paul asks their prayers for himself, that utterance may be given him to open his mouth boldly and make known the mystery of the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. He is an ambassador in bonds for that gospel, that he may speak boldly as he ought, his chains not silencing his embassy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Tychicus, a beloved brother and faithful minister in the Lord, will make known to them all Paul's affairs, so they may know how he fares. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Paul sent Tychicus for this purpose, that they might know his affairs and that he might comfort their hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Peace be to the brethren, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, the apostle's closing blessing on the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Grace be with all who love our Lord Jesus Christ in sincerity, Henry's final word resting on sincere, not pretended, love of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
