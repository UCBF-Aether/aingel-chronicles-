The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 50 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word that the LORD spake against Babylon and against the land of the Chaldeans by Jeremiah the prophet.

2 Declare ye among the nations, and publish, and set up a standard; publish, and conceal not: say, Babylon is taken, Bel is confounded, Merodach is broken in pieces; her idols are confounded, her images are broken in pieces.

3 For out of the north there cometh up a nation against her, which shall make her land desolate, and none shall dwell therein: they shall remove, they shall depart, both man and beast.

4 In those days, and in that time, saith the LORD, the children of Israel shall come, they and the children of Judah together, going and weeping: they shall go, and seek the LORD their God.

5 They shall ask the way to Zion with their faces thitherward, saying, Come, and let us join ourselves to the LORD in a perpetual covenant that shall not be forgotten.

6 My people hath been lost sheep: their shepherds have caused them to go astray, they have turned them away on the mountains: they have gone from mountain to hill, they have forgotten their restingplace.

7 All that found them have devoured them: and their adversaries said, We offend not, because they have sinned against the LORD, the habitation of justice, even the LORD, the hope of their fathers.

8 Remove out of the midst of Babylon, and go forth out of the land of the Chaldeans, and be as the he goats before the flocks.

9 For, lo, I will raise and cause to come up against Babylon an assembly of great nations from the north country: and they shall set themselves in array against her; from thence she shall be taken: their arrows shall be as of a mighty expert man; none shall return in vain.

10 And Chaldea shall be a spoil: all that spoil her shall be satisfied, saith the LORD.

11 Because ye were glad, because ye rejoiced, O ye destroyers of mine heritage, because ye are grown fat as the heifer at grass, and bellow as bulls;

12 Your mother shall be sore confounded; she that bare you shall be ashamed: behold, the hindermost of the nations shall be a wilderness, a dry land, and a desert.

13 Because of the wrath of the LORD it shall not be inhabited, but it shall be wholly desolate: every one that goeth by Babylon shall be astonished, and hiss at all her plagues.

14 Put yourselves in array against Babylon round about: all ye that bend the bow, shoot at her, spare no arrows: for she hath sinned against the LORD.

15 Shout against her round about: she hath given her hand: her foundations are fallen, her walls are thrown down: for it is the vengeance of the LORD: take vengeance upon her; as she hath done, do unto her.

16 Cut off the sower from Babylon, and him that handleth the sickle in the time of harvest: for fear of the oppressing sword they shall turn every one to his people, and they shall flee every one to his own land.

17 Israel is a scattered sheep; the lions have driven him away: first the king of Assyria hath devoured him; and last this Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon hath broken his bones.

18 Therefore thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Behold, I will punish the king of Babylon and his land, as I have punished the king of Assyria.

19 And I will bring Israel again to his habitation, and he shall feed on Carmel and Bashan, and his soul shall be satisfied upon mount Ephraim and Gilead.

20 In those days, and in that time, saith the LORD, the iniquity of Israel shall be sought for, and there shall be none; and the sins of Judah, and they shall not be found: for I will pardon them whom I reserve.

21 Go up against the land of Merathaim, even against it, and against the inhabitants of Pekod: waste and utterly destroy after them, saith the LORD, and do according to all that I have commanded thee.

22 A sound of battle is in the land, and of great destruction.

23 How is the hammer of the whole earth cut asunder and broken! how is Babylon become a desolation among the nations!

24 I have laid a snare for thee, and thou art also taken, O Babylon, and thou wast not aware: thou art found, and also caught, because thou hast striven against the LORD.

25 The LORD hath opened his armoury, and hath brought forth the weapons of his indignation: for this is the work of the Lord GOD of hosts in the land of the Chaldeans.

26 Come against her from the utmost border, open her storehouses: cast her up as heaps, and destroy her utterly: let nothing of her be left.

27 Slay all her bullocks; let them go down to the slaughter: woe unto them! for their day is come, the time of their visitation.

28 The voice of them that flee and escape out of the land of Babylon, to declare in Zion the vengeance of the LORD our God, the vengeance of his temple.

29 Call together the archers against Babylon: all ye that bend the bow, camp against it round about; let none thereof escape: recompense her according to her work; according to all that she hath done, do unto her: for she hath been proud against the LORD, against the Holy One of Israel.

30 Therefore shall her young men fall in the streets, and all her men of war shall be cut off in that day, saith the LORD.

31 Behold, I am against thee, O thou most proud, saith the Lord GOD of hosts: for thy day is come, the time that I will visit thee.

32 And the most proud shall stumble and fall, and none shall raise him up: and I will kindle a fire in his cities, and it shall devour all round about him.

33 Thus saith the LORD of hosts; The children of Israel and the children of Judah were oppressed together: and all that took them captives held them fast; they refused to let them go.

34 Their Redeemer is strong; the LORD of hosts is his name: he shall throughly plead their cause, that he may give rest to the land, and disquiet the inhabitants of Babylon.

35 A sword is upon the Chaldeans, saith the LORD, and upon the inhabitants of Babylon, and upon her princes, and upon her wise men.

36 A sword is upon the liars; and they shall dote: a sword is upon her mighty men; and they shall be dismayed.

37 A sword is upon their horses, and upon their chariots, and upon all the mingled people that are in the midst of her; and they shall become as women: a sword is upon her treasures; and they shall be robbed.

38 A drought is upon her waters; and they shall be dried up: for it is the land of graven images, and they are mad upon their idols.

39 Therefore the wild beasts of the desert with the wild beasts of the islands shall dwell there, and the owls shall dwell therein: and it shall be no more inhabited for ever; neither shall it be dwelt in from generation to generation.

40 As God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah and the neighbour cities thereof, saith the LORD; so shall no man abide there, neither shall any son of man dwell therein.

41 Behold, a people shall come from the north, and a great nation, and many kings shall be raised up from the coasts of the earth.

42 They shall hold the bow and the lance: they are cruel, and will not shew mercy: their voice shall roar like the sea, and they shall ride upon horses, every one put in array, like a man to the battle, against thee, O daughter of Babylon.

43 The king of Babylon hath heard the report of them, and his hands waxed feeble: anguish took hold of him, and pangs as of a woman in travail.

44 Behold, he shall come up like a lion from the swelling of Jordan unto the habitation of the strong: but I will make them suddenly run away from her: and who is a chosen man, that I may appoint over her? for who is like me? and who will appoint me the time? and who is that shepherd that will stand before me?

45 Therefore hear ye the counsel of the LORD, that he hath taken against Babylon; and his purposes, that he hath purposed against the land of the Chaldeans: Surely the least of the flock shall draw them out: surely he shall make their habitation desolate with them.

46 At the noise of the taking of Babylon the earth is moved, and the cry is heard among the nations.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The long oracle against Babylon opens with a standard set up among the nations: Babylon is taken, Bel is confounded, Merodach is broken. Israel and Judah, scattered sheep, will return to Zion and to an everlasting covenant, while Babylon's pride, her idols and her oppressors are repaid, and the noise of her taking is heard among the nations. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry notes Babylon's judgment is put last among the prophecies against the Gentiles because it was last accomplished, the king of Sheshach drinking last of the cup of fury. Babylon was the rod in God's hand for chastising the other nations, and now at length that rod shall be thrown into the fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry says Babylon's destruction is spoken of as a thing done, to be published to the nations and not concealed, with the flag hung out on a day of triumph. Even Babylon's principal idols, Bel and Merodach, which the people protected with all care and expected protection from, shall be confounded and broken to pieces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry says the nation from the north is Media, lying north of Babylon, with Assyria the route by which Cyrus descended upon her, and God will find nations yet further north to come upon those who threatened evil from the north. Her land shall be made desolate, with man and beast departing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry promises first a return to God and then to the land: Israel and Judah together shall go weeping to seek the LORD their God. These tears, he notes, flow not from worldly sorrow as at the captivity, but from godly sorrow, tears of repentance for sin and of joy at the dawning of deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry says they shall ask the way to Zion with their faces thitherward, resolving not to take up short of it, and shall join themselves to the LORD in a perpetual covenant never to be forgotten. In all true converts, he observes, there are both a sincere desire to attain the end and a constant care to keep in the way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry laments God's people as lost sheep, hurried from mountain to hill, who have forgotten their resting-place. Their misery was aggravated because their own shepherds, their princes and priests, led them astray from duty and so provoked God to turn them out of their land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry notes the devourers excused themselves by saying, We offend not, because they have sinned against the LORD, the habitation of justice and the hope of their fathers. That excuse, he says, aggravated Israel's sin and justified God, but did not justify the adversaries in what they did to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry calls the people to hasten away as soon as the door of liberty opens, removing not merely out of Babylon's borders but out of its midst, and to be as the he-goats before the flocks. It is a graceful thing, he remarks, to be forward in a good work and set others a good example. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry calls the invading army an assembly of great nations, the Medes and Persians with all their allies, regularly formed by the divine will and counsel for this execution. God will raise them up, incline and fit them for the service, and cause them to come up, so that their arrows, like those of a mighty expert man, shall none return in vain, for when God gives commission he gives success. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry says Babylon's wealth shall be a rich and easy prey, and, strangely, all that spoil her shall be satisfied, having so much that even they themselves shall say they have enough. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry says the Chaldeans rejoiced and were glad as destroyers of God's heritage, growing fat as the heifer at grass and bellowing as bulls on the spoils of Jerusalem. God does not afflict his people willingly, he notes, and takes it very ill if the instruments he employs afflict them willingly, so those who swallowed down riches must vomit them up again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry reads the mother confounded and ashamed as Babylon the mother-city, deserted by those who should have been her guards, and become the hindermost of the nations, a wilderness, a dry land and a desert. The great admirers of Babylon shall see it rendered very despicable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry says it is because of the wrath of the LORD that Babylon shall be wholly desolate and uninhabited, and everyone going by shall be astonished and hiss at her plagues. Instead of condoling with her, passers-by shall triumph in her fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry says the archers are bidden to put themselves in array and shoot, sparing no arrows, because she has sinned against the LORD. Sin, he notes, makes men a mark for the arrows of God's judgments, and the injuries done to God's people are laid to Babylon's charge as a heinous crime. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry says the shout against Babylon is the triumphant shout of those already sure of victory, for those whom God directs to shoot may shout, sure not to miss the mark. She has given her hand, surrendering tamely, her foundations have fallen and her walls are thrown down, and now vengeance may be taken as reprisal, doing unto her as she has done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry says not even the poor of the land shall be left in Babylon, as there were in Israel: the sower and the sickle-handler shall be cut off, so that harvest comes with no reapers and seed-time with no sower. Her hired auxiliary forces shall also desert, turning every one to his own people for fear of the oppressing sword, as mercenary men often do on the approach of danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry pictures Israel as a scattered sheep worried by dogs and roared upon by lions: Assyria first devoured the ten tribes, and at last Nebuchadnezzar has broken Judah's bones. Those who pursue the sins of their predecessors, he notes, must expect to be pursued by their plagues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry says the king of Babylon must be punished as the king of Assyria was, for pursuing the same course. If they do as their predecessors did, he remarks, let them fare as their predecessors fared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry says God will return the captivity and bring Israel again to his own habitation, for the discontinuance of possession was not the destruction of their right to Canaan. They shall feed on Carmel and Bashan, the richest parts of the country, and their souls shall be satisfied, for those who seek first the kingdom of God shall have other things added to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry calls pardon the root of all the rest: Israel's iniquity shall be sought for and there shall be none, and Judah's sins shall not be found, blotted out as a cloud, crossed out as a debt, cast into the depth of the sea. Deliverances out of trouble, he notes, are comforts indeed when they are fruits of the forgiveness of sin, and this pardon includes a thorough reformation of heart and life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry identifies Merathaim as the country of the Mardi, lying part in Assyria and part in Armenia, and Pekod as another country Cyrus took on his way to Babylon. The command to waste and utterly destroy after them, doing all God has commanded, matches the word of Cyrus, who shall perform all God's pleasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry treats the sound of battle and of great destruction as the assurance of success for those who do what God commands: they shall accomplish what he threatens, and Babylon shall become a desolation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry calls Babylon the hammer of the whole earth, long beating down all nations far and near, and says it is time now that it be cut asunder and broken. The God of the whole earth, he notes, will break the hammer of the whole earth and assert the injured rights of nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry says God has laid a snare for Babylon, and she is taken unaware because she has striven against the LORD, joining issue with him as in law or battle. Those who strive against the LORD, he remarks, will soon find themselves over-matched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry says the LORD has opened his armoury and brought forth the weapons of his indignation, with Media and Persia as God's armoury, whence he fetches Cyrus and his officers and armies. Great men, he notes, are but instruments which the great God makes use of to serve his own purposes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry says all shall come together against Babylon from the utmost border, for there will be both work and pay enough for them all, and distance of place must not hinder them. Her storehouses shall be opened, her wealth shovelled up as heaps of ruins, and she shall be destroyed utterly, showing how little account the great God makes of what men so much value. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry reads the bullocks as Babylon's princes and great men, fat and bulky, who shall fall by the sword not as men of war on a bed of honour but as beasts by the butcher's hand. Woe unto them, he adds, for their day has come and they are not aware of it, which makes their case the more sad for the little sense they have of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry says the voice of those who flee and escape out of Babylon declares in Zion the vengeance of the LORD, the vengeance of his temple. The burning of the temple and the carrying away of its vessels were the articles in the charge on which greater stress was laid than on Babylon's being the hammer of the earth, for whatever wrong is done to God's church will certainly be reckoned for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry says the archers are called together to camp against Babylon round about and let none escape, recompensing her according to her work and according to all she has done. She has been proud against the LORD, against the Holy One of Israel, insulting him in insulting over his people, and so has made him her enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 30. Henry says Babylon's young men shall fall in the streets and all her men of war be cut off in that day which should have been her defence. God is against her, and none can stand before him when he is angry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 31. Henry takes the address, O thou most proud, literally as pride itself, and says the pride of men's hearts sets God against them and ripens them apace for ruin. God resists the proud and will bring them down, for it is his glory to look upon the proud and abase them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 32. Henry says the most proud shall stumble and fall, and none shall raise him up, falling not so much by others thrusting him down as by his own stumbling, since he holds his head so high he never looks under his feet. The fire God kindles in his cities shall devour all round about him, and who can help up those whom God will throw down? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 33. Henry notes Israel and Judah were oppressed together, the remnants of the ten tribes having mingled with the two tribes after the kingdoms of Assyria and Chaldea were united. Their captors held them fast and refused to let them go, and they were humble suppliants who could attempt nothing towards their own liberty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 34. Henry calls this the people's comfort in distress: though they are weak, their Redeemer is strong, their Avenger who has a right to them and will make good his claim. The LORD of hosts shall thoroughly plead their cause, pleading it with jealousy and carrying it, that he may give rest to the land and disquiet Babylon's inhabitants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 35. Henry says the Chaldeans had long been God's sword for executing judgment on the nations, but now, being as bad as any of them, a sword of justice is brought upon them. It falls on their princes, whose dignity, wealth and power shall not secure them, and on their wise men, whose learning and policy shall neither secure them nor stand the public in any stead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 36. Henry calls the liars Babylon's soothsayers and astrologers, who cheated with prognostications of peace and prosperity, and says the sword shall make them dote and talk like fools. The sword upon her mighty men shall dismay them, for what stead will their hands stand them in when their hearts fail them? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 37. Henry says the sword upon horses and chariots means the invaders shall master all Babylon's warlike stores, seizing or destroying them, while the mingled foreign troops in her service become as weak and timorous as women. The sword upon her treasures, the sinews of war, means they shall be robbed and used by the enemy against her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 38. Henry says the waters that secured Babylon shall be dried up, for Cyrus drew the Euphrates into so many channels that it became passable for his army to reach walls thought inaccessible. He adds that the land of graven images was mad upon its idols, doting on frightful scarecrow images, which he calls the maddest thing in the world, making a god of a creature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 39. Henry says the fruitful country, its waters dried up, shall be turned to barrenness and inhabited no more by men but by wild beasts of the desert and islands and by owls. This was foretold concerning Babylon in Isaiah 13:19-22, that it shall be no more inhabited forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 40. Henry says Babylon shall become like Sodom and Gomorrah, so that no man shall abide there and no son of man dwell in it. As the Chaldeans had laid Edom waste, he notes, so they shall themselves be laid waste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 41. Henry says the people from the north, the great nation and many kings from the coasts of the earth, shall put Babylon into the utmost confusion and consternation. The expressions for the invaders' terrors are those used before for the Chaldeans invading Judah, to intimate that Babylon shall be paid in her own coin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 42. Henry describes the invaders holding bow and lance, cruel and without mercy, their voice roaring like the sea, riding on horses arrayed as a man for battle. God can find out such as shall be for terror and destruction to those who have been for terror and destruction to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 43. Henry notes the one difference from the earlier passage: there it is said, We have heard the fame thereof and our hands waxed feeble, but here the king of Babylon himself hears the report and his hands wax feeble. That proud and daring prince, he says, shall in his distress be as weak and dispirited as the meanest Israelites were in theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 44. Henry says the lion coming up from the swelling of Jordan is the invader who shall tear and destroy and make Babylon's habitation desolate. These verses repeat the Edom prophecy of chapter 49:19-21 with necessary alterations, showing that though providence may seem unequal for a time, its retributions will be equal at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 45. Henry says the counsel and purposes of the LORD against Babylon are settled, and surely the least of the flock shall draw them out and make their habitations desolate. When thou shalt make an end to spoil, he notes, thou shalt be spoiled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 46. Henry says the desolation of Babylon shall be so astonishing that at the noise of her taking the earth is moved and the cry is heard among the nations. All the nations about shall be terrified by it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

Connections to other passages

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry pictures Israel as a scattered sheep worried by dogs and roared upon by lions: Assyria first devoured the ten tribes, and at last Nebuchadnezzar has broken Judah's bones. Those who pursue the sins of their predecessors, he notes, must expect to be pursued by their plagues. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry says the king of Babylon must be punished as the king of Assyria was, for pursuing the same course. If they do as their predecessors did, he remarks, let them fare as their predecessors fared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry says God will return the captivity and bring Israel again to his own habitation, for the discontinuance of possession was not the destruction of their right to Canaan. They shall feed on Carmel and Bashan, the richest parts of the country, and their souls shall be satisfied, for those who seek first the kingdom of God shall have other things added to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry calls pardon the root of all the rest: Israel's iniquity shall be sought for and there shall be none, and Judah's sins shall not be found, blotted out as a cloud, crossed out as a debt, cast into the depth of the sea. Deliverances out of trouble, he notes, are comforts indeed when they are fruits of the forgiveness of sin, and this pardon includes a thorough reformation of heart and life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry identifies Merathaim as the country of the Mardi, lying part in Assyria and part in Armenia, and Pekod as another country Cyrus took on his way to Babylon. The command to waste and utterly destroy after them, doing all God has commanded, matches the word of Cyrus, who shall perform all God's pleasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry treats the sound of battle and of great destruction as the assurance of success for those who do what God commands: they shall accomplish what he threatens, and Babylon shall become a desolation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry calls Babylon the hammer of the whole earth, long beating down all nations far and near, and says it is time now that it be cut asunder and broken. The God of the whole earth, he notes, will break the hammer of the whole earth and assert the injured rights of nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry says God has laid a snare for Babylon, and she is taken unaware because she has striven against the LORD, joining issue with him as in law or battle. Those who strive against the LORD, he remarks, will soon find themselves over-matched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry says the LORD has opened his armoury and brought forth the weapons of his indignation, with Media and Persia as God's armoury, whence he fetches Cyrus and his officers and armies. Great men, he notes, are but instruments which the great God makes use of to serve his own purposes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry says all shall come together against Babylon from the utmost border, for there will be both work and pay enough for them all, and distance of place must not hinder them. Her storehouses shall be opened, her wealth shovelled up as heaps of ruins, and she shall be destroyed utterly, showing how little account the great God makes of what men so much value. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry reads the bullocks as Babylon's princes and great men, fat and bulky, who shall fall by the sword not as men of war on a bed of honour but as beasts by the butcher's hand. Woe unto them, he adds, for their day has come and they are not aware of it, which makes their case the more sad for the little sense they have of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry says the voice of those who flee and escape out of Babylon declares in Zion the vengeance of the LORD, the vengeance of his temple. The burning of the temple and the carrying away of its vessels were the articles in the charge on which greater stress was laid than on Babylon's being the hammer of the earth, for whatever wrong is done to God's church will certainly be reckoned for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry says the archers are called together to camp against Babylon round about and let none escape, recompensing her according to her work and according to all she has done. She has been proud against the LORD, against the Holy One of Israel, insulting him in insulting over his people, and so has made him her enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 30. Henry says Babylon's young men shall fall in the streets and all her men of war be cut off in that day which should have been her defence. God is against her, and none can stand before him when he is angry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 31. Henry takes the address, O thou most proud, literally as pride itself, and says the pride of men's hearts sets God against them and ripens them apace for ruin. God resists the proud and will bring them down, for it is his glory to look upon the proud and abase them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 32. Henry says the most proud shall stumble and fall, and none shall raise him up, falling not so much by others thrusting him down as by his own stumbling, since he holds his head so high he never looks under his feet. The fire God kindles in his cities shall devour all round about him, and who can help up those whom God will throw down? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 33. Henry notes Israel and Judah were oppressed together, the remnants of the ten tribes having mingled with the two tribes after the kingdoms of Assyria and Chaldea were united. Their captors held them fast and refused to let them go, and they were humble suppliants who could attempt nothing towards their own liberty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 34. Henry calls this the people's comfort in distress: though they are weak, their Redeemer is strong, their Avenger who has a right to them and will make good his claim. The LORD of hosts shall thoroughly plead their cause, pleading it with jealousy and carrying it, that he may give rest to the land and disquiet Babylon's inhabitants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 35. Henry says the Chaldeans had long been God's sword for executing judgment on the nations, but now, being as bad as any of them, a sword of justice is brought upon them. It falls on their princes, whose dignity, wealth and power shall not secure them, and on their wise men, whose learning and policy shall neither secure them nor stand the public in any stead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 36. Henry calls the liars Babylon's soothsayers and astrologers, who cheated with prognostications of peace and prosperity, and says the sword shall make them dote and talk like fools. The sword upon her mighty men shall dismay them, for what stead will their hands stand them in when their hearts fail them? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 37. Henry says the sword upon horses and chariots means the invaders shall master all Babylon's warlike stores, seizing or destroying them, while the mingled foreign troops in her service become as weak and timorous as women. The sword upon her treasures, the sinews of war, means they shall be robbed and used by the enemy against her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 38. Henry says the waters that secured Babylon shall be dried up, for Cyrus drew the Euphrates into so many channels that it became passable for his army to reach walls thought inaccessible. He adds that the land of graven images was mad upon its idols, doting on frightful scarecrow images, which he calls the maddest thing in the world, making a god of a creature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 39. Henry says the fruitful country, its waters dried up, shall be turned to barrenness and inhabited no more by men but by wild beasts of the desert and islands and by owls. This was foretold concerning Babylon in Isaiah 13:19-22, that it shall be no more inhabited forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 40. Henry says Babylon shall become like Sodom and Gomorrah, so that no man shall abide there and no son of man dwell in it. As the Chaldeans had laid Edom waste, he notes, so they shall themselves be laid waste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 41. Henry says the people from the north, the great nation and many kings from the coasts of the earth, shall put Babylon into the utmost confusion and consternation. The expressions for the invaders' terrors are those used before for the Chaldeans invading Judah, to intimate that Babylon shall be paid in her own coin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 42. Henry describes the invaders holding bow and lance, cruel and without mercy, their voice roaring like the sea, riding on horses arrayed as a man for battle. God can find out such as shall be for terror and destruction to those who have been for terror and destruction to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 43. Henry notes the one difference from the earlier passage: there it is said, We have heard the fame thereof and our hands waxed feeble, but here the king of Babylon himself hears the report and his hands wax feeble. That proud and daring prince, he says, shall in his distress be as weak and dispirited as the meanest Israelites were in theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 44. Henry says the lion coming up from the swelling of Jordan is the invader who shall tear and destroy and make Babylon's habitation desolate. These verses repeat the Edom prophecy of chapter 49:19-21 with necessary alterations, showing that though providence may seem unequal for a time, its retributions will be equal at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 45. Henry says the counsel and purposes of the LORD against Babylon are settled, and surely the least of the flock shall draw them out and make their habitations desolate. When thou shalt make an end to spoil, he notes, thou shalt be spoiled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 46. Henry says the desolation of Babylon shall be so astonishing that at the noise of her taking the earth is moved and the cry is heard among the nations. All the nations about shall be terrified by it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
