The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he went out from thence, and came into his own country; and his disciples follow him.

2 And when the sabbath day was come, he began to teach in the synagogue: and many hearing him were astonished, saying, From whence hath this man these things? and what wisdom is this which is given unto him, that even such mighty works are wrought by his hands?

3 Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary, the brother of James, and Joses, and of Juda, and Simon? and are not his sisters here with us? And they were offended at him.

4 But Jesus said unto them, A prophet is not without honour, but in his own country, and among his own kin, and in his own house.

5 And he could there do no mighty work, save that he laid his hands upon a few sick folk, and healed them.

6 And he marvelled because of their unbelief. And he went round about the villages, teaching.

7 And he called unto him the twelve, and began to send them forth by two and two; and gave them power over unclean spirits;

8 And commanded them that they should take nothing for their journey, save a staff only; no scrip, no bread, no money in their purse:

9 But be shod with sandals; and not put on two coats.

10 And he said unto them, In what place soever ye enter into an house, there abide till ye depart from that place.

11 And whosoever shall not receive you, nor hear you, when ye depart thence, shake off the dust under your feet for a testimony against them. Verily I say unto you, It shall be more tolerable for Sodom and Gomorrha in the day of judgment, than for that city.

12 And they went out, and preached that men should repent.

13 And they cast out many devils, and anointed with oil many that were sick, and healed them.

14 And king Herod heard of him; (for his name was spread abroad:) and he said, That John the Baptist was risen from the dead, and therefore mighty works do shew forth themselves in him.

15 Others said, That it is Elias. And others said, That it is a prophet, or as one of the prophets.

16 But when Herod heard thereof, he said, It is John, whom I beheaded: he is risen from the dead.

17 For Herod himself had sent forth and laid hold upon John, and bound him in prison for Herodias’ sake, his brother Philip’s wife: for he had married her.

18 For John had said unto Herod, It is not lawful for thee to have thy brother’s wife.

19 Therefore Herodias had a quarrel against him, and would have killed him; but she could not:

20 For Herod feared John, knowing that he was a just man and an holy, and observed him; and when he heard him, he did many things, and heard him gladly.

21 And when a convenient day was come, that Herod on his birthday made a supper to his lords, high captains, and chief estates of Galilee;

22 And when the daughter of the said Herodias came in, and danced, and pleased Herod and them that sat with him, the king said unto the damsel, Ask of me whatsoever thou wilt, and I will give it thee.

23 And he sware unto her, Whatsoever thou shalt ask of me, I will give it thee, unto the half of my kingdom.

24 And she went forth, and said unto her mother, What shall I ask? And she said, The head of John the Baptist.

25 And she came in straightway with haste unto the king, and asked, saying, I will that thou give me by and by in a charger the head of John the Baptist.

26 And the king was exceeding sorry; yet for his oath’s sake, and for their sakes which sat with him, he would not reject her.

27 And immediately the king sent an executioner, and commanded his head to be brought: and he went and beheaded him in the prison,

28 And brought his head in a charger, and gave it to the damsel: and the damsel gave it to her mother.

29 And when his disciples heard of it, they came and took up his corpse, and laid it in a tomb.

30 And the apostles gathered themselves together unto Jesus, and told him all things, both what they had done, and what they had taught.

31 And he said unto them, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat.

32 And they departed into a desert place by ship privately.

33 And the people saw them departing, and many knew him, and ran afoot thither out of all cities, and outwent them, and came together unto him.

34 And Jesus, when he came out, saw much people, and was moved with compassion toward them, because they were as sheep not having a shepherd: and he began to teach them many things.

35 And when the day was now far spent, his disciples came unto him, and said, This is a desert place, and now the time is far passed:

36 Send them away, that they may go into the country round about, and into the villages, and buy themselves bread: for they have nothing to eat.

37 He answered and said unto them, Give ye them to eat. And they say unto him, Shall we go and buy two hundred pennyworth of bread, and give them to eat?

38 He saith unto them, How many loaves have ye? go and see. And when they knew, they say, Five, and two fishes.

39 And he commanded them to make all sit down by companies upon the green grass.

40 And they sat down in ranks, by hundreds, and by fifties.

41 And when he had taken the five loaves and the two fishes, he looked up to heaven, and blessed, and brake the loaves, and gave them to his disciples to set before them; and the two fishes divided he among them all.

42 And they did all eat, and were filled.

43 And they took up twelve baskets full of the fragments, and of the fishes.

44 And they that did eat of the loaves were about five thousand men.

45 And straightway he constrained his disciples to get into the ship, and to go to the other side before unto Bethsaida, while he sent away the people.

46 And when he had sent them away, he departed into a mountain to pray.

47 And when even was come, the ship was in the midst of the sea, and he alone on the land.

48 And he saw them toiling in rowing; for the wind was contrary unto them: and about the fourth watch of the night he cometh unto them, walking upon the sea, and would have passed by them.

49 But when they saw him walking upon the sea, they supposed it had been a spirit, and cried out:

50 For they all saw him, and were troubled. And immediately he talked with them, and saith unto them, Be of good cheer: it is I; be not afraid.

51 And he went up unto them into the ship; and the wind ceased: and they were sore amazed in themselves beyond measure, and wondered.

52 For they considered not the miracle of the loaves: for their heart was hardened.

53 And when they had passed over, they came into the land of Gennesaret, and drew to the shore.

54 And when they were come out of the ship, straightway they knew him,

55 And ran through that whole region round about, and began to carry about in beds those that were sick, where they heard he was.

56 And whithersoever he entered, into villages, or cities, or country, they laid the sick in the streets, and besought him that they might touch if it were but the border of his garment: and as many as touched him were made whole.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In his own country Jesus is rejected — “the carpenter, the son of Mary” — and can do little there because of unbelief (vv. 1–6). He sends the twelve out two by two to preach repentance, cast out devils, and anoint the sick (vv. 7–13). Herod’s court then recalls how John the Baptist was imprisoned and beheaded at Herodias’s daughter’s request (vv. 14–29). The apostles return, and the chapter turns to the feeding of about five thousand, Jesus walking on the sea at night, and healings wherever he lands in Gennesaret (vv. 30–56). What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He went out from there and came into his own country, and his disciples followed him. Nazareth, his home town, was the hardest place for his ministry, and his disciples went with him to see it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When the sabbath came, he began to teach in the synagogue, and many hearing him were astonished, asking, from where has this man these things, and what wisdom is given him, and what mighty works are wrought by his hands? Their astonishment owned his wisdom and works, but stopped short of faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They asked, Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary, the brother of James, Joses, Juda, and Simon? Are not his sisters here with us? And they were offended at him. Familiarity bred contempt: they knew his trade and his kin, and used that knowledge to reject his doctrine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Jesus said, A prophet is not without honour, but in his own country, and among his own kin, and in his own house. Those nearest a prophet know his meanness and forget his mission, so honour follows him everywhere but home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He could there do no mighty work, save that he laid his hands on a few sick folk and healed them. Unbelief tied his hands, not for lack of power, but because mighty works require a people willing to receive them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He marvelled because of their unbelief, and went round about the villages teaching. Christ marvels at unbelief that persists against evidence, and turns from one town to the villages that will hear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He called to him the twelve and began to send them forth by two and two, and gave them power over unclean spirits. Two by two they could witness, comfort, and confirm one another, and their power matched their commission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He commanded them to take nothing for their journey, save a staff only: no scrip, no bread, no money in their purse. Bare provision taught them to trust God's providence and their hearers' hospitality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. But they were to be shod with sandals, and not put on two coats. Needful dress was allowed, but superfluity was forbidden: a minister's wants should be few and plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. He said, in whatever place you enter a house, there abide till you depart from that place. Staying put avoids the appearance of seeking better quarters and honours the first welcome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Whoever shall not receive you nor hear you, when you depart, shake off the dust under your feet for a testimony against them; it shall be more tolerable for Sodom and Gomorrah in judgment than for that city. Dust shaken off is a witness, not revenge, and rejecting the gospel is worse than Sodom's sin because the light was greater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They went out and preached that men should repent. The apostles' first sermon was the same as John's and Christ's: repentance, the gate of the kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They cast out many devils, anointed with oil many sick, and healed them. Oil was the common medicine of the time, used as a sign while the healing itself was God's, confirming their word as it had confirmed Christ's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. King Herod heard of him, for his name was spread abroad, and he said, John the Baptist is risen from the dead, and therefore mighty works show forth themselves in him. A guilty conscience turned fame into fear, and Herod heard John in every miracle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Others said, It is Elijah, and others, That it is a prophet, or as one of the prophets. Popular guesses reached for past prophets because they would not confess the present Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. But when Herod heard, he said, It is John, whom I beheaded: he is risen from the dead. Herod's own verdict convicted him: the murdered prophet haunted the murderer's conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For Herod himself had laid hold on John, bound him in prison for Herodias' sake, his brother Philip's wife, for he had married her. The imprisonment began in unlawful marriage, and John's prison was the price of his faithfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. For John had said to Herod, It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife. John's reproof was plain and personal, naming the sin and the sinner without flattery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Therefore Herodias had a quarrel against him and would have killed him, but she could not. Herodias hated the reprover, but Herod's protection stood between her malice and John's life for a time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. For Herod feared John, knowing he was a just and holy man, and observed him, and when he heard him, he did many things and heard him gladly. Herod's conscience honoured John even while his lust defied him, and glad hearing without obedience is the pattern of a half-reformed hearer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. When a convenient day came, Herod on his birthday made a supper for his lords, high captains, and chief estates of Galilee. A birthday feast of the great gave malice its convenient opportunity, and revelry became the stage for murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. When the daughter of Herodias came in and danced, and pleased Herod and those who sat with him, the king said to the damsel, Ask of me whatever you will, and I will give it you. Dancing won a rash promise, and pleasure loosened a king's tongue before his court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. He swore to her, Whatever you ask of me, I will give it you, unto the half of my kingdom. An oath made in mirth bound him before witnesses, and he valued his word to a dancer above justice to a prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. She went forth and said to her mother, What shall I ask? And she said, The head of John the Baptist. The daughter asked her mother, and malice that had waited found its mouthpiece in a child's question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. She came in straightway with haste to the king and asked, I will that you give me at once in a charger the head of John the Baptist. Haste betrayed eagerness, and the charger turned a feast dish into a platter for murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The king was exceeding sorry, yet for his oath's sake and for those who sat with him, he would not reject her. Sorrow without repentance yielded to reputation: he feared his guests' opinion more than God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Immediately the king sent an executioner and commanded his head to be brought, and he went and beheaded him in the prison. A prophet's head was the price of a dance, and the prison that could not silence John became his place of martyrdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. He brought his head in a charger and gave it to the damsel, and the damsel gave it to her mother. The ghastly gift passed from hand to hand, showing how sin shared spreads its guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. When his disciples heard of it, they came and took up his corpse and laid it in a tomb. John's disciples gave their master burial, a last loyalty that foreshadowed the burial of Christ himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The apostles gathered to Jesus and told him all things, both what they had done and what they had taught. Returning ministers report to Christ, whose commission they carried, and his hearing is their account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. He said to them, Come apart into a desert place and rest a while, for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat. Christ grants rest after labour, and even holy work must pause for the body's need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. They departed into a desert place by ship privately. Privacy was sought for rest, but the people's love would not let them rest long. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. The people saw them departing, and many knew him, and ran on foot out of all cities, and outwent them, and came together to him. Foot travellers outran the ship, so eager were they for teaching that distance and desert were nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. When Jesus came out, he saw much people and was moved with compassion toward them, because they were as sheep not having a shepherd, and he began to teach them many things. Compassion turned rest into teaching, for shepherdless sheep are his first care, and teaching is his first mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. When the day was far spent, his disciples came to him and said, This is a desert place, and now the time is far past. The disciples saw the place and the hour, but not yet the provision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. Send them away, that they may go into the country and villages and buy themselves bread, for they have nothing to eat. Their counsel was reasonable and kind, but it sent a hungry multitude from the only table that could feed them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. He answered, Give you them to eat, and they said, Shall we go and buy two hundred pennyworth of bread and give them to eat? His command exposed their resources: two hundred pennyworth was beyond them, and their arithmetic proved their need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. He said, How many loaves have you? Go and see, and when they knew, they said, Five, and two fishes. Five loaves and two fishes were all the disciples' stock, and Christ begins his miracles with what his people actually have. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. He commanded them to make all sit down by companies upon the green grass. Orderly companies turned a desert crowd into a banquet, and the green grass was their tablecloth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. They sat down in ranks, by hundreds and by fifties. Ranked seating made distribution possible and counting certain, and no one was overlooked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. When he had taken the five loaves and the two fishes, he looked up to heaven, blessed, broke the loaves, and gave them to his disciples to set before them, and the two fishes he divided among them all. Blessing, breaking, and giving through the disciples' hands is the pattern of his provision: the gift comes from heaven through human hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. They all ate and were filled. Five loaves fed five thousand and filled them, for Christ's blessing multiplies what is given to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. They took up twelve baskets full of fragments, and of the fishes. Twelve baskets of remainder showed abundance beyond need, one basket for each apostle to carry as proof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. They that ate of the loaves were about five thousand men. The number counts men only, so women and children besides make the miracle greater still. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. Straightway he constrained his disciples to get into the ship and go to the other side before to Bethsaida, while he sent away the people. He parted the disciples from the crowd's excitement, and dismissed the multitude himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. When he had sent them away, he departed into a mountain to pray. After feeding thousands, Christ sought the mountain for prayer, and public work ended in private communion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. When evening came, the ship was in the midst of the sea, and he alone on the land. The disciples were alone on the water, and Christ alone on the land, yet neither was out of his sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. He saw them toiling in rowing, for the wind was contrary, and about the fourth watch of the night he came to them, walking upon the sea, and would have passed by them. He watched their toiling from the shore and came at the darkest watch, walking where they rowed, as if to pass by and be recognised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. But when they saw him walking upon the sea, they supposed it had been a spirit, and cried out. Fear mistook their Saviour for a spirit, for trouble blinds eyes even to a friend's approach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. For they all saw him and were troubled, and immediately he talked with them and said, Be of good cheer: it is I; be not afraid. His voice identified him before his form could, and 'it is I' is enough to quiet any storm of fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. He went up to them into the ship, and the wind ceased, and they were sore amazed in themselves beyond measure, and wondered. His presence, not their rowing, ended the storm, and amazement filled the place fear had held. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. For they considered not the miracle of the loaves, for their heart was hardened. The loaves should have taught them his power over nature, but a hardened heart forgets one miracle before the next arrives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. When they had passed over, they came into the land of Gennesaret and drew to the shore. Gennesaret's plain received them, and a new field of labour opened at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. When they came out of the ship, straightway they knew him. The people of Gennesaret recognised him at once, for his fame had arrived before his boat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. They ran through the whole region and began to carry about in beds those that were sick, where they heard he was. News of his landing moved the whole region to bring its sick to him, bed and all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 56. Wherever he entered, into villages, cities, or country, they laid the sick in the streets and besought him that they might touch if it were but the border of his garment, and as many as touched him were made whole. The border of his garment, touched in faith, healed as surely as his hand, and no place was too small for his mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a parallel examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a possible parallel. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

Five thousand fed, and walking on the sea — Mark 6, Luke 9, and John 6

Mark 6:30–52, Luke 9:10–17, and John 6:1–21 all tell of five loaves and two fishes feeding about five thousand, with twelve baskets of fragments left. Mark and John continue to the night crossing: the disciples toiling against the wind, Jesus walking on the sea, and the wind ceasing when he enters the ship. Luke tells the feeding but not the sea-walking in this chapter. John adds details the others lack — the lad with the loaves, Philip’s question about buying bread, and the crowd’s call of Jesus “that prophet” — and says the ship was immediately at land. No account here explains how the three narratives relate historically, and this page does not claim one depends on another.
