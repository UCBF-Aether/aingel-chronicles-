The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Zechariah 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The burden of the word of the LORD in the land of Hadrach, and Damascus shall be the rest thereof: when the eyes of man, as of all the tribes of Israel, shall be toward the LORD.

2 And Hamath also shall border thereby; Tyrus, and Zidon, though it be very wise.

3 And Tyrus did build herself a strong hold, and heaped up silver as the dust, and fine gold as the mire of the streets.

4 Behold, the LORD will cast her out, and he will smite her power in the sea; and she shall be devoured with fire.

5 Ashkelon shall see it, and fear; Gaza also shall see it, and be very sorrowful, and Ekron; for her expectation shall be ashamed; and the king shall perish from Gaza, and Ashkelon shall not be inhabited.

6 And a bastard shall dwell in Ashdod, and I will cut off the pride of the Philistines.

7 And I will take away his blood out of his mouth, and his abominations from between his teeth: but he that remaineth, even he, shall be for our God, and he shall be as a governor in Judah, and Ekron as a Jebusite.

8 And I will encamp about mine house because of the army, because of him that passeth by, and because of him that returneth: and no oppressor shall pass through them any more: for now have I seen with mine eyes.

9 Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass.

10 And I will cut off the chariot from Ephraim, and the horse from Jerusalem, and the battle bow shall be cut off: and he shall speak peace unto the heathen: and his dominion shall be from sea even to sea, and from the river even to the ends of the earth.

11 As for thee also, by the blood of thy covenant I have sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit wherein is no water.

12 Turn you to the strong hold, ye prisoners of hope: even to day do I declare that I will render double unto thee;

13 When I have bent Judah for me, filled the bow with Ephraim, and raised up thy sons, O Zion, against thy sons, O Greece, and made thee as the sword of a mighty man.

14 And the LORD shall be seen over them, and his arrow shall go forth as the lightning: and the LORD God shall blow the trumpet, and shall go with whirlwinds of the south.

15 The LORD of hosts shall defend them; and they shall devour, and subdue with sling stones; and they shall drink, and make a noise as through wine; and they shall be filled like bowls, and as the corners of the altar.

16 And the LORD their God shall save them in that day as the flock of his people: for they shall be as the stones of a crown, lifted up as an ensign upon his land.

17 For how great is his goodness, and how great is his beauty! corn shall make the young men cheerful, and new wine the maids.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The burden of the word falls first on Hadrach, Damascus, Hamath, Tyre, and Zidon, whose wealth heaped as dust will be cast out and devoured with fire, and on the Philistine cities whose pride will be cut off, though a remnant will remain for God. Then the chapter turns to Zion's King: rejoice greatly, daughter of Jerusalem, behold thy King comes unto thee, just and having salvation, lowly and riding upon an ass, upon a colt the foal of an ass, cutting off chariot, horse, and battle bow and speaking peace to the nations, with dominion from sea to sea. Prisoners of hope are told to turn to the stronghold, for God will render double, defend his flock, and save them as the stones of a crown lifted up upon his land. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The burden of the word of the Lord in the land of Hadrach, and Damascus shall be the rest thereof, when the eyes of man, as of all the tribes of Israel, shall be toward the Lord, because the burden begins with Hadrach and Damascus, the Syrian powers, whose rest is the Lord's judgment, watched by the eyes of all men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And Hamath also shall border thereby, Tyrus, and Zidon, though it be very wise, because Hamath on the border, and Tyre and Zidon with all their wisdom, are included in the same burden, since wisdom without God cannot avert it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And Tyrus did build herself a strong hold, and heaped up silver as the dust, and fine gold as the mire of the streets, because Tyre trusted in a fortress and in silver heaped like dust, which is the very wealth that will be taken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Behold, the Lord will cast her out, and he will smite her power in the sea, and she shall be devoured with fire, because the Lord himself casts Tyre out, smites her sea power, and devours her with fire, undoing fortress and wealth together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Ashkelon shall see it, and fear, Gaza also shall see it, and be very sorrowful, and Ekron, for her expectation shall be ashamed, and the king shall perish from Gaza, and Ashkelon shall not be inhabited, because the Philistine cities see Tyre's fall and fear, Gaza sorrows, Ekron's expectation is shamed, and Gaza loses her king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And a bastard shall dwell in Ashdod, and I will cut off the pride of the Philistines, because Ashdod will be peopled by strangers, and the pride of the Philistines, their trust in themselves, will be cut off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And I will take away his blood out of his mouth, and his abominations from between his teeth, but he that remains, even he, shall be for our God, and he shall be as a governor in Judah, and Ekron as a Jebusite, because the Philistine remnant will be cleansed from blood and abominations, and will be joined to God as a governor in Judah, with Ekron as a Jebusite, adopted into Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. And I will encamp about mine house because of the army, because of him that passes by, and because of him that returns, and no oppressor shall pass through them any more, for now have I seen with mine eyes, because God himself encamps about his house against every army, and no oppressor will pass through again, since he has seen with his eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion, shout, O daughter of Jerusalem, behold, thy King comes unto thee, he is just, and having salvation, lowly, and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass, because Zion's King comes just and saving, yet lowly, riding an ass's colt, which Henry reads as the Messiah's peaceful entry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. And I will cut off the chariot from Ephraim, and the horse from Jerusalem, and the battle bow shall be cut off, and he shall speak peace unto the heathen, and his dominion shall be from sea even to sea, and from the river even to the ends of the earth, because the King's reign removes the instruments of war and speaks peace to the nations, with dominion from sea to sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. As for thee also, by the blood of thy covenant I have sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit wherein is no water, because by the blood of the covenant the prisoners are sent out of the waterless pit, which is captivity without refreshment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Turn you to the strong hold, ye prisoners of hope, even to day do I declare that I will render double unto thee, because the prisoners are prisoners of hope, and turning to the stronghold brings a double rendering, twice what was lost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. When I have bent Judah for me, filled the bow with Ephraim, and raised up thy sons, O Zion, against thy sons, O Greece, and made thee as the sword of a mighty man, because God bends Judah as his bow and fills it with Ephraim, raising Zion's sons against Greece, as the sword of a mighty man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. And the Lord shall be seen over them, and his arrow shall go forth as the lightning, and the Lord God shall blow the trumpet, and shall go with whirlwinds of the south, because the Lord appears over his people, his arrow like lightning, with trumpet and southern whirlwinds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The Lord of hosts shall defend them, and they shall devour, and subdue with sling stones, and they shall drink, and make a noise as through wine, and they shall be filled like bowls, and as the corners of the altar, because the Lord defends, and his people devour and subdue, filled like bowls and like the altar's corners with the victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. And the Lord their God shall save them in that day as the flock of his people, for they shall be as the stones of a crown, lifted up as an ensign upon his land, because the saved are kept as a flock and lifted as crown stones, an ensign over the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For how great is his goodness, and how great is his beauty, corn shall make the young men cheerful, and new wine the maids, because the chapter ends in goodness and beauty, with corn and new wine cheering young men and maids, the fruit of the King's reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Ephraim. the leading tribe of the northern kingdom, used in the prophets for the northern kingdom of Israel itself, as distinct from Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Daughter of Zion. Zion, that is Jerusalem, pictured as a daughter told to rejoice greatly because her King comes to her, lowly and riding upon an ass. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
