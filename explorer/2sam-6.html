Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Again, David gathered together all the chosen men of Israel, thirty thousand.

2 And David arose, and went with all the people that were with him from Baale of Judah, to bring up from thence the ark of God, whose name is called by the name of the LORD of hosts that dwelleth between the cherubims.

3 And they set the ark of God upon a new cart, and brought it out of the house of Abinadab that was in Gibeah: and Uzzah and Ahio, the sons of Abinadab, drave the new cart.

4 And they brought it out of the house of Abinadab which was at Gibeah, accompanying the ark of God: and Ahio went before the ark.

5 And David and all the house of Israel played before the LORD on all manner of instruments made of fir wood, even on harps, and on psalteries, and on timbrels, and on cornets, and on cymbals.

6 And when they came to Nachon’s threshingfloor, Uzzah put forth his hand to the ark of God, and took hold of it; for the oxen shook it.

7 And the anger of the LORD was kindled against Uzzah; and God smote him there for his error; and there he died by the ark of God.

8 And David was displeased, because the LORD had made a breach upon Uzzah: and he called the name of the place Perezuzzah to this day.

9 And David was afraid of the LORD that day, and said, How shall the ark of the LORD come to me?

10 So David would not remove the ark of the LORD unto him into the city of David: but David carried it aside into the house of Obededom the Gittite.

11 And the ark of the LORD continued in the house of Obededom the Gittite three months: and the LORD blessed Obededom, and all his household.

12 And it was told king David, saying, The LORD hath blessed the house of Obededom, and all that pertaineth unto him, because of the ark of God. So David went and brought up the ark of God from the house of Obededom into the city of David with gladness.

13 And it was so, that when they that bare the ark of the LORD had gone six paces, he sacrificed oxen and fatlings.

14 And David danced before the LORD with all his might; and David was girded with a linen ephod.

15 So David and all the house of Israel brought up the ark of the LORD with shouting, and with the sound of the trumpet.

16 And as the ark of the LORD came into the city of David, Michal Saul’s daughter looked through a window, and saw king David leaping and dancing before the LORD; and she despised him in her heart.

17 And they brought in the ark of the LORD, and set it in his place, in the midst of the tabernacle that David had pitched for it: and David offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the LORD.

18 And as soon as David had made an end of offering burnt offerings and peace offerings, he blessed the people in the name of the LORD of hosts.

19 And he dealt among all the people, even among the whole multitude of Israel, as well to the women as men, to every one a cake of bread, and a good piece of flesh, and a flagon of wine. So all the people departed every one to his house.

20 Then David returned to bless his household. And Michal the daughter of Saul came out to meet David, and said, How glorious was the king of Israel to day, who uncovered himself to day in the eyes of the handmaids of his servants, as one of the vain fellows shamelessly uncovereth himself!

21 And David said unto Michal, It was before the LORD, which chose me before thy father, and before all his house, to appoint me ruler over the people of the LORD, over Israel: therefore will I play before the LORD.

22 And I will yet be more vile than thus, and will be base in mine own sight: and of the maidservants which thou hast spoken of, of them shall I be had in honour.

23 Therefore Michal the daughter of Saul had no child unto the day of her death.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

David brings the ark of God up to the city of David, but the journey is interrupted when Uzzah touches the ark and dies, and David leaves it at Obed-edom's house until the blessing there reassures him. The ark finally enters the city with music and dancing, and Michal, watching David dance, despises him for it. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David again gathers all the chosen men of Israel, thirty thousand. Now that David is settled on the throne, Henry notes, the honour of the ark begins to revive after long neglect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. David goes with all the people from Baale of Judah to bring up the ark, named by the name of the Lord of hosts dwelling between the cherubim. The ark is the token of God's presence, and Henry says it typified Christ and his mediation, in which Jehovah's name and glories are displayed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. They set the ark on a new cart from Abinadab's house, driven by Uzzah and Ahio. Henry warns that the priests should have carried it on their shoulders: Philistines might carry it in a cart unpunished, but Israelites who disobey God's appointment do so at their peril. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The ark is brought out with Ahio going before it. The procession honours the ark outwardly while still breaking the appointed manner of carrying it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. David and all Israel play before the Lord on instruments of fir wood, harps, psalteries, timbrels, cornets, and cymbals. Joy before God is expressed with the whole body and every endowment, Henry observes, though joy must be joined to reverence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. At Nachon's threshingfloor the oxen shake the cart, and Uzzah puts forth his hand and takes hold of the ark. Familiarity even with what is most awful, Henry warns, is apt to breed contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The Lord's anger is kindled against Uzzah, and God smites him there for his error, and he dies by the ark. God saw presumption and irreverence in Uzzah's heart, not merely a helpful hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. David is displeased because the Lord made a breach upon Uzzah, and names the place Perezuzzah. The breach interrupts the celebration and teaches that God's holiness is not to be managed by good intentions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. David is afraid of the Lord that day and asks how the ark shall come to him. Fear replaces festivity, and the question is honest: a holy God cannot be brought in on man's terms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. David will not bring the ark into the city of David, but carries it aside into Obededom the Gittite's house. Delay, for David, is better than a second breach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The ark remains in Obededom's house three months, and the Lord blesses Obededom and all his household. The same hand that punished Uzzah's proud presumption rewarded Obededom's humble boldness, and it is good to live in a family that entertains the ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. David hears the Lord blessed Obededom's house because of the ark, and goes to bring it up with gladness. It became evident, Henry says, that happy is the man who has the ark near him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. When the bearers have gone six paces, David sacrifices oxen and fatlings. At first setting out he offered sacrifices to God, for we are likely to speed in our enterprises when we begin with God and seek peace with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. David dances before the Lord with all his might, girded with a linen ephod. He lays aside his royal robes for a plain linen dress, serving God with his whole body and soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. David and all Israel bring up the ark with shouting and the sound of the trumpet. The second procession is marked by worship that has learned reverence from the first failure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Michal, watching from a window, sees David leaping and dancing and despises him in her heart. Exercises of religion appear mean, Henry notes, in the eyes of those who have little or no religion themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The ark is set in the tabernacle David pitched for it, and he offers burnt and peace offerings before the Lord. Our joy in God, Henry reminds, must be connected with repentance and faith in the Redeemer's atoning blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Having finished the offerings, David blesses the people in the name of the Lord of hosts. He prays with and for the people and, as a prophet, solemnly blesses them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. David deals to every person, women and men alike, a cake of bread, a good piece of flesh, and a flagon of wine, and the people depart. The national mercy is shared as a feast, so that all Israel tastes the blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. David returns to bless his household, but Michal meets him mocking how glorious the king was, uncovering himself before his servants' eyes. Even princes' palaces, Henry observes, are not free from family troubles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. David answers that it was before the Lord, who chose him before her father and all his house to rule Israel, that he will play. If we can approve ourselves to God in what we do in religion, Henry says, we need not heed reproach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. David will be yet more vile and base in his own sight, and expects honour from the maidservants Michal mentioned. It is angels' work to worship God, and that cannot lower the greatest of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Therefore Michal has no child to the day of her death. David does not further reprove her, Henry notes, but God punishes her: those who honour God he will honour, but those who despise him and his service shall be lightly esteemed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
