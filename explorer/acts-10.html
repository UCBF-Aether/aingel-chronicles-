The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 There was a certain man in Caesarea called Cornelius, a centurion of the band called the Italian band,

2 A devout man, and one that feared God with all his house, which gave much alms to the people, and prayed to God alway.

3 He saw in a vision evidently about the ninth hour of the day an angel of God coming in to him, and saying unto him, Cornelius.

4 And when he looked on him, he was afraid, and said, What is it, Lord? And he said unto him, Thy prayers and thine alms are come up for a memorial before God.

5 And now send men to Joppa, and call for one Simon, whose surname is Peter:

6 He lodgeth with one Simon a tanner, whose house is by the sea side: he shall tell thee what thou oughtest to do.

7 And when the angel which spake unto Cornelius was departed, he called two of his household servants, and a devout soldier of them that waited on him continually;

8 And when he had declared all these things unto them, he sent them to Joppa.

9 On the morrow, as they went on their journey, and drew nigh unto the city, Peter went up upon the housetop to pray about the sixth hour:

10 And he became very hungry, and would have eaten: but while they made ready, he fell into a trance,

11 And saw heaven opened, and a certain vessel descending upon him, as it had been a great sheet knit at the four corners, and let down to the earth:

12 Wherein were all manner of fourfooted beasts of the earth, and wild beasts, and creeping things, and fowls of the air.

13 And there came a voice to him, Rise, Peter; kill, and eat.

14 But Peter said, Not so, Lord; for I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean.

15 And the voice spake unto him again the second time, What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common.

16 This was done thrice: and the vessel was received up again into heaven.

17 Now while Peter doubted in himself what this vision which he had seen should mean, behold, the men which were sent from Cornelius had made enquiry for Simon’s house, and stood before the gate,

18 And called, and asked whether Simon, which was surnamed Peter, were lodged there.

19 While Peter thought on the vision, the Spirit said unto him, Behold, three men seek thee.

20 Arise therefore, and get thee down, and go with them, doubting nothing: for I have sent them.

21 Then Peter went down to the men which were sent unto him from Cornelius; and said, Behold, I am he whom ye seek: what is the cause wherefore ye are come?

22 And they said, Cornelius the centurion, a just man, and one that feareth God, and of good report among all the nation of the Jews, was warned from God by an holy angel to send for thee into his house, and to hear words of thee.

23 Then called he them in, and lodged them. And on the morrow Peter went away with them, and certain brethren from Joppa accompanied him.

24 And the morrow after they entered into Caesarea. And Cornelius waited for them, and he had called together his kinsmen and near friends.

25 And as Peter was coming in, Cornelius met him, and fell down at his feet, and worshipped him.

26 But Peter took him up, saying, Stand up; I myself also am a man.

27 And as he talked with him, he went in, and found many that were come together.

28 And he said unto them, Ye know how that it is an unlawful thing for a man that is a Jew to keep company, or come unto one of another nation; but God hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean.

29 Therefore came I unto you without gainsaying, as soon as I was sent for: I ask therefore for what intent ye have sent for me?

30 And Cornelius said, Four days ago I was fasting until this hour; and at the ninth hour I prayed in my house, and, behold, a man stood before me in bright clothing,

31 And said, Cornelius, thy prayer is heard, and thine alms are had in remembrance in the sight of God.

32 Send therefore to Joppa, and call hither Simon, whose surname is Peter; he is lodged in the house of one Simon a tanner by the sea side: who, when he cometh, shall speak unto thee.

33 Immediately therefore I sent to thee; and thou hast well done that thou art come. Now therefore are we all here present before God, to hear all things that are commanded thee of God.

34 Then Peter opened his mouth, and said, Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons:

35 But in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him.

36 The word which God sent unto the children of Israel, preaching peace by Jesus Christ: (he is Lord of all:)

37 That word, I say, ye know, which was published throughout all Judaea, and began from Galilee, after the baptism which John preached;

38 How God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power: who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil; for God was with him.

39 And we are witnesses of all things which he did both in the land of the Jews, and in Jerusalem; whom they slew and hanged on a tree:

40 Him God raised up the third day, and shewed him openly;

41 Not to all the people, but unto witnesses chosen before God, even to us, who did eat and drink with him after he rose from the dead.

42 And he commanded us to preach unto the people, and to testify that it is he which was ordained of God to be the Judge of quick and dead.

43 To him give all the prophets witness, that through his name whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins.

44 While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word.

45 And they of the circumcision which believed were astonished, as many as came with Peter, because that on the Gentiles also was poured out the gift of the Holy Ghost.

46 For they heard them speak with tongues, and magnify God. Then answered Peter,

47 Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we?

48 And he commanded them to be baptized in the name of the Lord. Then prayed they him to tarry certain days.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Cornelius, a God-fearing Roman centurion at Caesarea, is told in a vision to send for Peter, while Peter, in a vision of clean and unclean animals, learns not to call any person common or unclean. Peter preaches Christ to Cornelius's household, the Holy Spirit falls on these Gentile hearers as it had on the Jews, and Peter commands them to be baptized. Interpreted Overview of this chapter's text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. There was a certain man in Caesarea called Cornelius, a centurion of the band called the Italian band. Henry marks the turning point: hitherto none had been baptized into the Christian church but Jews, Samaritans, and converts who had been circumcised and observed the ceremonial law, but now the Gentiles were to be called to partake all the privileges of God's people, without first becoming Jews, and pure and undefiled religion is sometimes found where we least expect it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. A devout man, and one that feared God with all his house, which gave much alms to the people, and prayed to God alway. Henry says wherever the fear of God rules in the heart, it will appear both in works of charity and of piety, neither excusing the other, and Cornelius had true faith in God's word as far as he understood it, though not as yet clear faith in Christ, this being the work of the Spirit of God through the mediation of Jesus, even before Cornelius knew him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He saw in a vision evidently about the ninth hour of the day an angel of God coming in to him, and saying unto him, Cornelius. Henry's lesson for the whole direction is obedience: without dispute or delay Cornelius was obedient to the heavenly vision, and in the affairs of our souls, let us not lose time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. When he looked on him, he was afraid, and said, What is it, Lord? And he said unto him, Thy prayers and thine alms are come up for a memorial before God. Henry adds the ground of acceptance: through Christ also his prayers and alms were accepted, which otherwise would have been rejected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And now send men to Joppa, and call for one Simon, whose surname is Peter. Henry's obedience point governs the sending: without dispute or delay Cornelius was obedient to the heavenly vision, and in the affairs of our souls we should not lose time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When he had declared all these things unto them, he sent them to Joppa. Henry repeats the obedience: without dispute or delay Cornelius acted on the heavenly vision, declaring it fully to his household and sending at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. There came a voice to him, Rise, Peter; kill, and eat. Henry explains the force of the command: to tell a Jew that God had directed those animals to be reckoned clean which were hitherto deemed unclean was in effect saying that the law of Moses was done away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. But Peter said, Not so, Lord; for I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean. Henry names the obstacle plainly: the prejudices of Peter against the Gentiles would have prevented his going to Cornelius, unless the Lord had prepared him for this service, and his refusal shows the prejudice at work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The voice spake unto him again the second time, What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common. Henry says Peter was soon made to know the meaning of it: what God has cleansed is not to be called common, and the vision prepares him for a service his prejudices would have refused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. This was done thrice, and the vessel was received up again into heaven. Henry's preparation point is the repetition itself: God knows what services are before us, and how to prepare us, and the thrice-repeated vision drives the cleansing home before the messengers arrive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Now while Peter doubted in himself what this vision which he had seen should mean, behold, the men which were sent from Cornelius had made enquiry for Simon's house, and stood before the gate. Henry says we know the meaning of what God has taught us when we find what occasion we have to make use of it, and the men at the gate are that occasion arriving while Peter still doubts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. While Peter thought on the vision, the Spirit said unto him, Behold, three men seek thee. Henry's preparation is completed by the Spirit's word: God knows what services are before us and how to prepare us, and the Spirit joins the vision to the men at the gate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Arise therefore, and get thee down, and go with them, doubting nothing: for I have sent them. Henry's rule for a clear call fits: when we see our call clear to any service, we should not be perplexed with doubts and scruples arising from prejudices or former ideas. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. They said, Cornelius the centurion, a just man, and one that feareth God, and of good report among all the nation of the Jews, was warned from God by an holy angel to send for thee into his house, and to hear words of thee. Henry's later summary of Cornelius's side is the declaration of the direction God gave him to send for Peter, and the messengers' report carries both his character and that heavenly warning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. The morrow after they entered into Caesarea. And Cornelius waited for them, and he had called together his kinsmen and near friends. Henry commends the gathering: Cornelius had called together his friends to partake with him of the heavenly wisdom he expected from Peter, for we should not covet to eat our spiritual morsels alone, and it is kindness and respect to kindred and friends to invite them to join us in religious exercises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. As he talked with him, he went in, and found many that were come together. Henry's invited company is this many: Cornelius called his kinsmen and near friends to partake of the heavenly wisdom he expected, and Peter finds a company gathered, not a solitary hearer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. He said unto them, Ye know how that it is an unlawful thing for a man that is a Jew to keep company, or come unto one of another nation; but God hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean. Henry says Peter was soon made to know the meaning of the vision, and here he applies it: the cleansing of the animals meant the cleansing of men, and his prejudice against the Gentiles gives way to God's showing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Therefore came I unto you without gainsaying, as soon as I was sent for: I ask therefore for what intent ye have sent for me? Henry's rule is fulfilled in the phrase without gainsaying: when our call is clear, we should not be perplexed with doubts and scruples arising from prejudices or former ideas. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Cornelius said, Four days ago I was fasting until this hour, and at the ninth hour I prayed in my house, and, behold, a man stood before me in bright clothing. Henry says Cornelius declared the direction God gave him to send for Peter, and we are right in our aims in attending a gospel ministry when we do it with regard to the divine appointment requiring us to make use of that ordinance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And said, Cornelius, thy prayer is heard, and thine alms are had in remembrance in the sight of God. Henry's ground from the chapter's opening returns: through Christ his prayers and alms were accepted, which otherwise would have been rejected, and the angel's word confirms that acceptance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Immediately therefore I sent to thee, and thou hast well done that thou art come. Now therefore are we all here present before God, to hear all things that are commanded thee of God. Henry marvels at the company: how seldom ministers are called to speak to such companies, however small, in which it may be said that they are all present in the sight of God, to hear all things that are commanded of God, but these were ready to hear what Peter was commanded to say. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Then Peter opened his mouth, and said, Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons. Henry states the ground and the limit together: acceptance cannot be obtained on any other ground than that of the covenant of mercy through the atonement of Christ, but wherever true religion is found, God will accept it without regarding names or sects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. But in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him. Henry explains that the fear of God and works of righteousness are the substance of true religion, the effects of special grace, and though these are not the cause of a man's acceptance, yet they show it, and whatever may be wanting in knowledge or faith will in due time be given by him who has begun it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. The word which God sent unto the children of Israel, preaching peace by Jesus Christ: he is Lord of all. Henry gives the purport of the word: God by it published the good tidings of peace by Jesus Christ, and let them know that this Jesus, by whom peace is made between God and man, is Lord of all, not only as over all, God blessed for evermore, but as Mediator, with all power in heaven and earth put into his hand and all judgment committed to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. That word, I say, ye know, which was published throughout all Judaea, and began from Galilee, after the baptism which John preached. Henry notes what they already knew: they knew in general the word, the gospel God sent to the children of Israel, they knew the several matters of fact relating to the gospel, and they knew the baptism of repentance which John preached. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. How God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power, who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil, for God was with him. Henry's assurance for the anointed is that God will go with those whom he anoints, and he will be with those to whom he has given his Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. And we are witnesses of all things which he did both in the land of the Jews, and in Jerusalem, whom they slew and hanged on a tree. Henry grounds faith in testimony: faith has reference to a testimony, and the Christian faith is built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, on the testimony given by them, and Peter's witness is that testimony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Him God raised up the third day, and shewed him openly. Henry's centre of the discourse is the resurrection declared with its proofs: Peter declares Christ's resurrection from the dead and the proofs of it, and the sermon turns on that fact. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Not to all the people, but unto witnesses chosen before God, even to us, who did eat and drink with him after he rose from the dead. Henry's testimony point continues: the Christian faith is built on the testimony of apostles and prophets, and these chosen witnesses, who ate and drank with the risen Lord, are its foundation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. He commanded us to preach unto the people, and to testify that it is he which was ordained of God to be the Judge of quick and dead. Henry draws the accountability: we are all accountable to Christ as our Judge, so every one must seek his favour and have him as our Friend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. To him give all the prophets witness, that through his name whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins. Henry closes the sermon on remission: if we believe in him, we shall all be justified by him as our Righteousness, and the remission of sins lays a foundation for all other favours and blessings, by taking out of the way that which hinders the bestowing of them, for if sin be pardoned, all is well and shall end well for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word. Henry marks the order as the point: the Holy Ghost fell upon these Gentiles before they were baptized, to show that God does not confine himself to outward signs, for it is the Spirit that quickeneth, and the flesh profiteth nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. They of the circumcision which believed were astonished, as many as came with Peter, because that on the Gentiles also was poured out the gift of the Holy Ghost. Henry explains the astonishment: by mistaken notions of things, we make difficult for ourselves as to the methods of divine providence and grace, and the believing Jews had not expected the gift on the Gentiles also. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. For they heard them speak with tongues, and magnify God. Then answered Peter. Henry says they magnified God and spake of Christ and the benefits of redemption, and whatever gift we are endued with, we ought to honour God with it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we? Henry calls the argument conclusive: as they were undeniably baptized with the Holy Ghost, Peter concluded they were not to be refused the baptism of water, for can we deny the sign to those who have received the things signified? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. He commanded them to be baptized in the name of the Lord. Then prayed they him to tarry certain days. Henry adds the ongoing need: those who have some acquaintance with Christ cannot but desire more, and even those who have received the Holy Ghost must see their need of daily learning more of the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • centurion. A Roman officer commanding about a hundred soldiers; Cornelius commands the band called the Italian band. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • Italian band. The cohort, or company of soldiers, to which Cornelius belonged at Caesarea. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • common or unclean. In Jewish law, foods and persons regarded as ritually impure; Peter's vision overturns that distinction for persons. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

  • Verse 37. That word, I say, ye know, which was published throughout all Judaea, and began from Galilee, after the baptism which John preached. Henry notes what they already knew: they knew in general the word, the gospel God sent to the children of Israel, they knew the several matters of fact relating to the gospel, and they knew the baptism of repentance which John preached. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. How God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power, who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil, for God was with him. Henry's assurance for the anointed is that God will go with those whom he anoints, and he will be with those to whom he has given his Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. And we are witnesses of all things which he did both in the land of the Jews, and in Jerusalem, whom they slew and hanged on a tree. Henry grounds faith in testimony: faith has reference to a testimony, and the Christian faith is built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, on the testimony given by them, and Peter's witness is that testimony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Him God raised up the third day, and shewed him openly. Henry's centre of the discourse is the resurrection declared with its proofs: Peter declares Christ's resurrection from the dead and the proofs of it, and the sermon turns on that fact. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Not to all the people, but unto witnesses chosen before God, even to us, who did eat and drink with him after he rose from the dead. Henry's testimony point continues: the Christian faith is built on the testimony of apostles and prophets, and these chosen witnesses, who ate and drank with the risen Lord, are its foundation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. He commanded us to preach unto the people, and to testify that it is he which was ordained of God to be the Judge of quick and dead. Henry draws the accountability: we are all accountable to Christ as our Judge, so every one must seek his favour and have him as our Friend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. To him give all the prophets witness, that through his name whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins. Henry closes the sermon on remission: if we believe in him, we shall all be justified by him as our Righteousness, and the remission of sins lays a foundation for all other favours and blessings, by taking out of the way that which hinders the bestowing of them, for if sin be pardoned, all is well and shall end well for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word. Henry marks the order as the point: the Holy Ghost fell upon these Gentiles before they were baptized, to show that God does not confine himself to outward signs, for it is the Spirit that quickeneth, and the flesh profiteth nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. They of the circumcision which believed were astonished, as many as came with Peter, because that on the Gentiles also was poured out the gift of the Holy Ghost. Henry explains the astonishment: by mistaken notions of things, we make difficult for ourselves as to the methods of divine providence and grace, and the believing Jews had not expected the gift on the Gentiles also. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. For they heard them speak with tongues, and magnify God. Then answered Peter. Henry says they magnified God and spake of Christ and the benefits of redemption, and whatever gift we are endued with, we ought to honour God with it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we? Henry calls the argument conclusive: as they were undeniably baptized with the Holy Ghost, Peter concluded they were not to be refused the baptism of water, for can we deny the sign to those who have received the things signified? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. He commanded them to be baptized in the name of the Lord. Then prayed they him to tarry certain days. Henry adds the ongoing need: those who have some acquaintance with Christ cannot but desire more, and even those who have received the Holy Ghost must see their need of daily learning more of the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
