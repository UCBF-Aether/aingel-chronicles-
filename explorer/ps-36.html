The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 36 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 The transgression of the wicked saith within my heart, that there is no fear of God before his eyes.

2 For he flattereth himself in his own eyes, until his iniquity be found to be hateful.

3 The words of his mouth are iniquity and deceit: he hath left off to be wise, and to do good.

4 He deviseth mischief upon his bed; he setteth himself in a way that is not good; he abhorreth not evil.

5 Thy mercy, O LORD, is in the heavens; and thy faithfulness reacheth unto the clouds.

6 Thy righteousness is like the great mountains; thy judgments are a great deep: O LORD, thou preservest man and beast.

7 How excellent is thy lovingkindness, O God! therefore the children of men put their trust under the shadow of thy wings.

8 They shall be abundantly satisfied with the fatness of thy house; and thou shalt make them drink of the river of thy pleasures.

9 For with thee is the fountain of life: in thy light shall we see light.

10 O continue thy lovingkindness unto them that know thee; and thy righteousness to the upright in heart.

11 Let not the foot of pride come against me, and let not the hand of the wicked remove me.

12 There are the workers of iniquity fallen: they are cast down, and shall not be able to rise.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The wicked's portrait against God's vastness: no fear of God, self-flattery, deceitful speech, mischief devised on his bed, set against mercy in the heavens, faithfulness to the clouds, righteousness like mountains, judgments like the deep. Under the shadow of wings, men drink from the river of pleasures, for with God is the fountain of life. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The transgression of the wicked says within my heart that there is no fear of God before his eyes: the wicked man's sin itself testifies — his life declares his godlessness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He flatters himself in his own eyes, until his iniquity is found hateful: self-flattery blinds him until his sin, long hidden, is exposed as hateful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The words of his mouth are iniquity and deceit; he has left off being wise and doing good: speech betrays him, and he has ceased both wisdom and goodness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He devises mischief on his bed, sets himself in a way not good, and abhors not evil: night plotting, a chosen bad way, and no hatred of evil complete the portrait. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Your mercy, Lord, is in the heavens, and your faithfulness reaches to the clouds: against man's small wickedness stands a mercy as high as heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Your righteousness is like the great mountains, your judgments a great deep; Lord, you preserve man and beast: righteousness is mountain-firm, judgments ocean-deep, and preservation extends even to beasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. How excellent is your lovingkindness, O God! Therefore the children of men trust under the shadow of your wings: excellent lovingkindness is itself the reason men shelter under God's wings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They shall be abundantly satisfied with the fatness of your house, and drink of the river of your pleasures: God's house provides a feast and a river — abundance, not scarcity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. For with you is the fountain of life; in your light shall we see light: life's fountain is in God, and only in his light is any other light truly seen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. O continue your lovingkindness to those who know you, and your righteousness to the upright in heart: continuance is prayed for — the known God and the upright heart are the recipients. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Let not the foot of pride come against me, nor the hand of the wicked remove me: pride's foot and the wicked's hand are the two threats to his standing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. There the workers of iniquity are fallen; cast down, they shall not rise: the fall is already seen as accomplished — cast down beyond rising. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
