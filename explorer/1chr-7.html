The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the sons of Issachar were, Tola, and Puah, Jashub, and Shimrom, four.

2 And the sons of Tola; Uzzi, and Rephaiah, and Jeriel, and Jahmai, and Jibsam, and Shemuel, heads of their father’s house, to wit, of Tola: they were valiant men of might in their generations; whose number was in the days of David two and twenty thousand and six hundred.

3 And the sons of Uzzi; Izrahiah: and the sons of Izrahiah; Michael, and Obadiah, and Joel, Ishiah, five: all of them chief men.

4 And with them, by their generations, after the house of their fathers, were bands of soldiers for war, six and thirty thousand men: for they had many wives and sons.

5 And their brethren among all the families of Issachar were valiant men of might, reckoned in all by their genealogies fourscore and seven thousand.

6 The sons of Benjamin; Bela, and Becher, and Jediael, three.

7 And the sons of Bela; Ezbon, and Uzzi, and Uzziel, and Jerimoth, and Iri, five; heads of the house of their fathers, mighty men of valour; and were reckoned by their genealogies twenty and two thousand and thirty and four.

8 And the sons of Becher; Zemira, and Joash, and Eliezer, and Elioenai, and Omri, and Jerimoth, and Abiah, and Anathoth, and Alameth. All these are the sons of Becher.

9 And the number of them, after their genealogy by their generations, heads of the house of their fathers, mighty men of valour, was twenty thousand and two hundred.

10 The sons also of Jediael; Bilhan: and the sons of Bilhan; Jeush, and Benjamin, and Ehud, and Chenaanah, and Zethan, and Tharshish, and Ahishahar.

11 All these the sons of Jediael, by the heads of their fathers, mighty men of valour, were seventeen thousand and two hundred soldiers, fit to go out for war and battle.

12 Shuppim also, and Huppim, the children of Ir, and Hushim, the sons of Aher.

13 The sons of Naphtali; Jahziel, and Guni, and Jezer, and Shallum, the sons of Bilhah.

14 The sons of Manasseh; Ashriel, whom she bare: (but his concubine the Aramitess bare Machir the father of Gilead:

15 And Machir took to wife the sister of Huppim and Shuppim, whose sister’s name was Maachah;) and the name of the second was Zelophehad: and Zelophehad had daughters.

16 And Maachah the wife of Machir bare a son, and she called his name Peresh; and the name of his brother was Sheresh; and his sons were Ulam and Rakem.

17 And the sons of Ulam; Bedan. These were the sons of Gilead, the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh.

18 And his sister Hammoleketh bare Ishod, and Abiezer, and Mahalah.

19 And the sons of Shemidah were, Ahian, and Shechem, and Likhi, and Aniam.

20 And the sons of Ephraim; Shuthelah, and Bered his son, and Tahath his son, and Eladah his son, and Tahath his son,

21 And Zabad his son, and Shuthelah his son, and Ezer, and Elead, whom the men of Gath that were born in that land slew, because they came down to take away their cattle.

22 And Ephraim their father mourned many days, and his brethren came to comfort him.

23 And when he went in to his wife, she conceived, and bare a son, and he called his name Beriah, because it went evil with his house.

24 (And his daughter was Sherah, who built Bethhoron the nether, and the upper, and Uzzensherah.)

25 And Rephah was his son, also Resheph, and Telah his son, and Tahan his son.

26 Laadan his son, Ammihud his son, Elishama his son.

27 Non his son, Jehoshuah his son.

28 And their possessions and habitations were, Bethel and the towns thereof, and eastward Naaran, and westward Gezer, with the towns thereof; Shechem also and the towns thereof, unto Gaza and the towns thereof:

29 And by the borders of the children of Manasseh, Bethshean and her towns, Taanach and her towns, Megiddo and her towns, Dor and her towns. In these dwelt the children of Joseph the son of Israel.

30 The sons of Asher; Imnah, and Isuah, and Ishuai, and Beriah, and Serah their sister.

31 And the sons of Beriah; Heber, and Malchiel, who is the father of Birzavith.

32 And Heber begat Japhlet, and Shomer, and Hotham, and Shua their sister.

33 And the sons of Japhlet; Pasach, and Bimhal, and Ashvath. These are the children of Japhlet.

34 And the sons of Shamer; Ahi, and Rohgah, Jehubbah, and Aram.

35 And the sons of his brother Helem; Zophah, and Imna, and Shelesh, and Amal.

36 The sons of Zophah; Suah, and Harnepher, and Shual, and Beri, and Imrah,

37 Bezer, and Hod, and Shamma, and Shilshah, and Ithran, and Beera.

38 And the sons of Jether; Jephunneh, and Pispah, and Ara.

39 And the sons of Ulla; Arah, and Haniel, and Rezia.

40 All these were the children of Asher, heads of their father’s house, choice and mighty men of valour, chief of the princes. And the number throughout the genealogy of them that were apt to the war and to battle was twenty and six thousand men.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter lists the northern and eastern tribes — Issachar, Benjamin, Naphtali, Manasseh, Ephraim, and Asher — with Ephraim's family breach and its repair through Joshua son of Nun. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The sons of Issachar were Tola, Puah, Jashub, and Shimrom. Henry compares Issachar to Jacob's strong ass couching between burdens, an industrious tribe that minded country business and rejoiced in its tents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Tola's sons were heads of their father's house, valiant men of might, 22,600 in David's days. Henry notes those inured to labour and business were fittest to serve their country when occasion arose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Their brethren among all Issachar's families were valiant men of might, 87,000 in all. Henry totals Issachar's fighting men above 145,000, a numerous tribe that saw no danger of over-stocking its fruitful pasture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Bela's sons were heads of their father's house, mighty men of valour, 22,034 by genealogy. Henry notes Benjamin's militia scarcely reached 60,000, yet they were mighty men, and the tribe's honour was adhering to David's house when others revolted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Their number, heads of fathers' houses and mighty men of valour, was 20,200. Henry cites Benjamin's counts as proof of a small but valiant tribe, ravening as a wolf as Jacob foretold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The sons of Jediael, by their fathers' heads, mighty men of valour, were 17,200 soldiers fit for war. Henry completes Benjamin's militia account with this third muster. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Shuppim and Huppim, children of Ir, and Hushim, sons of Aher, close Benjamin's short list. Henry reads Aher appellatively, sons of another, since Hushim is elsewhere Dan's son, a hint at Dan's estrangement after setting up idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The sons of Naphtali were Jahziel, Guni, Jezer, and Shallum, sons of Bilhah. Henry notes only the first fathers are named, the same as in Genesis except Shillem there is Shallum here, and no descendants, perhaps because their genealogies were lost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Manasseh's son Ashriel was borne, but his Aramitess concubine bore Machir, father of Gilead. Henry notes one Manassite married a Syrian during the Egyptian bondage, so early did they begin to mingle with the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Machir took to wife the sister of Huppim and Shuppim, Maachah, and Zelophehad had daughters. Henry notes Machir, son of that mixed marriage, took a Benjaminite wife, a warning for children not to stumble at their fathers' stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The sons of Ulam were Bedan, of Gilead, son of Machir, son of Manasseh. Henry suggests this Bedan may be the deliverer named among Israel's judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Ezer and Elead, sons of Ephraim's line, were slain by the men of Gath born in that land, because they came down to take away their cattle. Henry records the dispute over who were aggressors, and JFB notes the episode, before the Exodus, shows Hebrew shepherds raided by Gathites or rashly raiding themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Ephraim their father mourned many days, and his brethren came to comfort him. Henry calls it the burden of old age to follow the young to the grave, and notes the brethren's comfort probably recalled Jacob's promise of increase. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. He went in to his wife, she bore a son, and he called him Beriah, because it went evil with his house. Henry compares this to Seth, another seed after Abel, and notes the melancholy name keeps affliction in remembrance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. His daughter was Sherah, who built Beth-horon the nether and the upper, and Uzzen-sherah. Henry honours her as proof a virtuous woman may be as great a blessing to a family as a mighty man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Non his son, Jehoshuah his son. Henry notes Joshua son of Nun, of Ephraim, was employed in the conquest of Canaan, further repairing the breach made on Ephraim's family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. All Asher's children were heads of fathers' houses, choice and mighty men of valour, chief of the princes, 26,000 apt for war. Henry notes Asher's militia was not numerous compared with others, but its princes were choice, preferring a few serviceable men to a large trained band. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Connections to other passages

  • The study note for verse 13 connects this passage to the Genesis list of Naphtali's sons, where Shillem stands for Shallum here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • The study note for verse 27 connects this passage to the Joshua account of the conquest of Canaan, in which Joshua son of Nun was employed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
