The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 25 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Amaziah was twenty and five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned twenty and nine years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Jehoaddan of Jerusalem.

2 And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, but not with a perfect heart.

3 Now it came to pass, when the kingdom was established to him, that he slew his servants that had killed the king his father.

4 But he slew not their children, but did as it is written in the law in the book of Moses, where the LORD commanded, saying, The fathers shall not die for the children, neither shall the children die for the fathers, but every man shall die for his own sin.

5 Moreover Amaziah gathered Judah together, and made them captains over thousands, and captains over hundreds, according to the houses of their fathers, throughout all Judah and Benjamin: and he numbered them from twenty years old and above, and found them three hundred thousand choice men, able to go forth to war, that could handle spear and shield.

6 He hired also an hundred thousand mighty men of valour out of Israel for an hundred talents of silver.

7 But there came a man of God to him, saying, O king, let not the army of Israel go with thee; for the LORD is not with Israel, to wit, with all the children of Ephraim.

8 But if thou wilt go, do it; be strong for the battle: God shall make thee fall before the enemy: for God hath power to help, and to cast down.

9 And Amaziah said to the man of God, But what shall we do for the hundred talents which I have given to the army of Israel? And the man of God answered, The LORD is able to give thee much more than this.

10 Then Amaziah separated them, to wit, the army that was come to him out of Ephraim, to go home again: wherefore their anger was greatly kindled against Judah, and they returned home in great anger.

11 And Amaziah strengthened himself, and led forth his people, and went to the valley of salt, and smote of the children of Seir ten thousand.

12 And other ten thousand left alive did the children of Judah carry away captive, and brought them unto the top of the rock, and cast them down from the top of the rock, that they all were broken in pieces.

13 But the soldiers of the army which Amaziah sent back, that they should not go with him to battle, fell upon the cities of Judah, from Samaria even unto Bethhoron, and smote three thousand of them, and took much spoil.

14 Now it came to pass, after that Amaziah was come from the slaughter of the Edomites, that he brought the gods of the children of Seir, and set them up to be his gods, and bowed down himself before them, and burned incense unto them.

15 Wherefore the anger of the LORD was kindled against Amaziah, and he sent unto him a prophet, which said unto him, Why hast thou sought after the gods of the people, which could not deliver their own people out of thine hand?

16 And it came to pass, as he talked with him, that the king said unto him, Art thou made of the king’s counsel? forbear; why shouldest thou be smitten? Then the prophet forbare, and said, I know that God hath determined to destroy thee, because thou hast done this, and hast not hearkened unto my counsel.

17 Then Amaziah king of Judah took advice, and sent to Joash, the son of Jehoahaz, the son of Jehu, king of Israel, saying, Come, let us see one another in the face.

18 And Joash king of Israel sent to Amaziah king of Judah, saying, The thistle that was in Lebanon sent to the cedar that was in Lebanon, saying, Give thy daughter to my son to wife: and there passed by a wild beast that was in Lebanon, and trode down the thistle.

19 Thou sayest, Lo, thou hast smitten the Edomites; and thine heart lifteth thee up to boast: abide now at home; why shouldest thou meddle to thine hurt, that thou shouldest fall, even thou, and Judah with thee?

20 But Amaziah would not hear; for it came of God, that he might deliver them into the hand of their enemies, because they sought after the gods of Edom.

21 So Joash the king of Israel went up; and they saw one another in the face, both he and Amaziah king of Judah, at Bethshemesh, which belongeth to Judah.

22 And Judah was put to the worse before Israel, and they fled every man to his tent.

23 And Joash the king of Israel took Amaziah king of Judah, the son of Joash, the son of Jehoahaz, at Bethshemesh, and brought him to Jerusalem, and brake down the wall of Jerusalem from the gate of Ephraim to the corner gate, four hundred cubits.

24 And he took all the gold and the silver, and all the vessels that were found in the house of God with Obededom, and the treasures of the king’s house, the hostages also, and returned to Samaria.

25 And Amaziah the son of Joash king of Judah lived after the death of Joash son of Jehoahaz king of Israel fifteen years.

26 Now the rest of the acts of Amaziah, first and last, behold, are they not written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel?

27 Now after the time that Amaziah did turn away from following the LORD they made a conspiracy against him in Jerusalem; and he fled to Lachish: but they sent to Lachish after him, and slew him there.

28 And they brought him upon horses, and buried him with his fathers in the city of Judah.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Amaziah's victory over Edom, his dismissal of hired Israelite troops, his turn to Edom's gods, his defeat by Joash of Israel, and his eventual slaying at Lachish. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Amaziah was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Jehoaddan of Jerusalem. Henry notes his reign was not one of the worse and yet far from good, covering the passages already related more briefly in Kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And he did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, but not with a perfect heart. Henry notes he worshipped the true God and countenanced religion, but he was not a man of serious piety or devotion, nor zealous for religion's exercises, a cool and indifferent friend to it, doing good but not with the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Now it came to pass, when the kingdom was established to him, that he slew his servants that had killed the king his father. Henry notes Amaziah did what became him in calling his father's murderers to account, a necessary piece of justice upon the traitors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. But he slew not their children, but did as written in the law in Moses' book, where the Lord commanded, the fathers shall not die for the children, neither the children for the fathers, but every man shall die for his own sin. Henry notes he forbade putting children to death for parents' sin, for even if the murderers claimed to avenge the prophet, they presumptuously took God's work out of his hands and their wickedness was not justified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Moreover Amaziah gathered Judah together, and made captains over thousands and hundreds, according to their fathers' houses, throughout Judah and Benjamin, and he numbered them from twenty years old and above, and found them three hundred thousand choice men, able to go forth to war, that could handle spear and shield. Henry notes he mustered and marshalled his forces, finding Judah and Benjamin but 300,000 fit for war, whereas in Jehoshaphat's time, fifty or sixty years before, they were four times as many, for sin weakens a people, diminishes and dispirits them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He hired also an hundred thousand mighty men of valour out of Israel for an hundred talents of silver. Henry notes finding his kingdom defective in men, he thought to make up the deficiency with money, taking 100,000 Israelites into pay, though had he advised with a prophet or considered how little his ancestors got by alliances with Israel, he would not have had this to undo, for rashness makes work for repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. But there came a man of God to him, saying, O king, let not the army of Israel go with thee, for the Lord is not with Israel, to wit, with all the children of Ephraim. Henry notes God sent a prophet commanding him to dismiss Israel's forces: he would not have him call in any assistance at all, for it looked like distrust of God, and particularly not theirs, for the Lord is not with Ephraim because they are not with him but worship calves, so no good could be expected from those without God with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. But if thou wilt go, do it, be strong for the battle: God shall make thee fall before the enemy, for God hath power to help, and to cast down. Henry notes the prophet assured him that if he persisted in taking idolatrous Israelites, they would prove a dead weight, sink and betray his army, for God has power to help without them and to cast down though he has them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And Amaziah said to the man of God, But what shall we do for the hundred talents which I have given to the army of Israel? And the man of God answered, The Lord is able to give thee much more than this. Henry notes this is the objection men often make against duty, fearing loss, and the prophet's answer stands: the Lord is able to give much more, and will not see thee lose by him, for he is just, good and solvent, and a firm belief of God's all-sufficiency will make his yoke easy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Then Amaziah separated them, to wit, the army come out of Ephraim, to go home again, wherefore their anger was greatly kindled against Judah, and they returned home in great anger. Henry notes to his honour he would rather lose his money, disoblige allies and dismiss a fourth part of his army as they took the field, than offend God, while the dismissed men went home in great anger at the affront to profit and reputation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And Amaziah strengthened himself, and led forth his people, and went to the valley of salt, and smote of the children of Seir ten thousand. Henry notes his triumph over Edom: he left 10,000 dead on the field of battle in the valley of salt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And other ten thousand left alive did Judah's children carry away captive, and brought them unto the top of the rock, and cast them down from the top of the rock, that they all were broken in pieces. Henry notes 10,000 more were taken prisoners and barbarously killed by being thrown down a steep, craggy precipice, a cruelty certainly very severe, for which no provocation is told. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. But the soldiers of the army which Amaziah sent back, that they should not go with him to battle, fell upon Judah's cities, from Samaria even unto Bethhoron, and smote three thousand of them, and took much spoil. Henry notes the disbanded soldiers, enraged at being sent home, made a prey of Judah, plundering border cities and killing 3,000, and God doubtless intended hereby to chastise those cities for idolatries learned from Israel, who had corrupted them and now were made a plague to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Now it came to pass, after Amaziah was come from the slaughter of the Edomites, that he brought the gods of the children of Seir, and set them up to be his gods, and bowed down himself before them, and burned incense unto them. Henry notes egregious folly: to worship the gods of those he had conquered, who could not protect their own worshippers, was the greatest absurdity, and if he had broken the idols instead of the prisoners he would have shown more piety and pity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Wherefore the anger of the Lord was kindled against Amaziah, and he sent unto him a prophet, which said unto him, Why hast thou sought after the gods of the people, which could not deliver their own people out of thine hand? Henry notes God, before sending to destroy him, sent to convince and reclaim him, and the prophet reasoned fairly and mildly: why seek the favour of gods that could not deliver their own people, for if men considered the inability of what they resort to when forsaking God, they would not be such enemies to themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And it came to pass, as he talked with him, that the king said unto him, Art thou made of the king's counsel? forbear, why shouldest thou be smitten? Then the prophet forbare, and said, I know that God hath determined to destroy thee, because thou hast done this, and hast not hearkened unto my counsel. Henry notes he could say nothing in excuse, but fell into passion, taunting the prophet as meddling, silencing him and threatening him, reminding him of Zechariah's fate, and so making himself guilty of both prophets' blood, while the prophet forbore, saying God hath determined to destroy thee, for those deaf to reproof ripen apace for destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Then Amaziah king of Judah took advice, and sent to Joash, son of Jehoahaz, son of Jehu, king of Israel, saying, Come, let us see one another in the face. Henry notes Amaziah took advice before challenging Israel, but not of the prophet, whom he had silenced, but of statesmen who would flatter him and bid him go up and prosper, for those who will not take advice from God's word are justly left to bad advisers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And Joash king of Israel sent to Amaziah king of Judah, saying, The thistle in Lebanon sent to the cedar in Lebanon, saying, Give thy daughter to my son to wife, and there passed by a wild beast in Lebanon, and trode down the thistle. Henry notes Joash answered with haughty scorn, the thistle and cedar parable, and friendly advice to sit still and know when he was well off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Thou sayest, Lo, thou hast smitten the Edomites, and thine heart lifteth thee up to boast: abide now at home, why shouldest thou meddle to thine hurt, that thou shouldest fall, even thou, and Judah with thee? Henry notes Joash's friendly advice was to abide at home and not meddle to his hurt, lest he fall with Judah, for a man's pride will bring him low and he that goes forth hastily to strive will not know what to do in the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. But Amaziah would not hear, for it came of God, that he might deliver them into the hand of their enemies, because they sought after the gods of Edom. Henry notes Amaziah wilfully persisted, and his imprudence was made the punishment of his impiety, for it was of the Lord: he left him to himself to act foolishly, that he and his people might be delivered to enemies, because they had forsaken God for Edom's gods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. So Joash king of Israel went up, and they saw one another in the face, both he and Amaziah king of Judah, at Bethshemesh, which belongeth to Judah. Henry notes the wilful challenge came to battle at Beth-shemesh, where Amaziah's pride was thoroughly mortified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And Judah was put to the worse before Israel, and they fled every man to his tent. Henry notes defeat was given him as Solomon's proverbs verify: pride goes before destruction and is often its immediate occasion, and he that exalteth himself shall be abased. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And Joash king of Israel took Amaziah king of Judah, son of Joash, son of Jehoahaz, at Bethshemesh, and brought him to Jerusalem, and brake down Jerusalem's wall from the gate of Ephraim to the corner gate, four hundred cubits. Henry notes the calamity he brought on himself and his city thereby: captured at Beth-shemesh, brought to Jerusalem, and the wall broken down four hundred cubits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And he took all the gold and silver, and all the vessels found in God's house with Obededom, and the treasures of the king's house, the hostages also, and returned to Samaria. Henry notes Joash took the temple's gold and silver and vessels, the king's treasures and hostages, and returned to Samaria, the full price of Amaziah's rashness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And Amaziah son of Joash king of Judah lived after the death of Joash son of Jehoahaz king of Israel fifteen years. Henry notes Amaziah lived fifteen years after Joash of Israel died, ending his days ingloriously, for his subjects' hearts had departed from him from the time he departed from the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Now the rest of the acts of Amaziah, first and last, behold, are they not written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel? Henry notes the rest of his acts are referred to the book of the kings of Judah and Israel, as with other kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Now after the time that Amaziah did turn away from following the Lord they made a conspiracy against him in Jerusalem, and he fled to Lachish, but they sent to Lachish after him, and slew him there. Henry notes from the time he departed from the Lord his subjects' hearts departed from him and formed a design in Jerusalem, exasperated perhaps more by his rash war than his idolatry, and though he fled to Lachish as private or strong place, they sent after him and slew him there deliberately. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And they brought him upon horses, and buried him with his fathers in the city of Judah. Henry notes he was brought upon horses and buried with his fathers in the city of Judah, ending a reign not one of the worse yet far from good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry notes from the time he departed from the Lord his subjects' hearts departed from him and formed a design in Jerusalem, exasperated perhaps more by his rash war than his idolatry, and though he fled to Lachish as private or strong place, they sent after him and slew him there deliberately. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And they brought him upon horses, and buried him with his fathers in the city of Judah. Henry notes he was brought upon horses and buried with his fathers in the city of Judah, ending a reign not one of the worse yet far from good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
