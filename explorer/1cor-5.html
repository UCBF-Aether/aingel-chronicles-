The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Corinthians 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 It is reported commonly that there is fornication among you, and such fornication as is not so much as named among the Gentiles, that one should have his father’s wife.

2 And ye are puffed up, and have not rather mourned, that he that hath done this deed might be taken away from among you.

3 For I verily, as absent in body, but present in spirit, have judged already, as though I were present, concerning him that hath so done this deed,

4 In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, when ye are gathered together, and my spirit, with the power of our Lord Jesus Christ,

5 To deliver such an one unto Satan for the destruction of the flesh, that the spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord Jesus.

6 Your glorying is not good. Know ye not that a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump?

7 Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us:

8 Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

9 I wrote unto you in an epistle not to company with fornicators:

10 Yet not altogether with the fornicators of this world, or with the covetous, or extortioners, or with idolaters; for then must ye needs go out of the world.

11 But now I have written unto you not to keep company, if any man that is called a brother be a fornicator, or covetous, or an idolater, or a railer, or a drunkard, or an extortioner; with such an one no not to eat.

12 For what have I to do to judge them also that are without? do not ye judge them that are within?

13 But them that are without God judgeth. Therefore put away from among yourselves that wicked person.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul confronts a case of open immorality in Corinth, a man living with his father's wife, which even Gentiles do not tolerate, and which the church has tolerated with pride instead of mourning. He orders the offender delivered to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, urges purging out the old leaven at Passover, and explains that the church judges those within, while God judges those outside. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. It was actually reported — and the Corinthians themselves had not told Paul, though they wrote to him about other matters — that a gross fornication existed among them not even found among the heathen: a man keeping his father's wife, his stepmother, while his father still lived. The woman was perhaps a heathen, which is why Paul's rebuke is directed at the man rather than her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Instead of mourning, the Corinthians were puffed up with their own wisdom and their favorite teachers' eloquence, at a time when they ought to have mourned the scandal to religion. Paul mourned because they did not mourn; believers ought to mourn over others' transgressions and repent of their own. Their mourning should have led to the offender being removed from among them by excommunication — a discipline that in fact brought him to bitter repentance before the second epistle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Though absent in body, Paul was present in spirit and had already judged the one who perpetrated so scandalous a deed while called a brother. The word for what he did is stronger than mere doing — it marks a deed done scandalously. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The sentence was to be passed in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, by his authority and as representing his person and will, when the church was gathered together with Paul's spirit and the power of the Lord Jesus. Christ promised his power to his church gathered in his name, and Paul by inspiration gave a special promise that his apostolic judgment, guided by the Spirit, would ratify their decree as if he were present in person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Beyond excommunication, which the church itself could enact, Paul delegated his own apostolic power to deliver such a one to Satan for the destruction of the flesh — affliction of the body, even death, so as to destroy fleshly lust, not the body itself, which will share in redemption. The aim was that the spirit might be saved in the day of the Lord, since temporary affliction often leads to permanent salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Their glorying in their attainments and favorite teachers, while conniving at such a scandal, was unseemly. A little leaven leavens the whole lump, both by present complicity in the guilt and by the danger of future contagion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The old leaven — the remnant of heathenish and natural corruption — must be purged out, as Jews searched every corner of their houses for leaven before the Passover. Christians are continually to search and purify their hearts, and to correspond in fact with their normal state as unleavened, since Christ our Passover has already been sacrificed; the Christian feast of joy in his finished work lasts all our time, not one season, and in no part of it is the leaven of evil to be admitted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The feast is to be kept not with the old leaven of the unconverted state, but with unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. Malice is the opposite of sincerity, which allows no leaven of evil mixed with good; wickedness is the opposite of truth, which does not mistake evil for good. Malice is the evil habit of mind, wickedness its outworking in word and deed, and sincerity is what, examined by sunlight, is found pure and unadulterated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Paul refers to a former epistle, now lost, in which he wrote not to keep company with fornicators. That he means a former letter, not the present one, is proved because no such direction occurs earlier in this epistle; it was probably a brief reply to Corinthian inquiries, superseded by this fuller epistle and not preserved because the Spirit did not design it for the church's general guidance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The prohibition is limited: it does not mean utterly foregoing intercourse with fornicators, covetous, extortioners, or idolaters of the unbelieving world, for in dissolute Corinth that would mean companying with almost no one. Believers are to remain in the world but keep themselves from its evil; attempting to get out of the world altogether led to monasticism and its evils. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The case is different with a man called a brother who is a fornicator, covetous, an idolater, a railer, a drunkard, or an extortioner — with such a one believers are not even to eat, whether at love-feasts or in private, much less at the Lord's table. There is less danger in associating with open worldlings than with carnal professors, and covetousness is joined with fornication because both spring from the creature's fierce longing, turned from God, to fill itself with inferior objects of sense. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Paul's concern is not with unbelievers outside the church but with those within, whom the church itself must judge — you judge your fellow citizens, not strangers. God will judge those outside, and he is the Judge of the heathen's salvation, not believers. Paul here also anticipates his censure of their lawsuits before heathen tribunals instead of judging causes among themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Those outside God judges; the church's sentence is to put away from among yourselves that wicked person, in language taken from Deuteronomy. Excommunication is the church's own act of purging. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • leaven. Yeast, used for the corrupting influence of tolerated sin, and for the old life to be purged out at Passover. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • delivered unto Satan. Paul's severe sentence of exclusion, for the destruction of the flesh, that the spirit may be saved. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • Passover. The feast Paul invokes: Christ our Passover is sacrificed for us, so the feast is kept with sincerity and truth. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
