The Text — Sirach 18 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 He that liveth for ever Hath created all things in general.

2 The Lord only is righteous, and there is none other but he,

3 Who governeth the world with the palm of his hand, and all things obey his will: for he is the King of all, by his power dividing holy things among them from profane.

4 To whom hath he given power to declare his works? and who shall find out his noble acts?

5 Who shall number the strength of his majesty? and who shall also tell out his mercies?

6 As for the wondrous works of the Lord, there may nothing be taken from them, neither may any thing be put unto them, neither can the ground of them be found out.

7 When a man hath done, then he beginneth; and when he leaveth off, then he shall be doubtful.

8 What is man, and whereto serveth he? what is his good, and what is his evil?

9 The number of a man's days at the most are an hundred years.

10 As a drop of water unto the sea, and a gravelstone in comparison of the sand; so are a thousand years to the days of eternity.

11 Therefore is God patient with them, and poureth forth his mercy upon them.

12 He saw and perceived their end to be evil; therefore he multiplied his compassion.

13 The mercy of man is toward his neighbour; but the mercy of the Lord is upon all flesh: he reproveth, and nurtureth, and teacheth and bringeth again, as a shepherd his flock.

14 He hath mercy on them that receive discipline, and that diligently seek after his judgments.

15 My son, blemish not thy good deeds, neither use uncomfortable words when thou givest any thing.

16 Shall not the dew asswage the heat? so is a word better than a gift.

17 Lo, is not a word better than a gift? but both are with a gracious man.

18 A fool will upbraid churlishly, and a gift of the envious consumeth the eyes.

19 Learn before thou speak, and use physick or ever thou be sick.

20 Before judgment examine thyself, and in the day of visitation thou shalt find mercy.

21 Humble thyself before thou be sick, and in the time of sins shew repentance.

22 Let nothing hinder thee to pay thy vow in due time, and defer not until death to be justified.

23 Before thou prayest, prepare thyself; and be not as one that tempteth the Lord.

24 Think upon the wrath that shall be at the end, and the time of vengeance, when he shall turn away his face.

25 When thou hast enough, remember the time of hunger: and when thou art rich, think upon poverty and need.

26 From the morning until the evening the time is changed, and all things are soon done before the Lord.

27 A wise man will fear in every thing, and in the day of sinning he will beware of offence: but a fool will not observe time.

28 Every man of understanding knoweth wisdom, and will give praise unto him that found her.

29 They that were of understanding in sayings became also wise themselves, and poured forth exquisite parables.

30 Go not after thy lusts, but refrain thyself from thine appetites.

31 If thou givest thy soul the desires that please her, she will make thee a laughingstock to thine enemies that malign thee.

32 Take not pleasure in much good cheer, neither be tied to the expence thereof.

33 Be not made a beggar by banqueting upon borrowing, when thou hast nothing in thy purse: for thou shalt lie in wait for thine own life, and be talked on.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

He who lives for ever created all things in general, and the chapter moves from that vast Creator to small human conduct: a word can be better than a gift, yet the gracious man gives both, and no one should banquet on borrowing until he lies in wait for his own livelihood. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He who lives for ever has created all things in general. The eternal One is the creator of all things without exception. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. The Lord only is righteous, and there is none other but he. Righteousness belongs to the Lord alone; no other shares it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. He governs the world with the palm of his hand, and all things obey his will, for he is the King of all, by his power dividing holy things from profane. The world is governed as if held in his palm, all obeying his will, and his power divides holy from profane. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. To whom has he given power to declare his works, and who will find out his noble acts? No one has been given power to declare God's works fully, nor to find out all his noble acts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Who will number the strength of his majesty, and who will also tell out his mercies? Majesty's strength cannot be numbered, nor mercies told out to their end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. As for the wondrous works of the Lord, nothing may be taken from them, nor anything put to them, neither can the ground of them be found out. God's wondrous works admit neither subtraction nor addition, and their ground cannot be discovered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. When a man has done, then he begins; and when he leaves off, then he will be doubtful. Human finishing is really beginning, and human ceasing ends in doubt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. What is man, and wherefore does he serve? What is his good, and what is his evil? Man's nature, purpose, good, and evil are all questions that humble him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. The number of a man's days at the most are a hundred years. A man's days reach at most a hundred years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. As a drop of water to the sea, and a gravelstone in comparison of the sand, so are a thousand years to the days of eternity. A thousand years beside eternity are as a drop beside the sea or a pebble beside sand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. Therefore God is patient with them, and pours forth his mercy upon them. Because man's days are so brief, God is patient and pours out mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. He saw and perceived their end to be evil, therefore he multiplied his compassion. Seeing man's end to be evil, God multiplied compassion rather than withdrawing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. The mercy of man is toward his neighbour, but the mercy of the Lord is upon all flesh; he reproves, nurtures, teaches, and brings again, as a shepherd his flock. Human mercy reaches a neighbour; God's mercy reaches all flesh, reproving, nurturing, teaching, and restoring like a shepherd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. He has mercy on those who receive discipline, and who diligently seek after his judgments. Mercy is shown to those who receive discipline and diligently seek God's judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. My son, do not blemish your good deeds, nor use uncomfortable words when you give anything. Good deeds should not be spoiled, nor gifts accompanied by wounding words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. Will not the dew assuage the heat? So is a word better than a gift. As dew eases heat, so a kind word is better than a gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. Lo, is not a word better than a gift? But both are with a gracious man. A word is indeed better than a gift, yet the gracious man gives both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. A fool will upbraid churlishly, and a gift of the envious consumes the eyes. A fool's giving comes with churlish upbraiding, and an envious gift pains the eyes of the receiver. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. Learn before you speak, and use physic before you are sick. Learning should precede speech, as medicine precedes sickness: preparation comes first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 20. Before judgment examine yourself, and in the day of visitation you will find mercy. Self-examination before judgment prepares a person to find mercy when visited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 21. Humble yourself before you are sick, and in the time of sins show repentance. Humility before sickness and repentance in the time of sinning are both timely. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 22. Let nothing hinder you to pay your vow in due time, and do not defer until death to be justified. Vows must be paid on time, and justification must not be deferred until death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 23. Before you pray, prepare yourself, and do not be as one who tempts the Lord. Prayer requires preparation; unprepared prayer is like tempting God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 24. Think upon the wrath that will be at the end, and the time of vengeance, when he will turn away his face. The end's wrath and vengeance, when God turns away his face, should be kept in mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 25. When you have enough, remember the time of hunger; and when you are rich, think upon poverty and need. Plenty should remember hunger, and riches should remember poverty and need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 26. From the morning until the evening the time is changed, and all things are soon done before the Lord. Time changes from morning to evening, and all things are swiftly accomplished before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 27. A wise man will fear in everything, and in the day of sinning he will beware of offence; but a fool will not observe time. The wise fear in everything and watch against offence, while the fool ignores the times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 28. Every man of understanding knows wisdom, and will give praise to him who found her. The understanding man knows wisdom and praises the one who found her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 29. Those who were of understanding in sayings became also wise themselves, and poured forth exquisite parables. Understanding of sayings ripens into wisdom itself, which then pours out fine parables. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 30. Do not go after your lusts, but refrain yourself from your appetites. Lusts must not be followed, and appetites must be restrained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 31. If you give your soul the desires that please her, she will make you a laughingstock to your enemies who malign you. Giving the soul its pleasing desires ends in becoming a laughingstock to maligning enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 32. Do not take pleasure in much good cheer, nor be tied to the expense thereof. Much feasting should not be delighted in, nor its expense become a bond. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 33. Do not be made a beggar by banqueting upon borrowing, when you have nothing in your purse, for you will lie in wait for your own life and be talked about. Banqueting on borrowed money when the purse is empty makes a beggar, endangers life, and becomes public talk. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
