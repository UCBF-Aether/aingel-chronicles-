The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the year that king Uzziah died I saw also the LORD sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up, and his train filled the temple.

2 Above it stood the seraphims: each one had six wings; with twain he covered his face, and with twain he covered his feet, and with twain he did fly.

3 And one cried unto another, and said, Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory.

4 And the posts of the door moved at the voice of him that cried, and the house was filled with smoke.

5 Then said I, Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips: for mine eyes have seen the King, the LORD of hosts.

6 Then flew one of the seraphims unto me, having a live coal in his hand, which he had taken with the tongs from off the altar:

7 And he laid it upon my mouth, and said, Lo, this hath touched thy lips; and thine iniquity is taken away, and thy sin purged.

8 Also I heard the voice of the Lord, saying, Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Then said I, Here am I; send me.

9 And he said, Go, and tell this people, Hear ye indeed, but understand not; and see ye indeed, but perceive not.

10 Make the heart of this people fat, and make their ears heavy, and shut their eyes; lest they see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and convert, and be healed.

11 Then said I, Lord, how long? And he answered, Until the cities be wasted without inhabitant, and the houses without man, and the land be utterly desolate,

12 And the LORD have removed men far away, and there be a great forsaking in the midst of the land.

13 But yet in it shall be a tenth, and it shall return, and shall be eaten: as a teil tree, and as an oak, whose substance is in them, when they cast their leaves: so the holy seed shall be the substance thereof.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the year King Uzziah died, Isaiah sees the Lord enthroned in the temple, attended by seraphim crying 'Holy, holy, holy.' Cleansed by a live coal from the altar, he volunteers — 'Here am I; send me' — and receives a hard commission: to preach to a people who will hear without understanding, until only a stump remains, and that stump is the holy seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up, with his train filling the temple. This is not the first beginning of Isaiah's prophecies but his inauguration to a higher degree of the prophetic office, and he saw the Son, not the divine essence itself, with a flowing robe and train as the badge of an Eastern monarch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Above the throne stood the seraphim in attendance, each with six wings: with two they covered their faces as unworthy to look on the holy God, with two they covered their feet in reverence, and with two they flew, ready for instant flight in God's service. "Seraph" means to burn, implying burning zeal, dazzling brightness, and serpent-like rapidity in God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. One seraph cried to another, Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts, and the whole earth is full of his glory — the fulness of the whole earth is his glory. The thrice-repeated Holy implies the Trinity in unity, and God's holiness is the keynote of Isaiah's whole prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. At the voice of him that cried, the foundations of the thresholds moved and the house was filled with smoke. The posts are the foundations of the thresholds, and the smoke is the Shekinah cloud that filled the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Then Isaiah said, Woe is me, for I am undone, because I am a man of unclean lips dwelling among a people of unclean lips, yet my eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts. The lips are named because the seraphim's praises are sung by lips, and because Isaiah is about to be commissioned to speak as God's prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. One of the seraphim flew to Isaiah with a live coal — literally a hot stone such as was used to roast the meat of the sacrifices — taken with tongs from the altar of burnt offering. The fire on that altar was first kindled by God and kept continually burning, and fire is a symbol of purification, taking dross out of metals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The seraph laid the coal on Isaiah's mouth and said, this has touched your lips, your iniquity is taken away and your sin purged — literally covered, expiated. The purging is not by any physical effect of fire but in relation to the altar sacrifices, of which Messiah, who here commissions Isaiah, was to be the antitype in his death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Then Isaiah heard the voice of the Lord saying, Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? The change from "I" to "us" indicates the Trinity, and the question implies that few would be willing to bear the self-denial of delivering so unwelcome a message. Isaiah answered with prompt zeal, now specially qualified: Here am I, send me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. God's message is: Go and tell this people, Hear indeed but understand not, and see indeed but perceive not. Though they hear the warnings again and again, they are doomed by their perverse will not to understand, while light enough is given to guide those sincerely seeking to do God's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Isaiah is told to make the heart of this people fat, their ears heavy, and their eyes shut, lest they see, hear, understand, convert, and be healed of their spiritual malady, sin. This hardening is the fruit not of truth in itself but of the corrupt state of their hearts, to which God judicially gives them over; corruption flows from the heart into ears and eyes, while healing reaches the heart through eyes and ears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Isaiah asks, Lord, how long? — how long will this hardening to destruction continue? The answer runs until the cities are wasted without inhabitant, the houses without man, and the land utterly desolate, fulfilled primarily at the Babylonian captivity and more fully at the dispersion under Titus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The Lord will have removed men far away, and there will be a great forsaking — abandonment of dwellings by their inhabitants — in the midst of the land. The verse points to the deportations that emptied Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Yet if a tenth remains in the land, it will again be given over to be consumed, as a terebinth or oak cast down still keeps its stock or trunk. That stock is the holy seed, the elect remnant alone destined to salvation, with seeds of vitality waiting for the returning spring of God's favour; God shows unchangeable severity toward sin but covenant faithfulness in preserving a remnant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Seraphim. 'Seraph' means to burn, the note on verse 2 says: fiery attendants of God's throne, each with six wings, covering face and feet in reverence and flying instantly at God's command. Interpreted Study note on verse v2, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
