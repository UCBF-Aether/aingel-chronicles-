The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And after these things I saw four angels standing on the four corners of the earth, holding the four winds of the earth, that the wind should not blow on the earth, nor on the sea, nor on any tree.

2 And I saw another angel ascending from the east, having the seal of the living God: and he cried with a loud voice to the four angels, to whom it was given to hurt the earth and the sea,

3 Saying, Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads.

4 And I heard the number of them which were sealed: and there were sealed an hundred and forty and four thousand of all the tribes of the children of Israel.

5 Of the tribe of Juda were sealed twelve thousand. Of the tribe of Reuben were sealed twelve thousand. Of the tribe of Gad were sealed twelve thousand.

6 Of the tribe of Aser were sealed twelve thousand. Of the tribe of Nephthalim were sealed twelve thousand. Of the tribe of Manasses were sealed twelve thousand.

7 Of the tribe of Simeon were sealed twelve thousand. Of the tribe of Levi were sealed twelve thousand. Of the tribe of Issachar were sealed twelve thousand.

8 Of the tribe of Zabulon were sealed twelve thousand. Of the tribe of Joseph were sealed twelve thousand. Of the tribe of Benjamin were sealed twelve thousand.

9 After this I beheld, and, lo, a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues, stood before the throne, and before the Lamb, clothed with white robes, and palms in their hands;

10 And cried with a loud voice, saying, Salvation to our God which sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb.

11 And all the angels stood round about the throne, and about the elders and the four beasts, and fell before the throne on their faces, and worshipped God,

12 Saying, Amen: Blessing, and glory, and wisdom, and thanksgiving, and honour, and power, and might, be unto our God for ever and ever. Amen.

13 And one of the elders answered, saying unto me, What are these which are arrayed in white robes? and whence came they?

14 And I said unto him, Sir, thou knowest. And he said to me, These are they which came out of great tribulation, and have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.

15 Therefore are they before the throne of God, and serve him day and night in his temple: and he that sitteth on the throne shall dwell among them.

16 They shall hunger no more, neither thirst any more; neither shall the sun light on them, nor any heat.

17 For the Lamb which is in the midst of the throne shall feed them, and shall lead them unto living fountains of waters: and God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Between the sixth and seventh seals the vision pauses: four angels hold the four winds until God's servants are sealed, a hundred and forty-four thousand from the tribes of Israel, which Henry explains as a definite number standing for an indefinite remnant God knows and keeps. Then an uncountable multitude from all nations, kindreds, peoples and tongues stands before the throne in white robes with palms, crying salvation to God and the Lamb — those who came out of great tribulation and washed their robes in the Lamb's blood, now hungering and thirsting no more while the Lamb feeds them and God wipes away their tears. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John saw four angels standing on the four corners of the earth, holding the four winds, so wind would not blow on earth, sea, or any tree. Henry reads the winds as errors and corruptions that trouble the church, restrained by angels, since the spirit of error cannot go forth until God permits it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Another angel ascended from the east, having the seal of the living God, and cried loudly to the four angels given power to hurt the earth and sea. Henry notes that while some angels restrain Satan and his agents, another is employed to mark out and distinguish God's faithful servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The angel charged, "Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads." Henry notes the restraint lasted only until the sealing: God first establishes his servants, then tries them, holding the timing of their trials in his own hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. John heard the number of those sealed: a hundred and forty-four thousand of all the tribes of Israel. Henry explains the definite number stands for an indefinite remnant, and that God knows who are his and will keep them safe in dangerous temptation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Of Judah, Reuben, and Gad, twelve thousand were sealed from each tribe. Henry notes the list omits Dan, perhaps because that tribe was greatly addicted to idolatry, and alters the tribes' order, perhaps according to their faithfulness to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Of Asher, Naphtali, and Manasses, twelve thousand were sealed from each tribe. Henry takes this sealed remnant either as Jews reserved for mercy at Jerusalem's destruction or, more generally, as God's chosen remnant in the world according to the election of grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Of Simeon, Levi, and Issachar, twelve thousand were sealed from each tribe. Henry notes the sealing sets God's servants apart for mercy and safety in the worst of times, with a seal known to God and plain as if it appeared on their foreheads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Of Zebulon, Joseph, and Benjamin, twelve thousand were sealed from each tribe, completing the hundred and forty-four thousand. Henry observes that though the church is a little flock compared with the wicked world, it is no contemptible society, but really large and to be still more enlarged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. After this John saw a great multitude no man could number, of all nations, kindreds, peoples, and tongues, standing before the throne and the Lamb, clothed in white robes, with palms in their hands. Henry notes God will have a greater harvest among the Gentiles than among the Jews, and that the white robes are justification, holiness, and victory, with palms as in a conqueror's triumph. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They cried with a loud voice, "Salvation to our God which sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb." Henry reads this as both hosannah and hallelujah: the Father contrived salvation, the Son purchased it, and those who enjoy it bless Lord and Lamb publicly and fervently. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. All the angels round the throne, the elders, and the four living creatures fell on their faces and worshipped God. Henry marvels at the humility: the most excellent creatures, who never sinned and stand continually before God, fall on their faces, teaching frail creatures reverence in approaching him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The angels said, "Amen, Blessing, and glory, and wisdom, and thanksgiving, and honour, and power, and might, be unto our God for ever and ever. Amen." Henry notes they consented to the saints' praises in perfect harmony, then added their own acknowledgement of God's wisdom, power, and might. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. One of the elders asked John, "What are these which are arrayed in white robes? and whence came they?" Henry notes the question was asked for John's instruction, not the elder's information, and that faithful Christians deserve notice, since the lowest saint in heaven knows more than the greatest apostle on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. John answered, "Sir, thou knowest," and was told these are they who came out of great tribulation and washed their robes white in the blood of the Lamb. Henry stresses it is not the martyrs' own blood but the Lamb's that washes away sin, and that tribulation, however great, cannot separate us from God's love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Therefore they are before the throne of God, serving him day and night in his temple, and he who sits on the throne shall dwell among them. Henry notes their happiness in station, in the presence where there is fulness of joy, and in employment: heaven is service without weariness, rest without sloth, a praising delightful rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. They shall hunger no more, neither thirst any more, neither shall the sun light on them, nor any heat. Henry notes their freedom from all want and from all sickness and pain, with every uneasiness of this present life removed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The Lamb in the midst of the throne shall feed them and lead them to living fountains of waters, and God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes. Henry closes with the tender Father image: God himself, with his own gentle hand, wipes away tears shed over sin and affliction, turning sorrow into rejoicing, so that those who sow in tears reap in joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Sealed. Marked out and distinguished as God's faithful servants before trial, the sealing lasting only until it is complete, in Henry's notes. Interpreted Study notes, verses 2–3, Matthew Henry
  • Hundred and forty-four thousand. A definite number standing for an indefinite remnant: God knows who are his and will keep them safe, in Henry's note. Interpreted Study note, verse 4, Matthew Henry
