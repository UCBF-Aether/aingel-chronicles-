The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Peter 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Wherefore laying aside all malice, and all guile, and hypocrisies, and envies, all evil speakings,

2 As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that ye may grow thereby:

3 If so be ye have tasted that the Lord is gracious.

4 To whom coming, as unto a living stone, disallowed indeed of men, but chosen of God, and precious,

5 Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ.

6 Wherefore also it is contained in the scripture, Behold, I lay in Sion a chief corner stone, elect, precious: and he that believeth on him shall not be confounded.

7 Unto you therefore which believe he is precious: but unto them which be disobedient, the stone which the builders disallowed, the same is made the head of the corner,

8 And a stone of stumbling, and a rock of offence, even to them which stumble at the word, being disobedient: whereunto also they were appointed.

9 But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light;

10 Which in time past were not a people, but are now the people of God: which had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy.

11 Dearly beloved, I beseech you as strangers and pilgrims, abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul;

12 Having your conversation honest among the Gentiles: that, whereas they speak against you as evildoers, they may by your good works, which they shall behold, glorify God in the day of visitation.

13 Submit yourselves to every ordinance of man for the Lord’s sake: whether it be to the king, as supreme;

14 Or unto governors, as unto them that are sent by him for the punishment of evildoers, and for the praise of them that do well.

15 For so is the will of God, that with well doing ye may put to silence the ignorance of foolish men:

16 As free, and not using your liberty for a cloke of maliciousness, but as the servants of God.

17 Honour all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honour the king.

18 Servants, be subject to your masters with all fear; not only to the good and gentle, but also to the froward.

19 For this is thankworthy, if a man for conscience toward God endure grief, suffering wrongfully.

20 For what glory is it, if, when ye be buffeted for your faults, ye shall take it patiently? but if, when ye do well, and suffer for it, ye take it patiently, this is acceptable with God.

21 For even hereunto were ye called: because Christ also suffered for us, leaving us an example, that ye should follow his steps:

22 Who did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth:

23 Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again; when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously:

24 Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.

25 For ye were as sheep going astray; but are now returned unto the Shepherd and Bishop of your souls.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Lay aside malice, deceit, hypocrisy, envy and slander, Peter urges, and crave the pure milk of the word as newborn children. Believers are living stones built into a spiritual house, a chosen generation, royal priesthood and holy nation, called out of darkness into light. As strangers and pilgrims they should live honourably among Gentiles, submit to authorities for the Lord's sake, use freedom not as a cover for evil, and let servants endure unjust suffering following Christ, who bore their sins on the tree. Interpreted Editorial overview of the chapter text; verse-by-verse study-note summaries follow

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Lay aside all malice, all guile, hypocrisies, envies, and evil speakings. Henry notes evil-speaking is a sign of malice and guile in the heart and hinders profiting by the word of God, so the old temper must be put off before the new food can nourish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that you may grow thereby. A new life needs suitable food, and as infants desire milk with their best endeavours, so must a Christian's desire after the word of God be eager and constant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. If indeed you have tasted that the Lord is gracious, that taste should draw you on to the word. Even the best of God's servants in this life have only a taste of his consolations, yet Christ is very merciful to miserable sinners and has a fullness of grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Coming to Christ as to a living stone, disallowed indeed by men but chosen of God and precious, believers find their protection and security. He is called a Stone to teach his servants that he is the foundation on which they are built, precious in the excellence of his nature, the dignity of his office, and the glory of his services. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Believers also, as living stones, are built up a spiritual house, a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God by Jesus Christ. All true believers are sacred to God and serviceable to others, endowed with heavenly gifts, yet even their best sacrifices in prayer and praise are acceptable only through Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Scripture says, Behold, I lay in Zion a chief cornerstone, elect and precious, and whoever believes on him shall not be confounded. Christ is the chief cornerstone who unites all believers into one everlasting temple and bears the weight of the whole fabric, chosen for a foundation that is everlasting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. To believers, therefore, he is precious, but to the disobedient the stone the builders disallowed has become the head of the corner. To be built on Christ means to believe in him, and many deceive themselves by not considering what that is, nor the necessity of partaking of the salvation he wrought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. To the disobedient he is also a stone of stumbling and a rock of offence, even to those who stumble at the word, being disobedient. The same foundation that secures the believer confounds those who reject it, and though the frame of the world were falling, the person built on this foundation may hear it without fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. But believers are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a peculiar people, to show forth the praises of him who called them out of darkness into his marvellous light. They make one family, distinct from the world in spirit, principle, and practice, which they could never be unless chosen in Christ and sanctified by his Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. In time past they were not a people, but are now the people of God, having not obtained mercy but now having obtained mercy. Their first state was gross darkness, but they are called into joy, pleasure, and prosperity, and their obligations to him who made them his people and showed them mercy are vast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Dearly beloved, as strangers and pilgrims, abstain from fleshly lusts which war against the soul. Even the chosen generation and people of God need exhortation against the worst sins, and fleshly lusts are most destructive to the soul, a sore judgment to be given up to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Keep your conversation honest among the Gentiles, so that, whereas they speak against you as evildoers, they may by your good works, which they behold, glorify God in the day of visitation. Holy lives may promote the happy change when God calls people to repentance by his word and grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Submit to every ordinance of man for the Lord's sake, whether to the king as supreme or to governors sent by him. Regard for relative duties is the will of God and the Christian's duty, and the way to silence the base slanders of ignorant and foolish people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Governors are sent for the punishment of evildoers and the praise of those who do well, so submission to them belongs to an honest Christian conversation. The apostle treats these duties distinctly, because a Christian life cannot be honest without a just and careful discharge of all relative duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. For so is the will of God, that with well doing you may put to silence the ignorance of foolish men. Well-doing, not argument alone, answers slander, and Christians must behave aright in all relations so as not to make liberty a cloak for wickedness or neglect of duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Live as free people, not using liberty for a cloak of maliciousness, but as servants of God. Freedom in Christ is service to God, not licence, and believers must remember in every relation that they are God's servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Honour all people, love the brotherhood, fear God, honour the king. These four short commands sum the relative duties: universal respect, special brotherly love, reverent fear of God, and honour to lawful authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Servants, be subject to your masters with all fear, not only to the good and gentle but also to the froward. Servants in those days were generally slaves with heathen masters who often used them cruelly, yet the apostle directs subjection to masters placed over them by providence, with a fear of dishonouring God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. This is thankworthy, if a person for conscience toward God endures grief, suffering wrongfully. The sinful misconduct of one relation does not justify sinful behaviour in the other, and the servant remains bound to duty though the master is froward and perverse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. What glory is it to take patiently buffeting for your faults, but if, when you do well and suffer for it, you take it patiently, this is acceptable with God? Patience under deserved correction earns no distinction, but patient endurance of ill treatment for well-doing, without peevish complaints or purposes of revenge, is a distinguishing effect of grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Even hereunto were you called, because Christ also suffered for us, leaving us an example that we should follow his steps. Christ's death was designed not only as an example of patience under suffering, but as the bearing of our sins, and from it believers draw both example and power for a holy life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Christ did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth. His perfect sinlessness is the pattern: no sin in deed, no deceit in speech, even under the provocation his followers also face. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. When reviled, he reviled not again, and when he suffered, he threatened not, but committed himself to him who judges righteously. Henry's example of patience is precisely this refusal of revenge, leaving judgment to God rather than answering evil with evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. He himself bore our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness, and by his stripes we were healed. Christ bore the punishment of our sins and thereby satisfied divine justice, taking them away from us, and the fruits of his sufferings are the death of sin and a new holy life of righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. You were as sheep going astray, but are now returned to the Shepherd and Bishop of your souls. Before conversion, sinners are always going astray, a continued error away from pasture, Shepherd, and flock, exposing themselves to numberless dangers, and return is the effect of divine grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
