The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And after the uproar was ceased, Paul called unto him the disciples, and embraced them, and departed for to go into Macedonia.

2 And when he had gone over those parts, and had given them much exhortation, he came into Greece,

3 And there abode three months. And when the Jews laid wait for him, as he was about to sail into Syria, he purposed to return through Macedonia.

4 And there accompanied him into Asia Sopater of Berea; and of the Thessalonians, Aristarchus and Secundus; and Gaius of Derbe, and Timotheus; and of Asia, Tychicus and Trophimus.

5 These going before tarried for us at Troas.

6 And we sailed away from Philippi after the days of unleavened bread, and came unto them to Troas in five days; where we abode seven days.

7 And upon the first day of the week, when the disciples came together to break bread, Paul preached unto them, ready to depart on the morrow; and continued his speech until midnight.

8 And there were many lights in the upper chamber, where they were gathered together.

9 And there sat in a window a certain young man named Eutychus, being fallen into a deep sleep: and as Paul was long preaching, he sunk down with sleep, and fell down from the third loft, and was taken up dead.

10 And Paul went down, and fell on him, and embracing him said, Trouble not yourselves; for his life is in him.

11 When he therefore was come up again, and had broken bread, and eaten, and talked a long while, even till break of day, so he departed.

12 And they brought the young man alive, and were not a little comforted.

13 And we went before to ship, and sailed unto Assos, there intending to take in Paul: for so had he appointed, minding himself to go afoot.

14 And when he met with us at Assos, we took him in, and came to Mitylene.

15 And we sailed thence, and came the next day over against Chios; and the next day we arrived at Samos, and tarried at Trogyllium; and the next day we came to Miletus.

16 For Paul had determined to sail by Ephesus, because he would not spend the time in Asia: for he hasted, if it were possible for him, to be at Jerusalem the day of Pentecost.

17 And from Miletus he sent to Ephesus, and called the elders of the church.

18 And when they were come to him, he said unto them, Ye know, from the first day that I came into Asia, after what manner I have been with you at all seasons,

19 Serving the LORD with all humility of mind, and with many tears, and temptations, which befell me by the lying in wait of the Jews:

20 And how I kept back nothing that was profitable unto you, but have shewed you, and have taught you publickly, and from house to house,

21 Testifying both to the Jews, and also to the Greeks, repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ.

22 And now, behold, I go bound in the spirit unto Jerusalem, not knowing the things that shall befall me there:

23 Save that the Holy Ghost witnesseth in every city, saying that bonds and afflictions abide me.

24 But none of these things move me, neither count I my life dear unto myself, so that I might finish my course with joy, and the ministry, which I have received of the Lord Jesus, to testify the gospel of the grace of God.

25 And now, behold, I know that ye all, among whom I have gone preaching the kingdom of God, shall see my face no more.

26 Wherefore I take you to record this day, that I am pure from the blood of all men.

27 For I have not shunned to declare unto you all the counsel of God.

28 Take heed therefore unto yourselves, and to all the flock, over the which the Holy Ghost hath made you overseers, to feed the church of God, which he hath purchased with his own blood.

29 For I know this, that after my departing shall grievous wolves enter in among you, not sparing the flock.

30 Also of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them.

31 Therefore watch, and remember, that by the space of three years I ceased not to warn every one night and day with tears.

32 And now, brethren, I commend you to God, and to the word of his grace, which is able to build you up, and to give you an inheritance among all them which are sanctified.

33 I have coveted no man’s silver, or gold, or apparel.

34 Yea, ye yourselves know, that these hands have ministered unto my necessities, and to them that were with me.

35 I have shewed you all things, how that so labouring ye ought to support the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he said, It is more blessed to give than to receive.

36 And when he had thus spoken, he kneeled down, and prayed with them all.

37 And they all wept sore, and fell on Paul’s neck, and kissed him,

38 Sorrowing most of all for the words which he spake, that they should see his face no more. And they accompanied him unto the ship.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After the Ephesian uproar Paul travels through Macedonia and Greece, raises Eutychus at Troas after the young man falls from a window during a long sermon, and at Miletus bids a tearful farewell to the Ephesian elders. He warns them that grievous wolves will enter after his departure, commends them to God, and kneels to pray with them before sailing on toward Jerusalem. Interpreted Overview of this chapter's text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After the uproar was ceased, Paul called unto him the disciples, and embraced them, and departed for to go into Macedonia. Henry says tumults or opposition may constrain a Christian to remove from his station or alter his purpose, but his work and his pleasure will be the same, wherever he goes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. When he had gone over those parts, and had given them much exhortation, he came into Greece. Henry's work-same-wherever point is the exhortation journey: tumults may alter purpose, but the work and pleasure remain the same wherever he goes, and Paul gives much exhortation over those parts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. There abode three months. And when the Jews laid wait for him, as he was about to sail into Syria, he purposed to return through Macedonia. Henry's altered purpose is the plot's effect: tumults or opposition may constrain a Christian to alter his purpose, but his work and pleasure will be the same, and the laid wait turns the voyage into a return through Macedonia. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. We sailed away from Philippi after the days of unleavened bread, and came unto them to Troas in five days, where we abode seven days. Henry says Paul thought it worth while to bestow five days in going to Troas, though it was but for seven days' stay there, but he knew, and so should we, how to redeem even journeying time, and to make it turn to some good account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Upon the first day of the week, when the disciples came together to break bread, Paul preached unto them, ready to depart on the morrow, and continued his speech until midnight. Henry says though the disciples read, and meditated, and prayed, and sung apart, and thereby kept up communion with God, yet they came together to worship God, and so kept up their communion with one another, on the first day of the week, the Lord's day, to be religiously observed by all disciples of Christ, and in the breaking of the bread, not only the breaking of Christ's body for us, to be a sacrifice for our sins, is remembered, but the breaking of Christ's body to us, to be food and a feast for our souls, is signified, while the preaching of the gospel ought to go with the sacraments, and they were willing to hear, so he continued his speech till midnight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. There sat in a window a certain young man named Eutychus, being fallen into a deep sleep: and as Paul was long preaching, he sunk down with sleep, and fell down from the third loft, and was taken up dead. Henry says sleeping when hearing the word is an evil thing, a sign of low esteem of the word of God, and we must do what we can to prevent being sleepy, not put ourselves to sleep, but get our hearts affected with the word we hear, so as to drive sleep far away, though infirmity requires tenderness and contempt requires severity, and the interruption was made to confirm Paul's preaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Paul went down, and fell on him, and embracing him said, Trouble not yourselves; for his life is in him. Henry's confirmation point is the embrace: the interruption of the preaching was made to confirm his preaching, and Paul's going down and falling on Eutychus, with life in him, is that confirmation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. When he therefore was come up again, and had broken bread, and eaten, and talked a long while, even till break of day, so he departed. Henry says as they knew not when they should have Paul's company again, they made the best use of it they could, and reckoned a night's sleep well lost for that purpose, and the talking till break of day is that best use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. They brought the young man alive, and were not a little comforted. Henry says Eutychus was brought to life again, and they were not a little comforted, though he adds the rebuke that how seldom are hours of repose broken for the purposes of devotion, but how often for mere amusement or sinful revelry, so hard is it for spiritual life to thrive in the heart of man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. We went before to ship, and sailed unto Assos, there intending to take in Paul: for so had he appointed, minding himself to go afoot. Henry says Paul hastened to Jerusalem, but tried to do good by the way, when going from place to place, as every good man should do, and in doing God's work, our own wills and those of our friends must often be crossed, and we must not spend time with them when duty calls us another way, and Paul's going afoot to Assos is that crossed will for duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. For Paul had determined to sail by Ephesus, because he would not spend the time in Asia: for he hasted, if it were possible for him, to be at Jerusalem the day of Pentecost. Henry's hastening rule is the determination: Paul hastened to Jerusalem, but tried to do good by the way, and must not spend time with friends when duty calls another way, so he sails by Ephesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. From Miletus he sent to Ephesus, and called the elders of the church. Henry's farewell discourse begins with the elders' knowledge: they knew that Paul was no designing, self-seeking man, and those who would in any office serve the Lord acceptably, and profitably to others, must do it with humility, and the sending for the elders is the humble farewell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. When they were come to him, he said unto them, Ye know, from the first day that I came into Asia, after what manner I have been with you at all seasons. Henry says the elders knew Paul was no designing, self-seeking man, and he was a plain preacher, one that spoke his message so as to be understood, and his appeal to what they know from the first day is that plainness on record. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Serving the Lord with all humility of mind, and with many tears, and temptations, which befell me by the lying in wait of the Jews. Henry says those who would serve the Lord acceptably must do it with humility, and Paul was a painful preacher, very industrious in his work, serving with humility of mind, many tears, and temptations from the Jews' lying in wait. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. How I kept back nothing that was profitable unto you, but have shewed you, and have taught you publickly, and from house to house. Henry says he was a profitable preacher, one that aimed to inform their judgments, and reform their hearts and lives, and a faithful preacher, who did not keep back reproofs when necessary, nor keep back the preaching of the cross, and keeping back nothing profitable is that faithfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Testifying both to the Jews, and also to the Greeks, repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ. Henry says he was a truly Christian, evangelical preacher, who did not preach notions or doubtful matters, nor affairs of state or the civil government, but he preached faith and repentance, and a better summary of these things, without which there is no salvation, cannot be given: even repentance towards God, and faith towards our Lord Jesus Christ, with their fruits and effects, for without these no sinner can escape, and with these none will come short of eternal life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Now, behold, I go bound in the spirit unto Jerusalem, not knowing the things that shall befall me there. Henry says let them not think that Paul left Asia for fear of persecution, for he was in full expectation of trouble, yet resolved to go on, well assured that it was by divine direction, and thanks be to God that we know not the things which shall befall us during the year, the week, the day which has begun, for it is enough for the child of God to know that his strength shall be equal to his day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Save that the Holy Ghost witnesseth in every city, saying that bonds and afflictions abide me. Henry says the powerful influences of the Holy Spirit bind the true Christian to his duty, and even when he expects persecution and affliction, the love of Christ constrains him to proceed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. But none of these things move me, neither count I my life dear unto myself, so that I might finish my course with joy, and the ministry, which I have received of the Lord Jesus, to testify the gospel of the grace of God. Henry says none of these things moved Paul from his work, and they did not deprive him of his comfort, for it is the business of our life to provide for a joyful death, and finishing the course with joy is that business. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Now, behold, I know that ye all, among whom I have gone preaching the kingdom of God, shall see my face no more. Henry says believing that this was the last time they should see him, he appeals concerning his integrity, for he had preached to them the whole counsel of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Wherefore I take you to record this day, that I am pure from the blood of all men. Henry's integrity appeal is the purity claim: as he had preached to them the gospel purely, so he had preached it to them entire, and he faithfully did his work, whether men would bear or forbear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. For I have not shunned to declare unto you all the counsel of God. Henry's whole-counsel point is the declaration itself: he had preached to them the whole counsel of God, purely and entire, faithfully whether men would bear or forbear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Take heed therefore unto yourselves, and to all the flock, over the which the Holy Ghost hath made you overseers, to feed the church of God, which he hath purchased with his own blood. Henry says if the Holy Ghost has made ministers overseers of the flock, that is, shepherds, they must be true to their trust, and let them consider their Master's concern for the flock committed to their charge, for it is the church he has purchased with his own blood, and the blood was his as Man, yet so close is the union between the divine and human nature, that it is there called the blood of God, for it was the blood of him who is God, and this put such dignity and worth into it, as to ransom believers from all evil, and purchase all good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For I know this, that after my departing shall grievous wolves enter in among you, not sparing the flock. Henry's watch warning begins with the wolves: after Paul's departing, grievous wolves shall enter in among them, not sparing the flock, and therefore they must watch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Also of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them. Henry's watch warning continues within: of their own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them, and the elders must watch against their own as well as against wolves from without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Therefore watch, and remember, that by the space of three years I ceased not to warn every one night and day with tears. Henry says Paul spake about their souls with affection and concern, and the three years of warning every one night and day with tears are that affection in action, commanding watchfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Now, brethren, I commend you to God, and to the word of his grace, which is able to build you up, and to give you an inheritance among all them which are sanctified. Henry says Paul directs them to look up to God with faith, and commends them to the word of God's grace, not only as the foundation of their hope and the fountain of their joy, but as the rule of their walking, and the most advanced Christians are capable of growing, and will find the word of grace help their growth, while to all who are born again, and on whom the image of God is renewed, the inheritance is sure, as almighty power and eternal truth make it so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. I have coveted no man's silver, or gold, or apparel. Henry says he recommends himself to them as an example of not caring as to things of the present world, and coveting no man's silver, gold, or apparel is that example, which they would find help forward their comfortable passage through the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Yea, ye yourselves know, that these hands have ministered unto my necessities, and to them that were with me. Henry's example continues in the hands: these hands ministered unto his necessities, and to them that were with him, showing not caring as to things of the present world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. I have shewed you all things, how that so labouring ye ought to support the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he said, It is more blessed to give than to receive. Henry says it might seem a hard saying, therefore Paul adds to it a saying of their Master's, which he would have them always remember, It is more blessed to give than to receive, for the opinion of the children of this world is contrary to this, since they are afraid of giving, unless in hope of getting, and clear gain is with them the most blessed thing that can be, but Christ tells us what is more blessed, more excellent, for it makes us more like to God, who gives to all, and receives from none, and to the Lord Jesus, who went about doing good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. When he had thus spoken, he kneeled down, and prayed with them all. Henry says it is good for friends, when they part, to part with prayer, and Paul kneeling down and praying with them all is that parting prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. They all wept sore, and fell on Paul's neck, and kissed him. Henry says those who exhort and pray for one another may have many weeping seasons and painful separations, but they will meet before the throne of God, to part no more, and the weeping, falling on Paul's neck, and kissing are those weeping seasons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Sorrowing most of all for the words which he spake, that they should see his face no more. And they accompanied him unto the ship. Henry says it was a comfort to all, that the presence of Christ both went with him and stayed with them, and their sorrowing most of all that they should see his face no more is met by that comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Take heed therefore unto yourselves, and to all the flock, over the which the Holy Ghost hath made you overseers, to feed the church of God, which he hath purchased with his own blood. Henry says if the Holy Ghost has made ministers overseers of the flock, that is, shepherds, they must be true to their trust, and let them consider their Master's concern for the flock committed to their charge, for it is the church he has purchased with his own blood, and the blood was his as Man, yet so close is the union between the divine and human nature, that it is there called the blood of God, for it was the blood of him who is God, and this put such dignity and worth into it, as to ransom believers from all evil, and purchase all good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For I know this, that after my departing shall grievous wolves enter in among you, not sparing the flock. Henry's watch warning begins with the wolves: after Paul's departing, grievous wolves shall enter in among them, not sparing the flock, and therefore they must watch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Also of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them. Henry's watch warning continues within: of their own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them, and the elders must watch against their own as well as against wolves from without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Therefore watch, and remember, that by the space of three years I ceased not to warn every one night and day with tears. Henry says Paul spake about their souls with affection and concern, and the three years of warning every one night and day with tears are that affection in action, commanding watchfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Now, brethren, I commend you to God, and to the word of his grace, which is able to build you up, and to give you an inheritance among all them which are sanctified. Henry says Paul directs them to look up to God with faith, and commends them to the word of God's grace, not only as the foundation of their hope and the fountain of their joy, but as the rule of their walking, and the most advanced Christians are capable of growing, and will find the word of grace help their growth, while to all who are born again, and on whom the image of God is renewed, the inheritance is sure, as almighty power and eternal truth make it so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. I have coveted no man's silver, or gold, or apparel. Henry says he recommends himself to them as an example of not caring as to things of the present world, and coveting no man's silver, gold, or apparel is that example, which they would find help forward their comfortable passage through the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Yea, ye yourselves know, that these hands have ministered unto my necessities, and to them that were with me. Henry's example continues in the hands: these hands ministered unto his necessities, and to them that were with him, showing not caring as to things of the present world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. I have shewed you all things, how that so labouring ye ought to support the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he said, It is more blessed to give than to receive. Henry says it might seem a hard saying, therefore Paul adds to it a saying of their Master's, which he would have them always remember, It is more blessed to give than to receive, for the opinion of the children of this world is contrary to this, since they are afraid of giving, unless in hope of getting, and clear gain is with them the most blessed thing that can be, but Christ tells us what is more blessed, more excellent, for it makes us more like to God, who gives to all, and receives from none, and to the Lord Jesus, who went about doing good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. When he had thus spoken, he kneeled down, and prayed with them all. Henry says it is good for friends, when they part, to part with prayer, and Paul kneeling down and praying with them all is that parting prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. They all wept sore, and fell on Paul's neck, and kissed him. Henry says those who exhort and pray for one another may have many weeping seasons and painful separations, but they will meet before the throne of God, to part no more, and the weeping, falling on Paul's neck, and kissing are those weeping seasons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Sorrowing most of all for the words which he spake, that they should see his face no more. And they accompanied him unto the ship. Henry says it was a comfort to all, that the presence of Christ both went with him and stayed with them, and their sorrowing most of all that they should see his face no more is met by that comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
