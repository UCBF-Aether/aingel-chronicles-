The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 27 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When a man shall make a singular vow, the persons shall be for the LORD by thy estimation.

3 And thy estimation shall be of the male from twenty years old even unto sixty years old, even thy estimation shall be fifty shekels of silver, after the shekel of the sanctuary.

4 And if it be a female, then thy estimation shall be thirty shekels.

5 And if it be from five years old even unto twenty years old, then thy estimation shall be of the male twenty shekels, and for the female ten shekels.

6 And if it be from a month old even unto five years old, then thy estimation shall be of the male five shekels of silver, and for the female thy estimation shall be three shekels of silver.

7 And if it be from sixty years old and above; if it be a male, then thy estimation shall be fifteen shekels, and for the female ten shekels.

8 But if he be poorer than thy estimation, then he shall present himself before the priest, and the priest shall value him; according to his ability that vowed shall the priest value him.

9 And if it be a beast, whereof men bring an offering unto the LORD, all that any man giveth of such unto the LORD shall be holy.

10 He shall not alter it, nor change it, a good for a bad, or a bad for a good: and if he shall at all change beast for beast, then it and the exchange thereof shall be holy.

11 And if it be any unclean beast, of which they do not offer a sacrifice unto the LORD, then he shall present the beast before the priest:

12 And the priest shall value it, whether it be good or bad: as thou valuest it, who art the priest, so shall it be.

13 But if he will at all redeem it, then he shall add a fifth part thereof unto thy estimation.

14 And when a man shall sanctify his house to be holy unto the LORD, then the priest shall estimate it, whether it be good or bad: as the priest shall estimate it, so shall it stand.

15 And if he that sanctified it will redeem his house, then he shall add the fifth part of the money of thy estimation unto it, and it shall be his.

16 And if a man shall sanctify unto the LORD some part of a field of his possession, then thy estimation shall be according to the seed thereof: an homer of barley seed shall be valued at fifty shekels of silver.

17 If he sanctify his field from the year of jubile, according to thy estimation it shall stand.

18 But if he sanctify his field after the jubile, then the priest shall reckon unto him the money according to the years that remain, even unto the year of the jubile, and it shall be abated from thy estimation.

19 And if he that sanctified the field will in any wise redeem it, then he shall add the fifth part of the money of thy estimation unto it, and it shall be assured to him.

20 And if he will not redeem the field, or if he have sold the field to another man, it shall not be redeemed any more.

21 But the field, when it goeth out in the jubile, shall be holy unto the LORD, as a field devoted; the possession thereof shall be the priest’s.

22 And if a man sanctify unto the LORD a field which he hath bought, which is not of the fields of his possession;

23 Then the priest shall reckon unto him the worth of thy estimation, even unto the year of the jubile: and he shall give thine estimation in that day, as a holy thing unto the LORD.

24 In the year of the jubile the field shall return unto him of whom it was bought, even to him to whom the possession of the land did belong.

25 And all thy estimations shall be according to the shekel of the sanctuary: twenty gerahs shall be the shekel.

26 Only the firstling of the beasts, which should be the LORD’s firstling, no man shall sanctify it; whether it be ox, or sheep: it is the LORD’s.

27 And if it be of an unclean beast, then he shall redeem it according to thine estimation, and shall add a fifth part of it thereto: or if it be not redeemed, then it shall be sold according to thy estimation.

28 Notwithstanding no devoted thing, that a man shall devote unto the LORD of all that he hath, both of man and beast, and of the field of his possession, shall be sold or redeemed: every devoted thing is most holy unto the LORD.

29 None devoted, which shall be devoted of men, shall be redeemed; but shall surely be put to death.

30 And all the tithe of the land, whether of the seed of the land, or of the fruit of the tree, is the LORD’s: it is holy unto the LORD.

31 And if a man will at all redeem ought of his tithes, he shall add thereto the fifth part thereof.

32 And concerning the tithe of the herd, or of the flock, even of whatsoever passeth under the rod, the tenth shall be holy unto the LORD.

33 He shall not search whether it be good or bad, neither shall he change it: and if he change it at all, then both it and the change thereof shall be holy; it shall not be redeemed.

34 These are the commandments, which the LORD commanded Moses for the children of Israel in mount Sinai.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter closes Leviticus with the law of vows and valuations. A person vowed to the LORD is redeemed at a stated valuation by age and sex, with a poorer scale at the priest's estimation; animals, houses, and fields are likewise valued, with a fifth added on redemption, and a field's value reckoned by the years remaining to the jubilee. The firstborn already belongs to the LORD, a devoted thing cannot be redeemed, and the tithe of herd, flock, and land is holy to the LORD. The book ends by noting that these are the commandments given to Moses for Israel on Mount Sinai. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Estimation (valuation). The priest's reckoning of a vowed person, animal, house, or field in shekels, with a field's value adjusted by the years remaining to the jubilee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the vow laws that close Leviticus, prescribing conditions for the voluntary duty of votive offerings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When a man makes a singular vow, persons shall be for the LORD by estimation, persons being accustomed at all times to present votive offerings from gratitude or deliverance from apprehended evil, and Moses being empowered to prescribe the conditions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The estimation shall be, for a male from twenty years old even unto sixty, fifty shekels of silver after the shekel of the sanctuary, mature age being rated highest as capable of greatest service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. If it be a female, the estimation shall be thirty shekels, the female valuation being set below the male's at the same age. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. If from five years old even unto twenty, the estimation shall be for a male twenty shekels and for a female ten, young people being rated less because not so serviceable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. If from a month old even unto five years, the estimation shall be for a male five shekels of silver and for a female three, infants being devotable by parents before birth but not offered nor redeemed till a month after birth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. If from sixty years old and above, if a male, the estimation shall be fifteen shekels, and for a female ten, old people being valued below the young but above children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. But if he is poorer than the estimation, then he shall present himself before the priest, and the priest shall value him according to his ability that vowed, the poor in no case being freed from payment, to prevent rash vows, but rated according to means, with priestly discretion to reduce. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. If it is a beast whereof men bring an offering unto the LORD, all that he gives of such unto the LORD shall be holy, a clean beast after vowing being neither usable for common purposes nor exchangeable, but sacrificed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. He shall not alter it nor change it, a good for a bad or a bad for a good, and if he changes beast for beast, then it and the exchange thereof shall be holy, the vow binding both animals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. If it is any unclean beast of which they do not offer a sacrifice unto the LORD, then he shall present the beast before the priest, an ass or camel, for instance, being appropriated to the priest at estimated value. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The priest shall value it, whether good or bad, and as the priest values it, so shall it stand, the priest's estimation fixing the redemption price. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But if he will at all redeem it, then he shall add a fifth part thereof unto the estimation, the unclean beast being redeemable by the vower on payment of value plus a fifth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. When a man sanctifies his house to be holy unto the LORD, then the priest shall estimate it, whether good or bad, and as the priest estimates it, so shall it stand, the house being valued and sold with proceeds dedicated to the sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. If he who sanctified it will redeem his house, then he shall add the fifth part of the money of the estimation unto it, and it shall be his, redemption on second thought costing value plus a fifth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. If a man sanctifies unto the LORD some part of a field of his possession, then the estimation shall be according to the seed thereof, a homer of barley seed valued at fifty shekels of silver, the field's value being reckoned by its sowing capacity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. If he sanctifies his field from the year of jubilee, according to the estimation it shall stand, a field sanctified at jubilee bearing the full valuation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. But if he sanctifies his field after the jubilee, then the priest shall reckon the money according to the years remaining even unto the jubilee, and it shall be abated from the estimation, later sanctification costing only the remaining years' proportion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. If he who sanctified the field will in any wise redeem it, then he shall add the fifth part of the money of the estimation unto it, and it shall be assured to him, redemption of a hereditary field costing value plus a fifth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. If he will not redeem the field, or if he has sold the field to another man, it shall not be redeemed any more, an unredeemed hereditary field passing beyond redemption once sold to another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. But the field, when it goes out in the jubilee, shall be holy unto the LORD, as a field devoted, the possession thereof shall be the priest's, a vowed hereditary estate not reverting to the owner but remaining attached in perpetuity to the sanctuary, to oblige redemption or kinsman redemption lest a patrimonial inheritance leave a family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. If a man sanctifies unto the LORD a field which he has bought, which is not of the fields of his possession, acquired property in land being treated differently from hereditary estate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Then the priest shall reckon unto him the worth of the estimation, even unto the year of jubilee, and he shall give the estimation in that day, as a holy thing unto the LORD, the purchased field's value being reckoned to jubilee and paid at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. In the year of jubilee the field shall return unto him of whom it was bought, even to him to whom the possession of the land did belong, acquired land, if not redeemed, returning to the donor at jubilee, unlike hereditary land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. All estimations shall be according to the shekel of the sanctuary, twenty gerahs shall be the shekel, the sanctuary standard fixing every valuation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Only the firstling of the beasts, which should be the LORD's firstling, no man shall sanctify it, whether ox or sheep, it is the LORD's, clean firstlings being consecrated by universal standing law and therefore not devotable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. If it is of an unclean beast, then he shall redeem it according to the estimation, and shall add a fifth part unto it, or if it is not redeemed, then it shall be sold according to the estimation, unclean firstlings following the unclean-beast rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Notwithstanding, no devoted thing that a man shall devote unto the LORD of all that he has, both man and beast, or field of his possession, shall be sold or redeemed, every devoted thing is most holy unto the LORD, this relating to vows of the most solemn kind, accompanied by a solemn imprecation not to fail. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. None devoted, which shall be devoted of men, shall be redeemed, but shall surely be put to death, this importing not sacrifice or violent death, but remaining till death unalterably in the devoted condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. All the tithe of the land, whether of the seed of the land or of the fruit of the tree, is the LORD's, it is holy unto the LORD, the law sanctioning an ancient usage, the whole produce being subjected to a yearly rent paid by tenants to God, the land's owner, as thank offering for providence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. If a man will at all redeem aught of his tithes, he shall add thereto the fifth part thereof, tithe redemption costing value plus a fifth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Concerning the tithe of herd or flock, even of whatsoever passes under the rod, the tenth shall be holy unto the LORD, cattle being made to pass singly through a narrow gateway where a man with a rod dipped in ochre marked every tenth beast, male or female, sound or unsound. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. He shall not search whether it is good or bad, neither shall he change it, and if he changes it at all, then both it and the change thereof shall be holy, it shall not be redeemed, the tenth being taken without selection or exchange. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. These are the commandments which the LORD commanded Moses for Israel in Mount Sinai, the book's laws, for the most part ceremonial, having important spiritual bearing, a burdensome yoke yet in the Church's infancy the necessary discipline of a schoolmaster to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
