The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ezekiel 34 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the word of the LORD came unto me, saying,

2 Son of man, prophesy against the shepherds of Israel, prophesy, and say unto them, Thus saith the Lord GOD unto the shepherds; Woe be to the shepherds of Israel that do feed themselves! should not the shepherds feed the flocks?

3 Ye eat the fat, and ye clothe you with the wool, ye kill them that are fed: but ye feed not the flock.

4 The diseased have ye not strengthened, neither have ye healed that which was sick, neither have ye bound up that which was broken, neither have ye brought again that which was driven away, neither have ye sought that which was lost; but with force and with cruelty have ye ruled them.

5 And they were scattered, because there is no shepherd: and they became meat to all the beasts of the field, when they were scattered.

6 My sheep wandered through all the mountains, and upon every high hill: yea, my flock was scattered upon all the face of the earth, and none did search or seek after them.

7 Therefore, ye shepherds, hear the word of the LORD;

8 As I live, saith the Lord GOD, surely because my flock became a prey, and my flock became meat to every beast of the field, because there was no shepherd, neither did my shepherds search for my flock, but the shepherds fed themselves, and fed not my flock;

9 Therefore, O ye shepherds, hear the word of the LORD;

10 Thus saith the Lord GOD; Behold, I am against the shepherds; and I will require my flock at their hand, and cause them to cease from feeding the flock; neither shall the shepherds feed themselves any more; for I will deliver my flock from their mouth, that they may not be meat for them.

11 For thus saith the Lord GOD; Behold, I, even I, will both search my sheep, and seek them out.

12 As a shepherd seeketh out his flock in the day that he is among his sheep that are scattered; so will I seek out my sheep, and will deliver them out of all places where they have been scattered in the cloudy and dark day.

13 And I will bring them out from the people, and gather them from the countries, and will bring them to their own land, and feed them upon the mountains of Israel by the rivers, and in all the inhabited places of the country.

14 I will feed them in a good pasture, and upon the high mountains of Israel shall their fold be: there shall they lie in a good fold, and in a fat pasture shall they feed upon the mountains of Israel.

15 I will feed my flock, and I will cause them to lie down, saith the Lord GOD.

16 I will seek that which was lost, and bring again that which was driven away, and will bind up that which was broken, and will strengthen that which was sick: but I will destroy the fat and the strong; I will feed them with judgment.

17 And as for you, O my flock, thus saith the Lord GOD; Behold, I judge between cattle and cattle, between the rams and the he goats.

18 Seemeth it a small thing unto you to have eaten up the good pasture, but ye must tread down with your feet the residue of your pastures? and to have drunk of the deep waters, but ye must foul the residue with your feet?

19 And as for my flock, they eat that which ye have trodden with your feet; and they drink that which ye have fouled with your feet.

20 Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD unto them; Behold, I, even I, will judge between the fat cattle and between the lean cattle.

21 Because ye have thrust with side and with shoulder, and pushed all the diseased with your horns, till ye have scattered them abroad;

22 Therefore will I save my flock, and they shall no more be a prey; and I will judge between cattle and cattle.

23 And I will set up one shepherd over them, and he shall feed them, even my servant David; he shall feed them, and he shall be their shepherd.

24 And I the LORD will be their God, and my servant David a prince among them; I the LORD have spoken it.

25 And I will make with them a covenant of peace, and will cause the evil beasts to cease out of the land: and they shall dwell safely in the wilderness, and sleep in the woods.

26 And I will make them and the places round about my hill a blessing; and I will cause the shower to come down in his season; there shall be showers of blessing.

27 And the tree of the field shall yield her fruit, and the earth shall yield her increase, and they shall be safe in their land, and shall know that I am the LORD, when I have broken the bands of their yoke, and delivered them out of the hand of those that served themselves of them.

28 And they shall no more be a prey to the heathen, neither shall the beast of the land devour them; but they shall dwell safely, and none shall make them afraid.

29 And I will raise up for them a plant of renown, and they shall be no more consumed with hunger in the land, neither bear the shame of the heathen any more.

30 Thus shall they know that I the LORD their God am with them, and that they, even the house of Israel, are my people, saith the Lord GOD.

31 And ye my flock, the flock of my pasture, are men, and I am your God, saith the Lord GOD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The shepherds of Israel are indicted for feeding themselves instead of the flock: they eat the fat, clothe themselves with wool, and leave the scattered sheep unhealed and unsought. God himself will seek his flock, gather them, and set over them one shepherd, his servant David; a covenant of peace and showers of blessing will follow. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The word of the LORD comes against Israel's shepherds, who neglected duty and abused trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Prophesy against Israel's shepherds: Woe to shepherds feeding themselves! Should not shepherds feed the flocks? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. No rank or office exempts neglect: the shepherds abused the trust reposed in them, feeding themselves while the flock went unfed, duty neglected and trust abused together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Diseased not strengthened, sick not healed, broken not bound, driven not brought again, lost not sought, but ruled with force and cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The people became as sheep without a shepherd and were given up as prey to their enemies: scattering was not the worst of it, exposure to enemies was. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The charge is total neglect: none of the shepherds searched or sought the strays at all, so the flock wandered unlooked-for until God himself undertook the search. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. This summons to hear opens Henry's mercy section (vv 7-16): the Lord himself intends mercy toward the scattered flock, finding them in darkness and ignorance and bringing them to his fold, as the good Shepherd's tender care of his people's souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. As God lives, because his flock became prey and meat to beasts, with no shepherd searching, while shepherds fed themselves and not the flock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The summons is repeated after the charge is restated, shepherds who fed themselves and never searched for the flock; Henry's point is that the Lord will require the flock at their hand, cause them to cease feeding it, and deliver it from their mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. God is against the shepherds, will require his flock at their hand, cause them to cease feeding, and deliver his flock from their mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. For God himself, even he, will both search his sheep and seek them out, in tender care of the good Shepherd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. As a shepherd seeks his scattered flock in the day he is among them, so God will seek his sheep and deliver them from all scattered places in the cloudy and dark day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The mercy is restoration first: a people dispersed among nations are brought home to their own land, and the good Shepherd's tender care is the larger picture behind the return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. He finds them in darkness and ignorance and brings them to his fold: good pasture and a good fold together mean provision with safety, on the mountains where the Shepherd leads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. He leads them in righteousness and causes them to rest on his love and faithfulness: lying down is rest for disquieted saints under the good Shepherd, not mere idleness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. He will seek the lost, bring again the driven, bind the broken, strengthen the sick, but destroy the fat and strong, feeding them with judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The whole nation seemed one flock, yet they were very different characters, and God knew how to distinguish between them: judgment sorts character, not membership. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Seemeth it small to eat up good pasture but tread down the residue, and drink deep waters but foul the residue with feet? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. And as for God's flock, they eat what the strong trod with feet and drink what they fouled with feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Judgment between fat and lean is judgment between oppressor and oppressed: the strong who fed themselves are fed with judgment, while the lean are delivered from their mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. With force and cruelty they ruled: side-thrusts, shoulder-pushes and horn-pushes against the diseased scattered the weak abroad, rule by force rather than shepherding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Deliverance and discrimination come together: the flock is delivered from the shepherds' mouth so it is meat for them no more, and cattle is judged against cattle within it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Verses 23-31 prophesy of Christ: one shepherd over the flock, David's greater Son, under whom as good Shepherd the church becomes a blessing to all around. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. The LORD is their God and David his prince among them: covenant order restored, with the shepherd-prince standing under God rather than in God's place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. The covenant of peace ends the prey: evil beasts cease and the flock dwells safely and sleeps in woods and wilderness, safety in the very places that once exposed it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Under the good Shepherd the church would be a blessing to all around, with showers of blessings wherever Christ's truth is preached, blessing that falls in season on the hill and its surroundings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Field trees shall yield fruit, earth her increase, and they shall be safe, knowing he is the LORD when yoke bands are broken and they are delivered from self-servers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. No more prey to heathen nor devoured by beasts, but dwelling safely, none making them afraid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. God will raise up a plant of renown, and they shall be no more consumed with hunger nor bear heathen shame any more, the Tree of life bearing salvation's fruits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. Knowledge comes by deliverance: they know the LORD is with them and they are his people because the bands of their yoke are broken and they are delivered from self-serving hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. The closing word seals the bond: the flock of his pasture are men, and he is their God, a people owned by a Shepherd who feeds rather than feeds on them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
