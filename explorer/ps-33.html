The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 33 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Rejoice in the LORD, O ye righteous: for praise is comely for the upright.

2 Praise the LORD with harp: sing unto him with the psaltery and an instrument of ten strings.

3 Sing unto him a new song; play skilfully with a loud noise.

4 For the word of the LORD is right; and all his works are done in truth.

5 He loveth righteousness and judgment: the earth is full of the goodness of the LORD.

6 By the word of the LORD were the heavens made; and all the host of them by the breath of his mouth.

7 He gathereth the waters of the sea together as an heap: he layeth up the depth in storehouses.

8 Let all the earth fear the LORD: let all the inhabitants of the world stand in awe of him.

9 For he spake, and it was done; he commanded, and it stood fast.

10 The LORD bringeth the counsel of the heathen to nought: he maketh the devices of the people of none effect.

11 The counsel of the LORD standeth for ever, the thoughts of his heart to all generations.

12 Blessed is the nation whose God is the LORD; and the people whom he hath chosen for his own inheritance.

13 The LORD looketh from heaven; he beholdeth all the sons of men.

14 From the place of his habitation he looketh upon all the inhabitants of the earth.

15 He fashioneth their hearts alike; he considereth all their works.

16 There is no king saved by the multitude of an host: a mighty man is not delivered by much strength.

17 An horse is a vain thing for safety: neither shall he deliver any by his great strength.

18 Behold, the eye of the LORD is upon them that fear him, upon them that hope in his mercy;

19 To deliver their soul from death, and to keep them alive in famine.

20 Our soul waiteth for the LORD: he is our help and our shield.

21 For our heart shall rejoice in him, because we have trusted in his holy name.

22 Let thy mercy, O LORD, be upon us, according as we hope in thee.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A new song for the Creator-King: praise fits the upright, and by God's word alone the heavens were made and the seas stored. Human counsels dissolve while his counsel stands for ever; no king is saved by armies, nor a horse by strength. The Lord's eye rests on those who fear and hope, and the people wait for help and shield. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Rejoice in the Lord, you righteous, for praise is comely for the upright: praise fits the upright as a garment fits its wearer — it is becoming, not forced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Praise the Lord with harp; sing to him with the psaltery and an instrument of ten strings: instruments are named because praise should use the best skill and fullest sound available. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Sing to him a new song; play skilfully with a loud noise: new mercies deserve new songs, played with skill and with joyful loudness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For the word of the Lord is right, and all his works are done in truth: the ground of praise is God's right word and truthful works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He loves righteousness and judgment; the earth is full of the goodness of the Lord: love of justice and a goodness that fills the earth are the character praised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. By the word of the Lord the heavens were made, and all their host by the breath of his mouth: creation by word alone — no tools, no struggle — is the proof of his power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He gathers the waters of the sea as a heap, and lays up the depth in storehouses: the seas, which threaten chaos, are stored like goods in a warehouse under God's control. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Let all the earth fear the Lord; let all the world's inhabitants stand in awe of him: the Creator's power commands universal fear and awe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. For he spoke, and it was done; he commanded, and it stood fast: speech and standing fast — the word creates and sustains in one act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The Lord brings the counsel of the heathen to nought, and makes the devices of the people of none effect: human counsels, however grand, dissolve before God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The counsel of the Lord stands for ever, the thoughts of his heart to all generations: against vanishing human plans stands a counsel that outlives generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Blessed is the nation whose God is the Lord, and the people he has chosen for his own inheritance: national blessedness is not size or wealth but having the Lord as God and being his chosen inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The Lord looks from heaven and beholds all the sons of men: his gaze is universal — no man is beneath his notice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. From the place of his habitation he looks upon all the earth's inhabitants: heaven is not distance but vantage for seeing all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He fashions their hearts alike, and considers all their works: the one Fashioner of hearts knows every heart he made and weighs every work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. No king is saved by the multitude of a host, and a mighty man is not delivered by much strength: armies and strength fail as saviours, however multiplied. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. A horse is a vain thing for safety, neither shall it deliver any by its great strength: the war-horse, the ancient tank, is vanity as a saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Behold, the eye of the Lord is upon those who fear him, upon those who hope in his mercy: the same eye that sees all rests with special care on the fearing and hoping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. To deliver their soul from death, and keep them alive in famine: God's eye has a purpose — deliverance from death and preservation in famine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Our soul waits for the Lord; he is our help and shield: waiting is the people's posture, and God is both help and shield in the waiting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. For our heart shall rejoice in him, because we have trusted in his holy name: joy follows trust, and the name trusted is holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Let your mercy, Lord, be upon us, according as we hope in you: the closing prayer measures mercy by hope — as hope rises, so may mercy rest on us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
