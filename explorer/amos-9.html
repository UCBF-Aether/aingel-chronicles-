The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Amos 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 I saw the LORD standing upon the altar: and he said, Smite the lintel of the door, that the posts may shake: and cut them in the head, all of them; and I will slay the last of them with the sword: he that fleeth of them shall not flee away, and he that escapeth of them shall not be delivered.

2 Though they dig into hell, thence shall mine hand take them; though they climb up to heaven, thence will I bring them down:

3 And though they hide themselves in the top of Carmel, I will search and take them out thence; and though they be hid from my sight in the bottom of the sea, thence will I command the serpent, and he shall bite them:

4 And though they go into captivity before their enemies, thence will I command the sword, and it shall slay them: and I will set mine eyes upon them for evil, and not for good.

5 And the Lord GOD of hosts is he that toucheth the land, and it shall melt, and all that dwell therein shall mourn: and it shall rise up wholly like a flood; and shall be drowned, as by the flood of Egypt.

6 It is he that buildeth his stories in the heaven, and hath founded his troop in the earth; he that calleth for the waters of the sea, and poureth them out upon the face of the earth: The LORD is his name.

7 Are ye not as children of the Ethiopians unto me, O children of Israel? saith the LORD. Have not I brought up Israel out of the land of Egypt? and the Philistines from Caphtor, and the Syrians from Kir?

8 Behold, the eyes of the Lord GOD are upon the sinful kingdom, and I will destroy it from off the face of the earth; saving that I will not utterly destroy the house of Jacob, saith the LORD.

9 For, lo, I will command, and I will sift the house of Israel among all nations, like as corn is sifted in a sieve, yet shall not the least grain fall upon the earth.

10 All the sinners of my people shall die by the sword, which say, The evil shall not overtake nor prevent us.

11 In that day will I raise up the tabernacle of David that is fallen, and close up the breaches thereof; and I will raise up his ruins, and I will build it as in the days of old:

12 That they may possess the remnant of Edom, and of all the heathen, which are called by my name, saith the LORD that doeth this.

13 Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that the plowman shall overtake the reaper, and the treader of grapes him that soweth seed; and the mountains shall drop sweet wine, and all the hills shall melt.

14 And I will bring again the captivity of my people of Israel, and they shall build the waste cities, and inhabit them; and they shall plant vineyards, and drink the wine thereof; they shall also make gardens, and eat the fruit of them.

15 And I will plant them upon their land, and they shall no more be pulled up out of their land which I have given them, saith the LORD thy God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The LORD is seen standing on the altar, and no hiding place escapes: not hell, not heaven, not Carmel, not the bottom of the sea. Yet the sinful kingdom will not be utterly destroyed; Israel will be sifted among all nations like corn in a sieve, and afterward the tabernacle of David that is fallen will be raised up, the cities rebuilt, the vineyards planted, and the people planted on their land, never again to be pulled up. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Amos saw the Lord standing upon the altar, saying, smite the lintel that the posts may shake, cut them in the head, all of them, and slay the last with the sword, none fleeing or escaping delivered, the Lord standing on the idolatrous altar at Bethel itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Though they dig into hell, God's hand shall take them there, and though they climb to heaven, he will bring them down, wherever sinners flee from God's justice, it will overtake them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Though they hide in Carmel's top, God will search and take them out, and though hid in the sea's bottom, he will command the serpent to bite them, no depth hiding from the Judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Though they go into captivity before enemies, God will command the sword to slay them there, setting his eyes upon them for evil and not for good, wretched being those on whom the Lord looks for evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The Lord God of hosts is he who touches the land and it shall melt, all dwelling therein mourning, rising wholly like a flood and drowned as by Egypt's flood, the Creator's touch unmaking what he made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. It is he who builds his stories in heaven, founded his troop in the earth, calls the sea's waters and pours them on the earth's face: the Lord is his name, the same power that orders heaven and earth ordering judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Are you not as Ethiopians' children to me, O children of Israel, says the Lord, have I not brought up Israel from Egypt, Philistines from Caphtor, and Syrians from Kir, God's migrations of other nations showing Israel's election did not make them exempt, and professors made like the world being leveled with the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Behold, the Lord God's eyes are upon the sinful kingdom, and he will destroy it from the earth's face, saving that he will not utterly destroy Jacob's house, says the Lord, judgment on the kingdom but preservation of a remnant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. For God will command and sift Israel among all nations like corn sifted in a sieve, yet not the least grain shall fall to the earth, the astonishing preservation of the Jews as a distinct people foretold, the sieve scattering chaff while keeping grain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. All the sinners of God's people shall die by the sword, who say, the evil shall not overtake nor prevent us, self-flattering profession not protecting those who presume on it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. In that day God will raise up David's fallen tabernacle, close its breaches, raise its ruins, and build it as in days of old, Christ dying to gather God's scattered children, those called by his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. That they may possess Edom's remnant and all the heathen called by God's name, says the Lord who does this, who can do it, who has determined to do it, grace's power engaged for doing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Behold, days come, says the Lord, when the plowman shall overtake the reaper, and the grape-treader him who sows seed, mountains dropping sweet wine and hills melting, harvests so abundant that sowing and reaping overlap. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And God will bring again his people Israel's captivity, and they shall build waste cities and inhabit them, plant vineyards and drink their wine, make gardens and eat their fruit, restoration as tangible as the ruin was. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And God will plant them upon their land, and they shall no more be pulled up out of their given land, says the Lord their God, God marvelously preserving his elect amid confusions and miseries, reviving his church, and leaving not one good thing promised unfulfilled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms
