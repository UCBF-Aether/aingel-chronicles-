The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 31 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And on the new moon of the month Jacob spake to all the people of his house, saying : " Purify yourselves and change your garments, and let us arise and go up to Bethel, where I vowed a vow to Him on the day when I fled from the face of Esau my brother, because He hath been with me and brought me into this land in peace, and put ye away the strange gods that are among you." 2.

2 And they gave up the strange gods and that which was in their ears and which was on their necks, and the idols which Rachel stole from Laban her father she gave wholly to Jacob.

3 And he burnt and brake them to pieces and destroyed them, and hid them under an oak which is in the land of Shechem.

4 And he went up on the new moon of the seventh month to Bethel.

5 And he built an altar at the place where he had slept, and he set up a pillar there, and he sent word to his father Isaac to come to him to his sacrifice, and to his mother Rebecca.

6 And Isaac said : " Let my son Jacob come, and let me see him before I die." 5.

7 And Jacob went to his father Isaac and to his mother Rebecca, to the house of his father Abraham and he took two of his sons with him, Levi and Judah, and he came to his father Isaac and to his mother Rebecca.

8 And Rebecca came forth from the tower to the front of it to kiss Jacob and embrace him; for her spirit had revived when she heard : " Behold Jacob thy son hath come " ; and she kissed him.

9 And she saw his two sons, and she recognised them, and said unto him : " Are these thy sons, my son?

10 " and she embraced them and kissed them, and blessed them, saying: " In you shall the seed of Abraham become illustrious, and ye will prove a blessing on the earth." 8.

11 And Jacob went in to Isaac his father, to the chamber where he lay, and his two sons were with him, and he took the hand of his father, and stooping down he kissed him, and Isaac clung to the neck of Jacob his son, and wept upon his neck.

12 And the darkness left the eyes of Isaac, and he saw the two sons of Jacob, Levi and Judah, and he said : " Are these thy sons, my son ? for they are like thee." 10.

13 And he said unto him that they were truly his sons : " And thou hast truly seen that they are truly my sons." 11.

14 And they came near to him, and he turned and kissed them and embraced them both together.

15 And the spirit of prophecy came down into his mouth, and he took Levi by his right hand and Judah by his left.

16 And he turned to Levi first and began to bless him first, and said unto him : " May the God of all, the very Lord of all the ages, bless thee and thy children throughout all the ages.

17 And may the Lord give to thee and to thy seed greatness and great

18 glory, and cause thee and thy seed, from among all flesh, to approach Him to serve in His sanctuary as the angels of the presence and as the holy ones.

19 (Even) as they, will the seed of thy sons be for glory and greatness and holiness, and may He make them great unto all the ages.

20 And they will be princes and judges, and chiefs of all the seed of the sons of Jacob ;

21 They will speak the word of the Lord in righteousness,

22 And they will judge all His judgments in righteousness.

23 And they will declare My ways to Jacob

24 And My paths to Israel.

25 The blessing of the Lord will be given in their mouths

26 To bless all the seed of the beloved.

27 Thy mother hath called thy name Levi, And justly hath she called thy name; Thou wilt be joined to the Lord

28 And be the companion of all the sons of Jacob ;

29 Let His table be thine

30 And do thou and thy sons eat thereof ;

31 And may thy table be full unto all generations,

32 And thy food fail not unto all the ages.

33 And let all who hate thee fall down before thee, And let all thy adversaries be rooted out and

34 perish ; And blessed be he that blesseth thee, And cursed be every nation that curseth thee.

35 And to Judah he said :

36 May the Lord give thee strength and power

37 To tread down all that hate thee ;

38 A prince shalt thou be, thou and one of thy sons

39 over the sons of Jacob ; May thy name and the name of thy sons go

40 forth and traverse every land and region.

41 Then will the Gentiles fear before thy face, And all the nations will quake [And all the peoples will quake].

42 In thee shall be the help of Jacob,

43 And in thee be found the salvation of Israel.

44 And when thou sittest on the throne of the

45 honour of thy righteousness,

46 There will be great peace for all the seed of the sons of the beloved

47 And blessed will he be that blesseth thee ;

48 And all that hate thee and afflict thee and curse thee

49 Shall be rooted out and destroyed from the earth and accursed." 21.

50 And turning he kissed him again and embraced him, and rejoiced greatly ; for he had seen the sons of Jacob his son in very truth.

51 And he went forth from between his feet and fell down and worshipped him.

52 And he blessed them.

53 And (Jacob) rested there with Isaac his father that night, and they ate and drank with joy.

54 And he made the two sons of Jacob sleep, the one on his right hand and the other on his left, and it was counted to him for righteousness.

55 And Jacob told his father everything during the night, how the Lord had shown him great mercy, and how He had prospered (him in) all his ways, and protected him from all evil.

56 And Isaac blessed the God of his father Abraham, who had not withdrawn His mercy and His righteousness from the sons of His servant Isaac.

57 Rebecca journeys with Jacob to Bethel

58 And in the morning Jacob told his father Isaac the vow which he had vowed to the Lord, and the vision which he had seen, and that he had built an altar and that everything was ready for the sacrifice to be made before the Lord as he had vowed, and that he had come to set him on an ass.

59 And Isaac said unto Jacob his son : "I am not able to go with thee ; for I am old, and not able to bear the way : go, my son, in peace ; for I am one hundred and sixty-five years this day ; I am no longer able to journey; set thy mother (on an ass) and let her go with thee.

60 And I know, my son, that thou hast come on my account, and may this day be blessed on which thou hast seen me alive, and I also have seen thee, my son.

61 Mayest thou prosper and fulfil the vow which thou hast vowed; and put not off thy vow; for thou wilt be called to account as touching the vow; now therefore make haste to perform it, and may He be pleased who hath made all things, to whom thou hast vowed the vow." 30.

62 And he said to Rebecca : " Go with Jacob thy son " ; and Rebecca went with Jacob her son, and Deborah with her, and they came to Bethel.

63 And Jacob remembered the prayer with which his father had blessed him and his two sons, Levi and Judah, and he rejoiced and blessed the God of his fathers, Abraham and Isaac.

64 And he said : " Now I know that I have an eternal hope, and my sons also, before the God of all ; " and thus is it ordained concerning the two; and they record it as an eternal testimony unto them on the heavenly tables how Isaac blessed them.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: On the new moon Jacob told his whole household to purify themselves, change garments, go up to Bethel where he had vowed his vow when fleeing Esau, and put away the strange gods among them. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. On the new moon Jacob told his whole household to purify themselves, change garments, go up to Bethel where he had vowed his vow when fleeing Esau, and put away the strange gods among them. Henry stresses that masters of families should use their authority to keep up religion in their families, and that outward cleansing signified purifying and changing the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The household gave up the strange gods, the ornaments of ears and necks, and the idols Rachel had stolen from Laban, giving them wholly to Jacob. Henry notes that in families where there is a face of religion, yet many times there is much amiss, and more strange gods than one would suppose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Jacob burnt, broke, and destroyed the idols, hiding them under an oak in the land of Shechem. Henry's rule applies: we must be wholly separated from our sins, as we are from those that are dead and buried out of sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Jacob went up on the new moon of the seventh month to Bethel. Henry notes that Bethel had been forgotten, but God reminds those he loves of neglected duties, and that when we have vowed a vow to God, it is best not to defer the payment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Jacob built an altar at the place where he had slept, set up a pillar, and sent word to his father Isaac and mother Rebecca to come to his sacrifice. Henry observes that the comfort of holy ordinances lies not in Bethel the house of God, but in El-bethel, the God of the house, whom Jacob now worshipped with his family gathered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Isaac answered that his son Jacob should come, so that he might see him before he died. Henry notes that Isaac lived long after making his will, and that timely setting of heart and house in order is the wise course for an aged father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Jacob went to his father Isaac and mother Rebecca at the house of his father Abraham, taking two sons, Levi and Judah, with him. The choice of these two sons is the point: Jubilees is preparing the priestly and royal lines, the same two tribes Henry finds singled out in Jacob's later blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Rebecca came forth from the tower to kiss and embrace Jacob, for her spirit had revived when she heard her son had come. The reunion answers the long separation that began with the flight to Haran, and Henry's account of Isaac's household stresses the agreement and affection that God had restored between its members. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Rebecca saw Jacob's two sons, recognised them, and asked whether these were his sons. The grandmother's question opens her blessing on the next generation, the seed through whom the covenant would continue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Rebecca embraced and kissed the boys, blessing them that in them the seed of Abraham would become illustrious and prove a blessing on the earth. Henry's reading of the tribal blessings fits: these addresses declare God's purpose respecting the tribes, traced in their histories, not private affection alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Jacob went in to Isaac's chamber with his two sons, took his father's hand, kissed him, and Isaac clung to Jacob's neck and wept. The embrace reverses the blind blessing scene: the father who once felt for a son in disguise now holds his true son openly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The darkness left Isaac's eyes, and he saw Levi and Judah, asking whether these were Jacob's sons, for they were like him. Henry's account of Isaac's dim eyes in the blessing chapter makes this restoration pointed: the father who could not discern now sees clearly, and the blessing that follows is given with open eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Jacob affirmed that the boys were truly his sons, and that Isaac had truly seen them. The affirmation removes any shadow of the old deception: these are Jacob's sons, presented plainly before their grandfather. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The boys came near, and Isaac turned, kissed, and embraced them both together. The embrace joins the two lines, priestly and royal, in one grandfather's arms before their separate blessings are spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The spirit of prophecy came down into Isaac's mouth, and he took Levi by his right hand and Judah by his left. Henry insists that such blessings are the language of the Holy Ghost, declaring God's purpose for the tribes, not the expression of private partiality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Isaac turned to Levi first and began to bless him first, praying that the God of all, the Lord of all the ages, would bless him and his children throughout all the ages. Jubilees puts in Isaac's mouth the priestly destiny that Genesis gives to Levi through Jacob, and Henry agrees on the substance: Levi's sentence was turned into a blessing, and the tribe was set apart to God as priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Isaac prayed that the Lord would give Levi and his seed greatness and great glory, and cause them to approach him to serve in his sanctuary as the angels of the presence and the holy ones. Henry notes that Levi's tribe, set apart to God as priests, was scattered through the nation in that character, serving before the Lord continually. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The blessing continued that Levi's seed would serve in the sanctuary as the angels of the presence and the holy ones. The comparison lifts the priesthood to the heavenly pattern: earthly service mirrors the worship of the angels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Even as the angels, Levi's sons would be for glory, greatness, and holiness, made great unto all the ages. The priesthood's honour is not wealth or land but nearness to God, which Henry calls the acceptable service of the tribe set apart to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Levi's sons would be princes, judges, and chiefs of all the seed of Jacob. The priestly tribe would also govern and judge, teaching and ruling the nation it served, a breadth of office Henry sees in Levi's scattering through Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. They would speak the word of the Lord in righteousness, the priest's first office being to declare God's word truly. The blessing makes speech, not sacrifice alone, the centre of Levi's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. They would judge all God's judgments in righteousness, deciding cases by his law rather than by favour or bribery. Judgment and teaching go together in the blessing, as they did in the tribe's later office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. They would declare God's ways to Jacob, carrying the knowledge of his dealings to the whole people. The tribe that knew the law would be the tribe that taught it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. They would declare God's paths to Israel, completing the pair: his ways to Jacob, his paths to Israel. The two names for the people frame the teaching office as covering the whole nation under both its names. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The blessing of the Lord would be given in Levi's mouth, to bless all the seed of the beloved. The priest blesses in God's name, and the blessing he speaks is the Lord's own, not his invention. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The blessing in Levi's mouth is for all the seed of the beloved, the whole chosen people. No tribe is excluded from the priestly blessing, though one tribe is set apart to pronounce it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Isaac played on Levi's name, which his mother had justly called him, for he would be joined to the Lord. Henry notes the same name-play in the tribal blessings, where names declare character and destiny. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Levi would be the companion of all the sons of Jacob, joined to every tribe rather than settled in one territory. Henry explains the scattering of Levi as the form his blessing took: priests spread through the nation, companions to all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. The Lord's table would be Levi's, the portion of the priest being the offerings of the altar. The table stands for the priest's livelihood from the sanctuary, not from land of his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Levi and his sons would eat of the Lord's table, sharing the holy food of the sanctuary. The eating is fellowship as well as provision: the priest eats where he serves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Levi's table would be full unto all generations, and his food would not fail unto all the ages. The promise secures the priesthood's support across time, matching the blessing's extension to all ages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The blessing adds that Levi's food would not fail unto all the ages, repeating the assurance for emphasis. Perpetuity is the mark of this blessing: what God gives the priesthood, no age takes away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. All who hate Levi would fall before him, his adversaries would be rooted out and perish, and the ancient formula would guard him: blessed be he that blesseth thee, and cursed be every nation that curseth thee. The formula first guarded Abraham, and now it guards the priestly tribe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The blessing's closing curse and blessing are then gathered up in the next verse's turn to Judah. The formula binds Levi's enemies to the same fate as Abraham's, and his friends to the same blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Isaac turned to Judah, the second son to be blessed. The turn from Levi to Judah sets the priestly and royal blessings side by side, as Genesis sets them in Jacob's blessing of his sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Isaac prayed that the Lord would give Judah strength and power to tread down all that hate him. Henry describes Judah as a strong and courageous tribe, a lion enjoying the satisfaction of his power without creating vexation to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Judah would tread down all that hate him, the royal tribe prevailing over its enemies. Henry notes that Judah's brethren would praise him, for his name signifies praise, and God was praised in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Judah would be a prince, he and one of his sons, over the sons of Jacob. Henry states the substance: Judah should be the royal tribe, the tribe from which Messiah the Prince should come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Judah's name and his sons' names would go forth and traverse every land and region. The renown of the royal tribe would spread beyond Israel, as the name of its greatest Son would reach all nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. The Gentiles would fear before Judah's face, and all nations would quake. The fear of the nations answers the praise of the brethren: Judah's royalty would be felt abroad as well as at home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. The nations would quake before Judah, completing the picture of a royalty acknowledged by the world. Henry sees this fulfilled in the Prince who came from Judah, in whom the earth should be blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. In Judah would be the help of Jacob, and in him the salvation of Israel. Henry applies the point directly: much said concerning Judah is to be applied to our Lord Jesus, the Shiloh in whom the earth should be blessed, the peaceable and prosperous Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. In Judah would be found the salvation of Israel, the deliverance the nation waited for. Jacob himself, Henry notes, waited for the salvation of the Lord, the promised Seed to whom the gathering of the people would be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. When Judah sat on the throne of the honour of his righteousness, there would be great peace for all the seed of the sons of the beloved. The throne of righteousness brings peace, the fruit Henry finds in the Prince of Peace from Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. The throne's honour is righteousness, and its fruit is peace for the whole beloved seed. The blessing thus joins kingship, justice, and peace in one reign, the pattern of the Messiah's rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Great peace would cover all the seed of the sons of the beloved under Judah's righteous throne. Peace is the blessing's end, not conquest alone, and Henry's Shiloh is the peaceable One who brings it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Blessed would be he that blessed Judah, and all that hated, afflicted, or cursed him would be rooted out, destroyed from the earth, and accursed. The Abrahamic formula closes Judah's blessing as it closed Levi's, guarding the royal tribe as it guarded the priestly one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Those who hate, afflict, and curse Judah would be rooted out and destroyed from the earth and accursed. The triple description of enmity meets a triple judgment, leaving no form of opposition unanswered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. The curse on Judah's enemies closes with the word accursed, sealing the blessing's protection. The formula's severity matches the blessing's height: the throne it guards is the throne of the promised Prince. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Isaac turned, kissed Judah again, embraced him, and rejoiced greatly, for he had seen Jacob's sons in very truth. The joy closes the prophetic scene: the blind father's restored sight has confirmed what his spirit spoke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. Jacob went forth from between his father's feet, fell down, and worshipped him. The son's reverence answers the father's blessing, honour given where honour was received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Isaac blessed them, sealing the blessings of Levi and Judah with a final blessing on them all. The seal confirms that the prophetic words were not a father's wishes but a blessing given in God's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. Jacob rested that night with his father Isaac, and they ate and drank with joy. The shared meal after the blessings is fellowship restored, the household eating together in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. During the night Jacob told his father everything: how the Lord had shown him great mercy, prospered all his ways, and protected him from all evil. Henry's account of Jacob's return stresses the same testimony: God had been with him, brought him into the land in peace, and renewed the covenant with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Isaac blessed the God of Abraham, who had not withdrawn his mercy and righteousness from the sons of his servant Isaac. The father's praise answers the son's testimony: mercy kept through exile is mercy proved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 58. In the morning Jacob told Isaac the vow he had vowed, the vision he had seen, and that he had built an altar with everything ready for the sacrifice, and that he had come to set his father on an ass. Henry notes that when we have vowed a vow to God, it is best not to defer the payment, and Jacob now came to pay his Bethel vow in full. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 59. Isaac answered that he could not go with Jacob, for he was old and unable to bear the way, being one hundred and sixty-five years old that day; Jacob should go in peace, and set his mother on an ass instead. Henry notes Isaac's great age and the timely ordering of his house, and the father's release of his son to fulfil the vow without him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 60. Isaac said he knew Jacob had come on his account, and blessed the day on which he had seen his son alive, and his son had seen him. The mutual sight is itself the blessing: father and son alive together after years of separation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 61. Isaac charged Jacob to prosper and fulfil the vow he had vowed, not to put it off, for he would be called to account concerning it, and to make haste to perform it. Henry's warning stands behind the charge: better late than never, but a vow deferred is a duty left unpaid, and God will require it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 62. Isaac told Rebecca to go with Jacob, and Rebecca went with Jacob and Deborah, and they came to Bethel. Deborah, Rebecca's nurse, travels with the household to the place of the vow, the faithful old servant still in the family's company. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 63. Jacob remembered the prayer with which his father had blessed him and his two sons, Levi and Judah, and rejoiced, blessing the God of Abraham and Isaac. The memory of the blessing became itself a cause of praise on the road to Bethel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 64. Jacob said he now knew he had an eternal hope, and his sons also, before the God of all, and that this was ordained concerning the two, recorded as an eternal testimony on the heavenly tables. Henry's reading of the tribal blessings agrees on the substance: these words declare God's purpose for the tribes, traced through their histories to the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Jubilees puts in Isaac's mouth the priestly destiny that Genesis gives to Levi through Jacob, and Henry agrees on the substance: Levi's sentence was turned into a blessing, and the tribe was set apart to God as priests.   • Verse 41. The nations would quake before Judah, completing the picture of a royalty acknowledged by the world. Henry sees this fulfilled in the Prince who came from Judah, in whom the earth should be blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 48. Those who hate, afflict, and curse Judah would be rooted out and destroyed from the earth and accursed. The triple description of enmity meets a triple judgment, leaving no form of opposition unanswered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. The curse on Judah's enemies closes with the word accursed, sealing the blessing's protection. The formula's severity matches the blessing's height: the throne it guards is the throne of the promised Prince. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 52. Isaac blessed them, sealing the blessings of Levi and Judah with a final blessing on them all. The seal confirms that the prophetic words were not a father's wishes but a blessing given in God's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
