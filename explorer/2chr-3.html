The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Solomon began to build the house of the LORD at Jerusalem in mount Moriah, where the Lord appeared unto David his father, in the place that David had prepared in the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite.

2 And he began to build in the second day of the second month, in the fourth year of his reign.

3 Now these are the things wherein Solomon was instructed for the building of the house of God. The length by cubits after the first measure was threescore cubits, and the breadth twenty cubits.

4 And the porch that was in the front of the house, the length of it was according to the breadth of the house, twenty cubits, and the height was an hundred and twenty: and he overlaid it within with pure gold.

5 And the greater house he cieled with fir tree, which he overlaid with fine gold, and set thereon palm trees and chains.

6 And he garnished the house with precious stones for beauty: and the gold was gold of Parvaim.

7 He overlaid also the house, the beams, the posts, and the walls thereof, and the doors thereof, with gold; and graved cherubims on the walls.

8 And he made the most holy house, the length whereof was according to the breadth of the house, twenty cubits, and the breadth thereof twenty cubits: and he overlaid it with fine gold, amounting to six hundred talents.

9 And the weight of the nails was fifty shekels of gold. And he overlaid the upper chambers with gold.

10 And in the most holy house he made two cherubims of image work, and overlaid them with gold.

11 And the wings of the cherubims were twenty cubits long: one wing of the one cherub was five cubits, reaching to the wall of the house: and the other wing was likewise five cubits, reaching to the wing of the other cherub.

12 And one wing of the other cherub was five cubits, reaching to the wall of the house: and the other wing was five cubits also, joining to the wing of the other cherub.

13 The wings of these cherubims spread themselves forth twenty cubits: and they stood on their feet, and their faces were inward.

14 And he made the vail of blue, and purple, and crimson, and fine linen, and wrought cherubims thereon.

15 Also he made before the house two pillars of thirty and five cubits high, and the chapiter that was on the top of each of them was five cubits.

16 And he made chains, as in the oracle, and put them on the heads of the pillars; and made an hundred pomegranates, and put them on the chains.

17 And he reared up the pillars before the temple, one on the right hand, and the other on the left; and called the name of that on the right hand Jachin, and the name of that on the left Boaz.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the building of the temple itself on Mount Moriah: its measurements, its gold overlay, the cherubim of the most holy place, and the two pillars before it, Jachin and Boaz. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Solomon began to build the house of the Lord at Jerusalem on Mount Moriah, where the Lord appeared to David, in the place David prepared on Ornan's threshingfloor. Henry notes Solomon was neither at liberty to choose nor at a loss to fix the place: it was determined before, at the place of atonement by fire, fitly where Abraham's offering typified Christ's sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He began on the second day of the second month, in the fourth year of his reign. Henry notes the first three years were not trifled away but spent in necessary preparations, gathering so many hands, and possibly this was a sabbatical year when the people had leisure for the work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Solomon was instructed for the building: the length by the first measure was sixty cubits, and the breadth twenty cubits. Henry notes these dimensions, given by divine wisdom and taught him by his father, were the rule he went by, and what God does shall be for ever, nothing to be added or taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The porch in front was twenty cubits long, according to the breadth of the house, and a hundred and twenty high, overlaid within with pure gold. Henry groups the porch with the temple's rich ornaments: fine timber within overlaid with pure gold, substantially rich beyond any modern house's paint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The greater house he ceiled with fir tree, overlaid with fine gold, and set thereon palm trees and chains. Henry notes the gold was embossed with palm trees and chains, the timber-work very fine yet within overlaid with fine gold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He garnished the house with precious stones for beauty, and the gold was gold of Parvaim, the best gold. Henry notes the precious stones dedicated to God were set where they would show to best advantage, as a type of the new Jerusalem whose walls and foundations are of precious stones. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He overlaid the house, its beams, posts, walls and doors, with gold, and graved cherubim on the walls. Henry notes beams, posts, walls and doors alike were overlaid, so that the whole interior shone with gold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He made the most holy house twenty cubits long and twenty broad, overlaid with fine gold amounting to six hundred talents. Henry notes the most holy place, ten yards square, was overlaid top, bottom and sides with fine gold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The weight of the nails was fifty shekels of gold, and he overlaid the upper chambers with gold. Henry notes every nail, screw or pin fastening the golden plates weighed fifty shekels, workmanship and all, and even the upper chambers were overlaid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. In the most holy house he made two cherubim of image work and overlaid them with gold. Henry notes these large cherubim were added, doubtless by divine appointment, to fill the enlarged holy place, which otherwise would have looked bare like an unfurnished room. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The wings of the cherubim were twenty cubits long: one wing of one cherub, five cubits, reached to the wall, and the other wing likewise five cubits reached to the wing of the other cherub. Henry notes each wing extended five cubits, so the whole was twenty cubits, just the breadth of the most holy place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. One wing of the other cherub, five cubits, reached to the wall, and its other wing, five cubits, joined to the wing of the other cherub. Henry's point is the same span: wing met wing across the whole breadth, filling the holy place from wall to wall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The wings spread forth twenty cubits, and the cherubim stood on their feet, their faces inward. Henry notes they stood as servants, faces toward the ark, not to be adored but as attendants on the invisible God: we must not worship angels, but worship with angels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He made the veil of blue, purple, crimson and fine linen, and wrought cherubim thereon. Henry notes the veil denoted the darkness of that dispensation and the distance worshippers were kept at, but at Christ's death it was rent, so that through him we have boldness to enter the holiest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Also he made before the house two pillars thirty-five cubits high, with a chapiter of five cubits on each. Henry notes both together were somewhat above thirty-five cubits, about eighteen cubits high apiece. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He made chains, as in the oracle, and put them on the heads of the pillars, and made a hundred pomegranates and put them on the chains. Henry groups these ornaments with the pillars Jachin and Boaz, whose names signify establishment and strength in temple-work and by it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. He reared the pillars before the temple, one on the right hand and the other on the left, calling the right Jachin and the left Boaz. Henry notes the names mean establishment and strength, a standing witness that temple-work stands firm by God's strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • cherubim. Great winged figures in the most holy place, standing as attendants with faces toward the ark — not figures to be worshipped, but servants of the invisible God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
