The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now it came to pass in the third year of Hoshea son of Elah king of Israel, that Hezekiah the son of Ahaz king of Judah began to reign.

2 Twenty and five years old was he when he began to reign; and he reigned twenty and nine years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name also was Abi, the daughter of Zachariah.

3 And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, according to all that David his father did.

4 He removed the high places, and brake the images, and cut down the groves, and brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made: for unto those days the children of Israel did burn incense to it: and he called it Nehushtan.

5 He trusted in the LORD God of Israel; so that after him was none like him among all the kings of Judah, nor any that were before him.

6 For he clave to the LORD, and departed not from following him, but kept his commandments, which the LORD commanded Moses.

7 And the LORD was with him; and he prospered whithersoever he went forth: and he rebelled against the king of Assyria, and served him not.

8 He smote the Philistines, even unto Gaza, and the borders thereof, from the tower of the watchmen to the fenced city.

9 And it came to pass in the fourth year of king Hezekiah, which was the seventh year of Hoshea son of Elah king of Israel, that Shalmaneser king of Assyria came up against Samaria, and besieged it.

10 And at the end of three years they took it: even in the sixth year of Hezekiah, that is in the ninth year of Hoshea king of Israel, Samaria was taken.

11 And the king of Assyria did carry away Israel unto Assyria, and put them in Halah and in Habor by the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes:

12 Because they obeyed not the voice of the LORD their God, but transgressed his covenant, and all that Moses the servant of the LORD commanded, and would not hear them, nor do them.

13 Now in the fourteenth year of king Hezekiah did Sennacherib king of Assyria come up against all the fenced cities of Judah, and took them.

14 And Hezekiah king of Judah sent to the king of Assyria to Lachish, saying, I have offended; return from me: that which thou puttest on me will I bear. And the king of Assyria appointed unto Hezekiah king of Judah three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold.

15 And Hezekiah gave him all the silver that was found in the house of the LORD, and in the treasures of the king’s house.

16 At that time did Hezekiah cut off the gold from the doors of the temple of the LORD, and from the pillars which Hezekiah king of Judah had overlaid, and gave it to the king of Assyria.

17 And the king of Assyria sent Tartan and Rabsaris and Rabshakeh from Lachish to king Hezekiah with a great host against Jerusalem. And they went up and came to Jerusalem. And when they were come up, they came and stood by the conduit of the upper pool, which is in the highway of the fuller’s field.

18 And when they had called to the king, there came out to them Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, which was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah the son of Asaph the recorder.

19 And Rabshakeh said unto them, Speak ye now to Hezekiah, Thus saith the great king, the king of Assyria, What confidence is this wherein thou trustest?

20 Thou sayest, (but they are but vain words,) I have counsel and strength for the war. Now on whom dost thou trust, that thou rebellest against me?

21 Now, behold, thou trustest upon the staff of this bruised reed, even upon Egypt, on which if a man lean, it will go into his hand, and pierce it: so is Pharaoh king of Egypt unto all that trust on him.

22 But if ye say unto me, We trust in the LORD our God: is not that he, whose high places and whose altars Hezekiah hath taken away, and hath said to Judah and Jerusalem, Ye shall worship before this altar in Jerusalem?

23 Now therefore, I pray thee, give pledges to my lord the king of Assyria, and I will deliver thee two thousand horses, if thou be able on thy part to set riders upon them.

24 How then wilt thou turn away the face of one captain of the least of my master’s servants, and put thy trust on Egypt for chariots and for horsemen?

25 Am I now come up without the LORD against this place to destroy it? The LORD said to me, Go up against this land, and destroy it.

26 Then said Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, and Shebna, and Joah, unto Rabshakeh, Speak, I pray thee, to thy servants in the Syrian language; for we understand it: and talk not with us in the Jews’ language in the ears of the people that are on the wall.

27 But Rabshakeh said unto them, Hath my master sent me to thy master, and to thee, to speak these words? hath he not sent me to the men which sit on the wall, that they may eat their own dung, and drink their own piss with you?

28 Then Rabshakeh stood and cried with a loud voice in the Jews’ language, and spake, saying, Hear the word of the great king, the king of Assyria:

29 Thus saith the king, Let not Hezekiah deceive you: for he shall not be able to deliver you out of his hand:

30 Neither let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD, saying, The LORD will surely deliver us, and this city shall not be delivered into the hand of the king of Assyria.

31 Hearken not to Hezekiah: for thus saith the king of Assyria, Make an agreement with me by a present, and come out to me, and then eat ye every man of his own vine, and every one of his fig tree, and drink ye every one the waters of his cistern:

32 Until I come and take you away to a land like your own land, a land of corn and wine, a land of bread and vineyards, a land of oil olive and of honey, that ye may live, and not die: and hearken not unto Hezekiah, when he persuadeth you, saying, The LORD will deliver us.

33 Hath any of the gods of the nations delivered at all his land out of the hand of the king of Assyria?

34 Where are the gods of Hamath, and of Arpad? where are the gods of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivah? have they delivered Samaria out of mine hand?

35 Who are they among all the gods of the countries, that have delivered their country out of mine hand, that the LORD should deliver Jerusalem out of mine hand?

36 But the people held their peace, and answered him not a word: for the king’s commandment was, saying, Answer him not.

37 Then came Eliakim the son of Hilkiah, which was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah the son of Asaph the recorder, to Hezekiah with their clothes rent, and told him the words of Rabshakeh.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Hezekiah, who trusted the LORD and broke the high places and the bronze serpent, faces Sennacherib's invasion and the Rabshakeh's taunts at Jerusalem's wall, in the Jews' language, for all to hear. Hezekiah's officers answer in silence and report, clothes torn, to the king. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the third year of Hoshea son of Elah king of Israel, Hezekiah son of Ahaz king of Judah began to reign. JFB explains the chronology: Hezekiah began to reign in Ahaz's fourteenth year, in partnership with his father, so he may have been twenty-two or twenty-three at his joint accession, resolving the apparent difficulty of Ahaz's age at his son's birth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Hezekiah was twenty-five when he began to reign and reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem, his mother Abi daughter of Zachariah. JFB discusses the ancient computation of years to reconcile his age with his father's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Hezekiah did right in the LORD's sight, according to all David his father did. JFB's section "Hezekiah's good reign" opens with this Davidic standard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Hezekiah removed the high places, broke the images, cut down the groves, and broke in pieces the brazen serpent Moses had made, for Israel burned incense to it, calling it Nehushtan. JFB notes destroying the brazen serpent required great courage, since it might have remained an instructive monument of God's wilderness mercy, but it had become an object of idolatrous worship, and its demolition consulted God's glory and the country's good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Hezekiah trusted in the LORD God of Israel, so that after him none was like him among Judah's kings, before or after. JFB notes he trusted without invoking foreign aid or purchasing succour like Asa and Ahaz, and, David and Solomon excepted, Josiah alone had a similar testimony, yet Hezekiah surpassed him by beginning national reformation at his reign's start, prompted by his father's calamities and Isaiah's counsels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Hezekiah clave to the LORD, departed not from following him, and kept his commandments. JFB presents this cleaving as the root of his trust, in contrast to kings who bought foreign help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The LORD was with Hezekiah, and he prospered wherever he went, rebelling against Assyria's king and serving him not. JFB explains he withdrew the yearly tribute his father had stipulated, and, through divine blessing on his theocratic policy, recovered from the Philistines territory they had taken from Ahaz. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Hezekiah smote the Philistines to Gaza and its borders, from the tower of the watchmen to the fenced city. JFB notes this energetic movement against the Philistines recovered territory taken from his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. In Hezekiah's fourteenth year Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against all Judah's fenced cities and took them. JFB identifies Sennacherib as Shalmaneser's son and successor, notes not absolutely all fenced cities fell since some strong fortresses held out, and explains Judah was the key to Egypt, the highway Assyria had to pass, and the expedition was aimed at Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Hezekiah sent to Assyria's king at Lachish, saying, "I have offended; return from me: that which thou puttest on me will I bear." JFB explains that, disappointed in Egyptian aid and unable to resist so mighty a conqueror menacing Jerusalem, Hezekiah submitted, and the payment of 300 talents of silver and 30 of gold brought temporary respite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Hezekiah gave Assyria's king all the silver found in the LORD's house and the king's treasures. JFB notes raising the tribute obliged him to drain palace and temple treasures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. At that time Hezekiah cut off the gold from the temple doors and pillars which he himself had overlaid, and gave it to Assyria's king. JFB notes he even stripped the sacred edifice's doors and pillars of their gold adornment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Assyria's king sent Tartan, Rabsaris, and Rabshakeh from Lachish to Hezekiah at Jerusalem with a great host, and they stood by the conduit of the upper pool in the fuller's field highway. JFB identifies Tartan as general, Rabsaris as chief of the eunuchs, and Rabshakeh as chief cupbearer, great officers whose portraits appear on Sennacherib's palace walls, and explains the conduit ran from the Upper Gihon reservoir to the lower pool. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. When they called to the king, Eliakim son of Hilkiah over the household, Shebna the scribe, and Joah son of Asaph the recorder came out to them. JFB notes Hezekiah did not appear personally but commissioned his three principal ministers to meet the deputies outside the walls, Eliakim lately promoted over the household, Shebna removed from that office for pride yet still secretary, and Joah keeper of the chronicles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Rabshakeh told them to speak to Hezekiah the message of the great king of Assyria: what confidence was this wherein he trusted? JFB notes the insolent tone and highly coloured picture of Assyria's powers and the impossibility of effective resistance, heightened by Oriental imagination, paralleled by other Eastern messages of defiance except for the blasphemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Rabshakeh dismissed Hezekiah's counsel and strength for war as vain words, asking on whom he trusted that he rebelled. JFB's discussion of the boasting message covers this challenge to Judah's confidence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Rabshakeh mocked trust in Egypt as leaning on a bruised reed that would pierce the hand. JFB's account of the defiant message includes this figure for Egypt's useless aid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Rabshakeh argued that if Judah trusted in the LORD, that was the God whose high places and altars Hezekiah had taken away. JFB's exposition of the message covers this attempt to turn Hezekiah's own reformation against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Rabshakeh offered two thousand horses if Hezekiah could set riders on them, challenging Judah's strength. JFB presents this as part of the boasting picture of Assyrian resources. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Rabshakeh asked how Hezekiah could turn away one captain of the least of Assyria's servants while trusting in Egypt for chariots and horsemen. JFB's discussion covers this contemptuous comparison of forces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Rabshakeh claimed he had not come up without the LORD, but that the LORD had said to him, "Go up against this land, and destroy it." JFB notes the message's blasphemy exceeded even parallel Eastern defiances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah asked Rabshakeh to speak in the Syrian language, which they understood, and not in the Jews' language within the people's hearing. JFB's account of the conference covers this request to keep the parley from the wall's hearers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Rabshakeh answered that his master had sent him not only to Hezekiah and the ministers but to the men on the wall, that they might eat their own dung and drink their own piss with them. JFB explains this was designed to show the dreadful extremities of the threatened siege to which Jerusalem would be reduced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
