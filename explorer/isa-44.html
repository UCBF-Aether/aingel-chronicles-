The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 44 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Yet now hear, O Jacob my servant; and Israel, whom I have chosen:

2 Thus saith the LORD that made thee, and formed thee from the womb, which will help thee; Fear not, O Jacob, my servant; and thou, Jesurun, whom I have chosen.

3 For I will pour water upon him that is thirsty, and floods upon the dry ground: I will pour my spirit upon thy seed, and my blessing upon thine offspring:

4 And they shall spring up as among the grass, as willows by the water courses.

5 One shall say, I am the LORD’s; and another shall call himself by the name of Jacob; and another shall subscribe with his hand unto the LORD, and surname himself by the name of Israel.

6 Thus saith the LORD the King of Israel, and his redeemer the LORD of hosts; I am the first, and I am the last; and beside me there is no God.

7 And who, as I, shall call, and shall declare it, and set it in order for me, since I appointed the ancient people? and the things that are coming, and shall come, let them shew unto them.

8 Fear ye not, neither be afraid: have not I told thee from that time, and have declared it? ye are even my witnesses. Is there a God beside me? yea, there is no God; I know not any.

9 They that make a graven image are all of them vanity; and their delectable things shall not profit; and they are their own witnesses; they see not, nor know; that they may be ashamed.

10 Who hath formed a god, or molten a graven image that is profitable for nothing?

11 Behold, all his fellows shall be ashamed: and the workmen, they are of men: let them all be gathered together, let them stand up; yet they shall fear, and they shall be ashamed together.

12 The smith with the tongs both worketh in the coals, and fashioneth it with hammers, and worketh it with the strength of his arms: yea, he is hungry, and his strength faileth: he drinketh no water, and is faint.

13 The carpenter stretcheth out his rule; he marketh it out with a line; he fitteth it with planes, and he marketh it out with the compass, and maketh it after the figure of a man, according to the beauty of a man; that it may remain in the house.

14 He heweth him down cedars, and taketh the cypress and the oak, which he strengtheneth for himself among the trees of the forest: he planteth an ash, and the rain doth nourish it.

15 Then shall it be for a man to burn: for he will take thereof, and warm himself; yea, he kindleth it, and baketh bread; yea, he maketh a god, and worshippeth it; he maketh it a graven image, and falleth down thereto.

16 He burneth part thereof in the fire; with part thereof he eateth flesh; he roasteth roast, and is satisfied: yea, he warmeth himself, and saith, Aha, I am warm, I have seen the fire:

17 And the residue thereof he maketh a god, even his graven image: he falleth down unto it, and worshippeth it, and prayeth unto it, and saith, Deliver me; for thou art my god.

18 They have not known nor understood: for he hath shut their eyes, that they cannot see; and their hearts, that they cannot understand.

19 And none considereth in his heart, neither is there knowledge nor understanding to say, I have burned part of it in the fire; yea, also I have baked bread upon the coals thereof; I have roasted flesh, and eaten it: and shall I make the residue thereof an abomination? shall I fall down to the stock of a tree?

20 He feedeth on ashes: a deceived heart hath turned him aside, that he cannot deliver his soul, nor say, Is there not a lie in my right hand?

21 Remember these, O Jacob and Israel; for thou art my servant: I have formed thee; thou art my servant: O Israel, thou shalt not be forgotten of me.

22 I have blotted out, as a thick cloud, thy transgressions, and, as a cloud, thy sins: return unto me; for I have redeemed thee.

23 Sing, O ye heavens; for the LORD hath done it: shout, ye lower parts of the earth: break forth into singing, ye mountains, O forest, and every tree therein: for the LORD hath redeemed Jacob, and glorified himself in Israel.

24 Thus saith the LORD, thy redeemer, and he that formed thee from the womb, I am the LORD that maketh all things; that stretcheth forth the heavens alone; that spreadeth abroad the earth by myself;

25 That frustrateth the tokens of the liars, and maketh diviners mad; that turneth wise men backward, and maketh their knowledge foolish;

26 That confirmeth the word of his servant, and performeth the counsel of his messengers; that saith to Jerusalem, Thou shalt be inhabited; and to the cities of Judah, Ye shall be built, and I will raise up the decayed places thereof:

27 That saith to the deep, Be dry, and I will dry up thy rivers:

28 That saith of Cyrus, He is my shepherd, and shall perform all my pleasure: even saying to Jerusalem, Thou shalt be built; and to the temple, Thy foundation shall be laid.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God pours his Spirit on his offspring like water on thirsty ground, while the chapter mocks idol-making at length: a man plants a tree, burns half of it to warm himself, and fashions the rest into a god to worship. It closes by naming Cyrus, God's shepherd, who will say to Jerusalem 'you shall be built' and to the temple 'your foundation shall be laid.' Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Yet now hear, O Jacob my servant, and Israel, whom I have chosen. This chapter continues the previous chapter. Yet means though thou hast sinned, yet hear God's gracious promise as to thy deliverance. Chosen recalls chapter 41 verse 8. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Thus says the Lord that made you, and formed you from the womb, which will help you: Fear not, O Jacob, my servant, and you, Jesurun, whom I have chosen. This recalls chapters 43 verses 1 and 7. Formed from the womb is, so chapters 44 verse 24 and 49, and the sense is similar to that in chapter 1 verse 2, I have nourished and brought up children. Jesurun is a diminutive term of endearment applied to Israel; the full title of affection was Israelun, contracted it became Jeshurun, with an allusion to the Hebrew root, jashar, upright, perfect, as in chapter 42 verse 19, on He that is perfect, and Deuteronomy 32. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For I will pour water upon him that is thirsty, and floods upon the dry ground; I will pour my spirit upon your seed, and my blessing upon your offspring. This recalls chapter 41 verse 18. Him thirsty is rather the land, as in chapter 35, figuratively for man thirsting after righteousness. Floods are the abundant influences of the Holy Spirit, stronger than water. Spirit includes all spiritual and temporal gifts, as the parallel, blessing, proves. Seed recalls chapter 59. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They will spring up as among the grass, as willows by the water courses. They are thy seed and offspring in verse 3. As among is needlessly inserted in the English version; rather, The seed shall spring up as willows among the grass beside canals of water. Or, They shall spring up among the grass, that is, luxuriantly, for what grows in the midst of grass grows luxuriantly, as willows by the water-courses, which makes the parallel clauses better balanced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. One will say: I am the Lord's, and another will call himself by the name of Jacob, and another will subscribe with his hand unto the Lord, and surname himself by the name of Israel. The third clause answers in parallelism to the first, the fourth to the second. I am the Lord's recalls Jeremiah, Corinthians. Call himself by the name of Jacob means the Gentiles, as the result of the outpouring of the Holy Spirit on Israel, the Lord's seed, first, shall join themselves to the children of Jacob, in order to worship their God, as in chapter 43 and Psalm 49. Or, calls, that is, invokes and celebrates the name of Jacob, attaches himself to his nation and religion, as in Psalm 24. Subscribe with hand unto the Lord means in solemn and public covenant, pledging himself to God's service, as in Nehemiah, before witnesses, after the manner of a civil contract, as in Jeremiah. So the Christian in the sacraments. Literally, shall fill his hand with letters in honour of Jehovah, or shall write upon his hand, I am Jehovah's, as in chapters 49 and Revelation, alluding to the puncture with ink on the hand, whereby a soldier marked himself as bound to his commander, and whereby the Christians used to mark themselves with the name of Christ. The former view is simpler. Surname himself Israel is, Maurer and Gesenius interpret this as the Hebrew sanctions, answering to their rendering of the parallel second clause, calls blandly, speaks in honourable terms of, the name of Israel. Retaining the English version, we must, from the Hebrew, understand it thus, Surname himself by the honourable name of Israel, as in chapter 45. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Thus says the Lord the King of Israel, and his redeemer the Lord of hosts: I am the first, and I am the last, and beside me there is no God. Here follows an argument for Jehovah, as the only God, and against the idols, as vanity, as in chapters 41, 43 verses 1 and 10 to 12. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Who, as I, will call, and will declare it, and set it in order for me, since I appointed the ancient people? And the things that are coming, and will come, let them show unto them. Who but God can predict future events and declare also the order and time of each, as in chapters 41 and 45? Call means openly proclaim things to come, or call forth the event, command that it happen, as in chapters 46 and 48. Set in order means there is no chance or confusion; all events occur in the order best fitted to subserve God's plans. For me means it is for God that all things exist and take place; but some translate, Let him set it forth to me. Since ancient people means I have given the Jews predictions of the future ever since I appointed them as My people in ancient times, therefore they were qualified to be His witnesses in verse 8. As to their being God's ancient, everlasting, people, see Deuteronomy and Jeremiah; the type of the redeemed church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Fear not, neither be afraid; have not I told you from that time, and have declared it? You are even my witnesses. Is there a God beside me? Yes, there is no God; I know not any. Be afraid is literally be astounded, or distracted with fear. From that time means namely, from the time that I appointed the ancient people in verse 7. From the time of Abraham's call, his family were the depositories of the predictions of the Redeemer, whereas the promise of Cyrus was not heard of till Isaiah's time; therefore, the event to the prediction and accomplishment of which God appeals in proof of His sole Godhead, is the redemption of man by a descendant of Abraham, in whose person the ancient people was first formally appointed. The deliverance of the Jews, by Cyrus, is mentioned afterwards only as an earnest of that greater mercy. No God is Hebrew, tsur, rock, that is, a stronghold to take refuge in, and a solid foundation to build on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They that make a graven image are all of them vanity, and their delectable things will not profit, and they are their own witnesses; they see not, nor know, that they may be ashamed. This recalls chapters 40 and 41. Delectable things are the idols in which they take such pride and delight. Not profit recalls Habakkuk. They are their own witnesses is contrasted with, Ye are My witnesses in verse 8: They, that is, both the makers and the idols, are witnesses against themselves, for the idols palpably see and know nothing. That they may be ashamed is the consequence deducible from the whole previous argument, not merely from the words immediately preceding; I say all this to show that they are doomed to perish with shame, which is their only fitting end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Who has formed a god, or molten a graven image that is profitable for nothing? Who? is a sarcastic question: How debased the man must be who forms a god! It is a contradiction in terms: a made god, worshipped by its maker. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Behold, all his fellows will be ashamed, and the workmen, they are of men; let them all be gathered together, let them stand up, yet they will fear, and they will be ashamed together. His fellows are the associates of him who makes an idol, or of the idol. They are of men means they are mortal men themselves; what better, then, can the idol be than its maker? Gathered together, stand up means as in a court of justice, to try the issue between God and them, as in chapter 41. Yet is wrongly inserted in the English version; the issue of the trial shall be, they shall fear, etc. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The smith with the tongs both works in the coals, and fashions it with hammers, and works it with the strength of his arms; yes, he is hungry, and his strength fails; he drinks no water, and is faint. Tongs is rather, prepareth, to be supplied, an axe, namely, with which to cut down the tree designed as the material of the idol. The smith, Hebrew, workman in iron, here answers to the carpenter, Hebrew, workman in wood. He worketh it, the axe, not the idol, which was wood, not metal, in the coals, etc. The axe was wrought, not cast; the smith makes the axe for the carpenter. Hungry and drinketh no water means so eager is he to expedite his work while the iron is hot. If the god were worth anything, it would not let him grow faint with hunger and thirst. Williams, the missionary, states that the South Sea islanders when they make an idol abstain from food and drink. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The carpenter stretches out his rule, he marks it out with a line, he fits it with planes, and he marks it out with the compass, and makes it after the figure of a man, according to the beauty of a man, that it may remain in the house. After the smith's work in preparing the instruments comes the carpenter's work in forming the idol. Rule is rather line. With a line is rather a pencil, literally red ochre, which he uses to mark on the wood the outline of the figure, or best, the stylus or graver, with which the incision of the outline is made. Planes are rather chisels or carving tools, for a plane would not answer for carving. Compass is from a Hebrew root, to make a circle; by it, symmetry of form is secured. According to the beauty of a man is irony: the highest idea the heathen could form of a god was one of a form like their own. Jerome says, The more handsome the statue the more august the god was thought. The incarnation of the Son of God condescends to this anthropomorphic feeling so natural to man, but in such a way as to raise man's thoughts up to the infinite God who is a spirit. That it may remain in the house means the only thing it was good for; it could not hear nor save. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He hews him down cedars, and takes the cypress and the oak, which he strengthens for himself among the trees of the forest; he plants an ash, and the rain nourishes it. This is a description of the material out of which the idol is formed. Cypress is rather, from a Hebrew root, to be hard, the holm oak, an evergreen abundant in Palestine. Strengtheneth is literally, and he getteth strength to himself in the trees of the forest, that is, he layeth in a great store of timber; or, chooseth, as madest strong for thyself, that is, hast chosen; but the English version gives a good sense: strengtheneth, that is, rears to maturity, a meaning suitable also to the context of Psalm 80, where Israel is compared to a vine planted by Jehovah. Rain doth nourish it means though the man planted the tree, yet he could not make it grow; in preparing to make an idol, he has to depend on the true God for rain from heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Then will it be for a man to burn, for he will take thereof, and warm himself, yes, he kindles it, and bakes bread, yes, he makes a god, and worships it; he makes it a graven image, and falls down thereto. The same tree that furnishes the material for the god is in part used as fuel for a fire to cook his meals and warm himself! Thereto is rather, he falleth down before them, that is, such images. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He burns part thereof in the fire; with part thereof he eats flesh; he roasts roast, and is satisfied, yes, he warms himself, and says: Aha, I am warm, I have seen the fire. Part means not distinct parts, but the same part of the wood, as in verse 17. Eateth means that is, cooks so as to eat, as in verse 19. I have seen means I feel its power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They have not known nor understood, for he has shut their eyes, that they cannot see, and their hearts, that they cannot understand. He, etc., means God hath given them over to judicial blindness; not His direct physical, but His providential agency in administering His moral government, is meant, as in chapter 6. Shut is literally, daubed, plastered up; it is an Eastern custom in some cases to seal up the eyes of offenders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. None considers in his heart, neither is there knowledge nor understanding to say: I have burned part of it in the fire, yes, also I have baked bread upon the coals thereof; I have roasted flesh, and eaten it, and will I make the residue thereof an abomination? Will I fall down to the stock of a tree? Considereth is literally, layeth it to heart, as in chapters 42 and Jeremiah. Abomination is the scriptural term for an idol, not merely abominable, but the essence of what is so, in the eyes of a jealous God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He feeds on ashes; a deceived heart has turned him aside, that he cannot deliver his soul, nor say: Is there not a lie in my right hand? Feedeth on ashes is figuratively, for the idolater delights in what is vain; feedeth on wind. There is an allusion, perhaps, also, to the god being made of a tree, the half of which was reduced to ashes by fire in verses 15 to 17; the idol, it is implied, was no better, and could, and ought, to have been reduced to ashes like the other half. Deceived heart means the heart and will first go astray, then the intellect and life. Lie in my right hand means Is not my handiwork, the idol, a self-deceit? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Remember these, O Jacob and Israel, for you are my servant; I have formed you; you are my servant; O Israel, you will not be forgotten of me. Remember means be not like the idolaters who consider not in their heart in verse 19. These means things just said as to the folly of idol-worship. My servant means not like the idolaters, slaves to the stock of a tree in verse 19, as in verses 1 and 2. Thou not forgotten of me means therefore thou oughtest to remember Me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. I have blotted out, as a thick cloud, your transgressions, and, as a cloud, your sins; return unto me, for I have redeemed you. Blotted out means the debt of thy sin from the account-book in which it was entered. As a thick cloud means scattered away by the wind. As a cloud is a descending gradation: not only the thick cloud of the heavier transgressions, but the cloud, vapour, not so dense, but covering the sky as a mist, of the countless sins. These latter, though not thought much of by man, need, as much as the former, to be cleared away by the Sun of righteousness; else they will be a mist separating us from heaven. Return, for means the antecedent redemption is the ground of, and motive to, repentance. We do not repent in order that He may redeem us, but because He hath redeemed us. He who believes in his being forgiven cannot but love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Sing, O heavens, for the Lord has done it; shout, you lower parts of the earth; break forth into singing, you mountains, O forest, and every tree therein, for the Lord has redeemed Jacob, and glorified himself in Israel. This is a call to inanimate nature to praise God, for it also shall share in the coming deliverance from the bondage of corruption. Done it means effected redemption for both the literal and spiritual Israel. Lower parts, etc., are antithetical to heavens; mountains, forest, and tree, are the intermediate objects in a descending gradation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Thus says the Lord, your redeemer, and he that formed you from the womb: I am the Lord that makes all things, that stretches forth the heavens alone, that spreads abroad the earth by myself. Verses 24 to 28 are a confirmation of His promises to the church and Israel, by various instances of His omnipotence, among these the restoration of the Jews by Cyrus. Alone is literally, Who was with Me? namely, when I did it, answering to by Myself, in the parallel clause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. That frustrates the tokens of the liars, and makes diviners mad, that turns wise men backward, and makes their knowledge foolish. Tokens are prognostics, the pretended miracles which they gave as proofs of their supernatural powers. Liars means, as in Jeremiah, conjurers, or astrologers, men leading a retired contemplative life in order to study divination by the signs of the stars. Backward means with shame at their predictions not being verified; to turn away the face is to frustrate defeat. The wise men are the diviners who, when Babylon was attacked by Cyrus, predicted his overthrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. That confirms the word of his servant, and performs the counsel of his messengers, that says to Jerusalem: You will be inhabited, and to the cities of Judah: You will be built, and I will raise up the decayed places thereof. Servant is in a collective sense, for the prophets in general, who foretold the return from Babylon, answering to His messengers, plural, in the parallel clause. Antitypically, and ultimately, Messiah, who is the consummating embodiment of all the prophets and messengers of God, hence the singular, His servant. Counsel means predictions; prophets' counsels concern the future, as in counsellor in chapter 41. Jerusalem is regarded prophetically, as lying in ruins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. That says to the deep: Be dry, and I will dry up your rivers. This refers to the Euphrates, which was turned into a different channel, close to Babylon, by Cyrus, who thereby took the city. The deep is applied to Euphrates as sea, as in Jeremiah. Rivers refers to the artificial canals from the Euphrates made to irrigate the country; when it was turned off into a different bed, namely, a lake, forty miles square, which was originally formed to receive the superfluous water in an inundation, the canals became dry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. That says of Cyrus: He is my shepherd, and will perform all my pleasure, even saying to Jerusalem: You will be built, and to the temple: Your foundation will be laid. My shepherd is a type of Messiah. All my pleasure is so Messiah, as in chapters 42 and 53. This is the first time Cyrus is named expressly, and that, a hundred and fifty years before the time when in 550 B.C. he began his reign. The name comes from the Persian khorschid, the sun; kings often taking their names from the gods, and the sun was worshipped as a god in Persia. Saying is rather, and that saith, construed with God, not with Cyrus; God's word is instantaneously efficient in accomplishing His will. To, to means or, of Jerusalem, of the temple, as previously, the same Hebrew word is translated, of Cyrus. The English version is more graphic. Cyrus, according to Josephus, heard of this prophecy of Isaiah delivered so long before; hence he was induced to do that which was so contrary to Oriental policy, to aid in restoring the captive Jews and rebuilding their temple and city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Since ancient people means I have given the Jews predictions of the future ever since I appointed them as My people in ancient times, therefore they were qualified to be His witnesses in verse 8. As to their being God's ancient, everlasting, people, see Deuteronomy and Jeremiah; the type of the redeemed church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Fear not, neither be afraid; have not I told you from that time, and have declared it? You are even my witnesses. Is there a God beside me? Yes, there is no God; I know not any. Be afraid is literally be astounded, or distracted with fear. From that time means namely, from the time that I appointed the ancient people in verse 7. From the time of Abraham's call, his family were the depositories of the predictions of the Redeemer, whereas the promise of Cyrus was not heard of till Isaiah's time; therefore, the event to the prediction and accomplishment of which God appeals in proof of His sole Godhead, is the redemption of man by a descendant of Abraham, in whose person the ancient people was first formally appointed. The deliverance of the Jews, by Cyrus, is mentioned afterwards only as an earnest of that greater mercy. No God is Hebrew, tsur, rock, that is, a stronghold to take refuge in, and a solid foundation to build on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They that make a graven image are all of them vanity, and their delectable things will not profit, and they are their own witnesses; they see not, nor know, that they may be ashamed. This recalls chapters 40 and 41. Delectable things are the idols in which they take such pride and delight. Not profit recalls Habakkuk. They are their own witnesses is contrasted with, Ye are My witnesses in verse 8: They, that is, both the makers and the idols, are witnesses against themselves, for the idols palpably see and know nothing. That they may be ashamed is the consequence deducible from the whole previous argument, not merely from the words immediately preceding; I say all this to show that they are doomed to perish with shame, which is their only fitting end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Who has formed a god, or molten a graven image that is profitable for nothing? Who? is a sarcastic question: How debased the man must be who forms a god! It is a contradiction in terms: a made god, worshipped by its maker. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Behold, all his fellows will be ashamed, and the workmen, they are of men; let them all be gathered together, let them stand up, yet they will fear, and they will be ashamed together. His fellows are the associates of him who makes an idol, or of the idol. They are of men means they are mortal men themselves; what better, then, can the idol be than its maker? Gathered together, stand up means as in a court of justice, to try the issue between God and them, as in chapter 41. Yet is wrongly inserted in the English version; the issue of the trial shall be, they shall fear, etc. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The smith with the tongs both works in the coals, and fashions it with hammers, and works it with the strength of his arms; yes, he is hungry, and his strength fails; he drinks no water, and is faint. Tongs is rather, prepareth, to be supplied, an axe, namely, with which to cut down the tree designed as the material of the idol. The smith, Hebrew, workman in iron, here answers to the carpenter, Hebrew, workman in wood. He worketh it, the axe, not the idol, which was wood, not metal, in the coals, etc. The axe was wrought, not cast; the smith makes the axe for the carpenter. Hungry and drinketh no water means so eager is he to expedite his work while the iron is hot. If the god were worth anything, it would not let him grow faint with hunger and thirst. Williams, the missionary, states that the South Sea islanders when they make an idol abstain from food and drink. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The carpenter stretches out his rule, he marks it out with a line, he fits it with planes, and he marks it out with the compass, and makes it after the figure of a man, according to the beauty of a man, that it may remain in the house. After the smith's work in preparing the instruments comes the carpenter's work in forming the idol. Rule is rather line. With a line is rather a pencil, literally red ochre, which he uses to mark on the wood the outline of the figure, or best, the stylus or graver, with which the incision of the outline is made. Planes are rather chisels or carving tools, for a plane would not answer for carving. Compass is from a Hebrew root, to make a circle; by it, symmetry of form is secured. According to the beauty of a man is irony: the highest idea the heathen could form of a god was one of a form like their own. Jerome says, The more handsome the statue the more august the god was thought. The incarnation of the Son of God condescends to this anthropomorphic feeling so natural to man, but in such a way as to raise man's thoughts up to the infinite God who is a spirit. That it may remain in the house means the only thing it was good for; it could not hear nor save. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He hews him down cedars, and takes the cypress and the oak, which he strengthens for himself among the trees of the forest; he plants an ash, and the rain nourishes it. This is a description of the material out of which the idol is formed. Cypress is rather, from a Hebrew root, to be hard, the holm oak, an evergreen abundant in Palestine. Strengtheneth is literally, and he getteth strength to himself in the trees of the forest, that is, he layeth in a great store of timber; or, chooseth, as madest strong for thyself, that is, hast chosen; but the English version gives a good sense: strengtheneth, that is, rears to maturity, a meaning suitable also to the context of Psalm 80, where Israel is compared to a vine planted by Jehovah. Rain doth nourish it means though the man planted the tree, yet he could not make it grow; in preparing to make an idol, he has to depend on the true God for rain from heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Then will it be for a man to burn, for he will take thereof, and warm himself, yes, he kindles it, and bakes bread, yes, he makes a god, and worships it; he makes it a graven image, and falls down thereto. The same tree that furnishes the material for the god is in part used as fuel for a fire to cook his meals and warm himself! Thereto is rather, he falleth down before them, that is, such images. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He burns part thereof in the fire; with part thereof he eats flesh; he roasts roast, and is satisfied, yes, he warms himself, and says: Aha, I am warm, I have seen the fire. Part means not distinct parts, but the same part of the wood, as in verse 17. Eateth means that is, cooks so as to eat, as in verse 19. I have seen means I feel its power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They have not known nor understood, for he has shut their eyes, that they cannot see, and their hearts, that they cannot understand. He, etc., means God hath given them over to judicial blindness; not His direct physical, but His providential agency in administering His moral government, is meant, as in chapter 6. Shut is literally, daubed, plastered up; it is an Eastern custom in some cases to seal up the eyes of offenders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. None considers in his heart, neither is there knowledge nor understanding to say: I have burned part of it in the fire, yes, also I have baked bread upon the coals thereof; I have roasted flesh, and eaten it, and will I make the residue thereof an abomination? Will I fall down to the stock of a tree? Considereth is literally, layeth it to heart, as in chapters 42 and Jeremiah. Abomination is the scriptural term for an idol, not merely abominable, but the essence of what is so, in the eyes of a jealous God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He feeds on ashes; a deceived heart has turned him aside, that he cannot deliver his soul, nor say: Is there not a lie in my right hand? Feedeth on ashes is figuratively, for the idolater delights in what is vain; feedeth on wind. There is an allusion, perhaps, also, to the god being made of a tree, the half of which was reduced to ashes by fire in verses 15 to 17; the idol, it is implied, was no better, and could, and ought, to have been reduced to ashes like the other half. Deceived heart means the heart and will first go astray, then the intellect and life. Lie in my right hand means Is not my handiwork, the idol, a self-deceit? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Remember these, O Jacob and Israel, for you are my servant; I have formed you; you are my servant; O Israel, you will not be forgotten of me. Remember means be not like the idolaters who consider not in their heart in verse 19. These means things just said as to the folly of idol-worship. My servant means not like the idolaters, slaves to the stock of a tree in verse 19, as in verses 1 and 2. Thou not forgotten of me means therefore thou oughtest to remember Me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. I have blotted out, as a thick cloud, your transgressions, and, as a cloud, your sins; return unto me, for I have redeemed you. Blotted out means the debt of thy sin from the account-book in which it was entered. As a thick cloud means scattered away by the wind. As a cloud is a descending gradation: not only the thick cloud of the heavier transgressions, but the cloud, vapour, not so dense, but covering the sky as a mist, of the countless sins. These latter, though not thought much of by man, need, as much as the former, to be cleared away by the Sun of righteousness; else they will be a mist separating us from heaven. Return, for means the antecedent redemption is the ground of, and motive to, repentance. We do not repent in order that He may redeem us, but because He hath redeemed us. He who believes in his being forgiven cannot but love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Sing, O heavens, for the Lord has done it; shout, you lower parts of the earth; break forth into singing, you mountains, O forest, and every tree therein, for the Lord has redeemed Jacob, and glorified himself in Israel. This is a call to inanimate nature to praise God, for it also shall share in the coming deliverance from the bondage of corruption. Done it means effected redemption for both the literal and spiritual Israel. Lower parts, etc., are antithetical to heavens; mountains, forest, and tree, are the intermediate objects in a descending gradation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Thus says the Lord, your redeemer, and he that formed you from the womb: I am the Lord that makes all things, that stretches forth the heavens alone, that spreads abroad the earth by myself. Verses 24 to 28 are a confirmation of His promises to the church and Israel, by various instances of His omnipotence, among these the restoration of the Jews by Cyrus. Alone is literally, Who was with Me? namely, when I did it, answering to by Myself, in the parallel clause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. That frustrates the tokens of the liars, and makes diviners mad, that turns wise men backward, and makes their knowledge foolish. Tokens are prognostics, the pretended miracles which they gave as proofs of their supernatural powers. Liars means, as in Jeremiah, conjurers, or astrologers, men leading a retired contemplative life in order to study divination by the signs of the stars. Backward means with shame at their predictions not being verified; to turn away the face is to frustrate defeat. The wise men are the diviners who, when Babylon was attacked by Cyrus, predicted his overthrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. That confirms the word of his servant, and performs the counsel of his messengers, that says to Jerusalem: You will be inhabited, and to the cities of Judah: You will be built, and I will raise up the decayed places thereof. Servant is in a collective sense, for the prophets in general, who foretold the return from Babylon, answering to His messengers, plural, in the parallel clause. Antitypically, and ultimately, Messiah, who is the consummating embodiment of all the prophets and messengers of God, hence the singular, His servant. Counsel means predictions; prophets' counsels concern the future, as in counsellor in chapter 41. Jerusalem is regarded prophetically, as lying in ruins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. That says to the deep: Be dry, and I will dry up your rivers. This refers to the Euphrates, which was turned into a different channel, close to Babylon, by Cyrus, who thereby took the city. The deep is applied to Euphrates as sea, as in Jeremiah. Rivers refers to the artificial canals from the Euphrates made to irrigate the country; when it was turned off into a different bed, namely, a lake, forty miles square, which was originally formed to receive the superfluous water in an inundation, the canals became dry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. That says of Cyrus: He is my shepherd, and will perform all my pleasure, even saying to Jerusalem: You will be built, and to the temple: Your foundation will be laid. My shepherd is a type of Messiah. All my pleasure is so Messiah, as in chapters 42 and 53. This is the first time Cyrus is named expressly, and that, a hundred and fifty years before the time when in 550 B.C. he began his reign. The name comes from the Persian khorschid, the sun; kings often taking their names from the gods, and the sun was worshipped as a god in Persia. Saying is rather, and that saith, construed with God, not with Cyrus; God's word is instantaneously efficient in accomplishing His will. To, to means or, of Jerusalem, of the temple, as previously, the same Hebrew word is translated, of Cyrus. The English version is more graphic. Cyrus, according to Josephus, heard of this prophecy of Isaiah delivered so long before; hence he was induced to do that which was so contrary to Oriental policy, to aid in restoring the captive Jews and rebuilding their temple and city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
