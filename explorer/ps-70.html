The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 70 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Make haste, O God, to deliver me; make haste to help me, O Lord.

2 Let them be ashamed and confounded that seek after my soul: let them be turned backward, and put to confusion, that desire my hurt.

3 Let them be turned back for a reward of their shame that say, Aha, aha.

4 Let all those that seek thee rejoice and be glad in thee: and let such as love thy salvation say continually, Let God be magnified.

5 But I am poor and needy: make haste unto me, O God: thou art my help and my deliverer; O LORD, make no tarrying.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The urgent short psalm, Psalm 40's closing cry standing alone: haste twice asked, soul-seekers ashamed and turned back, 'Aha' mocked and turned back as shame's reward, seekers rejoicing and lovers saying continually, 'Let God be magnified,' and the poor and needy close: make haste to me, my help and deliverer, make no tarrying. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Psalm 70 and Psalm 40: a closing cry standing alone?

This section investigates a parallel between two texts. It does not conclude how the parallel came to be, and it claims no direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty about either text's origin. Interpreted Standard corpus, ps.json (Psalms 40 and 70)

Parallels. This psalm, all five verses of it, reproduces the closing cry of Psalm 40 (verses 13-17) nearly verse for verse: haste asked for deliverance and help; soul-seekers ashamed, confounded, and turned backward; the mocking 'Aha, aha' turned back as shame's reward; all who seek God rejoicing and glad, lovers of his salvation saying continually that God be magnified; and the close, poor and needy, God as help and deliverer, make no tarrying.

Differences. This psalm opens 'Make haste, O God, to deliver me' where Psalm 40 verse 13 opens 'Be pleased, O LORD, to deliver me.' Verse 4 here says 'Let God be magnified' where Psalm 40 verse 16 says 'The LORD be magnified.' And this psalm stands without Psalm 40's first twelve verses: the pit and miry clay, the new song, the wonderful works beyond reckoning, opened ears, and righteousness preached in the great congregation.

Whether one text was adapted from the other, both were drawn from a shared source, or the likeness arose another way, is not established by the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved No source consulted for this page establishes the relation between the two texts
