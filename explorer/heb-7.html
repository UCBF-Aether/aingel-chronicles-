The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hebrews 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 For this Melchisedec, king of Salem, priest of the most high God, who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings, and blessed him;

2 To whom also Abraham gave a tenth part of all; first being by interpretation King of righteousness, and after that also King of Salem, which is, King of peace;

3 Without father, without mother, without descent, having neither beginning of days, nor end of life; but made like unto the Son of God; abideth a priest continually.

4 Now consider how great this man was, unto whom even the patriarch Abraham gave the tenth of the spoils.

5 And verily they that are of the sons of Levi, who receive the office of the priesthood, have a commandment to take tithes of the people according to the law, that is, of their brethren, though they come out of the loins of Abraham:

6 But he whose descent is not counted from them received tithes of Abraham, and blessed him that had the promises.

7 And without all contradiction the less is blessed of the better.

8 And here men that die receive tithes; but there he receiveth them, of whom it is witnessed that he liveth.

9 And as I may so say, Levi also, who receiveth tithes, payed tithes in Abraham.

10 For he was yet in the loins of his father, when Melchisedec met him.

11 If therefore perfection were by the Levitical priesthood, (for under it the people received the law,) what further need was there that another priest should rise after the order of Melchisedec, and not be called after the order of Aaron?

12 For the priesthood being changed, there is made of necessity a change also of the law.

13 For he of whom these things are spoken pertaineth to another tribe, of which no man gave attendance at the altar.

14 For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood.

15 And it is yet far more evident: for that after the similitude of Melchisedec there ariseth another priest,

16 Who is made, not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life.

17 For he testifieth, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec.

18 For there is verily a disannulling of the commandment going before for the weakness and unprofitableness thereof.

19 For the law made nothing perfect, but the bringing in of a better hope did; by the which we draw nigh unto God.

20 And inasmuch as not without an oath he was made priest:

21 (For those priests were made without an oath; but this with an oath by him that said unto him, The Lord sware and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec:)

22 By so much was Jesus made a surety of a better testament.

23 And they truly were many priests, because they were not suffered to continue by reason of death:

24 But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood.

25 Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.

26 For such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens;

27 Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people’s: for this he did once, when he offered up himself.

28 For the law maketh men high priests which have infirmity; but the word of the oath, which was since the law, maketh the Son, who is consecrated for evermore.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Melchisedec, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, to whom Abraham gave a tenth, is greater even than Levi's line, the writer argues, since Levi, still in Abraham's loins, in effect paid tithes to him. A priesthood after Melchisedec's order, not Aaron's, means a changed law and a better covenant: Jesus, priest forever by oath, not by descent, lives always to intercede and is able to save completely those who come to God through him. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. For this Melchisedec, king of Salem, priest of the most high God, who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings, and blessed him — Melchizedek met Abraham returning from the rescue of Lot, and as king of Salem, "Peace," he typified Christ, the Prince of Peace, the great Reconciler of God and man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. To whom also Abraham gave a tenth part of all; first being by interpretation King of righteousness, and after that also King of Salem, which is, King of peace — his name, "King of Righteousness," marked him as a type of the Messiah and his kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Without father, without mother, without descent, having neither beginning of days, nor end of life; but made like unto the Son of God; abideth a priest continually — nothing is recorded of the beginning or end of his life, so he typically resembled the Son of God, whose existence is from everlasting to everlasting, with no successor in his priesthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Now consider how great this man was, unto whom even the patriarch Abraham gave the tenth of the spoils — Abraham's gift shows Melchizedek's greatness, and the High Priest of whom he was a type must be much superior to the Levitical priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. And verily they that are of the sons of Levi, who receive the office of the priesthood, have a commandment to take tithes of the people according to the law, that is, of their brethren, though they come out of the loins of Abraham — the Levites took tithes by law from their own brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. But he whose descent is not counted from them received tithes of Abraham, and blessed him that had the promises — Melchizedek, outside the Levitical descent, received tithes from Abraham and blessed him who had the promises; that man is rich and happy indeed who has the promises, of this life and of that to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. And without all contradiction the less is blessed of the better — the blessing proves the superiority: the less is blessed by the better. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. And here men that die receive tithes; but there he receiveth them, of whom it is witnessed that he liveth — Levitical tithe-takers are dying men, but Melchizedek is witnessed as living, a type of the ever-living Priest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. And as I may so say, Levi also, who receiveth tithes, payed tithes in Abraham — Levi himself, in Abraham, paid tithes to Melchizedek. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. For he was yet in the loins of his father, when Melchisedec met him — Levi was still in Abraham's loins when Melchizedek met him, so the Levitical priesthood itself acknowledged Melchizedek's superiority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. If therefore perfection were by the Levitical priesthood, (for under it the people received the law,) what further need was there that another priest should rise after the order of Melchisedec, and not be called after the order of Aaron? — the priesthood and law by which perfection could not come are done away; a Priest is risen, and a dispensation now set up, by which true believers may be made perfect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. For the priesthood being changed, there is made of necessity a change also of the law — a changed priesthood necessarily brings a changed law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. For he of whom these things are spoken pertaineth to another tribe, of which no man gave attendance at the altar — Christ pertains to a tribe, Judah, from which no man served at the altar, proving his priesthood is of a different order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood — our Lord's descent from Judah, a tribe Moses never joined to priesthood, confirms the change. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. And it is yet far more evident: for that after the similitude of Melchisedec there ariseth another priest — it is still more evident, since another Priest arises after Melchizedek's similitude, not Aaron's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Who is made, not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life — the law that made Levitical priests showed them frail, dying creatures, unable to save their own lives, much less others' souls; but our High Priest holds his office by the power of endless life in himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. For he testifieth, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec — the testimony confirms an everlasting priesthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. For there is verily a disannulling of the commandment going before for the weakness and unprofitableness thereof — the former commandment is disannulled for its weakness and unprofitableness; it could not perfect anyone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. For the law made nothing perfect, but the bringing in of a better hope did; by the which we draw nigh unto God — by the better hope we now draw nigh to God, into clearer light, more perfect liberty, and more abundant privileges than the Sinai covenant gave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. And inasmuch as not without an oath he was made priest — Christ was made priest with an oath, unlike the Levitical priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. (For those priests were made without an oath; but this with an oath by him that said unto him, The Lord sware and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec:) — the oath, "The Lord sware and will not repent," secures Christ's priesthood for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. By so much was Jesus made a surety of a better testament — Jesus is Surety of a better covenant, distinguished from the Sinai covenant and the legal dispensation under which the church so long remained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. And they truly were many priests, because they were not suffered to continue by reason of death — in Aaron's order there was a multitude of priests, high priests one after another, because death ended each one's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood — in Christ's priesthood there is only one and the same Priest, ever continuing, so his priesthood is unchangeable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them — this is the believer's safety and happiness: the everlasting High Priest is able to save to the uttermost, in all times and in all cases, because he ever lives to intercede. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. For such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens — observe Christ's personal holiness: free from all habits or principles of sin, with not the least disposition to it in his nature, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and exalted above the heavens. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people's: for this he did once, when he offered up himself — unlike priests who offered daily, first for themselves and then for the people, Christ offered himself once, and that once suffices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. For the law maketh men high priests which have infirmity; but the word of the oath, which was since the law, maketh the Son, who is consecrated for evermore — the law made infirm men priests, but the word of the oath makes the Son, consecrated for evermore; so none who come to God in his beloved Son's name need be dismayed, for he will deliver them in trial, prosperity, death, and judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
