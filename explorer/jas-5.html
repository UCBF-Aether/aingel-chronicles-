The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — James 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Go to now, ye rich men, weep and howl for your miseries that shall come upon you.

2 Your riches are corrupted, and your garments are motheaten.

3 Your gold and silver is cankered; and the rust of them shall be a witness against you, and shall eat your flesh as it were fire. Ye have heaped treasure together for the last days.

4 Behold, the hire of the labourers who have reaped down your fields, which is of you kept back by fraud, crieth: and the cries of them which have reaped are entered into the ears of the Lord of sabaoth.

5 Ye have lived in pleasure on the earth, and been wanton; ye have nourished your hearts, as in a day of slaughter.

6 Ye have condemned and killed the just; and he doth not resist you.

7 Be patient therefore, brethren, unto the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the early and latter rain.

8 Be ye also patient; stablish your hearts: for the coming of the Lord draweth nigh.

9 Grudge not one against another, brethren, lest ye be condemned: behold, the judge standeth before the door.

10 Take, my brethren, the prophets, who have spoken in the name of the Lord, for an example of suffering affliction, and of patience.

11 Behold, we count them happy which endure. Ye have heard of the patience of Job, and have seen the end of the Lord; that the Lord is very pitiful, and of tender mercy.

12 But above all things, my brethren, swear not, neither by heaven, neither by the earth, neither by any other oath: but let your yea be yea; and your nay, nay; lest ye fall into condemnation.

13 Is any among you afflicted? let him pray. Is any merry? let him sing psalms.

14 Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders of the church; and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord:

15 And the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up; and if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven him.

16 Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.

17 Elias was a man subject to like passions as we are, and he prayed earnestly that it might not rain: and it rained not on the earth by the space of three years and six months.

18 And he prayed again, and the heaven gave rain, and the earth brought forth her fruit.

19 Brethren, if any of you do err from the truth, and one convert him;

20 Let him know, that he which converteth the sinner from the error of his way shall save a soul from death, and shall hide a multitude of sins.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Weep and howl, James tells the rich who have hoarded wealth, withheld labourers' wages and lived in luxury while condemning the just. Believers instead are to be patient until the Lord's coming, like a farmer waiting for early and late rain, not grumbling against one another, taking prophets and Job as examples of endurance. Above all, do not swear; pray in affliction, sing in cheerfulness, call elders for the sick, confess faults to one another, and restore anyone wandering from the truth. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Go to now, ye rich men, weep and howl for your miseries that shall come upon you. Public troubles are most grievous to those who live in pleasure and are secure and sensual, Henry notes, though all ranks suffer deeply at such times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Your riches are corrupted, and your garments are motheaten. All idolized treasures will soon perish, Henry says, except as they will rise up in judgment against their possessors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Your gold and silver is cankered, and the rust shall be a witness against you and eat your flesh as fire, for ye have heaped treasure for the last days. Take heed of defrauding and oppressing, Henry warns, and avoid the very appearance of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The hire of the labourers kept back by fraud cries out, and their cries have entered into the ears of the Lord of sabaoth. God does not forbid lawful pleasures, Henry notes, but living in pleasure, especially sinful pleasure, is a provoking sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Ye have lived in pleasure on the earth and been wanton, nourishing your hearts as in a day of slaughter. Is it no harm, Henry asks, for people to unfit themselves for minding the concerns of their souls by indulging bodily appetites? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Ye have condemned and killed the just, and he does not resist you. When the just suffer by oppressors, Henry says, this is marked by God; and above all their other crimes, the Jews had condemned and crucified that Just One, Jesus Christ the righteous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Be patient therefore unto the coming of the Lord, as the husbandman waits for the precious fruit of the earth until he receives the early and latter rain. Consider him that waits for a crop of corn, Henry says, and will not you wait for a crown of glory? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Be ye also patient and stablish your hearts, for the coming of the Lord draws nigh. In every sense the coming of the Lord drew nigh, Henry notes, and all his people's losses, hardships, and sufferings would be repaid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Grudge not one against another, lest ye be condemned, for the judge stands before the door. Men count time long because they measure it by their own lives, Henry observes, but all time is as nothing to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Take the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord as an example of suffering affliction and of patience. Let us serve our God and bear our trials as those who believe that the end will crown all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. We count them happy which endure; ye have heard of the patience of Job and seen the end of the Lord, that the Lord is very pitiful and of tender mercy. God brought about things in Job's case so as plainly to prove this, Henry says, though it did not appear during his troubles but was seen in the event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Above all things, swear not, neither by heaven nor earth nor any other oath, but let your yea be yea and your nay, nay, lest ye fall into condemnation. The sin of swearing is condemned, Henry notes, and how many make light of common profane swearing, which expressly throws contempt upon God's name and authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Is any among you afflicted? Let him pray. Is any merry? Let him sing psalms. In a day of affliction nothing is more seasonable than prayer, Henry says, for the spirit is then most humble and the heart broken and tender. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Is any sick among you? Let him call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord. It is necessary to exercise faith and hope under afflictions, Henry notes, and prayer is the appointed means for obtaining and increasing these graces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up, and if he has committed sins they shall be forgiven him. The saving of the sick is ascribed not to the anointing with oil but to prayer, Henry stresses, and the great thing to beg in sickness is the pardon of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed, for the effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much. To acknowledge our faults to each other tends greatly to peace and brotherly love, Henry says. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Elias was a man subject to like passions as we are, and he prayed earnestly that it might not rain, and it rained not for three years and six months. The power of prayer is proved from the history of Elijah, Henry notes, and in prayer we must look not to the merit of man but to the grace of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. He prayed again, and the heaven gave rain, and the earth brought forth her fruit. It is not enough to say a prayer, Henry says, but we must pray in prayer: thoughts fixed, desires firm and ardent, graces exercised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. If any of you err from the truth and one convert him, that is true conversion. It is no mark of a wise or holy man to boast of being free from error, Henry notes, and there is some doctrinal mistake at the bottom of every practical mistake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. He which converts the sinner from the error of his way shall save a soul from death and hide a multitude of sins. Conversion turns a sinner from the error of his ways, not merely from one party to another, Henry says, and the salvation of one soul is of infinitely greater importance than preserving the lives of multitudes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
