2 These are the generations of Jacob.

3 And Joseph was seventeen years old 16 when they took him down into the land of Egypt, and Potiphar, an eunuch of Pharaoh,

5 And he set Joseph over all his house, and the blessing of the Lord came upon the house of the Egyptian on account of Joseph, and the Lord prospered him in all that lie did.

6 And the Egyptian committed everything into the hands of Joseph ; for he saw that the Lord was with him, and that the Lord prospered him in all that he did.

7 And Joseph's appearance was comely and verv beautiful was his appearance, and his master's wife lifted up her eves and saw Joseph, and she loved him, and besought him to he with her.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: Jacob dwelt in the land of his father's sojournings in Canaan. The chief butler forgot Joseph in the prison, although Joseph had told him what would befall him, and did not remember to inform Pharaoh. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. Jacob dwelt in the land of his father's sojournings in Canaan. The verse sets Joseph's story in the land of promise, where his father still sojourned rather than possessed, and Henry notes that Joseph's history displays the singular providence of God making use of good and bad workings alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. These are the generations of Jacob, the heading of the Joseph history. Henry observes that in Joseph's history we see something of Christ, first humbled and then exalted, and the lot of Christians who through many tribulations enter into the kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Joseph was seventeen years old when they took him down into Egypt, and Potiphar, Pharaoh's eunuch, bought him. Henry notes that Joseph's enemies could strip him of outward distinctions and separate him from friends and country, but could not take from him the presence of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Potiphar, the chief cook, bought Joseph, and the purchase made him a slave in a foreign house. The low beginning is the point: God's presence, not rank, would make Joseph prosper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Potiphar set Joseph over all his house, and the blessing of the Lord came upon the Egyptian's house on Joseph's account, prospering him in all he did. Henry notes that good men are the blessings of the place where they live, and that a wicked family was blessed for the sake of one good servant in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The Egyptian committed everything into Joseph's hands, for he saw the Lord was with him and prospered all he did. Henry stresses that God's presence with us makes all we do prosperous, and that wisdom and grace cannot be taken from us even in slavery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Joseph's appearance was comely and very beautiful, and his master's wife lifted up her eyes, saw him, loved him, and besought him to lie with her. Henry warns that beauty often proves a snare to themselves and others, forbidding pride in it and requiring constant watchfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Joseph did not surrender his soul, but remembered the Lord and the words his father used to read from Abraham, that no man should commit fornication with another man's wife, for death has been ordained in heaven for it, and the sin is recorded in the eternal books before the Lord. Henry gives Joseph's own three arguments: who he was, a man in covenant with God; what the sin was, great wickedness, not a small matter; and against whom, God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Joseph remembered these words and refused to lie with her. The remembrance was his defence: the law read in his father's house stood between him and the temptation in a foreign house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. She besought him for a year, but he refused and would not listen. Henry notes the frequent, constant urging, and that the tempter was his mistress, whose favour would help him forward, while slighting her made her his enemy at his utmost peril. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. She embraced him and held him fast in the house to force him, closing the doors, but he left his garment in her hands, broke through the door, and fled from her presence. Henry praises the flight: Joseph would not stay to parley with temptation, but fled from it as escaping for his life, and his escape was as great an instance of divine power as deliverance from the fiery furnace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The woman saw Joseph would not lie with her and calumniated him before his lord, saying the Hebrew servant had sought to force her, and that when she lifted her voice he fled, leaving his garment and breaking through the door. Henry notes that having tried in vain to make him guilty, she endeavoured to be avenged on him, and that it is no new thing for the best of men to be falsely accused of the worst crimes by the worst of criminals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The Egyptian saw Joseph's garment and the broken door, heard his wife's words, and cast Joseph into prison where the king's prisoners were kept. Henry notes that Potiphar likely chose the worst prison, but God designed it to open the way to Joseph's honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Joseph was in the prison, and the Lord gave him favour and compassion in the sight of the chief of the prison guards, who saw the Lord was with him and made all he did prosper. Henry observes that those with a good conscience in a prison have a good God there, and that a good man will do good wherever he is, even in bonds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The favour Joseph found with the prison chief is the same favour he found with Potiphar: the Lord was with him, and men saw it. Henry notes that Joseph was owned and righted by his God, away from all friends and relations, with none to help or comfort him but the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The prison chief committed all things into Joseph's hands and knew nothing of what was with him, for Joseph did everything and the Lord perfected it. The pattern repeats from Potiphar's house: trust earned by faithfulness, and prosperity given by God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Joseph remained in prison two years, the long waiting between the false charge and the dreams that would free him. The two years are the hidden part of the providence Henry traces through the whole Joseph history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. In those days Pharaoh was wroth against his two eunuchs, the chief butler and the chief baker, and put them in ward in the house of the chief cook, in the prison where Joseph was kept. Henry notes that it was not so much the prison that saddened them as their dreams, and that Joseph had compassion on their sadness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The chief of the prison guards appointed Joseph to serve the butler and baker, and he served before them. The service placed Joseph where their dreams would be told to him, the next link in the chain toward Pharaoh's court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Both men dreamed a dream, the butler and the baker, and they told their dreams to Joseph. Henry notes that Joseph was careful to ascribe the glory to God, and that the butler's dream foretold advancement while the baker's foretold death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. As Joseph interpreted to them, so it befell them: Pharaoh restored the chief butler to his office and slew the chief baker, as Joseph had interpreted. Henry observes that ministers are but interpreters who cannot make the thing otherwise than it is, and that Joseph's interpretations came to pass on the very day fixed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The chief butler forgot Joseph in the prison, although Joseph had told him what would befall him, and did not remember to inform Pharaoh. Henry warns that those at ease are apt to forget others in distress, and that Joseph learned by disappointment to trust in God only, since we cannot expect too little from man nor too much from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
