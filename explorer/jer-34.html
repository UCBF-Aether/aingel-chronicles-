The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 34 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word which came unto Jeremiah from the LORD, when Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, and all his army, and all the kingdoms of the earth of his dominion, and all the people, fought against Jerusalem, and against all the cities thereof, saying,

2 Thus saith the LORD, the God of Israel; Go and speak to Zedekiah king of Judah, and tell him, Thus saith the LORD; Behold, I will give this city into the hand of the king of Babylon, and he shall burn it with fire:

3 And thou shalt not escape out of his hand, but shalt surely be taken, and delivered into his hand; and thine eyes shall behold the eyes of the king of Babylon, and he shall speak with thee mouth to mouth, and thou shalt go to Babylon.

4 Yet hear the word of the LORD, O Zedekiah king of Judah; Thus saith the LORD of thee, Thou shalt not die by the sword:

5 But thou shalt die in peace: and with the burnings of thy fathers, the former kings which were before thee, so shall they burn odours for thee; and they will lament thee, saying, Ah lord! for I have pronounced the word, saith the LORD.

6 Then Jeremiah the prophet spake all these words unto Zedekiah king of Judah in Jerusalem,

7 When the king of Babylon’s army fought against Jerusalem, and against all the cities of Judah that were left, against Lachish, and against Azekah: for these defenced cities remained of the cities of Judah.

8 This is the word that came unto Jeremiah from the LORD, after that the king Zedekiah had made a covenant with all the people which were at Jerusalem, to proclaim liberty unto them;

9 That every man should let his manservant, and every man his maidservant, being an Hebrew or an Hebrewess, go free; that none should serve himself of them, to wit, of a Jew his brother.

10 Now when all the princes, and all the people, which had entered into the covenant, heard that every one should let his manservant, and every one his maidservant, go free, that none should serve themselves of them any more, then they obeyed, and let them go.

11 But afterward they turned, and caused the servants and the handmaids, whom they had let go free, to return, and brought them into subjection for servants and for handmaids.

12 Therefore the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah from the LORD, saying,

13 Thus saith the LORD, the God of Israel; I made a covenant with your fathers in the day that I brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondmen, saying,

14 At the end of seven years let ye go every man his brother an Hebrew, which hath been sold unto thee; and when he hath served thee six years, thou shalt let him go free from thee: but your fathers hearkened not unto me, neither inclined their ear.

15 And ye were now turned, and had done right in my sight, in proclaiming liberty every man to his neighbour; and ye had made a covenant before me in the house which is called by my name:

16 But ye turned and polluted my name, and caused every man his servant, and every man his handmaid, whom ye had set at liberty at their pleasure, to return, and brought them into subjection, to be unto you for servants and for handmaids.

17 Therefore thus saith the LORD; Ye have not hearkened unto me, in proclaiming liberty, every one to his brother, and every man to his neighbour: behold, I proclaim a liberty for you, saith the LORD, to the sword, to the pestilence, and to the famine; and I will make you to be removed into all the kingdoms of the earth.

18 And I will give the men that have transgressed my covenant, which have not performed the words of the covenant which they had made before me, when they cut the calf in twain, and passed between the parts thereof,

19 The princes of Judah, and the princes of Jerusalem, the eunuchs, and the priests, and all the people of the land, which passed between the parts of the calf;

20 I will even give them into the hand of their enemies, and into the hand of them that seek their life: and their dead bodies shall be for meat unto the fowls of the heaven, and to the beasts of the earth.

21 And Zedekiah king of Judah and his princes will I give into the hand of their enemies, and into the hand of them that seek their life, and into the hand of the king of Babylon’s army, which are gone up from you.

22 Behold, I will command, saith the LORD, and cause them to return to this city; and they shall fight against it, and take it, and burn it with fire: and I will make the cities of Judah a desolation without an inhabitant.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

While Nebuchadnezzar's army fights against Jerusalem, Zedekiah is told he will see the king of Babylon face to face and be carried there, and the people are charged with breaking their own covenant: having freed their Hebrew servants, they brought them back into subjection. The broken covenant is answered in kind, and the city will be returned to the Chaldeans to be taken and burned. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

  • Verse 1. The word which came unto Jeremiah from the Lord, when Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, and all his army, and all the kingdoms of the earth of his dominion, and all the people, fought against Jerusalem, and against all the cities thereof, saying. Henry notes Zedekiah is told that the city shall be taken, and that he shall die a captive, but he shall die a natural death: the word when Nebuchadnezzar and all armies and kingdoms fought against Jerusalem and all cities is the taking foretold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel; Go and speak to Zedekiah king of Judah, and tell him, Thus says the Lord; Behold, I will give this city into the hand of the king of Babylon, and he shall burn it with fire. Henry's go and speak to Zedekiah, behold, I will give this city to Babylon's king, and he shall burn it with fire, is the taking told: the city shall be taken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. And thou shalt not escape out of his hand, but shalt surely be taken, and delivered into his hand; and thine eyes shall behold the eyes of the king of Babylon, and he shall speak with thee mouth to mouth, and thou shalt go to Babylon. Henry's thou shalt not escape, surely taken, delivered into his hand, eyes beholding Babylon's king's eyes, speaking mouth to mouth, going to Babylon, is the captive's fate: he shall die a captive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Yet hear the word of the Lord, O Zedekiah king of Judah; Thus says the Lord of thee, Thou shalt not die by the sword. Henry's yet hear, O Zedekiah, thou shalt not die by the sword, is the natural death: he shall die a captive, but not by the sword. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. But thou shalt die in peace: and with the burnings of thy fathers, the former kings which were before thee, so shall they burn odours for thee; and they will lament thee, saying, Ah lord! for I have pronounced the word, says the Lord. Henry notes it is better to live and die penitent in a prison, than to live and die impenitent in a palace: thou shalt die in peace, with fathers' burnings, odours burned for thee, lamented, Ah lord, is the penitent prisoner's peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Then Jeremiah the prophet spoke all these words unto Zedekiah king of Judah in Jerusalem. Henry's Jeremiah spoke all these words unto Zedekiah in Jerusalem is the telling done: the city taken, captive dying naturally, penitent in prison, is spoken to the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. When the king of Babylon's army fought against Jerusalem, and against all the cities of Judah that were left, against Lachish, and against Azekah: for these defenced cities remained of the cities of Judah. Henry's Babylon army fought against Jerusalem and all Judah's left cities, Lachish and Azekah, defenced cities remaining, is the siege's remnant: the taking foretold was already narrowing to these. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. This is the word that came unto Jeremiah from the Lord, after that the king Zedekiah had made a covenant with all the people which were at Jerusalem, to proclaim liberty unto them. Henry notes a Jew should not be held in servitude above seven years: this word after Zedekiah covenanted with all Jerusalem's people to proclaim liberty is the liberty covenant, and the seven-year law behind it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. That every man should let his manservant, and every man his maidservant, being an Hebrew or an Hebrewess, go free; that none should serve himself of them, to wit, of a Jew his brother. Henry's every man let Hebrew manservant and maidservant go free, none serve himself of a Jew his brother, is the law: this law they and their fathers had broken. Henry's princes and people entered the covenant, heard every one let servants go free, none serve themselves any more, then obeyed and let them go, is the partial reformation: obeyed for a time.   • Verse 11. But afterward they turned, and caused the servants and the handmaids, whom they had let go free, to return, and brought them into subjection for servants and for handmaids. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Therefore the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah from the Lord, saying. Henry's therefore the word came to Jeremiah is the reproof: the Jews reproved for compelling poor brethren to return to unlawful bondage begins here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel; I made a covenant with your fathers in the day that I brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondmen, saying. Henry's I made a covenant with fathers in the day brought forth from Egypt, house of bondmen, is the covenant recalled: the Egypt deliverance covenant is the seven-year law's root. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. At the end of seven years let ye go every man his brother an Hebrew, which has been sold unto thee; and when he has served thee six years, thou shalt let him go free from thee: but your fathers hearkened not unto me, neither inclined their ear. Henry's at seven years' end let go every Hebrew brother sold, served six years, let go free, but fathers hearkened not, inclined not ear, is the broken law: they and their fathers had broken it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. And ye were now turned, and had done right in my sight, in proclaiming liberty every man to his neighbour; and ye had made a covenant before me in the house which is called by my name. Henry notes those who think to cheat God by dissembled repentance and partial reformation put the greatest cheat upon their own souls: ye were now turned, done right in my sight, proclaiming liberty every man to neighbour, covenanted before me in the house called by my name, is the dissembled repentance, right for a moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. But ye turned and polluted my name, and caused every man his servant, and every man his handmaid, whom he had set at liberty, to return, and brought them into subjection, to be unto you for servants and for handmaids. Henry notes this shows that liberty to sin is really only liberty to have the sorest judgments: ye turned, polluted my name, caused freed servants and handmaids to return, brought into subjection, is the polluting turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Therefore thus says the Lord; Ye have not hearkened unto me, in proclaiming liberty, every one to his brother, and every man to his neighbour: behold, I proclaim a liberty for you, says the Lord, to the sword, to the pestilence, and to the famine; and I will make you to be removed into all the kingdoms of the earth. Henry notes it is just with God to disappoint expectations of mercy, when we disappoint the expectations of duty: ye have not hearkened in proclaiming liberty to brother and neighbour, behold, I proclaim liberty for you to sword, pestilence, famine, removed into all kingdoms, is mercy disappointed for duty disappointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. And I will give the men that have transgressed my covenant, which have not performed the words of the covenant which they had made before me, when they cut the calf in twain, and passed between the parts thereof. Henry notes when reformation springs only from terror, it is seldom lasting: I will give the men that transgressed my covenant, not performed its words made before me, when they cut the calf in twain and passed between parts, is the terror-born covenant broken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. The princes of Judah, and the princes of Jerusalem, the eunuchs, and the priests, and all the people of the land, which passed between the parts of the calf. Henry notes solemn vows thus entered into profane the ordinances of God, and the most forward to bind themselves by appeals to God are commonly most ready to break them: princes of Judah and Jerusalem, eunuchs, priests, all the land's people, passing between the calf's parts, are the forward binders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. I will even give them into the hand of their enemies, and into the hand of them that seek their life: and their dead bodies shall be for meat unto the fowls of the heaven, and to the beasts of the earth. Henry's I will give them into enemies' hands, into seekers of life, dead bodies for heaven's fowls and earth's beasts, is the vow-breakers' doom: profaned ordinances bring sorest judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. And Zedekiah king of Judah and his princes will I give into the hand of their enemies, and into the hand of them that seek their life, and into the hand of the king of Babylon's army, which are gone up from you. Henry's Zedekiah and princes given to enemies, life-seekers, Babylon's army gone up from you, is the king's share: the army hoped gone returns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Behold, I will command, says the Lord, and cause them to return to this city; and they shall fight against it, and take it, and burn it with fire: and I will make the cities of Judah a desolation without an inhabitant. Henry notes let us look to our hearts, that our repentance may be real, and take care that the law of God regulates our conduct: behold, I will command, cause them to return to this city, fight, take, burn with fire, make Judah's cities desolation without inhabitant, is the real repentance called for, and the desolation for the unreal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
