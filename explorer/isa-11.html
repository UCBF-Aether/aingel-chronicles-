The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And there shall come forth a rod out of the stem of Jesse, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots:

2 And the spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD;

3 And shall make him of quick understanding in the fear of the LORD: and he shall not judge after the sight of his eyes, neither reprove after the hearing of his ears:

4 But with righteousness shall he judge the poor, and reprove with equity for the meek of the earth: and he shall smite the earth: with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips shall he slay the wicked.

5 And righteousness shall be the girdle of his loins, and faithfulness the girdle of his reins.

6 The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the kid; and the calf and the young lion and the fatling together; and a little child shall lead them.

7 And the cow and the bear shall feed; their young ones shall lie down together: and the lion shall eat straw like the ox.

8 And the sucking child shall play on the hole of the asp, and the weaned child shall put his hand on the cockatrice’ den.

9 They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain: for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea.

10 And in that day there shall be a root of Jesse, which shall stand for an ensign of the people; to it shall the Gentiles seek: and his rest shall be glorious.

11 And it shall come to pass in that day, that the Lord shall set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people, which shall be left, from Assyria, and from Egypt, and from Pathros, and from Cush, and from Elam, and from Shinar, and from Hamath, and from the islands of the sea.

12 And he shall set up an ensign for the nations, and shall assemble the outcasts of Israel, and gather together the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth.

13 The envy also of Ephraim shall depart, and the adversaries of Judah shall be cut off: Ephraim shall not envy Judah, and Judah shall not vex Ephraim.

14 But they shall fly upon the shoulders of the Philistines toward the west; they shall spoil them of the east together: they shall lay their hand upon Edom and Moab; and the children of Ammon shall obey them.

15 And the LORD shall utterly destroy the tongue of the Egyptian sea; and with his mighty wind shall he shake his hand over the river, and shall smite it in the seven streams, and make men go over dryshod.

16 And there shall be an highway for the remnant of his people, which shall be left, from Assyria; like as it was to Israel in the day that he came up out of the land of Egypt.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

From the cut-down stump of Jesse's house a humble shoot grows: a Branch on whom the Spirit rests, judging with righteousness and bringing a peace in which wolf and lamb dwell together. The chapter closes with a highway for the remnant scattered in Assyria and beyond, as clear as the way through the sea at the exodus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. There will come forth a rod out of the stem of Jesse, and a Branch will grow out of his roots. When the proud boughs of Lebanon, the Assyrians, are lopped and the forests cut down, a seemingly humble rod will come from Jesse to retrieve the injuries done by the Assyrian rod; the stem is literally the stump of a tree cut close by the roots, expressing the depressed state of David's royal house when Messiah arises from it to more than pristine glory, and he is both Branch and Root. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The Spirit of the Lord will rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and might, of knowledge and of the fear of the Lord. Seven gifts are specified to imply their perfection in him, as the seven Spirits signify the Holy Ghost in perfect fullness; the Spirit rests permanently on him, not merely coming upon him, and the prophets had only a portion out of the fullness that is in the Son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He will be of quick understanding in the fear of the Lord — literally quick-scented in the fear of Jehovah, endowed with singular sagacity in discerning genuine religious fear even dormant in the unawakened sinner — and he will not judge after the sight of his eyes or reprove after the hearing of his ears. He judges not by mere external appearances or plausible hearsays but by the true merits of each case, as a just Judge and Ruler. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. With righteousness he will judge the poor and reprove with equity for the meek of the earth, and he will smite the earth with the rod of his mouth and slay the wicked with the breath of his lips. The rod of his mouth is his condemning sentences against the wicked, and the breath of his lips his judicial decisions; as the Word of God he comes to strike the blow deciding his claim to the kingdom usurped by Satan and the beast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Righteousness will be the girdle of his loins, and faithfulness the girdle of his reins. As the girdle secures firmly the rest of the garments, so truth gives firm consistency to his whole character, and he is the antitypical High Priest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The wolf will dwell with the lamb, the leopard lie down with the kid, the calf and young lion and fatling together, and a little child will lead them. Each animal is coupled with its natural prey, a fit state under the Prince of Peace; the animals may figure men of corresponding characters, yet a literal change restoring the state in Eden, with man's lost dominion over animals restored in the Son of Man, is the more likely interpretation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The cow and the bear will feed together and their young ones lie down together, and the lion will eat straw like the ox. The feeding together completes the picture of peace among former enemies, with predators no longer living on flesh and blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The sucking child will play on the hole of the asp, and the weaned child will put his hand on the cockatrice's den. The child delights himself in sport where danger once reigned, and the cockatrice is a kind of adder more venomous than the asp, a fabulous serpent supposed hatched from a cock's egg. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They will not hurt nor destroy in all God's holy mountain, for the earth will be full of the knowledge of the Lord as the waters cover the sea. The holy mountain, Zion and Jerusalem, seat of Messiah's throne, stands for the whole earth, and as waters find their way into every cavern of the sea's depths, so the knowledge of the Lord will pervade every recess of the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. In that day there will be a root of Jesse, standing for an ensign of the people, to which the Gentiles will seek, and his rest will be glorious. The root is rather a shoot from the root, standing permanently and prominently as a banner and rallying point, to which the peoples give their allegiance and resort as to an oracle, and his resting-place is glorious like the sanctuary where the ark rested. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. In that day the Lord will set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people from Assyria, Egypt, Pathros, Cush, Elam, Shinar, Hamath, and the islands of the sea. This coming restoration is distinct from that after Babylon yet resembles it, and like the first literal restoration so the second will be literal, only much more universal, gathering the remnant from Upper Egypt, Ethiopia, Persia, Babylonian Mesopotamia, and the far western regions beyond the sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. He will set up an ensign for the nations and assemble the outcasts of Israel and gather together the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth. In the first restoration Judah alone was restored with a few of Israel, but in the future restoration both are expressly specified: outcasts are ascribed to Israel, dispersed to Judah, the masculine and feminine conjoined expressing the universality of the restoration, from the wings of the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The envy of Ephraim will depart and the adversaries of Judah will be cut off: Ephraim will not envy Judah, and Judah will not vex Ephraim. The jealousy began as early as Joshua's time, increased through Saul and David, and ended in disruption under Jeroboam; in the restoration the old envy and the adversaries from Judah hostile to Ephraim both cease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. With united forces they will fly upon the shoulders of the Philistines toward the west and spoil them of the east together, laying their hand upon Edom and Moab, while the children of Ammon obey them. As a bird of prey they will make a sudden victorious descent upon their southwestern borders, and take possession of Edom south of Judah, Moab east of Jordan, and Ammon north of Moab, while the Arabs of the east are only despoiled, not reduced to regular government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The Lord will utterly destroy the tongue of the Egyptian sea and shake his hand over the river with his mighty wind, smiting it into seven streams so men may go over dryshod. There will be a second exodus eclipsing even the former one from Egypt: the Red Sea's tongue will be dried up as God dooms it, and the Euphrates, the obstruction to return from Assyria, will be divided into many streams, as Cyrus divided the Gyndes, so that even in sandals the people cross unwetted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. There will be a highway for the remnant of his people left from Assyria, as there was for Israel in the day he came up out of Egypt. The highway will be clear of obstructions, like the way through the sea, for the remnant returning from Assyria. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms
