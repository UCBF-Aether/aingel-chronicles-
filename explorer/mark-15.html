The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And straightway in the morning the chief priests held a consultation with the elders and scribes and the whole council, and bound Jesus, and carried him away, and delivered him to Pilate.

2 And Pilate asked him, Art thou the King of the Jews? And he answering said unto them, Thou sayest it.

3 And the chief priests accused him of many things: but he answered nothing.

4 And Pilate asked him again, saying, Answerest thou nothing? behold how many things they witness against thee.

5 But Jesus yet answered nothing; so that Pilate marvelled.

6 Now at that feast he released unto them one prisoner, whomsoever they desired.

7 And there was one named Barabbas, which lay bound with them that had made insurrection with him, who had committed murder in the insurrection.

8 And the multitude crying aloud began to desire him to do as he had ever done unto them.

9 But Pilate answered them, saying, Will ye that I release unto you the King of the Jews?

10 For he knew that the chief priests had delivered him for envy.

11 But the chief priests moved the people, that he should rather release Barabbas unto them.

12 And Pilate answered and said again unto them, What will ye then that I shall do unto him whom ye call the King of the Jews?

13 And they cried out again, Crucify him.

14 Then Pilate said unto them, Why, what evil hath he done? And they cried out the more exceedingly, Crucify him.

15 And so Pilate, willing to content the people, released Barabbas unto them, and delivered Jesus, when he had scourged him, to be crucified.

16 And the soldiers led him away into the hall, called Praetorium; and they call together the whole band.

17 And they clothed him with purple, and platted a crown of thorns, and put it about his head,

18 And began to salute him, Hail, King of the Jews!

19 And they smote him on the head with a reed, and did spit upon him, and bowing their knees worshipped him.

20 And when they had mocked him, they took off the purple from him, and put his own clothes on him, and led him out to crucify him.

21 And they compel one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country, the father of Alexander and Rufus, to bear his cross.

22 And they bring him unto the place Golgotha, which is, being interpreted, The place of a skull.

23 And they gave him to drink wine mingled with myrrh: but he received it not.

24 And when they had crucified him, they parted his garments, casting lots upon them, what every man should take.

25 And it was the third hour, and they crucified him.

26 And the superscription of his accusation was written over, THE KING OF THE JEWS.

27 And with him they crucify two thieves; the one on his right hand, and the other on his left.

28 And the scripture was fulfilled, which saith, And he was numbered with the transgressors.

29 And they that passed by railed on him, wagging their heads, and saying, Ah, thou that destroyest the temple, and buildest it in three days,

30 Save thyself, and come down from the cross.

31 Likewise also the chief priests mocking said among themselves with the scribes, He saved others; himself he cannot save.

32 Let Christ the King of Israel descend now from the cross, that we may see and believe. And they that were crucified with him reviled him.

33 And when the sixth hour was come, there was darkness over the whole land until the ninth hour.

34 And at the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, saying, Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani? which is, being interpreted, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?

35 And some of them that stood by, when they heard it, said, Behold, he calleth Elias.

36 And one ran and filled a spunge full of vinegar, and put it on a reed, and gave him to drink, saying, Let alone; let us see whether Elias will come to take him down.

37 And Jesus cried with a loud voice, and gave up the ghost.

38 And the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom.

39 And when the centurion, which stood over against him, saw that he so cried out, and gave up the ghost, he said, Truly this man was the Son of God.

40 There were also women looking on afar off: among whom was Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James the less and of Joses, and Salome;

41 (Who also, when he was in Galilee, followed him, and ministered unto him;) and many other women which came up with him unto Jerusalem.

42 And now when the even was come, because it was the preparation, that is, the day before the sabbath,

43 Joseph of Arimathaea, an honourable counsellor, which also waited for the kingdom of God, came, and went in boldly unto Pilate, and craved the body of Jesus.

44 And Pilate marvelled if he were already dead: and calling unto him the centurion, he asked him whether he had been any while dead.

45 And when he knew it of the centurion, he gave the body to Joseph.

46 And he bought fine linen, and took him down, and wrapped him in the linen, and laid him in a sepulchre which was hewn out of a rock, and rolled a stone unto the door of the sepulchre.

47 And Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses beheld where he was laid.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the morning the council binds Jesus and delivers him to Pilate, who asks “Art thou the King of the Jews?” and, pressed by the crowd, releases Barabbas and sentences Jesus (vv. 1–15). Soldiers mock him with purple and thorns, Simon of Cyrene carries his cross, and he is crucified between two thieves, mocked by passers-by and rulers, until darkness, the ninth-hour cry, and his death, when the temple veil tears (vv. 16–39). Joseph of Arimathaea begs his body and lays it in a rock tomb, watched by Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses (vv. 40–47). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 15

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Straightway in the morning the chief priests held a consultation with the elders, scribes, and the whole council, bound Jesus, carried him away, and delivered him to Pilate. Morning consultation bound him for Pilate, for the council, having condemned him, needed Roman power to kill him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Pilate asked him, Are you the King of the Jews? And he answering said, You say it. Pilate's question names the political charge, and 'You say it' affirms it while leaving Pilate to weigh what kind of king he is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The chief priests accused him of many things, but he answered nothing. Many accusations met silence, for Christ would not answer charges that answered themselves by their multitude and malice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Pilate asked him again, Answer you nothing? Behold how many things they witness against you. Pilate, astonished at silence, pressed him to answer the many witnesses, as if number made a case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. But Jesus yet answered nothing, so that Pilate marvelled. Continued silence marvelled Pilate, for innocence that will not defend itself is rarer than guilt that pleads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Now at that feast he released to them one prisoner, whomever they desired. The feast custom of releasing a prisoner gave the crowd a choice, and Pilate hoped it would free Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. There was one named Barabbas, who lay bound with those who had made insurrection with him, who had committed murder in the insurrection. Barabbas, bound for insurrection and murder, was the alternative to Christ, a true criminal set against an innocent king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The multitude crying aloud began to desire him to do as he had ever done to them. The crowd, crying aloud, demanded the custom, and their demand became the priests' opportunity to steer the choice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Pilate answered, Will you that I release to you the King of the Jews? Pilate named Jesus King of the Jews, hoping the title would win the crowd, but the title itself became the priests' weapon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For he knew that the chief priests had delivered him for envy. Pilate knew envy, not justice, delivered Jesus, for the priests envied his following, and a judge who knows the motive yet yields is himself condemned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. But the chief priests moved the people, that he should rather release Barabbas to them. The priests moved the people to choose Barabbas, for a stirred crowd will choose a murderer over a reprover. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Pilate answered again, What will you then that I shall do to him whom you call the King of the Jews? Pilate's question, asking the crowd to sentence its king, abdicated judgment to the mob he should have restrained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They cried out again, Crucify him. The crowd's cry, repeated, demanded crucifixion, the Roman death for slaves and rebels, for the king they would not have. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Then Pilate said, Why, what evil has he done? And they cried out the more exceedingly, Crucify him. Pilate's question about evil met a louder cry, for a mob answered with volume what it could not answer with reason. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. So Pilate, willing to content the people, released Barabbas to them, and delivered Jesus, when he had scourged him, to be crucified. Willing to content the people, Pilate released the murderer and scourged the innocent for crucifixion, and contenting a crowd cost a judge his conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The soldiers led him away into the hall, called Praetorium, and called together the whole band. The whole band gathered in the Praetorium, for mockery, like the trial, was done in company. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. They clothed him with purple, platted a crown of thorns, and put it about his head. Purple and thorns mocked his kingship: royal colour with a crown of pain, a parody that unknowingly crowned the true king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They began to salute him, Hail, King of the Jews! The salute, Hail, King, mocked Caesar's own greeting, and soldiers' jest became unwilling homage to the king they derided. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. They smote him on the head with a reed, and spit upon him, and bowing their knees worshipped him. Reed, spit, and kneeling worship together show mockery's cruelty, for they bowed to the king they struck. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. When they had mocked him, they took off the purple, put his own clothes on him, and led him out to crucify him. Mockery ended, his own clothes returned, and leading him out began the journey to Golgotha, where mockery would become execution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. They compelled one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country, the father of Alexander and Rufus, to bear his cross. Simon, passing by from the country, was compelled to bear the cross, and his sons' names preserve a family that later knew the faith his burden introduced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. They brought him to the place Golgotha, which is, being interpreted, The place of a skull. Golgotha, the skull's place, names the hill of death, and the cross stood where skulls marked execution's ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. They gave him to drink wine mingled with myrrh, but he received it not. Myrrhed wine, offered to deaden pain, he refused, for he would bear the cross with senses clear, and drink the cup his Father gave, not man's stupefaction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. When they had crucified him, they parted his garments, casting lots upon them, what every man should take. Crucifixion done, soldiers parted his garments by lot, fulfilling Scripture in the very act of dividing his clothes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. It was the third hour, and they crucified him. The third hour, nine in the morning, marks the crucifixion's time, that the day's hours may be counted against the darkness and death to follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The superscription of his accusation was written over, THE KING OF THE JEWS. The superscription, written over him, accused him of kingship, and Pilate's title, meant as charge, proclaimed the truth to every passer-by. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. With him they crucified two thieves, one on his right hand and the other on his left. Two thieves, right and left, flanked him, fulfilling the places James and John had sought, now filled by criminals in shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. The scripture was fulfilled, which says, And he was numbered with the transgressors. Numbered with transgressors, he bore the transgressors' place, and Scripture's word was fulfilled in his company on the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. They that passed by railed on him, wagging their heads, and saying, Ah, you that destroy the temple and build it in three days. Passers-by railed and wagged their heads, repeating the false temple charge as a taunt, for the cross seemed to refute the builder's claim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Save yourself, and come down from the cross. 'Save yourself' was the taunt's logic: a Saviour who cannot save himself is no saviour, yet saving himself would have unsaved them all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Likewise also the chief priests mocking said among themselves with the scribes, He saved others; himself he cannot save. Priests and scribes mocked among themselves, confessing he saved others while denying he could save himself, and their confession is the gospel in a taunt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Let Christ the King of Israel descend now from the cross, that we may see and believe. And they that were crucified with him reviled him. Descending would have won their sight, they claimed, but faith that waits for a sign from the cross mistakes the cross itself, and even fellow-sufferers reviled him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. When the sixth hour was come, there was darkness over the whole land until the ninth hour. Darkness from noon to three covered the land, for creation mourned its maker, and midday night marked the atonement's horror. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. At the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani? which is, My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? The ninth-hour cry, in his own tongue, quotes the psalm of forsakenness, for he bore the Father's hiding that sinners might never be forsaken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Some standing by, when they heard it, said, Behold, he calls Elijah. Hearers mistook Eloi for Elijah, for they listened for a name, not for the psalm, and their error shows how little the cross was understood even as it happened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. One ran and filled a sponge full of vinegar, put it on a reed, and gave him to drink, saying, Let alone; let us see whether Elijah will come to take him down. Vinegar on a reed was offered while watchers waited for Elijah, and thirst's relief became another delay in the spectacle of his dying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Jesus cried with a loud voice, and gave up the ghost. A loud cry, not a whisper, ended his life, for he gave up his spirit willingly, not as one whose strength merely failed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. The veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom. The veil rent from top to bottom, by heaven's hand, not man's, opened the holy place at his death, for the way to God was now open through the torn veil of his flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. When the centurion, who stood over against him, saw that he so cried out and gave up the ghost, he said, Truly this man was the Son of God. The centurion, facing the cross, confessed him Son of God, for his manner of dying convinced a Roman soldier whom priests had not convinced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. There were also women looking on afar off, among whom was Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James the less and Joses, and Salome. Women looked on afar off, for love kept them near while fear kept them distant, and their names preserve the witnesses of his death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Who also, when he was in Galilee, followed him and ministered to him, and many other women who came up with him to Jerusalem. Galilean followers who had ministered to him followed to Jerusalem, and women's ministry, begun in Galilee, ended in faithful watching at the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. When the evening was come, because it was the preparation, the day before the sabbath. Evening and preparation pressed burial, for the sabbath would forbid work, and the body must be laid before the holy day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. Joseph of Arimathea, an honourable counsellor, who also waited for the kingdom of God, came, went in boldly to Pilate, and craved the body of Jesus. Joseph, honourable and waiting for the kingdom, went boldly to Pilate, for a secret disciple became bold when boldness was most needed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. Pilate marvelled if he were already dead, and calling the centurion, asked whether he had been any while dead. Pilate marvelled at so speedy a death, and the centurion's testimony confirmed it, for the judge required proof before releasing the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. When he knew it of the centurion, he gave the body to Joseph. Pilate gave the body on the centurion's word, and the gift to Joseph honoured the dead as the trial had dishonoured the living. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. He bought fine linen, took him down, wrapped him in the linen, laid him in a sepulchre hewn out of a rock, and rolled a stone to the door of the sepulchre. Fine linen, a rock tomb, and a rolled stone gave him honourable burial, and the stone's rolling seemed to seal the story, though it sealed only the tomb. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses beheld where he was laid. Two Marys beheld where he was laid, for witnesses of burial would become witnesses of resurrection, and they noted the place to return to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Golgotha (v. 22): The place of crucifixion, which the chapter text itself glosses as “being interpreted, The place of a skull.” Documented Standard corpus, Mark 15:22; study note on Mark 15:22

  • Verse 27. With him they crucified two thieves, one on his right hand and the other on his left. Two thieves, right and left, flanked him, fulfilling the places James and John had sought, now filled by criminals in shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. The scripture was fulfilled, which says, And he was numbered with the transgressors. Numbered with transgressors, he bore the transgressors' place, and Scripture's word was fulfilled in his company on the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. They that passed by railed on him, wagging their heads, and saying, Ah, you that destroy the temple and build it in three days. Passers-by railed and wagged their heads, repeating the false temple charge as a taunt, for the cross seemed to refute the builder's claim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Save yourself, and come down from the cross. 'Save yourself' was the taunt's logic: a Saviour who cannot save himself is no saviour, yet saving himself would have unsaved them all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Likewise also the chief priests mocking said among themselves with the scribes, He saved others; himself he cannot save. Priests and scribes mocked among themselves, confessing he saved others while denying he could save himself, and their confession is the gospel in a taunt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Let Christ the King of Israel descend now from the cross, that we may see and believe. And they that were crucified with him reviled him. Descending would have won their sight, they claimed, but faith that waits for a sign from the cross mistakes the cross itself, and even fellow-sufferers reviled him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. When the sixth hour was come, there was darkness over the whole land until the ninth hour. Darkness from noon to three covered the land, for creation mourned its maker, and midday night marked the atonement's horror. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. At the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani? which is, My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? The ninth-hour cry, in his own tongue, quotes the psalm of forsakenness, for he bore the Father's hiding that sinners might never be forsaken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Some standing by, when they heard it, said, Behold, he calls Elijah. Hearers mistook Eloi for Elijah, for they listened for a name, not for the psalm, and their error shows how little the cross was understood even as it happened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. One ran and filled a sponge full of vinegar, put it on a reed, and gave him to drink, saying, Let alone; let us see whether Elijah will come to take him down. Vinegar on a reed was offered while watchers waited for Elijah, and thirst's relief became another delay in the spectacle of his dying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Jesus cried with a loud voice, and gave up the ghost. A loud cry, not a whisper, ended his life, for he gave up his spirit willingly, not as one whose strength merely failed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. The veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom. The veil rent from top to bottom, by heaven's hand, not man's, opened the holy place at his death, for the way to God was now open through the torn veil of his flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. When the centurion, who stood over against him, saw that he so cried out and gave up the ghost, he said, Truly this man was the Son of God. The centurion, facing the cross, confessed him Son of God, for his manner of dying convinced a Roman soldier whom priests had not convinced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. There were also women looking on afar off, among whom was Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James the less and Joses, and Salome. Women looked on afar off, for love kept them near while fear kept them distant, and their names preserve the witnesses of his death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Who also, when he was in Galilee, followed him and ministered to him, and many other women who came up with him to Jerusalem. Galilean followers who had ministered to him followed to Jerusalem, and women's ministry, begun in Galilee, ended in faithful watching at the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. When the evening was come, because it was the preparation, the day before the sabbath. Evening and preparation pressed burial, for the sabbath would forbid work, and the body must be laid before the holy day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. Joseph of Arimathea, an honourable counsellor, who also waited for the kingdom of God, came, went in boldly to Pilate, and craved the body of Jesus. Joseph, honourable and waiting for the kingdom, went boldly to Pilate, for a secret disciple became bold when boldness was most needed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. Pilate marvelled if he were already dead, and calling the centurion, asked whether he had been any while dead. Pilate marvelled at so speedy a death, and the centurion's testimony confirmed it, for the judge required proof before releasing the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. When he knew it of the centurion, he gave the body to Joseph. Pilate gave the body on the centurion's word, and the gift to Joseph honoured the dead as the trial had dishonoured the living. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. He bought fine linen, took him down, wrapped him in the linen, laid him in a sepulchre hewn out of a rock, and rolled a stone to the door of the sepulchre. Fine linen, a rock tomb, and a rolled stone gave him honourable burial, and the stone's rolling seemed to seal the story, though it sealed only the tomb. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses beheld where he was laid. Two Marys beheld where he was laid, for witnesses of burial would become witnesses of resurrection, and they noted the place to return to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
