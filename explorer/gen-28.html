The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 28 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Isaac called Jacob, and blessed him, and charged him, and said unto him, Thou shalt not take a wife of the daughters of Canaan.

2 Arise, go to Padanaram, to the house of Bethuel thy mother’s father; and take thee a wife from thence of the daughters of Laban thy mother’s brother.

3 And God Almighty bless thee, and make thee fruitful, and multiply thee, that thou mayest be a multitude of people;

4 And give thee the blessing of Abraham, to thee, and to thy seed with thee; that thou mayest inherit the land wherein thou art a stranger, which God gave unto Abraham.

5 And Isaac sent away Jacob: and he went to Padanaram unto Laban, son of Bethuel the Syrian, the brother of Rebekah, Jacob’s and Esau’s mother.

6 When Esau saw that Isaac had blessed Jacob, and sent him away to Padanaram, to take him a wife from thence; and that as he blessed him he gave him a charge, saying, Thou shalt not take a wife of the daughters of Canaan;

7 And that Jacob obeyed his father and his mother, and was gone to Padanaram;

8 And Esau seeing that the daughters of Canaan pleased not Isaac his father;

9 Then went Esau unto Ishmael, and took unto the wives which he had Mahalath the daughter of Ishmael Abraham’s son, the sister of Nebajoth, to be his wife.

10 And Jacob went out from Beersheba, and went toward Haran.

11 And he lighted upon a certain place, and tarried there all night, because the sun was set; and he took of the stones of that place, and put them for his pillows, and lay down in that place to sleep.

12 And he dreamed, and behold a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven: and behold the angels of God ascending and descending on it.

13 And, behold, the LORD stood above it, and said, I am the LORD God of Abraham thy father, and the God of Isaac: the land whereon thou liest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed;

14 And thy seed shall be as the dust of the earth, and thou shalt spread abroad to the west, and to the east, and to the north, and to the south: and in thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed.

15 And, behold, I am with thee, and will keep thee in all places whither thou goest, and will bring thee again into this land; for I will not leave thee, until I have done that which I have spoken to thee of.

16 And Jacob awaked out of his sleep, and he said, Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not.

17 And he was afraid, and said, How dreadful is this place! this is none other but the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven.

18 And Jacob rose up early in the morning, and took the stone that he had put for his pillows, and set it up for a pillar, and poured oil upon the top of it.

19 And he called the name of that place Bethel: but the name of that city was called Luz at the first.

20 And Jacob vowed a vow, saying, If God will be with me, and will keep me in this way that I go, and will give me bread to eat, and raiment to put on,

21 So that I come again to my father’s house in peace; then shall the LORD be my God:

22 And this stone, which I have set for a pillar, shall be God’s house: and of all that thou shalt give me I will surely give the tenth unto thee.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Isaac blesses Jacob, charges him not to marry a Canaanite, and sends him to Paddan-aram. On the way Jacob dreams at Bethel of a ladder reaching to heaven with angels ascending and descending, hears the covenant promise renewed, wakes in awe, and vows to give God a tenth of all he receives. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Isaac called Jacob, blessed him and charged him not to take a wife from the daughters of Canaan, joining blessing to a solemn charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Isaac told Jacob to go to Paddan-aram, to Bethuel his mother's father, and take a wife from Laban's daughters, keeping marriage within the kindred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Isaac prayed that God Almighty would bless Jacob, make him fruitful and multiply him into a multitude of peoples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He prayed that Jacob would receive the blessing of Abraham, for himself and his seed, so he would inherit the land where he was a stranger, which God gave to Abraham. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Isaac sent Jacob away, and Jacob went to Paddan-aram to Laban son of Bethuel the Aramean, brother of Rebekah, travelling alone and on foot, unlike his father's grand escort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Esau saw that Isaac had blessed Jacob and sent him to Paddan-aram for a wife, with the charge not to marry a Canaanite, and he took note of the example. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Esau also saw that Jacob had obeyed his father and mother and gone to Paddan-aram, obedience that shamed his own disregard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Esau saw that the daughters of Canaan displeased Isaac his father, a judgment he might have heeded long before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Esau then went to Ishmael and took Mahalath, Ishmael's daughter and Nebaioth's sister, as a wife in addition to his other wives, a partial reform that made matters worse by joining a rejected family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Jacob went out from Beersheba toward Haran, an exile even though he carried the blessing, blessed with plenty yet going away poor, and blessed with rule yet going out to service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Jacob came to a place and stayed the night because the sun had set, taking stones for a pillow and lying down, exposed and cold, yet trusting God's protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. He dreamed of a ladder set on earth reaching to heaven, with angels ascending and descending on it, a vision of providence at work and, as Henry reads it, of Christ as the way between heaven and earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The Lord stood above the ladder and said he was the God of Abraham and Isaac, promising the land on which Jacob lay to him and his seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. God promised that Jacob's seed would be as the dust of the earth, spreading west, east, north and south, and that in him and his seed all families of the earth would be blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. God promised to be with Jacob, keep him wherever he went, bring him back to this land, and not leave him until all that had been promised was done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Jacob awoke and said, surely the Lord is in this place, and I did not know it, recognising God's presence where he had expected only a lonely lodging. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Jacob was afraid and said, how awesome is this place, this is none other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Jacob rose early, took the stone he had used as a pillow, set it up as a pillar and poured oil on top of it, marking the place with a memorial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. He called the place Bethel, house of God, though the city had formerly been called Luz, renaming it for the vision he had received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Jacob vowed that if God would be with him, keep him on his journey, give him bread to eat and clothing to wear, he would trust him as his God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. He added that if he returned in peace to his father's house, then the Lord would be his God, making safe return the seal of the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. He promised that the stone he had set up would be God's house, and that of all God gave him he would surely give a tenth, a vow of worship and thanksgiving in return for protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Bethel. House of God; Jacob renamed the place, formerly called Luz, for the vision he had received there. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 28:19, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
