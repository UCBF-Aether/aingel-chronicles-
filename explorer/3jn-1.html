The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 3 John 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The elder unto the wellbeloved Gaius, whom I love in the truth.

2 Beloved, I wish above all things that thou mayest prosper and be in health, even as thy soul prospereth.

3 For I rejoiced greatly, when the brethren came and testified of the truth that is in thee, even as thou walkest in the truth.

4 I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth.

5 Beloved, thou doest faithfully whatsoever thou doest to the brethren, and to strangers;

6 Which have borne witness of thy charity before the church: whom if thou bring forward on their journey after a godly sort, thou shalt do well:

7 Because that for his name’s sake they went forth, taking nothing of the Gentiles.

8 We therefore ought to receive such, that we might be fellowhelpers to the truth.

9 I wrote unto the church: but Diotrephes, who loveth to have the preeminence among them, receiveth us not.

10 Wherefore, if I come, I will remember his deeds which he doeth, prating against us with malicious words: and not content therewith, neither doth he himself receive the brethren, and forbiddeth them that would, and casteth them out of the church.

11 Beloved, follow not that which is evil, but that which is good. He that doeth good is of God: but he that doeth evil hath not seen God.

12 Demetrius hath good report of all men, and of the truth itself: yea, and we also bear record; and ye know that our record is true.

13 I had many things to write, but I will not with ink and pen write unto thee:

14 But I trust I shall shortly see thee, and we shall speak face to face. Peace be to thee. Our friends salute thee. Greet the friends by name.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The elder writes to the wellbeloved Gaius, whom he loves in truth, praying his health and prosperity match his soul's prosperity, and rejoicing that brethren testified of his truth and walking in it. Gaius is commended for faithful hospitality to travelling brethren who went out for Christ's name taking nothing from Gentiles, whom fellow believers ought to support. Diotrephes, by contrast, loves preeminence, refuses the brethren and casts out those who would receive them, while Demetrius has a good report from all, and the elder hopes to speak face to face rather than with ink and pen. Interpreted Editorial overview of the chapter text; verse-by-verse study-note summaries follow

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The elder writes to the wellbeloved Gaius, whom he loves in the truth. Henry notes John lays aside grander titles for the common pastoral title, and JFB stresses the personal emphasis: I, for my part, love him in the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. John wishes Gaius prosperity and health even as his soul prospers. Henry calls soul-prosperity the greatest blessing on this side of heaven, and JFB notes bodily health is wished next after, not above, spiritual health. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. John rejoiced greatly when brethren came and testified of the truth in Gaius, even as he walks in the truth. Henry reads the testimony as the reality of his faith shown by loving deeds, and JFB notes the contrast with Diotrephes to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. John has no greater joy than to hear his children walk in truth. Henry calls walking in truth the best evidence of having the truth, and JFB notes children here means members of the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Gaius does faithfully whatever he does to the brethren and to strangers. Henry praises a catholic spirit that welcomes all who bear Christ's image, and JFB notes the strangers were brethren unknown to him, which enhances the love shown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Those brethren bore witness of Gaius's charity before the church, and John urges him to bring them forward on their journey after a godly sort. Henry calls this doing common kindness in a manner worthy of God, and JFB explains it as giving provisions for the way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The brethren went forth for Christ's name's sake, taking nothing of the Gentiles. Henry stresses they carried a free gospel, and JFB notes they refused maintenance by choice, as Paul did at Corinth, so as not to burden infant Gentile churches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Fellow believers therefore ought to receive such workers, that they may be fellowhelpers to the truth. Henry argues those who cannot preach may still advance the gospel by receiving and helping those who do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. John wrote to the church, but Diotrephes, who loves pre-eminence, receives them not. Henry names pride and ambition as the root, and JFB notes Diotrephes rejected the brethren John recommended, which was virtually rejecting John himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. If John comes, he will remember Diotrephes's deeds: prating with malicious words, refusing the brethren, forbidding others to receive them, and casting them out of the church. Henry warns it is worse to hinder good than merely to do none, and JFB notes the casting out was excommunication by a leading man's influence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Gaius is told to follow not evil but good, for he that does good is of God and he that does evil has not seen God. Henry reads doing good as evidence of birth from God, and JFB points the contrast: evil as in Diotrephes, good as in Demetrius. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Demetrius has a good report of all men and of the truth itself, confirmed by John's own record. Henry calls a name in the gospel better than that of sons and daughters, and JFB notes the truth itself bears witness that his deeds match the gospel standard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. John had many things to write but will not write with ink and pen. Henry observes many things are more proper for immediate communication than for letter, and JFB renders the sense as a wish not to write more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. John trusts to see Gaius shortly and speak face to face, wishing him peace, sending friends' greetings, and asking him to greet the friends by name. Henry notes good Christians wish others the happiness they know themselves, and JFB explains peace as inward, fraternal, and supernal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
