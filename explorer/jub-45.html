The Text — Jubilees 45 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And Israel went into the country of Egypt, into the land of Goshen, on the new moon of the fourth month, in the second year of the third week of the forty-fifth jubilee.

2 And Joseph went to meet his father Jacob, to the land of Goshen, and he fell on his father's neck and wept.

3 And Israel said unto Joseph : " Now let me die since I have seen thee, and now may the Lord God of Israel be blessed, the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac who hath not withheld His mercy and His grace from His servant Jacob.

4 It is enough for me that I have seen thy face whilst ]l amj 3 yet alive; yea, true is the vision which I saw at Bethel.

5 Blessed be the

6 Lord my God for ever and ever, and blessed be His name." 5.

7 And Joseph and his brothers ate bread before their father and drank wine, and Jacob rejoiced with exceeding great joy because he saw Joseph eating with his brothers and drinking before him, and he blessed the Creator of all things who had preserved him, and had preserved for him his twelve sons. 6.

8 And Joseph had given to his father and to his brothers as a gift the right of dwelling in the land of Goshen and in Rameses and all the region round about, which he ruled over before Pharaoh.

9 And Israel and his sons dwelt in the land of Goshen, the best part of the land of Egypt; and Israel was one hundred and thirty years old when he came into Egypt.

10 And Joseph nourished his father and his brethren and also their possessions with bread as much as sufficed them 2 for the seven years of the famine.

11 And the land of Egypt suffered by reason of the famine, and Joseph acquired all the land of Egypt for Pharaoh in return for food, and he got possession of the people and their cattle and everything for Pharaoh.

12 And the years of the famine were accomplished, and Joseph gave to the people in the land seed and food that they might sow (the land) fn the eighth year, for the river had overflowed all the land of Egypt.

13 For in the seven years of the famine it had not overflowed and had irrigated only a few places on the banks of the river, but now it overflowed and the Egyptians sowed the land, and it bore much corn that year.

14 And this was the nrst year of the fourth week of the forty-fifth jubilee.

15 And Joseph took of the corn of the harvest the fifth part for the kirg and left four parts for them for food and for seed, and Joseph made it an ordinance for the land of Egypt until this day.

16 And Israel lived in the land of Egypt seventeen years, and all the days which he lived were three jubilees, one hundred and forty-seven years, and he died in the

17 fourth year of the fifth week of the forty-fifth jubilee.

18 And Israel blessed his sons before he died and told them everything that would befall them in the land of Egypt ; and he made known to them what would come upon them in the last days, and blessed them and gave to Joseph two portions 2 in the land.

19 And he slept with his fathers, and he was buried in the double cave in the land of Canaan, near Abraham his father in the grave which he dug for himself in the double cave in the land of Hebron.

20 And he gave all his books and the books of his fathers to Levi his son that he might preserve them and renew them for his children until this day.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. Israel went into Egypt, into the land of Goshen, on the new moon of the fourth month, in the second year of the third week of the forty-fifth jubilee. Henry notes that Joseph contrived to settle his brethren in Goshen, nearest to Canaan, where they might live by themselves and not forget the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Joseph went to meet his father Jacob in Goshen, fell on his father's neck, and wept. Henry notes that Joseph owned his brethren though he was great in Egypt, and that the meeting in Goshen was the reunion the wagons had promised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Israel said to Joseph, now let me die since I have seen thee, and blessed the Lord God of Israel, the God of Abraham and Isaac, who had not withheld mercy and grace from his servant Jacob. Henry notes that Jacob, old and not expecting to live long, wished his eyes refreshed with this sight before they closed, and then needed no more to be happy in this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Israel said it was enough to have seen Joseph's face while yet alive, and that the vision he saw at Bethel was true. The Bethel promise, that God would bring him back, is fulfilled in the son found alive in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The blessing continues: blessed be the Lord his God for ever and ever, and blessed be his name. The father's praise turns the reunion into worship, the God of Bethel blessed in Goshen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The blessing is completed: blessed be the Lord his God for ever, and blessed be his name. The double blessing seals Jacob's testimony that mercy had not been withheld through all his pilgrimage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Joseph and his brothers ate bread before their father and drank wine, and Jacob rejoiced with exceeding great joy to see Joseph eating with his brothers and drinking before him, blessing the Creator who had preserved him and preserved his twelve sons. Henry notes that after tokens of true reconciliation, sweet communion follows, and the family table in Goshen is that communion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Joseph had given his father and brothers as a gift the right of dwelling in Goshen and Rameses and all the region round about, which he ruled over before Pharaoh. Henry notes that Pharaoh offered them Goshen and that Joseph owned his brethren, settling them to their advantage and rendering good for evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Israel and his sons dwelt in Goshen, the best part of Egypt, and Israel was one hundred and thirty years old when he came into Egypt. Henry notes Jacob's answer to Pharaoh: his days were few and evil, a pilgrimage reckoned by days, though he had lived one hundred and thirty years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Joseph nourished his father, his brethren, and their possessions with bread as much as sufficed them for the seven years of the famine. Henry notes that care of Jacob and his family was especially designed by providence in Joseph's advancement, and that Joseph supplied his father with bread so that he would not die by famine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Egypt suffered by reason of the famine, and Joseph acquired all the land of Egypt for Pharaoh in return for food, getting possession of the people, their cattle, and everything for Pharaoh. Henry notes that the Egyptians parted with all their property and even liberty for the saving of their lives, and regarded Joseph as a public benefactor who had saved the kingdom from ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. When the famine years were accomplished, Joseph gave the people seed and food to sow in the eighth year, for the river had overflowed all Egypt. The return of the Nile's overflow ended the famine's grip, and the stored wisdom of the plenty years gave way to sowing again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. In the seven famine years the river had not overflowed, irrigating only a few places on its banks, but now it overflowed and the Egyptians sowed and reaped much corn that year. Henry notes Egypt's dependence on the river's overflow, and that providence dispenses its gifts in many ways, yet dependence on the First Cause remains the same. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. This was the first year of the fourth week of the forty-fifth jubilee, the year the famine broke. Jubilees dates the renewal of sowing as precisely as it dated the famine's beginning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Joseph took a fifth part of the harvest corn for the king and left four parts for the people for food and seed, and made it an ordinance for Egypt until this day. Henry notes the settlement: the Egyptians confessed Joseph had saved their lives, and the fifth part became the standing law of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Israel lived in Egypt seventeen years, and all his days were three jubilees, one hundred and forty-seven years, and he died in the fourth year of the fifth week of the forty-fifth jubilee. Henry notes that Israel, a prince with God, must die, and that his candle gradually burnt down to the socket, so that he saw the time drawing nigh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The account of Jacob's life is completed: seventeen years in Egypt, one hundred and forty-seven years in all, and death in the fourth year of the fifth week. The long pilgrimage Henry calls few and evil days ends in Egypt, awaiting burial in Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Before he died Israel blessed his sons, told them everything that would befall them in Egypt, made known what would come upon them in the last days, blessed them, and gave Joseph two portions in the land. Henry notes that Jacob blessed every son according to the blessings God intended to bestow, speaking by the Spirit of prophecy of the tribes' character and circumstances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Israel slept with his fathers and was buried in the double cave in Canaan, near Abraham his father, in the grave he dug for himself in the double cave in Hebron. Henry notes that Jacob spoke of his burial from faith in the promise that Canaan should be his seed's inheritance, and that burial in Canaan was a type of the better country he expected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
