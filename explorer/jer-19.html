The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Thus saith the LORD, Go and get a potter’s earthen bottle, and take of the ancients of the people, and of the ancients of the priests;

2 And go forth unto the valley of the son of Hinnom, which is by the entry of the east gate, and proclaim there the words that I shall tell thee,

3 And say, Hear ye the word of the LORD, O kings of Judah, and inhabitants of Jerusalem; Thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Behold, I will bring evil upon this place, the which whosoever heareth, his ears shall tingle.

4 Because they have forsaken me, and have estranged this place, and have burned incense in it unto other gods, whom neither they nor their fathers have known, nor the kings of Judah, and have filled this place with the blood of innocents;

5 They have built also the high places of Baal, to burn their sons with fire for burnt offerings unto Baal, which I commanded not, nor spake it, neither came it into my mind:

6 Therefore, behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that this place shall no more be called Tophet, nor The valley of the son of Hinnom, but The valley of slaughter.

7 And I will make void the counsel of Judah and Jerusalem in this place; and I will cause them to fall by the sword before their enemies, and by the hands of them that seek their lives: and their carcases will I give to be meat for the fowls of the heaven, and for the beasts of the earth.

8 And I will make this city desolate, and an hissing; every one that passeth thereby shall be astonished and hiss because of all the plagues thereof.

9 And I will cause them to eat the flesh of their sons and the flesh of their daughters, and they shall eat every one the flesh of his friend in the siege and straitness, wherewith their enemies, and they that seek their lives, shall straiten them.

10 Then shalt thou break the bottle in the sight of the men that go with thee,

11 And shalt say unto them, Thus saith the LORD of hosts; Even so will I break this people and this city, as one breaketh a potter’s vessel, that cannot be made whole again: and they shall bury them in Tophet, till there be no place to bury.

12 Thus will I do unto this place, saith the LORD, and to the inhabitants thereof, and even make this city as Tophet:

13 And the houses of Jerusalem, and the houses of the kings of Judah, shall be defiled as the place of Tophet, because of all the houses upon whose roofs they have burned incense unto all the host of heaven, and have poured out drink offerings unto other gods.

14 Then came Jeremiah from Tophet, whither the LORD had sent him to prophesy; and he stood in the court of the LORD’s house; and said to all the people,

15 Thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Behold, I will bring upon this city and upon all her towns all the evil that I have pronounced against it, because they have hardened their necks, that they might not hear my words.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jeremiah is sent with elders of the people and priests to the valley of the son of Hinnom carrying a potter's earthen bottle, and there he breaks it: as the bottle cannot be made whole again, so Jerusalem will be broken for its idolatry and child sacrifice in Tophet. He repeats the message in the court of the Lord's house, and the chapter ends with all the evil pronounced against the city and its towns. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Thus says the Lord: Go and get a potter's earthen bottle, and take of the ancients of the people, and of the ancients of the priests. Henry notes the prophet must give notice of ruin coming upon Judah and Jerusalem, and both rulers and ruled must attend to it: the bottle is got, and ancients of people and priests are taken as witnesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And go forth unto the valley of the son of Hinnom, which is by the entry of the east gate, and proclaim there the words that I shall tell thee. Henry's notice is proclaimed in the valley of Hinnom by the east gate: the place of the sin becomes the place of the sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And say: Hear ye the word of the Lord, O kings of Judah, and inhabitants of Jerusalem; Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel: Behold, I will bring evil upon this place, the which whosoever hears, his ears shall tingle. Henry notes that place which holiness made the joy of the whole earth, sin made the reproach and shame of the whole earth: kings and inhabitants must hear an evil that makes ears tingle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Because they have forsaken me, and have estranged this place, and have burned incense in it unto other gods, whom neither they nor their fathers have known, nor the kings of Judah, and have filled this place with the blood of innocents. Henry's cause is forsaking, estranging, incense to unknown gods, and innocent blood filling the place: the ruin's reason is set out before the ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They have built also the high places of Baal, to burn their sons with fire for burnt offerings unto Baal, which I commanded not, nor spoke it, neither came it into my mind. Henry's high places of Baal, with sons burned for offerings God never commanded, never spoke, never thought, are the sin that made the holy place a reproach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Therefore, behold, the days come, says the Lord, that this place shall no more be called Tophet, nor The valley of the son of Hinnom, but The valley of slaughter. Henry's renaming is the sentence: Tophet, the valley of Hinnom, becomes the valley of slaughter, for the place of child-sacrifice becomes the place of slaughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And I will make void the counsel of Judah and Jerusalem in this place; and I will cause them to fall by the sword before their enemies, and by the hands of them that seek their lives: and their carcases will I give to be meat for the fowls of the heaven, and for the beasts of the earth. Henry's voided counsel and sword-fall, with carcases meat for fowls and beasts, is the ruin of which the prophet must give notice: there is no fleeing from God's justice, but by fleeing to his mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And I will make this city desolate, and an hissing; every one that passes thereby shall be astonished and hiss because of all the plagues thereof. Henry's desolate city, an hissing, with every passer-by astonished and hissing at its plagues, is Jerusalem the joy of the earth become its reproach and shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And I will cause them to eat the flesh of their sons and the flesh of their daughters, and they shall eat every one the flesh of his friend in the siege and straitness, wherewith their enemies, and they that seek their lives, shall straiten them. Henry's siege straitness, eating sons, daughters, and friends' flesh, is the ruin's extremity: no fleeing from justice except by fleeing to mercy, and mercy was refused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Then shalt thou break the bottle in the sight of the men that go with thee. Henry notes the potter's vessel, after it is hardened, can never be pieced again when it is broken: the bottle broken before the witnesses' eyes is Judah broken beyond human piecing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And shalt say unto them: Thus says the Lord of hosts: Even so will I break this people and this city, as one breaks a potter's vessel, that cannot be made whole again: and they shall bury them in Tophet, till there be no place to bury. Henry notes as the bottle was broken, so shall Judah and Jerusalem be broken by the Chaldeans: no human hand can repair it, but if they return to the Lord he will heal, and Tophet shall be filled with burials till no place remains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Thus will I do unto this place, says the Lord, and to the inhabitants thereof, and even make this city as Tophet. Henry notes as they filled Tophet with the slain sacrificed to their idols, so will God fill the whole city with the slain that shall fall as sacrifices to his justice: the city itself becomes Tophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And the houses of Jerusalem, and the houses of the kings of Judah, shall be defiled as the place of Tophet, because of all the houses upon whose roofs they have burned incense unto all the host of heaven, and have poured out drink offerings unto other gods. Henry notes whatever men may think, God will appear as terrible against sin and sinners as the Scriptures state, nor shall the unbelief of men make his promise or his threatenings of no effect: houses defiled as Tophet, for rooftop incense to heaven's host and drink offerings to other gods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Then came Jeremiah from Tophet, whither the Lord had sent him to prophesy; and he stood in the court of the Lord's house, and said to all the people. Henry's prophet ratifies in the temple court what he signed in the valley: from Tophet to the Lord's house, the same word is spoken to all the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel: Behold, I will bring upon this city and upon all her towns all the evil that I have pronounced against it, because they have hardened their necks, that they might not hear my words. Henry closes that the obstinacy of sinners in sinful ways is their own fault: if they are deaf to the word of God, it is because they have stopped their ears, and hardened necks bring all the pronounced evil upon city and towns. We have need to pray that God, by his grace, would deliver us from hardness of heart, and contempt of his word and commandments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
