Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter moves from creatures, soul and angels, cattle and creeping things, to God's creative word: 'Let us make man in our image' and 'Let there be light' are spoken by his word and by his Spirit. The Son is presented as the word born from the heart, for ever and without separation, and the chapter closes with the incarnate Son and the Holy Spirit as word. Investigation (editorial — investigating, not concluding)

This section investigates a question raised by the chapter itself; it does not conclude it. The chapter quotes or echoes creation language, including “Let us make man in our image” and “Let there be light”, and reads those words as spoken by God's word and by his Spirit, with the Son as the word from the heart. Interpreted Chapter text as printed above

Parallels

The plural “Let us make” and the creative speech of Genesis stand beside the chapter's picture of a Son who is God's word and a Spirit by whom God speaks. Both present creation as happening through divine speech rather than by hands alone. Interpreted Chapter text as printed above; Genesis creation narrative as received in the canon

Differences

The Genesis passages in their own narrative context do not name Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and do not call the creative word the incarnate Son. The Trinitarian identification is the chapter's reading of those passages, not a statement the quoted verses make on their face.
