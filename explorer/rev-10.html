The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And I saw another mighty angel come down from heaven, clothed with a cloud: and a rainbow was upon his head, and his face was as it were the sun, and his feet as pillars of fire:

2 And he had in his hand a little book open: and he set his right foot upon the sea, and his left foot on the earth,

3 And cried with a loud voice, as when a lion roareth: and when he had cried, seven thunders uttered their voices.

4 And when the seven thunders had uttered their voices, I was about to write: and I heard a voice from heaven saying unto me, Seal up those things which the seven thunders uttered, and write them not.

5 And the angel which I saw stand upon the sea and upon the earth lifted up his hand to heaven,

6 And sware by him that liveth for ever and ever, who created heaven, and the things that therein are, and the earth, and the things that therein are, and the sea, and the things which are therein, that there should be time no longer:

7 But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound, the mystery of God should be finished, as he hath declared to his servants the prophets.

8 And the voice which I heard from heaven spake unto me again, and said, Go and take the little book which is open in the hand of the angel which standeth upon the sea and upon the earth.

9 And I went unto the angel, and said unto him, Give me the little book. And he said unto me, Take it, and eat it up; and it shall make thy belly bitter, but it shall be in thy mouth sweet as honey.

10 And I took the little book out of the angel’s hand, and ate it up; and it was in my mouth sweet as honey: and as soon as I had eaten it, my belly was bitter.

11 And he said unto me, Thou must prophesy again before many peoples, and nations, and tongues, and kings.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A mighty angel, clothed with cloud and rainbow, face as the sun and feet as pillars of fire, stands on sea and earth with a little book open, and when seven thunders utter, John is told to seal up what they said and write it not. The angel swears by the eternal Creator that there shall be time no longer, but that in the days of the seventh angel the mystery of God shall be finished. John is told to take the little book and eat it: sweet as honey in his mouth, bitter in his belly — pleasure in receiving God's word, then bitterness as its judgments are digested — and is commissioned to prophesy again before many peoples, nations, tongues and kings. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John saw another mighty angel come down from heaven, clothed with a cloud, a rainbow on his head, his face as the sun, and his feet as pillars of fire. Henry notes the figure seems none other than Christ: veiled in cloud, mindful of his covenant by the rainbow, bright as the sun, with ways pure and steady as pillars of fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He had in his hand a little book open, and set his right foot on the sea and his left foot on the earth. Henry reads the stance as absolute dominion over the world, and the open little book as the formerly sealed book now opened and gradually fulfilled by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He cried with a loud voice, as when a lion roars, and when he cried, seven thunders uttered their voices. Henry notes the awful voice was echoed by seven solemn and terrible ways of discovering God's mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. When the seven thunders had uttered their voices, John was about to write, but a voice from heaven said, "Seal up those things which the seven thunders uttered, and write them not." Henry notes the apostle would have published everything he saw and heard, but the time had not yet come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The angel who stood on the sea and on the earth lifted up his hand to heaven. Henry notes the manner of the oath: lifted hand, sworn by him who lives for ever, by God as God, to whom the angel as Lord, Redeemer, and ruler appeals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He swore by him who lives for ever and ever, who created heaven and its contents, earth and its contents, and the sea and its contents, that there should be time no longer. Henry gives the oath two senses: no longer delay in fulfilling the predictions once the last angel sounds, and, when the mystery is finished, time itself swallowed up in eternity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound, the mystery of God should be finished, as he declared to his servants the prophets. Henry notes this is the matter of the oath: speedy execution of everything, until the whole mystery of God's counsel is finished in the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The voice from heaven told John to go and take the little book open in the hand of the angel standing on the sea and on the earth. Henry notes the charge came from the same heavenly voice that had forbidden writing the thunders, showing John's commission was directed step by step. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. John went to the angel and said, "Give me the little book," and the angel said, "Take it, and eat it up; and it shall make thy belly bitter, but it shall be in thy mouth sweet as honey." Henry explains the charge: before publishing, John must thoroughly digest the predictions and be suitably affected by them himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. John took the little book from the angel's hand and ate it up, and it was sweet as honey in his mouth, but his belly became bitter. Henry notes the twofold relish: pleasure in receiving a word from God and foresight of the future, then bitterness as the awful persecutions and desolations were digested, as with Ezekiel's roll. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The angel said to John, "Thou must prophesy again before many peoples, and nations, and tongues, and kings." Henry notes the book was not given merely to gratify curiosity or to please or pain him, but to be communicated: his prophetical commission was renewed for an embassy to the world and its greatest men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Little book. The open book in the mighty angel's hand, which John eats: sweet as honey in the mouth, bitter in the belly. Interpreted Study notes, verses 9–10, Matthew Henry

Connections to other passages

  • Ezekiel's roll: John eating the little book, sweet then bitter, as with Ezekiel's roll (verse 10, Henry). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
