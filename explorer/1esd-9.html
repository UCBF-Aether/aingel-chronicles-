The Text — 1 Esdras 9 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Then Esdras rising from the court of the temple went to the chamber of Joanan the son of Eliasib,

2 And remained there, and did eat no meat nor drink water, mourning for the great iniquities of the multitude.

3 And there was a proclamation in all Jewry and Jerusalem to all them that were of the captivity, that they should be gathered together at Jerusalem:

4 And that whosoever met not there within two or three days according as the elders that bare rule appointed, their cattle should be seized to the use of the temple, and himself cast out from them that were of the captivity.

5 And in three days were all they of the tribe of Judah and Benjamin gathered together at Jerusalem the twentieth day of the ninth month.

6 And all the multitude sat trembling in the broad court of the temple because of the present foul weather.

7 So Esdras arose up, and said unto them, Ye have transgressed the law in marrying strange wives, thereby to increase the sins of Israel.

8 And now by confessing give glory unto the Lord God of our fathers,

9 And do his will, and separate yourselves from the heathen of the land, and from the strange women.

10 Then cried the whole multitude, and said with a loud voice, Like as thou hast spoken, so will we do.

11 But forasmuch as the people are many, and it is foul weather, so that we cannot stand without, and this is not a work of a day or two, seeing our sin in these things is spread far:

12 Therefore let the rulers of the multitude stay, and let all them of our habitations that have strange wives come at the time appointed,

13 And with them the rulers and judges of every place, till we turn away the wrath of the Lord from us for this matter.

14 Then Jonathan the son of Azael and Ezechias the son of Theocanus accordingly took this matter upon them: and Mosollam and Levis and Sabbatheus helped them.

15 And they that were of the captivity did according to all these things.

16 And Esdras the priest chose unto him the principal men of their families, all by name: and in the first day of the tenth month they sat together to examine the matter.

17 So their cause that held strange wives was brought to an end in the first day of the first month.

18 And of the priests that were come together, and had strange wives, there were found:

19 Of the sons of Jesus the son of Josedec, and his brethren; Matthelas and Eleazar, and Joribus and Joadanus.

20 And they gave their hands to put away their wives and to offer rams to make reconcilement for their errors.

21 And of the sons of Emmer; Ananias, and Zabdeus, and Eanes, and Sameius, and Hiereel, and Azarias.

22 And of the sons of Phaisur; Elionas, Massias Israel, and Nathanael, and Ocidelus and Talsas.

23 And of the Levites; Jozabad, and Semis, and Colius, who was called Calitas, and Patheus, and Judas, and Jonas.

24 Of the holy singers; Eleazurus, Bacchurus.

25 Of the porters; Sallumus, and Tolbanes.

26 Of them of Israel, of the sons of Phoros; Hiermas, and Eddias, and Melchias, and Maelus, and Eleazar, and Asibias, and Baanias.

27 Of the sons of Ela; Matthanias, Zacharias, and Hierielus, and Hieremoth, and Aedias.

28 And of the sons of Zamoth; Eliadas, Elisimus, Othonias, Jarimoth, and Sabatus, and Sardeus.

29 Of the sons of Babai; Johannes, and Ananias and Josabad, and Amatheis.

30 Of the sons of Mani; Olamus, Mamuchus, Jedeus, Jasubus, Jasael, and Hieremoth.

31 And of the sons of Addi; Naathus, and Moosias, Lacunus, and Naidus, and Mathanias, and Sesthel, Balnuus, and Manasseas.

32 And of the sons of Annas; Elionas and Aseas, and Melchias, and Sabbeus, and Simon Chosameus.

33 And of the sons of Asom; Altaneus, and Matthias, and Baanaia, Eliphalet, and Manasses, and Semei.

34 And of the sons of Maani; Jeremias, Momdis, Omaerus, Juel, Mabdai, and Pelias, and Anos, Carabasion, and Enasibus, and Mamnitanaimus, Eliasis, Bannus, Eliali, Samis, Selemias, Nathanias: and of the sons of Ozora; Sesis, Esril, Azaelus, Samatus, Zambis, Josephus.

35 And of the sons of Ethma; Mazitias, Zabadaias, Edes, Juel, Banaias.

36 All these had taken strange wives, and they put them away with their children.

37 And the priests and Levites, and they that were of Israel, dwelt in Jerusalem, and in the country, in the first day of the seventh month: so the children of Israel were in their habitations.

38 And the whole multitude came together with one accord into the broad place of the holy porch toward the east:

39 And they spake unto Esdras the priest and reader, that he would bring the law of Moses, that was given of the Lord God of Israel.

40 So Esdras the chief priest brought the law unto the whole multitude from man to woman, and to all the priests, to hear law in the first day of the seventh month.

41 And he read in the broad court before the holy porch from morning unto midday, before both men and women; and the multitude gave heed unto the law.

42 And Esdras the priest and reader of the law stood up upon a pulpit of wood, which was made for that purpose.

43 And there stood up by him Mattathias, Sammus, Ananias, Azarias, Urias, Ezecias, Balasamus, upon the right hand:

44 And upon his left hand stood Phaldaius, Misael, Melchias, Lothasubus, and Nabarias.

45 Then took Esdras the book of the law before the multitude: for he sat honourably in the first place in the sight of them all.

46 And when he opened the law, they stood all straight up. So Esdras blessed the Lord God most High, the God of hosts, Almighty.

47 And all the people answered, Amen; and lifting up their hands they fell to the ground, and worshipped the Lord.

48 Also Jesus, Anus, Sarabias, Adinus, Jacubus, Sabateas, Auteas, Maianeas, and Calitas, Asrias, and Joazabdus, and Ananias, Biatas, the Levites, taught the law of the Lord, making them withal to understand it.

49 Then spake Attharates unto Esdras the chief priest. and reader, and to the Levites that taught the multitude, even to all, saying,

50 This day is holy unto the Lord; (for they all wept when they heard the law:)

51 Go then, and eat the fat, and drink the sweet, and send part to them that have nothing;

52 For this day is holy unto the Lord: and be not sorrowful; for the Lord will bring you to honour.

53 So the Levites published all things to the people, saying, This day is holy to the Lord; be not sorrowful.

54 Then went they their way, every one to eat and drink, and make merry, and to give part to them that had nothing, and to make great cheer;

55 Because they understood the words wherein they were instructed, and for the which they had been assembled. Go back to the Bible

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Esdras rises from the temple court to the chamber of Joanan, and the princes and elders resolve in council that all who have taken foreign wives must put them away; commissioners investigate case by case for three months, and the lists of offenders are recorded. Then, on the first day of the seventh month, the people gather, Esdras reads the law, the Levites cause them to understand it, and the chapter closes with the people understanding the words wherein they were instructed. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Chamber of Joanan. the temple chamber where Esdras withdraws and the council's resolution is formed (verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Levites. who here cause the people to understand the law, by translating or explaining what Esdras read (study note on verse 55). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. JFB notes that at a private council of the princes and elders held in the chamber under Ezra's presidency, it was resolved to enter into a general covenant to put away foreign wives and children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. JFB notes the resolution was that a proclamation should be made for all who had returned from Babylon to repair within three days to Jerusalem, under pain of excommunication and confiscation of property. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. JFB notes the proclamation required all returned captives to repair within three days to Jerusalem under pain of excommunication and confiscation, so that whoever failed to meet there had his cattle seized for the temple and himself cast out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. JFB notes all the men of Judah and Benjamin gathered, the returned captives belonging chiefly to those tribes, and that the ninth month fell between the end of December and the beginning of January, the coldest and most rainy season in Palestine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. JFB notes the people sat in the court in the ninth month, the coldest and most rainy season, and Bissell notes the Hebrew assigns an additional reason for their trembling, namely the matter that had called them together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, E. C. Bissell
  • Verse 7. JFB notes Ezra fully represented the enormity of their sin and urged them to dissolve their unlawful connections, having first set forth the transgression of marrying strange wives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. JFB notes Ezra was gratified by receiving a prompt acknowledgment of the justice of his reproof and a promise of compliance with his recommendation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. JFB notes Ezra urged the people to dissolve their unlawful connections, and was gratified by their prompt acknowledgment and promise of compliance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. JFB notes the people gave a prompt acknowledgment of the justice of Ezra's reproof and promised compliance with his recommendation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. JFB notes that as the weather was ungenial and the defaulters too numerous to be passed in review at one time, it was resolved that a commission should be appointed to examine the whole matter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. JFB notes a commission was appointed to examine into the whole matter, assisted by the judges and elders of the respective cities, making a minute investigation into every case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. JFB notes the commissioners, assisted by judges and elders, made a minute investigation into every case, and after three months' labor completely removed all traces of the abuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Bissell notes Bertheau, Keil, the Speaker's Commentary, and other authorities would render the corresponding Hebrew passage as though Jonathan and Jahaziah opposed the proposal, and that it is not easy to see, if the Greek reading be followed, what office these men held, since Ezra is said in verse 16 to have chosen men for the special purpose of taking this matter upon them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing E. C. Bissell
  • Verse 15. JFB notes the commissioners made a minute investigation into every case, and after three months' labor completely removed all traces of the abuse, with adequate provision doubtless made for the repudiated wives and children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. JFB notes the commissioners, assisted by judges and elders, made a minute investigation into every case, and Bissell notes the Hebrew text gives Ezra the priest with certain chief of the fathers as separated, likely having lost the letter vav, whose absence probably led the Greek translator to introduce the change in thought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, E. C. Bissell
  • Verse 17. JFB notes the commission's minute investigation lasted three months, and Bissell notes the Hebrew differs in phrasing, the idea being that the matter was settled by the first day of the first month, as the Septuagint at Ezra 10:17 also makes clear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, E. C. Bissell
  • Verse 18. JFB notes that from the names of so many men of rank appearing in the following list, some idea may be formed of the great and complicated difficulties attending the reformatory work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. JFB notes the delinquents gave their hands, that is, came under a solemn engagement usually ratified by pledging the right hand, and sought to expiate their sin by sacrificing a ram as a trespass offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. JFB notes the commissioners' minute investigation, after three months' labor, completely removed all traces of the abuse, with adequate provision doubtless made for the repudiated wives and children according to the husbands' means. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. JFB notes the occasion was the celebration of the feast of the seventh month, the commencement of the seventh month being kept with distinguished honor as the feast of trumpets, and the first day of the seventh ecclesiastical year and new year's day of the civil year, held as a great day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. JFB notes all the people gathered themselves together as one man for the feast of the seventh month, in a spacious area before the gate, with a strong and general desire among the returned exiles to hear the word of God read to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. JFB notes the people spoke unto Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses, since the public reading of the Scriptures, required every seventh year, had fallen into neglect during the captivity until revived on this occasion, and their desire to hear it indicated a greatly improved tone of religious feeling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. JFB notes the occasion was the feast of trumpets on the first day of the seventh month, a great day, when the public reading of the law, long neglected in captivity, was revived before the whole multitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. JFB notes Ezra's duty was very laborious, as he continued reading aloud from morning until midday, lightened by the aid of other priests present, whose presence showed their cordial agreement and shared the duty of publicly reading and expounding Scripture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. JFB notes Ezra stood upon a pulpit of wood, not made in the form known to us, but only a raised scaffold or platform broad enough to allow fourteen persons to stand with ease upon it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. JFB notes the other priests present shared the important duty of publicly reading and expounding the Scripture, and their presence showed cordial agreement with Ezra's declaration of divine truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. JFB notes the priests present with Ezra shared the duty of publicly reading and expounding the Scripture, standing with him on the platform. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. JFB notes when he opened the book all the people stood up, either from respect to God's word or because the reading was prefaced by a solemn prayer concluded by a general expression of Amen, Amen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. JFB notes the reading was prefaced by a solemn prayer concluded by a general expression of Amen, Amen, with the people answering, lifting up their hands, falling to the ground, and worshipping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. JFB notes commentators are divided on the Levites' work: some think Ezra read in pure Hebrew while the Levites translated sentence by sentence into Chaldee, the vernacular of the exiles, while others maintain the Levites explained to the people, many grown very ignorant, what Ezra had read. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. JFB notes Nehemiah the governor, Ezra, and the Levites comforted the people with the declaration that this day is holy unto the Lord, so they should mourn not nor weep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. JFB notes a deep sense of national sins, impressively brought to remembrance by the reading of the law and its denunciations, affected the people with penitential sorrow, yet they were exhorted to cherish the joy and thankfulness associated with a sacred festival. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. JFB notes the people were exhorted to send portions of the feast to their poorer brethren, as in Deuteronomy 16:11 and 14 and Esther 9:19, so they too could participate in the public rejoicings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. JFB notes that notwithstanding painful remembrances of national sins awakened by the law, the people were exhorted to cherish the feelings of joy and thankfulness associated with a sacred festival, and not to be sorrowful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. JFB notes the Levites published the exhortation to the people, that the day was holy to the Lord and they should not be sorrowful, so the festival joy could replace penitential weeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. JFB notes the people, by sending portions to poorer brethren, enabled them to participate in the public rejoicings, going their way to eat, drink, make merry, and give part to those who had nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. JFB notes the Levites caused the people to understand the law and gave the sense, whether by translating into the vernacular or by explaining what Ezra had read, so that the people understood the words wherein they were instructed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
