The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 58 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Do ye indeed speak righteousness, O congregation? do ye judge uprightly, O ye sons of men?

2 Yea, in heart ye work wickedness; ye weigh the violence of your hands in the earth.

3 The wicked are estranged from the womb: they go astray as soon as they be born, speaking lies.

4 Their poison is like the poison of a serpent: they are like the deaf adder that stoppeth her ear;

5 Which will not hearken to the voice of charmers, charming never so wisely.

6 Break their teeth, O God, in their mouth: break out the great teeth of the young lions, O LORD.

7 Let them melt away as waters which run continually: when he bendeth his bow to shoot his arrows, let them be as cut in pieces.

8 As a snail which melteth, let every one of them pass away: like the untimely birth of a woman, that they may not see the sun.

9 Before your pots can feel the thorns, he shall take them away as with a whirlwind, both living, and in his wrath.

10 The righteous shall rejoice when he seeth the vengeance: he shall wash his feet in the blood of the wicked.

11 So that a man shall say, Verily there is a reward for the righteous: verily he is a God that judgeth in the earth.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Judges judged: those who should speak righteousness work wickedness in heart, estranged from the womb, serpent poison, deaf adders stopping the ear. Break their teeth, melt them like waters and snails, whirlwind before thorns heat the pots, so that men shall say: verily there is a reward for the righteous, a God who judges in the earth. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Do you indeed speak righteousness, O congregation? Do you judge uprightly, O sons of men? The judges themselves are questioned — do they speak righteousness or judge uprightly at all? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Yea, in heart you work wickedness; you weigh the violence of your hands in the earth: in heart they work wickedness, weighing out violence with their hands as in scales — injustice is deliberate and measured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The wicked are estranged from the womb; they go astray as soon as they are born, speaking lies: estrangement from the womb, straying from birth, speaking lies — wickedness is early and native. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Their poison is like the poison of a serpent; they are like the deaf adder that stops her ear: serpent poison and the deaf adder's stopped ear — venomous and wilfully unhearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Which will not hearken to the voice of charmers, charming never so wisely: even the wisest charming cannot make the adder hear — the wicked are beyond persuasion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Break their teeth, O God, in their mouth; break out the great teeth of the young lions, O Lord: teeth broken in the mouth, great teeth of young lions broken out — disarm the biters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Let them melt away as waters which run continually; when he bends his bow to shoot his arrows, let them be as cut in pieces: melting waters and arrows cut in pieces — the wicked dissolve and their weapons fail. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. As a snail which melts, let every one of them pass away; like the untimely birth of a woman, that they may not see the sun: snail melting and untimely birth never seeing the sun — vanishing without ever truly living. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Before your pots can feel the thorns, he shall take them away as with a whirlwind, both living, and in his wrath: before thorns heat the pots, the whirlwind takes them, living, in wrath — judgment swift and sudden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The righteous shall rejoice when he sees the vengeance; he shall wash his feet in the blood of the wicked: rejoicing at vengeance seen, feet washed in the wicked's blood — severe language for just retribution celebrated, not for private revenge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. So that a man shall say, Verily there is a reward for the righteous; verily he is a God who judges in the earth: the conclusion drawn by onlookers — reward for the righteous, and a God who judges in the earth, here and now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
