The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 57 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Be merciful unto me, O God, be merciful unto me: for my soul trusteth in thee: yea, in the shadow of thy wings will I make my refuge, until these calamities be overpast.

2 I will cry unto God most high; unto God that performeth all things for me.

3 He shall send from heaven, and save me from the reproach of him that would swallow me up. Selah. God shall send forth his mercy and his truth.

4 My soul is among lions: and I lie even among them that are set on fire, even the sons of men, whose teeth are spears and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword.

5 Be thou exalted, O God, above the heavens; let thy glory be above all the earth.

6 They have prepared a net for my steps; my soul is bowed down: they have digged a pit before me, into the midst whereof they are fallen themselves. Selah.

7 My heart is fixed, O God, my heart is fixed: I will sing and give praise.

8 Awake up, my glory; awake, psaltery and harp: I myself will awake early.

9 I will praise thee, O Lord, among the people: I will sing unto thee among the nations.

10 For thy mercy is great unto the heavens, and thy truth unto the clouds.

11 Be thou exalted, O God, above the heavens: let thy glory be above all the earth.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Refuge under wings until calamities pass: the soul among lions whose teeth are spears, nets and pits prepared, yet the refrain lifts it, be exalted above the heavens, glory above all the earth. The heart fixed, psaltery and harp awake early, praise among peoples and nations, for mercy to the heavens and truth to the clouds. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Heading note: the text panel prints the verses only, without a heading. The title setting in verse 1, the cave, fleeing Saul, comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. Authorship and date remain Unresolved (see Trace). Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Be merciful to me, O God, be merciful to me, for my soul trusts in you; yea, in the shadow of your wings will I make my refuge, until these calamities be overpast: mercy twice asked, trust as its ground, and wings' shadow as refuge until calamities pass — the title sets this in the cave, fleeing Saul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. I will cry to God Most High, to God who performs all things for me: crying to the Most High, who performs all things for his servant — performance, not mere promise, is God's character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He shall send from heaven and save me from the reproach of him who would swallow me up; God shall send forth his mercy and his truth: heaven-sent salvation from swallowing reproach, by mercy and truth sent forth together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. My soul is among lions, and I lie even among them set on fire, even the sons of men, whose teeth are spears and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword: lions, fire-set men, teeth as spears and arrows, tongue as sword — the cave's danger is human and bestial at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Be exalted, O God, above the heavens; let your glory be above all the earth: the refrain lifts the prayer above the cave — exaltation above heavens, glory above earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They have prepared a net for my steps; my soul is bowed down; they have dug a pit before me, into the midst whereof they are fallen themselves: net for steps, bowed soul, dug pit — and the diggers fallen into their own pit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. My heart is fixed, O God, my heart is fixed; I will sing and give praise: a fixed heart, twice stated, issues in singing and praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Awake up, my glory; awake, psaltery and harp; I myself will awake early: glory, psaltery, and harp are bidden awake, and he himself will awake early — praise rises before dawn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. I will praise you, O Lord, among the people; I will sing to you among the nations: praise among peoples and song among nations — the cave's song goes worldwide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For your mercy is great unto the heavens, and your truth unto the clouds: mercy to the heavens, truth to the clouds — the measures of praise match God's attributes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Be exalted, O God, above the heavens; let your glory be above all the earth: the refrain closes the psalm as it broke its middle — exaltation and glory above all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
