The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 38 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he made the altar of burnt offering of shittim wood: five cubits was the length thereof, and five cubits the breadth thereof; it was foursquare; and three cubits the height thereof.

2 And he made the horns thereof on the four corners of it; the horns thereof were of the same: and he overlaid it with brass.

3 And he made all the vessels of the altar, the pots, and the shovels, and the basons, and the fleshhooks, and the firepans: all the vessels thereof made he of brass.

4 And he made for the altar a brasen grate of network under the compass thereof beneath unto the midst of it.

5 And he cast four rings for the four ends of the grate of brass, to be places for the staves.

6 And he made the staves of shittim wood, and overlaid them with brass.

7 And he put the staves into the rings on the sides of the altar, to bear it withal; he made the altar hollow with boards.

8 And he made the laver of brass, and the foot of it of brass, of the lookingglasses of the women assembling, which assembled at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

9 And he made the court: on the south side southward the hangings of the court were of fine twined linen, an hundred cubits:

10 Their pillars were twenty, and their brasen sockets twenty; the hooks of the pillars and their fillets were of silver.

11 And for the north side the hangings were an hundred cubits, their pillars were twenty, and their sockets of brass twenty; the hooks of the pillars and their fillets of silver.

12 And for the west side were hangings of fifty cubits, their pillars ten, and their sockets ten; the hooks of the pillars and their fillets of silver.

13 And for the east side eastward fifty cubits.

14 The hangings of the one side of the gate were fifteen cubits; their pillars three, and their sockets three.

15 And for the other side of the court gate, on this hand and that hand, were hangings of fifteen cubits; their pillars three, and their sockets three.

16 All the hangings of the court round about were of fine twined linen.

17 And the sockets for the pillars were of brass; the hooks of the pillars and their fillets of silver; and the overlaying of their chapiters of silver; and all the pillars of the court were filleted with silver.

18 And the hanging for the gate of the court was needlework, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen: and twenty cubits was the length, and the height in the breadth was five cubits, answerable to the hangings of the court.

19 And their pillars were four, and their sockets of brass four; their hooks of silver, and the overlaying of their chapiters and their fillets of silver.

20 And all the pins of the tabernacle, and of the court round about, were of brass.

21 This is the sum of the tabernacle, even of the tabernacle of testimony, as it was counted, according to the commandment of Moses, for the service of the Levites, by the hand of Ithamar, son to Aaron the priest.

22 And Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah, made all that the LORD commanded Moses.

23 And with him was Aholiab, son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan, an engraver, and a cunning workman, and an embroiderer in blue, and in purple, and in scarlet, and fine linen.

24 All the gold that was occupied for the work in all the work of the holy place, even the gold of the offering, was twenty and nine talents, and seven hundred and thirty shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary.

25 And the silver of them that were numbered of the congregation was an hundred talents, and a thousand seven hundred and threescore and fifteen shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary:

26 A bekah for every man, that is, half a shekel, after the shekel of the sanctuary, for every one that went to be numbered, from twenty years old and upward, for six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty men.

27 And of the hundred talents of silver were cast the sockets of the sanctuary, and the sockets of the vail; an hundred sockets of the hundred talents, a talent for a socket.

28 And of the thousand seven hundred seventy and five shekels he made hooks for the pillars, and overlaid their chapiters, and filleted them.

29 And the brass of the offering was seventy talents, and two thousand and four hundred shekels.

30 And therewith he made the sockets to the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and the brasen altar, and the brasen grate for it, and all the vessels of the altar,

31 And the sockets of the court round about, and the sockets of the court gate, and all the pins of the tabernacle, and all the pins of the court round about.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Bezaleel makes the altar of burnt offering and the bronze laver from the women's looking-glasses, sets up the court with its hangings and pillars, and the chapter closes with the accounting: the gold, silver, and brass given, and the silver counted by the census, a bekah for every man numbered. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And he made the altar of burnt offering of shittim wood, five cubits long, five cubits broad, foursquare, and three cubits high. Henry notes while looking at the altar of burnt-offering, let us see Jesus, for in him, his righteousness, and salvation, is a full and sufficient offering for sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And he made the horns thereof on the four corners of it, the horns thereof were of the same, and he overlaid it with brass. Henry observes the horns were of the altar itself, overlaid with brass like the whole, the places of refuge at the four corners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And he made all the vessels of the altar, the pots, and the shovels, and the basons, and the fleshhooks, and the firepans, all the vessels thereof made he of brass. Henry notes every vessel of the altar's service was brass, fitted to fire, blood, and ash. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And he made for the altar a brasen grate of network under the compass thereof beneath unto the midst of it. Henry observes the grate of network, hung hollow, let the ashes fall through, that the fire might be kept in the midst. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And he cast four rings for the four ends of the grate of brass, to be places for the staves. Henry notes the four rings at the grate's ends received the staves, that the altar might be borne by its grate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And he made the staves of shittim wood, and overlaid them with brass. Henry observes the staves were wood overlaid with brass, matching the altar, that the place of sacrifice might travel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And he put the staves into the rings on the sides of the altar, to bear it withal, and he made the altar hollow with boards. Henry notes the altar was hollow with boards, as shown on the mount, for the fire's altar was a hollow frame, not a solid mass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And he made the laver of brass, and the foot of it of brass, of the lookingglasses of the women assembling, which assembled at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation. Henry notes in all ages some persons are more devoted to God and more willing to part even with lawful things for his sake than others, and some women, devoted to God and zealous for tabernacle worship, expressed zeal by parting with their mirrors, which were polished plates of brass, and in the laver of regeneration, by the Holy Spirit, let our souls be washed and they shall be clean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And he made the court, and on the south side southward the hangings of the court were of fine twined linen, a hundred cubits. Henry notes the walls of the court, being of curtains only, intimated that the state of the Jewish church itself was movable and changeable, and in due time to be taken down and folded up, when the tent should be enlarged and its cords lengthened to make room for the Gentile world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Their pillars were twenty, and their brasen sockets twenty, and the hooks of the pillars and their fillets were of silver. Henry observes the south pillars were twenty in brass sockets, with silver hooks and fillets, the court's humbler metal below and silver above. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And for the north side the hangings were a hundred cubits, their pillars were twenty, and their sockets of brass twenty, and the hooks of the pillars and their fillets of silver. Henry notes the north side matched the south, a hundred cubits on twenty pillars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And for the west side were hangings of fifty cubits, their pillars ten, and their sockets ten, and the hooks of the pillars and their fillets of silver. Henry observes the west end was fifty cubits on ten pillars, closing the court behind the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And for the east side eastward fifty cubits. Henry notes the east side, the front, was also fifty cubits, but broken by the gate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The hangings of the one side of the gate were fifteen cubits, their pillars three, and their sockets three. Henry observes the gate's one side was fifteen cubits on three pillars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And for the other side of the court gate, on this hand and that hand, were hangings of fifteen cubits, their pillars three, and their sockets three. Henry notes the other side matched, fifteen cubits on three pillars, so the gate stood centred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. All the hangings of the court round about were of fine twined linen. Henry observes the whole court's hangings were fine twined linen, one material round about, for the enclosure was one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And the sockets for the pillars were of brass, and the hooks of the pillars and their fillets of silver, and the overlaying of their chapiters of silver, and all the pillars of the court were filleted with silver. Henry notes the brass sockets below and silver above ran round the whole court, binding the pillars in one enclosure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And the hanging for the gate of the court was needlework, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, and twenty cubits was the length, and the height in the breadth was five cubits, answerable to the hangings of the court. Henry observes the gate hanging was needlework in the tabernacle's colours, twenty cubits long and five high, matching the court's hangings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And their pillars were four, and their sockets of brass four, and their hooks of silver, and the overlaying of their chapiters and their fillets of silver. Henry notes the gate stood on four pillars in four brass sockets, with silver hooks and fillets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And all the pins of the tabernacle, and of the court round about, were of brass. Henry observes every pin, of house and court alike, was brass, for the outer work was all of the fire's metal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. This is the sum of the tabernacle, even of the tabernacle of testimony, as it was counted, according to the commandment of Moses, for the service of the Levites, by the hand of Ithamar, son to Aaron the priest. Henry notes the sum was counted by command and by hand: Ithamar counted it for the Levites' service, that the holy things might be accounted for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah, made all that the LORD commanded Moses. Henry observes Bezaleel of Judah made all that the LORD commanded, the master-workman completing the whole commission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And with him was Aholiab, son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan, an engraver, and a cunning workman, and an embroiderer in blue, and in purple, in scarlet, and fine linen. Henry notes Aholiab of Dan stood with him, engraver, cunning workman, and embroiderer, for the house required every craft. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. All the gold that was occupied for the work in all the work of the holy place, even the gold of the offering, was twenty and nine talents, and seven hundred and thirty shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary. Henry notes the gold of the offering was counted: twenty-nine talents and seven hundred and thirty shekels, the offering of a willing people weighed and recorded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And the silver of them that were numbered of the congregation was a hundred talents, and a thousand seven hundred and threescore and fifteen shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary. Henry observes the silver came from the numbering: a hundred talents and seventeen hundred and seventy-five shekels, the ransom money of the counted congregation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. A bekah for every man, that is, half a shekel, after the shekel of the sanctuary, for every one that went to be numbered, from twenty years old and upward, for six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty men. Henry notes the bekah, half a shekel each, from 603,550 men, showed the foundation of massy pieces of silver, the solidity and purity of the truth upon which the church is founded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And of the hundred talents of silver were cast the sockets of the sanctuary, and the sockets of the vail, a hundred sockets of the hundred talents, a talent for a socket. Henry observes the hundred talents became a hundred sockets, a talent each, so the house stood on the ransom silver of the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And of the thousand seven hundred seventy and five shekels he made hooks for the pillars, and overlaid their chapiters, and filleted them. Henry notes the remaining seventeen hundred and seventy-five shekels became hooks, chapiters, and fillets, so no silver was left unaccounted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And the brass of the offering was seventy talents, and two thousand and four hundred shekels. Henry observes the brass of the offering was seventy talents and twenty-four hundred shekels, the people's third metal, weighed like the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And therewith he made the sockets to the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and the brasen altar, and the brasen grate for it, and all the vessels of the altar. Henry notes the brass became the door sockets, the altar, the grate, and all the altar's vessels, the outer service in the outer metal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And the sockets of the court round about, and the sockets of the court gate, and all the pins of the tabernacle, and all the pins of the court round about. Henry closes on the accounting: as the people offered willingly, so may our souls be made willing, and let us be ready to part with anything, and count all but loss to win Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
