The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when Saul’s son heard that Abner was dead in Hebron, his hands were feeble, and all the Israelites were troubled.

2 And Saul’s son had two men that were captains of bands: the name of the one was Baanah, and the name of the other Rechab, the sons of Rimmon a Beerothite, of the children of Benjamin: (for Beeroth also was reckoned to Benjamin.

3 And the Beerothites fled to Gittaim, and were sojourners there until this day.)

4 And Jonathan, Saul’s son, had a son that was lame of his feet. He was five years old when the tidings came of Saul and Jonathan out of Jezreel, and his nurse took him up, and fled: and it came to pass, as she made haste to flee, that he fell, and became lame. And his name was Mephibosheth.

5 And the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, Rechab and Baanah, went, and came about the heat of the day to the house of Ishbosheth, who lay on a bed at noon.

6 And they came thither into the midst of the house, as though they would have fetched wheat; and they smote him under the fifth rib: and Rechab and Baanah his brother escaped.

7 For when they came into the house, he lay on his bed in his bedchamber, and they smote him, and slew him, and beheaded him, and took his head, and gat them away through the plain all night.

8 And they brought the head of Ishbosheth unto David to Hebron, and said to the king, Behold the head of Ishbosheth the son of Saul thine enemy, which sought thy life; and the LORD hath avenged my lord the king this day of Saul, and of his seed.

9 And David answered Rechab and Baanah his brother, the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, and said unto them, As the LORD liveth, who hath redeemed my soul out of all adversity,

10 When one told me, saying, Behold, Saul is dead, thinking to have brought good tidings, I took hold of him, and slew him in Ziklag, who thought that I would have given him a reward for his tidings:

11 How much more, when wicked men have slain a righteous person in his own house upon his bed? shall I not therefore now require his blood of your hand, and take you away from the earth?

12 And David commanded his young men, and they slew them, and cut off their hands and their feet, and hanged them up over the pool in Hebron. But they took the head of Ishbosheth, and buried it in the sepulchre of Abner in Hebron.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

With Abner dead, Ish-bosheth's nerve breaks, and two of his own captains, Rechab and Baanah, murder him in his bed and carry his head to David expecting reward. David instead executes them for murdering an innocent man, and buries Ish-bosheth's head honourably at Hebron. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When Saul's son hears Abner is dead in Hebron, his hands grow feeble and all Israel is troubled. Henry's lesson is that difficulties which should sharpen our endeavours, when they dispirit us instead, betray both heavenly crowns and earthly lives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Saul's son has two captains of bands, Baanah and Rechab, sons of Rimmon the Beerothite of Benjamin. The men positioned to guard a king are the very men who will betray him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The Beerothites had fled to Gittaim and remained sojourners there. The aside explains the family's displaced standing, a clan already uprooted before its sons turn to murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Jonathan's son Mephibosheth, lame in his feet since a fall at five years old when his nurse fled with the tidings from Jezreel, is introduced here. His helplessness stands in contrast to the strong men who plot around the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Rechab and Baanah come at midday heat to Ishbosheth's house while he lies on his bed at noon. Henry warns against loving sleep lest we come to poverty and ruin, for the idle soul is an easy prey to the destroyer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. They enter as though to fetch wheat and smite him under the fifth rib, then escape. Murder is disguised as an ordinary errand, and no hour is safe: we know not when or from what hand the death-blow may come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. They find him on his bed in his bedchamber, slay and behead him, and carry his head away through the plain all night. The grisly trophy is meant to purchase favour with David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. They present Ishbosheth's head to David, claiming the Lord has avenged his lord on Saul's house. A man may be glad to obtain his just wishes, Henry notes, and yet truly regret the means by which he receives them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. David answers by the Lord who redeemed his soul out of all adversity. He will not be beholden to anyone who helps him by unlawful practices, trusting God to crown and complete his own work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. David recalls the man who brought tidings of Saul's death expecting a reward, and whom he slew at Ziklag. The precedent is set: David does not pay for blood, even the blood of his enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. David asks how much more these wicked men, who slew a righteous person in his own house upon his bed, deserve to have their blood required. They shed innocent blood from the basest motives, and David justly executes vengeance upon them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. David's young men slay them, cut off their hands and feet, and hang them over the pool in Hebron, while Ishbosheth's head is buried in Abner's sepulchre. The public punishment answers the public crime, and the murdered king receives an honourable burial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
