The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto all the congregation of the children of Israel, and say unto them, Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy.

3 Ye shall fear every man his mother, and his father, and keep my sabbaths: I am the LORD your God.

4 Turn ye not unto idols, nor make to yourselves molten gods: I am the LORD your God.

5 And if ye offer a sacrifice of peace offerings unto the LORD, ye shall offer it at your own will.

6 It shall be eaten the same day ye offer it, and on the morrow: and if ought remain until the third day, it shall be burnt in the fire.

7 And if it be eaten at all on the third day, it is abominable; it shall not be accepted.

8 Therefore every one that eateth it shall bear his iniquity, because he hath profaned the hallowed thing of the LORD: and that soul shall be cut off from among his people.

9 And when ye reap the harvest of your land, thou shalt not wholly reap the corners of thy field, neither shalt thou gather the gleanings of thy harvest.

10 And thou shalt not glean thy vineyard, neither shalt thou gather every grape of thy vineyard; thou shalt leave them for the poor and stranger: I am the LORD your God.

11 Ye shall not steal, neither deal falsely, neither lie one to another.

12 And ye shall not swear by my name falsely, neither shalt thou profane the name of thy God: I am the LORD.

13 Thou shalt not defraud thy neighbour, neither rob him: the wages of him that is hired shall not abide with thee all night until the morning.

14 Thou shalt not curse the deaf, nor put a stumblingblock before the blind, but shalt fear thy God: I am the LORD.

15 Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honor the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour.

16 Thou shalt not go up and down as a talebearer among thy people: neither shalt thou stand against the blood of thy neighbour; I am the LORD.

17 Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him.

18 Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the LORD.

19 Ye shall keep my statutes. Thou shalt not let thy cattle gender with a diverse kind: thou shalt not sow thy field with mingled seed: neither shall a garment mingled of linen and woollen come upon thee.

20 And whosoever lieth carnally with a woman, that is a bondmaid, betrothed to an husband, and not at all redeemed, nor freedom given her; she shall be scourged; they shall not be put to death, because she was not free.

21 And he shall bring his trespass offering unto the LORD, unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, even a ram for a trespass offering.

22 And the priest shall make an atonement for him with the ram of the trespass offering before the LORD for his sin which he hath done: and the sin which he hath done shall be forgiven him.

23 And when ye shall come into the land, and shall have planted all manner of trees for food, then ye shall count the fruit thereof as uncircumcised: three years shall it be as uncircumcised unto you: it shall not be eaten of.

24 But in the fourth year all the fruit thereof shall be holy to praise the LORD withal.

25 And in the fifth year shall ye eat of the fruit thereof, that it may yield unto you the increase thereof: I am the LORD your God.

26 Ye shall not eat any thing with the blood: neither shall ye use enchantment, nor observe times.

27 Ye shall not round the corners of your heads, neither shalt thou mar the corners of thy beard.

28 Ye shall not make any cuttings in your flesh for the dead, nor print any marks upon you: I am the LORD.

29 Do not prostitute thy daughter, to cause her to be a whore; lest the land fall to whoredom, and the land become full of wickedness.

30 Ye shall keep my sabbaths, and reverence my sanctuary: I am the LORD.

31 Regard not them that have familiar spirits, neither seek after wizards, to be defiled by them: I am the LORD your God.

32 Thou shalt rise up before the hoary head, and honour the face of the old man, and fear thy God: I am the LORD.

33 And if a stranger sojourn with thee in your land, ye shall not vex him.

34 But the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself; for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God.

35 Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment, in meteyard, in weight, or in measure.

36 Just balances, just weights, a just ephah, and a just hin, shall ye have: I am the LORD your God, which brought you out of the land of Egypt.

37 Therefore shall ye observe all my statutes, and all my judgments, and do them: I am the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gathers laws for the whole congregation under the heading, be holy, for the LORD is holy. It joins worship duties, honouring parents and keeping the sabbaths, and refusing idols, with everyday duties: eating peace offerings within their appointed days, leaving field corners and gleanings for the poor and the stranger, not stealing, lying, defrauding, or holding back a hired man's wages, judging impartially, not hating a brother or bearing a grudge but loving a neighbour as oneself, and keeping honest weights and measures. It also forbids mixtures, idolatrous hair and cuttings, consulting familiar spirits, and vexing the stranger, who is to be loved as one born among them. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Gleanings. What is left after reaping: the corners of the field and the fallen grapes, which must be left for the poor and the stranger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD speaks to Moses, rehearsing sundry laws of general application to the whole congregation, not to particular classes alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. All the congregation shall be holy, for the LORD their God is holy, separated from the world, since His character, laws, and service are holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Every man shall fear his mother and father and keep the LORD's sabbaths, obedience to parents and Sabbath observance lying together at the foundation of practical religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They shall not turn to idols nor make molten gods, for the LORD is their God, the first religious duty after holiness, parents, and Sabbath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. A peace offering shall be offered at his own will, thank and vow offerings being always freewill offerings, eaten by offerer and friends except the wave and heave portions for priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. It shall be eaten the same day and on the next day, a freewill offering being allowed a second day, unlike a thank offering confined to its day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. If it is eaten at all on the third day, it is abomination and shall not be accepted, the strict prohibition preventing superstitious virtue being attached to altar meat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Everyone who eats it bears iniquity, because he has profaned the LORD's hallowed thing, and that soul shall be cut off, deep criminality attaching to third-day eating. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. When reaping, they shall not wholly reap the field corners nor gather gleanings, the poor's right to glean and to unreaped corners being secured by positive statute, the earliest poor law in any people's code. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They shall not glean the vineyard nor gather every grape, but leave them for poor and stranger, combining public duty with private benevolence at a time when rich hearts would be inclined to liberality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They shall not steal, deal falsely, or lie to one another, social duties inculcated against petty frauds and violations of truth in business. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They shall not swear falsely by the LORD's name nor profane God's name, truth in speech being part of the same social honesty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They shall not defraud or rob a neighbour, nor let a hired man's wages remain all night until morning, exploitation of a neighbour being forbidden alongside theft. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. They shall not curse the deaf nor put a stumbling block before the blind, but fear God, humanity forbidding ridicule of bodily infirmities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They shall do no unrighteousness in judgment, not respecting the poor nor honouring the mighty, but judging a neighbour in righteousness, impartiality being required in law as in dealings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. They shall not go up and down as a talebearer among the people, nor stand against the blood of a neighbour, circulating stories to others' prejudice being a little-thought-of vice opposed by brotherly kindness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. They shall not hate a brother in heart, but in any wise rebuke a neighbour and not suffer sin upon him, remonstrating calmly and kindly instead of cherishing malice or revenge, lest they participate in his sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They shall not avenge or bear grudge against their people, but love a neighbour as themselves, neighbour meaning fellow creature, a narrow national interpretation refuted by the parable of the Good Samaritan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. They shall not let cattle gender with a diverse kind, sow mingled seed, or wear linen-woollen mingled garments, prohibitions aimed at unnatural lusts and Zabian magical sowing, and at mixtures injurious to crops and, in hot climates, to the body through electricity passing off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. When they plant fruit trees, they shall count the fruit uncircumcised for three years and not eat it, gardeners teaching that trees thrive better and bear more abundantly afterwards if blossoms are pinched off in early years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. In the fourth year all the fruit shall be holy to praise the LORD, the first allowed crop being devoted as a holy thing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. In the fifth year they shall eat its fruit, that it may yield its increase, the delayed enjoyment following the years of restraint and dedication. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. They shall not eat anything with the blood, as in chapter 17, nor use enchantment nor observe times, divination by serpents and cloud-observation being forbidden as implying want of faith in God's providence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. They shall not round the corners of their heads nor mar the corners of their beard, an Egyptian idolatrous coiffure, cropped in a circle with a square beard, being forbidden to separate Israel from neighbouring idolaters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. They shall not make cuttings in their flesh for the dead nor print marks upon themselves, heathen bereavement gashes and tattooed idol marks being signs of apostasy and obstacles to return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. A father shall not profane his daughter to cause her to be a whore, lest the land fall to whoredom and be full of wickedness, the same separation from heathen practice that forbids pagan hair, cuttings, and necromancy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. They shall keep the LORD's sabbaths and reverence His sanctuary, a precept frequently repeated with prohibitions of idolatrous practice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. They shall not regard familiar spirits nor seek wizards to be defiled by them, ventriloquists pretending communication with the invisible world and palmistry fortune-tellers being derogatory to God's honour and subversive of covenant relations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. They shall rise up before the hoary head and honour the face of the old man and fear God, the humanity and brotherly kindness enforced in the neighbour duties extending to the aged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. If a stranger sojourns with them, they shall not vex him, but encourage strangers to settle that they may come to know and worship the true God, treating them as friends, not aliens. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. The stranger dwelling with them shall be as one born among them, and they shall love him as themselves, for they were strangers in Egypt, their own kindly reception there grounding the duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. They shall do no unrighteousness in judgment, in meteyard, in weight, or in measure, the honesty required in judgment and dealings extending to measures kept to tabernacle standards. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. Just balances, just weights, a just ephah, and a just hin shall they have, for the LORD is their God who brought them out of Egypt, false balances being worse than open robbery, robbing under colour of equity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Therefore they shall observe all His statutes and judgments and do them, for He is the LORD, Creator, Deliverer, and Sovereign, with wisdom to establish laws and power to punish their violation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
