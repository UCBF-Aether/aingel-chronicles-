The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Song of Songs 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 O that thou wert as my brother, that sucked the breasts of my mother! when I should find thee without, I would kiss thee; yea, I should not be despised.

2 I would lead thee, and bring thee into my mother’s house, who would instruct me: I would cause thee to drink of spiced wine of the juice of my pomegranate.

3 His left hand should be under my head, and his right hand should embrace me.

4 I charge you, O daughters of Jerusalem, that ye stir not up, nor awake my love, until he please.

5 Who is this that cometh up from the wilderness, leaning upon her beloved? I raised thee up under the apple tree: there thy mother brought thee forth: there she brought thee forth that bare thee.

6 Set me as a seal upon thine heart, as a seal upon thine arm: for love is strong as death; jealousy is cruel as the grave: the coals thereof are coals of fire, which hath a most vehement flame.

7 Many waters cannot quench love, neither can the floods drown it: if a man would give all the substance of his house for love, it would utterly be contemned.

8 We have a little sister, and she hath no breasts: what shall we do for our sister in the day when she shall be spoken for?

9 If she be a wall, we will build upon her a palace of silver: and if she be a door, we will inclose her with boards of cedar.

10 I am a wall, and my breasts like towers: then was I in his eyes as one that found favour.

11 Solomon had a vineyard at Baalhamon; he let out the vineyard unto keepers; every one for the fruit thereof was to bring a thousand pieces of silver.

12 My vineyard, which is mine, is before me: thou, O Solomon, must have a thousand, and those that keep the fruit thereof two hundred.

13 Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the companions hearken to thy voice: cause me to hear it.

14 Make haste, my beloved, and be thou like to a roe or to a young hart upon the mountains of spices.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The book's climax states love's strength: set me as a seal on heart and arm, for love is strong as death, and many waters cannot quench it, nor can all a household's wealth buy it. A little sister, vineyards at Baalhamon, and the lovers' own vineyard frame the closing exchange, ending with the call for the beloved to make haste like a roe on the mountains of spices. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. O that thou wert as my brother, that sucked the breasts of my mother! When I should find thee without, I would kiss thee, yea, I should not be despised. The church wishes for the constant intimacy and freedom with the Lord Jesus that a sister has with a brother, Henry says, for Christ is become as our Brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. I would lead thee and bring thee into my mother's house, who would instruct me; I would cause thee to drink of spiced wine of the juice of my pomegranate. Wherever we find him, Henry says, let us be ready to own our relation to him and affection for him, and not fear being despised for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. His left hand should be under my head, and his right hand should embrace me. Is there in us an ardent wish to serve Christ more and better, Henry asks? What then have we laid up in store to show our affection to the Beloved of our souls, and what fruit unto holiness? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. I charge you, O daughters of Jerusalem, that ye stir not up nor awake my love until he please. The church charges all her children that they never provoke Christ to withdraw, Henry notes, and we should reason with ourselves when tempted to do what would grieve the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Who is this that comes up from the wilderness, leaning upon her beloved? I raised thee up under the apple tree; there thy mother brought thee forth. The Jewish church came up from the wilderness supported by divine power, Henry says, and believers by grace are brought up from the wilderness, a sinful state with no true comfort; there is no coming out but leaning on Christ as our Beloved by faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Set me as a seal upon thine heart, as a seal upon thine arm, for love is strong as death, jealousy cruel as the grave, the coals thereof coals of fire with a most vehement flame. The words entreat an abiding place in his love and protection by his power, Henry explains: let me always have a place in thine heart, and an impression of love upon it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Many waters cannot quench love, neither can the floods drown it; if a man would give all the substance of his house for love, it would utterly be contemned. No waters can quench Christ's love to us, Henry says, nor any floods drown it, and love of Christ will enable us to repel temptations from the smiles of the world as well as its frowns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. We have a little sister, and she has no breasts; what shall we do for our sister in the day when she shall be spoken for? The church pleads for the Gentiles, Henry explains, who then had not the word of God nor the means of grace, and those brought to Christ themselves should contrive what they may do to help others to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. If she be a wall, we will build upon her a palace of silver; and if she be a door, we will enclose her with boards of cedar. If the beginning of this work were likened to a wall built upon Christ the Foundation, Henry says, then the Gentile church would become as a palace for the great King, built of solid silver. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. I am a wall, and my breasts like towers; then was I in his eyes as one that found favour. She shall be carefully and effectually protected, Henry notes, enclosed so as to receive no damage, and Christ says he will do all that is necessary for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Solomon had a vineyard at Baalhamon; he let out the vineyard unto keepers; every one for the fruit thereof was to bring a thousand pieces of silver. Our hearts are our vineyards, Henry says, which we must keep with all diligence, and to Christ and his praise all our fruits must be dedicated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. My vineyard, which is mine, is before me; thou, O Solomon, must have a thousand, and those that keep the fruit thereof two hundred. See with what satisfaction we should look back upon the times when we were in his eyes as those that find favour, Henry says, and all that work for Christ work for themselves and shall be unspeakable gainers by it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the companions hearken to thy voice; cause me to hear it. These verses close the conference between Christ and his church, Henry says: he addresses her as dwelling in the gardens, the assemblies and ordinances of his saints, and exhorts her to be constant and frequent in prayers, supplications, and praises, in which he delights. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Make haste, my beloved, and be thou like to a roe or to a young hart upon the mountains of spices. She replies craving his speedy return to take her wholly with him, Henry says; the heavens must contain Christ till the times come when every eye shall see him, and true believers, looking for that day, hasten to it. Even so, come, Lord Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
