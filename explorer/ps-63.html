The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 63 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 O God, thou art my God; early will I seek thee: my soul thirsteth for thee, my flesh longeth for thee in a dry and thirsty land, where no water is;

2 To see thy power and thy glory, so as I have seen thee in the sanctuary.

3 Because thy lovingkindness is better than life, my lips shall praise thee.

4 Thus will I bless thee while I live: I will lift up my hands in thy name.

5 My soul shall be satisfied as with marrow and fatness; and my mouth shall praise thee with joyful lips:

6 When I remember thee upon my bed, and meditate on thee in the night watches.

7 Because thou hast been my help, therefore in the shadow of thy wings will I rejoice.

8 My soul followeth hard after thee: thy right hand upholdeth me.

9 But those that seek my soul, to destroy it, shall go into the lower parts of the earth.

10 They shall fall by the sword: they shall be a portion for foxes.

11 But the king shall rejoice in God; every one that sweareth by him shall glory: but the mouth of them that speak lies shall be stopped.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Thirst in a dry land: early seeking, soul thirsting and flesh longing, to see power and glory as once seen in the sanctuary. Lovingkindness is better than life, lips praising, soul satisfied as with marrow, night-watch meditation, following hard after God upheld by his right hand, while soul-seekers fall by the sword, the king rejoices in God, and lying mouths are stopped. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. O God, you are my God; early will I seek you; my soul thirsts for you, my flesh longs for you in a dry and thirsty land, where no water is: early seeking, thirsting soul, longing flesh, dry land without water — the title sets this in the wilderness of Judah, and thirst is the psalm's image. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. To see your power and your glory, so as I have seen you in the sanctuary: seeing power and glory as once seen in the sanctuary — wilderness worship longs for sanctuary sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Because your lovingkindness is better than life, my lips shall praise you: lovingkindness better than life itself is the ground of lips' praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Thus will I bless you while I live; I will lift up my hands in your name: blessing while living, hands lifted in God's name — praise is bodily and lifelong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. My soul shall be satisfied as with marrow and fatness, and my mouth shall praise you with joyful lips: marrow-and-fatness satisfaction, joyful lips' praise — spiritual feasting satisfies as rich food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. When I remember you upon my bed, and meditate on you in the night watches: bed remembrance and night-watch meditation — praise continues through the night. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Because you have been my help, therefore in the shadow of your wings will I rejoice: past help grounds present rejoicing under wings' shadow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. My soul follows hard after you; your right hand upholds me: hard following after God, upheld by his right hand — pursuit and upholding meet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. But those who seek my soul, to destroy it, shall go into the lower parts of the earth: soul-seekers to destroy shall go down to earth's lower parts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They shall fall by the sword; they shall be a portion for foxes: falling by the sword, portion for foxes — the destroyers become scavengers' prey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. But the king shall rejoice in God; every one who swears by him shall glory, but the mouth of those who speak lies shall be stopped: the king's rejoicing in God, swearers' glory, liars' stopped mouths — truth's triumph closes the wilderness psalm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
