The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 24 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now upon the first day of the week, very early in the morning, they came unto the sepulchre, bringing the spices which they had prepared, and certain others with them.

2 And they found the stone rolled away from the sepulchre.

3 And they entered in, and found not the body of the Lord Jesus.

4 And it came to pass, as they were much perplexed thereabout, behold, two men stood by them in shining garments:

5 And as they were afraid, and bowed down their faces to the earth, they said unto them, Why seek ye the living among the dead?

6 He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee,

7 Saying, The Son of man must be delivered into the hands of sinful men, and be crucified, and the third day rise again.

8 And they remembered his words,

9 And returned from the sepulchre, and told all these things unto the eleven, and to all the rest.

10 It was Mary Magdalene and Joanna, and Mary the mother of James, and other women that were with them, which told these things unto the apostles.

11 And their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not.

12 Then arose Peter, and ran unto the sepulchre; and stooping down, he beheld the linen clothes laid by themselves, and departed, wondering in himself at that which was come to pass.

13 And, behold, two of them went that same day to a village called Emmaus, which was from Jerusalem about threescore furlongs.

14 And they talked together of all these things which had happened.

15 And it came to pass, that, while they communed together and reasoned, Jesus himself drew near, and went with them.

16 But their eyes were holden that they should not know him.

17 And he said unto them, What manner of communications are these that ye have one to another, as ye walk, and are sad?

18 And the one of them, whose name was Cleopas, answering said unto him, Art thou only a stranger in Jerusalem, and hast not known the things which are come to pass there in these days?

19 And he said unto them, What things? And they said unto him, Concerning Jesus of Nazareth, which was a prophet mighty in deed and word before God and all the people:

20 And how the chief priests and our rulers delivered him to be condemned to death, and have crucified him.

21 But we trusted that it had been he which should have redeemed Israel: and beside all this, to day is the third day since these things were done.

22 Yea, and certain women also of our company made us astonished, which were early at the sepulchre;

23 And when they found not his body, they came, saying, that they had also seen a vision of angels, which said that he was alive.

24 And certain of them which were with us went to the sepulchre, and found it even so as the women had said: but him they saw not.

25 Then he said unto them, O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken:

26 Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory?

27 And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.

28 And they drew nigh unto the village, whither they went: and he made as though he would have gone further.

29 But they constrained him, saying, Abide with us: for it is toward evening, and the day is far spent. And he went in to tarry with them.

30 And it came to pass, as he sat at meat with them, he took bread, and blessed it, and brake, and gave to them.

31 And their eyes were opened, and they knew him; and he vanished out of their sight.

32 And they said one to another, Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?

33 And they rose up the same hour, and returned to Jerusalem, and found the eleven gathered together, and them that were with them,

34 Saying, The Lord is risen indeed, and hath appeared to Simon.

35 And they told what things were done in the way, and how he was known of them in breaking of bread.

36 And as they thus spake, Jesus himself stood in the midst of them, and saith unto them, Peace be unto you.

37 But they were terrified and affrighted, and supposed that they had seen a spirit.

38 And he said unto them, Why are ye troubled? and why do thoughts arise in your hearts?

39 Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.

40 And when he had thus spoken, he shewed them his hands and his feet.

41 And while they yet believed not for joy, and wondered, he said unto them, Have ye here any meat?

42 And they gave him a piece of a broiled fish, and of an honeycomb.

43 And he took it, and did eat before them.

44 And he said unto them, These are the words which I spake unto you, while I was yet with you, that all things must be fulfilled, which were written in the law of Moses, and in the prophets, and in the psalms, concerning me.

45 Then opened he their understanding, that they might understand the scriptures,

46 And said unto them, Thus it is written, and thus it behoved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead the third day:

47 And that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.

48 And ye are witnesses of these things.

49 And, behold, I send the promise of my Father upon you: but tarry ye in the city of Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high.

50 And he led them out as far as to Bethany, and he lifted up his hands, and blessed them.

51 And it came to pass, while he blessed them, he was parted from them, and carried up into heaven.

52 And they worshipped him, and returned to Jerusalem with great joy:

53 And were continually in the temple, praising and blessing God. Amen.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Very early on the first day of the week the women find the stone rolled away and hear from two shining men that he is risen, but their words seem idle tales to the apostles until Peter runs to the empty tomb (vv. 1–12). Two disciples on the road to Emmaus walk with a stranger who opens the Scriptures, and recognize him only in the breaking of bread (vv. 13–35). In Jerusalem he stands among them, eats broiled fish, opens their understanding to the law, the prophets, and the psalms concerning himself, promises the Father’s power from on high, and is carried up into heaven at Bethany as they worship and return with great joy (vv. 36–53). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 24

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Now upon the first day of the week, very early in the morning, they came unto the sepulchre, bringing the spices which they had prepared, and certain others with them. Henry notes the affection and respect the women showed to Christ after he was dead and buried, coming very early with prepared spices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. They found the stone rolled away from the sepulchre. The stone they expected to move is already rolled away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They entered in, and found not the body of the Lord Jesus. Entering, they find absence, not the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. As they were much perplexed thereabout, behold, two men stood by them in shining garments. Perplexity meets two men in shining garments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. As they were afraid, and bowed down their faces to the earth, they said unto them, Why seek ye the living among the dead? Henry notes Christians often perplex themselves about what should comfort them, looking rather to find their Master in grave-clothes than angels in shining garments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spoke unto you when he was yet in Galilee. The angels bring no new gospel, Henry notes, but remind the women of Christ's words and teach them to apply them; he is risen by his own power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Saying, The Son of man must be delivered into the hands of sinful men, and be crucified, and the third day rise again. The remembered words name delivery, crucifixion, and third-day rising as musts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And they remembered his words. Remembering is the turn from perplexity to report. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And returned from the sepulchre, and told all these things unto the eleven, and to all the rest. Return becomes report to the eleven and all the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. It was Mary Magdalene and Joanna, and Mary the mother of James, and other women with them, which told these things unto the apostles. The witnesses are named: Magdalene, Joanna, Mary mother of James, and others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not. The apostles hear resurrection as idle tales and do not believe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Then arose Peter, and ran unto the sepulchre, and stooping down, he beheld the linen clothes laid by themselves, and departed, wondering in himself at that which was come to pass. Henry notes Peter, who so lately ran from his Master, now ran to the sepulchre, and was amazed; many puzzling things would be plain if we rightly understood Christ's words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Behold, two of them went that same day to a village called Emmaus, which was from Jerusalem about threescore furlongs. The same day, two disciples walk to Emmaus, threescore furlongs from Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. They talked together of all these things which had happened. Talking together of death and resurrection is their employment on the road. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. While they communed together and reasoned, Jesus himself drew near, and went with them. Henry notes it well becomes disciples to talk of his death and resurrection, improving one another's knowledge and stirring devout affections, and where but two are well employed thus, he will come and make a third. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. But their eyes were holden that they should not know him. Eyes holden, not absent, keep recognition back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And he said unto them, What manner of communications are these that ye have one to another, as ye walk, and are sad? The stranger's question names their sadness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The one of them, whose name was Cleopas, answering said, Art thou only a stranger in Jerusalem, and hast not known the things which are come to pass there in these days? Henry notes those are strangers in Jerusalem who know not of Jesus' death and sufferings, and Cleopas treats the stranger as the only ignorant one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And he said, What things? And they said, Concerning Jesus of Nazareth, which was a prophet mighty in deed and word before God and all the people. The summary names a prophet mighty in deed and word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And how the chief priests and our rulers delivered him to be condemned to death, and have crucified him. Delivery, condemnation, and crucifixion by rulers complete the summary's tragedy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. But we trusted that it had been he which should have redeemed Israel, and beside all this, today is the third day since these things were done. Trust in past tense and the third day: hope deferred by the calendar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Yea, and certain women also of our company made us astonished, which were early at the sepulchre. The women's early visit astonished the company, without convincing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. When they found not his body, they came, saying, that they had also seen a vision of angels, which said that he was alive. Empty tomb plus angels' vision, reported secondhand, still leaves him unseen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Certain of them which were with us went to the sepulchre, and found it even so as the women had said, but him they saw not. The men's visit confirms the women's words and repeats the absence: him they saw not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Then he said unto them, O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken. Henry notes the Lord reproved them for weakness of faith in Old Testament Scriptures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory? Suffering as the appointed way to glory is the question that reframes everything. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself. Henry notes Christ, the best expositor, led them not by new notions but by showing Scripture fulfilled; a golden thread of gospel grace runs through the whole Old Testament web, and we cannot go far in any part without meeting something concerning Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. They drew nigh unto the village, whither they went, and he made as though he would have gone further. The stranger makes as though to go further, testing whether they will constrain him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. But they constrained him, saying, Abide with us, for it is toward evening, and the day is far spent. And he went in to tarry with them. Henry notes if we would have Christ dwell with us, we must be earnest with him, and those who have tasted communion cannot but desire more of his company. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. As he sat at meat with them, he took bread, and blessed it, and brake, and gave to them. The familiar manner — took, blessed, broke, gave — precedes recognition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Their eyes were opened, and they knew him, and he vanished out of their sight. Henry notes Christ by Spirit and grace makes himself known, opening Scriptures and meeting at table, known in breaking of bread, but the work is completed by opening eyes of mind, and our views here are short, for in heaven we shall see him for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And they said one to another, Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures? Henry notes they had found the preaching powerful even when they knew not the preacher, and Scriptures speaking of Christ will warm true disciples' hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. They rose up the same hour, and returned to Jerusalem, and found the eleven gathered together, and them that were with them. The same hour's return reverses the day's walk from Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Saying, The Lord is risen indeed, and has appeared to Simon. The gathered company already confesses: risen indeed, appeared to Simon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And they told what things were done in the way, and how he was known of them in breaking of bread. Henry notes it is the duty of those to whom he has shown himself to let others know what he has done, and of great use to compare experiences and tell them to each other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. As they thus spoke, Jesus himself stood in the midst of them, and says unto them, Peace be unto you. Henry notes Jesus appeared miraculously, assuring disciples of his peace, though they had so lately forsaken him, promising spiritual peace with every blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. But they were terrified and affrighted, and supposed that they had seen a spirit. Presence produces terror: they suppose a spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. And he said, Why are ye troubled? And why do thoughts arise in your hearts? Henry notes many troublesome thoughts disquieting minds rise from mistakes concerning Christ, and all thoughts rising in hearts are known to the Lord and displeasing to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see, for a spirit has not flesh and bones, as ye see me have. Hands, feet, handling, seeing: flesh and bones answer the spirit theory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. When he had thus spoken, he showed them his hands and his feet. Showing follows saying, wounds as evidence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. While they yet believed not for joy, and wondered, he said, Have ye here any meat? Unbelief for joy is answered with a request for food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. They gave him a piece of a broiled fish, and of a honeycomb. Fish and honeycomb are given, ordinary food for an extraordinary proof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. He took it, and did eat before them. Eating before them completes the bodily proof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. And he said, These are the words which I spoke unto you, while I was yet with you, that all things must be fulfilled, which were written in the law of Moses, and in the prophets, and in the psalms, concerning me. Henry notes nothing had passed but what was foretold by prophets and necessary for sinners' salvation, and even good men need understandings opened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Then opened he their understanding, that they might understand the scriptures. Opening understanding, not merely opening the book, is the gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. And said, Thus it is written, and thus it behoved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead the third day. Written necessity joins suffering and third-day rising. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. And that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem. Henry notes all men should be taught repentance's nature and necessity, in order to forgiveness, sought by faith in Jesus' name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. And ye are witnesses of these things. The opened, fed, and taught disciples are now witnesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. And behold, I send the promise of my Father upon you, but tarry ye in the city of Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high. Tarrying in Jerusalem precedes power from on high. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. He led them out as far as to Bethany, and he lifted up his hands, and blessed them. Henry notes Christ ascended from Bethany, near Olivet, where his sufferings began; those who would go to heaven must ascend from the house of sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. While he blessed them, he was parted from them, and carried up into heaven. Blessing and parting coincide: he goes away in blessing, not displeasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. They worshipped him, and returned to Jerusalem with great joy. Henry notes this fresh display of glory drew fresh acknowledgments, and they returned with great joy, for Christ's glory is the joy of true believers even here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. And were continually in the temple, praising and blessing God. Amen. Henry closes: while waiting for God's promises, we must meet them with praises, for nothing better prepares the mind for the Holy Ghost; fears silenced, sorrows sweetened, hopes kept up, and the Father's throne is the throne of grace because it is also our Mediator's throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Investigation (editorial — a parallel examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a possible parallel. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

Resurrection morning and evening — Luke 24, Mark 16, and John 20

Luke 24:1–53, Mark 16:1–14, and John 20:1–29 all begin with the empty tomb on the first day and end with appearances that the first hearers struggle to believe. The differences are extensive: Luke names Mary Magdalene, Joanna, and Mary the mother of James, adds the Emmaus road and a Jerusalem appearance in which Jesus eats broiled fish, and closes with the ascension at Bethany; Mark’s first report ends in fearful silence, with later appearances summarized; John follows Mary Magdalene alone, the race of Peter and the beloved disciple, the garden meeting, and Thomas’s confession a week later. The sources consulted for this page do not reconcile these accounts into one timeline, and this page does not attempt to.
