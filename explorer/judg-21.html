The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 21 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the men of Israel had sworn in Mizpeh, saying, There shall not any of us give his daughter unto Benjamin to wife.

2 And the people came to the house of God, and abode there till even before God, and lifted up their voices, and wept sore;

3 And said, O LORD God of Israel, why is this come to pass in Israel, that there should be to day one tribe lacking in Israel?

4 And it came to pass on the morrow, that the people rose early, and built there an altar, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings.

5 And the children of Israel said, Who is there among all the tribes of Israel that came not up with the congregation unto the LORD? For they had made a great oath concerning him that came not up to the LORD to Mizpeh, saying, He shall surely be put to death.

6 And the children of Israel repented them for Benjamin their brother, and said, There is one tribe cut off from Israel this day.

7 How shall we do for wives for them that remain, seeing we have sworn by the LORD that we will not give them of our daughters to wives?

8 And they said, What one is there of the tribes of Israel that came not up to Mizpeh to the LORD? And, behold, there came none to the camp from Jabeshgilead to the assembly.

9 For the people were numbered, and, behold, there were none of the inhabitants of Jabeshgilead there.

10 And the congregation sent thither twelve thousand men of the valiantest, and commanded them, saying, Go and smite the inhabitants of Jabeshgilead with the edge of the sword, with the women and the children.

11 And this is the thing that ye shall do, Ye shall utterly destroy every male, and every woman that hath lain by man.

12 And they found among the inhabitants of Jabeshgilead four hundred young virgins, that had known no man by lying with any male: and they brought them unto the camp to Shiloh, which is in the land of Canaan.

13 And the whole congregation sent some to speak to the children of Benjamin that were in the rock Rimmon, and to call peaceably unto them.

14 And Benjamin came again at that time; and they gave them wives which they had saved alive of the women of Jabeshgilead: and yet so they sufficed them not.

15 And the people repented them for Benjamin, because that the LORD had made a breach in the tribes of Israel.

16 Then the elders of the congregation said, How shall we do for wives for them that remain, seeing the women are destroyed out of Benjamin?

17 And they said, There must be an inheritance for them that be escaped of Benjamin, that a tribe be not destroyed out of Israel.

18 Howbeit we may not give them wives of our daughters: for the children of Israel have sworn, saying, Cursed be he that giveth a wife to Benjamin.

19 Then they said, Behold, there is a feast of the LORD in Shiloh yearly in a place which is on the north side of Bethel, on the east side of the highway that goeth up from Bethel to Shechem, and on the south of Lebonah.

20 Therefore they commanded the children of Benjamin, saying, Go and lie in wait in the vineyards;

21 And see, and, behold, if the daughters of Shiloh come out to dance in dances, then come ye out of the vineyards, and catch you every man his wife of the daughters of Shiloh, and go to the land of Benjamin.

22 And it shall be, when their fathers or their brethren come unto us to complain, that we will say unto them, Be favourable unto them for our sakes: because we reserved not to each man his wife in the war: for ye did not give unto them at this time, that ye should be guilty.

23 And the children of Benjamin did so, and took them wives, according to their number, of them that danced, whom they caught: and they went and returned unto their inheritance, and repaired the cities, and dwelt in them.

24 And the children of Israel departed thence at that time, every man to his tribe and to his family, and they went out from thence every man to his inheritance.

25 In those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Having sworn at Mizpeh that none will give his daughter to Benjamin, Israel weeps at the house of God because one tribe is lacking, then compounds one rash oath with another: Jabesh-gilead, absent from the assembly, is smitten under the ban, and its four hundred young virgins are given to Benjamin, but they do not suffice for six hundred men. The elders then direct the remnant to lie in wait in Shiloh's vineyards at the yearly feast and catch wives from among the dancing daughters, answering fathers and brothers that the givers have not broken the oath since they did not give. Benjamin rebuilds and dwells in its inheritance, and the book closes with its refrain: in those days there was no king in Israel, every man did what was right in his own eyes. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The men of Israel had sworn in Mizpeh that none of them would give his daughter to Benjamin to wife, an oath whose purport, JFB notes, was to treat the Gibeah perpetrators as the doomed Canaanites, entered into with the same inconsiderate haste as the rest of the affair. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The people came to the house of God, abode till even, lifted up their voices, and wept sore, and JFB notes their characteristic fickleness: scarcely cooled from sanguinary vengeance, they rushed to the opposite extreme of self-accusation and grief, in a public service of expiation that vented emotion without relieving the fetter of their vow and anathema. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They asked, O Lord God of Israel, why has this come to pass in Israel, that today one tribe should be lacking, turning victory into lamentation over the gap it had made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. On the morrow the people rose early, built an altar there, and offered burnt and peace offerings, formalising their grief in sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. They asked who among all the tribes had not come up to the assembly, for a great oath had been made that whoever came not to Mizpeh should surely be put to death, so that a second oath now compounded the first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Israel repented for Benjamin their brother, saying one tribe is cut off from Israel this day, and JFB explains cut off as in danger of extinction, since all Benjamin's women and children had been massacred and only six hundred men survived, a blank in the twelve tribes too painful to contemplate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They asked how to do for wives for those who remained, seeing they had sworn by the Lord not to give them their daughters, the oath now standing between the remnant and its survival. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. When the people were numbered, no one from Jabesh-gilead had come to the camp, and JFB notes Jabesh lay in eastern Manasseh about fifteen miles east of Jordan, and the Mizpeh ban seemed to impose punishing its inhabitants, so that to repair one rash proceeding they rushed to another, smaller tragedy, with the additional object of supplying wives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The people were numbered, and behold there were none of Jabesh-gilead's inhabitants among them, confirming the city's absence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The congregation sent twelve thousand valiant men, commanding them to smite Jabesh-gilead with the sword, with women and children, the ban executed as a crusade. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They were commanded to utterly destroy every male and every woman that had lain by man, and JFB notes this shows the intemperate fury of the Israelites in the indiscriminate slaughter of women and children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Among Jabesh's inhabitants they found four hundred young virgins who had known no man, and brought them to the camp at Shiloh in Canaan, the spared remnant that was to become Benjamin's wives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The whole congregation sent to speak peaceably to Benjamin's children at the rock Rimmon and call them, ending the remnant's four months in the caves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Benjamin came again, and they gave them the women saved alive of Jabesh-gilead, but they sufficed not for them, since four hundred wives could not match six hundred men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The people repented for Benjamin, because the Lord had made a breach in Israel's tribes, and the breach itself was now acknowledged as the Lord's doing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The elders asked how to do for wives for those who remained, since Benjamin's women were destroyed, and JFB notes the carefully spared young women of Jabesh had proved inadequate, so another expedient must be found. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. They answered that there must be an inheritance for those escaped of Benjamin, that a tribe be not destroyed out of Israel, and JFB notes that as the only rightful owners of the territory, provision must be made to transmit it to legitimate heirs, though a new act of violence was meditated to do it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They said they could not give them wives of their daughters, for Israel had sworn, Cursed be he that giveth a wife to Benjamin, so that the oath barred the honest remedy and forced a dishonest one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. But, they said, there is a yearly feast of the Lord in Shiloh, north of Bethel, on the east side of the highway from Bethel to Shechem, and south of Lebonah, and JFB notes the exact site was described evidently for the Benjamites' direction, while the feast, generally supposed to be tabernacles, was Shiloh's most social and hilarious festival. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. They commanded Benjamin to go and lie in wait in the vineyards, stationing the remnant for the seizure the elders had planned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. When Shiloh's daughters came out to dance in dances, each Benjamite was to catch himself a wife and go to Benjamin's land, and JFB notes dancing was anciently part of religious observance, done in the open air with women by themselves, and their light, buoyant spirits, apprehensive of no danger, facilitated a scheme closely resembling the Sabine rape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. When the daughters' fathers or brethren complained, the elders would say, Be favourable unto them, since they had not reserved wives in the war, and the givers had not given, so they would not be guilty, and JFB notes the elders' public sanction of this violence was new evidence of the evils into which precipitancy had involved them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Benjamin did so, taking wives according to their number from among the dancers they caught, and went and returned to their inheritance, repaired the cities, and dwelt in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Then all Israel departed, every man to his tribe and family, to his inheritance, and the great assembly dissolved into its separate tribes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. In those days there was no king in Israel, and every man did that which was right in his own eyes, and the book's closing sentence stands as the explanation of all the disorder, violence, and idolatry that fills its appendix. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
