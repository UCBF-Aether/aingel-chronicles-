The Text — Nehemiah 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now those that sealed were, Nehemiah, the Tirshatha, the son of Hachaliah, and Zidkijah,

2 Seraiah, Azariah, Jeremiah,

3 Pashur, Amariah, Malchijah,

4 Hattush, Shebaniah, Malluch,

5 Harim, Meremoth, Obadiah,

6 Daniel, Ginnethon, Baruch,

7 Meshullam, Abijah, Mijamin,

8 Maaziah, Bilgai, Shemaiah: these were the priests.

9 And the Levites: both Jeshua the son of Azaniah, Binnui of the sons of Henadad, Kadmiel;

10 And their brethren, Shebaniah, Hodijah, Kelita, Pelaiah, Hanan,

11 Micha, Rehob, Hashabiah,

12 Zaccur, Sherebiah, Shebaniah,

13 Hodijah, Bani, Beninu.

14 The chief of the people; Parosh, Pahathmoab, Elam, Zatthu, Bani,

15 Bunni, Azgad, Bebai,

16 Adonijah, Bigvai, Adin,

17 Ater, Hizkijah, Azzur,

18 Hodijah, Hashum, Bezai,

19 Hariph, Anathoth, Nebai,

20 Magpiash, Meshullam, Hezir,

21 Meshezabeel, Zadok, Jaddua,

22 Pelatiah, Hanan, Anaiah,

23 Hoshea, Hananiah, Hashub,

24 Hallohesh, Pileha, Shobek,

25 Rehum, Hashabnah, Maaseiah,

26 And Ahijah, Hanan, Anan,

27 Malluch, Harim, Baanah.

28 And the rest of the people, the priests, the Levites, the porters, the singers, the Nethinims, and all they that had separated themselves from the people of the lands unto the law of God, their wives, their sons, and their daughters, every one having knowledge, and having understanding;

29 They clave to their brethren, their nobles, and entered into a curse, and into an oath, to walk in God’s law, which was given by Moses the servant of God, and to observe and do all the commandments of the LORD our Lord, and his judgments and his statutes;

30 And that we would not give our daughters unto the people of the land, not take their daughters for our sons:

31 And if the people of the land bring ware or any victuals on the sabbath day to sell, that we would not buy it of them on the sabbath, or on the holy day: and that we would leave the seventh year, and the exaction of every debt.

32 Also we made ordinances for us, to charge ourselves yearly with the third part of a shekel for the service of the house of our God;

33 For the shewbread, and for the continual meat offering, and for the continual burnt offering, of the sabbaths, of the new moons, for the set feasts, and for the holy things, and for the sin offerings to make an atonement for Israel, and for all the work of the house of our God.

34 And we cast the lots among the priests, the Levites, and the people, for the wood offering, to bring it into the house of our God, after the houses of our fathers, at times appointed year by year, to burn upon the altar of the LORD our God, as it is written in the law:

35 And to bring the firstfruits of our ground, and the firstfruits of all fruit of all trees, year by year, unto the house of the LORD:

36 Also the firstborn of our sons, and of our cattle, as it is written in the law, and the firstlings of our herds and of our flocks, to bring to the house of our God, unto the priests that minister in the house of our God:

37 And that we should bring the firstfruits of our dough, and our offerings, and the fruit of all manner of trees, of wine and of oil, unto the priests, to the chambers of the house of our God; and the tithes of our ground unto the Levites, that the same Levites might have the tithes in all the cities of our tillage.

38 And the priest the son of Aaron shall be with the Levites, when the Levites take tithes: and the Levites shall bring up the tithe of the tithes unto the house of our God, to the chambers, into the treasure house.

39 For the children of Israel and the children of Levi shall bring the offering of the corn, of the new wine, and the oil, unto the chambers, where are the vessels of the sanctuary, and the priests that minister, and the porters, and the singers: and we will not forsake the house of our God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

This chapter names those who seal the covenant, Nehemiah the Tirshatha first, then priests, Levites, and chiefs of the people, and then records what they bind themselves to: no marriages with the peoples of the land, no buying on the sabbath or holy day, a yearly third of a shekel for the house of God, wood offerings by lot, firstfruits and tithes brought to the chambers, and the closing resolve: 'we will not forsake the house of our God.' Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Tirshatha. the Persian governor's title borne by Nehemiah, who signs the covenant first (verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Firstfruits and tithes. the first produce and the tenth part, brought to the temple chambers for the priests, Levites, porters, and singers (verses 35-39). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Those who sealed were Nehemiah the Tirshatha son of Hachaliah, and Zidkijah. Henry notes Nehemiah signed first to show his forwardness and set a good example, since those above others in dignity and power should go before them in the way of God; the sealers included twenty-two priests, seventeen Levites, and forty-four chiefs of the people as representatives of the congregation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The rest of the people joined: priests, Levites, porters, singers, Nethinims, and all who had separated themselves from the peoples of the lands to the law of God, with their wives, sons, and daughters, everyone having knowledge and understanding. Henry notes that as soon as young people can distinguish good and evil they ought to make joining the Lord their own act, and that conversion is separation from the world's course to the conduct of God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. They clave to their brethren, their nobles, and entered into a curse and an oath to walk in God's law given by Moses, observing all the commandments, judgments, and statutes. Henry notes the nobles are called brethren, for in God's things rich and poor meet together, and that every oath carries a conditional curse, making it a strong bond on the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. They covenanted not to give their daughters to the peoples of the land nor take their daughters for their sons. Henry notes this article struck at their besetting sin, and that in covenants with God we should engage particularly against the sins by which we have been most frequently overtaken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. If the peoples of the land brought ware or victuals on the sabbath to sell, they would not buy it on the sabbath or a holy day, and they would leave the seventh year and the exaction of every debt. Henry notes they would not even encourage heathen selling by buying victuals, but buy provisions the day before, and that the seventh-year release corrected the fault of chapter 5. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. They made an ordinance to charge themselves yearly with the third part of a shekel for the service of the house of God. Henry notes the Tirshatha did not impose this tax; the people charged themselves with it, and when everyone gives even a little toward a good work, the whole amount becomes considerable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. The shekel funded the showbread, the continual meat and burnt offerings, the sabbaths, new moons, set feasts, holy things, and sin offerings to make atonement, with all the work of God's house. Henry notes the constant charge: showbread, two lambs daily, more for sabbaths and festivals, with offerings for them all, formerly supplied from temple treasures now gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. They cast lots among priests, Levites, and people for the wood offering, to bring it by their fathers' houses at appointed times year by year to burn on the altar. Henry notes the lots ordered the supply so there would be no scarcity at one time and overplus at another, keeping the altar fire always burning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. They covenanted to bring the firstfruits of their ground and of all fruit of trees, year by year, to the house of the LORD. Henry places this among the dues they resolved to pay justly, since the priests could not afford the temple's provision from their small maintenance alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. They would also bring the firstborn of their sons and cattle, and the firstlings of herds and flocks, to the priests ministering in God's house, as written in the law. Henry cites the law's provision for redemption money for sons and the giving of firstborn cattle to the priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. They would bring the firstfruits of their dough, their offerings, and the fruit of trees, wine, and oil to the priests in the chambers, and the tithes of their ground to the Levites, who would take tithes in all the cities of their tillage. Henry notes they refused to make Persian taxes an excuse for withholding God's dues, rendering to God what was his as well as to Caesar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. The priest, son of Aaron, would be with the Levites when they took tithes, and the Levites would bring the tithe of the tithes to the house of God, to the chambers of the treasure house. Henry notes this was the law of Numbers 18, withheld before to their shame, now resolved so that there might be meat in God's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. The children of Israel and Levi would bring the offering of corn, new wine, and oil to the chambers where the sanctuary vessels, ministering priests, porters, and singers were, resolving not to forsake the house of God. Henry closes with the general resolution: they would never forsake God's house for another god, for high places, or for farms and merchandise, for those who forsake God's worship forsake God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
