The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Matthew 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 At that time Jesus went on the sabbath day through the corn; and his disciples were an hungred, and began to pluck the ears of corn and to eat.

2 But when the Pharisees saw it, they said unto him, Behold, thy disciples do that which is not lawful to do upon the sabbath day.

3 But he said unto them, Have ye not read what David did, when he was an hungred, and they that were with him;

4 How he entered into the house of God, and did eat the shewbread, which was not lawful for him to eat, neither for them which were with him, but only for the priests?

5 Or have ye not read in the law, how that on the sabbath days the priests in the temple profane the sabbath, and are blameless?

6 But I say unto you, That in this place is one greater than the temple.

7 But if ye had known what this meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice, ye would not have condemned the guiltless.

8 For the Son of man is Lord even of the sabbath day.

9 And when he was departed thence, he went into their synagogue:

10 And, behold, there was a man which had his hand withered. And they asked him, saying, Is it lawful to heal on the sabbath days? that they might accuse him.

11 And he said unto them, What man shall there be among you, that shall have one sheep, and if it fall into a pit on the sabbath day, will he not lay hold on it, and lift it out?

12 How much then is a man better than a sheep? Wherefore it is lawful to do well on the sabbath days.

13 Then saith he to the man, Stretch forth thine hand. And he stretched it forth; and it was restored whole, like as the other.

14 Then the Pharisees went out, and held a council against him, how they might destroy him.

15 But when Jesus knew it, he withdrew himself from thence: and great multitudes followed him, and he healed them all;

16 And charged them that they should not make him known:

17 That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet, saying,

18 Behold my servant, whom I have chosen; my beloved, in whom my soul is well pleased: I will put my spirit upon him, and he shall shew judgment to the Gentiles.

19 He shall not strive, nor cry; neither shall any man hear his voice in the streets.

20 A bruised reed shall he not break, and smoking flax shall he not quench, till he send forth judgment unto victory.

21 And in his name shall the Gentiles trust.

22 Then was brought unto him one possessed with a devil, blind, and dumb: and he healed him, insomuch that the blind and dumb both spake and saw.

23 And all the people were amazed, and said, Is not this the son of David?

24 But when the Pharisees heard it, they said, This fellow doth not cast out devils, but by Beelzebub the prince of the devils.

25 And Jesus knew their thoughts, and said unto them, Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation; and every city or house divided against itself shall not stand:

26 And if Satan cast out Satan, he is divided against himself; how shall then his kingdom stand?

27 And if I by Beelzebub cast out devils, by whom do your children cast them out? therefore they shall be your judges.

28 But if I cast out devils by the Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God is come unto you.

29 Or else how can one enter into a strong man’s house, and spoil his goods, except he first bind the strong man? and then he will spoil his house.

30 He that is not with me is against me; and he that gathereth not with me scattereth abroad.

31 Wherefore I say unto you, All manner of sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven unto men: but the blasphemy against the Holy Ghost shall not be forgiven unto men.

32 And whosoever speaketh a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him: but whosoever speaketh against the Holy Ghost, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, neither in the world to come.

33 Either make the tree good, and his fruit good; or else make the tree corrupt, and his fruit corrupt: for the tree is known by his fruit.

34 O generation of vipers, how can ye, being evil, speak good things? for out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.

35 A good man out of the good treasure of the heart bringeth forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things.

36 But I say unto you, That every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment.

37 For by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned.

38 Then certain of the scribes and of the Pharisees answered, saying, Master, we would see a sign from thee.

39 But he answered and said unto them, An evil and adulterous generation seeketh after a sign; and there shall no sign be given to it, but the sign of the prophet Jonas:

40 For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale’s belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.

41 The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here.

42 The queen of the south shall rise up in the judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: for she came from the uttermost parts of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and, behold, a greater than Solomon is here.

43 When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through dry places, seeking rest, and findeth none.

44 Then he saith, I will return into my house from whence I came out; and when he is come, he findeth it empty, swept, and garnished.

45 Then goeth he, and taketh with himself seven other spirits more wicked than himself, and they enter in and dwell there: and the last state of that man is worse than the first. Even so shall it be also unto this wicked generation.

46 While he yet talked to the people, behold, his mother and his brethren stood without, desiring to speak with him.

47 Then one said unto him, Behold, thy mother and thy brethren stand without, desiring to speak with thee.

48 But he answered and said unto him that told him, Who is my mother? and who are my brethren?

49 And he stretched forth his hand toward his disciples, and said, Behold my mother and my brethren!

50 For whosoever shall do the will of my Father which is in heaven, the same is my brother, and sister, and mother.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

On the sabbath Jesus defends his disciples plucking corn and heals a withered hand, citing David eating the shewbread and the priests profaning the sabbath in the temple, and declaring himself Lord of the sabbath and greater than the temple. He heals a blind and dumb demoniac, is charged with casting out devils by Beelzebub, warns of blasphemy against the Holy Ghost, of idle words at judgment, and of the sign of Jonah, and ends by naming whoever does the Father's will as his brother, sister and mother. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. At that time Jesus went on the sabbath day through the corn, and his disciples were an hungred — not merely before regular meals, but evidently from shortness of provisions, for Jesus defends their plucking on the plea of necessity — and began to pluck the ears of corn, and to eat, rubbing them in their hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. But when the Pharisees saw it, they said unto him, Behold, thy disciples do that which is not lawful to do upon the sabbath day. The act itself was expressly permitted (Deuteronomy 23:25), but as “servile work” prohibited on the sabbath it was regarded as sinful. The Pharisees quarrelled not for taking another man's corn, but for doing it on the sabbath; Christ came to free his followers not only from Pharisaic corruptions but from their unscriptural rules. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. But he said unto them, Have ye not read — Mark has it, “Have ye never read” — what David did, when he was an hungered, and they that were with him. The greatest shall not have their lusts indulged, but the meanest shall have their wants considered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. How he entered into the house of God, and did eat the shewbread, which was not lawful for him to eat, neither for them which were with him, but only for the priests? No example could be more apposite: the man after God's own heart, of whom the Jews ever boasted, when suffering in God's cause and straitened for provisions, obtained from the high priest what the law reserved to priests alone. Mark's “in the days of Abiathar” means in his time, not his high priesthood, which was his father Ahimelech's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Or have ye not read in the law, how that on the sabbath days the priests in the temple profane the sabbath, and are blameless? The double sabbath offerings and the weekly showbread could not be presented without much servile work by the priests, not to speak of circumcision falling on a sabbath. Those labours are lawful on the sabbath which are necessary, and sabbath rest is to forward, not hinder, sabbath worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But I say unto you, That in this place is one greater than the temple — better read, “something greater.” The ordinary rules of sabbath observance give way before the temple's requirements, but there are rights here before which the temple itself must give way; thus indirectly, but not less decidedly, our Lord puts in his own claims to consideration, to be stated even more nakedly next. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But if ye had known what this meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice (Hosea 6:6), ye would not have condemned the guiltless. Had they understood the great principle of all religion — that ceremonial observances must give way before moral duties, particularly nature's necessities — they would have refrained from captious complaints against blameless men. Mark preserves the specific application: the sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath, a glorious maxim for both the sabbath's establishment and the true freedom of its observance. No law must be understood so as to contradict its own end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For the Son of man is Lord even of the sabbath day. Not to abolish it — a strange lordship, especially just after saying it was made for man — but to own it, interpret it, preside over it and ennoble it, merging it in the Lord's Day, breathing into it liberty and love unknown before, making it the nearest resemblance to the eternal sabbatism. As Christ is Lord of the sabbath, it is fit the day and its work be dedicated to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. When he was departed thence — but “on another sabbath,” says Luke — he went into their synagogue. Christ shows works of mercy are lawful and proper on the Lord's day: attending the sick, relieving the poor, helping those needing speedy relief, teaching the young to care for their souls — these are doing good, and must be done from love and charity, with humility and self-denial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Behold, there was a man which had his hand withered — disabled by paralysis, his right hand, Luke notes. And they asked him, saying, Is it lawful to heal on the sabbath days? that they might accuse him. Mark and Luke say they watched whether he would heal; they had come to dogging his steps to collect materials for a charge of impiety, and it was probably to their thoughts rather than their words that Jesus addressed himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. He said unto them, What man shall there be among you, that shall have one sheep, and if it fall into a pit on the sabbath day, will he not lay hold on it, and lift it out? A righteous man regards the life of his beast and would instinctively rescue it from death or suffering on the sabbath; how much more his nobler fellow man! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. How much then is a man better than a sheep? Wherefore it is lawful to do well on the sabbath days. The reasoning in the other Gospels is singularly striking: “Is it lawful to do good, or to do evil? to save life, or to destroy it?” — or, in Mark, “to kill?” Not to do good when it is in our power is to do evil; not to save life when we can is to kill. Must the letter of sabbath rest be kept at this expense? The thrust shut their mouths. Mark adds he looked round with anger, being grieved for the hardness of their hearts — one of the very few passages revealing our Lord's feelings, holy anger mingled with grief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Then saith he to the man, Stretch forth thine hand. And he stretched it forth; and it was restored whole, like as the other — the power to obey going forth with the word of command. This cure had a spiritual meaning: by nature our hands are withered and we cannot of ourselves do anything good; Christ only, by his grace, puts life into the dead soul, working in us both to will and to do, for with the command there is a promise of grace given by the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Then the Pharisees went out, and held a council against him, how they might destroy him — the first explicit mention of their murderous designs. Luke says they were filled with madness and communed what they might do to Jesus; their doubt was not whether to get rid of him, but how. Mark adds they straightway took counsel with the Herodians, supporters of Herod's dynasty, a political party the Pharisees deemed untrue to religion and country, now combining against Christ as a common enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. But when Jesus knew it, he withdrew himself from thence — Mark says to the sea, to some distance from the scene of miracle, madness and plotting — and great multitudes followed him, and he healed them all. Mark details a multitude from Galilee, Judea, Jerusalem, Idumea, beyond Jordan, and about Tyre and Sidon, pressing to touch him, unclean spirits falling down crying, Thou art the Son of God, and he straitly charging them not to make him known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. And charged them that they should not make him known. As his whole course was sublimely noiseless, so he repeatedly took steps to prevent matters prematurely coming to a crisis; and as his time was not come, he retired rather than force the confrontation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet, saying — Isaiah 42:1. Face does not more exactly answer to face in water than the character of Christ drawn by the prophet answers to his temper and conduct as described by the evangelists. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Behold my servant, whom I have chosen; my beloved, in whom my soul is well pleased: I will put my Spirit upon him, and he shall shew judgment to the Gentiles. Let us with cheerful confidence commit our souls to so kind and faithful a Friend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. He shall not strive, nor cry; neither shall any man hear his voice in the streets. Far from breaking, he will strengthen the bruised reed; far from quenching the smoking flax, he will blow it into a flame; let us lay aside contentious and angry debates and receive one another as Christ receives us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. A bruised reed shall he not break, and smoking flax shall he not quench, till he send forth judgment unto victory — “unto truth” in the Hebrew and Septuagint, but our Evangelist seizes the spirit rather than the letter. The grandeur and completeness of Messiah's victories would prove not more wonderful than the unobtrusive noiselessness with which they were achieved; his it should be, with matchless tenderness, love and skill, to lift up the meek, strengthen weak hands, confirm feeble knees, comfort all that mourn, and say to the fearful heart, Be strong, fear not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. And in his name shall the Gentiles trust. Part of his present audience were Gentiles from Tyre and Sidon, first-fruits of the great Gentile harvest contemplated in the prophecy; and while encouraged by our Lord's gracious kindness, we should pray his Spirit may rest upon us and make us able to copy his example. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Then was brought unto him one possessed with a devil, blind and dumb, and he healed him, insomuch that the blind and the dumb both spake and saw. A soul under Satan's power is blind in the things of God and dumb at the throne of grace, seeing nothing and saying nothing to purpose; Satan blinds eyes by unbelief and seals lips from prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. All the people were amazed, and said, Is not this the son of David? The Greek interrogative expects rather a negative answer: “Can it possibly be?” — the people indicating their secret impression that this must be he, yet saving themselves from the ecclesiastics' wrath which a direct assertion would bring. The more people magnified Christ, the more desirous the Pharisees were to vilify him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. But when the Pharisees heard it — Mark says the scribes which came down from Jerusalem, a hostile party come all the way to collect materials for a charge — they said, This fellow doth not cast out devils, but by Beelzebub the prince of the devils. Two things are implied: the bitterest enemies could not deny the reality of his miracles, and they believed in an organized kingdom of evil under one chief — a belief our Lord immediately sets his seal to. Stung by the people's testimony, they had no way to hold out but the desperate shift of ascribing his miracles to Satan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Jesus knew their thoughts, and said unto them, Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation; and every city or house divided against itself shall not stand — “house,” that is, household. It was evident: if Satan aided Jesus in casting out devils, the kingdom of hell was divided against itself; how then could it stand! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. And if Satan cast out Satan, he is divided against himself; how shall then his kingdom stand? The argument is irresistible: no organized society can stand, kingdom, city or household, when turned against itself; such intestine war is suicidal. But the works Christ does are destructive of Satan's kingdom, so league with Satan is incredible and absurd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. And if I by Beelzebub cast out devils, by whom do your children cast them out? therefore they shall be your judges. “Your sons” means the Pharisees' disciples or pupils, after the sons of the prophets; our Lord seems to admit such works were wrought by them, in which case the Pharisees stood self-condemned. If they said Jesus cast out by the prince of devils, they could not prove their children cast them out by any other power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. But if I cast out devils by the Spirit of God — Luke has “by the finger of God,” a figurative expression for God's power, while Matthew names the living Personal Agent made use of by the Lord Jesus in every exercise of that power — then the kingdom of God is come unto you, rather “upon you.” If this expulsion of Satan can be by no other than the Spirit of God, then his Destroyer is already in the midst of you, and the kingdom destined to supplant his is already rising on its ruins. There are two great interests in the world, and when unclean spirits are cast out by the Holy Spirit in conversion, the kingdom of God is come unto us; all who do not aid or rejoice in such a change are against Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Or else how can one enter into a strong man's house, and spoil his goods, except he first bind the strong man? and then he will spoil his house. The strong man must first be bound before his house is spoiled, and Christ's casting out of demons is the binding itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. He that is not with me is against me; and he that gathereth not with me scattereth abroad. On this parable, in connection with 12:43-45, see Luke 11:21-26; neutrality in this war is opposition, for there are but two kingdoms with their proper workings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Wherefore I say unto you, All manner of sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven unto men: but the blasphemy against the Holy Ghost shall not be forgiven unto men. “Blasphemy” properly signifies detraction or slander, here vituperation against God in aggravated form; yet all sin, ordinary or aggravated, shall find forgiveness — Mark stronger still, “all sin shall be forgiven unto the sons of men.” This glorious assurance is not to be limited by what follows; rather, what follows is to be explained by it. Here is a gracious assurance of pardon of all sin upon gospel terms, and Christ sets an example of readiness to forgive words spoken against us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Whosoever speaketh a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him: but whosoever speaketh against the Holy Ghost, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, neither in the world to come. Mark adds, awfully, “hath never forgiveness, but is in danger of eternal damnation,” and the reason: “Because they said, He hath an unclean spirit.” The unpardonableness cannot lie in sin's nature, contradicting verse 31, nor in greater sanctity of one Person over another; it is the contrast between slandering the Son of man in his veiled condition and unfinished work, which might be done ignorantly in unbelief, and slandering him after the blaze of glory the Holy Ghost would throw around his claims, with eyes open and presumptuously — hating the light the clearer it became, which precludes salvation. The Pharisees had not yet done this, but in charging Jesus with league with hell they displayed beforehand a malignant determination to shut their eyes to all evidence, bordering upon and in spirit committing the unpardonable sin. Humble believers tempted to think they have committed it, while fearing it, give good sign they have not, for repentance and faith are gifts God would not bestow on one determined never to pardon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. Either make the tree good, and his fruit good; or else make the tree corrupt, and his fruit corrupt: for the tree is known by his fruit. Men's language discovers what country they are of and what manner of spirit; the heart is the fountain, words the streams, and only the salt of grace cast into the spring heals the waters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. O generation of vipers, how can ye, being evil, speak good things? for out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh — a principle obvious yet of deepest significance and vast application, uttered also in Luke 6:45. An evil man has an evil treasure in his heart, and out of it brings forth evil things; lusts and corruptions reigning in the heart are an evil treasure, producing bad words and actions to dishonour God and hurt others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. A good man out of the good treasure of the heart bringeth forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things. “Putteth forth” indicates spontaneity, for out of the heart's abundance the mouth speaks. Here is a new application of the tree-and-fruit saying: there are but two kingdoms, interests, parties, with the proper workings of each; if I promote one I cannot belong to the other, and those in wilful opposition to the kingdom of light openly proclaim to what other kingdom they belong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. But I say unto you, That every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment. They might plead it was nothing, no evil meant, merely a supposition thrown out to account for the miracle; Jesus replies it was not nothing, and at the great day will not be treated as nothing: words, as the index of the heart, however idle they seem, will be taken account of, good or bad, in estimating character. Let us keep constant watch over ourselves, that we may speak words agreeable to the Christian character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. For by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned. Words are the streams from the heart's fountain; a troubled fountain and corrupt spring send forth muddy streams, and judgment will weigh the streams as evidence of the spring. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Then certain of the scribes and of the Pharisees answered, saying, Master, we would see a sign from thee — “a sign from heaven,” Luke adds, something immediate and decisive, to show not that his miracles were real, which they seemed willing to concede, but that they were from above, not from beneath. These were not the same class as those charging league with Satan, but the spirit was similar, and the severe rebuke continues. Though Christ is always ready to hear holy desires, those who ask amiss, ask and have not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. But he answered and said unto them, An evil and adulterous generation seeketh after a sign; and there shall no sign be given to it, but the sign of the prophet Jonas. “Adulterous” is explained by Jeremiah 3:20: as a wife treacherously departeth from her husband, so Israel dealt treacherously with the Lord, to whom he stood married. This class were but spokesmen of their generation, exponents of reigning unbelief. Signs were granted to those desiring them to confirm faith, as Abraham and Gideon, but denied to those demanding them to excuse unbelief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale's belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth. Jonah was a sign unto the Ninevites, so shall the Son of man be to this generation; his case was a signal judgment reversed in three days, followed by a glorious mission to the Gentiles. This was the second public announcement of his resurrection three days after death. “In the heart of the earth” means simply the grave, the most emphatic expression of real and total entombment, and the period is in round numbers after the Jewish way, any part of a day counting as a full day. The resurrection by his own power, called the sign of Jonah, was the great proof of his Messiahship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here. The Ninevites, though heathens, repented at a man's preaching, while God's covenant people repented not at the preaching of the Son of God, whose supreme dignity is implied rather than expressed; the Ninevites would shame the Jews for not repenting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. The queen of the south shall rise up in the judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: for she came from the uttermost parts of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and, behold, a greater than Solomon is here. The queen of Sheba, from Arabia near the Red Sea, came from a remote country south of Judea to hear a mere gifted man, and was transported with wonder; they, when a Greater than Solomon had come, despised, rejected, slighted and slandered him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through dry places, seeking rest, and findeth none. This parable represents the Jewish church and nation, and is applicable to all who hear the word and are in part reformed but not truly converted; the spirit leaves for a time, but returning finds Christ is not there to shut him out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Then he saith, I will return into my house from whence I came out; and when he is come, he findeth it empty, swept, and garnished. The heart is swept by outward reformation but garnished by preparation to comply with evil suggestions, and the man becomes a more decided enemy of the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Then goeth he, and taketh with himself seven other spirits more wicked than himself, and they enter in and dwell there: and the last state of that man is worse than the first. Even so shall it be also unto this wicked generation. Every heart is the residence of unclean spirits, except those which are temples of the Holy Ghost by faith in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. While he yet talked to the people, behold, his mother and his brethren stood without, desiring to speak with him — and could not come at him for the press, Luke adds. For what purpose they came, Mark shows: in his zeal he seemed indifferent to food and repose, and they went to lay hold of him as one beside himself. Christ's preaching was plain, easy and familiar, suited to his hearers; his mother and brethren stood without desiring to speak, when they should have been within desiring to hear him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. Then one said unto him, Behold, thy mother and thy brethren stand without, desiring to speak with thee. Absorbed in the awful warnings he was pouring forth, he felt this an unseasonable interruption, fitted to dissipate the impression on the large audience, such as duty to nearest relatives did not require him to give way to; but instead of direct rebuke, he seizes the incident to convey a sublime lesson in inimitable condescension. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. But he answered and said unto him that told him, Who is my mother? and who are my brethren? Frequently those nearest to the means of knowledge and grace are most negligent, apt to neglect what they think they may have any day, forgetting tomorrow is not ours; we often meet hindrances from friends about us, taken off by care for this life from concerns of our souls. Christ was so intent on his work that no natural or other duty took him from it — not that religion may make us disrespectful to parents or unkind to relations, but the lesser duty must stand by while the greater is done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. And he stretched forth his hand toward his disciples, and said, Behold my mother and my brethren! How graphic is this, the language evidently of an eye-witness; let us cease from men and cleave to Christ, looking upon every Christian, whatever their condition, as the brother, sister or mother of the Lord of glory, to be loved, respected and kindly treated for his sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. For whosoever shall do the will of my Father which is in heaven, the same is my brother, and sister, and mother. There stand here members of a family transcending and surviving this of earth: filial subjection to the will of the Father in heaven is the indissoluble bond between him and all its members, and whosoever enters this hallowed circle becomes to him brother, sister and mother. Perspectives (editorial — views side by side, not adjudicated)

Readings below are grouped by the commentator voice each study note summarizes. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations.

Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 1. At that time Jesus went on the sabbath day through the corn, and his disciples were an hungred — not merely before regular meals, but evidently from shortness of provisions, for Jesus defends their plucking on the plea of necessity — and began to pluck the ears of corn, and to eat, rubbing them in their hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. But when the Pharisees saw it, they said unto him, Behold, thy disciples do that which is not lawful to do upon the sabbath day. The act itself was expressly permitted (Deuteronomy 23:25), but as “servile work” prohibited on the sabbath it was regarded as sinful. The Pharisees quarrelled not for taking another man's corn, but for doing it on the sabbath; Christ came to free his followers not only from Pharisaic corruptions but from their unscriptural rules. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. But he said unto them, Have ye not read — Mark has it, “Have ye never read” — what David did, when he was an hungered, and they that were with him. The greatest shall not have their lusts indulged, but the meanest shall have their wants considered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. How he entered into the house of God, and did eat the shewbread, which was not lawful for him to eat, neither for them which were with him, but only for the priests? No example could be more apposite: the man after God's own heart, of whom the Jews ever boasted, when suffering in God's cause and straitened for provisions, obtained from the high priest what the law reserved to priests alone. Mark's “in the days of Abiathar” means in his time, not his high priesthood, which was his father Ahimelech's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Or have ye not read in the law, how that on the sabbath days the priests in the temple profane the sabbath, and are blameless? The double sabbath offerings and the weekly showbread could not be presented without much servile work by the priests, not to speak of circumcision falling on a sabbath. Those labours are lawful on the sabbath which are necessary, and sabbath rest is to forward, not hinder, sabbath worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But I say unto you, That in this place is one greater than the temple — better read, “something greater.” The ordinary rules of sabbath observance give way before the temple's requirements, but there are rights here before which the temple itself must give way; thus indirectly, but not less decidedly, our Lord puts in his own claims to consideration, to be stated even more nakedly next. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But if ye had known what this meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice (Hosea 6:6), ye would not have condemned the guiltless. Had they understood the great principle of all religion — that ceremonial observances must give way before moral duties, particularly nature's necessities — they would have refrained from captious complaints against blameless men. Mark preserves the specific application: the sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath, a glorious maxim for both the sabbath's establishment and the true freedom of its observance. No law must be understood so as to contradict its own end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For the Son of man is Lord even of the sabbath day. Not to abolish it — a strange lordship, especially just after saying it was made for man — but to own it, interpret it, preside over it and ennoble it, merging it in the Lord's Day, breathing into it liberty and love unknown before, making it the nearest resemblance to the eternal sabbatism. As Christ is Lord of the sabbath, it is fit the day and its work be dedicated to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. When he was departed thence — but “on another sabbath,” says Luke — he went into their synagogue. Christ shows works of mercy are lawful and proper on the Lord's day: attending the sick, relieving the poor, helping those needing speedy relief, teaching the young to care for their souls — these are doing good, and must be done from love and charity, with humility and self-denial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Behold, there was a man which had his hand withered — disabled by paralysis, his right hand, Luke notes. And they asked him, saying, Is it lawful to heal on the sabbath days? that they might accuse him. Mark and Luke say they watched whether he would heal; they had come to dogging his steps to collect materials for a charge of impiety, and it was probably to their thoughts rather than their words that Jesus addressed himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. How much then is a man better than a sheep? Wherefore it is lawful to do well on the sabbath days. The reasoning in the other Gospels is singularly striking: “Is it lawful to do good, or to do evil? to save life, or to destroy it?” — or, in Mark, “to kill?” Not to do good when it is in our power is to do evil; not to save life when we can is to kill. Must the letter of sabbath rest be kept at this expense? The thrust shut their mouths. Mark adds he looked round with anger, being grieved for the hardness of their hearts — one of the very few passages revealing our Lord's feelings, holy anger mingled with grief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Then saith he to the man, Stretch forth thine hand. And he stretched it forth; and it was restored whole, like as the other — the power to obey going forth with the word of command. This cure had a spiritual meaning: by nature our hands are withered and we cannot of ourselves do anything good; Christ only, by his grace, puts life into the dead soul, working in us both to will and to do, for with the command there is a promise of grace given by the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Then the Pharisees went out, and held a council against him, how they might destroy him — the first explicit mention of their murderous designs. Luke says they were filled with madness and communed what they might do to Jesus; their doubt was not whether to get rid of him, but how. Mark adds they straightway took counsel with the Herodians, supporters of Herod's dynasty, a political party the Pharisees deemed untrue to religion and country, now combining against Christ as a common enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. But when Jesus knew it, he withdrew himself from thence — Mark says to the sea, to some distance from the scene of miracle, madness and plotting — and great multitudes followed him, and he healed them all. Mark details a multitude from Galilee, Judea, Jerusalem, Idumea, beyond Jordan, and about Tyre and Sidon, pressing to touch him, unclean spirits falling down crying, Thou art the Son of God, and he straitly charging them not to make him known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. And charged them that they should not make him known. As his whole course was sublimely noiseless, so he repeatedly took steps to prevent matters prematurely coming to a crisis; and as his time was not come, he retired rather than force the confrontation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet, saying — Isaiah 42:1. Face does not more exactly answer to face in water than the character of Christ drawn by the prophet answers to his temper and conduct as described by the evangelists. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Behold my servant, whom I have chosen; my beloved, in whom my soul is well pleased: I will put my Spirit upon him, and he shall shew judgment to the Gentiles. Let us with cheerful confidence commit our souls to so kind and faithful a Friend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. He shall not strive, nor cry; neither shall any man hear his voice in the streets. Far from breaking, he will strengthen the bruised reed; far from quenching the smoking flax, he will blow it into a flame; let us lay aside contentious and angry debates and receive one another as Christ receives us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. A bruised reed shall he not break, and smoking flax shall he not quench, till he send forth judgment unto victory — “unto truth” in the Hebrew and Septuagint, but our Evangelist seizes the spirit rather than the letter. The grandeur and completeness of Messiah's victories would prove not more wonderful than the unobtrusive noiselessness with which they were achieved; his it should be, with matchless tenderness, love and skill, to lift up the meek, strengthen weak hands, confirm feeble knees, comfort all that mourn, and say to the fearful heart, Be strong, fear not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. And in his name shall the Gentiles trust. Part of his present audience were Gentiles from Tyre and Sidon, first-fruits of the great Gentile harvest contemplated in the prophecy; and while encouraged by our Lord's gracious kindness, we should pray his Spirit may rest upon us and make us able to copy his example. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Then was brought unto him one possessed with a devil, blind and dumb, and he healed him, insomuch that the blind and the dumb both spake and saw. A soul under Satan's power is blind in the things of God and dumb at the throne of grace, seeing nothing and saying nothing to purpose; Satan blinds eyes by unbelief and seals lips from prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. All the people were amazed, and said, Is not this the son of David? The Greek interrogative expects rather a negative answer: “Can it possibly be?” — the people indicating their secret impression that this must be he, yet saving themselves from the ecclesiastics' wrath which a direct assertion would bring. The more people magnified Christ, the more desirous the Pharisees were to vilify him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. But when the Pharisees heard it — Mark says the scribes which came down from Jerusalem, a hostile party come all the way to collect materials for a charge — they said, This fellow doth not cast out devils, but by Beelzebub the prince of the devils. Two things are implied: the bitterest enemies could not deny the reality of his miracles, and they believed in an organized kingdom of evil under one chief — a belief our Lord immediately sets his seal to. Stung by the people's testimony, they had no way to hold out but the desperate shift of ascribing his miracles to Satan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Jesus knew their thoughts, and said unto them, Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation; and every city or house divided against itself shall not stand — “house,” that is, household. It was evident: if Satan aided Jesus in casting out devils, the kingdom of hell was divided against itself; how then could it stand! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. And if Satan cast out Satan, he is divided against himself; how shall then his kingdom stand? The argument is irresistible: no organized society can stand, kingdom, city or household, when turned against itself; such intestine war is suicidal. But the works Christ does are destructive of Satan's kingdom, so league with Satan is incredible and absurd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. And if I by Beelzebub cast out devils, by whom do your children cast them out? therefore they shall be your judges. “Your sons” means the Pharisees' disciples or pupils, after the sons of the prophets; our Lord seems to admit such works were wrought by them, in which case the Pharisees stood self-condemned. If they said Jesus cast out by the prince of devils, they could not prove their children cast them out by any other power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. But if I cast out devils by the Spirit of God — Luke has “by the finger of God,” a figurative expression for God's power, while Matthew names the living Personal Agent made use of by the Lord Jesus in every exercise of that power — then the kingdom of God is come unto you, rather “upon you.” If this expulsion of Satan can be by no other than the Spirit of God, then his Destroyer is already in the midst of you, and the kingdom destined to supplant his is already rising on its ruins. There are two great interests in the world, and when unclean spirits are cast out by the Holy Spirit in conversion, the kingdom of God is come unto us; all who do not aid or rejoice in such a change are against Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Or else how can one enter into a strong man's house, and spoil his goods, except he first bind the strong man? and then he will spoil his house. The strong man must first be bound before his house is spoiled, and Christ's casting out of demons is the binding itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. He that is not with me is against me; and he that gathereth not with me scattereth abroad. On this parable, in connection with 12:43-45, see Luke 11:21-26; neutrality in this war is opposition, for there are but two kingdoms with their proper workings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Wherefore I say unto you, All manner of sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven unto men: but the blasphemy against the Holy Ghost shall not be forgiven unto men. “Blasphemy” properly signifies detraction or slander, here vituperation against God in aggravated form; yet all sin, ordinary or aggravated, shall find forgiveness — Mark stronger still, “all sin shall be forgiven unto the sons of men.” This glorious assurance is not to be limited by what follows; rather, what follows is to be explained by it. Here is a gracious assurance of pardon of all sin upon gospel terms, and Christ sets an example of readiness to forgive words spoken against us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Whosoever speaketh a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him: but whosoever speaketh against the Holy Ghost, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, neither in the world to come. Mark adds, awfully, “hath never forgiveness, but is in danger of eternal damnation,” and the reason: “Because they said, He hath an unclean spirit.” The unpardonableness cannot lie in sin's nature, contradicting verse 31, nor in greater sanctity of one Person over another; it is the contrast between slandering the Son of man in his veiled condition and unfinished work, which might be done ignorantly in unbelief, and slandering him after the blaze of glory the Holy Ghost would throw around his claims, with eyes open and presumptuously — hating the light the clearer it became, which precludes salvation. The Pharisees had not yet done this, but in charging Jesus with league with hell they displayed beforehand a malignant determination to shut their eyes to all evidence, bordering upon and in spirit committing the unpardonable sin. Humble believers tempted to think they have committed it, while fearing it, give good sign they have not, for repentance and faith are gifts God would not bestow on one determined never to pardon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. Either make the tree good, and his fruit good; or else make the tree corrupt, and his fruit corrupt: for the tree is known by his fruit. Men's language discovers what country they are of and what manner of spirit; the heart is the fountain, words the streams, and only the salt of grace cast into the spring heals the waters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. O generation of vipers, how can ye, being evil, speak good things? for out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh — a principle obvious yet of deepest significance and vast application, uttered also in Luke 6:45. An evil man has an evil treasure in his heart, and out of it brings forth evil things; lusts and corruptions reigning in the heart are an evil treasure, producing bad words and actions to dishonour God and hurt others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. A good man out of the good treasure of the heart bringeth forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things. “Putteth forth” indicates spontaneity, for out of the heart's abundance the mouth speaks. Here is a new application of the tree-and-fruit saying: there are but two kingdoms, interests, parties, with the proper workings of each; if I promote one I cannot belong to the other, and those in wilful opposition to the kingdom of light openly proclaim to what other kingdom they belong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. But I say unto you, That every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment. They might plead it was nothing, no evil meant, merely a supposition thrown out to account for the miracle; Jesus replies it was not nothing, and at the great day will not be treated as nothing: words, as the index of the heart, however idle they seem, will be taken account of, good or bad, in estimating character. Let us keep constant watch over ourselves, that we may speak words agreeable to the Christian character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. Then certain of the scribes and of the Pharisees answered, saying, Master, we would see a sign from thee — “a sign from heaven,” Luke adds, something immediate and decisive, to show not that his miracles were real, which they seemed willing to concede, but that they were from above, not from beneath. These were not the same class as those charging league with Satan, but the spirit was similar, and the severe rebuke continues. Though Christ is always ready to hear holy desires, those who ask amiss, ask and have not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. But he answered and said unto them, An evil and adulterous generation seeketh after a sign; and there shall no sign be given to it, but the sign of the prophet Jonas. “Adulterous” is explained by Jeremiah 3:20: as a wife treacherously departeth from her husband, so Israel dealt treacherously with the Lord, to whom he stood married. This class were but spokesmen of their generation, exponents of reigning unbelief. Signs were granted to those desiring them to confirm faith, as Abraham and Gideon, but denied to those demanding them to excuse unbelief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale's belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth. Jonah was a sign unto the Ninevites, so shall the Son of man be to this generation; his case was a signal judgment reversed in three days, followed by a glorious mission to the Gentiles. This was the second public announcement of his resurrection three days after death. “In the heart of the earth” means simply the grave, the most emphatic expression of real and total entombment, and the period is in round numbers after the Jewish way, any part of a day counting as a full day. The resurrection by his own power, called the sign of Jonah, was the great proof of his Messiahship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here. The Ninevites, though heathens, repented at a man's preaching, while God's covenant people repented not at the preaching of the Son of God, whose supreme dignity is implied rather than expressed; the Ninevites would shame the Jews for not repenting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. The queen of the south shall rise up in the judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: for she came from the uttermost parts of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and, behold, a greater than Solomon is here. The queen of Sheba, from Arabia near the Red Sea, came from a remote country south of Judea to hear a mere gifted man, and was transported with wonder; they, when a Greater than Solomon had come, despised, rejected, slighted and slandered him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. While he yet talked to the people, behold, his mother and his brethren stood without, desiring to speak with him — and could not come at him for the press, Luke adds. For what purpose they came, Mark shows: in his zeal he seemed indifferent to food and repose, and they went to lay hold of him as one beside himself. Christ's preaching was plain, easy and familiar, suited to his hearers; his mother and brethren stood without desiring to speak, when they should have been within desiring to hear him. A righteous man regards the life of his beast and would instinctively rescue it from death or suffering on the sabbath; how much more his nobler fellow man! Investigation (editorial — a parallel question, not a conclusion)

Parallel. The sabbath cornfield, the withered hand, the Beelzebub charge, the sign, and the mother and brethren are told in Matthew and, throughout the notes, in Mark and Luke as well. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Difference. The notes record several wording and detail differences: Mark has “Have ye never read”; Luke notes the withered hand was the right hand; Luke has “by the finger of God” where Matthew has “by the Spirit of God”; Luke adds “a sign from heaven” to the request; Mark says the scribes came down from Jerusalem; and Luke adds that the mother and brethren could not come at him for the press. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
