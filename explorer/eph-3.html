The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ephesians 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 For this cause I Paul, the prisoner of Jesus Christ for you Gentiles,

2 If ye have heard of the dispensation of the grace of God which is given me to you-ward:

3 How that by revelation he made known unto me the mystery; (as I wrote afore in few words,

4 Whereby, when ye read, ye may understand my knowledge in the mystery of Christ)

5 Which in other ages was not made known unto the sons of men, as it is now revealed unto his holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit;

6 That the Gentiles should be fellowheirs, and of the same body, and partakers of his promise in Christ by the gospel:

7 Whereof I was made a minister, according to the gift of the grace of God given unto me by the effectual working of his power.

8 Unto me, who am less than the least of all saints, is this grace given, that I should preach among the Gentiles the unsearchable riches of Christ;

9 And to make all men see what is the fellowship of the mystery, which from the beginning of the world hath been hid in God, who created all things by Jesus Christ:

10 To the intent that now unto the principalities and powers in heavenly places might be known by the church the manifold wisdom of God,

11 According to the eternal purpose which he purposed in Christ Jesus our Lord:

12 In whom we have boldness and access with confidence by the faith of him.

13 Wherefore I desire that ye faint not at my tribulations for you, which is your glory.

14 For this cause I bow my knees unto the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ,

15 Of whom the whole family in heaven and earth is named,

16 That he would grant you, according to the riches of his glory, to be strengthened with might by his Spirit in the inner man;

17 That Christ may dwell in your hearts by faith; that ye, being rooted and grounded in love,

18 May be able to comprehend with all saints what is the breadth, and length, and depth, and height;

19 And to know the love of Christ, which passeth knowledge, that ye might be filled with all the fulness of God.

20 Now unto him that is able to do exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think, according to the power that worketh in us,

21 Unto him be glory in the church by Christ Jesus throughout all ages, world without end. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul describes his stewardship of a mystery once hidden and now revealed: Gentiles are fellow-heirs, members of the same body, sharers in the promise in Christ. He asks readers not to lose heart at his imprisonment, then prays Christ will dwell in their hearts by faith, so they may grasp a love that surpasses knowledge and be filled with God's fullness. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul calls himself the prisoner of Jesus Christ for the Gentiles, suffering for preaching the truth yet under his Lord's special protection and care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He assumes they have heard of the stewardship of God's grace entrusted to him for their sake, since the gospel offers come from God's rich grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. By revelation God made the mystery known to Paul, as he had already written briefly, showing that this knowledge came by divine disclosure, not human discovery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Reading Paul's words, they can perceive his understanding in the mystery of Christ, the secret purpose of salvation now openly declared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. In earlier ages this mystery was not made known to the sons of men as it is now revealed to the holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The heart of the mystery is that Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of God's promise in Christ through the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Paul was made a minister of this gospel according to the gift of God's grace, given to him by the effectual working of divine power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. To Paul, who counts himself less than the least of all saints, was given the grace to preach among the Gentiles the unsearchable riches of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. His task is to make all see the fellowship of the mystery, hidden from the beginning in God who created all things by Jesus Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Through the church God's manifold wisdom is now made known to principalities and powers in heavenly places, while angels adore what self-wise men count foolishness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. All this follows the eternal purpose God purposed in Christ Jesus our Lord, the same purpose behind both the first creation and the new creation of sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. In Christ believers have boldness and access with confidence through faith in him, approaching God not timidly but as children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Paul therefore asks them not to faint at his tribulations for them, since his sufferings are their glory rather than a cause for discouragement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. For this cause he bows his knees to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, turning from argument to prayer for spiritual blessings, the best blessings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. From this Father the whole family in heaven and earth is named, one household gathered under one name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He prays they would be strengthened with might by God's Spirit in the inner man, according to the riches of his glory, since strength of soul is what the Christian life requires. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. That inner strength serves this end: that Christ may dwell in their hearts by faith, they being rooted and grounded in love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Rooted in love, they may be able with all saints to comprehend the breadth, length, depth, and height of Christ's love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. They are to know that love which passes knowledge, and so be filled with all the fullness of God, a fullness that should satisfy where trifles cannot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. God is able to do exceeding abundantly above all we ask or think, according to the power that works in us, so prayer should expect more and ask for more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. To him be glory in the church by Christ Jesus throughout all ages, world without end, since the conversion of sinners and the comfort of believers are to his glory forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
