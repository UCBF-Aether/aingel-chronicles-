The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth.

2 And the fear of you and the dread of you shall be upon every beast of the earth, and upon every fowl of the air, upon all that moveth upon the earth, and upon all the fishes of the sea; into your hand are they delivered.

3 Every moving thing that liveth shall be meat for you; even as the green herb have I given you all things.

4 But flesh with the life thereof, which is the blood thereof, shall ye not eat.

5 And surely your blood of your lives will I require; at the hand of every beast will I require it, and at the hand of man; at the hand of every man’s brother will I require the life of man.

6 Whoso sheddeth man’s blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image of God made he man.

7 And you, be ye fruitful, and multiply; bring forth abundantly in the earth, and multiply therein.

8 And God spake unto Noah, and to his sons with him, saying,

9 And I, behold, I establish my covenant with you, and with your seed after you;

10 And with every living creature that is with you, of the fowl, of the cattle, and of every beast of the earth with you; from all that go out of the ark, to every beast of the earth.

11 And I will establish my covenant with you, neither shall all flesh be cut off any more by the waters of a flood; neither shall there any more be a flood to destroy the earth.

12 And God said, This is the token of the covenant which I make between me and you and every living creature that is with you, for perpetual generations:

13 I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth.

14 And it shall come to pass, when I bring a cloud over the earth, that the bow shall be seen in the cloud:

15 And I will remember my covenant, which is between me and you and every living creature of all flesh; and the waters shall no more become a flood to destroy all flesh.

16 And the bow shall be in the cloud; and I will look upon it, that I may remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is upon the earth.

17 And God said unto Noah, This is the token of the covenant, which I have established between me and all flesh that is upon the earth.

18 And the sons of Noah, that went forth of the ark, were Shem, and Ham, and Japheth: and Ham is the father of Canaan.

19 These are the three sons of Noah: and of them was the whole earth overspread.

20 And Noah began to be an husbandman, and he planted a vineyard:

21 And he drank of the wine, and was drunken; and he was uncovered within his tent.

22 And Ham, the father of Canaan, saw the nakedness of his father, and told his two brethren without.

23 And Shem and Japheth took a garment, and laid it upon both their shoulders, and went backward, and covered the nakedness of their father; and their faces were backward, and they saw not their father’s nakedness.

24 And Noah awoke from his wine, and knew what his younger son had done unto him.

25 And he said, Cursed be Canaan; a servant of servants shall he be unto his brethren.

26 And he said, Blessed be the LORD God of Shem; and Canaan shall be his servant.

27 God shall enlarge Japheth, and he shall dwell in the tents of Shem; and Canaan shall be his servant.

28 And Noah lived after the flood three hundred and fifty years.

29 And all the days of Noah were nine hundred and fifty years: and he died.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God blesses Noah and his sons with a renewed charge to be fruitful and fill the earth, gives animals for food with a ban on eating blood, and sets the rainbow as the sign of a covenant never again to destroy all flesh by flood. The chapter closes with Noah's vineyard, Ham's offence, the blessings on Shem and Japheth, and Noah's death at nine hundred and fifty years. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God blessed Noah and his sons, saying be fruitful, multiply and replenish the earth. The blessing renews the creation mandate of chapter 1 for a post-flood world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The fear and dread of people would be on every beast, bird, creeping thing and fish, delivered into human hands. Henry notes this fear is God's protection for humanity, keeping dangerous creatures at a distance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Every moving thing that lives would be food for them, as the green herb had been given before. The grant of animal food is new after the flood, but Henry warns it warrants use, not abuse by gluttony or cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. But flesh with its life, that is, its blood, they must not eat. Blood is set apart as the sign of life itself, and therefore not to be treated as ordinary food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God would require the blood of human life at the hand of every beast and of every man, even a brother. Human life is uniquely protected: its taking demands an accounting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Whoever sheds man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed, for in God's image man was made. The image of God, not mere usefulness, is the ground of the law against murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Noah's family is again told to be fruitful, multiply abundantly and increase on the earth. Repetition stresses the point: the world is to be repopulated, not left empty after judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. God spoke to Noah and his sons with him, establishing the covenant that follows. The covenant is addressed to the whole household, not to Noah alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The covenant is with Noah's sons and their seed after them. It reaches beyond the ark's generation to all their descendants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. It is also with every living creature with them: birds, cattle and beasts that came out of the ark. The covenant's scope is wider than humanity alone, covering the animal order preserved in the ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. God promises never again to cut off all flesh by flood waters, nor to destroy the earth by flood. This is a universal negative promise: whatever judgments come later, a flood will not again unmake the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. God said the bow in the cloud would be the token of the covenant between him and the earth. A visible sign accompanies the promise, so future generations can see and remember it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. God set his bow in the cloud, and it would be the token of the covenant between him and the earth. The bow, a weapon turned upward, is read by Henry as a sign of peace: war is over, and the sign hangs in the sky where storms gather. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. When God brings a cloud over the earth and the bow is seen, the sign appears precisely when fear would naturally arise. The token is given for stormy times, not only for fair weather. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. God will remember the everlasting covenant between himself and every living creature, and waters will no more become a flood to destroy all flesh. Remembering here means acting on the promise, as in chapter 8. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The bow in the cloud will be looked on to remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature. The sign is for God to remember in the sense of keeping, and for people to trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. God tells Noah this bow is the token of the covenant he has established between himself and all flesh on earth. The verse seals the covenant with a plain summary: sign, parties and promise all named together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The sons of Noah who went out of the ark were Shem, Ham and Japheth, and Ham is the father of Canaan. The genealogy now narrows: Ham's line will matter through Canaan in the story ahead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. These three sons of Noah are the ancestors from whom the whole earth was overspread. All post-flood humanity traces to this one household, which is why the flood story is a story of all peoples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Noah began to be a husbandman and planted a vineyard. After worship and covenant, ordinary work resumes: farming the renewed earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Noah drank of the wine and became drunk, and lay uncovered in his tent. Henry notes even a righteous man can fall through excess: Noah's drunkenness is recorded without excuse, as a warning, not as approval. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Ham, father of Canaan, saw his father's nakedness and told his two brothers outside. The fault is not merely seeing but exposing: Ham spreads his father's shame instead of covering it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Shem and Japheth took a garment, laid it on their shoulders, walked backward and covered their father, without looking. Their care is the opposite of Ham's: they protect Noah's dignity and refuse to gaze on his shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. When Noah awoke from his wine and learned what his younger son had done, he pronounced judgment. The story turns from the act to its consequence, which falls on Canaan, Ham's son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Noah said cursed be Canaan, a servant of servants shall he be to his brethren. The curse names Canaan, not Ham directly, and looks ahead to the later subjection of Canaan's descendants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Noah blessed the LORD, the God of Shem, and said Canaan shall be his servant. Shem's blessing is stated as God's blessing: the LORD is called Shem's God, marking the line through which God's people will come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. God shall enlarge Japheth, and he shall dwell in the tents of Shem, and Canaan shall be his servant. Japheth's enlargement and sharing in Shem's tents is read as the later inclusion of Japheth's peoples in the blessings of Shem's line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Noah lived after the flood 350 years. The post-flood lifespan shows Noah living long into the new world he helped repopulate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. All Noah's days were 950 years, and he died. The patriarch who survived the flood still dies, closing his life under the same sentence as Adam's line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages

  • Genesis 1 — The blessing on Noah renews the creation mandate of chapter 1 for a post-flood world. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 9:1, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Genesis 8 — God's remembering of the covenant is described as acting on the promise, as in chapter 8. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 9:15, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
