The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron, saying,

2 Take the sum of the sons of Kohath from among the sons of Levi, after their families, by the house of their fathers,

3 From thirty years old and upward even until fifty years old, all that enter into the host, to do the work in the tabernacle of the congregation.

4 This shall be the service of the sons of Kohath in the tabernacle of the congregation, about the most holy things:

5 And when the camp setteth forward, Aaron shall come, and his sons, and they shall take down the covering vail, and cover the ark of testimony with it:

6 And shall put thereon the covering of badgers’ skins, and shall spread over it a cloth wholly of blue, and shall put in the staves thereof.

7 And upon the table of shewbread they shall spread a cloth of blue, and put thereon the dishes, and the spoons, and the bowls, and covers to cover withal: and the continual bread shall be thereon:

8 And they shall spread upon them a cloth of scarlet, and cover the same with a covering of badgers’ skins, and shall put in the staves thereof.

9 And they shall take a cloth of blue, and cover the candlestick of the light, and his lamps, and his tongs, and his snuffdishes, and all the oil vessels thereof, wherewith they minister unto it:

10 And they shall put it and all the vessels thereof within a covering of badgers’ skins, and shall put it upon a bar.

11 And upon the golden altar they shall spread a cloth of blue, and cover it with a covering of badgers’ skins, and shall put to the staves thereof:

12 And they shall take all the instruments of ministry, wherewith they minister in the sanctuary, and put them in a cloth of blue, and cover them with a covering of badgers’ skins, and shall put them on a bar:

13 And they shall take away the ashes from the altar, and spread a purple cloth thereon:

14 And they shall put upon it all the vessels thereof, wherewith they minister about it, even the censers, the fleshhooks, and the shovels, and the basons, all the vessels of the altar; and they shall spread upon it a covering of badgers’ skins, and put to the staves of it.

15 And when Aaron and his sons have made an end of covering the sanctuary, and all the vessels of the sanctuary, as the camp is to set forward; after that, the sons of Kohath shall come to bear it: but they shall not touch any holy thing, lest they die. These things are the burden of the sons of Kohath in the tabernacle of the congregation.

16 And to the office of Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest pertaineth the oil for the light, and the sweet incense, and the daily meat offering, and the anointing oil, and the oversight of all the tabernacle, and of all that therein is, in the sanctuary, and in the vessels thereof.

17 And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron saying,

18 Cut ye not off the tribe of the families of the Kohathites from among the Levites:

19 But thus do unto them, that they may live, and not die, when they approach unto the most holy things: Aaron and his sons shall go in, and appoint them every one to his service and to his burden:

20 But they shall not go in to see when the holy things are covered, lest they die.

21 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

22 Take also the sum of the sons of Gershon, throughout the houses of their fathers, by their families;

23 From thirty years old and upward until fifty years old shalt thou number them; all that enter in to perform the service, to do the work in the tabernacle of the congregation.

24 This is the service of the families of the Gershonites, to serve, and for burdens:

25 And they shall bear the curtains of the tabernacle, and the tabernacle of the congregation, his covering, and the covering of the badgers’ skins that is above upon it, and the hanging for the door of the tabernacle of the congregation,

26 And the hangings of the court, and the hanging for the door of the gate of the court, which is by the tabernacle and by the altar round about, and their cords, and all the instruments of their service, and all that is made for them: so shall they serve.

27 At the appointment of Aaron and his sons shall be all the service of the sons of the Gershonites, in all their burdens, and in all their service: and ye shall appoint unto them in charge all their burdens.

28 This is the service of the families of the sons of Gershon in the tabernacle of the congregation: and their charge shall be under the hand of Ithamar the son of Aaron the priest.

29 As for the sons of Merari, thou shalt number them after their families, by the house of their fathers;

30 From thirty years old and upward even unto fifty years old shalt thou number them, every one that entereth into the service, to do the work of the tabernacle of the congregation.

31 And this is the charge of their burden, according to all their service in the tabernacle of the congregation; the boards of the tabernacle, and the bars thereof, and the pillars thereof, and sockets thereof,

32 And the pillars of the court round about, and their sockets, and their pins, and their cords, with all their instruments, and with all their service: and by name ye shall reckon the instruments of the charge of their burden.

33 This is the service of the families of the sons of Merari, according to all their service, in the tabernacle of the congregation, under the hand of Ithamar the son of Aaron the priest.

34 And Moses and Aaron and the chief of the congregation numbered the sons of the Kohathites after their families, and after the house of their fathers,

35 From thirty years old and upward even unto fifty years old, every one that entereth into the service, for the work in the tabernacle of the congregation:

36 And those that were numbered of them by their families were two thousand seven hundred and fifty.

37 These were they that were numbered of the families of the Kohathites, all that might do service in the tabernacle of the congregation, which Moses and Aaron did number according to the commandment of the LORD by the hand of Moses.

38 And those that were numbered of the sons of Gershon, throughout their families, and by the house of their fathers,

39 From thirty years old and upward even unto fifty years old, every one that entereth into the service, for the work in the tabernacle of the congregation,

40 Even those that were numbered of them, throughout their families, by the house of their fathers, were two thousand and six hundred and thirty.

41 These are they that were numbered of the families of the sons of Gershon, of all that might do service in the tabernacle of the congregation, whom Moses and Aaron did number according to the commandment of the LORD.

42 And those that were numbered of the families of the sons of Merari, throughout their families, by the house of their fathers,

43 From thirty years old and upward even unto fifty years old, every one that entereth into the service, for the work in the tabernacle of the congregation,

44 Even those that were numbered of them after their families, were three thousand and two hundred.

45 These be those that were numbered of the families of the sons of Merari, whom Moses and Aaron numbered according to the word of the LORD by the hand of Moses.

46 All those that were numbered of the Levites, whom Moses and Aaron and the chief of Israel numbered, after their families, and after the house of their fathers,

47 From thirty years old and upward even unto fifty years old, every one that came to do the service of the ministry, and the service of the burden in the tabernacle of the congregation.

48 Even those that were numbered of them, were eight thousand and five hundred and fourscore,

49 According to the commandment of the LORD they were numbered by the hand of Moses, every one according to his service, and according to his burden: thus were they numbered of him, as the LORD commanded Moses.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter numbers the Levites for active service, from thirty years old to fifty, and assigns each family its burden. The Kohathites carry the most holy things, but Aaron and his sons must first cover the ark, table, candlestick, and altars, and no Kohathite may touch or look on the holy things lest he die. The Gershonites carry the curtains, coverings, and hangings, and the Merarites carry the boards, bars, pillars, and sockets, each man numbered by name according to his service and burden. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. The sons of Kohath were summed by families and fathers' houses for the work of the tabernacle. The census here is not for war but for service: Levi's host is counted for the warfare of worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Only men from thirty years old to fifty, entering into the host to do the work, were counted for service. Henry's rule is that the service of God requires the best of our strength and the prime portion of our time, and should be done when we are most lively and active, not put off to old age. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. This was the service of the Kohathites: the most holy things. Theirs was the highest charge among the Levites, carrying what no other family might carry, and therefore hedged with the strictest reverence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. When the camp set forward, Aaron and his sons took down the covering veil and covered the ark of testimony with it. The priests covered first; the Kohathites never saw the holy things uncovered, which marked both reverence for holy things and the mystery of the types under a dark dispensation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The table of shewbread was spread with blue cloth and furnished with its dishes, spoons, bowls and covers, with the continual bread left thereon. Even in transit the table kept its character: provision before the Lord was never interrupted by the march. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The candlestick of the light was covered with blue cloth, with its lamps and vessels. The light of the sanctuary travelled veiled, seen by none on the road, a sign that the full light of the gospel was not yet unveiled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The golden altar was spread with blue cloth and covered with badgers' skins. Altar, table and lampstand all travelled in the same guarded way: holy things were carried, not displayed, and handled only after priestly covering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The ashes were taken away from the altar and a purple cloth spread on it. The altar was thus prepared for travel as a clean, covered thing, its service paused in order, not abandoned in haste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. All the altar's vessels, wherewith they ministered about it, were put upon it under the covering. The completeness matters: nothing belonging to the altar was left to travel loose or unguarded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. When Aaron and his sons finished covering the sanctuary, the Kohathites came to bear it, but they might not touch any holy thing, lest they die. Henry notes the case of Uzza, struck dead for breaching this law: even those who bore the vessels of the Lord were kept in fear under that dispensation of terror. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. To Eleazar, now Aaron's eldest son, pertained the oil for the light, the sweet incense, the daily meat offering and the anointing oil, with oversight of all the tabernacle. Henry observes that as a priest he had more honour than the Levites, but also more care, a heavier burden on his heart than all the burdens on their shoulders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The Lord warned: cut not off the tribe of the Kohathite families from among the Levites. Henry's reading is that those who do not what they can to keep others from sin do what they can to cut them off, so the warning is itself a guard for their lives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Aaron and his sons were to act so that the Kohathites might live and not die when approaching the most holy things, assigning every man his service and burden. The rule protected life by assigning order: each man knew exactly what he might touch and what he might not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The Kohathites might not go in to see when the holy things were covered, lest they die. Henry notes they saw not what they bore, so much were even those who expounded the law in the dark concerning the gospel; now, through Christ, believers are encouraged to come boldly to the throne of grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The sons of Gershon were also summed by their fathers' houses, for the same service age, thirty to fifty. The second family is counted with the same care as the first: no family of Levi was left unordered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Gershon's men, from thirty to fifty, entered the host to do the work in the tabernacle. The age bounds applied to every Levitical family alike, since the work required full strength, whether the burden was heavy or light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. This was the service of the Gershonites, to serve and for burdens. Their charge, though less honourable than the Kohathites', was necessary and to be done regularly, and Henry notes all things were delivered to them by name, intimating God's care for his church and every member of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The Gershonites bore the curtains of the tabernacle, the tabernacle of the congregation, its covering and the badgers' skins above it. Their burden was the soft fabric of the dwelling, the shelter under which the holy things rested. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. All the service of the Gershonites was at the appointment of Aaron and his sons, and their burden was in their charge. Even a lesser charge was not self-directed: the priests assigned it, and the Levites bore what was assigned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. This was the service of the Gershonite families under Ithamar, son of Aaron. Each family had its priestly overseer, so that the work of carrying the tabernacle was supervised as carefully as the work within it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. The sons of Merari were numbered after their families and fathers' houses. The third family completes the pattern: every son of Levi had his appointed place in the moving sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Merari's men were numbered from thirty to fifty, every one entering the host to do the service. The same prime of life was required for the heaviest burden as for the lightest, since all of it was the Lord's work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Merari's charge was the boards of the tabernacle, its bars, pillars and sockets. Their burden was the solid framework, the heaviest and least showy part, without which curtains and coverings had nothing to stand on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The pillars of the court, their sockets, pins and cords were also Merari's charge. Court as well as tabernacle travelled in their hands: the whole enclosure of worship, not only its inner rooms, was carried in order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. This was the service of the Merarite families, under the hand of Ithamar, son of Aaron. Like Gershon, Merari worked under Aaron's younger son, a division of oversight that kept the whole camp's labour accountable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Moses, Aaron and the chief of the congregation numbered the Kohathites. The counting was a public, shared act, witnessed by the congregation's chiefs, not a private reckoning of Moses alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The Kohathites numbered were those from thirty to fifty entering the service of the tabernacle. The age rule held at the count as at the command: only men in their prime strength were enrolled for the host of the sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. The Kohathites numbered by their families were two thousand seven hundred and fifty. Henry notes they were eight thousand six hundred in all from a month old, so fewer than a third were serviceable men, a reminder that many add to the numbers of the church while comparatively few contribute to its service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. The Gershonites were numbered throughout their families and fathers' houses. Their enrolment followed the same pattern as Kohath's, family by family, so that every burden-bearer was known and assigned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. The Gershonites numbered were two thousand six hundred and thirty, from seven thousand five hundred in all, little more than a third serviceable. The proportion matches Kohath's: the working strength of the tribe was always a minority of its numbers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. The Merarites were numbered after their families, completing the enrolment of the three Levitical houses. Each family's serviceable men were counted separately, so that every burden of the tabernacle had its own assigned bearers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. The Merarites numbered after their families were three thousand and two hundred. Henry marvels at God's ordering: though Merari was the fewest in all, only six thousand two hundred, yet more than half were serviceable men, because the greatest burden lay on them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. All the Levites numbered by Moses, Aaron and the chiefs of Israel were eight thousand five hundred and eighty. Henry contrasts this with the other tribes: the least of them had almost four times as many able men, since those engaged in the world's service are many more than those devoted to God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. The total of the serviceable Levites was eight thousand five hundred and eighty, every man from thirty to fifty coming to do the service of the tabernacle and the service of burden. The sum closes the chapter as a complete host, small beside Israel's army, yet sufficient for the sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. They were numbered by the hand of Moses according to the Lord's commandment, every one according to his service and burden. Henry's closing lesson is that whatever service God calls men to, he will furnish them for it and give strength in proportion to the work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
