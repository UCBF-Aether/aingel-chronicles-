The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the children of Israel set forward, and pitched in the plains of Moab on this side Jordan by Jericho.

2 And Balak the son of Zippor saw all that Israel had done to the Amorites.

3 And Moab was sore afraid of the people, because they were many: and Moab was distressed because of the children of Israel.

4 And Moab said unto the elders of Midian, Now shall this company lick up all that are round about us, as the ox licketh up the grass of the field. And Balak the son of Zippor was king of the Moabites at that time.

5 He sent messengers therefore unto Balaam the son of Beor to Pethor, which is by the river of the land of the children of his people, to call him, saying, Behold, there is a people come out from Egypt: behold, they cover the face of the earth, and they abide over against me:

6 Come now therefore, I pray thee, curse me this people; for they are too mighty for me: peradventure I shall prevail, that we may smite them, and that I may drive them out of the land: for I wot that he whom thou blessest is blessed, and he whom thou cursest is cursed.

7 And the elders of Moab and the elders of Midian departed with the rewards of divination in their hand; and they came unto Balaam, and spake unto him the words of Balak.

8 And he said unto them, Lodge here this night, and I will bring you word again, as the LORD shall speak unto me: and the princes of Moab abode with Balaam.

9 And God came unto Balaam, and said, What men are these with thee?

10 And Balaam said unto God, Balak the son of Zippor, king of Moab, hath sent unto me, saying,

11 Behold, there is a people come out of Egypt, which covereth the face of the earth: come now, curse me them; peradventure I shall be able to overcome them, and drive them out.

12 And God said unto Balaam, Thou shalt not go with them; thou shalt not curse the people: for they are blessed.

13 And Balaam rose up in the morning, and said unto the princes of Balak, Get you into your land: for the LORD refuseth to give me leave to go with you.

14 And the princes of Moab rose up, and they went unto Balak, and said, Balaam refuseth to come with us.

15 And Balak sent yet again princes, more, and more honourable than they.

16 And they came to Balaam, and said to him, Thus saith Balak the son of Zippor, Let nothing, I pray thee, hinder thee from coming unto me:

17 For I will promote thee unto very great honour, and I will do whatsoever thou sayest unto me: come therefore, I pray thee, curse me this people.

18 And Balaam answered and said unto the servants of Balak, If Balak would give me his house full of silver and gold, I cannot go beyond the word of the LORD my God, to do less or more.

19 Now therefore, I pray you, tarry ye also here this night, that I may know what the LORD will say unto me more.

20 And God came unto Balaam at night, and said unto him, If the men come to call thee, rise up, and go with them; but yet the word which I shall say unto thee, that shalt thou do.

21 And Balaam rose up in the morning, and saddled his ass, and went with the princes of Moab.

22 And God’s anger was kindled because he went: and the angel of the LORD stood in the way for an adversary against him. Now he was riding upon his ass, and his two servants were with him.

23 And the ass saw the angel of the LORD standing in the way, and his sword drawn in his hand: and the ass turned aside out of the way, and went into the field: and Balaam smote the ass, to turn her into the way.

24 But the angel of the LORD stood in a path of the vineyards, a wall being on this side, and a wall on that side.

25 And when the ass saw the angel of the LORD, she thrust herself unto the wall, and crushed Balaam’s foot against the wall: and he smote her again.

26 And the angel of the LORD went further, and stood in a narrow place, where was no way to turn either to the right hand or to the left.

27 And when the ass saw the angel of the LORD, she fell down under Balaam: and Balaam’s anger was kindled, and he smote the ass with a staff.

28 And the LORD opened the mouth of the ass, and she said unto Balaam, What have I done unto thee, that thou hast smitten me these three times?

29 And Balaam said unto the ass, Because thou hast mocked me: I would there were a sword in mine hand, for now would I kill thee.

30 And the ass said unto Balaam, Am not I thine ass, upon which thou hast ridden ever since I was thine unto this day? was I ever wont to do so unto thee? And he said, Nay.

31 Then the LORD opened the eyes of Balaam, and he saw the angel of the LORD standing in the way, and his sword drawn in his hand: and he bowed down his head, and fell flat on his face.

32 And the angel of the LORD said unto him, Wherefore hast thou smitten thine ass these three times? behold, I went out to withstand thee, because thy way is perverse before me:

33 And the ass saw me, and turned from me these three times: unless she had turned from me, surely now also I had slain thee, and saved her alive.

34 And Balaam said unto the angel of the LORD, I have sinned; for I knew not that thou stoodest in the way against me: now therefore, if it displease thee, I will get me back again.

35 And the angel of the LORD said unto Balaam, Go with the men: but only the word that I shall speak unto thee, that thou shalt speak. So Balaam went with the princes of Balak.

36 And when Balak heard that Balaam was come, he went out to meet him unto a city of Moab, which is in the border of Arnon, which is in the utmost coast.

37 And Balak said unto Balaam, Did I not earnestly send unto thee to call thee? wherefore camest thou not unto me? am I not able indeed to promote thee to honour?

38 And Balaam said unto Balak, Lo, I am come unto thee: have I now any power at all to say any thing? the word that God putteth in my mouth, that shall I speak.

39 And Balaam went with Balak, and they came unto Kirjathhuzoth.

40 And Balak offered oxen and sheep, and sent to Balaam, and to the princes that were with him.

41 And it came to pass on the morrow, that Balak took Balaam, and brought him up into the high places of Baal, that thence he might see the utmost part of the people.

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter brings Israel to the plains of Moab, across the Jordan from Jericho, where Balak king of Moab, fearing the host that has defeated Sihon and Og, sends for Balaam to curse them. Balaam first refuses at God's word, then goes with Balak's princes after a second message, but on the way his donkey sees the angel of the LORD standing in the path with a drawn sword, turns aside three times, and finally speaks to Balaam. The angel confronts him, Balaam confesses he has sinned, and he is told to go on but to speak only the word God puts in his mouth. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Israel set forward and pitched in the plains of Moab on this side Jordan by Jericho. The host now lay across the Jordan from Jericho, within sight of the land, and Moab, watching from its plains, saw the conquerors of Sihon and Og encamped on its border. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Balak son of Zippor saw all that Israel had done to the Amorites. The sight of two Amorite kingdoms fallen was Balak's motive: Moab's king measured Israel by what it had done to his neighbours, and feared he would be next. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Moab was sore afraid of the people, because they were many, and Moab was distressed because of the children of Israel. Fear, not grievance, drove Moab: Israel had done Moab no wrong, but its numbers alone distressed a smaller kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Moab said to the elders of Midian, now shall this company lick up all that are round about us, as the ox licks up the grass of the field. The image is of total consumption: Moab joined with Midian, its old neighbour, to find a weapon against a host it pictured as devouring everything around it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He sent messengers to Balaam son of Beor, to Pethor by the river of the land of the children of his people, to call him. Balak sent a great way for the prophet of greatest reputation, since his plan needed not an army but a curse: to set God against a people for whom God had hitherto fought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Come now therefore, I pray you, curse me this people, for they are too mighty for me: peradventure I shall prevail, that we may smite them, and drive them out of the land, for I know that he whom you bless is blessed, and he whom you curse is cursed. Henry notes Balak's false notion: that if a prophet prayed evil on Israel and blessing on Moab's forces, he could then deal with them, as if God's favour could be hired by a curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The elders of Moab and Midian departed with the rewards of divination in their hand, and came to Balaam and spoke Balak's words to him. The wages of unrighteousness travelled with the message: Balak paid in advance for the curse he wanted, and the elders carried the fee openly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He said to them, lodge here this night, and I will bring you word again, as the Lord shall speak to me, and the princes of Moab abode with Balaam. Henry notes Balaam ought to have answered at once that he would never curse a people God had blessed, but he took a night to consider, and when we parley with temptations we are in great danger of being overcome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. God came to Balaam and said, what men are these with you? The question exposed the embassy: God asked not for information but to bring Balaam to own the company he was keeping and the errand he was entertaining. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Balaam said to God, Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab, has sent to me, saying, behold, there is a people come out of Egypt, which covers the face of the earth. Balaam reported the embassy faithfully in its facts, yet without the refusal that knowledge of Israel's cause should have produced at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Behold, there is a people come out of Egypt, which covers the face of the earth: come now, curse me them, peradventure I shall be able to overcome them and drive them out. The message as Balaam relayed it made Israel's multitude the very reason for cursing: a people covering the earth was, to Balak, a people to be shrunk by a prophet's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. God said to Balaam, you shall not go with them, you shall not curse the people, for they are blessed. The restraint was absolute and grounded in blessing: a people God had blessed could not be cursed, whatever fee was offered or embassy sent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Balaam rose up in the morning and said to Balak's princes, get into your land, for the Lord refuses to give me leave to go with you. Henry notes Balaam was not faithful in returning God's answer: he lessened the divine restraint, as if going against God's law were only going without his leave, instead of reporting the flat prohibition and the blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The princes of Moab rose up, went to Balak, and said, Balaam refuses to come with us. The messengers were likewise unfaithful, reporting a refusal rather than God's prohibition, so that Balak was abused by flattery and prevented from seeing the fault in his own design. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Balak sent yet again princes, more, and more honourable than they. The second embassy was grander than the first, as if honour could succeed where a fee had failed, and Henry notes it were well if we were as earnest in a good work, notwithstanding disappointments, as Balak was in this evil one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. They came to Balaam and said, thus says Balak son of Zippor, let nothing, I pray you, hinder you from coming to me. The urgency grew with the embassy: Balak would let nothing hinder, neither God's refusal nor Balaam's reluctance, and pressed the prophet as if refusal were merely a matter of persuasion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. For I will promote you to very great honour, and I will do whatsoever you say to me: come therefore, I pray you, curse me this people. Henry notes Balak laid a bait not only for covetousness but for pride and ambition, and sinners stick at no pains, spare no cost, and care not how low they stoop, to gratify malice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Balaam answered the servants of Balak, if Balak would give me his house full of silver and gold, I cannot go beyond the word of the Lord my God, to do less or more. Henry notes no man could have spoken better, yet words do not judge a man: Balaam's convictions charged him to keep God's command, while his corruptions inclined him to go contrary to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Now therefore, I pray you, tarry you also here this night, that I may know what the Lord will speak to me more. Henry's verdict is sharp: he seemed to refuse the temptation but expressed no abhorrence of it, having a strong desire to accept and hoping God might give him leave, and it is certain evidence of corruption ruling the heart to beg leave to sin after already being told God's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. God came to Balaam at night and said, if the men come to call you, rise up and go with them, but yet the word which I shall say to you, that shall you do. Henry explains God gave Balaam up to his own heart's lusts: as God sometimes denies his people's prayers in love, so sometimes he grants the wicked's desires in wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Balaam rose up in the morning, saddled his ass, and went with the princes of Moab. The eagerness of the rising and saddling shows which way his heart already leaned: permission was taken as encouragement, and the journey began at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. God's anger was kindled because he went, and the angel of the Lord stood in the way for an adversary against him, while he rode upon his ass with two servants. Henry warns we must not think God's providence in not restraining men from sin means he approves it: the holy angels oppose sin, and this angel was an adversary to Balaam precisely because Balaam counted him an adversary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The ass saw the angel of the Lord standing in the way, his sword drawn in his hand, and the ass turned aside out of the way and went into the field, and Balaam smote the ass to turn her into the way. The beast saw what the prophet could not: the drawn sword in the road, and her turning was the first mercy that stopped his progress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. But the angel of the Lord stood in a path of the vineyards, a wall on this side and a wall on that side. The second standing narrowed the way: where the open field had allowed escape, the vineyard walls shut the prophet in with the angel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. When the ass saw the angel of the Lord, she thrust herself to the wall and crushed Balaam's foot against the wall, and he smote her again. The crush was the second warning, costing Balaam pain, yet he answered it with a second beating, pushing on violently through the difficulties Providence laid in his way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The angel of the Lord went further and stood in a narrow place, where there was no way to turn either to the right hand or to the left. The third standing left no escape at all: the prophet's road was now a corridor with the angel at its end, and the beast could go neither way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. When the ass saw the angel of the Lord, she fell down under Balaam, and Balaam's anger was kindled, and he smote the ass with a staff. The falling was the beast's last resort, and Balaam's anger, kindled against the creature that had saved him three times, shows a heart fully set on evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The Lord opened the mouth of the ass, and she said to Balaam, what have I done to you, that you have smitten me these three times? Henry notes this great miracle was wrought by God's power: he who made man speak could, when he pleased, make the ass speak with man's voice, and the ass complained of Balaam's cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Balaam said to the ass, because you have mocked me: I would there were a sword in my hand, for now would I kill you. The prophet, who could not see the angel's sword, wished for a sword of his own to kill the beast that had seen it, a blindness Henry holds up as the picture of a hard heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The ass said to Balaam, am not I your ass, upon which you have ridden ever since I was yours to this day? Was I ever wont to do so to you? And he said, nay. Henry notes the righteous God does not allow the meanest or weakest to be abused, but they shall speak in their own defence, or he will somehow speak for them, and the beast's faithful record condemned its rider. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Then the Lord opened the eyes of Balaam, and he saw the angel of the Lord standing in the way, and his sword drawn in his hand, and he bowed down his head and fell flat on his face. Henry notes God has many ways to bring down the hard and unhumbled heart, and when our eyes are opened we see the danger of sinful ways and how much it was for our advantage to be crossed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The angel of the Lord said to him, wherefore have you smitten your ass these three times? Behold, I went out to withstand you, because your way is perverse before me. The three smitings were now answered by three turnings: the beast had turned from the angel three times, and each turning had been punished by its rider. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And the ass saw me, and turned from me these three times: unless she had turned from me, surely now also I had slain you, and saved her alive. The angel's sentence reversed Balaam's values: the beast he would have killed had saved his life, and the prophet he counted himself stood condemned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Balaam said to the angel of the Lord, I have sinned, for I knew not that you stood in the way against me: now therefore, if it displease you, I will get me back again. Henry notes Balaam seemed to relent, but it does not appear he was sensible of his heart's wickedness: if he cannot go forward, he will be content to go back, as many leave their sins only because their sins have left them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The angel of the Lord said to Balaam, go with the men, but only the word that I shall speak to you, that you shall speak, and Balaam went with the princes of Balak. The permission continued, but with a sterner condition: he should not only be unable to curse Israel, but forced to bless them, more for God's glory and his own confusion than if he had turned back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. When Balak heard that Balaam was come, he went out to meet him to a city of Moab, which is in the border of Arnon, at the utmost coast. Balak travelled to his own border to meet the prophet, honouring the man he had hired before a single word had been spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Balak said to Balaam, did I not earnestly send to you to call you? Wherefore came you not to me? Am I not able indeed to promote you to honour? Balak's complaint was only that Balaam had not come sooner: the king who had been refused by God now reproached the prophet for delay, as if honour were the only question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Balaam said to Balak, lo, I am come to you: have I now any power at all to say anything? The word that God puts in my mouth, that shall I speak. Henry notes Balaam bids Balak not depend too much on him, speaking with seeming vexation, yet really as desirous to please Balak as ever he had pretended to be to please God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Balaam went with Balak, and they came to Kirjath-huzoth. The prophet travelled on with the king to the city of streets, where the sacrifices of welcome would prepare the way for the attempted curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Balak offered oxen and sheep, and sent to Balaam, and to the princes that were with him. The offerings feasted prophet and princes together, binding Balaam to Balak's table before he was taken to the high places to look on Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. It came to pass on the morrow, that Balak took Balaam, and brought him up into the high places of Baal, that thence he might see the utmost part of the people. The high places of Baal were chosen for the view: from there Balaam could see Israel's utmost part, and the curse was to be aimed at a people spread before his eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 22. God's anger was kindled because he went, and the angel of the Lord stood in the way for an adversary against him, while he rode upon his ass with two servants. Henry warns we must not think God's providence in not restraining men from sin means he approves it: the holy angels oppose sin, and this angel was an adversary to Balaam precisely because Balaam counted him an adversary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The ass saw the angel of the Lord standing in the way, his sword drawn in his hand, and the ass turned aside out of the way and went into the field, and Balaam smote the ass to turn her into the way. The beast saw what the prophet could not: the drawn sword in the road, and her turning was the first mercy that stopped his progress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. But the angel of the Lord stood in a path of the vineyards, a wall on this side and a wall on that side. The second standing narrowed the way: where the open field had allowed escape, the vineyard walls shut the prophet in with the angel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. When the ass saw the angel of the Lord, she thrust herself to the wall and crushed Balaam's foot against the wall, and he smote her again. The crush was the second warning, costing Balaam pain, yet he answered it with a second beating, pushing on violently through the difficulties Providence laid in his way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The angel of the Lord went further and stood in a narrow place, where there was no way to turn either to the right hand or to the left. The third standing left no escape at all: the prophet's road was now a corridor with the angel at its end, and the beast could go neither way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. When the ass saw the angel of the Lord, she fell down under Balaam, and Balaam's anger was kindled, and he smote the ass with a staff. The falling was the beast's last resort, and Balaam's anger, kindled against the creature that had saved him three times, shows a heart fully set on evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The Lord opened the mouth of the ass, and she said to Balaam, what have I done to you, that you have smitten me these three times? Henry notes this great miracle was wrought by God's power: he who made man speak could, when he pleased, make the ass speak with man's voice, and the ass complained of Balaam's cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Balaam said to the ass, because you have mocked me: I would there were a sword in my hand, for now would I kill you. The prophet, who could not see the angel's sword, wished for a sword of his own to kill the beast that had seen it, a blindness Henry holds up as the picture of a hard heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The ass said to Balaam, am not I your ass, upon which you have ridden ever since I was yours to this day? Was I ever wont to do so to you? And he said, nay. Henry notes the righteous God does not allow the meanest or weakest to be abused, but they shall speak in their own defence, or he will somehow speak for them, and the beast's faithful record condemned its rider. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Then the Lord opened the eyes of Balaam, and he saw the angel of the Lord standing in the way, and his sword drawn in his hand, and he bowed down his head and fell flat on his face. Henry notes God has many ways to bring down the hard and unhumbled heart, and when our eyes are opened we see the danger of sinful ways and how much it was for our advantage to be crossed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The angel of the Lord said to him, wherefore have you smitten your ass these three times? Behold, I went out to withstand you, because your way is perverse before me. The three smitings were now answered by three turnings: the beast had turned from the angel three times, and each turning had been punished by its rider. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And the ass saw me, and turned from me these three times: unless she had turned from me, surely now also I had slain you, and saved her alive. The angel's sentence reversed Balaam's values: the beast he would have killed had saved his life, and the prophet he counted himself stood condemned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Balaam said to the angel of the Lord, I have sinned, for I knew not that you stood in the way against me: now therefore, if it displease you, I will get me back again. Henry notes Balaam seemed to relent, but it does not appear he was sensible of his heart's wickedness: if he cannot go forward, he will be content to go back, as many leave their sins only because their sins have left them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The angel of the Lord said to Balaam, go with the men, but only the word that I shall speak to you, that you shall speak, and Balaam went with the princes of Balak. The permission continued, but with a sterner condition: he should not only be unable to curse Israel, but forced to bless them, more for God's glory and his own confusion than if he had turned back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. When Balak heard that Balaam was come, he went out to meet him to a city of Moab, which is in the border of Arnon, at the utmost coast. Balak travelled to his own border to meet the prophet, honouring the man he had hired before a single word had been spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Balak said to Balaam, did I not earnestly send to you to call you? Wherefore came you not to me? Am I not able indeed to promote you to honour? Balak's complaint was only that Balaam had not come sooner: the king who had been refused by God now reproached the prophet for delay, as if honour were the only question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Balaam said to Balak, lo, I am come to you: have I now any power at all to say anything? The word that God puts in my mouth, that shall I speak. Henry notes Balaam bids Balak not depend too much on him, speaking with seeming vexation, yet really as desirous to please Balak as ever he had pretended to be to please God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Balaam went with Balak, and they came to Kirjath-huzoth. The prophet travelled on with the king to the city of streets, where the sacrifices of welcome would prepare the way for the attempted curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Balak offered oxen and sheep, and sent to Balaam, and to the princes that were with him. The offerings feasted prophet and princes together, binding Balaam to Balak's table before he was taken to the high places to look on Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. It came to pass on the morrow, that Balak took Balaam, and brought him up into the high places of Baal, that thence he might see the utmost part of the people. The high places of Baal were chosen for the view: from there Balaam could see Israel's utmost part, and the curse was to be aimed at a people spread before his eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
