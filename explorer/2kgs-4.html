The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now there cried a certain woman of the wives of the sons of the prophets unto Elisha, saying, Thy servant my husband is dead; and thou knowest that thy servant did fear the LORD: and the creditor is come to take unto him my two sons to be bondmen.

2 And Elisha said unto her, What shall I do for thee? tell me, what hast thou in the house? And she said, Thine handmaid hath not any thing in the house, save a pot of oil.

3 Then he said, Go, borrow thee vessels abroad of all thy neighbours, even empty vessels; borrow not a few.

4 And when thou art come in, thou shalt shut the door upon thee and upon thy sons, and shalt pour out into all those vessels, and thou shalt set aside that which is full.

5 So she went from him, and shut the door upon her and upon her sons, who brought the vessels to her; and she poured out.

6 And it came to pass, when the vessels were full, that she said unto her son, Bring me yet a vessel. And he said unto her, There is not a vessel more. And the oil stayed.

7 Then she came and told the man of God. And he said, Go, sell the oil, and pay thy debt, and live thou and thy children of the rest.

8 And it fell on a day, that Elisha passed to Shunem, where was a great woman; and she constrained him to eat bread. And so it was, that as oft as he passed by, he turned in thither to eat bread.

9 And she said unto her husband, Behold now, I perceive that this is an holy man of God, which passeth by us continually.

10 Let us make a little chamber, I pray thee, on the wall; and let us set for him there a bed, and a table, and a stool, and a candlestick: and it shall be, when he cometh to us, that he shall turn in thither.

11 And it fell on a day, that he came thither, and he turned into the chamber, and lay there.

12 And he said to Gehazi his servant, Call this Shunammite. And when he had called her, she stood before him.

13 And he said unto him, Say now unto her, Behold, thou hast been careful for us with all this care; what is to be done for thee? wouldest thou be spoken for to the king, or to the captain of the host? And she answered, I dwell among mine own people.

14 And he said, What then is to be done for her? And Gehazi answered, Verily she hath no child, and her husband is old.

15 And he said, Call her. And when he had called her, she stood in the door.

16 And he said, About this season, according to the time of life, thou shalt embrace a son. And she said, Nay, my lord, thou man of God, do not lie unto thine handmaid.

17 And the woman conceived, and bare a son at that season that Elisha had said unto her, according to the time of life.

18 And when the child was grown, it fell on a day, that he went out to his father to the reapers.

19 And he said unto his father, My head, my head. And he said to a lad, Carry him to his mother.

20 And when he had taken him, and brought him to his mother, he sat on her knees till noon, and then died.

21 And she went up, and laid him on the bed of the man of God, and shut the door upon him, and went out.

22 And she called unto her husband, and said, Send me, I pray thee, one of the young men, and one of the asses, that I may run to the man of God, and come again.

23 And he said, Wherefore wilt thou go to him to day? it is neither new moon, nor sabbath. And she said, It shall be well.

24 Then she saddled an ass, and said to her servant, Drive, and go forward; slack not thy riding for me, except I bid thee.

25 So she went and came unto the man of God to mount Carmel. And it came to pass, when the man of God saw her afar off, that he said to Gehazi his servant, Behold, yonder is that Shunammite:

26 Run now, I pray thee, to meet her, and say unto her, Is it well with thee? is it well with thy husband? is it well with the child? And she answered, It is well:

27 And when she came to the man of God to the hill, she caught him by the feet: but Gehazi came near to thrust her away. And the man of God said, Let her alone; for her soul is vexed within her: and the LORD hath hid it from me, and hath not told me.

28 Then she said, Did I desire a son of my lord? did I not say, Do not deceive me?

29 Then he said to Gehazi, Gird up thy loins, and take my staff in thine hand, and go thy way: if thou meet any man, salute him not; and if any salute thee, answer him not again: and lay my staff upon the face of the child.

30 And the mother of the child said, As the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee. And he arose, and followed her.

31 And Gehazi passed on before them, and laid the staff upon the face of the child; but there was neither voice, nor hearing. Wherefore he went again to meet him, and told him, saying, The child is not awaked.

32 And when Elisha was come into the house, behold, the child was dead, and laid upon his bed.

33 He went in therefore, and shut the door upon them twain, and prayed unto the LORD.

34 And he went up, and lay upon the child, and put his mouth upon his mouth, and his eyes upon his eyes, and his hands upon his hands: and stretched himself upon the child; and the flesh of the child waxed warm.

35 Then he returned, and walked in the house to and fro; and went up, and stretched himself upon him: and the child sneezed seven times, and the child opened his eyes.

36 And he called Gehazi, and said, Call this Shunammite. So he called her. And when she was come in unto him, he said, Take up thy son.

37 Then she went in, and fell at his feet, and bowed herself to the ground, and took up her son, and went out.

38 And Elisha came again to Gilgal: and there was a dearth in the land; and the sons of the prophets were sitting before him: and he said unto his servant, Set on the great pot, and seethe pottage for the sons of the prophets.

39 And one went out into the field to gather herbs, and found a wild vine, and gathered thereof wild gourds his lap full, and came and shred them into the pot of pottage: for they knew them not.

40 So they poured out for the men to eat. And it came to pass, as they were eating of the pottage, that they cried out, and said, O thou man of God, there is death in the pot. And they could not eat thereof.

41 But he said, Then bring meal. And he cast it into the pot; and he said, Pour out for the people, that they may eat. And there was no harm in the pot.

42 And there came a man from Baalshalisha, and brought the man of God bread of the firstfruits, twenty loaves of barley, and full ears of corn in the husk thereof. And he said, Give unto the people, that they may eat.

43 And his servitor said, What, should I set this before an hundred men? He said again, Give the people, that they may eat: for thus saith the LORD, They shall eat, and shall leave thereof.

44 So he set it before them, and they did eat, and left thereof, according to the word of the LORD.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Elisha's miracles of provision and life fill the chapter: oil multiplied for a widow, a son promised and then raised for the Shunammite, poisoned pottage made harmless, and a hundred men fed with twenty loaves. It is Elisha as prophet of the household as much as of the court. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. A widow of a prophet's wife cried to Elisha that her husband, who feared the LORD, was dead, and the creditor had come to take her two sons as bondmen. Henry notes those who leave families under debt know not what trouble they cause, and Elisha readily received the poor widow's complaint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Elisha asked what she had in the house, and she answered nothing save a pot of oil. Henry presents this as the small beginning from which God would supply her, showing the best way to help the distressed is to help them improve by industry what little they have. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Elisha told her to borrow empty vessels from all her neighbours, and borrow not a few. Henry notes the oil would flow as long as she had vessels to receive it: we are never straitened in God, all our straitness is in ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. She was to shut the door on herself and her sons and pour out into all the vessels, setting aside those that were full. Henry observes the miracle was worked so as to show industry joined to God's supply. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. She did so, her sons bringing vessels to her while she poured out. Henry's lesson is that faith does not fail God's promise; he gives more than we ask, and were there more vessels, there is enough in God to fill them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. When the vessels were full and her son said there was no more vessel, the oil stayed. Henry notes the Redeemer's all-sufficiency is only stayed from supplying sinners when no more apply to him for salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Elisha told her to sell the oil, pay her debt, and live with her sons on the rest. Henry stresses that though her creditors were hard, they must be paid even before provision for her children, for paying every just debt is a main law of the Christian religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. One day Elisha passed to Shunem, where a great woman constrained him to eat bread. Henry notes Elisha was well thought of for his late services, and a good man takes as much pleasure in serving others as in raising himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The Shunammite told her husband she perceived Elisha was a holy man of God passing by continually. Henry presents her hospitality as the fruit of recognizing God's servant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. She proposed making a little chamber on the wall with a bed, table, stool, and candlestick for Elisha whenever he came. Henry notes it is a happiness to dwell among one's own people who love and respect us, and to do them good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. One day Elisha came, turned into the chamber, and lay there. Henry's outline treats this settled hospitality as the ground of the unasked mercy that would follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Elisha told Gehazi to call the Shunammite, and she stood before him. Henry notes the LORD sees the secret wish suppressed in obedience to his will, and hears his servants' prayers for their benefactors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Elisha offered to speak for her to the king or the captain of the host, but she answered she dwelt among her own people. Henry remarks it would be well with many if they knew when they are really well off, and needed no great offices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. When Elisha asked what could be done for her, Gehazi answered she had no child and her husband was old. Henry notes the LORD sees secret wishes and sends unasked-for, unexpected mercies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Elisha called her again, and she stood in the door. Henry stresses that the professions of men of God are not delusive like those of men of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Elisha promised that about this season, according to the time of life, she would embrace a son, though she begged him not to deceive her handmaid. Henry presents this as the unexpected mercy sent in answer to unspoken desire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The woman conceived and bore a son at the season Elisha had spoken. Henry's passage shows God's promise fulfilled exactly as spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. When the child was grown, he went out one day to his father among the reapers. Henry introduces the sudden death that follows: all a mother's tenderness cannot keep alive a child of promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. The child cried, "My head, my head," and his father had a lad carry him to his mother. Henry notes the suddenness of the affliction that struck a child of prayer, given in love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. The child sat on his mother's knees till noon, and then died. Henry praises the prudent, pious mother who guarded her lips under this sudden affliction, with not one peevish word escaping her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. She laid the child on the bed of the man of God, shut the door, and went out. Henry observes her confidence in God's goodness, ready to believe he would restore what he had taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. She asked her husband for a young man and an ass to run to the man of God and return. Henry notes she had not thought it enough to have Elisha's help sometimes in her family, but attended on public worship and sought him at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Her husband asked why she would go that day, neither new moon nor sabbath, and she answered, "It shall be well." Henry echoes her answer: all is well that God does, and all is well with us that stay behind if affliction furthers us on our way thither. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. She saddled an ass and told her servant to drive and go forward, slackening not for her unless she bade him. Henry presents her urgent, resolute journey to the prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. She came to the man of God at Mount Carmel, and when Elisha saw her afar off he sent Gehazi to meet her. Henry notes it well becomes men of God to inquire about the welfare of their friends and families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Gehazi was to ask whether it was well with her, her husband, and the child, and she answered, "It is well." Henry explains: all well, and yet the child dead in the house — yes, all is well that God does. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. When she came to the man of God on the hill she caught him by the feet, and Gehazi would have thrust her away, but Elisha said to let her alone, for her soul was vexed and the LORD had hid it from him. Henry praises her guarded lips and great faith under affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. She asked, "Did I desire a son of my lord? did I not say, Do not deceive me?" Henry notes her confidence: he who wrought such faith would not disappoint it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Elisha told Gehazi to gird up his loins, take his staff, and go, saluting no man by the way, and lay the staff on the child's face. Henry presents this as the prophet's first means toward restoring the child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. The mother swore by the LORD and by Elisha's soul that she would not leave him, so Elisha arose and followed her. Henry's theme is her steadfast faith that would not be put off with a servant's errand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. Gehazi went before them and laid the staff on the child's face, but there was neither voice nor hearing, so he returned saying the child was not awaked. Henry notes the minister cannot give divine life, yet must use every means with earnestness as if he could. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. When Elisha came into the house, the child was dead and laid upon his bed. Henry's passage moves to the prophet's own fervent prayer for the dead child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. Elisha went in, shut the door upon them two, and prayed unto the LORD. Henry stresses that those who would convey spiritual life to dead souls must feel deeply for their case and labour fervently in prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. Elisha lay upon the child, mouth to mouth, eyes to eyes, hands to hands, and the child's flesh waxed warm. Henry presents this earnest, bodily intercession as the means God honoured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. Elisha walked in the house to and fro, went up again and stretched himself upon the child, and the child sneezed seven times and opened his eyes. Henry concludes that Elisha cried unto God in faith, and the beloved son was restored alive to his mother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. Elisha called Gehazi to call the Shunammite, and when she came in he said, "Take up thy son." Henry's lesson is that faith which trusted God through death received its child back alive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. She went in, fell at Elisha's feet, bowed to the ground, took up her son, and went out. Henry notes, "O woman, great is thy faith!" — he that wrought it would not disappoint it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. Elisha came again to Gilgal during a dearth, with the sons of the prophets sitting before him, and he told his servant to set on the great pot and seethe pottage. Henry notes there was a famine of bread but not of hearing God's word, for Elisha had the prophets sitting before him to hear his wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. One went out to gather herbs, found a wild vine, and gathered wild gourds, shredding them into the pottage without knowing them. Henry observes that if a mess of pottage is all our dinner, this great prophet had no better for himself and his guests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. As they ate, they cried out, "There is death in the pot," and could not eat. Henry warns the table often becomes a snare, and when receiving life's supports we must keep up an expectation of death and a fear of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 41. Elisha said to bring meal, cast it into the pot, and pour out for the people, and there was no harm in the pot. Henry notes Elisha made hurtful food safe and wholesome, and we must acknowledge God's goodness in making our food nourishing: "I am the LORD that healeth thee." Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 42. A man from Baalshalisha brought the man of God twenty loaves of barley from the firstfruits, with full ears of corn. Henry presents this gift as the small provision God would multiply. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 43. Elisha's servitor objected, asking what setting this before a hundred men would do, but Elisha said again to give the people that they might eat, for the LORD said they would eat and leave thereof. Henry notes Elisha made a little food go a great way, having freely received and freely giving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 44. He set it before them, and they ate and left thereof according to the word of the LORD. Henry concludes God has promised to abundantly bless his church's provision and satisfy her poor with bread: whom he feeds, he fills, and what he blesses comes to much. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Henry concludes that Elisha cried unto God in faith, and the beloved son was restored alive to his mother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. Elisha called Gehazi to call the Shunammite, and when she came in he said, "Take up thy son." Henry's lesson is that faith which trusted God through death received its child back alive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. She went in, fell at Elisha's feet, bowed to the ground, took up her son, and went out. Henry notes, "O woman, great is thy faith!" — he that wrought it would not disappoint it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. Elisha came again to Gilgal during a dearth, with the sons of the prophets sitting before him, and he told his servant to set on the great pot and seethe pottage. Henry notes there was a famine of bread but not of hearing God's word, for Elisha had the prophets sitting before him to hear his wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. One went out to gather herbs, found a wild vine, and gathered wild gourds, shredding them into the pottage without knowing them. Henry observes that if a mess of pottage is all our dinner, this great prophet had no better for himself and his guests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. As they ate, they cried out, "There is death in the pot," and could not eat. Henry warns the table often becomes a snare, and when receiving life's supports we must keep up an expectation of death and a fear of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 41. Elisha said to bring meal, cast it into the pot, and pour out for the people, and there was no harm in the pot. Henry notes Elisha made hurtful food safe and wholesome, and we must acknowledge God's goodness in making our food nourishing: "I am the LORD that healeth thee." Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 42. A man from Baalshalisha brought the man of God twenty loaves of barley from the firstfruits, with full ears of corn. Henry presents this gift as the small provision God would multiply. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 43. Elisha's servitor objected, asking what setting this before a hundred men would do, but Elisha said again to give the people that they might eat, for the LORD said they would eat and leave thereof. Henry notes Elisha made a little food go a great way, having freely received and freely giving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 44. He set it before them, and they ate and left thereof according to the word of the LORD. Henry concludes God has promised to abundantly bless his church's provision and satisfy her poor with bread: whom he feeds, he fills, and what he blesses comes to much. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
