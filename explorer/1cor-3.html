The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Corinthians 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And I, brethren, could not speak unto you as unto spiritual, but as unto carnal, even as unto babes in Christ.

2 I have fed you with milk, and not with meat: for hitherto ye were not able to bear it, neither yet now are ye able.

3 For ye are yet carnal: for whereas there is among you envying, and strife, and divisions, are ye not carnal, and walk as men?

4 For while one saith, I am of Paul; and another, I am of Apollos; are ye not carnal?

5 Who then is Paul, and who is Apollos, but ministers by whom ye believed, even as the Lord gave to every man?

6 I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase.

7 So then neither is he that planteth any thing, neither he that watereth; but God that giveth the increase.

8 Now he that planteth and he that watereth are one: and every man shall receive his own reward according to his own labour.

9 For we are labourers together with God: ye are God’s husbandry, ye are God’s building.

10 According to the grace of God which is given unto me, as a wise masterbuilder, I have laid the foundation, and another buildeth thereon. But let every man take heed how he buildeth thereupon.

11 For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ.

12 Now if any man build upon this foundation gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay, stubble;

13 Every man’s work shall be made manifest: for the day shall declare it, because it shall be revealed by fire; and the fire shall try every man’s work of what sort it is.

14 If any man’s work abide which he hath built thereupon, he shall receive a reward.

15 If any man’s work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire.

16 Know ye not that ye are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwelleth in you?

17 If any man defile the temple of God, him shall God destroy; for the temple of God is holy, which temple ye are.

18 Let no man deceive himself. If any man among you seemeth to be wise in this world, let him become a fool, that he may be wise.

19 For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God. For it is written, He taketh the wise in their own craftiness.

20 And again, The Lord knoweth the thoughts of the wise, that they are vain.

21 Therefore let no man glory in men. For all things are yours;

22 Whether Paul, or Apollos, or Cephas, or the world, or life, or death, or things present, or things to come; all are yours;

23 And ye are Christ’s; and Christ is God’s.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul says he could only feed the Corinthians with milk, not meat, because they are still carnal, divided over Paul and Apollos, who are merely fellow workers in God's field and building. On the one foundation, Jesus Christ, each builder's work will be tested by fire, believers are God's temple indwelt by his Spirit, and all things, Paul, Apollos, Cephas, the world, life, and death, are theirs, while they are Christ's and Christ is God's. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. As the natural man cannot receive the deep things of God, Paul could not speak them to the Corinthians as he would to the spiritual, but had to speak to them as to men of flesh. They were not wholly unregenerate, but babes in Christ with weak life, still carnal in tendency when by now they ought to have grown to full maturity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Paul fed them with milk, the elementary principles of the doctrine of Christ, rather than solid food. They were not yet able to bear deeper teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Envying, strife, and divisions proved they were still carnal — envy in feelings, strife in words, divisions in actions, each feeding the next. Strife is a work of the flesh, and walking in it is walking as unregenerate men walk, after the flesh rather than after the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Saying 'I am of Paul' or 'I am of Apollos' was itself the proof of carnality. The question stands: are you not merely men, walking as unregenerate men do? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Who is Paul, and who is Apollos, that believers should strive over them? They are mere servants by whose ministry the Corinthians believed, each as the Lord gave to him. If so great an apostle reasons so humbly of himself, humility becomes ordinary ministers far more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Paul planted and Apollos watered, following up the work Paul had begun at Corinth, but God gave the growth. Though ministers are nothing and God is all in all, God still works through instruments and promises his Spirit in the faithful use of means. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Neither planter nor waterer is anything; God who gives the increase is all in all, and his name stands emphatically last. The whole discussion of glorying in men is set in contrast to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Planter and waterer are one in aim and ministry, so they must not be made occasions for separate parties. Yet each minister is separately responsible for his own work and will receive his own reward according to his own labor — not according to success, but faithfulness, as to a good and faithful servant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Ministers are fellow workers belonging to God and laboring under him, while the church is God's field and God's building. The building image suits what follows about different kinds of teaching and their results, since to edify the church is to build it up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. By the grace given him, proportioned to his work, Paul laid the foundation as a skilled master builder — skill shown precisely in laying a foundation, which the unskillful builder omits. His warning to every successor to take heed how he builds concerns the material and manner of teaching added afterward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. No man can lay any other foundation than the one already laid by God, Jesus Christ — the person himself, not merely doctrine about him. The warning concerns the superstructure, not the foundation, since the only foundation recognized by God is already in place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. On the solid foundation teachers build partly with durable gold, silver, and precious stones that withstand fire, and partly with perishable wood, hay, and stubble. The durable materials are teachings that will stand judgment; the perishable are teachings mixed with human philosophy and Judaism, curious rather than useful — not outright heresy, which would destroy the foundation. The superstructure also pictures the persons won by such teaching, whose conversion will be tested at the last day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The day of the Lord will make every teacher's work clear, for it is revealed in fire — a probatory fire, not purgatory. It tests works, not persons for cleansing, and it is universal, trying godly and ungodly alike at the last day, not beginning at death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. If a teacher's work abides the testing fire, he will receive wages as a builder. His converts, built on Christ through faithful teaching, will be his crown of rejoicing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. If a teacher's work is burned, he forfeits the special reward but not salvation, which is a free gift rather than wages, since he remains on the foundation. He is saved yet so as through fire — like a builder escaping a burning building, or a shipwrecked merchant saved through the waves with his merchandise lost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Believers together are not many temples but the one temple of God, living stones in a spiritual house. God's indwelling and the Spirit's indwelling are one, so the Holy Spirit is God; the New Testament recognizes no literal temple in the Christian church, only this spiritual temple of worshippers in whom the Spirit dwells. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Whoever destroys God's temple, God will destroy, repaying in kind — the same verb is used for both. This destroyer is distinct from the unskillful builder who keeps the foundation and is saved; the destroyer assails the foundation itself with false teaching and subverts the temple, and as violating the temple's sanctity he will perish eternally. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Whoever seems wise in this world's wisdom must become a fool in the world's sight by receiving the gospel in its unworldly simplicity. He must stop thinking himself wise and seek true wisdom from God, bringing his understanding captive to the obedience of faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The wisdom of this world is foolishness in God's judgment. As Job says, God takes the wise in their own craftiness, making their wisdom the very snare that catches them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Quoting the Psalm, Paul says the Lord knows the thoughts of the wise, that they are vanity. The Psalm speaks of men generally; Paul by inspiration names the class the Spirit meant — the proud and worldly-wise who boast of their wisdom while God calls them fools. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Let no man glory in men, resuming the argument from the party slogans: the true object of glorying is the Lord. Glorying in teachers lowers believers from their high position as heirs of all things, for teachers belong to Christ and therefore to them — they are for the believers' sake, not believers for theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. All things are enumerated as theirs: Paul, Apollos, Cephas, the world, life, death, things present, and things to come. These do not separate believers from God's love in Christ; they belong to them as they belong to Christ their Head, and work together for their good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Believers are Christ's — not Paul's, Apollos's, or Cephas's — all of them, not merely one section. And Christ is God's, God being the ultimate end of all, even of Christ his co-equal Son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • milk and meat. Paul's food images for elementary versus solid teaching; the Corinthians could bear only milk. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • God's husbandry. God's tilled field; Paul's image for the church, with Paul planting and Apollos watering. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • foundation. Jesus Christ, the only foundation on which any builder in the church can build. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • carnal. Of the flesh; Paul's word for believers still living by merely human standards. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
