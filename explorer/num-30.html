The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 30 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Moses spake unto the heads of the tribes concerning the children of Israel, saying, This is the thing which the LORD hath commanded.

2 If a man vow a vow unto the LORD, or swear an oath to bind his soul with a bond; he shall not break his word, he shall do according to all that proceedeth out of his mouth.

3 If a woman also vow a vow unto the LORD, and bind herself by a bond, being in her father’s house in her youth;

4 And her father hear her vow, and her bond wherewith she hath bound her soul, and her father shall hold his peace at her; then all her vows shall stand, and every bond wherewith she hath bound her soul shall stand.

5 But if her father disallow her in the day that he heareth; not any of her vows, or of her bonds wherewith she hath bound her soul, shall stand: and the LORD shall forgive her, because her father disallowed her.

6 And if she had at all an husband, when she vowed, or uttered ought out of her lips, wherewith she bound her soul;

7 And her husband heard it, and held his peace at her in the day that he heard it: then her vows shall stand, and her bonds wherewith she bound her soul shall stand.

8 But if her husband disallowed her on the day that he heard it; then he shall make her vow which she vowed, and that which she uttered with her lips, wherewith she bound her soul, of none effect: and the LORD shall forgive her.

9 But every vow of a widow, and of her that is divorced, wherewith they have bound their souls, shall stand against her.

10 And if she vowed in her husband’s house, or bound her soul by a bond with an oath;

11 And her husband heard it, and held his peace at her, and disallowed her not: then all her vows shall stand, and every bond wherewith she bound her soul shall stand.

12 But if her husband hath utterly made them void on the day he heard them; then whatsoever proceeded out of her lips concerning her vows, or concerning the bond of her soul, shall not stand: her husband hath made them void; and the LORD shall forgive her.

13 Every vow, and every binding oath to afflict the soul, her husband may establish it, or her husband may make it void.

14 But if her husband altogether hold his peace at her from day to day; then he establisheth all her vows, or all her bonds, which are upon her: he confirmeth them, because he held his peace at her in the day that he heard them.

15 But if he shall any ways make them void after that he hath heard them; then he shall bear her iniquity.

16 These are the statutes, which the LORD commanded Moses, between a man and his wife, between the father and his daughter, being yet in her youth in her father’s house.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the law of vows. A man's vow, or his oath to bind his soul, stands and must not be broken. A young woman still in her father's house may have her vow disallowed by her father on the day he hears it, and if he holds his peace it stands; the same rule applies between a husband and his wife, and if a husband first allows a vow and later makes it void, he bears her iniquity. But the vow of a widow or a divorced woman stands against her, with no one to disallow it, as do the statutes between a man and his wife and between a father and his daughter. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Disallow (make void) a vow. A father or husband may, on the day he hears it, disallow a vow made by a daughter in his house or a wife; if he holds his peace, the vow stands. Interpreted Chapter text in the Scripture panel above

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Moses spoke to the heads of the tribes concerning Israel, saying, this is the thing which the Lord has commanded. JFB notes vowing was an ancient usage allowed by the law to remain, by which people declared an intention to offer some gift on the altar, abstain from certain meat or drink, observe a private fast, or do something to God's honour beyond what was required, and this chapter's rules were given to remove doubts conscientious persons felt about performing vows in certain circumstances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. If a man vow a vow to the Lord, or swear an oath to bind his soul with a bond, he shall not break his word, he shall do according to all that proceeds out of his mouth. JFB notes a mere secret purpose was not enough to constitute a vow, it had to be actually expressed in words, and though purely voluntary, once made its performance became an indispensable duty, all the more because a sacred thing could not be neglected without the guilt of prevarication and unfaithfulness to God, while break his word literally means profane his word, render it vain and contemptible, and Henry adds no man can be bound by his own promise to do what the Divine precept already forbids, but in other matters he shall not break his word though he may change his mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. If a woman also vow a vow to the Lord, and bind herself by a bond, being in her father's house in her youth. JFB notes girls only are specified, but minors of the other sex under the parental roof were included, father comprehending all guardians of youth, the age deemed capable of vowing being thirteen for boys and twelve for girls, and a father or guardian's judgment might be given by expressed approval or by silence, construed as approval. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. And her father hear her vow, and her bond wherewith she has bound her soul, and her father shall hold his peace at her: then all her vows shall stand, and every bond wherewith she has bound her soul shall stand. Silence was approval: a father who heard and held his peace confirmed the vow as surely as by words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. But if her father disallow her in the day that he hears, not any of her vows, or of her bonds wherewith she has bound her soul, shall stand, and the Lord shall forgive her, because her father disallowed her. JFB notes natural superiors had the right of judging the propriety of vows, with discretionary power to sanction or interdict, allowed only a day for deliberation after the matter became known, and an unfavourable judgment released the devotee from all obligation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And if she had at all a husband, when she vowed, or uttered ought out of her lips, wherewith she bound her soul. A vow made before marriage did not vanish at marriage: the case of a maiden's vow carried into a husband's house needed its own rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And her husband heard it, and held his peace at her in the day that he heard it: then her vows shall stand, and her bonds wherewith she bound her soul shall stand. The husband's silence on the day he heard confirmed the premarital vow, just as the father's silence confirmed a daughter's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. But if her husband disallowed her on the day that he heard it, then he shall make her vow which she vowed, and that which she uttered with her lips, wherewith she bound her soul, of none effect, and the Lord shall forgive her. Henry notes the obligation to her husband takes place of the vow, for to him she ought to be in subjection as unto the Lord, and the Divine law consults the good order of families rather than let a vow break those bonds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. But every vow of a widow, and of her that is divorced, wherewith they have bound their souls, shall stand against her. JFB notes a doubt might arise whether a woman who, separated or widowed, returned to her father's house was again subject to paternal jurisdiction, and the law ordained her vow stood if made in her husband's lifetime without his veto, as when she vowed a portion of income to pious and charitable uses and repented as a widow, she was still required to fulfil it if her circumstances enabled her to redeem the pledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. And if she vowed in her husband's house, or bound her soul by a bond with an oath. The next case is the vow made within marriage itself, under her husband's roof, where his authority over it was direct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And her husband heard it, and held his peace at her, and disallowed her not: then all her vows shall stand, and every bond wherewith she bound her soul shall stand. Again silence confirmed: a husband who heard and did not disallow established every vow his wife had made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. But if her husband has utterly made them void on the day he heard them, then whatsoever proceeded out of her lips concerning her vows, or concerning the bond of her soul, shall not stand: her husband has made them void, and the Lord shall forgive her. Voiding on the day of hearing released the wife entirely, and the Lord's forgiveness followed the husband's veto rather than the wife's non-performance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Every vow, and every binding oath to afflict the soul, her husband may establish it, or her husband may make it void. The husband's authority is stated generally, covering especially vows to afflict the soul, such as fasts, where his judgment over the household's welfare was decisive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. But if her husband altogether hold his peace at her from day to day, then he establishes all her vows, and all her bonds, which are upon her: he establishes them, because he held his peace at her in the day that he heard them. Silence from day to day was establishment, not mere delay: once the day of hearing passed in silence, the vows stood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. But if he shall any ways make them void after that he has heard them, then he shall bear her iniquity. JFB notes that in the case of a husband who, after silence from day to day, should ultimately disapprove or hinder his wife's vow, the sin of non-performance was imputed to him and not to her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. These are the statutes, which the Lord commanded Moses, between a man and his wife, between the father and his daughter, being yet in her youth in her father's house. Henry closes that it is fit every man should bear rule in his own house and have wife and children in subjection, and rather than that great rule should be broken or encouragement given to inferior relations to break those bonds asunder, God releases the obligation even of a solemn vow, so much does religion secure the welfare of all societies, and in it the families of the earth have a blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
