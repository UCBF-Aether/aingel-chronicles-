The Text — Ezekiel 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now it came to pass in the thirtieth year, in the fourth month, in the fifth day of the month, as I was among the captives by the river of Chebar, that the heavens were opened, and I saw visions of God.

2 In the fifth day of the month, which was the fifth year of king Jehoiachin’s captivity,

3 The word of the LORD came expressly unto Ezekiel the priest, the son of Buzi, in the land of the Chaldeans by the river Chebar; and the hand of the LORD was there upon him.

4 And I looked, and, behold, a whirlwind came out of the north, a great cloud, and a fire infolding itself, and a brightness was about it, and out of the midst thereof as the colour of amber, out of the midst of the fire.

5 Also out of the midst thereof came the likeness of four living creatures. And this was their appearance; they had the likeness of a man.

6 And every one had four faces, and every one had four wings.

7 And their feet were straight feet; and the sole of their feet was like the sole of a calf’s foot: and they sparkled like the colour of burnished brass.

8 And they had the hands of a man under their wings on their four sides; and they four had their faces and their wings.

9 Their wings were joined one to another; they turned not when they went; they went every one straight forward.

10 As for the likeness of their faces, they four had the face of a man, and the face of a lion, on the right side: and they four had the face of an ox on the left side; they four also had the face of an eagle.

11 Thus were their faces: and their wings were stretched upward; two wings of every one were joined one to another, and two covered their bodies.

12 And they went every one straight forward: whither the spirit was to go, they went; and they turned not when they went.

13 As for the likeness of the living creatures, their appearance was like burning coals of fire, and like the appearance of lamps: it went up and down among the living creatures; and the fire was bright, and out of the fire went forth lightning.

14 And the living creatures ran and returned as the appearance of a flash of lightning.

15 Now as I beheld the living creatures, behold one wheel upon the earth by the living creatures, with his four faces.

16 The appearance of the wheels and their work was like unto the colour of a beryl: and they four had one likeness: and their appearance and their work was as it were a wheel in the middle of a wheel.

17 When they went, they went upon their four sides: and they turned not when they went.

18 As for their rings, they were so high that they were dreadful; and their rings were full of eyes round about them four.

19 And when the living creatures went, the wheels went by them: and when the living creatures were lifted up from the earth, the wheels were lifted up.

20 Whithersoever the spirit was to go, they went, thither was their spirit to go; and the wheels were lifted up over against them: for the spirit of the living creature was in the wheels.

21 When those went, these went; and when those stood, these stood; and when those were lifted up from the earth, the wheels were lifted up over against them: for the spirit of the living creature was in the wheels.

22 And the likeness of the firmament upon the heads of the living creature was as the colour of the terrible crystal, stretched forth over their heads above.

23 And under the firmament were their wings straight, the one toward the other: every one had two, which covered on this side, and every one had two, which covered on that side, their bodies.

24 And when they went, I heard the noise of their wings, like the noise of great waters, as the voice of the Almighty, the voice of speech, as the noise of an host: when they stood, they let down their wings.

25 And there was a voice from the firmament that was over their heads, when they stood, and had let down their wings.

26 And above the firmament that was over their heads was the likeness of a throne, as the appearance of a sapphire stone: and upon the likeness of the throne was the likeness as the appearance of a man above upon it.

27 And I saw as the colour of amber, as the appearance of fire round about within it, from the appearance of his loins even upward, and from the appearance of his loins even downward, I saw as it were the appearance of fire, and it had brightness round about.

28 As the appearance of the bow that is in the cloud in the day of rain, so was the appearance of the brightness round about. This was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD. And when I saw it, I fell upon my face, and I heard a voice of one that spake.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Exiled among the captives by the river Chebar, Ezekiel sees the heavens opened: a storm wind and infolding fire, four living creatures with four faces and four wings each, wheels within wheels moving straight forward, a crystal firmament above them, and over it a sapphire throne with a man-like figure shining like fire and ringed with rainbow brightness. He falls on his face before the glory of the LORD as a voice begins to speak. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Among the captives by the Chebar, in his thirtieth year, Ezekiel saw the heavens opened and visions of God, a mercy given even in affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The date is fixed to the fifth year of King Jehoiachin's captivity, grounding the vision in real exile history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The word of the LORD came expressly to Ezekiel the priest, son of Buzi, in Chaldea, and God's hand was upon him for the work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. A whirlwind from the north, a great cloud and infolding fire with brightness and amber colour, opens a vision meant to strike awe and raise expectation of blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Four living creatures came out of the fire, in the likeness of a man, representing God attended by a vast company of angels, his messengers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Each creature had four faces and four wings, showing angelic readiness and service in every direction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Their straight feet, like a calf's foot, sparkling like burnished brass, picture firm, steady, upright service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Hands of a man under their wings show that the angels have not only wings for motion but hands for action, doing what duty requires. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Their wings joined together, they went straight forward without turning, making no mistakes and needing no second doing of their work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The four faces — man, lion, ox, and eagle — picture understanding, strength and boldness, diligence and patience, and quick, piercing sight, all excelled by the angels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Two wings joined upward and two covering their bodies show both readiness for God's errands and reverent humility before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Each went straight forward wherever the Spirit was to go, turning not aside to trifle with anything. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Like burning coals and lamps, with lightning going out of the fire, the creatures are seraphim, or burners, ardent in love to God and zeal in his service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The creatures ran and returned like a flash of lightning, losing no time on whatever business God sent them upon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. A wheel upon the earth beside each living creature introduces providence, the ordered government of God working alongside his angels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The wheels, coloured like beryl and as a wheel within a wheel, look dark and perplexed to us, yet are wisely ordered for the best. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The wheels went on their four sides without turning, for the motion of providence on its axle is regular and steady. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Rings so high they were dreadful, and full of eyes round about, show providence directed by infinite wisdom, beholding evil and good everywhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. When the creatures went the wheels went, and when they were lifted up the wheels were lifted up, for angels are ministers of God's providence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. The spirit of the living creature was in the wheels, so the same wisdom, power, and holiness that guide the angels order all events below. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Creatures and wheels moved, stood, and rose together, a steady, constant motion with no chance or fortune in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. A firmament like terrible crystal stretched over the creatures' heads, glorious but awe-inspiring, as God's counsel is high and deep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Under the firmament their wings stood straight toward one another, two covering each body, in ordered reverence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. The noise of their wings, like great waters and the voice of the Almighty, roused a careless world to listen, then they let down their wings so God's voice might be heard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. A voice came from the firmament over their heads once they stood with wings lowered, for the voice of providence opens ears to the voice of the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Above the firmament was a sapphire throne, and upon it the likeness of a man, the eternal Son, who afterwards took human nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Amber and fire from his loins upward and downward, with brightness round about, mark a throne of glory, grace, triumph, government, and judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. The brightness like a rainbow in rain shows God's covenant mercy bounding even his wrath against Jerusalem, and Ezekiel fell on his face before the glory of the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

Glosses below are editorial help, not Scripture; where a gloss is uncertain the page says so.
