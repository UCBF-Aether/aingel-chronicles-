The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto Aaron and say unto him, When thou lightest the lamps, the seven lamps shall give light over against the candlestick.

3 And Aaron did so; he lighted the lamps thereof over against the candlestick, as the LORD commanded Moses.

4 And this work of the candlestick was of beaten gold, unto the shaft thereof, unto the flowers thereof, was beaten work: according unto the pattern which the LORD had shewed Moses, so he made the candlestick.

5 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

6 Take the Levites from among the children of Israel, and cleanse them.

7 And thus shalt thou do unto them, to cleanse them: Sprinkle water of purifying upon them, and let them shave all their flesh, and let them wash their clothes, and so make themselves clean.

8 Then let them take a young bullock with his meat offering, even fine flour mingled with oil, and another young bullock shalt thou take for a sin offering.

9 And thou shalt bring the Levites before the tabernacle of the congregation: and thou shalt gather the whole assembly of the children of Israel together:

10 And thou shalt bring the Levites before the LORD: and the children of Israel shall put their hands upon the Levites:

11 And Aaron shall offer the Levites before the LORD for an offering of the children of Israel, that they may execute the service of the LORD.

12 And the Levites shall lay their hands upon the heads of the bullocks: and thou shalt offer the one for a sin offering, and the other for a burnt offering, unto the LORD, to make an atonement for the Levites.

13 And thou shalt set the Levites before Aaron, and before his sons, and offer them for an offering unto the LORD.

14 Thus shalt thou separate the Levites from among the children of Israel: and the Levites shall be mine.

15 And after that shall the Levites go in to do the service of the tabernacle of the congregation: and thou shalt cleanse them, and offer them for an offering.

16 For they are wholly given unto me from among the children of Israel; instead of such as open every womb, even instead of the firstborn of all the children of Israel, have I taken them unto me.

17 For all the firstborn of the children of Israel are mine, both man and beast: on the day that I smote every firstborn in the land of Egypt I sanctified them for myself.

18 And I have taken the Levites for all the firstborn of the children of Israel.

19 And I have given the Levites as a gift to Aaron and to his sons from among the children of Israel, to do the service of the children of Israel in the tabernacle of the congregation, and to make an atonement for the children of Israel: that there be no plague among the children of Israel, when the children of Israel come nigh unto the sanctuary.

20 And Moses, and Aaron, and all the congregation of the children of Israel, did to the Levites according unto all that the LORD commanded Moses concerning the Levites, so did the children of Israel unto them.

21 And the Levites were purified, and they washed their clothes; and Aaron offered them as an offering before the LORD; and Aaron made an atonement for them to cleanse them.

22 And after that went the Levites in to do their service in the tabernacle of the congregation before Aaron, and before his sons: as the LORD had commanded Moses concerning the Levites, so did they unto them.

23 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

24 This is it that belongeth unto the Levites: from twenty and five years old and upward they shall go in to wait upon the service of the tabernacle of the congregation:

25 And from the age of fifty years they shall cease waiting upon the service thereof, and shall serve no more:

26 But shall minister with their brethren in the tabernacle of the congregation, to keep the charge, and shall do no service. Thus shalt thou do unto the Levites touching their charge.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the lighting of the lamps and the cleansing of the Levites. Aaron is to light the seven lamps so they give light toward the front of the candlestick. The Levites are then cleansed with water of purifying, shaving, and washing, and offered as a wave offering before the LORD in place of the firstborn, with burnt and sin offerings made for them. Their service runs from twenty-five years old, with full service from thirty to fifty, and after fifty they keep the charge with their brethren but do no heavy service. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. When Aaron lit the lamps, the seven lamps gave light over against the candlestick. JFB describes the candlestick as one solid mass of pure gold, six lamps on branches with a seventh on the central shaft, and Aaron first lighting the middle lamp from the altar fire, then the others from each other, a symbol that all heavenly light derives from Christ and is diffused by his ministers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Aaron did so, lighting the lamps over against the candlestick as the Lord commanded Moses. Henry notes Aaron himself lit them, representing his Divine Master, and the work of ministers is to light these lamps by expounding and applying the word of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The work of the candlestick was beaten gold, from shaft to flowers, made after the pattern shown to Moses. The single beaten piece, completed exactly to the pattern, shows the light of the sanctuary was not a human contrivance but God's own appointment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The Levites were taken from among Israel and cleansed. JFB stresses hereditary descent alone did not qualify them: they needed a special ceremony, simpler than the priests', with no washing, anointing or robes of office, yet still a real setting apart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. To cleanse them, Moses sprinkled water of purifying on them, shaved all their flesh, and they washed their clothes. JFB explains the combination: sprinkling with water mixed with red heifer ashes, shaving all over, and washing clothes reminded them of mortifying carnal desires and maintaining the purity fitting God's servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Then they took a young bullock with its meat offering of fine flour mingled with oil, and another young bullock for a sin offering. The sacrifices belonged to the consecration itself: cleansing by water was joined to atonement by blood before any Levite touched the sanctuary's work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The whole assembly of Israel was brought before the tabernacle. JFB notes the whole multitude could not literally do this, so a select portion is meant, supposed by some to be the firstborn transferring their privilege to Levi, and by others the princes blessing the tribe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The children of Israel laid their hands on the Levites. The imposition of hands was the ancient ceremony for consecrating persons to holy office, perpetuated in the Christian church from the example of the Lord and his apostles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Aaron offered the Levites before the Lord as an offering of the children of Israel, that they might execute the service of the Lord. JFB renders the Hebrew as a wave offering: the high priest likely brought them one by one to the altar with movements like those of a wave offering, first devoting them to God and then receiving them back for the priests' service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The Levites laid their hands on the heads of the bullocks, one offered for a sin offering and one for a burnt offering, to make atonement for them. By laying hands on the sacrifices, the Levites owned the atonement as their own before they were offered to God themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The Levites were set before Aaron and his sons and offered as an offering to the Lord. The setting before the priests marked their subordination: they were given to Aaron's house, not set up as a rival ministry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Thus the Levites were separated from among Israel, and the Levites shall be mine. JFB explains the separation: exempt from military duty and secular work, free from pecuniary imposition, wholly devoted to the custody and service of the sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. After that the Levites went in to do the service of the tabernacle, once Aaron had made atonement for them. JFB notes their service meant entering the court to assist the priests, and at removals standing at the tabernacle door to receive the covered furniture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The Levites are wholly given to God from among Israel, instead of all the firstborn that open the matrix. The exchange is stated as a gift in both directions: Israel gave Levi, and God gave Levi back to the priests for the people's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. All the firstborn of Israel are God's, man and beast, since on the day he smote Egypt's firstborn he sanctified Israel's to himself. The consecration of Levi reaches back to the exodus night, when the firstborn were spared and thereby claimed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. God took the Levites for all the firstborn of Israel. The substitution was total, tribe for firstborn, so that no family kept a private claim to priestly service once Levi had been taken in its place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. God gave the Levites as a gift to Aaron and his sons, to do the service of Israel in the tabernacle and make atonement for them, so that no plague would come on Israel when they came near the sanctuary. JFB renders the purpose as redemption: a sanctified body guarded the sanctuary so the people would not presumptuously meddle with holy things and expose themselves to judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Moses, Aaron and all the congregation did to the Levites according to all the Lord commanded Moses concerning them. Henry's point for the whole consecration is that all Israel must know the Levites took not this honour to themselves, but were called of God to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The Levites were purified, washed their clothes, and Aaron offered them as an offering and made atonement for them. The cleansing was completed by the priest's act, not by their own washing alone: God declared his acceptance of them through Aaron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. After that the Levites went in to do their service in the tabernacle before Aaron and his sons, as the Lord had commanded Moses concerning them. Henry's closing rule is that all who expect to share in the tabernacle's privileges must resolve to do the tabernacle's service: none of God's servants are merely honorary, doing nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. This belongs to the Levites: from twenty-five years old and upward they waited on the service of the tabernacle. JFB reconciles this with chapter 4: they entered at twenty-five as pupils and probationers under their senior brethren, and at thirty were admitted to the full discharge of office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. From fifty years old they ceased waiting upon the service of the tabernacle. JFB explains this as release from the laborious and exhausting parts of the work, not from all service, since age that can no longer carry may still direct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. But they ministered with their brethren in the tabernacle, to keep the charge, and did no service. JFB describes the easier and higher duties of age: instructing and directing the young, or superintending important trusts, in Milton's words, they also serve who only wait. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages
