The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And an angel of the LORD came up from Gilgal to Bochim, and said, I made you to go up out of Egypt, and have brought you unto the land which I sware unto your fathers; and I said, I will never break my covenant with you.

2 And ye shall make no league with the inhabitants of this land; ye shall throw down their altars: but ye have not obeyed my voice: why have ye done this?

3 Wherefore I also said, I will not drive them out from before you; but they shall be as thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare unto you.

4 And it came to pass, when the angel of the LORD spake these words unto all the children of Israel, that the people lifted up their voice, and wept.

5 And they called the name of that place Bochim: and they sacrificed there unto the LORD.

6 And when Joshua had let the people go, the children of Israel went every man unto his inheritance to possess the land.

7 And the people served the LORD all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders that outlived Joshua, who had seen all the great works of the LORD, that he did for Israel.

8 And Joshua the son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died, being an hundred and ten years old.

9 And they buried him in the border of his inheritance in Timnathheres, in the mount of Ephraim, on the north side of the hill Gaash.

10 And also all that generation were gathered unto their fathers: and there arose another generation after them, which knew not the LORD, nor yet the works which he had done for Israel.

11 And the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the LORD, and served Baalim:

12 And they forsook the LORD God of their fathers, which brought them out of the land of Egypt, and followed other gods, of the gods of the people that were round about them, and bowed themselves unto them, and provoked the LORD to anger.

13 And they forsook the LORD, and served Baal and Ashtaroth.

14 And the anger of the LORD was hot against Israel, and he delivered them into the hands of spoilers that spoiled them, and he sold them into the hands of their enemies round about, so that they could not any longer stand before their enemies.

15 Whithersoever they went out, the hand of the LORD was against them for evil, as the LORD had said, and as the LORD had sworn unto them: and they were greatly distressed.

16 Nevertheless the LORD raised up judges, which delivered them out of the hand of those that spoiled them.

17 And yet they would not hearken unto their judges, but they went a whoring after other gods, and bowed themselves unto them: they turned quickly out of the way which their fathers walked in, obeying the commandments of the LORD; but they did not so.

18 And when the LORD raised them up judges, then the LORD was with the judge, and delivered them out of the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge: for it repented the LORD because of their groanings by reason of them that oppressed them and vexed them.

19 And it came to pass, when the judge was dead, that they returned, and corrupted themselves more than their fathers, in following other gods to serve them, and to bow down unto them; they ceased not from their own doings, nor from their stubborn way.

20 And the anger of the LORD was hot against Israel; and he said, Because that this people hath transgressed my covenant which I commanded their fathers, and have not hearkened unto my voice;

21 I also will not henceforth drive out any from before them of the nations which Joshua left when he died:

22 That through them I may prove Israel, whether they will keep the way of the LORD to walk therein, as their fathers did keep it, or not.

23 Therefore the LORD left those nations, without driving them out hastily; neither delivered he them into the hand of Joshua.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The angel of the LORD rebukes Israel at Bochim for making leagues and leaving altars standing, and the people weep and name the place for weeping. Joshua dies, a new generation arises that does not know the LORD, and the book's whole pattern is stated: Israel serves Baalim, is sold to spoilers, cries out, and is delivered by judges raised up by the LORD, only to corrupt themselves again, more than their fathers, when the judge dies. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The angel of the Lord came from Gilgal to Bochim to call Israel to account, and Henry identifies this speaker as the great Angel of the covenant, the Son of God speaking with divine authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The charge was precise: Israel had made leagues with the inhabitants and left their altars standing, disobeying the voice that had commanded separation from idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. God therefore declared he would not drive the nations out, and that they would be thorns in Israel's sides and their gods a snare, making men's sin itself their punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. When the angel had spoken, the people lifted up their voice and wept, trembling at the word, though Henry observes that tears and even amendment cannot atone for sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They named the place Bochim, meaning weepers, and sacrificed there, so that the memory of the rebuke was fixed in the very name of the place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Joshua had sent the people away, each to his inheritance, closing the era of united conquest under one commander and leaving each tribe to finish its own lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The people served the Lord throughout Joshua's days and the days of the elders who had seen God's great works, showing how much a faithful generation can hold a nation steady. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Joshua the servant of the Lord died at a hundred and ten, ending the generation that had entered the land under God's hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. He was buried in Timnath-heres in Mount Ephraim, in the inheritance he himself had received, a leader laid to rest in the land he had helped to win. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Another generation arose that knew not the Lord nor his works, and Henry traces the whole dark history that follows to this failure of knowledge passed from fathers to children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Israel did evil in the sight of the Lord and served Baalim, exchanging the God who had delivered them for the gods of the peoples around them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. They forsook the Lord God of their fathers, followed other gods, bowed to them, and provoked the Lord to anger, a deliberate apostasy rather than a mere lapse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. They served Baal and Ashtaroth together, the male and female idols of Canaan, so that their worship became wholly conformed to their neighbours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The Lord's anger was hot against them, and he delivered them to spoilers and sold them into their enemies' hands, so that they could not stand before them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Wherever they went out, the hand of the Lord was against them for evil, as he had sworn, and they were greatly distressed, proving him as true to his threatenings as to his promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Nevertheless the Lord raised up judges who delivered them from the spoilers, and JFB explains that these judges were God's vicegerents, raised by a special call and the Spirit's impulse rather than by regular succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Yet Israel would not hearken to their judges but went whoring after other gods, turning quickly out of the way their fathers had walked, and Henry notes that those who forsake known good ways commonly grow most daring in sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. When the Lord raised a judge he was with him and delivered Israel all that judge's days, moved by their groanings under oppression, so that in the church's greatest distress God still finds or makes fit helpers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. But when the judge died they returned and corrupted themselves more than their fathers, following other gods and refusing to cease from their doings, so that no reformation outlived its reformer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The anger of the Lord was hot against Israel because this people had transgressed his covenant and not hearkened to his voice, and the sentence that follows is given as the reason for all the oppressions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. God declared he would no longer drive out the nations Joshua had left at his death, leaving the unfinished conquest as the standing consequence of Israel's disobedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Those nations were left to prove Israel, to test whether they would keep the way of the Lord as their fathers had, so that the enemy's presence became a trial of loyalty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The Lord therefore left those nations and did not speedily drive them out, nor deliver them into Joshua's hand, and Henry notes they were thus beaten with their own rod, left under the power of the sins they had cherished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Plain-language glosses for words in this chapter that a modern reader may stumble on. They explain the chapter text; they are not Scripture.
