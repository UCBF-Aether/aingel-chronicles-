The Text — Judith 6 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 And when the tumult of men that were about the council was ceased, Holofernes the chief captain of the army of Assur said unto Achior and all the Moabites before all the company of other nations,

2 And who art thou, Achior, and the hirelings of Ephraim, that thou hast prophesied against us as to day, and hast said, that we should not make war with the people of Israel, because their God will defend them? and who is God but Nabuchodonosor?

3 He will send his power, and will destroy them from the face of the earth, and their God shall not deliver them: but we his servants will destroy them as one man; for they are not able to sustain the power of our horses.

4 For with them we will tread them under foot, and their mountains shall be drunken with their blood, and their fields shall be filled with their dead bodies, and their footsteps shall not be able to stand before us, for they shall utterly perish, saith king Nabuchodonosor, lord of all the earth: for he said, None of my words shall be in vain.

5 And thou, Achior, an hireling of Ammon, which hast spoken these words in the day of thine iniquity, shalt see my face no more from this day, until I take vengeance of this nation that came out of Egypt.

6 And then shall the sword of mine army, and the multitude of them that serve me, pass through thy sides, and thou shalt fall among their slain, when I return.

7 Now therefore my servants shall bring thee back into the hill country, and shall set thee in one of the cities of the passages:

8 And thou shalt not perish, till thou be destroyed with them.

9 And if thou persuade thyself in thy mind that they shall be taken, let not thy countenance fall: I have spoken it, and none of my words shall be in vain.

10 Then Holofernes commanded his servants, that waited in his tent, to take Achior, and bring him to Bethulia, and deliver him into the hands of the children of Israel.

11 So his servants took him, and brought him out of the camp into the plain, and they went from the midst of the plain into the hill country, and came unto the fountains that were under Bethulia.

12 And when the men of the city saw them, they took up their weapons, and went out of the city to the top of the hill: and every man that used a sling kept them from coming up by casting of stones against them.

13 Nevertheless having gotten privily under the hill, they bound Achior, and cast him down, and left him at the foot of the hill, and returned to their lord.

14 But the Israelites descended from their city, and came unto him, and loosed him, and brought him to Bethulia, and presented him to the governors of the city:

15 Which were in those days Ozias the son of Micha, of the tribe of Simeon, and Chabris the son of Gothoniel, and Charmis the son of Melchiel.

16 And they called together all the ancients of the city, and all their youth ran together, and their women, to the assembly, and they set Achior in the midst of all their people. Then Ozias asked him of that which was done.

17 And he answered and declared unto them the words of the council of Holofernes, and all the words that he had spoken in the midst of the princes of Assur, and whatsoever Holofernes had spoken proudly against the house of Israel.

18 Then the people fell down and worshipped God, and cried unto God. saying,

19 O Lord God of heaven, behold their pride, and pity the low estate of our nation, and look upon the face of those that are sanctified unto thee this day.

20 Then they comforted Achior, and praised him greatly.

21 And Ozias took him out of the assembly unto his house, and made a feast to the elders; and they called on the God of Israel all that night for help.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Enraged at Achior's counsel, Holofernes orders him bound and delivered to Bethulia, to perish with the Israelites when the city falls. The men of Bethulia loose him, bring him to the assembly, and Ozias, Chabris, and Charmis hear his report; Ozias takes him home, makes a feast to the elders, and they call on the God of Israel all night for help. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Ozias. the ruler of Bethulia named in the chapter, son of Micha, of the tribe of Simeon (verse 15). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Assembly. the gathering of the city's ancients and people, called 'the church' in the study note's terms, where Achior is set in the midst (verse 16). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When the tumult of men about the council was ceased, Holofernes spoke unto Achior and all the Moabites; the Greek adds, "before all the populace of the strangers" — Achior stood before all this company of foreigners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. "And who art thou, Achior, and the hirelings of Ephraim, that thou hast prophesied against us as to day?" — the question is aimed at Achior before all the company of foreigners and the sons of Moab. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. He will send his power and destroy them from the face of the earth, and their God shall not deliver them; the Greek reads, "and who is God but Nabuchodonosor? He will" destroy them — they allow the title to none but their king, just as God will admit of no rival. Achior's generous advice was highly resented, as Charidemus, who spoke with like boldness to Darius when Alexander approached, was even put to death, though the king repented too late. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. Now therefore my servants shall bring thee back into the hill country and set thee in one of the cities of the passages; the Greek reads, "my slaves shall station thee on the mountainous country, and in one of the cities on the ascents, (of which thou hast so much spoken)." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. And thou shalt not perish till thou be destroyed with them; the Greek continues, "and thou shalt not perish till thou perish with them." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. And if thou persuade thyself that they shall be taken, let not thy countenance fall: I have spoken it, and none of my words shall be in vain; the Greek continues, "But if thou believest in thy heart that they will not be taken, let not thy countenance fall. I have spoken, and nothing that I have said shall be without effect." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. Then Holofernes commanded his servants to take Achior and bring him to Bethulia and deliver him into the hands of the children of Israel; Bethulia is placed by travellers, on uncertain traditions, in the tribe of Zebulon about three miles west of Tiberias, but St. Jerome places it near Egypt, and Scripture speaks of Bethul in the tribe of Simeon (Joshua 19:4), to which Judith and the ancients belonged. Holofernes left his camp near Scythopolis (chapter 7:1) when he proceeded to attack the southern countries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. The governors were in those days Ozias son of Micha of the tribe of Simeon, and Chabris son of Gothoniel, and Charmis son of Melchiel; the Greek adds, "Chabris, the son of Othoniel, and Charmis, the son of Melchiel" — the former is in effect mentioned in chapter 8:9. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. And they called together all the ancients of the city, and all their youth ran together, and their women, to the assembly, and they set Achior in the midst; "the church" here is the synagogue or place where they met in prayer — such places were established particularly after the captivity, though some have denied there were any synagogues even in the days of the Maccabees (see Matthew 4:23; Esther 4:16). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. "O Lord God of heaven, behold their pride, and pity the low estate of our nation, and look upon the face of those that are sanctified unto thee this day" — God will not fail to reward those who trust in him, and to humble the presumptuous; "saints" is in Syriac "sanctuary," and the Greek reads only, "Look upon the face of the persons (or things) sanctified unto thee on this day." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. Then they comforted Achior and praised him greatly; the Greek continues, "And they consoled Achior, and praised him greatly." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. And Ozias took him out of the assembly unto his house and made a feast to the elders, and they called on the God of Israel all that night for help; the Greek continues, "And Ozias took him from the assembly to his own house, and made a feast (literally, drinking) for the ancients, and they invoked the God of Israel to assist them during all that night." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
