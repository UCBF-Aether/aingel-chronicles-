The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 48 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Great is the LORD, and greatly to be praised in the city of our God, in the mountain of his holiness.

2 Beautiful for situation, the joy of the whole earth, is mount Zion, on the sides of the north, the city of the great King.

3 God is known in her palaces for a refuge.

4 For, lo, the kings were assembled, they passed by together.

5 They saw it, and so they marvelled; they were troubled, and hasted away.

6 Fear took hold upon them there, and pain, as of a woman in travail.

7 Thou breakest the ships of Tarshish with an east wind.

8 As we have heard, so have we seen in the city of the LORD of hosts, in the city of our God: God will establish it for ever. Selah.

9 We have thought of thy lovingkindness, O God, in the midst of thy temple.

10 According to thy name, O God, so is thy praise unto the ends of the earth: thy right hand is full of righteousness.

11 Let mount Zion rejoice, let the daughters of Judah be glad, because of thy judgments.

12 Walk about Zion, and go round about her: tell the towers thereof.

13 Mark ye well her bulwarks, consider her palaces; that ye may tell it to the generation following.

14 For this God is our God for ever and ever: he will be our guide even unto death.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Zion praised and inspected: great is the Lord in the city of our God, Mount Zion, beautiful, joy of the whole earth, city of the great King, where God is known in palaces for a refuge. Assembled kings saw and fled in travail-pain, ships of Tarshish broken. Walk about Zion, count towers, mark bulwarks, tell the next generation: this God is our God for ever, guide even unto death. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised in the city of our God, in the mountain of his holiness: greatness, great praise, and the city and mountain of holiness belong together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Beautiful for situation, the joy of the whole earth, is Mount Zion, on the sides of the north, the city of the great King: Zion's beauty and situation make it the joy of the whole earth, as the great King's city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God is known in her palaces for a refuge: palaces are the place where God is known — not for splendour alone, but as refuge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For, lo, the kings were assembled, they passed by together: assembled kings passing together picture a combined assault on Zion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. They saw it, and so they marvelled; they were troubled, and hasted away: sight produced marvel, trouble, and hasty flight — Zion's defence was seen, not fought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Fear took hold upon them there, and pain, as of a woman in travail: fear and travail-pain seized the attackers in the very place of their assault. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. You break the ships of Tarshish with an east wind: as an east wind shatters great merchant ships, so God shatters great kings' power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. As we have heard, so have we seen in the city of the Lord of hosts, in the city of our God; God will establish it for ever: heard history becomes seen fact in God's city, and establishment for ever is its future. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. We have thought of your lovingkindness, O God, in the midst of your temple: in the temple's midst, lovingkindness is meditated — worship remembers mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. According to your name, O God, so is your praise to the ends of the earth; your right hand is full of righteousness: praise as wide as God's name, to earth's ends, and a right hand full of righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Let Mount Zion rejoice, let the daughters of Judah be glad, because of your judgments: Zion's rejoicing and Judah's daughters' gladness answer God's judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Walk about Zion, and go round about her; tell the towers thereof: a walk round Zion, counting towers, is invited as an act of faith — inspect the defences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Mark well her bulwarks, consider her palaces, that you may tell it to the generation following: bulwarks marked and palaces considered become testimony to the next generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. For this God is our God for ever and ever; he will be our guide even unto death: the God of Zion is our God for ever, and guide even unto death — the psalm ends in lifelong and death-long guidance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
