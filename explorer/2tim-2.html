The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Timothy 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Thou therefore, my son, be strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus.

2 And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.

3 Thou therefore endure hardness, as a good soldier of Jesus Christ.

4 No man that warreth entangleth himself with the affairs of this life; that he may please him who hath chosen him to be a soldier.

5 And if a man also strive for masteries, yet is he not crowned, except he strive lawfully.

6 The husbandman that laboureth must be first partaker of the fruits.

7 Consider what I say; and the Lord give thee understanding in all things.

8 Remember that Jesus Christ of the seed of David was raised from the dead according to my gospel:

9 Wherein I suffer trouble, as an evil doer, even unto bonds; but the word of God is not bound.

10 Therefore I endure all things for the elect’s sakes, that they may also obtain the salvation which is in Christ Jesus with eternal glory.

11 It is a faithful saying: For if we be dead with him, we shall also live with him:

12 If we suffer, we shall also reign with him: if we deny him, he also will deny us:

13 If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself.

14 Of these things put them in remembrance, charging them before the Lord that they strive not about words to no profit, but to the subverting of the hearers.

15 Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth.

16 But shun profane and vain babblings: for they will increase unto more ungodliness.

17 And their word will eat as doth a canker: of whom is Hymenaeus and Philetus;

18 Who concerning the truth have erred, saying that the resurrection is past already; and overthrow the faith of some.

19 Nevertheless the foundation of God standeth sure, having this seal, The Lord knoweth them that are his. And, Let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity.

20 But in a great house there are not only vessels of gold and of silver, but also of wood and of earth; and some to honour, and some to dishonour.

21 If a man therefore purge himself from these, he shall be a vessel unto honour, sanctified, and meet for the master’s use, and prepared unto every good work.

22 Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart.

23 But foolish and unlearned questions avoid, knowing that they do gender strifes.

24 And the servant of the Lord must not strive; but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient,

25 In meekness instructing those that oppose themselves; if God peradventure will give them repentance to the acknowledging of the truth;

26 And that they may recover themselves out of the snare of the devil, who are taken captive by him at his will.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Be strong in grace, Paul tells Timothy, and entrust what he has heard to faithful people who can teach others. Soldier, athlete and farmer pictures press the point: endure hardship, compete by the rules, share in the harvest. Remember Jesus Christ risen from the dead, avoid quarrels over words and the claim that the resurrection has already happened, and be a vessel set apart, gentle in correcting opponents in hope God grants repentance. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. You therefore, my son, be strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus. Those who have work to do for God must stir themselves to do it, Henry says, and be strong not in their own strength, as Peter was when he promised rather to die than deny Christ, but in the grace that is in Christ Jesus, in whom there is grace enough for all of us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And the things that you have heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also. Timothy must train up successors, Henry notes, committing the gospel as a sacred trust to men chosen for fidelity and for ability to teach, so the office does not die with him, and none intrude themselves without such a commission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. You therefore endure hardness, as a good soldier of Jesus Christ. All Christians, but especially ministers, are soldiers of Jesus Christ, Henry says, fighting under his banner against his enemies, and must expect hardness, endure it, and not be moved by it from their integrity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. No man that wars entangles himself with the affairs of this life, that he may please him who has chosen him to be a soldier. A soldier leaves his calling to attend his captain's orders, Henry observes, and so we must sit loose to this world, that we may please him who chose us, free from entanglements that would hinder our holy warfare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And if a man also strive for masteries, yet is he not crowned, except he strive lawfully. We strive to master our lusts and excel in good, Henry says, but we cannot expect the prize unless we observe the laws, doing good in a right manner so that our good is not evil spoken of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The husbandman that labours must be first partaker of the fruits. If we would partake of the fruits, we must first labour as the husbandman does, with diligence and patience, Henry notes, doing the will of God before we receive the promises, for which reason we have need of patience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Consider what I say, and the Lord give you understanding in all things. Consideration is as necessary to a good conversation as to a sound conversion, Henry says, and it is God who gives understanding; the most intelligent man needs more of this gift, and without it the revelation in the word gives no understanding in the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Remember that Jesus Christ, of the seed of David, was raised from the dead according to my gospel. This is the great proof of his divine mission, Henry says, and the incarnation and resurrection of Christ, heartily believed and rightly considered, will support a Christian under all sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Wherein I suffer trouble, as an evil doer, even to bonds, but the word of God is not bound. Paul did good yet suffered as an evil-doer, Henry observes, but persecuting powers may silence ministers and restrain them while they cannot hinder the word of God upon men's hearts; that cannot be bound by any human force. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Therefore I endure all things for the elect's sakes, that they may also obtain the salvation which is in Christ Jesus with eternal glory. Good ministers may encourage themselves in the hardest sufferings with this, Henry says, that God will bring good to his church out of them, and next to our own salvation we should be willing to do and suffer anything to promote the salvation of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. It is a faithful saying: For if we be dead with him, we shall also live with him. If in conformity to Christ we be dead to this world, its pleasures, profits, and honours, Henry notes, we shall go to live with him in a better world, to be for ever with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. If we suffer, we shall also reign with him; if we deny him, he also will deny us. Those who suffered with David in his humiliation were preferred with him in his exaltation, Henry observes, and so it will be with those who suffer with the Son of David; but if we deny him before men out of fear or shame, he will deny us before his Father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. If we believe not, yet he abides faithful; he cannot deny himself. He is faithful to his threatenings and to his promises, Henry says, and neither shall fall to the ground; if we are faithful he will be faithful to us, and if we are false he will be faithful to his threatenings, for he cannot recede from any word he has spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Of these things put them in remembrance, charging them before the Lord that they strive not about words to no profit, but to the subverting of the hearers. The work of ministers is to put people in mind of what they know, Henry says, and strifes of words are not only useless but hurtful, drawing hearers from the great things of God and occasioning heats by which truth is often in danger of being lost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Study to show yourself approved to God, a workman that needs not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth. Ministers must be workmen who take pains in their work, Henry says, and their work is not to invent a new gospel but rightly to divide the gospel committed to their trust, speaking terror to whom terror belongs and comfort to whom comfort, giving every one his portion in due season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. But shun profane and vain babblings, for they will increase to more ungodliness. The heretics boasted of their notions, Henry notes, but the apostle calls them profane and vain babblings, and the way of error is downhill: one absurdity granted, a thousand follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And their word will eat as does a canker; of whom is Hymenaeus and Philetus. When errors come into the church, Henry observes, the infecting of one often proves the infecting of many, and the apostle names these corrupt teachers to brand them and warn all against hearkening to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Who concerning the truth have erred, saying that the resurrection is past already, and overthrow the faith of some. They did not deny the resurrection outright, Henry explains, but put a corrupt mystical interpretation on it, saying it meant a spiritual resurrection only, and to infer thence that there will be no real resurrection of the body is to dash one truth of Christ in pieces against another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Nevertheless the foundation of God stands sure, having this seal: The Lord knows them that are his. And, Let every one that names the name of Christ depart from iniquity. The unbelief of men cannot make God's promise of no effect, Henry says; the seal bears two mottoes, our comfort, that the Lord owns and will never lose his own, and our duty, that all who name Christ must depart from iniquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. But in a great house there are not only vessels of gold and of silver, but also of wood and of earth, and some to honour and some to dishonour. The church is a great house, Henry observes, and when discouraged by the badness of some we must encourage ourselves by the goodness of others, seeing to it that we be vessels of honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. If a man therefore purge himself from these, he shall be a vessel to honour, sanctified and meet for the master's use, and prepared to every good work. A man must purge himself from corrupt opinions before he can be a vessel of honour, Henry says, for sanctification in the heart is our preparation for every good work, as the tree must be made good before the fruit is good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Flee also youthful lusts, but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart. Though Timothy was a holy, mortified man, Paul still cautions him, Henry notes, and the exciting of our graces is the extinguishing of our corruptions: righteousness, faith, and love are excellent antidotes against youthful lusts, and the communion of saints takes us off from fellowship with unfruitful works of darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. But foolish and unlearned questions avoid, knowing that they do gender strifes. Those who advanced them thought themselves wise, Henry says, but Paul calls them foolish and unlearned, for they breed debates among Christians, and religion consists more in believing and practising what God requires than in subtle disputes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And the servant of the Lord must not strive, but be gentle to all men, apt to teach, patient. Nothing worse becomes the servant of the Lord, who himself did not strive nor cry, Henry observes, than strife; gentleness, aptness to teach, and patience show he is himself subject to the religion he preaches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. In meekness instructing those that oppose themselves, if God peradventure will give them repentance to the acknowledging of the truth. The erroneous are to be instructed, Henry says, for instruction is the Scripture method, likelier to convince than fire and faggot, and repentance is God's gift, with a peradventure in the case of opposers, so we must neither despair of grace nor presume upon it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And that they may recover themselves out of the snare of the devil, who are taken captive by him at his will. Sinners are in the devil's snare, slaves to the worst of taskmasters, Henry says, but when they repent the snare is broken and they escape, led from captivity into the glorious liberty of the children of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
