The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now Benjamin begat Bela his firstborn, Ashbel the second, and Aharah the third,

2 Nohah the fourth, and Rapha the fifth.

3 And the sons of Bela were, Addar, and Gera, and Abihud,

4 And Abishua, and Naaman, and Ahoah,

5 And Gera, and Shephuphan, and Huram.

6 And these are the sons of Ehud: these are the heads of the fathers of the inhabitants of Geba, and they removed them to Manahath:

7 And Naaman, and Ahiah, and Gera, he removed them, and begat Uzza, and Ahihud.

8 And Shaharaim begat children in the country of Moab, after he had sent them away; Hushim and Baara were his wives.

9 And he begat of Hodesh his wife, Jobab, and Zibia, and Mesha, and Malcham,

10 And Jeuz, and Shachia, and Mirma. These were his sons, heads of the fathers.

11 And of Hushim he begat Abitub, and Elpaal.

12 The sons of Elpaal; Eber, and Misham, and Shamed, who built Ono, and Lod, with the towns thereof:

13 Beriah also, and Shema, who were heads of the fathers of the inhabitants of Aijalon, who drove away the inhabitants of Gath:

14 And Ahio, Shashak, and Jeremoth,

15 And Zebadiah, and Arad, and Ader,

16 And Michael, and Ispah, and Joha, the sons of Beriah;

17 And Zebadiah, and Meshullam, and Hezeki, and Heber,

18 Ishmerai also, and Jezliah, and Jobab, the sons of Elpaal;

19 And Jakim, and Zichri, and Zabdi,

20 And Elienai, and Zilthai, and Eliel,

21 And Adaiah, and Beraiah, and Shimrath, the sons of Shimhi;

22 And Ishpan, and Heber, and Eliel,

23 And Abdon, and Zichri, and Hanan,

24 And Hananiah, and Elam, and Antothijah,

25 And Iphedeiah, and Penuel, the sons of Shashak;

26 And Shamsherai, and Shehariah, and Athaliah,

27 And Jaresiah, and Eliah, and Zichri, the sons of Jeroham.

28 These were heads of the fathers, by their generations, chief men. These dwelt in Jerusalem.

29 And at Gibeon dwelt the father of Gibeon; whose wife’s name was Maachah:

30 And his firstborn son Abdon, and Zur, and Kish, and Baal, and Nadab,

31 And Gedor, and Ahio, and Zacher.

32 And Mikloth begat Shimeah. And these also dwelt with their brethren in Jerusalem, over against them.

33 And Ner begat Kish, and Kish begat Saul, and Saul begat Jonathan, and Malchishua, and Abinadab, and Eshbaal.

34 And the son of Jonathan was Meribbaal; and Meribbaal begat Micah.

35 And the sons of Micah were, Pithon, and Melech, and Tarea, and Ahaz.

36 And Ahaz begat Jehoadah; and Jehoadah begat Alemeth, and Azmaveth, and Zimri; and Zimri begat Moza,

37 And Moza begat Binea: Rapha was his son, Eleasah his son, Azel his son:

38 And Azel had six sons, whose names are these, Azrikam, Bocheru, and Ishmael, and Sheariah, and Obadiah, and Hanan. All these were the sons of Azel.

39 And the sons of Eshek his brother were, Ulam his firstborn, Jehush the second, and Eliphelet the third.

40 And the sons of Ulam were mighty men of valour, archers, and had many sons, and sons’ sons, an hundred and fifty. All these are of the sons of Benjamin.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter is Benjamin's genealogy in full, ending with Saul's family: Kish, Saul, Jonathan, and their descendants down to Ulam's sons, the archers. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Benjamin begat Bela his firstborn, Ashbel, and Aharah. Henry explains this larger Benjamin catalogue is given because Saul came from Benjamin, and because Benjamin clave to Judah, dwelt much in Jerusalem, and shared captivity and return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. These are the sons of Ehud, heads of fathers of Geba's inhabitants, who removed them to Manahath. Henry inclines to identify this Ehud with the second judge, son of Gera the Benjamite, who delivered Israel from Moab. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Shaharaim begat children in Moab after he had sent them away, Hushim and Baara being his wives. Henry links his Moabite settlement to Ehud's sway in Moab after killing its king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Beriah and Shema were heads of fathers of Aijalon's inhabitants, who drove away the inhabitants of Gath. Henry suggests this was reprisal for the Gathites who slew the Ephraimites, and notes one avenger was also named Beriah, preserving the injury's memorial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. These were heads of the fathers, chief men, who dwelt in Jerusalem. Henry notes the repeated stress on Jerusalem dwellers was to induce returned captives to settle there, a post of danger few were willing to take. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Miklot begat Shimeah, and these also dwelt with their brethren in Jerusalem. Henry repeats the point: godly parents' conversation in the new Jerusalem should engage their children to set their faces thitherward whatever it cost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Ner begat Kish, and Kish begat Saul, and Saul begat Jonathan, Malchi-shua, Abinadab, and Esh-baal. Henry notes Kish is called son of Abiel elsewhere but son of Ner here, because he was Ner’s son and Abiel’s grandson, sons being used for grandsons in Hebrew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Jonathan's son was Merib-baal, and Merib-baal begat Micah. Henry notes after Saul, only Jonathan's posterity is traced, for about ten generations, honoured for Jonathan's sincere kindness to David and preserved for the covenant between their seeds forever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. The sons of Ulam were mighty men of valour, archers, with many sons and grandsons, 150. Henry notes Ulam's family became famous for valiant men, and that being qualified to serve one's country is more praise than pomp or wealth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
