The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And there appeared a great wonder in heaven; a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and upon her head a crown of twelve stars:

2 And she being with child cried, travailing in birth, and pained to be delivered.

3 And there appeared another wonder in heaven; and behold a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns, and seven crowns upon his heads.

4 And his tail drew the third part of the stars of heaven, and did cast them to the earth: and the dragon stood before the woman which was ready to be delivered, for to devour her child as soon as it was born.

5 And she brought forth a man child, who was to rule all nations with a rod of iron: and her child was caught up unto God, and to his throne.

6 And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she hath a place prepared of God, that they should feed her there a thousand two hundred and threescore days.

7 And there was war in heaven: Michael and his angels fought against the dragon; and the dragon fought and his angels,

8 And prevailed not; neither was their place found any more in heaven.

9 And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world: he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him.

10 And I heard a loud voice saying in heaven, Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night.

11 And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.

12 Therefore rejoice, ye heavens, and ye that dwell in them. Woe to the inhabiters of the earth and of the sea! for the devil is come down unto you, having great wrath, because he knoweth that he hath but a short time.

13 And when the dragon saw that he was cast unto the earth, he persecuted the woman which brought forth the man child.

14 And to the woman were given two wings of a great eagle, that she might fly into the wilderness, into her place, where she is nourished for a time, and times, and half a time, from the face of the serpent.

15 And the serpent cast out of his mouth water as a flood after the woman, that he might cause her to be carried away of the flood.

16 And the earth helped the woman, and the earth opened her mouth, and swallowed up the flood which the dragon cast out of his mouth.

17 And the dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A woman clothed with the sun, moon under her feet and a crown of twelve stars travails to bring forth a man child who is to rule all nations with a rod of iron, while a great red dragon with seven heads and ten horns waits to devour the child, who is caught up to God and his throne. War follows in heaven: Michael and his angels cast the dragon — identified in the text as that old serpent, the Devil and Satan — to the earth, where he persecutes the woman, who flees on eagles' wings into the wilderness, nourished for a time, times and half a time, and then makes war on the remnant of her seed who keep God's commandments and have the testimony of Jesus. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. There appeared a great wonder in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars. Henry reads the woman as the church, spouse of Christ and mother of saints, clothed in Christ's imputed righteousness as the Sun of righteousness, standing above the world, and crowned with the gospel doctrine preached by the twelve apostles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Being with child, she cried, travailing in birth, and pained to be delivered. Henry notes the church's travail: desiring that conviction begun in sinners would end in conversion, and that when children were brought to the birth there might be strength to bring forth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Another wonder appeared: a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns, and seven crowns upon his heads. Henry reads the dragon as Satan in strength and cruel fierceness, the seven heads as Rome on seven hills, the ten horns as the empire's ten provinces, and the seven crowns as seven kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. His tail drew the third part of the stars of heaven and cast them to the earth, and the dragon stood before the woman ready to devour her child as soon as it was born. Henry notes the dragon turned ministers and professors out of their places and stood vigilant to crush Christianity in its birth and prevent its growth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. She brought forth a man child, who was to rule all nations with a rod of iron, and her child was caught up unto God and to his throne. Henry takes the child as a race of true believers, strong and united, resembling Christ and ruling by doctrine and life now and as assessors with Christ at the great day, taken into God's special, powerful protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The woman fled into the wilderness, where she has a place prepared of God, that they should feed her there a thousand two hundred and threescore days. Henry notes the church's obscure, dispersed state proved her security through divine providence, though only for a limited time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. There was war in heaven: Michael and his angels fought against the dragon, and the dragon fought and his angels. Henry notes heaven espouses the church's quarrel: Christ, the great Angel of the covenant, with his faithful followers, against Satan and all his instruments, superior in outward strength but not in captain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The dragon and his angels prevailed not, neither was their place found any more in heaven. Henry notes the victory fell to Christ and his church, and pagan idolatry, a worshipping of devils, was extirpated from the empire in Constantine's time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world, cast out into the earth, and his angels cast out with him. Henry notes the cast-out is total: conquered and expelled from heaven's church, with his angels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. A loud voice in heaven said, "Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night." Henry notes the accuser, like against Job and Joshua, brought indictments continually, but now the indictments are quashed and the accuser turned out of court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. They overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony, and they loved not their lives unto the death. Henry notes the threefold victory: the Lamb's blood as meritorious cause, the gospel testimony as the Spirit's sword, and courage in suffering, since love of life was overcome by loyalty to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Therefore rejoice, you heavens and you who dwell in them, but woe to the inhabiters of the earth and of the sea, for the devil is come down to you, having great wrath, because he knows he has but a short time. Henry notes Satan's rage grows greater as he is limited in place and time, and being defeated against the church, he disturbs the world in general. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. When the dragon saw he was cast to the earth, he persecuted the woman who brought forth the man child. Henry notes the church's obscurity could not altogether protect her, as the old serpent followed her into the wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. To the woman were given two wings of a great eagle, that she might fly into the wilderness, into her place, where she is nourished for a time, and times, and half a time, from the face of the serpent. Henry notes God conveyed her as on eagles' wings to a place of safety for a certain space, in prophetic characters from Daniel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The serpent cast out of his mouth water as a flood after the woman, that he might cause her to be carried away by the flood. Henry reads the flood as error and heresy breathed by Arius, Nestorius, Pelagius, and many more, by which the church was in danger of being overwhelmed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The earth helped the woman, opened her mouth, and swallowed up the flood which the dragon cast out of his mouth. Henry suggests the Gothic and Vandal invasions gave the Arian rulers other work, so the flood was swallowed up and the church enjoyed respite, since God often sends the sword to avenge his covenant's quarrel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, who keep the commandments of God and have the testimony of Jesus Christ. Henry notes that, defeated against the universal church, the devil turns against particular persons, and that fidelity in doctrine, worship, and practice is what exposes believers to his rage to the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • The woman clothed with the sun. Henry reads the woman as the church, spouse of Christ and mother of believers, travailing to bring forth children to God. Interpreted Study note, verse 1, Matthew Henry
  • Time, times, and half a time. The period for which the woman is nourished in the wilderness, expressed in prophetic characters from Daniel, in Henry's note. Interpreted Study note, verse 14, Matthew Henry

Connections to other passages
