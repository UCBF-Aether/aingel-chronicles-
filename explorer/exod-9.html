The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then the LORD said unto Moses, Go in unto Pharaoh, and tell him, Thus saith the LORD God of the Hebrews, Let my people go, that they may serve me.

2 For if thou refuse to let them go, and wilt hold them still,

3 Behold, the hand of the LORD is upon thy cattle which is in the field, upon the horses, upon the asses, upon the camels, upon the oxen, and upon the sheep: there shall be a very grievous murrain.

4 And the LORD shall sever between the cattle of Israel and the cattle of Egypt: and there shall nothing die of all that is the children’s of Israel.

5 And the LORD appointed a set time, saying, To morrow the LORD shall do this thing in the land.

6 And the LORD did that thing on the morrow, and all the cattle of Egypt died: but of the cattle of the children of Israel died not one.

7 And Pharaoh sent, and, behold, there was not one of the cattle of the Israelites dead. And the heart of Pharaoh was hardened, and he did not let the people go.

8 And the LORD said unto Moses and unto Aaron, Take to you handfuls of ashes of the furnace, and let Moses sprinkle it toward the heaven in the sight of Pharaoh.

9 And it shall become small dust in all the land of Egypt, and shall be a boil breaking forth with blains upon man, and upon beast, throughout all the land of Egypt.

10 And they took ashes of the furnace, and stood before Pharaoh; and Moses sprinkled it up toward heaven; and it became a boil breaking forth with blains upon man, and upon beast.

11 And the magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils; for the boil was upon the magicians, and upon all the Egyptians.

12 And the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had spoken unto Moses.

13 And the LORD said unto Moses, Rise up early in the morning, and stand before Pharaoh, and say unto him, Thus saith the LORD God of the Hebrews, Let my people go, that they may serve me.

14 For I will at this time send all my plagues upon thine heart, and upon thy servants, and upon thy people; that thou mayest know that there is none like me in all the earth.

15 For now I will stretch out my hand, that I may smite thee and thy people with pestilence; and thou shalt be cut off from the earth.

16 And in very deed for this cause have I raised thee up, for to shew in thee my power; and that my name may be declared throughout all the earth.

17 As yet exaltest thou thyself against my people, that thou wilt not let them go?

18 Behold, to morrow about this time I will cause it to rain a very grievous hail, such as hath not been in Egypt since the foundation thereof even until now.

19 Send therefore now, and gather thy cattle, and all that thou hast in the field; for upon every man and beast which shall be found in the field, and shall not be brought home, the hail shall come down upon them, and they shall die.

20 He that feared the word of the LORD among the servants of Pharaoh made his servants and his cattle flee into the houses:

21 And he that regarded not the word of the LORD left his servants and his cattle in the field.

22 And the LORD said unto Moses, Stretch forth thine hand toward heaven, that there may be hail in all the land of Egypt, upon man, and upon beast, and upon every herb of the field, throughout the land of Egypt.

23 And Moses stretched forth his rod toward heaven: and the LORD sent thunder and hail, and the fire ran along upon the ground; and the LORD rained hail upon the land of Egypt.

24 So there was hail, and fire mingled with the hail, very grievous, such as there was none like it in all the land of Egypt since it became a nation.

25 And the hail smote throughout all the land of Egypt all that was in the field, both man and beast; and the hail smote every herb of the field, and brake every tree of the field.

26 Only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel were, was there no hail.

27 And Pharaoh sent, and called for Moses and Aaron, and said unto them, I have sinned this time: the LORD is righteous, and I and my people are wicked.

28 Intreat the LORD (for it is enough) that there be no more mighty thunderings and hail; and I will let you go, and ye shall stay no longer.

29 And Moses said unto him, As soon as I am gone out of the city, I will spread abroad my hands unto the LORD; and the thunder shall cease, neither shall there be any more hail; that thou mayest know how that the earth is the LORD’s.

30 But as for thee and thy servants, I know that ye will not yet fear the LORD God.

31 And the flax and the barley was smitten: for the barley was in the ear, and the flax was bolled.

32 But the wheat and the rie were not smitten: for they were not grown up.

33 And Moses went out of the city from Pharaoh, and spread abroad his hands unto the LORD: and the thunders and hail ceased, and the rain was not poured upon the earth.

34 And when Pharaoh saw that the rain and the hail and the thunders were ceased, he sinned yet more, and hardened his heart, he and his servants.

35 And the heart of Pharaoh was hardened, neither would he let the children of Israel go; as the LORD had spoken by Moses.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A murrain kills Egypt's livestock, boils break out on man and beast, and then a devastating hailstorm, announced in advance, strikes crops, trees, and all left in the field. Pharaoh confesses sin and asks for prayer, but when the hail stops he sins yet more and hardens his heart, refusing to let Israel go. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD told Moses to go in to Pharaoh with the same demand from the LORD God of the Hebrews: Let my people go, that they may serve me. Henry notes the fifth plague was prefaced by the same unchanging claim, so Pharaoh could not say he was unwarned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. If Pharaoh refused and held Israel still, the judgment would follow. Henry observes the condition was now explicit: continued detention was itself the crime that drew the stroke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The hand of the LORD would fall upon Egypt's cattle in the field, horses, asses, camels, oxen, and sheep, with a very grievous murrain. Henry notes the plague struck Egypt's wealth and its work, for cattle were the nation's riches and its gods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The LORD would sever between the cattle of Israel and the cattle of Egypt, so that nothing of Israel's would die. Henry observes the distinction was now extended to beasts: God's care marked his people's property as well as their persons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The LORD appointed a set time: tomorrow he would do this thing in the land. Henry notes the appointed time removed all excuse of chance, for the plague came on schedule, not by accident. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The LORD did that thing on the morrow, and all the cattle of Egypt died, but of Israel's cattle there died not one. Henry observes the completeness on both sides: total loss in Egypt, total preservation in Goshen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Pharaoh sent and found not one of Israel's cattle dead, yet his heart was hardened and he would not let the people go. Henry notes inquiry confirmed the wonder and still did not soften him: evidence alone does not convert. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The LORD told Moses and Aaron to take handfuls of ashes of the furnace and sprinkle them toward heaven in Pharaoh's sight. Henry observes the sign came from Egypt's own furnaces, the instruments of Israel's brick-making, now turned to judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The ashes would become small dust over all Egypt, and a boil breaking forth with blains upon man and beast. Henry notes the plague touched the body itself: after property and comfort, judgment reached the flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They took the ashes, stood before Pharaoh, and Moses sprinkled them toward heaven, and boils broke out upon man and beast. Henry observes the wonder was wrought in the king's presence, that he might see his own skin bear the sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils, for the boil was upon them and upon all Egyptians. Henry notes the final humiliation of the counterfeiters: they who imitated wonders could not endure this one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he hearkened not, as the LORD had spoken to Moses. Henry observes the hardening is now stated as God's act, the judicial sealing of a heart that had hardened itself six times over. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Moses was told to rise early and stand before Pharaoh with the demand from the LORD God of the Hebrews. Henry notes the morning summons came again, for Pharaoh was to hear God's claim before every new stroke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. At this time God would send all his plagues upon Pharaoh's heart, his servants, and his people, that he might know there is none like God in all the earth. Henry observes the coming hail was announced as a plague upon the heart itself, aimed at pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. God said he could now stretch out his hand and smite Pharaoh and his people with pestilence, cutting them off from the earth. Henry notes the restraint shown: Egypt was spared utter destruction only that God's power might be displayed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. For this very cause God had raised Pharaoh up, to show his power in him and declare his name throughout all the earth. Henry observes Pharaoh's greatness was itself God's instrument: the mightier the tyrant, the louder the deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. As yet exaltest thou thyself against my people, that thou wilt not let them go? Henry notes the charge went to the root: Pharaoh's sin was self-exaltation, setting his throne against God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Tomorrow about this time God would cause a very grievous hail, such as had not been in Egypt since its foundation. Henry observes the warning was precise in time and unmatched in severity, leaving no room for doubt when it fell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Pharaoh was told to send and gather his cattle and all he had in the field, for every man and beast left in the field would die. Henry notes mercy mingled with judgment: even now a way of escape was offered to whoever would heed the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. He that feared the word of the LORD among Pharaoh's servants made his servants and cattle flee into the houses. Henry observes the first Egyptians who believed: fear of God's word saved them, and their obedience marked them out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. He that regarded not the word of the LORD left his servants and cattle in the field. Henry notes the division in Egypt was now moral, not only national: those who despised the warning perished by their own contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Moses was told to stretch his hand toward heaven, that hail might fall on man, beast, herb, and tree throughout Egypt. Henry observes the plague reached from heaven itself, where Egypt's gods were supposed to reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Moses stretched his rod toward heaven, and the LORD sent thunder and hail, and fire ran along upon the ground. Henry notes the storm joined two elements at war with each other, hail and fire together, a wonder no nature could explain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. There was hail and fire mingled with the hail, very grievous, such as there was none like in Egypt since it became a nation. Henry observes the unprecedented severity matched the unprecedented warning: God had said it would be unmatched, and it was. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The hail smote throughout Egypt all in the field, both man and beast, and every herb, and broke every tree. Henry notes the ruin was universal in the open country, sparing only what shelter had hidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Only in the land of Goshen, where Israel was, there was no hail. Henry observes the storm drew a line on the map: one province calm while the nation shook, that all might see whose people Israel were. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Pharaoh sent and called Moses and Aaron, confessing, I have sinned this time; the LORD is righteous, and I and my people are wicked. Henry notes the confession was fuller than any before, naming both sin and God's righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Pharaoh asked them to intreat the LORD that there be no more mighty thunderings and hail, promising to let them go and stay no longer. Henry observes the promise was absolute this time, yet made under thunder, and thunder would soon pass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Moses answered that as soon as he was gone out of the city he would spread abroad his hands, and the thunder would cease and the hail be no more, that Pharaoh might know the earth is the LORD's. Henry notes Moses prayed outside the city, away from idols, and the prayer claimed the whole earth for God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. But as for thee and thy servants, said Moses, I know ye will not yet fear the LORD God. Henry observes Moses spoke the truth to Pharaoh's face even while interceding for him: the prayer was granted, but the prophet did not flatter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. The flax and the barley were smitten, for the barley was in the ear and the flax was bolled. Henry notes the detail of the ruin: the early crops perished, a blow at Egypt's bread and linen alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. But the wheat and the rie were not smitten, for they were not grown up. Henry observes a double providence: the later crops survived both to feed Egypt and to be smitten later, if Pharaoh persisted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Moses went out of the city, spread abroad his hands unto the LORD, and the thunders and hail ceased, and the rain was not poured upon the earth. Henry notes the answer was immediate and complete, matching the prayer exactly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. When Pharaoh saw the rain, hail, and thunders cease, he sinned yet more, and hardened his heart, he and his servants. Henry observes the saddest line in the chapter: mercy received became the occasion of greater sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Pharaoh's heart was hardened, neither would he let Israel go, as the LORD had spoken by Moses. Henry closes on the fixed pattern: the king who confessed under thunder denied under sunshine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
