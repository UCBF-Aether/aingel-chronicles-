The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now after this it came to pass, that David smote the Philistines, and subdued them, and took Gath and her towns out of the hand of the Philistines.

2 And he smote Moab; and the Moabites became David’s servants, and brought gifts.

3 And David smote Hadarezer king of Zobah unto Hamath, as he went to stablish his dominion by the river Euphrates.

4 And David took from him a thousand chariots, and seven thousand horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen: David also houghed all the chariot horses, but reserved of them an hundred chariots.

5 And when the Syrians of Damascus came to help Hadarezer king of Zobah, David slew of the Syrians two and twenty thousand men.

6 Then David put garrisons in Syriadamascus; and the Syrians became David’s servants, and brought gifts. Thus the LORD preserved David whithersoever he went.

7 And David took the shields of gold that were on the servants of Hadarezer, and brought them to Jerusalem.

8 Likewise from Tibhath, and from Chun, cities of Hadarezer, brought David very much brass, wherewith Solomon made the brasen sea, and the pillars, and the vessels of brass.

9 Now when Tou king of Hamath heard how David had smitten all the host of Hadarezer king of Zobah;

10 He sent Hadoram his son to king David, to enquire of his welfare, and to congratulate him, because he had fought against Hadarezer, and smitten him; (for Hadarezer had war with Tou;) and with him all manner of vessels of gold and silver and brass.

11 Them also king David dedicated unto the LORD, with the silver and the gold that he brought from all these nations; from Edom, and from Moab, and from the children of Ammon, and from the Philistines, and from Amalek.

12 Moreover Abishai the son of Zeruiah slew of the Edomites in the valley of salt eighteen thousand.

13 And he put garrisons in Edom; and all the Edomites became David’s servants. Thus the LORD preserved David whithersoever he went.

14 So David reigned over all Israel, and executed judgment and justice among all his people.

15 And Joab the son of Zeruiah was over the host; and Jehoshaphat the son of Ahilud, recorder.

16 And Zadok the son of Ahitub, and Abimelech the son of Abiathar, were the priests; and Shavsha was scribe;

17 And Benaiah the son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites; and the sons of David were chief about the king.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter recounts David's wars — against the Philistines, Moab, Syria, and Edom — the tribute he gathered, and the officers who administered his kingdom. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After this David smote the Philistines, subdued them, and took Gath and her towns out of their hand. Henry notes after sweet communion with God in the last chapter, David went on with extraordinary vigour, conquering and to conquer, and long enemies of Israel are brought down at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He smote Moab, and the Moabites became David's servants and brought gifts. Henry lists Moab among the conquests abroad that followed David's piety and prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. David smote Hadarezer king of Zobah unto Hamath, as he went to stablish his dominion by the river Euphrates. Henry notes the uncertainty of the world: Hadarezer was smitten as he went to establish his dominion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. David took from him a thousand chariots, seven thousand horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen, and houghed all the chariot horses but reserved a hundred chariots. Henry notes David believed a horse is a vain thing for safety, and resolved not to trust in chariots. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. When the Syrians of Damascus came to help Hadarezer, David slew twenty-two thousand Syrians. Henry notes enemies of God's church often ruin themselves by helping one another, gathered as sheaves into the floor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Then David put garrisons in Syria-damascus, and the Syrians became his servants and brought gifts, and the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went. Henry notes the wealth of the sinner laid up for the just: Syrian gifts, gold shields, and brass came to Jerusalem, as the temple would be built from Gentile spoils. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. David took the shields of gold on Hadarezer's servants and brought them to Jerusalem. Henry counts the gold shields among the spoils dedicated to the temple's enriching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. From Tibhath and Chun, cities of Hadarezer, David took very much brass, wherewith Solomon made the brasen sea, pillars, and vessels of brass. Henry notes the temple itself was built of Gentile spoils, a presage of Gentile interest in the gospel church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. When Tou king of Hamath heard how David had smitten all Hadarezer's host, he sent to David. Henry notes it is our interest to make friends of those who have God's presence: Tou courted David's favour rather than contending with the Son of David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. He sent Hadoram his son to enquire of David's welfare and congratulate him, because he had fought Hadarezer, with vessels of gold, silver, and brass. Henry notes the noble present, and that the presents we bring Christ are not gold vessels but hearts and whole selves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Them also David dedicated unto the Lord, with silver and gold from all nations: Edom, Moab, Ammon, Philistines, and Amalek. Henry notes what God blesses us with we must honour him with: friends' presents and enemies' spoils alike were laid up for the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Moreover Abishai son of Zeruiah slew eighteen thousand Edomites in the valley of salt. Henry lists the Edomite conquest among David's prosperity abroad. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. He put garrisons in Edom, and all Edomites became David's servants, and the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went. Henry repeats the refrain: those who take God along whithersoever they go may expect to prosper and be preserved whithersoever they go. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. So David reigned over all Israel, and executed judgment and justice among all his people. Henry notes God gives power not for looking great but for doing good: David was not so intent on conquests abroad as to neglect justice at home, an eminent type of the Messiah whose sceptre is right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Joab son of Zeruiah was over the host, and Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud, recorder. Henry lists the flourishing court: Joab commanding the army, Jehoshaphat recording. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Zadok son of Ahitub and Abimelech son of Abiathar were priests, and Shavsha was scribe. Henry notes the priests and scribe among the court officers in David's settled kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and Pelethites, and David's sons were chief about the king. Henry closes the court list with the bodyguard captain and the king's sons as chief about him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
