The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now after the death of Joshua it came to pass, that the children of Israel asked the LORD, saying, Who shall go up for us against the Canaanites first, to fight against them?

2 And the LORD said, Judah shall go up: behold, I have delivered the land into his hand.

3 And Judah said unto Simeon his brother, Come up with me into my lot, that we may fight against the Canaanites; and I likewise will go with thee into thy lot. So Simeon went with him.

4 And Judah went up; and the LORD delivered the Canaanites and the Perizzites into their hand: and they slew of them in Bezek ten thousand men.

5 And they found Adonibezek in Bezek: and they fought against him, and they slew the Canaanites and the Perizzites.

6 But Adonibezek fled; and they pursued after him, and caught him, and cut off his thumbs and his great toes.

7 And Adonibezek said, Threescore and ten kings, having their thumbs and their great toes cut off, gathered their meat under my table: as I have done, so God hath requited me. And they brought him to Jerusalem, and there he died.

8 Now the children of Judah had fought against Jerusalem, and had taken it, and smitten it with the edge of the sword, and set the city on fire.

9 And afterward the children of Judah went down to fight against the Canaanites, that dwelt in the mountain, and in the south, and in the valley.

10 And Judah went against the Canaanites that dwelt in Hebron: (now the name of Hebron before was Kirjatharba:) and they slew Sheshai, and Ahiman, and Talmai.

11 And from thence he went against the inhabitants of Debir: and the name of Debir before was Kirjathsepher:

12 And Caleb said, He that smiteth Kirjathsepher, and taketh it, to him will I give Achsah my daughter to wife.

13 And Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother, took it: and he gave him Achsah his daughter to wife.

14 And it came to pass, when she came to him, that she moved him to ask of her father a field: and she lighted from off her ass; and Caleb said unto her, What wilt thou?

15 And she said unto him, Give me a blessing: for thou hast given me a south land; give me also springs of water. And Caleb gave her the upper springs and the nether springs.

16 And the children of the Kenite, Moses’ father in law, went up out of the city of palm trees with the children of Judah into the wilderness of Judah, which lieth in the south of Arad; and they went and dwelt among the people.

17 And Judah went with Simeon his brother, and they slew the Canaanites that inhabited Zephath, and utterly destroyed it. And the name of the city was called Hormah.

18 Also Judah took Gaza with the coast thereof, and Askelon with the coast thereof, and Ekron with the coast thereof.

19 And the LORD was with Judah; and he drave out the inhabitants of the mountain; but could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley, because they had chariots of iron.

20 And they gave Hebron unto Caleb, as Moses said: and he expelled thence the three sons of Anak.

21 And the children of Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites that inhabited Jerusalem; but the Jebusites dwell with the children of Benjamin in Jerusalem unto this day.

22 And the house of Joseph, they also went up against Bethel: and the LORD was with them.

23 And the house of Joseph sent to descry Bethel. (Now the name of the city before was Luz.)

24 And the spies saw a man come forth out of the city, and they said unto him, Shew us, we pray thee, the entrance into the city, and we will shew thee mercy.

25 And when he shewed them the entrance into the city, they smote the city with the edge of the sword; but they let go the man and all his family.

26 And the man went into the land of the Hittites, and built a city, and called the name thereof Luz: which is the name thereof unto this day.

27 Neither did Manasseh drive out the inhabitants of Bethshean and her towns, nor Taanach and her towns, nor the inhabitants of Dor and her towns, nor the inhabitants of Ibleam and her towns, nor the inhabitants of Megiddo and her towns: but the Canaanites would dwell in that land.

28 And it came to pass, when Israel was strong, that they put the Canaanites to tribute, and did not utterly drive them out.

29 Neither did Ephraim drive out the Canaanites that dwelt in Gezer; but the Canaanites dwelt in Gezer among them.

30 Neither did Zebulun drive out the inhabitants of Kitron, nor the inhabitants of Nahalol; but the Canaanites dwelt among them, and became tributaries.

31 Neither did Asher drive out the inhabitants of Accho, nor the inhabitants of Zidon, nor of Ahlab, nor of Achzib, nor of Helbah, nor of Aphik, nor of Rehob:

32 But the Asherites dwelt among the Canaanites, the inhabitants of the land: for they did not drive them out.

33 Neither did Naphtali drive out the inhabitants of Bethshemesh, nor the inhabitants of Bethanath; but he dwelt among the Canaanites, the inhabitants of the land: nevertheless the inhabitants of Bethshemesh and of Bethanath became tributaries unto them.

34 And the Amorites forced the children of Dan into the mountain: for they would not suffer them to come down to the valley:

35 But the Amorites would dwell in mount Heres in Aijalon, and in Shaalbim: yet the hand of the house of Joseph prevailed, so that they became tributaries.

36 And the coast of the Amorites was from the going up to Akrabbim, from the rock, and upward.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After Joshua's death Israel asks who should lead the renewed war, and Judah goes first with Simeon's help, winning at Bezek, Hebron and Debir, where Caleb's offer of his daughter Achsah is won by Othniel. The chapter then turns tribe by tribe to the opposite result: Benjamin, Manasseh, Ephraim, Zebulun, Asher, Naphtali and Dan fail to drive out the inhabitants, settling instead for tribute or for dwelling among them, so that the conquest is left half-finished. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After Joshua's death the tribes did not presume on their own counsel but inquired of the Lord who should lead the renewed war, showing that direction must be sought from God when a former leader is gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. God appointed Judah to go first, and Henry notes the pattern that the tribe first in dignity must be first in duty, with success promised yet still requiring Judah's own action. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Judah, the strongest tribe, begged help from Simeon, the least, and Henry draws the lesson that the strong should desire the help of the weak and that mutual aid in a common cause may hope for God's help to both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The Lord delivered the Canaanites and Perizzites into Judah's hand at Bezek, where ten thousand were slain, showing that the promised success followed obedience in going up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Adoni-bezek, the local king, was found at Bezek and fought there, so that the campaign struck at the enemy's leadership as well as his army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Adoni-bezek fled, was caught, and had his thumbs and great toes cut off, a severe but deliberate act that disabled a warlord who had lived by conquest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Adoni-bezek himself confessed the justice of it, having done the same to seventy kings, and Henry notes how God in providence often makes the punishment answer the sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Judah then fought against Jerusalem, took it, struck it with the sword and set it on fire, pressing the conquest from the field into the enemy's strong city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Judah went on against the Canaanites of the mountains, the south, and the lowland, carrying the war into every kind of country rather than settling for one victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. At Hebron Judah slew Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai, striking down the famed Anakim whose size had once terrified Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. From Hebron Judah advanced against Debir, formerly Kirjath-sepher, showing the campaign moving steadily from one fortified city to the next. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Caleb offered his daughter Achsah to whoever took Kirjath-sepher, using honour and family reward to stir courage for a hard city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Othniel, Caleb's younger kinsman, took the city and married Achsah, and Henry's account presents him as a man already rising toward the judgeship he would later hold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Achsah urged Othniel to ask for a field, and herself alighted from her ass to press her father, showing a household that sought provision boldly rather than passively. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Caleb gave her the upper and lower springs as well as the land, so that the gift was made fruitful with water, the very thing that makes a southern holding liveable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The Kenites, a quiet and contented people who molested none, were allowed to settle where they pleased among Judah, and Henry notes that the meek are left unmolested and inherit the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Judah and Simeon together smote Zephath and utterly destroyed it, renaming it Hormah, a joint victory that repaid Simeon's help in kind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Judah also took Gaza, Askelon, and Ekron with their coasts, extending the conquest along the Philistine plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The Lord was with Judah in the mountains, yet they could not drive out the valley dwellers because of their iron chariots, and Henry blames not the chariots but fear prevailing against faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Hebron was given to Caleb, who drove out the three sons of Anak, fulfilling the old promise to the faithful spy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Benjamin failed to drive the Jebusites from Jerusalem, so that enemy and Israelite dwelt together there, the first of many half-conquests that stored up later trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The house of Joseph went up against Bethel with the Lord with them, a venture that began well where other tribes had already failed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Joseph's spies watched a man coming out of Bethel, using careful reconnaissance rather than a blind assault. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. The spies promised the man mercy if he showed them the entrance, trading life for the intelligence that would open the city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The man showed the entrance, the city was smitten, and he and his family were spared, so that mercy and judgment fell in the same hour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The spared man went to the Hittites and built Luz, carrying the old city's name into a new place, a small picture of how incomplete victories scatter rather than end the enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Manasseh failed to take Beth-shean and its towns, and the Canaanites continued to dwell in the land, so that a strong tribe left its own inheritance half-won. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. When Israel grew strong they put the Canaanites to tribute instead of driving them out, and Henry names covetousness alongside sloth and cowardice: they kept the enemy for profit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Ephraim likewise failed to drive out Gezer's Canaanites, who dwelt among them, repeating the same compromise in Joseph's other half-tribe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Zebulun failed to drive out Kitron and Nahalol, settling instead for tribute from the people they were commanded to dispossess. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Asher failed worst of all, leaving Acco, Sidon, and several other cities untaken, and Henry says Asher truckled to the Canaanites more than any tribe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Asher therefore dwelt among the Canaanites instead of ruling over them, the guest becoming the tolerated neighbour of the people he should have removed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Naphtali failed at Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath, and again the Canaanites became tributaries rather than exiles, a half-measure Henry traces to unbelief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The Amorites forced Dan into the mountains and would not let them come down to the valley, so that Dan was straitened and kept out of the full possession of its lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The Amorites held their ground in several towns until the house of Joseph prevailed and put them to tribute, a late and partial recovery of what should have been a complete conquest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. The Amorite border ran from the ascent of Akrabbim upward, marking on the map how much ground Israel had left unconquered through carelessness of duty and interest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
