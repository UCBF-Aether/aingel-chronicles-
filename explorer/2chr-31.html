The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 31 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now when all this was finished, all Israel that were present went out to the cities of Judah, and brake the images in pieces, and cut down the groves, and threw down the high places and the altars out of all Judah and Benjamin, in Ephraim also and Manasseh, until they had utterly destroyed them all. Then all the children of Israel returned, every man to his possession, into their own cities.

2 And Hezekiah appointed the courses of the priests and the Levites after their courses, every man according to his service, the priests and Levites for burnt offerings and for peace offerings, to minister, and to give thanks, and to praise in the gates of the tents of the LORD.

3 He appointed also the king’s portion of his substance for the burnt offerings, to wit, for the morning and evening burnt offerings, and the burnt offerings for the sabbaths, and for the new moons, and for the set feasts, as it is written in the law of the LORD.

4 Moreover he commanded the people that dwelt in Jerusalem to give the portion of the priests and the Levites, that they might be encouraged in the law of the LORD.

5 And as soon as the commandment came abroad, the children of Israel brought in abundance the firstfruits of corn, wine, and oil, and honey, and of all the increase of the field; and the tithe of all things brought they in abundantly.

6 And concerning the children of Israel and Judah, that dwelt in the cities of Judah, they also brought in the tithe of oxen and sheep, and the tithe of holy things which were consecrated unto the LORD their God, and laid them by heaps.

7 In the third month they began to lay the foundation of the heaps, and finished them in the seventh month.

8 And when Hezekiah and the princes came and saw the heaps, they blessed the LORD, and his people Israel.

9 Then Hezekiah questioned with the priests and the Levites concerning the heaps.

10 And Azariah the chief priest of the house of Zadok answered him, and said, Since the people began to bring the offerings into the house of the LORD, we have had enough to eat, and have left plenty: for the LORD hath blessed his people; and that which is left is this great store.

11 Then Hezekiah commanded to prepare chambers in the house of the LORD; and they prepared them,

12 And brought in the offerings and the tithes and the dedicated things faithfully: over which Cononiah the Levite was ruler, and Shimei his brother was the next.

13 And Jehiel, and Azaziah, and Nahath, and Asahel, and Jerimoth, and Jozabad, and Eliel, and Ismachiah, and Mahath, and Benaiah, were overseers under the hand of Cononiah and Shimei his brother, at the commandment of Hezekiah the king, and Azariah the ruler of the house of God.

14 And Kore the son of Imnah the Levite, the porter toward the east, was over the freewill offerings of God, to distribute the oblations of the LORD, and the most holy things.

15 And next him were Eden, and Miniamin, and Jeshua, and Shemaiah, Amariah, and Shecaniah, in the cities of the priests, in their set office, to give to their brethren by courses, as well to the great as to the small:

16 Beside their genealogy of males, from three years old and upward, even unto every one that entereth into the house of the LORD, his daily portion for their service in their charges according to their courses;

17 Both to the genealogy of the priests by the house of their fathers, and the Levites from twenty years old and upward, in their charges by their courses;

18 And to the genealogy of all their little ones, their wives, and their sons, and their daughters, through all the congregation: for in their set office they sanctified themselves in holiness:

19 Also of the sons of Aaron the priests, which were in the fields of the suburbs of their cities, in every several city, the men that were expressed by name, to give portions to all the males among the priests, and to all that were reckoned by genealogies among the Levites.

20 And thus did Hezekiah throughout all Judah, and wrought that which was good and right and truth before the LORD his God.

21 And in every work that he began in the service of the house of God, and in the law, and in the commandments, to seek his God, he did it with all his heart, and prospered.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the breaking of idols after the passover, the bringing of tithes and offerings in abundance, the chambers prepared to store them, and Hezekiah's faithful ordering of it all. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Now when all this was finished, all Israel present went out to Judah's cities, and brake the images in pieces, and cut down the groves, and threw down the high places and altars out of all Judah and Benjamin, in Ephraim also and Manasseh, until they had utterly destroyed them all. Then all Israel's children returned, every man to his possession, into their own cities. Henry notes what was wanting in preparation before the passover was made up in due improvement after, for when ordinances finish, the hardest work begins, exemplifying impressions in holy conversation, and the people applied themselves with vigour to destroy idolatry's monuments, utterly, sparing none however ancient, costly or beautiful, and would not return home till done, for communion's comfort should kindle holy zeal against sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And Hezekiah appointed the courses of the priests and Levites after their courses, every man according to his service, the priests and Levites for burnt offerings and peace offerings, to minister, and to give thanks, and to praise in the gates of the tents of the Lord. Henry notes Hezekiah revived David's courses, put out of course of late, putting temple service into its proper method again, every man knowing his work, place, time and what was expected, for good order contributes much to a good work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He appointed also the king's portion of his substance for the burnt offerings, to wit, for the morning and evening burnt offerings, and the burnt offerings for the sabbaths, and for the new moons, and for the set feasts, as written in the Lord's law. Henry notes he appropriated a branch of his crown revenue to the altar's maintenance, allowing out of his own estate for all those offerings rather than burden the people, a generous act consulting God's honour and his people's ease, as faithful servant to God and tender father to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Moreover he commanded the people that dwelt in Jerusalem to give the portion of the priests and Levites, that they might be encouraged in the law of the Lord. Henry notes he ordered Jerusalem's inhabitants first, that those nearest the temple might give good example, to pay dues according to law, for a scandalous maintenance makes a scandalous ministry, and the end was that priests and Levites might be encouraged in the law, in studying it and doing duty according to it, maintained not in idleness but in the Lord's law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And as soon as the commandment came abroad, Israel's children brought in abundance the firstfruits of corn, wine, and oil, and honey, and of all the increase of the field, and the tithe of all things brought they in abundantly. Henry notes the people brought in tithes very readily, wanting nothing but to be called upon, and as soon as the commandment came abroad, firstfruits and holy things were duly brought in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And concerning Israel and Judah's children, that dwelt in Judah's cities, they also brought in the tithe of oxen and sheep, and the tithe of holy things consecrated unto the Lord their God, and laid them by heaps. Henry notes what priests had occasion for they used, and the overplus was laid in heaps, all harvest-time increasing as earth's fruits were gathered, for God was to have his dues out of them all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. In the third month they began to lay the foundation of the heaps, and finished them in the seventh month. Henry notes when harvest ended they finished their heaps, begun in the third month and finished in the seventh, showing what plentiful provision God by law had made for priests if only collected and brought in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And when Hezekiah and the princes came and saw the heaps, they blessed the Lord, and his people Israel. Henry notes king and princes gave thanks to God for good providence giving something to bring and good grace giving hearts to bring it, and blessed the people, commending them for doing well now without reproaching former neglects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Then Hezekiah questioned with the priests and the Levites concerning the heaps. Henry notes Hezekiah questioned priests and Levites concerning the heaps, why they did not use what was paid in but hoarded it up, to which they answered they had used all they had occasion for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And Azariah the chief priest of the house of Zadok answered him, and said, Since the people began to bring the offerings into the house of the Lord, we have had enough to eat, and have left plenty, for the Lord hath blessed his people, and that which is left is this great store. Henry notes they answered they had enough to eat and left plenty, for the Lord had blessed his people, and this was the leftover store, not hoarded for covetousness, but showing God's plentiful provision and that those who conscientiously give God his dues bring blessing upon all they have. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Then Hezekiah commanded to prepare chambers in the house of the Lord, and they prepared them. Henry notes Hezekiah, having put church matters into good order, kept them so: tithes brought in should be carefully laid up, not left in loose heaps liable to waste and embezzlement, so he ordered store-chambers made ready in the temple courts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And brought in the offerings and the tithes and the dedicated things faithfully, over which Cononiah the Levite was ruler, and Shimei his brother was the next. Henry notes into the chambers offerings were brought and kept under lock and key, with treasurers appointed to see moth and rust did not corrupt them nor thieves steal, for laying up surplus in plenty we may learn from the ant, and well-husbanded contributions will be given cheerfully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And Jehiel, and Azaziah, and Nahath, and Asahel, and Jerimoth, and Jozabad, and Eliel, and Ismachiah, and Mahath, and Benaiah, were overseers under the hand of Cononiah and Shimei his brother, at the commandment of Hezekiah the king, and Azariah the ruler of the house of God. Henry notes overseers were appointed under Cononiah and Shimei at the king's command and Azariah's, men of approved wisdom and faithfulness to see treasures faithfully laid out according to their intended uses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And Kore son of Imnah the Levite, the porter toward the east, was over the freewill offerings of God, to distribute the oblations of the Lord, and the most holy things. Henry notes officers were appointed to distribute the Lord's oblations and most holy things among priests, that all had competent maintenance, going by a certain rule of proportion to prevent some having too little because others had too much. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And next him were Eden, and Miniamin, and Jeshua, and Shemaiah, Amariah, and Shecaniah, in the cities of the priests, in their set office, to give to their brethren by courses, as well to the great as to the small. Henry notes out of the offerings distribution was made to priests in the cities, who stayed at home while brethren went to Jerusalem and did good there teaching the Lord's good knowledge, for preaching priests were maintained as well as sacrificing priests, and those abiding by the stuff as well as those warring the warfare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Beside their genealogy of males, from three years old and upward, even unto every one that entereth into the house of the Lord, his daily portion for their service in their charges according to their courses. Henry notes distribution was made to those entering the Lord's house, all males from three years old upward, for male children even at that tender age were allowed into the temple with parents and shared in the distribution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Both to the genealogy of the priests by the house of their fathers, and the Levites from twenty years old and upward, in their charges by their courses. Henry notes Levites from twenty years old and upward had their share in their charges by their courses, alongside priests by their fathers' houses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And to the genealogy of all their little ones, their wives, and their sons, and their daughters, through all the congregation, for in their set office they sanctified themselves in holiness. Henry notes wives and children of priests and Levites had comfortable maintenance, for in maintaining ministers regard must be had to families, and in their set office they sanctified themselves in faith, attending ministry not doubting they should be provided for, and care was taken they should not be ashamed of their hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Also of Aaron's sons the priests, which were in the fields of the suburbs of their cities, in every several city, the men expressed by name, to give portions to all the males among the priests, and to all reckoned by genealogies among the Levites. Henry notes priests in the country, living in the fields of the suburbs, were not overlooked, and those in villages had their share though supposed to live at less expense. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And thus did Hezekiah throughout all Judah, and wrought that which was good and right and truth before the Lord his God. Henry notes his pious zeal reached all Judah's parts, not only those next him, and he sincerely designed to please God, working what was good before the Lord his God, right according to natural equity and truth according to divine revelation and covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And in every work that he began in the service of the house of God, and in the law, and in the commandments, to seek his God, he did it with all his heart, and prospered. Henry notes what he began he went through with, prosecuting it with vigour and doing it with all his heart, and all his good intentions were brought to good issue, for what is undertaken with sincere regard to God's glory will succeed to our own honour and comfort at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
