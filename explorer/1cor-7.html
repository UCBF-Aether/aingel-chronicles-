The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Corinthians 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now concerning the things whereof ye wrote unto me: It is good for a man not to touch a woman.

2 Nevertheless, to avoid fornication, let every man have his own wife, and let every woman have her own husband.

3 Let the husband render unto the wife due benevolence: and likewise also the wife unto the husband.

4 The wife hath not power of her own body, but the husband: and likewise also the husband hath not power of his own body, but the wife.

5 Defraud ye not one the other, except it be with consent for a time, that ye may give yourselves to fasting and prayer; and come together again, that Satan tempt you not for your incontinency.

6 But I speak this by permission, and not of commandment.

7 For I would that all men were even as I myself. But every man hath his proper gift of God, one after this manner, and another after that.

8 I say therefore to the unmarried and widows, It is good for them if they abide even as I.

9 But if they cannot contain, let them marry: for it is better to marry than to burn.

10 And unto the married I command, yet not I, but the Lord, Let not the wife depart from her husband:

11 But and if she depart, let her remain unmarried or be reconciled to her husband: and let not the husband put away his wife.

12 But to the rest speak I, not the Lord: If any brother hath a wife that believeth not, and she be pleased to dwell with him, let him not put her away.

13 And the woman which hath an husband that believeth not, and if he be pleased to dwell with her, let her not leave him.

14 For the unbelieving husband is sanctified by the wife, and the unbelieving wife is sanctified by the husband: else were your children unclean; but now are they holy.

15 But if the unbelieving depart, let him depart. A brother or a sister is not under bondage in such cases: but God hath called us to peace.

16 For what knowest thou, O wife, whether thou shalt save thy husband? or how knowest thou, O man, whether thou shalt save thy wife?

17 But as God hath distributed to every man, as the Lord hath called every one, so let him walk. And so ordain I in all churches.

18 Is any man called being circumcised? let him not become uncircumcised. Is any called in uncircumcision? let him not be circumcised.

19 Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of the commandments of God.

20 Let every man abide in the same calling wherein he was called.

21 Art thou called being a servant? care not for it: but if thou mayest be made free, use it rather.

22 For he that is called in the Lord, being a servant, is the Lord’s freeman: likewise also he that is called, being free, is Christ’s servant.

23 Ye are bought with a price; be not ye the servants of men.

24 Brethren, let every man, wherein he is called, therein abide with God.

25 Now concerning virgins I have no commandment of the Lord: yet I give my judgment, as one that hath obtained mercy of the Lord to be faithful.

26 I suppose therefore that this is good for the present distress, I say, that it is good for a man so to be.

27 Art thou bound unto a wife? seek not to be loosed. Art thou loosed from a wife? seek not a wife.

28 But and if thou marry, thou hast not sinned; and if a virgin marry, she hath not sinned. Nevertheless such shall have trouble in the flesh: but I spare you.

29 But this I say, brethren, the time is short: it remaineth, that both they that have wives be as though they had none;

30 And they that weep, as though they wept not; and they that rejoice, as though they rejoiced not; and they that buy, as though they possessed not;

31 And they that use this world, as not abusing it: for the fashion of this world passeth away.

32 But I would have you without carefulness. He that is unmarried careth for the things that belong to the Lord, how he may please the Lord:

33 But he that is married careth for the things that are of the world, how he may please his wife.

34 There is difference also between a wife and a virgin. The unmarried woman careth for the things of the Lord, that she may be holy both in body and in spirit: but she that is married careth for the things of the world, how she may please her husband.

35 And this I speak for your own profit; not that I may cast a snare upon you, but for that which is comely, and that ye may attend upon the Lord without distraction.

36 But if any man think that he behaveth himself uncomely toward his virgin, if she pass the flower of her age, and need so require, let him do what he will, he sinneth not: let them marry.

37 Nevertheless he that standeth stedfast in his heart, having no necessity, but hath power over his own will, and hath so decreed in his heart that he will keep his virgin, doeth well.

38 So then he that giveth her in marriage doeth well; but he that giveth her not in marriage doeth better.

39 The wife is bound by the law as long as her husband liveth; but if her husband be dead, she is at liberty to be married to whom she will; only in the Lord.

40 But she is happier if she so abide, after my judgment: and I think also that I have the Spirit of God.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Answering the Corinthians' written questions, Paul teaches that marriage is honourable and protects against temptation, that husbands and wives owe each other due benevolence, and that mixed marriages should continue if the unbelieving partner consents to dwell with the believer. He counsels contentment in one's calling, presents singleness as freeing a person for undistracted devotion in the present distress, and says the widow is free to remarry, only in the Lord. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Corinthians had asked questions tending to disparage marriage, implying it might be better to break it off with an unbeliever. Paul replies that it is good — expedient — for a man not to touch a woman, because of the present distress, the unsettled state of the world and likelihood of persecutions tearing marriage ties asunder. This is expediency in exceptional times, not a denial that marriage is honorable in all, and celibacy's other advantage is attending on the Lord without distraction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The general rule is given to avoid fornication — more literally, on account of fornications, the irregular lusts very prevalent at Corinth and not even counted sins among the heathen. Let every man have his own wife and every woman her own husband, a positive command to all who lack the gift of continency, in fact the great majority; marriage signifies the mystical union between Christ and the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Husband and wife owe each other the conjugal duty due by the marriage contract. The duty of cohabitation belongs to both parties alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The wife has not power over her own body, but her husband does, and likewise the husband's body is his wife's — a paradox explained by the oneness of body in marriage. Each complements the other, and neither without the other realizes the perfect ideal of man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Spouses must not defraud one another of the conjugal due except by mutual consent for a special season, to be at leisure for prayer. Satan often thrusts temptations to unholy thoughts even amid the holiest exercises, and would take advantage of incontinency, so they are to come together again lest Satan tempt them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. These directions about marriage and temporary separation are given by way of permission, not as a commandment. Paul allows them; he does not impose them as divine command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Paul wishes all men were even as himself, having the gift of continence — but this wish holds only relatively to the present distress, not absolutely, else the extension of mankind and of the church would cease. Each has his proper gift from God, one in one way and another in another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. To the unmarried in general, of both sexes, and to widows in particular, Paul says it is good for them to abide even as he does — unmarried. The counsel is for their good under present conditions, not a command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. If they cannot contain, let them marry, for it is better to marry than to burn with the secret flame of lust, which lays waste the whole inner man. The dew of God's grace is needed to stifle that flame, which otherwise would thrust men at last into hell-fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. To the married Paul gives not merely his own word but the Lord's direct authority: let not the wife be separated from her husband. The separation on either side, whether owing to husband or wife, is forbidden, resting on the Lord's own teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. If separation has nevertheless been committed, a second sin must not be added: the separated wife must remain unmarried or be reconciled to her husband, by appeasing his displeasure and recovering his good will. Likewise the husband must not put away his wife, the only exception being fornication. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. To the rest — those in mixed marriages with unbelievers, the other class the Corinthians asked about — Paul speaks with no direct command from the Lord, but with apostolic judgment. If an unbelieving husband consents to dwell with his believing wife, she must not put him away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Likewise, if a believing woman has an unbelieving husband who consents to dwell with her, she must not put him away. Under Greek and Roman law the wife had power to effect divorce, but the believer must not use it here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The unbelieving husband is sanctified in the believing wife, and the unbelieving wife in the believing husband — those inseparably connected with God's people are hallowed thereby, so the believer may retain the connection without impairing his own sanctity, and the unbeliever is prepared for becoming inwardly sanctified by faith. Otherwise the children would be unclean, beyond the hallowed pale; but now they are holy, since the faith of one Christian parent gives children a near relationship to the church, just as children were included in the covenant made with Abraham and his seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. If the unbelieving partner wishes for separation, offended by the believer's Christianity and refusing to live with her unless she renounce it, let him depart. The brother or sister is not under bondage in such a case — not bound to renounce the faith to retain the unbelieving spouse — though the believer is not bound to separate when the unbeliever stays. God has called us to peace, so the believer should not ordinarily depart, nor force the departing party to stay in continual discord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. What do you know but that by staying you may save your unbelieving partner? The precept to stay is enforced by this hope, as Ruth became a convert to her husband's faith and an unbelieving husband may be won by a believing wife. Conversely, if the unbeliever wishes to depart, let him go, for you cannot be sure of converting him so as to be obligated at all costs to stay against his will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The general principle stands: as the Lord has allotted to each and as God has called each, so let him walk. The heavenly calling does not set aside earthly callings, and this direction must not be made a ground for separating of oneself. Paul ordains the same in all churches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. If a man was called being circumcised, let him not become uncircumcised, as some in excess of anti-Jewish feeling might attempt; if called in uncircumcision, let him not be circumcised, as Judaizers demanded. Outward religious marks are not to be anxiously reversed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Circumcision is nothing and uncircumcision is nothing; what matters is the keeping of the commandments of God — defined elsewhere as faith working by love and the new creature. Circumcision was once God's commandment, but not forever, as love is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Let every man abide in the same calling — the same condition, Jew, Greek, slave, or freeman — in which he was called. The station itself is not the obstacle to godliness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. If called being a servant, let it not trouble him; he is to be content in his existing condition, since in union with Christ outward disparities are compensated. Yet if he may lawfully be made free, he should use the opportunity rather than neglect it — the gospel's spirit is against slavery, and the precept not to become servants of men shows slavery is abnormal, though unlawful means of escape are not advised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. He who is called in the Lord being a servant is the Lord's freedman — spiritually made free from sin, from the law, and from circumcision. And he who is called being free is Christ's servant, for love makes Christ's service perfect freedom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. All believers are bought with the blood of Christ, whatever their earthly state, so they must not become servants of men — neither externally, where that applies, nor spiritually, as servile adherents to party leaders at Corinth. The outward and inward conditions should correspond, with the former subservient to the latter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Let every man abide with God in the calling in which he was called, chiefly careful of his footing toward God rather than toward men. This limits the earlier precept: if a calling is unfavorable to abiding with God in holy fellowship, a man may use lawful means to change from it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Concerning virgins Paul has no express commandment from the Lord, but gives his judgment under the ordinary inspiration accompanying the apostles in their canonical writings — a recommendation the Corinthians are free to adopt, not a positive command like the Lord's own teaching on divorce. He attributes his apostleship and its gifts to God's mercy alone, and claims faithfulness in dispensing the directions he received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Paul considers it good for a man to remain in his present state, by reason of the present distress — the troubles beginning to fall on believers, which would prevail before the destruction of Jerusalem, making the married state less desirable than the single. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Neither the married, those bound to a wife, nor the unmarried, those loosed from a wife, are to seek a change of state. The principle of abiding applies to both alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Those who marry do not sin, but they will have trouble in the flesh — in their outward state by reason of the present distress, not sin, which is the trouble of the spirit. Paul's motive in advising as he does is to spare them such trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. The time, the season of this present dispensation up to the Lord's coming, is shortened, contracted. For the remaining time, those who have wives should be as though they had none — we ought to consider nothing as our own in real or permanent possession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Likewise those who weep should be as though they wept not, those who rejoice as though they rejoiced not, and those who buy as though they possessed not. The Christian will not hold fast to possessions as his enduring substance, for that is elsewhere; the men of Sodom were condemned not merely for open profligacy but for living as though their all were in this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Those who use this world should not abuse it by using it to the full as their chief aim. We are to use it, not take our fill of its pursuits; as planets turn on their own axes yet revolve round the sun, so while we do our part in our worldly sphere, God is to be the center of all desires. The fashion of this world is a fleeting form, a shifting stage scene, actually passing away now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Paul would have them without distracting cares. The unmarried man, if he uses aright the advantages of his condition, cares for the things of the Lord, how he may please the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. But the married man cares for the things of the world, how he may please his wife, and his attention is divided. This is the counterpart to the unmarried man's undistracted care for the Lord, and the reason celibacy can be expedient in distressful times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. The same difference holds for women: the unmarried woman and the virgin care for the things of the Lord, that she may be holy in body and spirit, while the married woman cares for the things of the world, how she may please her husband. A woman undergoes a greater change of condition than a man in marrying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Paul speaks for their own profit, not to display apostolic authority, and not to cast a snare over them — not to entangle them with fear of sin where there is no sin. His aim is what is comely under present circumstances, that they may attend upon the Lord without distraction, assiduously waiting on him as Mary sat and Anna served in the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. If a father behaves uncomely toward his virgin daughter by leaving her unmarried beyond the flower of her age, debarring her from the lawful gratification of her feelings as a marriageable woman, and need so requires out of regard for her welfare, let them marry — the daughter and her suitor — and he does not sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. But the father who stands steadfast, not turned from his purpose by the world's obloquy, having no necessity arising from his daughter's inclinations, and having power over his own will because his daughter's will does not oppose his, and who has so determined in his heart, does well to keep her unmarried. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. So he who gives his virgin daughter in marriage does well, but he who gives her not in marriage does better, under the present distress and for undistracted attendance on the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. The wife is bound as long as her husband lives, but if her husband dies she is free to marry whom she will, only in the Lord — only a Christian. The restriction guards the believer against unequal alliance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. In Paul's judgment, the widow is happier if she abides as she is, unmarried, for the reasons already given about trouble in the flesh and undistracted service. He adds that he also thinks he has the Spirit of God — 'think' here is not doubt but well-grounded assurance, his opinion given by inspiration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • due benevolence. The marital duty Paul says husband and wife owe one another, neither having sole power over their own body. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • present distress. The pressing troubles of the time, which Paul gives as a reason for his counsel favouring singleness. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • only in the Lord. Paul's condition for a widow's remarriage: her new husband must be a believer. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

  • Verse 27. Neither the married, those bound to a wife, nor the unmarried, those loosed from a wife, are to seek a change of state. The principle of abiding applies to both alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Those who marry do not sin, but they will have trouble in the flesh — in their outward state by reason of the present distress, not sin, which is the trouble of the spirit. Paul's motive in advising as he does is to spare them such trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. The time, the season of this present dispensation up to the Lord's coming, is shortened, contracted. For the remaining time, those who have wives should be as though they had none — we ought to consider nothing as our own in real or permanent possession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Likewise those who weep should be as though they wept not, those who rejoice as though they rejoiced not, and those who buy as though they possessed not. The Christian will not hold fast to possessions as his enduring substance, for that is elsewhere; the men of Sodom were condemned not merely for open profligacy but for living as though their all were in this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Those who use this world should not abuse it by using it to the full as their chief aim. We are to use it, not take our fill of its pursuits; as planets turn on their own axes yet revolve round the sun, so while we do our part in our worldly sphere, God is to be the center of all desires. The fashion of this world is a fleeting form, a shifting stage scene, actually passing away now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Paul would have them without distracting cares. The unmarried man, if he uses aright the advantages of his condition, cares for the things of the Lord, how he may please the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. But the married man cares for the things of the world, how he may please his wife, and his attention is divided. This is the counterpart to the unmarried man's undistracted care for the Lord, and the reason celibacy can be expedient in distressful times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. The same difference holds for women: the unmarried woman and the virgin care for the things of the Lord, that she may be holy in body and spirit, while the married woman cares for the things of the world, how she may please her husband. A woman undergoes a greater change of condition than a man in marrying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Paul speaks for their own profit, not to display apostolic authority, and not to cast a snare over them — not to entangle them with fear of sin where there is no sin. His aim is what is comely under present circumstances, that they may attend upon the Lord without distraction, assiduously waiting on him as Mary sat and Anna served in the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. If a father behaves uncomely toward his virgin daughter by leaving her unmarried beyond the flower of her age, debarring her from the lawful gratification of her feelings as a marriageable woman, and need so requires out of regard for her welfare, let them marry — the daughter and her suitor — and he does not sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. But the father who stands steadfast, not turned from his purpose by the world's obloquy, having no necessity arising from his daughter's inclinations, and having power over his own will because his daughter's will does not oppose his, and who has so determined in his heart, does well to keep her unmarried. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. So he who gives his virgin daughter in marriage does well, but he who gives her not in marriage does better, under the present distress and for undistracted attendance on the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. The wife is bound as long as her husband lives, but if her husband dies she is free to marry whom she will, only in the Lord — only a Christian. The restriction guards the believer against unequal alliance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. In Paul's judgment, the widow is happier if she abides as she is, unmarried, for the reasons already given about trouble in the flesh and undistracted service. He adds that he also thinks he has the Spirit of God — 'think' here is not doubt but well-grounded assurance, his opinion given by inspiration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
