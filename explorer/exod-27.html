The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 27 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And thou shalt make an altar of shittim wood, five cubits long, and five cubits broad; the altar shall be foursquare: and the height thereof shall be three cubits.

2 And thou shalt make the horns of it upon the four corners thereof: his horns shall be of the same: and thou shalt overlay it with brass.

3 And thou shalt make his pans to receive his ashes, and his shovels, and his basons, and his fleshhooks, and his firepans: all the vessels thereof thou shalt make of brass.

4 And thou shalt make for it a grate of network of brass; and upon the net shalt thou make four brasen rings in the four corners thereof.

5 And thou shalt put it under the compass of the altar beneath, that the net may be even to the midst of the altar.

6 And thou shalt make staves for the altar, staves of shittim wood, and overlay them with brass.

7 And the staves shall be put into the rings, and the staves shall be upon the two sides of the altar, to bear it.

8 Hollow with boards shalt thou make it: as it was shewed thee in the mount, so shall they make it.

9 And thou shalt make the court of the tabernacle: for the south side southward there shall be hangings for the court of fine twined linen of an hundred cubits long for one side:

10 And the twenty pillars thereof and their twenty sockets shall be of brass; the hooks of the pillars and their fillets shall be of silver.

11 And likewise for the north side in length there shall be hangings of an hundred cubits long, and his twenty pillars and their twenty sockets of brass; the hooks of the pillars and their fillets of silver.

12 And for the breadth of the court on the west side shall be hangings of fifty cubits: their pillars ten, and their sockets ten.

13 And the breadth of the court on the east side eastward shall be fifty cubits.

14 The hangings of one side of the gate shall be fifteen cubits: their pillars three, and their sockets three.

15 And on the other side shall be hangings fifteen cubits: their pillars three, and their sockets three.

16 And for the gate of the court shall be an hanging of twenty cubits, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, wrought with needlework: and their pillars shall be four, and their sockets four.

17 All the pillars round about the court shall be filleted with silver; their hooks shall be of silver, and their sockets of brass.

18 The length of the court shall be an hundred cubits, and the breadth fifty every where, and the height five cubits of fine twined linen, and their sockets of brass.

19 All the vessels of the tabernacle in all the service thereof, and all the pins thereof, and all the pins of the court, shall be of brass.

20 And thou shalt command the children of Israel, that they bring thee pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamp to burn always.

21 In the tabernacle of the congregation without the vail, which is before the testimony, Aaron and his sons shall order it from evening to morning before the LORD: it shall be a statute for ever unto their generations on the behalf of the children of Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Outside the tent stand the altar of burnt offering, made of shittim wood overlaid with brass, and the court of the tabernacle, hung with fine linen on pillars with silver hooks. The chapter closes with the command for pure beaten olive oil, so that Aaron and his sons keep the lamp burning continually before the LORD. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Thou shalt make an altar of shittim wood, five cubits long, and five cubits broad, the altar shall be foursquare, and the height thereof shall be three cubits. Henry notes in the court before the tabernacle, where the people attended, stood an altar to which they must bring their sacrifices, and it was of wood overlaid with brass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Thou shalt make the horns of it upon the four corners thereof, his horns shall be of the same, and thou shalt overlay it with brass. Henry observes the horns on the four corners were of the altar itself, the places of refuge where the guilty might lay hold in hope of mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Thou shalt make his pans to receive his ashes, and his shovels, and his basons, and his fleshhooks, and his firepans, all the vessels thereof thou shalt make of brass. Henry notes every vessel of the altar's service was brass, fitted to fire and blood and ash. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Thou shalt make for it a grate of network of brass, and upon the net shalt thou make four brasen rings in the four corners thereof. Henry observes the grate was made of network like a sieve, and hung hollow, that the ashes might fall through. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Thou shalt put it under the compass of the altar beneath, that the net may be even to the midst of the altar. Henry notes the grate was let into the hollow of the altar, about the middle of which the fire was kept and the sacrifice burnt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Thou shalt make staves for the altar, staves of shittim wood, and overlay them with brass. Henry observes the altar, like the ark and table, was made to be borne, with staves of wood overlaid with brass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And the staves shall be put into the rings, and the staves shall be upon the two sides of the altar, to bear it. Henry notes the rings on the two sides bore the altar steadily, that the fire's altar might travel with the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Hollow with boards shalt thou make it, as it was showed thee in the mount, so shall they make it. Henry observes this brazen altar was a type of Christ dying to make atonement for our sins: the wood would have been consumed by the fire from heaven if not secured by the brass, nor could Christ's human nature have borne the wrath of God if not supported by Divine power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Thou shalt make the court of the tabernacle, and for the south side southward there shall be hangings for the court of fine twined linen of a hundred cubits long for one side. Henry notes the tabernacle was enclosed in a court, about sixty yards long and thirty broad, formed by curtains hung upon brazen pillars fixed in brazen sockets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And the twenty pillars thereof and their twenty sockets shall be of brass, and the hooks of the pillars and their fillets shall be of silver. Henry observes the court pillars were brass in brass sockets, with silver hooks and fillets, a humbler metal for the outer enclosure than for the house itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And likewise for the north side in length there shall be hangings of a hundred cubits long, and his twenty pillars and their twenty sockets of brass, and the hooks of the pillars and their fillets of silver. Henry notes the north side matched the south, a hundred cubits of fine linen on twenty pillars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And for the breadth of the court on the west side shall be hangings of fifty cubits, their pillars ten, and their sockets ten. Henry observes the west end, behind the tabernacle, was fifty cubits on ten pillars, closing the court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And the breadth of the court on the east side eastward shall be fifty cubits. Henry notes the east side, the front, was also fifty cubits, but broken by the gate, for the court must have an entrance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The hangings of one side of the gate shall be fifteen cubits, their pillars three, and their sockets three. Henry observes the gate's one side was fifteen cubits on three pillars, leaving room for the entrance hanging. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And on the other side shall be hangings fifteen cubits, their pillars three, and their sockets three. Henry notes the other side matched, fifteen cubits on three pillars, so the gate stood centred in the east wall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And for the gate of the court shall be a hanging of twenty cubits, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, wrought with needlework, and their pillars shall be four, and their sockets four. Henry observes the gate hanging was coloured like the tabernacle door, blue, purple, and scarlet, that the entrance to the court might share the house's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. All the pillars round about the court shall be filleted with silver, their hooks shall be of silver, and their sockets of brass. Henry notes the silver fillets and hooks ran round the whole court, binding the pillars together in one enclosure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The length of the court shall be a hundred cubits, and the breadth fifty everywhere, and the height five cubits of fine twined linen, and their sockets of brass. Henry observes the court measured a hundred by fifty, five cubits high, within which enclosure the priests and Levites offered sacrifices, and thither the Jewish people were admitted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. All the vessels of the tabernacle in all the service thereof, and all the pins thereof, and all the pins of the court, shall be of brass. Henry notes every pin and vessel of the outer service was brass, for the court was the place of sacrifice and fire, and its metals matched its work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Thou shalt command the children of Israel, that they bring thee pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamp to burn always. Henry observes the pure oil signified the gifts and graces of the Spirit, which all believers receive from Christ, the good Olive, and without which our light cannot shine before men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. In the tabernacle of the congregation without the vail, which is before the testimony, Aaron and his sons shall order it from evening to morning before the LORD, and it shall be a statute forever unto their generations on the behalf of the children of Israel. Henry notes the priests were to light the lamps and tend them, and it is the work of ministers, by preaching and expounding the Scriptures, which are as a lamp, to enlighten the church, God's tabernacle upon earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
