The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 100 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Make a joyful noise unto the LORD, all ye lands.

2 Serve the LORD with gladness: come before his presence with singing.

3 Know ye that the LORD he is God: it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.

4 Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name.

5 For the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A five-verse call to all lands: make a joyful noise, serve the LORD with gladness, and come before his presence with singing. The ground of the praise is knowing that the LORD he is God — he made us, and not we ourselves; we are his people and the sheep of his pasture. Worshippers are to enter his gates with thanksgiving and his courts with praise, because the LORD is good, his mercy is everlasting, and his truth endures to all generations. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Making a joyful noise, all ye lands, is a strong and importunate invitation to worship, answering the psalm's title, A psalm of praise: the call to all the inhabitants of the earth will be fully answered when all nations are discipled and the gospel preached to every creature, and Henry suggests the psalm may have been intended for proselytes coming over from all lands to the Jews' religion. The invitation is not that God needs us or anything we have or can do, but that it is his will we should serve him, devoting and employing ourselves in his service — not only serving him in all obedience to his law, but coming before his presence in the ordinances he appointed and promised to manifest himself in, entering his gates and courts to attend upon him among his servants and keep where he keeps court. In secret or in families we come into his presence, but in public worship especially we enter his gates and courts; the people were not permitted into the holy place, where priests only ministered, so let them be thankful for their place in the courts, to which they were admitted and where they gave attendance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Serving the Lord with gladness is great encouragement to worship cheerfully: it predicts that in gospel times there should be special occasion for joy, and it prescribes a rule of worship — let God be served with gladness. By holy joy we do really serve God, and it is an honour to him to rejoice in him, so gospel-worshippers should be joyful worshippers, serving him in uprightness with gladness. We must be willing and forward to it, glad when called to go up to the house of the Lord, looking on communion with God as the comfort of our lives, and pleasant and cheerful in it, saying it is good to be here, approaching God in every duty as to God our exceeding joy — and coming before his presence with singing, not only songs of joy but songs of praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Knowing that the Lord he is God, that he made us and we are his people, the sheep of his pasture, is the matter and motive of praise, for knowledge is the mother of devotion and of all obedience: blind sacrifices will never please a seeing God. Know it, consider and apply it, and you will be more close and constant, more inward and serious, in worship. Henry lists seven things to know: that the Lord is God, the only living and true God, infinitely perfect, self-existent and self-sufficient, fountain of all being, an eternal Spirit, incomprehensible and independent, first cause and last end — unlike the heathen's gods, creatures of their own fancy made by workmen and therefore not God; that he is our Creator, for it is he that made us and not we ourselves, the former of our bodies and Father of our spirits, our being derived and depending, his prerogative being to be his own cause; that therefore he is our rightful owner, for because he made us and not we ourselves, we are not our own but his, to be actuated by his power, disposed of by his will, devoted to his honour; that he is our sovereign ruler, we his people and subjects and he our prince and governor giving law to us as moral agents and calling us to account; and that he is our bountiful benefactor, for we are not only his sheep, whom he is entitled to, but the sheep of his pasture, whom he takes care of — he that made us maintains us and gives us all good things richly to enjoy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Entering his gates with thanksgiving and his courts with praise means we must not only comfort ourselves but glorify God with our joy, letting him have the praise of that which we have the pleasure of. Being thankful to him and blessing his name means first taking it as a favour to be admitted into his service and giving thanks for liberty of access, for ordinances instituted and opportunity continued of waiting upon God in them; and second, intermixing praise and thanksgiving with all our services, this golden thread running through every duty, for it is the work of angels — in everything give thanks, in every ordinance as well as in every providence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. For the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations, is two of the seven things to know concerning the Lord Jehovah: sixth, that he is a God of infinite mercy and goodness: the Lord is good, and therefore does good; his mercy is everlasting; it is a fountain that can never be drawn dry. The saints, who are now the sanctified vessels of mercy, will be, to eternity, the glorified monuments of mercy. Seventh, that he is a God of inviolable truth and faithfulness: his truth endures to all generations, and no word of his shall fall to the ground as antiquated or revoked. The promise is sure to all the seed, from age to age. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
