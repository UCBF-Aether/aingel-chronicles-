The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 60 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 O God, thou hast cast us off, thou hast scattered us, thou hast been displeased; O turn thyself to us again.

2 Thou hast made the earth to tremble; thou hast broken it: heal the breaches thereof; for it shaketh.

3 Thou hast shewed thy people hard things: thou hast made us to drink the wine of astonishment.

4 Thou hast given a banner to them that fear thee, that it may be displayed because of the truth. Selah.

5 That thy beloved may be delivered; save with thy right hand, and hear me.

6 God hath spoken in his holiness; I will rejoice, I will divide Shechem, and mete out the valley of Succoth.

7 Gilead is mine, and Manasseh is mine; Ephraim also is the strength of mine head; Judah is my lawgiver;

8 Moab is my washpot; over Edom will I cast out my shoe: Philistia, triumph thou because of me.

9 Who will bring me into the strong city? who will lead me into Edom?

10 Wilt not thou, O God, which hadst cast us off? and thou, O God, which didst not go out with our armies?

11 Give us help from trouble: for vain is the help of man.

12 Through God we shall do valiantly: for he it is that shall tread down our enemies.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Cast off, scattered, earth trembling and broken, wine of astonishment drunk, yet a banner given to the fearing, God's holy speech dividing Shechem and Succoth, Gilead and Manasseh his, Judah his lawgiver, Moab his washpot, shoe cast over Edom. Who will bring him into the strong city? Only the God who cast them off, for vain is man's help, and through God they shall do valiantly. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. O God, you have cast us off, you have scattered us, you have been displeased; O turn yourself to us again: casting off, scattering, and displeasure are confessed as God's acts, and turning again is the prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. You have made the earth to tremble, you have broken it; heal the breaches thereof, for it shakes: a trembling, broken, shaking earth needs healing of its breaches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. You have shown your people hard things; you have made us to drink the wine of astonishment: hard things shown, wine of astonishment drunk — defeat has stunned the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. You have given a banner to those who fear you, that it may be displayed because of the truth: a banner given to the fearing, displayed because of truth — a rallying sign amid defeat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. That your beloved may be delivered; save with your right hand, and hear me: the banner's purpose is the beloved's deliverance, by God's right hand and hearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. God has spoken in his holiness; I will rejoice, I will divide Shechem, and mete out the valley of Succoth: God's holy speech grounds rejoicing, and dividing Shechem and meting Succoth picture the land apportioned by God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Gilead is mine, and Manasseh is mine; Ephraim also is the strength of my head; Judah is my lawgiver: Gilead, Manasseh, Ephraim as head's strength, Judah as lawgiver — the tribes are God's, each in its office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Moab is my washpot; over Edom will I cast out my shoe; Philistia, triumph because of me: Moab as washpot, Edom under the cast shoe, Philistia bidden triumph because of God — neighbouring nations are God's servants and subjects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Who will bring me into the strong city? Who will lead me into Edom? The strong city and Edom are the objectives — who can lead there but God? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Will not you, O God, who had cast us off? And you, O God, who did not go out with our armies? The God who cast off and did not go out with the armies is asked, in the very terms of the complaint, to go out now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Give us help from trouble, for vain is the help of man: help from trouble is asked of God, for man's help is vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Through God we shall do valiantly, for he it is that shall tread down our enemies: through God, valiant deeds, and God treading down enemies — the psalm ends in valour credited to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
