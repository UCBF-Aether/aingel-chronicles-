The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 133 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!

2 It is like the precious ointment upon the head, that ran down upon the beard, even Aaron’s beard: that went down to the skirts of his garments;

3 As the dew of Hermon, and as the dew that descended upon the mountains of Zion: for there the LORD commanded the blessing, even life for evermore.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Three verses on unity: behold how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity. It is like precious ointment on the head running down upon Aaron's beard to the skirts of his garments, and like the dew of Hermon descending on the mountains of Zion — for there the LORD commanded the blessing, even life for evermore. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry's brief encomium on unity and brotherly love — if miseries of discord not seen would think needless, cannot say too much, well if say enough to persuade live together in peace; conjecture David penned on union of tribes meeting unanimously to make him king, general use all societies smaller larger civil sacred, contents benefit of communion of saints; commended is brethren dwelling together in unity — not only not quarrelling devouring, but delighting with mutual endearments promoting welfare with mutual services; sometimes chosen best expedient peace brethren live asunder at distance, may prevent enmity strife (Gen 13:9), but goodness pleasantness dwell together dwell in unity dwell even as one, one heart one soul one interest; David many sons many wives probably penned for their instruction engage love another, if done much mischief in family happily prevented; tribes long separate interests under Judges often bad consequence, now united under one common head sensible advantage, especially ark fixed place of rendezvous public worship centre of unity — now live in love; behold how good pleasant — good in itself agreeable will of God conformity earth to heaven, good for us honour comfort, pleasant pleasing God and all good men constant delight, cannot conceive express, rare therefore admirable, wonder so much goodness pleasantness so much heaven on earth, amiable attract hearts, exemplary imitate with holy emulation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry fragrances unity as holy anointing oil strongly perfumed diffusing odours great delight bystanders poured upon head of Aaron or successor high priest so plentifully ran down face even collar binding garment: holy — brotherly love pure heart devoted God, love begotten for sake that begat (1 John 5:1); composition divine dispensatory God appointed ingredients quantities, believers taught of God love one another grace his working; very precious like not made common use, holy love sight of God great price precious indeed in God's sight; grateful both Aaron himself and all about, holy love like ointment perfume rejoice heart, Christ's love part of oil of gladness anointed above fellows; Aaron sons not admitted minister till anointed, nor services acceptable without holy love, if have it not nothing (1 Cor 13:1-2). Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry fructifies unity as dew: profitable as pleasing, brings abundance blessings numerous as drops, cools scorching heat of passions as evening dews cool air refresh earth, contributes fruitfulness in every good, moistens heart makes tender fit receive good seed of word, malice bitterness unfit (1 Pet 2:1); dew of Hermon common hill — brotherly love beauty benefit civil societies, dew descended mountains of Zion holy hill contributes fruitfulness sacred societies, both Hermon Zion wither without dew; dew tarrieth not for man nor waiteth sons of men (Mic 5:7), nor should love stay for theirs — publican's love, but go before — divine love; proof — loving people blessed people, there where brethren dwell unity Lord commands blessing complicated including all blessings, God's prerogative command blessing man can but beg, blessings according promise commanded for commanded covenant for ever, blessings take effect commanded for speaks done; everlastingly blessed — blessing commanded life for evermore blessing of blessings, dwell in love not only dwell in God but already dwell in heaven, perfection of love blessedness of heaven sincerity earnest, live love peace shall have God of love peace with them now and with him shortly for ever in world endless love peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
