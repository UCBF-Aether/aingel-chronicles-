The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Wisdom hath builded her house, she hath hewn out her seven pillars:

2 She hath killed her beasts; she hath mingled her wine; she hath also furnished her table.

3 She hath sent forth her maidens: she crieth upon the highest places of the city,

4 Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither: as for him that wanteth understanding, she saith to him,

5 Come, eat of my bread, and drink of the wine which I have mingled.

6 Forsake the foolish, and live; and go in the way of understanding.

7 He that reproveth a scorner getteth to himself shame: and he that rebuketh a wicked man getteth himself a blot.

8 Reprove not a scorner, lest he hate thee: rebuke a wise man, and he will love thee.

9 Give instruction to a wise man, and he will be yet wiser: teach a just man, and he will increase in learning.

10 The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding.

11 For by me thy days shall be multiplied, and the years of thy life shall be increased.

12 If thou be wise, thou shalt be wise for thyself: but if thou scornest, thou alone shalt bear it.

13 A foolish woman is clamorous: she is simple, and knoweth nothing.

14 For she sitteth at the door of her house, on a seat in the high places of the city,

15 To call passengers who go right on their ways:

16 Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither: and as for him that wanteth understanding, she saith to him,

17 Stolen waters are sweet, and bread eaten in secret is pleasant.

18 But he knoweth not that the dead are there; and that her guests are in the depths of hell.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Two houses invite the simple, and the chapter sets them side by side. Wisdom has built her house on seven pillars, killed her beasts, mingled her wine and sent her maidens to cry from the highest places: come, eat my bread, forsake the foolish and live. The foolish woman sits at her own door with the same invitation, but her stolen waters and secret bread lead to the dead in the depths of hell. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Wisdom building her house and hewing seven pillars is the stately palace Henry pictures: heaven as Wisdom's house, and the church supported by God's power and promise as by seven pillars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Killing beasts, mingling wine, and furnishing the table is the sumptuous sacred feast, for Christ offered himself a sacrifice, and his flesh and blood are meat and drink indeed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Sending forth maidens who cry on the highest places of the city is the gospel ministry commissioned to call all they meet, even in highways and hedges, to Wisdom's feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither, and him wanting understanding she addresses, for Wisdom invites precisely those who most need what she gives, excluding none who do not exclude themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Come, eat of my bread and drink of the wine mingled, is the invitation to taste true pleasures in the knowledge and fear of God, feeding by faith on the gospel promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Forsaking the foolish and living, going in the way of understanding, requires breaking from bad company and joining those who walk in wisdom, not merely leaving vice behind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Reproving a scorner gets shame, and rebuking a wicked man gets a blot, for those who scorn means already given have forfeited further means, and reproof only exasperates them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Reprove not a scorner lest he hate thee, but rebuke a wise man and he will love thee, for the wise reckon faithful dealers friends and thank them for plain dealing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Giving instruction to a wise man makes him yet wiser, and teaching a just man increases his learning, for none must think themselves too wise to learn or too good to be better. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom, and knowledge of the holy is understanding, for reverence of God's majesty is the first step of true religion, and knowledge of holy things its best understanding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. By Wisdom days are multiplied and years of life increased, contributing to bodily health here and to endless days in heaven, for there is no true life but in the end of religion's way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. If thou be wise, thou shalt be wise for thyself, but if thou scornest, thou alone shalt bear it, for man cannot profit God, and the scorner bears both blame and loss himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. The foolish woman, clamorous, simple, knowing nothing, is Folly herself, especially carnal sensual pleasure, which defiles the mind and stupefies conscience more than anything else. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Sitting at her house door, on a seat in the high places of the city, Folly affects authority and fashion, watching for prey not to do good but to entice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Calling to passers-by who go right on their ways shows the tempter pursuing even the well-educated who set out hopefully, calling after them when they will not look toward her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither, is Folly retorting wisdom's charge, calling the virtuous simple to cure them of religion's restraints, though the day will declare who are the fools. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Stolen waters are sweet, and bread eaten in secret is pleasant, is the bait of prohibited lusts and dishonest gain, prized precisely because forbidden, in defiance of God's law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. He knows not that the dead are there, and her guests are in the depths of hell, for those living in pleasure are dead in trespasses, already under sin's power and conscience's terrors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
