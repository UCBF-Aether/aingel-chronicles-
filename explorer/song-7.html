The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Song of Songs 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 How beautiful are thy feet with shoes, O prince’s daughter! the joints of thy thighs are like jewels, the work of the hands of a cunning workman.

2 Thy navel is like a round goblet, which wanteth not liquor: thy belly is like an heap of wheat set about with lilies.

3 Thy two breasts are like two young roes that are twins.

4 Thy neck is as a tower of ivory; thine eyes like the fishpools in Heshbon, by the gate of Bathrabbim: thy nose is as the tower of Lebanon which looketh toward Damascus.

5 Thine head upon thee is like Carmel, and the hair of thine head like purple; the king is held in the galleries.

6 How fair and how pleasant art thou, O love, for delights!

7 This thy stature is like to a palm tree, and thy breasts to clusters of grapes.

8 I said, I will go up to the palm tree, I will take hold of the boughs thereof: now also thy breasts shall be as clusters of the vine, and the smell of thy nose like apples;

9 And the roof of thy mouth like the best wine for my beloved, that goeth down sweetly, causing the lips of those that are asleep to speak.

10 I am my beloved’s, and his desire is toward me.

11 Come, my beloved, let us go forth into the field; let us lodge in the villages.

12 Let us get up early to the vineyards; let us see if the vine flourish, whether the tender grape appear, and the pomegranates bud forth: there will I give thee my loves.

13 The mandrakes give a smell, and at our gates are all manner of pleasant fruits, new and old, which I have laid up for thee, O my beloved.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The praise runs from feet to head, shoes, goblet, tower neck, eyes like the pools of Heshbon, head like Carmel, stature like a palm tree. The beloved answers that she is her lover's and his desire is toward her, and invites him out to the fields and vineyards, where mandrakes give their scent and all manner of pleasant fruits, new and old, are laid up for him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. How beautiful are thy feet with shoes, O prince's daughter! The joints of thy thighs are like jewels, the work of a cunning workman. The similitudes here refer in the original to glorious and splendid clothing, Henry says: having put on Christ, the saints are distinguished by their beautiful and glorious apparel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Thy navel is like a round goblet which wants not liquor; thy belly is like a heap of wheat set about with lilies. Such honour have all his saints, Henry notes: they adorn the doctrine of God their Saviour in all things, and consistent believers honour Christ, recommend the gospel, and awaken sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Thy two breasts are like two young roes that are twins. This image belongs to the same portrait of the church's beauty of holiness, Henry says, in which believers shall be presented to their heavenly Bridegroom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Thy neck is as a tower of ivory; thine eyes like the fishpools in Heshbon, by the gate of Bathrabbim; thy nose is as the tower of Lebanon which looks toward Damascus. The similitudes denote the beauty of holiness, Henry explains, and the church resembles the stately and spreading palm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Thine head upon thee is like Carmel, and the hair of thine head like purple; the king is held in the galleries. The King is held in the galleries, Henry says: Christ takes delight in the assemblies and ordinances of his people, and admires the fruit of his grace in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. How fair and how pleasant art thou, O love, for delights! When applied to the church and to each faithful Christian, Henry says, all this denotes that beauty of holiness in which they shall be presented to their heavenly Bridegroom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. This thy stature is like to a palm tree, and thy breasts to clusters of grapes. The church resembles the stately and spreading palm, Henry notes, while her love for Christ and the obedience resulting therefrom are precious fruit of the true Vine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. I said, I will go up to the palm tree, I will take hold of the boughs thereof; now also thy breasts shall be as clusters of the vine, and the smell of thy nose like apples. Christ takes delight in the fruit of his grace in his people, Henry says, and admires it in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. And the roof of thy mouth like the best wine for my beloved, that goes down sweetly, causing the lips of those that are asleep to speak. The best wine here is for the Beloved, Henry notes, as all the fruit of grace is for his honour and glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. I am my beloved's, and his desire is toward me. The church, the believing soul, triumphs in its relation to Christ and interest in him, Henry says, and humbly desires communion with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Come, my beloved, let us go forth into the field; let us lodge in the villages. Let us walk together, Henry paraphrases, that I may receive counsel, instruction, and comfort from thee, and make known my wants and grievances with freedom; those who would converse with Christ must go forth from the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Let us get up early to the vineyards; let us see if the vine flourish, whether the tender grape appear, and the pomegranates bud forth: there will I give thee my loves. Those who would go abroad with Christ must begin early, Henry says, seek him early and diligently, and our own souls are our vineyards, which we should often search to see whether we are fruitful in righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The mandrakes give a smell, and at our gates are all manner of pleasant fruits, new and old, which I have laid up for thee, O my beloved. The fruits and exercises of graces are pleasant to the Lord Jesus, Henry says; these must be laid up and always ready, that by bringing forth much fruit he may be glorified, for it is all from him, and therefore fit it should be all for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
