The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Deuteronomy 27 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Moses with the elders of Israel commanded the people, saying, Keep all the commandments which I command you this day.

2 And it shall be on the day when ye shall pass over Jordan unto the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee, that thou shalt set thee up great stones, and plaister them with plaister:

3 And thou shalt write upon them all the words of this law, when thou art passed over, that thou mayest go in unto the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee, a land that floweth with milk and honey; as the LORD God of thy fathers hath promised thee.

4 Therefore it shall be when ye be gone over Jordan, that ye shall set up these stones, which I command you this day, in mount Ebal, and thou shalt plaister them with plaister.

5 And there shalt thou build an altar unto the LORD thy God, an altar of stones: thou shalt not lift up any iron tool upon them.

6 Thou shalt build the altar of the LORD thy God of whole stones: and thou shalt offer burnt offerings thereon unto the LORD thy God:

7 And thou shalt offer peace offerings, and shalt eat there, and rejoice before the LORD thy God.

8 And thou shalt write upon the stones all the words of this law very plainly.

9 And Moses and the priests the Levites spake unto all Israel, saying, Take heed, and hearken, O Israel; this day thou art become the people of the LORD thy God.

10 Thou shalt therefore obey the voice of the LORD thy God, and do his commandments and his statutes, which I command thee this day.

11 And Moses charged the people the same day, saying,

12 These shall stand upon mount Gerizim to bless the people, when ye are come over Jordan; Simeon, and Levi, and Judah, and Issachar, and Joseph, and Benjamin:

13 And these shall stand upon mount Ebal to curse; Reuben, Gad, and Asher, and Zebulun, Dan, and Naphtali.

14 And the Levites shall speak, and say unto all the men of Israel with a loud voice,

15 Cursed be the man that maketh any graven or molten image, an abomination unto the LORD, the work of the hands of the craftsman, and putteth it in a secret place. And all the people shall answer and say, Amen.

16 Cursed be he that setteth light by his father or his mother. And all the people shall say, Amen.

17 Cursed be he that removeth his neighbour’s landmark. And all the people shall say, Amen.

18 Cursed be he that maketh the blind to wander out of the way. And all the people shall say, Amen.

19 Cursed be he that perverteth the judgment of the stranger, fatherless, and widow. And all the people shall say, Amen.

20 Cursed be he that lieth with his father’s wife; because he uncovereth his father’s skirt. And all the people shall say, Amen.

21 Cursed be he that lieth with any manner of beast. And all the people shall say, Amen.

22 Cursed be he that lieth with his sister, the daughter of his father, or the daughter of his mother. And all the people shall say, Amen.

23 Cursed be he that lieth with his mother in law. And all the people shall say, Amen.

24 Cursed be he that smiteth his neighbour secretly. And all the people shall say, Amen.

25 Cursed be he that taketh reward to slay an innocent person. And all the people shall say, Amen.

26 Cursed be he that confirmeth not all the words of this law to do them. And all the people shall say, Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

On entering the land, Israel is to set up great stones on Mount Ebal, plastered and written with the law, and build an altar there. Six tribes stand on Gerizim to bless and six on Ebal to curse, while the Levites pronounce twelve curses — on secret sins, from idolatry and dishonouring parents to perverting justice for the defenceless — and all the people answer Amen. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Moses with the elders commands the keeping of the law as the prelude to writing it on stones in Canaan; Henry stresses that word and prayer must go together, and that the law, with its curse annexed, is set up plainly before the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The law is written where it will be lived: great stones, plastered and set up on crossing Jordan, make the covenant a public monument in the land rather than a scroll kept by priests alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And you shall write upon them all the words of this law, when you are passed over, that you may go in to the land flowing with milk and honey, as the Lord God of your fathers has promised you. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Therefore when you are gone over Jordan, you shall set up these stones, which I command you this day, in Mount Ebal, and plaster them with plaster, a monument of the law in the land itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And there you shall build an altar to the Lord your God, an altar of stones, and you shall not lift up any iron tool upon them, the word and prayer going together, and the altar being of unhewn stones as found in the field. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Whole stones, untouched by iron, carry the burnt offerings: the altar must come to God as found, not shaped by human craft, and the offering rises from unhewn stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Peace offerings are eaten and rejoiced over before God: after the curse is set up in stone, the altar also feasts, so obedience ends in rejoicing, not only in fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And you shall write upon the stones all the words of this law very plainly, the end of ministry being to make God's word as plain as possible, though only the Spirit's power makes it wise to salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Moses and the priests speak together, and the day itself is the point: this day Israel becomes God's people, not by crossing Jordan alone but by hearkening to the voice that commands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Obedience follows identity: because this day they are become God's people, therefore they obey his voice and do his statutes, for the command rests on who they now are. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Moses' charge that day appoints the curses on Ebal, to which the people must answer Amen, professing their faith that these curses are real declarations of God's wrath, and acknowledging that those who do such things deserve to fall under them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. These shall stand upon Mount Gerizim to bless the people, when you are come over Jordan: Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Joseph, and Benjamin, the six tribes of blessing, children of the free women, to whom the promise belongs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Six tribes stand on Ebal for the curse as six stood on Gerizim for blessing: the division itself preaches that the same law which blesses obedience curses disobedience, and both mountains are heard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And the Levites shall speak, and say to all the men of Israel with a loud voice, ministers applying to themselves the blessing and curse they preach, and setting their own Amen to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Cursed be the man who makes any graven or molten image, an abomination to the Lord, the work of the hands of the craftsman, and puts it in a secret place, and all the people shall answer and say, Amen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The curse on dishonouring parents opens the list where family order begins: the people's Amen is not a curse they pronounce on others but a confession that such contempt deserves God's wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Moving a neighbour's landmark steals by stealth what no court may see: the curse names a quiet injustice, and the Amen confesses it worthy of wrath though no witness watched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Leading the blind astray abuses trust no victim can check: the curse reaches sins done where the sufferer cannot see the wrong, and the Amen owns that hidden cruelty as God's business. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Justice perverted against the defenceless is cursed precisely because they have no advocate: stranger, fatherless and widow depend on the court, and the Amen says God judges the judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The curse guards the father's household: uncovering his skirt is not a private liaison but a breach of family order, and the Amen acknowledges it under the law's wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Bestiality is cursed without a reason clause: the act itself, named bare, is enough for the people's Amen to own it as abomination under God's wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Incest within the family is cursed in both lines, father's daughter and mother's: the Amen covers the whole household, leaving no kinship loophole. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The curse extends the household boundary to marriage kin: a mother-in-law is family by covenant, not a stranger, and the Amen treats the breach accordingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Secret murder is cursed though no court can prove it: the Amen is the people's confession that God's wrath reaches the blow struck where no eye saw. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Bribed blood is cursed as hired murder: taking reward makes the killer a merchant of innocent life, and the Amen says such a bargain falls under God's wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Cursed be he who confirms not all the words of this law to do them, and all the people shall say, Amen; the last curse reaches all, not only those who do forbidden evil, but those who omit required good, and without Christ's atoning blood the law condemns every transgressor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
