The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — John 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Pilate therefore took Jesus, and scourged him.

2 And the soldiers platted a crown of thorns, and put it on his head, and they put on him a purple robe,

3 And said, Hail, King of the Jews! and they smote him with their hands.

4 Pilate therefore went forth again, and saith unto them, Behold, I bring him forth to you, that ye may know that I find no fault in him.

5 Then came Jesus forth, wearing the crown of thorns, and the purple robe. And Pilate saith unto them, Behold the man!

6 When the chief priests therefore and officers saw him, they cried out, saying, Crucify him, crucify him. Pilate saith unto them, Take ye him, and crucify him: for I find no fault in him.

7 The Jews answered him, We have a law, and by our law he ought to die, because he made himself the Son of God.

8 When Pilate therefore heard that saying, he was the more afraid;

9 And went again into the judgment hall, and saith unto Jesus, Whence art thou? But Jesus gave him no answer.

10 Then saith Pilate unto him, Speakest thou not unto me? knowest thou not that I have power to crucify thee, and have power to release thee?

11 Jesus answered, Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above: therefore he that delivered me unto thee hath the greater sin.

12 And from thenceforth Pilate sought to release him: but the Jews cried out, saying, If thou let this man go, thou art not Caesar’s friend: whosoever maketh himself a king speaketh against Caesar.

13 When Pilate therefore heard that saying, he brought Jesus forth, and sat down in the judgment seat in a place that is called the Pavement, but in the Hebrew, Gabbatha.

14 And it was the preparation of the passover, and about the sixth hour: and he saith unto the Jews, Behold your King!

15 But they cried out, Away with him, away with him, crucify him. Pilate saith unto them, Shall I crucify your King? The chief priests answered, We have no king but Caesar.

16 Then delivered he him therefore unto them to be crucified. And they took Jesus, and led him away.

17 And he bearing his cross went forth into a place called the place of a skull, which is called in the Hebrew Golgotha:

18 Where they crucified him, and two other with him, on either side one, and Jesus in the midst.

19 And Pilate wrote a title, and put it on the cross. And the writing was JESUS OF NAZARETH THE KING OF THE JEWS.

20 This title then read many of the Jews: for the place where Jesus was crucified was nigh to the city: and it was written in Hebrew, and Greek, and Latin.

21 Then said the chief priests of the Jews to Pilate, Write not, The King of the Jews; but that he said, I am King of the Jews.

22 Pilate answered, What I have written I have written.

23 Then the soldiers, when they had crucified Jesus, took his garments, and made four parts, to every soldier a part; and also his coat: now the coat was without seam, woven from the top throughout.

24 They said therefore among themselves, Let us not rend it, but cast lots for it, whose it shall be: that the scripture might be fulfilled, which saith, They parted my raiment among them, and for my vesture they did cast lots. These things therefore the soldiers did.

25 Now there stood by the cross of Jesus his mother, and his mother’s sister, Mary the wife of Cleophas, and Mary Magdalene.

26 When Jesus therefore saw his mother, and the disciple standing by, whom he loved, he saith unto his mother, Woman, behold thy son!

27 Then saith he to the disciple, Behold thy mother! And from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home.

28 After this, Jesus knowing that all things were now accomplished, that the scripture might be fulfilled, saith, I thirst.

29 Now there was set a vessel full of vinegar: and they filled a spunge with vinegar, and put it upon hyssop, and put it to his mouth.

30 When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.

31 The Jews therefore, because it was the preparation, that the bodies should not remain upon the cross on the sabbath day, (for that sabbath day was an high day,) besought Pilate that their legs might be broken, and that they might be taken away.

32 Then came the soldiers, and brake the legs of the first, and of the other which was crucified with him.

33 But when they came to Jesus, and saw that he was dead already, they brake not his legs:

34 But one of the soldiers with a spear pierced his side, and forthwith came there out blood and water.

35 And he that saw it bare record, and his record is true: and he knoweth that he saith true, that ye might believe.

36 For these things were done, that the scripture should be fulfilled, A bone of him shall not be broken.

37 And again another scripture saith, They shall look on him whom they pierced.

38 And after this Joseph of Arimathaea, being a disciple of Jesus, but secretly for fear of the Jews, besought Pilate that he might take away the body of Jesus: and Pilate gave him leave. He came therefore, and took the body of Jesus.

39 And there came also Nicodemus, which at the first came to Jesus by night, and brought a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about an hundred pound weight.

40 Then took they the body of Jesus, and wound it in linen clothes with the spices, as the manner of the Jews is to bury.

41 Now in the place where he was crucified there was a garden; and in the garden a new sepulchre, wherein was never man yet laid.

42 There laid they Jesus therefore because of the Jews’ preparation day; for the sepulchre was nigh at hand.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Pilate has Jesus scourged and crowned with thorns in purple, and presents him with “Behold the man,” but the chief priests’ cry “Crucify him” and their claim about Caesar prevail, and Pilate delivers him (vv. 1–16). Bearing his cross to Golgotha, Jesus is crucified between two others, under Pilate’s title “JESUS OF NAZARETH THE KING OF THE JEWS”; his garments are divided, his mother is given to the beloved disciple, and after “It is finished” his side is pierced, from which come blood and water (vv. 17–37). Joseph of Arimathaea and Nicodemus lay him in a new garden tomb nearby, because the Jews’ preparation day is at hand (vv. 38–42). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, John 19

  • Verse 2. Henry: the thorns and purple are scorn made visible — he comes forth willing to be exposed to it, and faith is bidden to behold him in it and love him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And they said, Hail, King of the Jews, and they smote him with their hands, for our Lord came forth willing to be exposed to their scorn, and it is good for everyone with faith to behold Christ in his sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Pilate therefore went forth again, and said unto them, Behold, I bring him forth to you, that you may know that I find no fault in him, repeating his declaration of innocence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Henry: Pilate brings him forth crowned and robed and bids them behold the man — scorn on display, yet the sight faith is told to keep looking upon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. When the chief priests therefore and officers saw him, they cried out, saying, Crucify him, crucify him, and Pilate said, Take ye him, and crucify him, for I find no fault in him, for their hatred sharpened their endeavours, and shall not our love quicken ours for him and his kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The Jews answered him, We have a law, and by our law he ought to die, because he made himself the Son of God, at last stating the charge plainly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When Pilate therefore heard that saying, he was the more afraid, for even natural conscience makes men afraid of being found fighting against God, and Pilate thought Jesus might be some person above the common order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And he went again into the judgment hall, and said unto Jesus, Whence art thou, seeking the origin of one he feared to condemn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Henry: Pilate presses his power to crucify or release, yet already fears he may be fighting against God — the boast and the fear sit in the same man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Jesus answered, Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above, therefore he that delivered me unto thee has the greater sin, for as our Lord suffered for the sins of both Jews and Gentiles, it was the counsel of Divine Wisdom that the Jews should first purpose his death and the Gentiles carry it into effect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And from thenceforth Pilate sought to release him, but the Jews cried out, saying, If thou let this man go, thou art not Caesar's friend; whosoever makes himself a king speaks against Caesar, for Pilate was willing to please all sides and governed more by worldly wisdom than by justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And it was the preparation of the Passover, and about the sixth hour, and he said unto the Jews, Behold your King, presenting their King to a people who would reject him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. But they cried out, Away with him, away with him, crucify him, and Pilate said, Shall I crucify your King, and the chief priests answered, We have no king but Caesar, for had not Christ been thus rejected of men, we had been forever rejected of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Henry: the sentence is carried out at once — yet the leading away is for us, that we might escape, the sacrifice bound to the altar in our place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Bearing his cross to Golgotha, Christ is led forth for us that we might escape; Henry sees him nailed to the cross as a sacrifice bound to the altar, dying among criminals sacrificed to public justice rather than at the altar among sacrifices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Where they crucified him, and two other with him, on either side one, and Jesus in the midst, nailed to the cross as a Sacrifice bound to the altar, fulfilling Scripture, for he did not die among sacrifices at the altar, but among criminals sacrificed to public justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Pilate would not let the chief priests alter the writing, which Henry ascribes to a secret power of God upon his heart, so that this statement of the Lord's character and authority might continue over the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Henry: the title is set where many will read it, near the city, in Hebrew, Greek, and Latin — the statement of his character and authority is published, not hidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Henry: the priests press Pilate to alter the writing, but he will not gratify them — a secret power on his heart keeps the statement of Christ's character and authority standing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Pilate answered, What I have written I have written, for a secret power of God upon his heart kept this statement of our Lord's character and authority from being altered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Henry: the parting of his clothes and the lottery over the seamless coat are prophecy fulfilled by men acting for gain, not for Scripture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Henry: the casting of lots over the coat is prophecy fulfilled by unwitting hands — all that was written shall be fulfilled, down to the last garment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Now there stood by the cross of Jesus his mother, and his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Cleophas, and Mary Magdalene, faithful women at the cross when most had fled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. When Jesus therefore saw his mother, and the disciple standing by, whom he loved, he said unto his mother, Woman, behold thy son, tenderly providing for his mother at his death, for sometimes when God removes one comfort, he raises up another where we looked not for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Then he said to the disciple, Behold thy mother, and from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home, for Christ's example teaches all men to honour their parents in life and death and to provide for their wants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Henry: knowing all is now accomplished, he speaks the thirst that fulfils Scripture — even the last bodily want is taken up into the finished work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished, and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost, for the counsels of the Father concerning his sufferings were now fulfilled, all types and prophecies accomplished, the ceremonial law abolished, an end made of transgression by everlasting righteousness, and the work of man's redemption completed, and his life was not taken by force, but freely given up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Henry: the high day sets the clock — before the Sabbath, legs are to be broken and bodies removed, and that haste will itself prove, at the cross, that he is already dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Henry: the soldiers break the legs of the two crucified beside him — the grim work done on the pair, which makes the exception for Jesus, found already dead, the point of the passage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. But when they came to Jesus, and saw that he was dead already, they brake not his legs, for he died in less time than persons crucified commonly did, showing he had laid down his life of himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. But one of the soldiers with a spear pierced his side, and forthwith came there out blood and water, signifying justification and sanctification, blood for atonement and water for purification, both flowing from the pierced side of our Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And he that saw it bare record, and his record is true, and he knows that he says true, that you might believe, for the attestation was solemn, because there was something peculiar in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. For these things were done, that the Scripture should be fulfilled, A bone of him shall not be broken, in Pilate's not allowing his legs to be broken, and there was a type of this in the paschal lamb. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. And again another Scripture says, They shall look on him whom they pierced, for we should ever look to him whom by our sins we have pierced, who shed both water and blood that we might be justified and sanctified in his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. And after this Joseph of Arimathea, being a disciple of Jesus, but secretly for fear of the Jews, besought Pilate that he might take away the body of Jesus, and Pilate gave him leave, and he came and took the body of Jesus, for disciples should openly own themselves, yet some who in lesser trials have been fearful, in greater have been courageous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. And there came also Nicodemus, who at the first came to Jesus by night, and brought a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about a hundred pound weight, for grace at first like a bruised reed may afterward resemble a strong cedar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Then they took the body of Jesus, and wound it in linen clothes with the spices, as the manner of the Jews is to bury, and hereby these two rich men showed the value they had for Christ's person and doctrine, not lessened by the reproach of the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Now in the place where he was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden a new sepulchre, wherein was never man yet laid, so it could not be said that it was not he, but some other, that rose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. There they laid Jesus therefore because of the Jews' preparation day, for the sepulchre was near at hand, and we must do our duty as the present day and opportunity are, leaving God to fulfil his promises in his own way and time, for here the Sun of Righteousness set for a while, to rise again in greater glory, and then to set no more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
