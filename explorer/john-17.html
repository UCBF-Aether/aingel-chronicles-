The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — John 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 These words spake Jesus, and lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said, Father, the hour is come; glorify thy Son, that thy Son also may glorify thee:

2 As thou hast given him power over all flesh, that he should give eternal life to as many as thou hast given him.

3 And this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent.

4 I have glorified thee on the earth: I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do.

5 And now, O Father, glorify thou me with thine own self with the glory which I had with thee before the world was.

6 I have manifested thy name unto the men which thou gavest me out of the world: thine they were, and thou gavest them me; and they have kept thy word.

7 Now they have known that all things whatsoever thou hast given me are of thee.

8 For I have given unto them the words which thou gavest me; and they have received them, and have known surely that I came out from thee, and they have believed that thou didst send me.

9 I pray for them: I pray not for the world, but for them which thou hast given me; for they are thine.

10 And all mine are thine, and thine are mine; and I am glorified in them.

11 And now I am no more in the world, but these are in the world, and I come to thee. Holy Father, keep through thine own name those whom thou hast given me, that they may be one, as we are.

12 While I was with them in the world, I kept them in thy name: those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost, but the son of perdition; that the scripture might be fulfilled.

13 And now come I to thee; and these things I speak in the world, that they might have my joy fulfilled in themselves.

14 I have given them thy word; and the world hath hated them, because they are not of the world, even as I am not of the world.

15 I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil.

16 They are not of the world, even as I am not of the world.

17 Sanctify them through thy truth: thy word is truth.

18 As thou hast sent me into the world, even so have I also sent them into the world.

19 And for their sakes I sanctify myself, that they also might be sanctified through the truth.

20 Neither pray I for these alone, but for them also which shall believe on me through their word;

21 That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us: that the world may believe that thou hast sent me.

22 And the glory which thou gavest me I have given them; that they may be one, even as we are one:

23 I in them, and thou in me, that they may be made perfect in one; and that the world may know that thou hast sent me, and hast loved them, as thou hast loved me.

24 Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am; that they may behold my glory, which thou hast given me: for thou lovedst me before the foundation of the world.

25 O righteous Father, the world hath not known thee: but I have known thee, and these have known that thou hast sent me.

26 And I have declared unto them thy name, and will declare it: that the love wherewith thou hast loved me may be in them, and I in them.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The whole chapter is one prayer. Jesus first prays for himself, that the Father glorify the Son so the Son may give eternal life — knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ whom he has sent — to all the Father has given him (vv. 1–5). He prays for the disciples he leaves in the world: keep them in thy name, sanctify them through thy truth, and keep them from the evil, as he himself was sent into the world (vv. 6–19). Finally he prays for all who will believe through their word, that they all may be one as Father and Son are one, and that the world may know the Father sent him and loved them as he loved the Son (vv. 20–26). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, John 17

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These words spoke Jesus, and lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said, Father, the hour is come; glorify thy Son, that thy Son also may glorify thee, for our Lord prayed as a man and as Mediator, yet spoke with majesty as one with and equal to the Father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. As thou hast given him power over all flesh, that he should give eternal life to as many as thou hast given him, for eternal life could not be given unless Christ, their Surety, both glorified the Father and was glorified of him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent, for this is the sinner's way to eternal life, and when this knowledge is made perfect, holiness and happiness will be fully enjoyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. I have glorified thee on the earth, he said; I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do, for the holiness and happiness of the redeemed are that glory of Christ and the Father which was the joy set before him, for which he endured the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And now, O Father, glorify thou me with thine own self with the glory which I had with thee before the world was, for he asked for the glory that was his before his humiliation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. I have manifested thy name unto the men which thou gavest me out of the world, he said; thine they were, and thou gavest them me, and they have kept thy word, for Christ prays for those that are his, given as sheep to the shepherd to be kept, as patients to the physician to be cured, as children to a tutor to be taught. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Now they have known that all things whatsoever thou hast given me are of thee, for he, all he is and has, and all he said and did, are of God, and this is our satisfaction in relying on Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. For I have given unto them the words which thou gavest me, he said, and they have received them, and have known surely that I came out from thee, and they have believed that thou didst send me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. I pray for them, he said; I pray not for the world, but for them which thou hast given me, for they are thine, for Christ offered this prayer for his people alone as believers, yet no one who desires to come to the Father need be discouraged, for he saves to the uttermost all who come unto God by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And all mine are thine, and thine are mine, he said, and I am glorified in them, for this speaks Father and Son to be one, and the Son owns none for his who are not devoted to the Father's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And now I am no more in the world, he said, but these are in the world, and I come to thee; Holy Father, keep through thine own name those whom thou hast given me, that they may be one, as we are, for he prayed not that they might be rich and great, but kept from sin, strengthened for duty, and brought safe to heaven, united as Father and Son are united. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. While I was with them in the world, I kept them in thy name, he said; those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost, but the son of perdition, that the Scripture might be fulfilled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And now come I to thee, he said, and these things I speak in the world, that they might have my joy fulfilled in themselves, for the prosperity of the soul is the best prosperity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. I have given them thy word, he said, and the world has hated them, because they are not of the world, even as I am not of the world, for the Spirit of God in true Christians is opposed to the spirit of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, he said, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil, for they had a great work to do for God's glory and mankind's benefit, and must pass through the world as through an enemy's country, kept from corruption, remaining sin, and Satan's power and craft. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. They are not of the world, even as I am not of the world, he repeated, for they are not left here to pursue the world's objects, but to glorify God and serve their generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Sanctify them through thy truth, he prayed; thy word is truth, for Christ prayed not only that they be kept from evil, but made good, and the means of this grace is through God's truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. As thou hast sent me into the world, even so have I also sent them into the world, for their mission follows his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And for their sakes I sanctify myself, he said, that they also might be sanctified through the truth, for Jesus entirely devoted himself to his undertaking, especially offering himself without spot unto God, and the real holiness of Christians is the fruit of his death, by which the Holy Ghost was purchased. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Neither pray I for these alone, he said, but for them also which shall believe on me through their word, for his prayer extends to all future believers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. That they all may be one, he prayed, as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us, that the world may believe that thou hast sent me, for believers are to be one body under one head, animated by one soul, through union with Christ and the Father in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And the glory which thou gavest me I have given them, he said, that they may be one, even as we are one, for unity is the gift that accompanies his glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. I in them, and thou in me, that they may be made perfect in one, he said, and that the world may know that thou hast sent me, and hast loved them, as thou hast loved me, for the more believers dispute about lesser things, the more they throw doubts upon Christianity, and unity convinces the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am, he said, that they may behold my glory, which thou hast given me, for thou lovedst me before the foundation of the world, for Christ, as one with the Father, claimed that the redeemed be brought to heaven to behold his glory as their beloved Friend and Brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. O righteous Father, the world has not known thee, he said, but I have known thee, and these have known that thou hast sent me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And I have declared unto them thy name, and will declare it, he said, that the love wherewith thou hast loved me may be in them, and I in them, for he had declared and would further declare God's name by his doctrine and Spirit, that, joined to him by one Spirit, they might be filled with all the fullness of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
