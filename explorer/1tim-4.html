The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Timothy 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the Spirit speaketh expressly, that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils;

2 Speaking lies in hypocrisy; having their conscience seared with a hot iron;

3 Forbidding to marry, and commanding to abstain from meats, which God hath created to be received with thanksgiving of them which believe and know the truth.

4 For every creature of God is good, and nothing to be refused, if it be received with thanksgiving:

5 For it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer.

6 If thou put the brethren in remembrance of these things, thou shalt be a good minister of Jesus Christ, nourished up in the words of faith and of good doctrine, whereunto thou hast attained.

7 But refuse profane and old wives’ fables, and exercise thyself rather unto godliness.

8 For bodily exercise profiteth little: but godliness is profitable unto all things, having promise of the life that now is, and of that which is to come.

9 This is a faithful saying and worthy of all acceptation.

10 For therefore we both labour and suffer reproach, because we trust in the living God, who is the Saviour of all men, specially of those that believe.

11 These things command and teach.

12 Let no man despise thy youth; but be thou an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity.

13 Till I come, give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine.

14 Neglect not the gift that is in thee, which was given thee by prophecy, with the laying on of the hands of the presbytery.

15 Meditate upon these things; give thyself wholly to them; that thy profiting may appear to all.

16 Take heed unto thyself, and unto the doctrine; continue in them: for in doing this thou shalt both save thyself, and them that hear thee.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The Spirit expressly warns of later departures, Paul says, when some will forbid marriage and command abstinence from foods, though every creature of God is good and sanctified by word and prayer. Timothy is to put the brothers in mind of these things, refuse godless myths, train himself in godliness, which holds promise for this life and the next, and be an example in speech, conduct, love, faith and purity. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Spirit speaks expressly that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits and doctrines of devils, and Henry notes this turning was foretold in both Testaments for the whole Christian dispensation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. They speak lies in hypocrisy, having their conscience seared with a hot iron, so that false teaching is carried by men whose moral sense has been cauterised rather than merely mistaken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They forbid marriage and command abstinence from meats God created to be received with thanksgiving, and Henry names the pattern: forbidding as evil what God allowed, and commanding as duty what he left indifferent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be refused if received with thanksgiving, so that the creation itself answers the ascetic error. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. It is sanctified by the word of God and prayer, and Henry adds that nothing will be good to us unless we seek the Lord's blessing upon it by prayer, while nothing justifies an intemperate or improper use of things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Timothy, putting the brethren in remembrance of these things, will be a good minister of Jesus Christ, nourished in the words of faith and good doctrine, and Henry notes that outward acts of self-denial profit little if we do not mortify sin itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He must refuse profane and old wives' fables and exercise himself unto godliness, trading idle tales for disciplined training in the faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Bodily exercise profits little, but godliness is profitable for all things, having promise of the life that now is and of that which is to come, and Henry observes that the gain of godliness lies much in the promise, especially for the life to come, so that though we lose for Christ we shall not lose by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. This is a faithful saying worthy of all acceptation, and Henry treats the promise of godliness as words to be depended on, not as pious exaggeration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Therefore believers labour and suffer reproach because they trust in the living God, the Saviour of all men, especially of those that believe, and Henry notes that if Christ is thus the Saviour of all, much more will he be the Rewarder of those who seek and serve him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. These things Timothy must command and teach, with authority, since the directions carry apostolic weight and are not offered as mere suggestions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. No man may despise Timothy's youth, but he must be an example in word, conversation, charity, spirit, faith, and purity, and Henry notes that youth will not be despised if kept from vanities and follies, and that those who teach by doctrine must teach by life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Until Paul comes, Timothy must give attendance to reading, exhortation, and doctrine, making public Scripture and its application his principal occupation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He must not neglect the gift given by prophecy with the laying on of the elders' hands, since ordination gifts are to be used, not buried. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. He must meditate on these things and give himself wholly to them, so that his profiting may appear to all, and Henry notes that a minister's doctrine must be scriptural, clear, evangelical, and practical, leaving no leisure for trifling visits or idle conversation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He must take heed to himself and to the doctrine, continuing in them, for in doing so he will save both himself and his hearers, and Henry closes by praying that every believer's profiting may appear, experiencing the gospel's power in his own soul and its fruits in his life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms
