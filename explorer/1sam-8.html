The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, when Samuel was old, that he made his sons judges over Israel.

2 Now the name of his firstborn was Joel; and the name of his second, Abiah: they were judges in Beersheba.

3 And his sons walked not in his ways, but turned aside after lucre, and took bribes, and perverted judgment.

4 Then all the elders of Israel gathered themselves together, and came to Samuel unto Ramah,

5 And said unto him, Behold, thou art old, and thy sons walk not in thy ways: now make us a king to judge us like all the nations.

6 But the thing displeased Samuel, when they said, Give us a king to judge us. And Samuel prayed unto the LORD.

7 And the LORD said unto Samuel, Hearken unto the voice of the people in all that they say unto thee: for they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them.

8 According to all the works which they have done since the day that I brought them up out of Egypt even unto this day, wherewith they have forsaken me, and served other gods, so do they also unto thee.

9 Now therefore hearken unto their voice: howbeit yet protest solemnly unto them, and shew them the manner of the king that shall reign over them.

10 And Samuel told all the words of the LORD unto the people that asked of him a king.

11 And he said, This will be the manner of the king that shall reign over you: He will take your sons, and appoint them for himself, for his chariots, and to be his horsemen; and some shall run before his chariots.

12 And he will appoint him captains over thousands, and captains over fifties; and will set them to ear his ground, and to reap his harvest, and to make his instruments of war, and instruments of his chariots.

13 And he will take your daughters to be confectionaries, and to be cooks, and to be bakers.

14 And he will take your fields, and your vineyards, and your oliveyards, even the best of them, and give them to his servants.

15 And he will take the tenth of your seed, and of your vineyards, and give to his officers, and to his servants.

16 And he will take your menservants, and your maidservants, and your goodliest young men, and your asses, and put them to his work.

17 He will take the tenth of your sheep: and ye shall be his servants.

18 And ye shall cry out in that day because of your king which ye shall have chosen you; and the LORD will not hear you in that day.

19 Nevertheless the people refused to obey the voice of Samuel; and they said, Nay; but we will have a king over us;

20 That we also may be like all the nations; and that our king may judge us, and go out before us, and fight our battles.

21 And Samuel heard all the words of the people, and he rehearsed them in the ears of the LORD.

22 And the LORD said to Samuel, Hearken unto their voice, and make them a king. And Samuel said unto the men of Israel, Go ye every man unto his city.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

When Samuel is old he makes his sons Joel and Abiah judges in Beersheba, but they walk not in his ways, turning aside after lucre, taking bribes and perverting judgment. All the elders gather at Ramah and demand, make us a king to judge us like all the nations. The thing displeases Samuel, and he prays; the LORD answers that they have not rejected Samuel but have rejected God, that he should not reign over them, yet he is to hearken and protest solemnly, showing them the manner of the king: sons for chariots and harvests, daughters for cooks and bakers, fields, vineyards, tenths and servants taken, until they shall cry out and the LORD will not hear them. Nevertheless the people refuse to obey, saying, Nay, but we will have a king over us, that our king may judge us, go out before us, and fight our battles, and the LORD says, Hearken to their voice and make them a king. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When Samuel was old, he made his sons judges over Israel. Age passed the burden to the next generation, but the sons would not carry it as he had. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. His firstborn was Joel and his second Abiah, judges in Beersheba. The southern post put them far from Samuel's eye at Ramah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. His sons walked not in his ways but turned aside after lucre, took bribes, and perverted judgment. They were not so profane as Eli's sons, Henry notes, but they were corrupt judges; Samuel took no bribes, but his sons did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Then all the elders of Israel gathered and came to Samuel at Ramah. The grievance was sharpened, Henry adds, by the threat of invasion from Nahash king of the Ammonites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. They said, Thou art old, thy sons walk not in thy ways; now make us a king to judge us like all the nations. The elders turned a real grievance into a demand for a new kind of government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The thing displeased Samuel when they said, Give us a king to judge us, and Samuel prayed unto the LORD. He could patiently bear what reflected on himself and his family, Henry observes, but this reflected upon God, and it drove him to his knees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The LORD said to Samuel, Hearken to the people's voice in all they say, for they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them. The demand was, at root, a rejection of God's own kingship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. According to all their works since Egypt, forsaking God and serving other gods, so they do also unto thee. The rejection of Samuel was of a piece with Israel's long history of forsaking God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Now therefore hearken to their voice, yet protest solemnly unto them and show them the manner of the king that shall reign over them. God would grant the request, but not without a solemn warning of its cost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Samuel told all the words of the LORD to the people who asked for a king, delivering God's answer faithfully, warning and all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. This will be the manner of the king, he said: he will take your sons and appoint them for his chariots and horsemen, and some shall run before his chariots. If they would have a king to rule as eastern kings ruled, Henry notes, they would find the yoke exceedingly heavy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. He will appoint captains over thousands and over fifties, and set them to ear his ground, reap his harvest, and make his instruments of war and of his chariots. The king's glory would be built on the people's sons and labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. He will take your daughters to be confectionaries, cooks, and bakers. The court would consume the household's daughters as well as its sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. He will take your fields, vineyards, and oliveyards, even the best of them, and give them to his servants. The law of God and the manner of men widely differ, Henry observes; the latter should measure our expectations from others, not rule our relations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. He will take the tenth of your seed and vineyards and give it to his officers and servants. A second tithe, owed not to God but to the crown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. He will take your menservants, maidservants, goodliest young men, and asses, and put them to his work. Nothing the household owned would be exempt from the king's taking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. He will take the tenth of your sheep, and ye shall be his servants. The people who asked for a king to judge them would end as the king's servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Ye shall cry out in that day because of your chosen king, and the LORD will not hear you in that day. When we bring ourselves into distress by our own wrong desires and projects, Henry warns, we justly forfeit the comfort of prayer and the benefit of Divine aid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Nevertheless the people refused to obey Samuel and said, Nay, but we will have a king over us. They were obstinate and urgent in their demand; sudden resolves and hasty desires make work for long and leisurely repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. That we also may be like all the nations, they said, and that our king may judge us, go out before us, and fight our battles. Likeness to the nations, not faithfulness to God, was the prize they wanted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Samuel heard all the people's words and rehearsed them in the ears of the LORD. When anything disturbs us, Henry counsels, it is our interest as well as our duty to show our trouble before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. The LORD said, Hearken to their voice and make them a king, and Samuel sent every man to his city. God was not pleased with their request, Henry notes, but as he sometimes opposes us from loving-kindness, so at other times he gratifies us in wrath; he knows how to bring glory to himself even by men's foolish counsels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
