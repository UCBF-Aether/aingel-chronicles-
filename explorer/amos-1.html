The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Amos 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The words of Amos, who was among the herdmen of Tekoa, which he saw concerning Israel in the days of Uzziah king of Judah, and in the days of Jeroboam the son of Joash king of Israel, two years before the earthquake.

2 And he said, The LORD will roar from Zion, and utter his voice from Jerusalem; and the habitations of the shepherds shall mourn, and the top of Carmel shall wither.

3 Thus saith the LORD; For three transgressions of Damascus, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof; because they have threshed Gilead with threshing instruments of iron:

4 But I will send a fire into the house of Hazael, which shall devour the palaces of Benhadad.

5 I will break also the bar of Damascus, and cut off the inhabitant from the plain of Aven, and him that holdeth the sceptre from the house of Eden: and the people of Syria shall go into captivity unto Kir, saith the LORD.

6 Thus saith the LORD; For three transgressions of Gaza, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof; because they carried away captive the whole captivity, to deliver them up to Edom:

7 But I will send a fire on the wall of Gaza, which shall devour the palaces thereof:

8 And I will cut off the inhabitant from Ashdod, and him that holdeth the sceptre from Ashkelon, and I will turn mine hand against Ekron: and the remnant of the Philistines shall perish, saith the Lord GOD.

9 Thus saith the LORD; For three transgressions of Tyrus, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof; because they delivered up the whole captivity to Edom, and remembered not the brotherly covenant:

10 But I will send a fire on the wall of Tyrus, which shall devour the palaces thereof.

11 Thus saith the LORD; For three transgressions of Edom, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof; because he did pursue his brother with the sword, and did cast off all pity, and his anger did tear perpetually, and he kept his wrath for ever:

12 But I will send a fire upon Teman, which shall devour the palaces of Bozrah.

13 Thus saith the LORD; For three transgressions of the children of Ammon, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof; because they have ripped up the women with child of Gilead, that they might enlarge their border:

14 But I will kindle a fire in the wall of Rabbah, and it shall devour the palaces thereof, with shouting in the day of battle, with a tempest in the day of the whirlwind:

15 And their king shall go into captivity, he and his princes together, saith the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Amos, a herdman of Tekoa, hears the LORD roar from Zion, and the chapter pronounces judgment nation by nation, Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, and Ammon, each for three transgressions and for four, for cruelties such as threshing Gilead with iron instruments and ripping up the women with child of Gilead. The pattern is fixed before it ever turns on Israel: the measure of sins is full and ripe for vengeance. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The words of Amos, a herdman of Tekoa, which he saw concerning Israel in the days of Uzziah of Judah and Jeroboam of Israel, two years before the earthquake: God employed a shepherd to reprove and warn, and those given abilities for his service ought not to be despised for their origin or employment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The Lord will roar from Zion and utter his voice from Jerusalem, and shepherds' habitations shall mourn and Carmel's top wither, judgment announced from God's chosen city and royal seat, from which the ten tribes had revolted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Damascus, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because they threshed Gilead with iron threshing instruments: the number means not exactly three or four but many, the measure of sins filled and ripe for vengeance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. God will send fire into the house of Hazael, devouring Benhadad's palaces, the Syrian royal house itself judged for its cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He will break Damascus's bar, cut off the inhabitant from the plain of Aven and the scepter-holder from the house of Eden, and Syria shall go captive to Kir, the method of judgment partly the same for each nation yet peculiar in each. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Gaza, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because they carried away the whole captivity to deliver them to Edom, Philistia handing God's people to their most inveterate enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. God will send fire on Gaza's wall, devouring its palaces, desolating judgment on Philistine power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He will cut off the inhabitant from Ashdod, the scepter-holder from Ashkelon, and turn his hand against Ekron, and the remnant of the Philistines shall perish, the whole Philistine coast judged city by city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Tyre, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because they delivered up the whole captivity to Edom and remembered not the brotherly covenant, the covenant between Hiram and David and Solomon broken for profit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. God will send fire on Tyre's wall, devouring its palaces, the merchant city's strength consumed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Edom, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because he pursued his brother with the sword, cast off all pity, and his anger tore perpetually, keeping wrath forever, bitterness against God's people shown in every age. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. God will send fire upon Teman, devouring the palaces of Bozrah, Edom's strength and glory threatened with utter overthrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Ammon, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because they ripped up the women with child of Gilead to enlarge their border, cruelty used to empty land of future claimants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. God will kindle fire in Rabbah's wall, devouring its palaces with shouting in battle and tempest in whirlwind, judgment coming with irresistible force and surprising swiftness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And Ammon's king shall go into captivity, he and his princes together, says the Lord, so that when the Lord reckons with his enemies, how tremendous are his judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Three transgressions, and for four. not a literal count: the study note for Amos 1 explains the number as meaning many transgressions, the measure of sins filled and ripe for vengeance. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
