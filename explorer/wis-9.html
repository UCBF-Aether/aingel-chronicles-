The Text — Wisdom of Solomon 9 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 O God of my fathers, and Lord of mercy, who hast made all things with thy word,

2 And ordained man through thy wisdom, that he should have dominion over the creatures which thou hast made,

3 And order the world according to equity and righteousness, and execute judgment with an upright heart:

4 Give me wisdom, that sitteth by thy throne; and reject me not from among thy children:

5 For I thy servant and son of thine handmaid am a feeble person, and of a short time, and too young for the understanding of judgment and laws.

6 For though a man be never so perfect among the children of men, yet if thy wisdom be not with him, he shall be nothing regarded.

7 Thou hast chosen me to be a king of thy people, and a judge of thy sons and daughters:

8 Thou hast commanded me to build a temple upon thy holy mount, and an altar in the city wherein thou dwellest, a resemblance of the holy tabernacle, which thou hast prepared from the beginning.

9 And wisdom was with thee: which knoweth thy works, and was present when thou madest the world, and knew what was acceptable in thy sight, and right in thy commandments.

10 O send her out of thy holy heavens, and from the throne of thy glory, that being present she may labour with me, that I may know what is pleasing unto thee.

11 For she knoweth and understandeth all things, and she shall lead me soberly in my doings, and preserve me in her power.

12 So shall my works be acceptable, and then shall I judge thy people righteously, and be worthy to sit in my father's seat.

13 For what man is he that can know the counsel of God? or who can think what the will of the Lord is?

14 For the thoughts of mortal men are miserable, and our devices are but uncertain.

15 For the corruptible body presseth down the soul, and the earthy tabernacle weigheth down the mind that museth upon many things.

16 And hardly do we guess aright at things that are upon earth, and with labour do we find the things that are before us: but the things that are in heaven who hath searched out?

17 And thy counsel who hath known, except thou give wisdom, and send thy Holy Spirit from above?

18 For so the ways of them which lived on the earth were reformed, and men were taught the things that are pleasing unto thee, and were saved through wisdom.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A prayer for the wisdom that sits by God's throne: the speaker, feeble and short-lived though chosen king and commanded to build a temple, asks to be sent wisdom from heaven to lead him soberly and make his works acceptable. Mortal thoughts are miserable and uncertain, the corruptible body weighs down the soul, and no one knows God's counsel except by wisdom and the Holy Spirit sent from above. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. O God of my fathers, and Lord of mercy, who made all things with your word. Haydock's prayer begins from creation by the word, the ground of asking wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. And ordained man through your wisdom, that he should have dominion over the creatures you made. Haydock notes man's dominion is ordained through wisdom, not by mere strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. And order the world according to equity and righteousness, and execute judgment with an upright heart. Haydock observes that ordering the world aright requires equity, righteousness, and an upright heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. Give me wisdom, who sits by your throne, and reject me not among your children. Haydock's petition is to sit, as it were, beside God's throne, and not to be rejected from sonship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. For I am your servant, and son of your handmaid, a weak man and of a short time, and too young to understand judgment and laws. Haydock notes the petitioner pleads weakness, shortness of life, and youth as reasons for the gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. For though a man is never so perfect among the children of men, yet if your wisdom is not with him, he shall be nothing regarded. Haydock's point is that perfection without wisdom counts for nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. You have chosen me to be king of your people, and a judge of your sons and daughters. Haydock observes the office itself creates the need: a king and judge must have wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. You commanded me to build a temple upon your holy mount, and an altar in the city of your dwelling, a resemblance of the holy tabernacle you prepared from the beginning. Haydock notes the temple is built after a heavenly pattern. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. With you is wisdom, who knows your works and was present when you made the world, and knew what was acceptable in your sight and right in your commandments. Haydock stresses wisdom's presence at creation and her knowledge of God's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. Send her out of your holy heavens, and from the throne of your glory, that being present she may labour with me, that I may know what is pleasing to you. Haydock's petition is for wisdom to be sent down to labour alongside the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. She knows and understands all things, and shall lead me soberly in my doings, and preserve me in her power. Haydock notes wisdom both guides soberly and preserves by her power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. So shall my works be acceptable, and I shall judge your people righteously, and be worthy to sit in my father's seat. Haydock observes that acceptable works and righteous judgment follow wisdom's guidance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. For what man is he that can know God's counsel, or who can think what the Lord's will is? Haydock's question is rhetorical: no man by himself knows God's counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. The thoughts of mortal men are miserable, and our devices are but uncertain. Haydock notes human thought is miserable and devices uncertain without wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. The corruptible body presses down the soul, and the earthly tabernacle weighs down the mind that muses upon many things. Haydock's image is weight: body and tabernacle press down the musing mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. We can hardly guess at things on earth, and with labour find things before us; but things in heaven, who has searched out? Haydock observes that even earthly things are hard, and heavenly things beyond searching without God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. Your counsel, who has known, except you give wisdom and send your Holy Spirit from above? Haydock's point is that counsel is known only by the gift of wisdom and the Holy Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. So the ways of those on earth were reformed, and men were taught the things pleasing to you, and were saved through wisdom. Haydock closes the prayer: reformation, teaching, and salvation all come through wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
