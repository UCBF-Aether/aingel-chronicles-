The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 34 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Dinah the daughter of Leah, which she bare unto Jacob, went out to see the daughters of the land.

2 And when Shechem the son of Hamor the Hivite, prince of the country, saw her, he took her, and lay with her, and defiled her.

3 And his soul clave unto Dinah the daughter of Jacob, and he loved the damsel, and spake kindly unto the damsel.

4 And Shechem spake unto his father Hamor, saying, Get me this damsel to wife.

5 And Jacob heard that he had defiled Dinah his daughter: now his sons were with his cattle in the field: and Jacob held his peace until they were come.

6 And Hamor the father of Shechem went out unto Jacob to commune with him.

7 And the sons of Jacob came out of the field when they heard it: and the men were grieved, and they were very wroth, because he had wrought folly in Israel in lying with Jacob’s daughter: which thing ought not to be done.

8 And Hamor communed with them, saying, The soul of my son Shechem longeth for your daughter: I pray you give her him to wife.

9 And make ye marriages with us, and give your daughters unto us, and take our daughters unto you.

10 And ye shall dwell with us: and the land shall be before you; dwell and trade ye therein, and get you possessions therein.

11 And Shechem said unto her father and unto her brethren, Let me find grace in your eyes, and what ye shall say unto me I will give.

12 Ask me never so much dowry and gift, and I will give according as ye shall say unto me: but give me the damsel to wife.

13 And the sons of Jacob answered Shechem and Hamor his father deceitfully, and said, because he had defiled Dinah their sister:

14 And they said unto them, We cannot do this thing, to give our sister to one that is uncircumcised; for that were a reproach unto us:

15 But in this will we consent unto you: If ye will be as we be, that every male of you be circumcised;

16 Then will we give our daughters unto you, and we will take your daughters to us, and we will dwell with you, and we will become one people.

17 But if ye will not hearken unto us, to be circumcised; then will we take our daughter, and we will be gone.

18 And their words pleased Hamor, and Shechem Hamor’s son.

19 And the young man deferred not to do the thing, because he had delight in Jacob’s daughter: and he was more honourable than all the house of his father.

20 And Hamor and Shechem his son came unto the gate of their city, and communed with the men of their city, saying,

21 These men are peaceable with us; therefore let them dwell in the land, and trade therein; for the land, behold, it is large enough for them; let us take their daughters to us for wives, and let us give them our daughters.

22 Only herein will the men consent unto us for to dwell with us, to be one people, if every male among us be circumcised, as they are circumcised.

23 Shall not their cattle and their substance and every beast of theirs be ours? only let us consent unto them, and they will dwell with us.

24 And unto Hamor and unto Shechem his son hearkened all that went out of the gate of his city; and every male was circumcised, all that went out of the gate of his city.

25 And it came to pass on the third day, when they were sore, that two of the sons of Jacob, Simeon and Levi, Dinah’s brethren, took each man his sword, and came upon the city boldly, and slew all the males.

26 And they slew Hamor and Shechem his son with the edge of the sword, and took Dinah out of Shechem’s house, and went out.

27 The sons of Jacob came upon the slain, and spoiled the city, because they had defiled their sister.

28 They took their sheep, and their oxen, and their asses, and that which was in the city, and that which was in the field,

29 And all their wealth, and all their little ones, and their wives took they captive, and spoiled even all that was in the house.

30 And Jacob said to Simeon and Levi, Ye have troubled me to make me to stink among the inhabitants of the land, among the Canaanites and the Perizzites: and I being few in number, they shall gather themselves together against me, and slay me; and I shall be destroyed, I and my house.

31 And they said, Should he deal with our sister as with an harlot?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Dinah, Jacob's daughter, is violated by Shechem son of Hamor, who then asks to marry her. Her brothers demand circumcision as the price of intermarriage, then Simeon and Levi kill the men of the city while they are sore, plunder it, and Jacob rebukes them for making his household a target. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Dinah, Leah's daughter, went out to see the daughters of the land. Henry warns that young people are never safer than under pious parents' care, and that going abroad among strangers to true religion exposes them to danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Shechem, son of Hamor the prince of the country, saw Dinah, took her, and defiled her. JFB notes the wrong followed from free mixing in the society of the place, where a young and inexperienced girl was flattered by a ruler's son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Shechem's soul clung to Dinah, he loved her and spoke kindly to her. His attachment, JFB notes, must have grown through time and opportunity, not a single glance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Shechem asked his father Hamor to get Dinah for him as his wife. The request treated marriage as the remedy for the wrong already done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Jacob heard that Dinah had been defiled, but his sons were in the field, so he held his peace until they came. JFB explains that in a family by different wives, full brothers were the guardians and avengers of a sister's welfare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Hamor came out to Jacob to talk with him about the matter. The fathers' meeting might have led to an amicable arrangement, before the brothers' arrival changed the temper of the talks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Jacob's sons came in from the field, grieved and very angry, because Shechem had wrought folly in Israel by lying with Jacob's daughter. JFB notes good men could not but grieve, but their anger should have been governed, not left to harden into revenge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Hamor proposed that Shechem's soul longed for Dinah, and asked Jacob's family to give her to him in marriage. JFB observes the prince and his son showed no jealousy of the powerful shepherds and seemed eager for friendly intercourse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Hamor broadened the offer into intermarriage and shared dwelling, trade, and possession of the land. The proposal would have joined the two peoples, not merely two persons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. He promised they could dwell, trade, and get possessions in a land open before them. The offer was generous in worldly terms, which made the brothers' answer more consequential. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Shechem himself then spoke to Dinah's father and brothers, asking to find grace in their eyes. JFB notes Jacob showed weakness in yielding so much of the negotiation to his fiery sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Shechem offered any dowry and gift they named, if only they would give him Dinah as wife. JFB explains the gift as betrothal presents to bride and kin, and the dowry as a settlement upon her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Jacob's sons answered Shechem and Hamor deceitfully, because Dinah had been defiled. JFB condemns the deceit sharply: they used a show of religion as a cloak for a cruel and treacherous design. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. They said they could not give their sister to an uncircumcised man, for that would be a reproach to them. The objection was true in form — circumcision was the sign of the covenant — but false in intent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They would consent only if every male among the Shechemites was circumcised, as Jacob's household was. JFB notes they required nothing more: no teaching of the true God, only the outward rite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. On that condition they would give their daughters and take Shechemite daughters, dwell together, and become one people. The bargain promised union on the basis of a rite they did not mean honestly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. If the Shechemites would not be circumcised, Jacob's sons said they would take Dinah and leave. The threat made the condition look like a genuine boundary rather than a trap. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The words pleased Hamor and Shechem. Their ready acceptance shows they took the proposal at face value. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The young man did not delay, because he delighted in Jacob's daughter and was more honourable than all his father's house. His eagerness, not suspicion, drove him to act at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Hamor and Shechem came to the gate of their city, the place where public business was done, and spoke to its men. JFB notes the gate was where every public communication was made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. They told the citizens the men were peaceable and the land was large enough for shared dwelling and trade. The pitch presented Jacob's household as profitable neighbours, not as a threat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The single condition repeated to the city was that every male be circumcised, as Jacob's men were. The leaders asked their people to accept a sacred rite for the sake of alliance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Hamor and Shechem added a worldly motive: the newcomers' cattle, substance, and beasts would become theirs if they consented. JFB sees in the people's ready submission either great affection for the ruling family or Eastern despotism. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. All who went out of the city gate listened to Hamor and Shechem, and every male was circumcised. Henry notes the Shechemites submitted to the rite only to please their prince and enrich themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. On the third day, when the men were sore, Simeon and Levi, Dinah's brothers, each took a sword and came boldly on the city. Henry traces the chain: foolish pleasure to seduction, wrath to revenge, revenge to treachery, treachery to murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. They slew all the males, then slew Hamor and Shechem with the sword and took Dinah out of Shechem's house. JFB stresses that Simeon and Levi alone, with their retainers, were the guilty actors in the massacre. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The other sons of Jacob then came upon the slain and spoiled the city, because their sister had been defiled. Revenge, once begun, widened into plunder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. They took sheep, oxen, asses, and all that was in city and field. The spoil shows the attack had become a raid, not a rescue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. They took all the wealth, the little ones, and the wives captive, and spoiled everything in the houses. Henry notes how one sin leads on to another, spreading desolation like fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Jacob told Simeon and Levi they had troubled him and made him stink among Canaanites and Perizzites, leaving his few household exposed to united vengeance. JFB notes Jacob dwelt on consequences, probably because that was the only argument likely to reach his hardened sons, and that only God's restraint saved the family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. The brothers answered with a question: should Shechem have dealt with their sister as with a harlot? Their retort shows no repentance, only the original grievance restated as justification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
