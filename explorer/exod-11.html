The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD said unto Moses, Yet will I bring one plague more upon Pharaoh, and upon Egypt; afterwards he will let you go hence: when he shall let you go, he shall surely thrust you out hence altogether.

2 Speak now in the ears of the people, and let every man borrow of his neighbour, and every woman of her neighbour, jewels of silver and jewels of gold.

3 And the LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians. Moreover the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh’s servants, and in the sight of the people.

4 And Moses said, Thus saith the LORD, About midnight will I go out into the midst of Egypt:

5 And all the firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the first born of Pharaoh that sitteth upon his throne, even unto the firstborn of the maidservant that is behind the mill; and all the firstborn of beasts.

6 And there shall be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as there was none like it, nor shall be like it any more.

7 But against any of the children of Israel shall not a dog move his tongue, against man or beast: that ye may know how that the LORD doth put a difference between the Egyptians and Israel.

8 And all these thy servants shall come down unto me, and bow down themselves unto me, saying, Get thee out, and all the people that follow thee: and after that I will go out. And he went out from Pharaoh in a great anger.

9 And the LORD said unto Moses, Pharaoh shall not hearken unto you; that my wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt.

10 And Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh: and the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart, so that he would not let the children of Israel go out of his land.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God announces one plague more, after which Pharaoh will drive Israel out altogether. At midnight the LORD will strike every firstborn in Egypt, from Pharaoh's heir to the servant's child, while against Israel not even a dog will move its tongue, so that Egypt will know the LORD has put a difference between Egypt and Israel. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD told Moses he would bring yet one plague more upon Pharaoh and Egypt, after which Pharaoh would let them go, and thrust them out altogether. Henry notes the last plague would finish what nine had begun: not a reluctant permission but an expulsion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Moses was to speak in the people's ears, that every man borrow of his neighbour, and every woman of her neighbour, jewels of silver and jewels of gold. Henry observes Israel was to ask openly, not plunder secretly, and the asking itself was part of the judgment on Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians, and Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh's servants and of the people. Henry notes the reversal: the despised shepherd stood great in the land that had enslaved him, and even Egypt's court honoured him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Moses said, Thus saith the LORD, About midnight will I go out into the midst of Egypt. Henry observes the time was named: midnight, when Egypt slept in security, would be the hour of its waking to death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. All the firstborn in Egypt would die, from Pharaoh's firstborn upon his throne to the firstborn of the maidservant behind the mill, and all the firstborn of beasts. Henry notes the stroke levelled every rank: throne and mill shared one sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. There would be a great cry throughout all Egypt, such as there was none like it before, nor shall be like it again. Henry observes the cry would be universal because the loss was universal: no house without its firstborn dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. But against any of Israel not a dog would move its tongue, against man or beast, that Egypt might know the LORD put a difference between Egyptians and Israel. Henry notes the contrast was total: Egypt wailed while Israel slept in silence, and even the dogs kept still. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. All Pharaoh's servants would come down and bow down to Moses, saying, Get thee out, and all the people that follow thee, and after that Moses would go out, and he went out from Pharaoh in great anger. Henry observes the proudest court in the world would beg the deliverer to leave, and Moses's anger was righteous grief at a king who would only learn by death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The LORD told Moses Pharaoh would not hearken, that his wonders might be multiplied in Egypt. Henry notes the final refusal was foreknown, yet the warning was still given, for God's justice is never without witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh, and the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, so that he would not let Israel go. Henry closes the nine plagues on this summary: all wonders done, all refused, and the tenth now stood at the door. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
