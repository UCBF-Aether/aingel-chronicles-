The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 So they brought the ark of God, and set it in the midst of the tent that David had pitched for it: and they offered burnt sacrifices and peace offerings before God.

2 And when David had made an end of offering the burnt offerings and the peace offerings, he blessed the people in the name of the LORD.

3 And he dealt to every one of Israel, both man and woman, to every one a loaf of bread, and a good piece of flesh, and a flagon of wine.

4 And he appointed certain of the Levites to minister before the ark of the LORD, and to record, and to thank and praise the LORD God of Israel:

5 Asaph the chief, and next to him Zechariah, Jeiel, and Shemiramoth, and Jehiel, and Mattithiah, and Eliab, and Benaiah, and Obededom: and Jeiel with psalteries and with harps; but Asaph made a sound with cymbals;

6 Benaiah also and Jahaziel the priests with trumpets continually before the ark of the covenant of God.

7 Then on that day David delivered first this psalm to thank the LORD into the hand of Asaph and his brethren.

8 Give thanks unto the LORD, call upon his name, make known his deeds among the people.

9 Sing unto him, sing psalms unto him, talk ye of all his wondrous works.

10 Glory ye in his holy name: let the heart of them rejoice that seek the LORD.

11 Seek the LORD and his strength, seek his face continually.

12 Remember his marvellous works that he hath done, his wonders, and the judgments of his mouth;

13 O ye seed of Israel his servant, ye children of Jacob, his chosen ones.

14 He is the LORD our God; his judgments are in all the earth.

15 Be ye mindful always of his covenant; the word which he commanded to a thousand generations;

16 Even of the covenant which he made with Abraham, and of his oath unto Isaac;

17 And hath confirmed the same to Jacob for a law, and to Israel for an everlasting covenant,

18 Saying, Unto thee will I give the land of Canaan, the lot of your inheritance;

19 When ye were but few, even a few, and strangers in it.

20 And when they went from nation to nation, and from one kingdom to another people;

21 He suffered no man to do them wrong: yea, he reproved kings for their sakes,

22 Saying, Touch not mine anointed, and do my prophets no harm.

23 Sing unto the LORD, all the earth; shew forth from day to day his salvation.

24 Declare his glory among the heathen; his marvellous works among all nations.

25 For great is the LORD, and greatly to be praised: he also is to be feared above all gods.

26 For all the gods of the people are idols: but the LORD made the heavens.

27 Glory and honour are in his presence; strength and gladness are in his place.

28 Give unto the LORD, ye kindreds of the people, give unto the LORD glory and strength.

29 Give unto the LORD the glory due unto his name: bring an offering, and come before him: worship the LORD in the beauty of holiness.

30 Fear before him, all the earth: the world also shall be stable, that it be not moved.

31 Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth rejoice: and let men say among the nations, The LORD reigneth.

32 Let the sea roar, and the fulness thereof: let the fields rejoice, and all that is therein.

33 Then shall the trees of the wood sing out at the presence of the LORD, because he cometh to judge the earth.

34 O give thanks unto the LORD; for he is good; for his mercy endureth for ever.

35 And say ye, Save us, O God of our salvation, and gather us together, and deliver us from the heathen, that we may give thanks to thy holy name, and glory in thy praise.

36 Blessed be the LORD God of Israel for ever and ever. And all the people said, Amen, and praised the LORD.

37 So he left there before the ark of the covenant of the LORD Asaph and his brethren, to minister before the ark continually, as every day’s work required:

38 And Obededom with their brethren, threescore and eight; Obededom also the son of Jeduthun and Hosah to be porters:

39 And Zadok the priest, and his brethren the priests, before the tabernacle of the LORD in the high place that was at Gibeon,

40 To offer burnt offerings unto the LORD upon the altar of the burnt offering continually morning and evening, and to do according to all that is written in the law of the LORD, which he commanded Israel;

41 And with them Heman and Jeduthun, and the rest that were chosen, who were expressed by name, to give thanks to the LORD, because his mercy endureth for ever;

42 And with them Heman and Jeduthun with trumpets and cymbals for those that should make a sound, and with musical instruments of God. And the sons of Jeduthun were porters.

43 And all the people departed every man to his house: and David returned to bless his house.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the ark's arrival in David's tent, the sacrifices offered there, David's psalm of thanksgiving, and the Levites appointed to minister before the ark continually. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. They brought the ark of God and set it in the midst of the tent David pitched for it, and offered burnt sacrifices and peace offerings before God. Henry calls it a glorious day: the ark, once obscure in a country town and borrowed in a private house, now had its own habitation in the royal city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. When David had made an end of offering, he blessed the people in the name of the Lord. Henry notes David gave the people both a royal treat and, far better, a blessing in the Lord's name, as father and prophet, praying and commending them to grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He dealt to every one of Israel, man and woman, a loaf of bread, a good piece of flesh, and a flagon of wine. Henry notes David's open-handed generosity, enlarged by holy joy, giving all what was worth coming for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. He appointed certain Levites to minister before the ark, to record, thank, and praise the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes David gave God glory by sacrifices and by songs, appointing Levites to record the story and celebrate it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Asaph the chief, and next to him Zechariah, Jeiel, Shemiramoth, Jehiel, Mattithiah, Eliab, Benaiah, Obed-edom, and Jeiel with psalteries and harps, but Asaph made a sound with cymbals. Henry names Asaph as chief musician to whom the thanksgiving psalm was delivered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Benaiah and Jahaziel the priests with trumpets continually before the ark of the covenant of God. Henry includes priestly trumpets in the settled ministry before the ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. On that day David delivered first this psalm to thank the Lord into Asaph's hand. Henry notes this thanksgiving psalm, gathered from several psalms, was now first delivered to Asaph for the church's use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Give thanks unto the Lord, call upon his name, make known his deeds among the people. Henry notes praise must glorify God and edify others: thanksgiving, prayer, song, and discourse, with deeds made known among the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Sing unto him, sing psalms unto him, talk of all his wondrous works. Henry includes singing and discourse among the ways to glorify God by praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Glory ye in his holy name: let the heart of them rejoice that seek the Lord. Henry notes those who give glory to God's name may glory in it, valuing their relation to God and venturing on his promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Seek the Lord and his strength, seek his face continually. Henry explains seeking him by the ark of his strength, in which he manifests himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Remember his marvellous works that he hath done, his wonders, and the judgments of his mouth. Henry urges commemorating God's former mercies to ancestors with thankfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. O ye seed of Israel his servant, ye children of Jacob, his chosen ones. Henry frames the psalm as addressed to the covenant people, the spiritual seed of the patriarchs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He is the Lord our God; his judgments are in all the earth. Henry glorifies God as creator and ruler of the whole creation, and as ours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Be ye mindful always of his covenant, the word he commanded to a thousand generations. Henry calls the everlasting covenant the great matter of joy: God never forgets it, so we never must. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Even of the covenant he made with Abraham, and his oath unto Isaac. Henry notes the covenant was ancient, made with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob long dead, yet still sure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And hath confirmed the same to Jacob for a law, and to Israel for an everlasting covenant. Henry stresses the covenant was commanded, God having authority to promise and ability to perform. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Saying, unto thee will I give the land of Canaan, the lot of your inheritance. Henry notes the promise of Canaan to the patriarchs as the covenant's concrete gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. When ye were but few, even a few, and strangers in it. Henry recalls the patriarchs as strangers and sojourners in Canaan, few and easily swallowed up, yet protected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And when they went from nation to nation, and from one kingdom to another people. Henry notes they were continually on the remove and exposed, yet no man was suffered to do them wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. He suffered no man to do them wrong: yea, he reproved kings for their sakes. Henry cites Pharaoh and Abimelech reproved and plagued for the patriarchs' sakes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Saying, touch not mine anointed, and do my prophets no harm. Henry explains the patriarchs were God's anointed, sanctified by grace and glory, and his prophets, instructed and commissioning others; to touch them was to touch the apple of God's eye. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Sing unto the Lord, all the earth; shew forth from day to day his salvation. Henry calls the great salvation, the promised salvation by Christ, a subject never exhausted, to be celebrated daily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Declare his glory among the heathen, his marvellous works among all nations. Henry urges declaring God's glory among the heathen, that strangers may be led to acquaintance, allegiance, and adoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. For great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised: he also is to be feared above all gods. Henry glorifies God as great, supreme, and sole God, greatly to be praised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. For all the gods of the people are idols: but the Lord made the heavens. Henry contrasts idols with the creator, who made the heavens. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Glory and honour are in his presence; strength and gladness are in his place. Henry describes God as most bright and blessed in himself, with glory and honour in his presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Give unto the Lord, ye kindreds of the people, give unto the Lord glory and strength. Henry calls for giving the Lord the glory due to his name, and owning even more his due. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Give unto the Lord the glory due unto his name: bring an offering, and come before him: worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness. Henry notes holiness is the beauty of the Lord and of all sanctified performances, and offerings now are the fruit of lips and heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Fear before him, all the earth: the world also shall be stable, that it be not moved. Henry urges reverence for God's universal monarchy, which establishes the world so it cannot be removed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth rejoice: and let men say among the nations, the Lord reigneth. Henry joins rejoicing to reverence: because the Lord reigns, heavens and earth may be glad. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Let the sea roar, and the fulness thereof: let the fields rejoice, and all that is therein. Henry extends rejoicing to sea, fields, and woods, even looking to the day of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Then shall the trees of the wood sing out at the presence of the Lord, because he cometh to judge the earth. Henry notes the prospect of judgment inspires awful pleasure: creation rejoices that he comes to judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. O give thanks unto the Lord; for he is good; for his mercy endureth for ever. Henry makes this the burden of the Gibeon singers' songs, and the psalm's repeated refrain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And say ye, save us, O God of our salvation, and gather us together, and deliver us from the heathen, that we may give thanks to thy holy name, and glory in thy praise. Henry insists that amid praise we must pray for afflicted brethren, scattered and oppressed, as our own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Blessed be the Lord God of Israel for ever and ever. And all the people said, Amen, and praised the Lord. Henry notes David begins with give thanks and concludes with blessed be the Lord, and the people signified consent with Amen, praising the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. So he left there before the ark Asaph and his brethren, to minister before the ark continually, as every day's work required. Henry stresses worship is not only for solemn days but every day, and Asaph's ministry at Jerusalem was continual song, without sacrifices, since the altars were at Gibeon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. And Obed-edom with their brethren, threescore and eight; Obed-edom also the son of Jeduthun and Hosah to be porters. Henry notes the porters and attendants settled in their posts as part of the daily method. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. And Zadok the priest, and his brethren the priests, before the tabernacle of the Lord in the high place at Gibeon. Henry explains the separation since Eli's time: ark at Jerusalem, altar at Gibeon, and David took care neither should be neglected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. To offer burnt offerings unto the Lord upon the altar continually morning and evening, according to all written in the law of Moses. Henry notes ceremonial worship, though inferior to prayer and praise, was kept up as a type of Christ's mediation, morning and evening by law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. And with them Heman and Jeduthun, and the rest chosen, who were expressed by name, to give thanks to the Lord, because his mercy endureth for ever. Henry notes Gibeon's singers also sang, with the burden that his mercy endures forever, on instruments appointed for holy service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. And with them Heman and Jeduthun with trumpets and cymbals for those that should make a sound, and with musical instruments of God. And the sons of Jeduthun were porters. Henry distinguishes common mirth from holy joy, with limits carefully kept in instruments and service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. And all the people departed every man to his house: and David returned to bless his house. Henry closes with satisfaction: the people went home pleased, and David returned to keep up family worship, which public worship must not supersede. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
