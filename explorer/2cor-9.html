The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Corinthians 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 For as touching the ministering to the saints, it is superfluous for me to write to you:

2 For I know the forwardness of your mind, for which I boast of you to them of Macedonia, that Achaia was ready a year ago; and your zeal hath provoked very many.

3 Yet have I sent the brethren, lest our boasting of you should be in vain in this behalf; that, as I said, ye may be ready:

4 Lest haply if they of Macedonia come with me, and find you unprepared, we (that we say not, ye) should be ashamed in this same confident boasting.

5 Therefore I thought it necessary to exhort the brethren, that they would go before unto you, and make up beforehand your bounty, whereof ye had notice before, that the same might be ready, as a matter of bounty, and not as of covetousness.

6 But this I say, He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully.

7 Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.

8 And God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work:

9 (As it is written, He hath dispersed abroad; he hath given to the poor: his righteousness remaineth for ever.

10 Now he that ministereth seed to the sower both minister bread for your food, and multiply your seed sown, and increase the fruits of your righteousness;)

11 Being enriched in every thing to all bountifulness, which causeth through us thanksgiving to God.

12 For the administration of this service not only supplieth the want of the saints, but is abundant also by many thanksgivings unto God;

13 Whiles by the experiment of this ministration they glorify God for your professed subjection unto the gospel of Christ, and for your liberal distribution unto them, and unto all men;

14 And by their prayer for you, which long after you for the exceeding grace of God in you.

15 Thanks be unto God for his unspeakable gift.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul says writing about the collection is almost superfluous, since Corinth's readiness has stirred Macedonia, yet he sends the brothers ahead so the gift will be ready as a blessing, not as covetousness. God loves a cheerful giver, is able to make all grace abound, and multiplies seed for sowing, as the psalm says of the liberal man whose righteousness remains for ever, and Paul closes with thanks for God's unspeakable gift. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. For as touching the ministering to the saints, it is superfluous for me to write to you. Henry notes Paul begins as if the subject needed no writing, because Corinth's readiness is already known, and the writing that follows is to secure the doing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. For I know the forwardness of your mind, for which I boast of you to them of Macedonia, that Achaia was ready a year ago; and your zeal has provoked very many. JFB notes the boasting is reciprocal: Paul boasted of Corinth to Macedonia, and Macedonia's zeal now provokes Corinth in return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Yet have I sent the brethren, lest our boasting of you should be in vain in this behalf; that, as I said, ye may be ready. Henry notes the brethren are sent to make the boast true: readiness a year ago must still be readiness when the collectors arrive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Lest haply if they of Macedonia come with me, and find you unprepared, we (that we say not, ye) should be ashamed in this same confident boasting. JFB notes the shame would fall on Paul first, and then on Corinth: a boast unfulfilled shames both boaster and boasted-of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Therefore I thought it necessary to exhort the brethren, that they would go before unto you, and make up beforehand your bounty, whereof ye had notice before, that the same might be ready, as a matter of bounty, and not as of covetousness. Henry notes the bounty must be ready beforehand, so that it looks like bounty, not like money wrung out by pressure at the last moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But this I say, He which sows sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which sows bountifully shall reap also bountifully. JFB notes the harvest rule: sowing and reaping keep proportion, sparing with sparing, bountiful with bountiful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Every man according as he purposes in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loves a cheerful giver. Henry notes the giving rule: purposed in the heart, not grudging, not of necessity, because God loves the cheerful giver, and cheerfulness is part of the gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. And God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work. JFB notes the ability is God's: all grace abounding produces all sufficiency in all things, and sufficiency is for abounding in every good work, not for hoarding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. (As it is written, He has dispersed abroad; he has given to the poor: his righteousness remains for ever.) Henry notes the psalm's liberal man disperses and gives, and his righteousness remains: giving does not diminish righteousness, it proves its permanence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Now he that ministers seed to the sower both minister bread for your food, and multiply your seed sown, and increase the fruits of your righteousness. JFB notes God supplies both seed and bread: he gives what is sown and what is eaten, and multiplies the seed while increasing righteousness's fruits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Being enriched in every thing to all bountifulness, which causes through us thanksgiving to God. Henry notes enrichment aims at bountifulness, and bountifulness aims at thanksgiving: the gift's end is praise to God through the givers' hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. For the administration of this service not only supplies the want of the saints, but is abundant also by many thanksgivings unto God. JFB notes the service has two abundances: it fills the saints' want, and it overflows in many thanksgivings to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Whiles by the experiment of this ministration they glorify God for your professed subjection unto the gospel of Christ, and for your liberal distribution unto them, and unto all men. Henry notes the Jerusalem saints read the gift as proof: Corinth's subjection to the gospel is proved by liberal distribution, to them and to all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. And by their prayer for you, which long after you for the exceeding grace of God in you. JFB notes the gift returns as prayer: the saints pray for Corinth and long after them, because they see God's exceeding grace in the givers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Thanks be unto God for his unspeakable gift. Henry notes the chapter ends beyond words: after all the reasoning about giving, Paul thanks God for the gift that cannot be spoken, Christ himself, the pattern and giver of all giving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • cheerful giver. Paul's principle for giving: not grudgingly or of necessity, for God loves a cheerful giver. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • sow sparingly. Paul's harvest image: whoever sows sparingly reaps sparingly, and whoever sows bountifully reaps bountifully. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • unspeakable gift. Paul's closing thanks for God's gift beyond words, the gift of Christ himself. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
