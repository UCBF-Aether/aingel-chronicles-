The Text — Judith 3 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 So they sent ambassadors unto him to treat of peace, saying,

2 Behold, we the servants of Nabuchodonosor the great king lie before thee; use us as shall be good in thy sight.

3 Behold, our houses, and all our places, and all our fields of wheat, and flocks, and herds, and all the lodges of our tents lie before thy face; use them as it pleaseth thee.

4 Behold, even our cities and the inhabitants thereof are thy servants; come and deal with them as seemeth good unto thee.

5 So the men came to Holofernes, and declared unto him after this manner.

6 Then came he down toward the sea coast, both he and his army, and set garrisons in the high cities, and took out of them chosen men for aid.

7 So they and all the country round about received them with garlands, with dances, and with timbrels.

8 Yet he did cast down their frontiers, and cut down their groves: for he had decreed to destroy all the gods of the land, that all nations should worship Nabuchodonosor only, and that all tongues and tribes should call upon him as god.

9 Also he came over against Esdraelon near unto Judea, over against the great strait of Judea.

10 And he pitched between Geba and Scythopolis, and there he tarried a whole month, that he might gather together all the carriages of his army.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

The peoples of the sea coast surrender before Holofernes can attack: they send ambassadors to treat of peace, offering themselves, their cities, and all they have as his servants. He receives them with their garlands, dances, and timbrels, yet breaks down their gods and high places, that all nations should worship Nabuchodonosor only, then pitches between Geba and Scythopolis and tarries a whole month gathering the carriages of his army. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Scythopolis. Bethsan, where Holofernes camps for a month gathering his army's baggage (verse 10). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Carriages. the baggage and burden of the army, gathered during the month's halt (verse 10). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Greek reads that they "sent to him ambassadors, with proposals for peace, saying: Behold we, the servants of Nabuchodonosor, the great king, lay before thee; use us as thou thinkest best"; these proposals were made by those on the sea-coast, from Sidon and Tyre to Ascalon, from whom Holofernes took the choicest men, being received with honour. Sobal, or Soba, lies near Damascus, and "Lybia" here is probably Lycia or Lydia, which Holofernes had cut in pieces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. "Great" was Nabuchodonosor's usual title (Daniel 2:30; 2 Kings 18:19), and the submission offered is unconditional slavery — yet they judge it better to submit to servitude than to be slaughtered like their brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. "At thy disposal" (Genesis 13:9; Proverbs 15:11): houses, places, fields of wheat, flocks, herds, and tents are all laid before Holofernes' face, to use as seems good to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. The reception with garlands, dances, and timbrels is testified by lights: torches were used to testify joy (2 Maccabees 4:22). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. Yet he cast down their frontiers and cut down their groves, for he had decreed to destroy all the gods of the land; the Greek reads, "He demolished all their borders, and cut down their groves," and, "Worshipped by all nations, and that all their tongues and tribes might call him god" — behold to what lengths ambition may be carried, and he is a figure of Antichrist, for whom all heretics pave the way (2 Thessalonians 2). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. He came over against Esdraelon near Judea, over against the great strait of Judea; the Greek reads, "He came to Esdrelon, near Dotaia (or Dothaim, north of Sichem), which is opposite the great saw of Judea," the range of Ephraim's mountains, and the kings of Israel had fortified the defiles (1 Kings 15:17). The order of conquests is not strictly observed, for while the commander-in-chief was in one place his generals were dispersed elsewhere, and he had now advanced as far as Gabaa of Saul when, meditating an attack upon Egypt, he concentrated his forces, little expecting such a check at Bethulia. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. He pitched between Geba and Scythopolis and tarried a whole month to gather together all the carriages of his army; the Greek reads, "encamped between Gaba and Scythopolis (Bethsan, Joshua 17:11), and he was there a month of days, to collect all the burden of his army." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
