The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 4 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Hear me when I call, O God of my righteousness: thou hast enlarged me when I was in distress; have mercy upon me, and hear my prayer.

2 O ye sons of men, how long will ye turn my glory into shame? how long will ye love vanity, and seek after leasing? Selah.

3 But know that the LORD hath set apart him that is godly for himself: the LORD will hear when I call unto him.

4 Stand in awe, and sin not: commune with your own heart upon your bed, and be still. Selah.

5 Offer the sacrifices of righteousness, and put your trust in the LORD.

6 There be many that say, Who will shew us any good? LORD, lift thou up the light of thy countenance upon us.

7 Thou hast put gladness in my heart, more than in the time that their corn and their wine increased.

8 I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

An evening prayer: appealing to past deliverance, the speaker answers slanderers who love vanity, calls for awe and quiet trust instead of sin, and closes in peace, with gladness greater than harvest joy, sleeping safely because God alone keeps him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David appeals to the God of his righteousness, who enlarged him in past distress; former deliverance is his plea for present mercy and a hearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He turns on the sons of men who turn his glory into shame, loving vanity and seeking falsehood — preferring empty honours and lies to truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The godly are set apart by God for himself, and therefore God will hear when they call; separation to God carries the promise of audience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Stand in awe and sin not: Henry urges trembling reverence, and quiet self-examination on one's bed, rather than answering slander with sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Offer right sacrifices and trust the Lord: outward worship is worthless without the inward trust it should express. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. While many ask who will show them any good, David asks for the light of God's countenance — his favour is the true good, beyond all earthly plenty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God puts gladness in his heart greater than harvest joy: inward, God-given gladness outlasts the increase of corn and wine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. In peace he lies down and sleeps, for the Lord alone makes him dwell in safety; safety is not in walls or armies but in God's keeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
