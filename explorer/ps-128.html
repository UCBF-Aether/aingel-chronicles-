The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 128 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Blessed is every one that feareth the LORD; that walketh in his ways.

2 For thou shalt eat the labour of thine hands: happy shalt thou be, and it shall be well with thee.

3 Thy wife shall be as a fruitful vine by the sides of thine house: thy children like olive plants round about thy table.

4 Behold, that thus shall the man be blessed that feareth the LORD.

5 The LORD shall bless thee out of Zion: and thou shalt see the good of Jerusalem all the days of thy life.

6 Yea, thou shalt see thy children’s children, and peace upon Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A blessing on the God-fearing household: blessed is everyone who fears the LORD and walks in his ways, for he shall eat the labour of his hands, be happy, and it shall be well with him. His wife shall be as a fruitful vine by the sides of his house, his children like olive plants round about his table — "behold, that thus shall the man be blessed that feareth the LORD." The blessing widens outward: the LORD shall bless him out of Zion, he shall see the good of Jerusalem all his life, see his children's children, and peace upon Israel. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry's second family-psalm: former taught prosperity depends on God's blessing, this teaches only way to obtain blessing making families comfortable is fear of God and obedience — godliness has promise of life now and to come; truly holy truly happy, fear deep reverence evidenced by regular constant conformity, in vain pretend fear if not keeping to ways nor trifling nor drawing back; blessed to all saints universally, every nation, high or low rich or poor, religion ruling protects and enriches, and to particular person in private interests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry applies promise by name: happy shalt thou be — it shall be well with thee, whatever befalls good brought out of it, well while livest, better when diest, best to eternity; honest livelihood not ease without care: something to do (idle life miserable uncomfortable), health strength capacity to do it, not beholden to others as disabled poor on others' labours, with quietness work and eat own bread (2 Thess 3:12); succeed in employments, they and theirs enjoy what they get, none eat bread out of mouths nor oppressive rulers nor invading enemies take it, God will not blast and blow (Hag 1:9), blessing makes little go great way, labouring man's food sweet as sleep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry delights in family-relations as much as care when grace makes them as they should be: wife as vine by sides of house — not only spreading ornament but fruitful vine for profit, fruit honouring God and man (Judg 9:13), weak tender plant needing support and cherishing yet valuable, place husband's house her castle (Sarah in tent), by side not under-foot to trample nor house-top to domineer, rib from side, loving cleaving, obedient pliable growing as directed, fruitful not only children but wisdom righteousness good management branches over wall (Gen 49:22; Ps 80:11), good fruit not sour grapes or Sodom; children olive plants likely olive-trees, wild by nature yet grafted into good olive partaking root and fatness (Rom 11:17), round table in health craving food not physic, straight green sucking sap of good education, likely in due time serviceable, pleasant table-talk, Job 29:5 prosperity children about him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry asserts with note commanding attention: Behold, thus shall the man be blessed — behold by faith in promise, by observation in performance, with assurance God faithful, with admiration we merit no favour no blessing; general truth repeated: fearer of Lord thus blessed, God pronouncing blessed makes them so, blessed now, still, for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry blesses out of Zion where ark was and pious Israelites attended devotions: blessings out of Zion best, flowing not from common providence but special grace (Ps 20:2); thou shalt see good of Jerusalem all days of life, blessed in Zion's blessing thinking thyself so, private comforts not allayed and embittered by public troubles though living long. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry longs to live to see families built up: see thy children's children as Joseph (Gen 50:23), family built and continued, pleasure of seeing it, good children's children crown of old men (Prov 17:6) fond of grandchildren; and peace upon Israel — little comfort seeing children's children unless withal see peace upon Israel and hopes of transmitting entail of religion pure and entire to those after, best inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

Connections to other passages
