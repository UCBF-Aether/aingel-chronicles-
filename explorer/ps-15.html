The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 15 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Lord, who shall abide in thy tabernacle? who shall dwell in thy holy hill?

2 He that walketh uprightly, and worketh righteousness, and speaketh the truth in his heart.

3 He that backbiteth not with his tongue, nor doeth evil to his neighbour, nor taketh up a reproach against his neighbour.

4 In whose eyes a vile person is contemned; but he honoureth them that fear the LORD. He that sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not.

5 He that putteth not out his money to usury, nor taketh reward against the innocent. He that doeth these things shall never be moved.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Who may dwell as God's guest? The portrait is whole-life integrity: an upright walk, a truthful heart, a tongue that does not backbite, honour for God-fearers over profit, and honest money dealings. Such a person shall never be moved. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Lord, who shall abide in your tabernacle, who shall dwell in your holy hill? The question is who may live in God's presence, as a guest in his house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He who walks uprightly, works righteousness, and speaks truth in his heart: character, conduct, and inward sincerity are joined, not separated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He does not backbite with his tongue, do evil to his neighbour, or take up a reproach against his neighbour: the tongue is the first test of a fit guest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. In his eyes a vile person is contemned, but he honours those who fear the Lord; he swears to his own hurt and changes not: he judges by godliness, not by profit, and keeps his oath even at cost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He does not put out money to usury nor take reward against the innocent; he who does these things shall never be moved: justice in money matters and steadfastness complete the portrait, and such a man stands firm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
