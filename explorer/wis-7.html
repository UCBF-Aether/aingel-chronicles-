The Text — Wisdom of Solomon 7 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 I myself also am a mortal man, like to all, and the offspring of him that was first made of the earth,

2 And in my mother's womb was fashioned to be flesh in the time of ten months, being compacted in blood, of the seed of man, and the pleasure that came with sleep.

3 And when I was born, I drew in the common air, and fell upon the earth, which is of like nature, and the first voice which I uttered was crying, as all others do.

4 I was nursed in swaddling clothes, and that with cares.

5 For there is no king that had any other beginning of birth.

6 For all men have one entrance into life, and the like going out.

7 Wherefore I prayed, and understanding was given me: I called upon God, and the spirit of wisdom came to me.

8 I preferred her before sceptres and thrones, and esteemed riches nothing in comparison of her.

9 Neither compared I unto her any precious stone, because all gold in respect of her is as a little sand, and silver shall be counted as clay before her.

10 I loved her above health and beauty, and chose to have her instead of light: for the light that cometh from her never goeth out.

11 All good things together came to me with her, and innumerable riches in her hands.

12 And I rejoiced in them all, because wisdom goeth before them: and I knew not that she was the mother of them.

13 I learned diligently, and do communicate her liberally: I do not hide her riches.

14 For she is a treasure unto men that never faileth: which they that use become the friends of God, being commended for the gifts that come from learning.

15 God hath granted me to speak as I would, and to conceive as is meet for the things that are given me: because it is he that leadeth unto wisdom, and directeth the wise.

16 For in his hand are both we and our words; all wisdom also, and knowledge of workmanship.

17 For he hath given me certain knowledge of the things that are, namely, to know how the world was made, and the operation of the elements:

18 The beginning, ending, and midst of the times: the alterations of the turning of the sun, and the change of seasons:

19 The circuits of years, and the positions of stars:

20 The natures of living creatures, and the furies of wild beasts: the violence of winds, and the reasonings of men: the diversities of plants and the virtues of roots:

21 And all such things as are either secret or manifest, them I know.

22 For wisdom, which is the worker of all things, taught me: for in her is an understanding spirit holy, one only, manifold, subtil, lively, clear, undefiled, plain, not subject to hurt, loving the thing that is good quick, which cannot be letted, ready to do good,

23 Kind to man, steadfast, sure, free from care, having all power, overseeing all things, and going through all understanding, pure, and most subtil, spirits.

24 For wisdom is more moving than any motion: she passeth and goeth through all things by reason of her pureness.

25 For she is the breath of the power of God, and a pure influence flowing from the glory of the Almighty: therefore can no defiled thing fall into her.

26 For she is the brightness of the everlasting light, the unspotted mirror of the power of God, and the image of his goodness.

27 And being but one, she can do all things: and remaining in herself, she maketh all things new: and in all ages entering into holy souls, she maketh them friends of God, and prophets.

28 For God loveth none but him that dwelleth with wisdom.

29 For she is more beautiful than the sun, and above all the order of stars: being compared with the light, she is found before it.

30 For after this cometh night: but vice shall not prevail against wisdom.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The speaker presents himself as a mortal like all others, born and nursed as any king is, who prayed and received understanding. He preferred wisdom before sceptres, thrones, riches and precious stones, and found all good things came with her. Wisdom, worker of all things, is then described as the breath of God's power, the brightness of everlasting light, an unspotted mirror, more beautiful than the sun, whom vice cannot prevail against. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. I myself am also a mortal man, like all others, and the offspring of him first made of the earth. Haydock notes Solomon, whose sayings are recorded here, acquired wisdom, and all others may do the same, for he starts from common mortality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock

  • Verse 3. When I was born, I drew in the common air, fell upon the earth of like nature, and my first voice was crying, as all others do. Haydock's point is that birth levels king and commoner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. I was nursed in swaddling clothes and with cares. Haydock notes the same nursing and cares attend every infant, royal or not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. No king had any other beginning of birth. Haydock stresses the universality: kings begin exactly as others do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. There is one entrance to life for all, and a like going out. Haydock quotes the dying Augustus asking whether he had acted his part well, to show life is a stage before and after which all are equal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. Wherefore I prayed, and understanding was given me; I called upon God, and wisdom's spirit came to me. Haydock notes that, having no natural advantage, Solomon prayed for the wisdom his office required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. I preferred her before sceptres and thrones, and esteemed riches nothing in comparison of her. Haydock observes he did not ask for dominions, and that some things said of wisdom refer to uncreated wisdom, others to created. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. Neither compared I to her any precious stone, because all gold in respect of her is as a little sand, and silver shall be counted as clay before her. Haydock's comparison makes wealth mere sand and clay beside wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. I loved her above health and beauty, and chose to have her instead of light, for the light that comes from her never goes out. Haydock notes wisdom's light, unlike the sun's, never sets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. All good things together came to me with her, and innumerable riches in her hands. Haydock observes that goods follow wisdom, rather than wisdom following goods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. I rejoiced in them all, because wisdom goes before them, and I knew not that she was their mother. Haydock's point is that wisdom is the mother of the goods she brings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. I learned diligently, and communicate her liberally; I do not hide her riches. Haydock notes such disinterestedness deserves imitation by all the learned, who should give what they received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. For she is a treasure to men that never fails, and those who use it become God's friends, commended by the gifts that come from learning. Haydock observes the wise do not seek glory, but it follows them, and use of the treasure makes friends of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. God has granted me to speak as I would, and to conceive what is worthy of his gifts, because he himself guides wisdom and directs the wise. Haydock notes speech must be sincere and sententious, and presumption is far from true wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. In God's hand are both we and our words, all wisdom also and knowledge of workmanship. Haydock's point is that speaker, speech, and skill are all in God's hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. He has given me certain knowledge of the things that are: to know the constitution of the world and the operation of the elements. Haydock notes this knowledge covers things past, present, and to come in their seasons and vicissitudes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. The beginning, ending, and midst of the times, the alterations of the turning of the sun and change of seasons. Haydock explains the times as the seasons and their changes of heat and cold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. The circuits of years and positions of stars. Haydock observes that astronomy itself is part of wisdom's gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. The natures of living creatures and the furies of wild beasts, the violence of winds and reasonings of men. Haydock notes that knowledge of creatures and of human reasonings alike come from wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. The diversities of plants and the virtues of roots. Haydock's point is that even plants and roots are within wisdom's teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. All things that are secret or manifest I have learned, for wisdom, the worker of all things, taught me. Haydock notes the epithets of wisdom's spirit are proper to the Holy Ghost, and also applicable to wisdom dwelling in man: one in essence, manifold in operations, subtle, knowing all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 23. In her is an understanding spirit, holy, one only, manifold, subtil, lively, clear, undefiled, plain, not subject to hurt, loving the thing that is good, quick, which cannot be letted, ready to do good. Haydock's list describes a spirit active, eloquent to persuade, and open. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 24. For wisdom is more moving than any motion; she passes and goes through all things by reason of her pureness. Haydock notes wisdom is the same as the Spirit and the Son of God, passing through all things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 25. She is the breath of God's power, a pure influence flowing from the Almighty's glory, therefore no defiled thing can fall into her. Haydock stresses wisdom's origin: breath and pure influence from God's glory itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 26. She is the brightness of everlasting light, the unspotted mirror of God's power, and the image of his goodness. Haydock's images are brightness, mirror, and image: wisdom shows God without spot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 27. Being but one, she can do all things, and remaining in herself, she makes all things new, and in all ages entering into holy souls, she makes them friends of God and prophets. Haydock notes one wisdom makes all new and makes prophets of holy souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 28. God loves none but him who dwells with wisdom. Haydock's point is exclusive: God's love rests on wisdom's companions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 29. She is more beautiful than the sun, and above all the order of stars; compared with light, she is found before it. Haydock observes wisdom outshines sun and stars, and precedes light itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 30. Night succeeds light, but against wisdom no evil can prevail. Haydock's close is that night follows day, but no evil overcomes wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
