The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 24 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Command the children of Israel, that they bring unto thee pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamps to burn continually.

3 Without the vail of the testimony, in the tabernacle of the congregation, shall Aaron order it from the evening unto the morning before the LORD continually: it shall be a statute for ever in your generations.

4 He shall order the lamps upon the pure candlestick before the LORD continually.

5 And thou shalt take fine flour, and bake twelve cakes thereof: two tenth deals shall be in one cake.

6 And thou shalt set them in two rows, six on a row, upon the pure table before the LORD.

7 And thou shalt put pure frankincense upon each row, that it may be on the bread for a memorial, even an offering made by fire unto the LORD.

8 Every sabbath he shall set it in order before the LORD continually, being taken from the children of Israel by an everlasting covenant.

9 And it shall be Aaron’s and his sons’; and they shall eat it in the holy place: for it is most holy unto him of the offerings of the LORD made by fire by a perpetual statute.

10 And the son of an Israelitish woman, whose father was an Egyptian, went out among the children of Israel: and this son of the Israelitish woman and a man of Israel strove together in the camp;

11 And the Israelitish woman’s son blasphemed the name of the Lord, and cursed. And they brought him unto Moses: (and his mother’s name was Shelomith, the daughter of Dibri, of the tribe of Dan:)

12 And they put him in ward, that the mind of the LORD might be shewed them.

13 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

14 Bring forth him that hath cursed without the camp; and let all that heard him lay their hands upon his head, and let all the congregation stone him.

15 And thou shalt speak unto the children of Israel, saying, Whosoever curseth his God shall bear his sin.

16 And he that blasphemeth the name of the LORD, he shall surely be put to death, and all the congregation shall certainly stone him: as well the stranger, as he that is born in the land, when he blasphemeth the name of the Lord, shall be put to death.

17 And he that killeth any man shall surely be put to death.

18 And he that killeth a beast shall make it good; beast for beast.

19 And if a man cause a blemish in his neighbour; as he hath done, so shall it be done to him;

20 Breach for breach, eye for eye, tooth for tooth: as he hath caused a blemish in a man, so shall it be done to him again.

21 And he that killeth a beast, he shall restore it: and he that killeth a man, he shall be put to death.

22 Ye shall have one manner of law, as well for the stranger, as for one of your own country: for I am the LORD your God.

23 And Moses spake to the children of Israel, that they should bring forth him that had cursed out of the camp, and stone him with stones. And the children of Israel did as the LORD commanded Moses.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gathers three matters: the sanctuary service, a blasphemy case, and the laws of injury. Israel brings pure beaten olive oil so the lamps burn continually before the veil, and twelve cakes of showbread are set in two rows every sabbath for Aaron and his sons. Then the son of an Israelite woman blasphemes the LORD's name and is stoned outside the camp, and the chapter states the general rules that follow from the case: life for life, fracture for fracture, and one manner of law for stranger and native alike. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. Israel shall bring pure olive oil beaten, cold-drawn and of great purity, for the light, to cause lamps to burn continually, repeating Exodus 27. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Without the veil, in the tabernacle, Aaron shall order the lamps from evening to morning continually, a perpetual statute, the priests' daily presence being necessary to clean and trim them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He shall order the lamps upon the pure candlestick, so called because of pure gold, continually, symbolical of the light ministers are to diffuse through the Church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Fine flour shall be taken and twelve cakes baked, two tenth deals, about thirteen and a half pounds, in each, for the showbread previously appointed in Exodus 25, baked by Levites with flour furnished by the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They shall be set in two rows, six on a row, upon the pure table before the LORD, the twelve cakes corresponding to the tribes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Pure frankincense shall be put upon each row, that it may be on the bread for a memorial, an offering made by fire, the frankincense being burnt so the showbread is called an offering by fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Every Sabbath he shall set it in order before the LORD continually, from Israel by an everlasting covenant, hot loaves replacing stale ones each week. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. It shall be Aaron's and his sons', eaten in a holy place, for it is most holy from offerings made by fire by a perpetual statute, stale loaves being eaten only by priests except in necessity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The son of an Israelite woman whose father was Egyptian goes out among Israel and strives with an Israelite in the camp, a mixed-multitude marriage connection not infrequent after the exodus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The Israelite woman's son blasphemes the LORD's name and curses, and is brought to Moses, his mother being Shelomith daughter of Dibri of Dan, the youth venting rage in Egyptian fashion, cursing the object of his opponent's reverence, the King and God of the Hebrews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He who cursed shall be brought forth without the camp, all who heard him laying hands on his head, and all the congregation stoning him, executions taking place outside the camp since flagrant offenders must be thrust out of a holy people, and hand-laying testifying publicly and devolving guilt on the criminal alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He who blasphemes the LORD's name shall surely be put to death, all the congregation stoning him, stranger as well as native, since strangers joining the camp, though not circumcised, become amenable to the law, especially blasphemy, from which Jews later derived the practice of not uttering Yahweh's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. He who kills any man shall surely be put to death, these verses repeating social laws whose penalties are inflicted not by private parties but through judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He who kills a beast shall make it good, beast for beast, the same judicial repetition governing property injury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. If a man causes a blemish in his neighbour, as he has done so shall it be done to him, the lex talionis being administered by judges, not private vengeance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Breach for breach, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, as he has caused a blemish in a man, so shall it be done to him, the measure of judicial penalty matching the injury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. He who kills a beast shall restore it, but he who kills a man shall be put to death, distinguishing property restoration from capital homicide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. There shall be one manner of law for stranger and native, for the LORD is their God, the same judicial law applying to the mixed multitude as to Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Moses speaks to Israel, and they bring forth him who cursed out of the camp and stone him, doing as the LORD commanded Moses, stoning thereafter becoming the established punishment for blasphemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
