The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 46 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Israel took his journey with all that he had, and came to Beersheba, and offered sacrifices unto the God of his father Isaac.

2 And God spake unto Israel in the visions of the night, and said, Jacob, Jacob. And he said, Here am I.

3 And he said, I am God, the God of thy father: fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will there make of thee a great nation:

4 I will go down with thee into Egypt; and I will also surely bring thee up again: and Joseph shall put his hand upon thine eyes.

5 And Jacob rose up from Beersheba: and the sons of Israel carried Jacob their father, and their little ones, and their wives, in the wagons which Pharaoh had sent to carry him.

6 And they took their cattle, and their goods, which they had gotten in the land of Canaan, and came into Egypt, Jacob, and all his seed with him:

7 His sons, and his sons’ sons with him, his daughters, and his sons’ daughters, and all his seed brought he with him into Egypt.

8 And these are the names of the children of Israel, which came into Egypt, Jacob and his sons: Reuben, Jacob’s firstborn.

9 And the sons of Reuben; Hanoch, and Phallu, and Hezron, and Carmi.

10 And the sons of Simeon; Jemuel, and Jamin, and Ohad, and Jachin, and Zohar, and Shaul the son of a Canaanitish woman.

11 And the sons of Levi; Gershon, Kohath, and Merari.

12 And the sons of Judah; Er, and Onan, and Shelah, and Pharez, and Zarah: but Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan. And the sons of Pharez were Hezron and Hamul.

13 And the sons of Issachar; Tola, and Phuvah, and Job, and Shimron.

14 And the sons of Zebulun; Sered, and Elon, and Jahleel.

15 These be the sons of Leah, which she bare unto Jacob in Padanaram, with his daughter Dinah: all the souls of his sons and his daughters were thirty and three.

16 And the sons of Gad; Ziphion, and Haggi, Shuni, and Ezbon, Eri, and Arodi, and Areli.

17 And the sons of Asher; Jimnah, and Ishuah, and Isui, and Beriah, and Serah their sister: and the sons of Beriah; Heber, and Malchiel.

18 These are the sons of Zilpah, whom Laban gave to Leah his daughter, and these she bare unto Jacob, even sixteen souls.

19 The sons of Rachel Jacob’s wife; Joseph, and Benjamin.

20 And unto Joseph in the land of Egypt were born Manasseh and Ephraim, which Asenath the daughter of Potipherah priest of On bare unto him.

21 And the sons of Benjamin were Belah, and Becher, and Ashbel, Gera, and Naaman, Ehi, and Rosh, Muppim, and Huppim, and Ard.

22 These are the sons of Rachel, which were born to Jacob: all the souls were fourteen.

23 And the sons of Dan; Hushim.

24 And the sons of Naphtali; Jahzeel, and Guni, and Jezer, and Shillem.

25 These are the sons of Bilhah, which Laban gave unto Rachel his daughter, and she bare these unto Jacob: all the souls were seven.

26 All the souls that came with Jacob into Egypt, which came out of his loins, besides Jacob’s sons’ wives, all the souls were threescore and six;

27 And the sons of Joseph, which were born him in Egypt, were two souls: all the souls of the house of Jacob, which came into Egypt, were threescore and ten.

28 And he sent Judah before him unto Joseph, to direct his face unto Goshen; and they came into the land of Goshen.

29 And Joseph made ready his chariot, and went up to meet Israel his father, to Goshen, and presented himself unto him; and he fell on his neck, and wept on his neck a good while.

30 And Israel said unto Joseph, Now let me die, since I have seen thy face, because thou art yet alive.

31 And Joseph said unto his brethren, and unto his father’s house, I will go up, and shew Pharaoh, and say unto him, My brethren, and my father’s house, which were in the land of Canaan, are come unto me;

32 And the men are shepherds, for their trade hath been to feed cattle; and they have brought their flocks, and their herds, and all that they have.

33 And it shall come to pass, when Pharaoh shall call you, and shall say, What is your occupation?

34 That ye shall say, Thy servants’ trade hath been about cattle from our youth even until now, both we, and also our fathers: that ye may dwell in the land of Goshen; for every shepherd is an abomination unto the Egyptians.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Israel journeys to Egypt with all he has, stops at Beersheba to sacrifice, and hears God tell him not to fear going down to Egypt. The chapter lists the household that entered Egypt, and Joseph meets his father in Goshen, where the family is settled as herdsmen. Verses 11, 14, 18, 19, 22, 24 and 25 are name lists carried in the Text panel, on which the study notes are silent. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Israel sets out with all he has and comes to Beer-sheba, the covenant spot of Abraham and Isaac on the direct route to Egypt, where he pauses to seek God's guidance before leaving Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. God speaks to Israel in visions of the night, a virtual renewal of the covenant that answers Jacob's prayer and removes his doubts about going down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God tells Jacob not to fear going to Egypt, for there he will be made a great nation, a promise fulfilled when seventy souls grew into an immense multitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. God promises to go down with Jacob and surely bring him up again, a promise realised for Jacob himself in his burial in Canaan and for his posterity in their return, while Joseph will close his eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Jacob rises from Beer-sheba and his sons carry him, with wives and little ones, in Pharaoh's wagons, the aged patriarch conveyed by slow stages while his sons manage the journey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They take their cattle and their goods, meaning their substance and precious things, not mere furniture, and come into Egypt, Jacob and all his seed with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Sons, grandsons, daughters, and granddaughters are all brought with him, the daughters-in-law likely included in the general account of the emigrants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The list that follows is introduced as the names of Israel's children who came into Egypt, and JFB explains it as heads of families at the time of immigration, not only those born in Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Reuben's sons are listed, and JFB notes Reuben seems to have had only two sons before the second grain journey, so the later sons must have been born in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Simeon's sons are listed, including Shaul, son of a Canaanite woman, and JFB notes variant names for Jemuel and Zohar and that Ohad's line later disappears from the tribal lists. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Judah's sons are listed, with Er and Onan noted as dead in Canaan and Pharez's sons Hezron and Hamul added, whom JFB reads as a parenthetical note supplying heads of families in place of the dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Issachar's sons are listed, and JFB notes the variant forms Pua for Phuvah and Jashub for Job in the later tribal reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Leah's sons, with Dinah, are summed as thirty-three souls, and JFB notes Dinah and Serah are the only women named, perhaps because Dinah became housekeeper after Jacob's wives died. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Gad's sons are listed, and JFB notes the variant forms Zephon for Ziphion and Ozni for Ezbon in the later lists. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Asher's sons are listed, with Serah their sister, and JFB notes the rhyming names and that Beriah's sons Heber and Malchiel appear as heads of families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Joseph's sons Manasseh and Ephraim, born in Egypt to Asenath, are classed in this catalogue as heads of families, though born outside Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Benjamin's ten names are listed, and JFB argues they include grandsons and a great-grandson, since Benjamin, at most about twenty-two, cannot have had ten sons born in Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Dan's son Hushim is listed, with the variant form Shuham in the later tribal list. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. All the souls that came with Jacob, besides his sons' wives, are counted as sixty-six, a broad reckoning that includes grandsons under the name of sons, since the migration is a new epoch in Israel's history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. With Joseph's two sons the whole house of Jacob is seventy souls, including Jacob, Joseph, and his sons, and JFB notes the preponderance of sons helped guard the family against Canaanite intermarriage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Judah is sent ahead to Joseph to direct the way to Goshen, a prudent step both to inform the king of so large a company and to receive Joseph's guidance to their settlement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Joseph makes ready his horse-drawn chariot, fitting his public dignity, and goes up to Goshen to meet his father, falling on his neck and weeping a good while. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Israel says he can now die in peace, since he has seen Joseph's face and knows he is alive, the happiness of a father reaching its height. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Joseph tells his brothers he will go up and inform Pharaoh of their arrival, a tribute of respect due to the king, with instructions worthy of a brother and a religious man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. He will describe them as shepherds who have brought their flocks, herds, and all they have, stating their occupation plainly rather than concealing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. Joseph anticipates that Pharaoh will call them and ask their occupation, and he coaches them in advance so their answer will be ready and consistent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. They are to answer that their occupation has been with livestock from youth, both they and their fathers, so they may dwell in Goshen apart from Egyptian society, since every shepherd is an abomination to the Egyptians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
