The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Kings 21 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass after these things, that Naboth the Jezreelite had a vineyard, which was in Jezreel, hard by the palace of Ahab king of Samaria.

2 And Ahab spake unto Naboth, saying, Give me thy vineyard, that I may have it for a garden of herbs, because it is near unto my house: and I will give thee for it a better vineyard than it; or, if it seem good to thee, I will give thee the worth of it in money.

3 And Naboth said to Ahab, The LORD forbid it me, that I should give the inheritance of my fathers unto thee.

4 And Ahab came into his house heavy and displeased because of the word which Naboth the Jezreelite had spoken to him: for he had said, I will not give thee the inheritance of my fathers. And he laid him down upon his bed, and turned away his face, and would eat no bread.

5 But Jezebel his wife came to him, and said unto him, Why is thy spirit so sad, that thou eatest no bread?

6 And he said unto her, Because I spake unto Naboth the Jezreelite, and said unto him, Give me thy vineyard for money; or else, if it please thee, I will give thee another vineyard for it: and he answered, I will not give thee my vineyard.

7 And Jezebel his wife said unto him, Dost thou now govern the kingdom of Israel? arise, and eat bread, and let thine heart be merry: I will give thee the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite.

8 So she wrote letters in Ahab’s name, and sealed them with his seal, and sent the letters unto the elders and to the nobles that were in his city, dwelling with Naboth.

9 And she wrote in the letters, saying, Proclaim a fast, and set Naboth on high among the people:

10 And set two men, sons of Belial, before him, to bear witness against him, saying, Thou didst blaspheme God and the king. And then carry him out, and stone him, that he may die.

11 And the men of his city, even the elders and the nobles who were the inhabitants in his city, did as Jezebel had sent unto them, and as it was written in the letters which she had sent unto them.

12 They proclaimed a fast, and set Naboth on high among the people.

13 And there came in two men, children of Belial, and sat before him: and the men of Belial witnessed against him, even against Naboth, in the presence of the people, saying, Naboth did blaspheme God and the king. Then they carried him forth out of the city, and stoned him with stones, that he died.

14 Then they sent to Jezebel, saying, Naboth is stoned, and is dead.

15 And it came to pass, when Jezebel heard that Naboth was stoned, and was dead, that Jezebel said to Ahab, Arise, take possession of the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite, which he refused to give thee for money: for Naboth is not alive, but dead.

16 And it came to pass, when Ahab heard that Naboth was dead, that Ahab rose up to go down to the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite, to take possession of it.

17 And the word of the LORD came to Elijah the Tishbite, saying,

18 Arise, go down to meet Ahab king of Israel, which is in Samaria: behold, he is in the vineyard of Naboth, whither he is gone down to possess it.

19 And thou shalt speak unto him, saying, Thus saith the LORD, Hast thou killed, and also taken possession? And thou shalt speak unto him, saying, Thus saith the LORD, In the place where dogs licked the blood of Naboth shall dogs lick thy blood, even thine.

20 And Ahab said to Elijah, Hast thou found me, O mine enemy? And he answered, I have found thee: because thou hast sold thyself to work evil in the sight of the LORD.

21 Behold, I will bring evil upon thee, and will take away thy posterity, and will cut off from Ahab him that pisseth against the wall, and him that is shut up and left in Israel,

22 And will make thine house like the house of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, and like the house of Baasha the son of Ahijah, for the provocation wherewith thou hast provoked me to anger, and made Israel to sin.

23 And of Jezebel also spake the LORD, saying, The dogs shall eat Jezebel by the wall of Jezreel.

24 Him that dieth of Ahab in the city the dogs shall eat; and him that dieth in the field shall the fowls of the air eat.

25 But there was none like unto Ahab, which did sell himself to work wickedness in the sight of the LORD, whom Jezebel his wife stirred up.

26 And he did very abominably in following idols, according to all things as did the Amorites, whom the LORD cast out before the children of Israel.

27 And it came to pass, when Ahab heard those words, that he rent his clothes, and put sackcloth upon his flesh, and fasted, and lay in sackcloth, and went softly.

28 And the word of the LORD came to Elijah the Tishbite, saying,

29 Seest thou how Ahab humbleth himself before me? because he humbleth himself before me, I will not bring the evil in his days: but in his son’s days will I bring the evil upon his house.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ahab covets Naboth's vineyard beside his palace; Naboth refuses to sell his inheritance, and Jezebel has him falsely accused of blasphemy and stoned, so Ahab takes the vineyard. Elijah meets Ahab there with judgment, dogs will lick his blood where they licked Naboth's, and Ahab humbles himself, so the evil is deferred to his son's days. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

  • Verse 10. She ordered two sons of Belial set before him to witness, Thou didst blaspheme God and the king, and then carry him out and stone him, law itself turned into a weapon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 12. They proclaimed a fast and set Naboth on high among the people, the solemn assembly staged exactly as commanded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Two men of Belial came in, sat before him, and witnessed against him, Thou didst blaspheme God and the king, and they carried him out of the city and stoned him to death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 18. Elijah was sent to meet Ahab in Naboth's vineyard at the very hour he took possession — Henry notes the timing is the charge: by taking possession he avowed all that was done and made himself guilty after the fact, as an accessory, for he took the garden and with it the guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. He was to speak, Thus saith the Lord, Hast thou killed, and also taken possession, and in the place where dogs licked Naboth's blood, dogs shall lick thy blood, even thine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Ahab greeted Elijah as his enemy, asking, Hast thou found me — Henry reads both hatred and fear in it: his conscience had made God his enemy, so he reckoned God's minister his enemy for telling the truth, and the prophet's sight struck him like the handwriting on Belshazzar's wall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Elijah pronounced that evil would be brought on Ahab and his posterity cut off — Henry notes the sentence deliberately repeats Jeroboam's and Baasha's: the house made like theirs, city dead for dogs and field dead for birds, and Ahab's own royal blood licked where Naboth's had been. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. His house would be made like Jeroboam's house and like Baasha's house, because of his provocation and because he made Israel sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Of Jezebel also the Lord spoke, saying, The dogs shall eat Jezebel by the wall of Jezreel, a sentence as particular as her crime. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Him that died of Ahab in the city the dogs would eat, and him that died in the field the fowls would eat, the same dishonour pronounced on Jeroboam and Baasha now pronounced on Ahab. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. But there was none like Ahab, who sold himself to work wickedness in the Lord's sight, whom Jezebel his wife stirred up, a king whose wickedness was both chosen and encouraged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. He did very abominably in following idols, according to all the Amorites did, whom the Lord cast out before Israel, so that Israel's king copied the nations God had judged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Hearing the sentence, Ahab rent his clothes, put on sackcloth, fasted and went softly — Henry calls him a kind of penitent only: the garments were rent but not the heart, an outward profession of repentance where the truth and substance were wanting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The word of the Lord came to Elijah, asking, Seest thou how Ahab humbleth himself before me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Because Ahab humbled himself, God said, I will not bring the evil in his days, but in his son's days will I bring it on his house, mercy delaying, not cancelling, the sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
