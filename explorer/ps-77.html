The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 77 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 I cried unto God with my voice, even unto God with my voice; and he gave ear unto me.

2 In the day of my trouble I sought the Lord: my sore ran in the night, and ceased not: my soul refused to be comforted.

3 I remembered God, and was troubled: I complained, and my spirit was overwhelmed. Selah.

4 Thou holdest mine eyes waking: I am so troubled that I cannot speak.

5 I have considered the days of old, the years of ancient times.

6 I call to remembrance my song in the night: I commune with mine own heart: and my spirit made diligent search.

7 Will the Lord cast off for ever? and will he be favourable no more?

8 Is his mercy clean gone for ever? doth his promise fail for evermore?

9 Hath God forgotten to be gracious? hath he in anger shut up his tender mercies? Selah.

10 And I said, This is my infirmity: but I will remember the years of the right hand of the most High.

11 I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old.

12 I will meditate also of all thy work, and talk of thy doings.

13 Thy way, O God, is in the sanctuary: who is so great a God as our God?

14 Thou art the God that doest wonders: thou hast declared thy strength among the people.

15 Thou hast with thine arm redeemed thy people, the sons of Jacob and Joseph. Selah.

16 The waters saw thee, O God, the waters saw thee; they were afraid: the depths also were troubled.

17 The clouds poured out water: the skies sent out a sound: thine arrows also went abroad.

18 The voice of thy thunder was in the heaven: the lightnings lightened the world: the earth trembled and shook.

19 Thy way is in the sea, and thy path in the great waters, and thy footsteps are not known.

20 Thou leddest thy people like a flock by the hand of Moses and Aaron.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A sleepless, troubled speaker asks whether the Lord has cast off for ever and whether his mercy and promise have failed. At verse 10 the psalm turns: instead of waiting to feel better, the speaker deliberately remembers God's wonders of old — the storm at the sea, the redemption of Jacob and Joseph, and God leading his people like a flock by Moses and Aaron. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The psalmist cried aloud to God, and God gave ear to him. Prayer in trouble is not wasted breath; it is heard before it is answered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. In the day of trouble he sought the Lord through a sleepless, uncomforted night, his sore running and not ceasing. Grief that will not be comforted still turns to God rather than away from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Remembering God troubled him, and his complaint overwhelmed his spirit. Even holy memory can sting when present favour seems withdrawn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. God held his eyes waking, and his trouble left him speechless. Sleeplessness and silence here are the marks of a burden too heavy for words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. He considered the days of old and the years of ancient times, setting former prosperity against present calamity. Henry warns that magnifying former days only feeds discontent with the present, rather than comforting it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. He recalled his song in the night and communed with his own heart, searching his spirit diligently. Faith examines itself rather than merely feeling its pain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. He asks whether the Lord will cast off for ever and be favourable no more. The question is fear speaking, not settled unbelief, and it is brought to God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. He asks whether mercy is clean gone and promise failed for evermore. The fear is that God's goodness has an end, which the rest of the psalm answers by remembering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. He asks whether God has forgotten to be gracious and shut up his tender mercies in anger. The psalm names the darkest suspicion so that it can be answered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. He calls this his infirmity, and resolves instead to remember the years of the right hand of the Most High. The cure for his fear is not feeling better but remembering God's hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. He will remember the works of the Lord and his wonders of old. Deliberate remembrance replaces anxious questioning as the psalm turns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. He will meditate on all God's work and talk of his doings. Meditation and speech together fix the mind on what God has done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. God's way is in the sanctuary, and no God is so great as Israel's God. Holiness and greatness meet in the God who is approached in worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. God does wonders and declares his strength among the peoples. His power is not hidden; it is shown openly for the nations to see. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. With his arm God redeemed his people, the sons of Jacob and Joseph. Redemption is God's own act, by strength, for a particular people he had chosen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. The waters saw God and were afraid, and the depths were troubled. Creation itself trembles before the God who commands the sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Clouds poured out water, skies sent out sound, and God's arrows went abroad. The storm at the Red Sea is read as God's artillery in Israel's deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Thunder sounded in heaven, lightning lightened the world, and the earth trembled and shook. God's coming in judgment and deliverance shakes all that stands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. God's way is in the sea and his path in the great waters, and his footsteps are not known. He works by ways that cannot be tracked, yet they lead his people through. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. God led his people like a flock by the hand of Moses and Aaron. The same God who rules the storm shepherds his people through human leaders he appoints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
