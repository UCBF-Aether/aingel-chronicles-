The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 When thou sittest to eat with a ruler, consider diligently what is before thee:

2 And put a knife to thy throat, if thou be a man given to appetite.

3 Be not desirous of his dainties: for they are deceitful meat.

4 Labour not to be rich: cease from thine own wisdom.

5 Wilt thou set thine eyes upon that which is not? for riches certainly make themselves wings; they fly away as an eagle toward heaven.

6 Eat thou not the bread of him that hath an evil eye, neither desire thou his dainty meats:

7 For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he: Eat and drink, saith he to thee; but his heart is not with thee.

8 The morsel which thou hast eaten shalt thou vomit up, and lose thy sweet words.

9 Speak not in the ears of a fool: for he will despise the wisdom of thy words.

10 Remove not the old landmark; and enter not into the fields of the fatherless:

11 For their redeemer is mighty; he shall plead their cause with thee.

12 Apply thine heart unto instruction, and thine ears to the words of knowledge.

13 Withhold not correction from the child: for if thou beatest him with the rod, he shall not die.

14 Thou shalt beat him with the rod, and shalt deliver his soul from hell.

15 My son, if thine heart be wise, my heart shall rejoice, even mine.

16 Yea, my reins shall rejoice, when thy lips speak right things.

17 Let not thine heart envy sinners: but be thou in the fear of the LORD all the day long.

18 For surely there is an end; and thine expectation shall not be cut off.

19 Hear thou, my son, and be wise, and guide thine heart in the way.

20 Be not among winebibbers; among riotous eaters of flesh:

21 For the drunkard and the glutton shall come to poverty: and drowsiness shall clothe a man with rags.

22 Hearken unto thy father that begat thee, and despise not thy mother when she is old.

23 Buy the truth, and sell it not; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding.

24 The father of the righteous shall greatly rejoice: and he that begetteth a wise child shall have joy of him.

25 Thy father and thy mother shall be glad, and she that bare thee shall rejoice.

26 My son, give me thine heart, and let thine eyes observe my ways.

27 For a whore is a deep ditch; and a strange woman is a narrow pit.

28 She also lieth in wait as for a prey, and increaseth the transgressors among men.

29 Who hath woe? who hath sorrow? who hath contentions? who hath babbling? who hath wounds without cause? who hath redness of eyes?

30 They that tarry long at the wine; they that go to seek mixed wine.

31 Look not thou upon the wine when it is red, when it giveth his colour in the cup, when it moveth itself aright.

32 At the last it biteth like a serpent, and stingeth like an adder.

33 Thine eyes shall behold strange women, and thine heart shall utter perverse things.

34 Yea, thou shalt be as he that lieth down in the midst of the sea, or as he that lieth upon the top of a mast.

35 They have stricken me, shalt thou say, and I was not sick; they have beaten me, and I felt it not: when shall I awake? I will seek it yet again.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Table manners with a ruler, the wings of riches, and the deceitful meat of a man with an evil eye open a chapter that keeps returning to appetite and instruction. Withhold not correction from the child, buy the truth and sell it not, envy not sinners but live in the fear of the LORD, and beware wine that bites like a serpent at the last and leaves its drinker beaten, insensible, and seeking another drink. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 9. Speak not in the ears of a fool, for he will despise the wisdom of thy words, for it is duty to take all fit occasions to speak of Divine things, but if a wise man's words will not be heard, let him hold his peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Remove not the old landmark, and enter not into the fields of the fatherless, for the fatherless are under God's special protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. For their redeemer is mighty, and he shall plead their cause with thee, for the fatherless' Redeemer will take their part, and he is mighty, almighty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Apply thine heart unto instruction, and thine ears to the words of knowledge, for here a parent instructs his child to give his mind to the Scriptures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 16. Yea, my reins shall rejoice, when thy lips speak right things, for a parent encouraging his child tells him what would be for his good, and what comfort his answering expectation would be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 27. For a whore is a deep ditch, and a strange woman is a narrow pit, for licentiousness takes away the heart that should be given to God, and we must take heed of any approaches toward it, for retreat from it is very hard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 31. Look not thou upon the wine when it is red, when it gives colour in the cup, when it moves itself aright, for those who would be kept from sin must keep from its beginnings and fear its allurements. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. At the last it bites like a serpent and stings like an adder, for we must foresee the punishment, what it will at last end in if repentance prevents not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. Thine eyes shall behold strange women, and thine heart shall utter perverse things, for drunkenness makes men impure and insolent, the tongue unruly, the heart uttering things contrary to reason, religion, and civility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. Yea, thou shalt be as he who lies down in the midst of the sea, or as he who lies upon the top of a mast, for the drunkard is in danger of death and damnation, exposed yet feeling secure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. They have stricken me, shalt thou say, and I was not sick, they have beaten me and I felt it not, when shall I awake, I will seek it yet again, for so lost to virtue and honour is the drunkard, his conscience so seared, that he is not ashamed to say he will seek it again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
