The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, as he went into the house of one of the chief Pharisees to eat bread on the sabbath day, that they watched him.

2 And, behold, there was a certain man before him which had the dropsy.

3 And Jesus answering spake unto the lawyers and Pharisees, saying, Is it lawful to heal on the sabbath day?

4 And they held their peace. And he took him, and healed him, and let him go;

5 And answered them, saying, Which of you shall have an ass or an ox fallen into a pit, and will not straightway pull him out on the sabbath day?

6 And they could not answer him again to these things.

7 And he put forth a parable to those which were bidden, when he marked how they chose out the chief rooms; saying unto them,

8 When thou art bidden of any man to a wedding, sit not down in the highest room; lest a more honourable man than thou be bidden of him;

9 And he that bade thee and him come and say to thee, Give this man place; and thou begin with shame to take the lowest room.

10 But when thou art bidden, go and sit down in the lowest room; that when he that bade thee cometh, he may say unto thee, Friend, go up higher: then shalt thou have worship in the presence of them that sit at meat with thee.

11 For whosoever exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.

12 Then said he also to him that bade him, When thou makest a dinner or a supper, call not thy friends, nor thy brethren, neither thy kinsmen, nor thy rich neighbours; lest they also bid thee again, and a recompence be made thee.

13 But when thou makest a feast, call the poor, the maimed, the lame, the blind:

14 And thou shalt be blessed; for they cannot recompense thee: for thou shalt be recompensed at the resurrection of the just.

15 And when one of them that sat at meat with him heard these things, he said unto him, Blessed is he that shall eat bread in the kingdom of God.

16 Then said he unto him, A certain man made a great supper, and bade many:

17 And sent his servant at supper time to say to them that were bidden, Come; for all things are now ready.

18 And they all with one consent began to make excuse. The first said unto him, I have bought a piece of ground, and I must needs go and see it: I pray thee have me excused.

19 And another said, I have bought five yoke of oxen, and I go to prove them: I pray thee have me excused.

20 And another said, I have married a wife, and therefore I cannot come.

21 So that servant came, and shewed his lord these things. Then the master of the house being angry said to his servant, Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city, and bring in hither the poor, and the maimed, and the halt, and the blind.

22 And the servant said, Lord, it is done as thou hast commanded, and yet there is room.

23 And the lord said unto the servant, Go out into the highways and hedges, and compel them to come in, that my house may be filled.

24 For I say unto you, That none of those men which were bidden shall taste of my supper.

25 And there went great multitudes with him: and he turned, and said unto them,

26 If any man come to me, and hate not his father, and mother, and wife, and children, and brethren, and sisters, yea, and his own life also, he cannot be my disciple.

27 And whosoever doth not bear his cross, and come after me, cannot be my disciple.

28 For which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it?

29 Lest haply, after he hath laid the foundation, and is not able to finish it, all that behold it begin to mock him,

30 Saying, This man began to build, and was not able to finish.

31 Or what king, going to make war against another king, sitteth not down first, and consulteth whether he be able with ten thousand to meet him that cometh against him with twenty thousand?

32 Or else, while the other is yet a great way off, he sendeth an ambassage, and desireth conditions of peace.

33 So likewise, whosoever he be of you that forsaketh not all that he hath, he cannot be my disciple.

34 Salt is good: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be seasoned?

35 It is neither fit for the land, nor yet for the dunghill; but men cast it out. He that hath ears to hear, let him hear.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

At a Pharisee’s table on the Sabbath Jesus heals a man with dropsy and asks whether it is lawful to heal on the Sabbath (vv. 1–6). Watching guests choose the chief seats, he teaches humility at feasts and urges inviting the poor, maimed, lame, and blind, who cannot repay — then tells the great supper, where the invited make excuses and the highways and hedges are searched instead (vv. 7–24). The chapter ends with the cost of discipleship counted like a tower-builder or a king going to war: unless a man forsakes all that he has, he cannot be Jesus’ disciple (vv. 25–35). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 14

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. As he went into the house of one of the chief Pharisees to eat bread on the sabbath, they watched him. Henry notes this Pharisee, like others, seems to have had an ill design in entertaining Jesus, yet the Lord would not be hindered from healing, though a clamour would be raised at doing it on the sabbath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Behold, there was a certain man before him which had the dropsy. The sick man is placed before Jesus, in the watching house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Jesus answering spoke to lawyers and Pharisees, Is it lawful to heal on the sabbath day? The question is put before the healing, to the watchers themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They held their peace. And he took him, healed him, and let him go. Silence answers the question, and healing follows it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Which of you shall have an ass or ox fallen into a pit, and will not straightway pull him out on the sabbath day? Necessity for beasts argues necessity for a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And they could not answer him again to these things. The pit argument leaves no reply. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He put forth a parable to those bidden, when he marked how they chose out the chief rooms. Henry notes Christ marks what we do even in common actions, not only in religious assemblies, but at our tables. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When thou art bidden to a wedding, sit not down in the highest room, lest a more honourable man than thou be bidden. Choosing the highest room risks public displacement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And he that bade thee and him come and say, Give this man place, and thou begin with shame to take the lowest room. The shame is the lesson: self-exaltation ends in demotion before witnesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. But when thou art bidden, go and sit in the lowest room, that when he that bade thee comes, he may say, Friend, go up higher; then shalt thou have worship in the presence of them that sit at meat with thee. Low seating leaves room for honour given, not seized. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. For whosoever exalts himself shall be abased, and he that humbles himself shall be exalted. Henry's rule is that pride brings low, and before honour is humility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Then said he to him that bade him, When thou makest a dinner or supper, call not friends, brethren, kinsmen, nor rich neighbours, lest they also bid thee again, and a recompense be made thee. Reciprocal hospitality is not charity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. But when thou makest a feast, call the poor, the maimed, the lame, the blind. The guest list is inverted toward those who cannot repay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And thou shalt be blessed, for they cannot recompense thee, for thou shalt be recompensed at the resurrection of the just. Henry notes works of charity are better than works of show, and the precept of doing good to poor and afflicted should be observed from love to Christ, not for proud liberality's reward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. When one that sat at meat heard these things, he said, Blessed is he that shall eat bread in the kingdom of God. The table talk turns to the kingdom's feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Then said he, A certain man made a great supper, and bade many. The kingdom feast is pictured as a great supper with many bidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And sent his servant at supper time to say to them that were bidden, Come, for all things are now ready. The second call announces readiness, not mere invitation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. They all with one consent began to make excuse. The first said, I have bought a piece of ground, and I must needs go and see it; I pray thee have me excused. Henry notes all found some pretence to put off attendance, reproving Israel's neglect of Christ's grace offers and our backwardness to close with the gospel call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Another said, I have bought five yoke of oxen, and I go to prove them; I pray thee have me excused. Business, newly bought, becomes the excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Another said, I have married a wife, and therefore I cannot come. Marriage, itself good, is pleaded as impossibility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. So the servant showed his lord these things. Then the master, being angry, said, Go out quickly into streets and lanes of the city, and bring in hither the poor, maimed, halt, and blind. Anger at slighted grace turns the invitation to the city's streets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And the servant said, Lord, it is done as thou hast commanded, and yet there is room. Obedience reports room remaining, and the house is not yet full. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And the lord said, Go out into highways and hedges, and compel them to come in, that my house may be filled. Henry notes the apostles were to turn to Gentiles when Jews refused, and with them the church was filled; the provision for precious souls has not been made in vain, for if some reject, others thankfully accept. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. For I say unto you, that none of those men which were bidden shall taste of my supper. Refusal, once settled, excludes from tasting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And there went great multitudes with him, and he turned and said unto them. Multitudes following prompt a hard word about discipleship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. If any man come to me, and hate not his father, mother, wife, children, brethren, sisters, yea, and his own life also, he cannot be my disciple. Henry notes disciples are not all crucified, yet all bear their cross in the way of duty, and must count upon it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And whosoever does not bear his cross, and come after me, cannot be my disciple. Cross-bearing is the condition, not an optional extra. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. For which of you, intending to build a tower, sits not down first and counts the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it? Henry explains the first similitude: we must consider the expenses of religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Lest haply, after he has laid the foundation and is not able to finish it, all that behold it begin to mock him. An unfinished tower invites mockery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Saying, This man began to build, and was not able to finish. The mockers' sentence is the warning itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Or what king, going to make war against another king, sits not down first and consults whether he be able with ten thousand to meet him that comes with twenty thousand? Henry explains the second similitude: we must consider the perils of religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Or else, while the other is yet a great way off, he sends an ambassage and desires conditions of peace. The outnumbered king seeks peace before battle, and Henry notes the proudest sinner cannot stand against God, so it is our interest to seek peace with him, whose conditions are offered to our advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. So likewise, whosoever he be of you that forsakes not all that he has, he cannot be my disciple. Counting cost ends in forsaking all, or not being a disciple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Salt is good, but if the salt has lost his savour, wherewith shall it be seasoned? Salt without savour has lost its use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. It is neither fit for the land, nor yet for the dunghill, but men cast it out. He that has ears to hear, let him hear. Henry urges seeking to be disciples indeed, careful not to grow slack or afraid of the cross, that we may be good salt seasoning those around us with Christ's savour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
