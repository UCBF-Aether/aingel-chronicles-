The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Sirach 7 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Do no evil, so shall no harm come unto thee.

2 Depart from the unjust, and iniquity shall turn away from thee.

3 My son, sow not upon the furrows of unrighteousness, and thou shalt not reap them sevenfold.

4 Seek not of the Lord preeminence, neither of the king the seat of honour.

5 justify not thyself before the Lord; and boast not of thy wisdom before the king.

6 Seek not to be judge, being not able to take away iniquity; lest at any time thou fear the person of the mighty, an stumblingblock in the way of thy uprightness.

7 Offend not against the multitude of a city, and then thou shalt not cast thyself down among the people.

8 Bind not one sin upon another; for in one thou shalt not be unpunished.

9 Say not, God will look upon the multitude of my oblations, and when I offer to the most high God, he will accept it.

10 Be not fainthearted when thou makest thy prayer, and neglect not to give alms.

11 Laugh no man to scorn in the bitterness of his soul: for there is one which humbleth and exalteth.

12 Devise not a lie against thy brother; neither do the like to thy friend.

13 Use not to make any manner of lie: for the custom thereof is not good.

14 Use not many words in a multitude of elders, and make not much babbling when thou prayest.

15 Hate not laborious work, neither husbandry, which the most High hath ordained.

16 Number not thyself among the multitude of sinners, but remember that wrath will not tarry long.

17 Humble thyself greatly: for the vengeance of the ungodly is fire and worms.

18 Change not a friend for any good by no means; neither a faithful brother for the gold of Ophir.

19 Forego not a wise and good woman: for her grace is above gold.

20 Whereas thy servant worketh truly, entreat him not evil. nor the hireling that bestoweth himself wholly for thee.

21 Let thy soul love a good servant, and defraud him not of liberty.

22 Hast thou cattle? have an eye to them: and if they be for thy profit, keep them with thee.

23 Hast thou children? instruct them, and bow down their neck from their youth.

24 Hast thou daughters? have a care of their body, and shew not thyself cheerful toward them.

25 Marry thy daughter, and so shalt thou have performed a weighty matter: but give her to a man of understanding.

26 Hast thou a wife after thy mind? forsake her not: but give not thyself over to a light woman.

27 Honour thy father with thy whole heart, and forget not the sorrows of thy mother.

28 Remember that thou wast begotten of them; and how canst thou recompense them the things that they have done for thee?

29 Fear the Lord with all thy soul, and reverence his priests.

30 Love him that made thee with all thy strength, and forsake not his ministers.

31 Fear the Lord, and honor the priest; and give him his portion, as it is commanded thee; the firstfruits, and the trespass offering, and the gift of the shoulders, and the sacrifice of sanctification, and the firstfruits of the holy things.

32 And stretch thine hand unto the poor, that thy blessing may be perfected.

33 A gift hath grace in the sight of every man living; and for the dead detain it not.

34 Fail not to be with them that weep, and mourn with them that mourn.

35 Be not slow to visit the sick: fir that shall make thee to be beloved.

36 Whatsoever thou takest in hand, remember the end, and thou shalt never do amiss.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Do no evil and no harm will follow is the chapter's plain opening rule, worked out across duties to God, neighbours, the household, and servants. A wise and good wife is not to be given up, since her grace is above gold, and every undertaking should be governed by remembering its end. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Do no evil, and no harm will come to you. Refraining from evil is presented as the plain way to escape harm's return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Depart from the unjust, and iniquity will turn away from you. Leaving unjust company is how iniquity itself is left behind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. Do not sow upon the furrows of unrighteousness, and you will not reap them sevenfold. Sowing in unrighteous ground yields a multiplied harvest of trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. Seek neither preeminence from the Lord nor the seat of honour from the king. Ambition for rank, whether from God or king, is not to be sought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Do not justify yourself before the Lord, and do not boast of your wisdom before the king. Self-justification before God and boasting of wisdom before a king are both out of place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. Do not seek to be judge if you cannot take away iniquity, lest you fear the person of the mighty and stumble in the way of your uprightness. Judging without power to remove wrong invites fear of the mighty, which becomes a stumbling block to uprightness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. Do not offend against the multitude of a city, and then you will not cast yourself down among the people. Offending the whole city degrades a person in the people's eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Do not bind one sin upon another, for in one you will not be unpunished. Piling sin on sin does not dilute guilt; even one sin does not go unpunished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. Do not say God will look upon the multitude of your oblations and accept them when offered to the Most High. Many offerings do not buy acceptance with God when the life behind them is wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Do not be fainthearted when you make your prayer, and do not neglect to give alms. Prayer should be bold, not fainthearted, and it belongs together with almsgiving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. Do not laugh any man to scorn in the bitterness of his soul, for there is one who humbles and exalts. Mocking a bitter soul forgets that God both humbles and exalts, and fortunes can reverse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. Do not devise a lie against your brother, nor do the like to your friend. Lying devised against brother or friend is doubly condemned, since both bonds should forbid it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. Do not make any manner of lie, for the custom of it is not good. Lying must not become a habit, for the custom itself corrupts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. Do not use many words in a multitude of elders, and do not make much babbling when you pray. Brevity is fitting before elders, and prayer is not improved by babbling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. Do not hate laborious work, nor husbandry, which the Most High has ordained. Hard work and farming are God's own ordinances, not things beneath a wise man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. Do not number yourself among the multitude of sinners, but remember that wrath will not tarry long. Joining the crowd of sinners is no safety, for wrath does not delay long. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. Humble yourself greatly, for the vengeance of the ungodly is fire and worms. Deep humility is urged because the end of the ungodly is corruption, pictured as fire and worms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. Do not change a friend for any good by no means, nor a faithful brother for the gold of Ophir. No material gain, not even Ophir's gold, is worth trading a friend or faithful brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. Do not forego a wise and good woman, for her grace is above gold. A wise, good wife is not to be given up, since her grace outvalues gold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 20. Whereas your servant works truly, do not entreat him evil, nor the hireling who bestows himself wholly for you. Faithful service deserves fair treatment, whether from a servant or a wholly devoted hireling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 21. Let your soul love a good servant, and do not defraud him of liberty. A good servant should be loved, not cheated of the liberty that is his due. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 22. If you have cattle, have an eye to them, and if they are for your profit, keep them with you. Livestock require oversight, and profitable cattle are worth keeping close. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 23. If you have children, instruct them, and bow down their neck from their youth. Children need instruction and early discipline, while the neck is still young enough to bow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 24. If you have daughters, have a care of their body, and do not show yourself cheerful toward them. Daughters require careful guarding, and a father should not be lax or familiar in a way that loosens discipline. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 25. Marry your daughter, and you will have performed a weighty matter, but give her to a man of understanding. Marrying a daughter well is a weighty duty, and understanding in the husband matters most. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 26. If you have a wife after your mind, do not forsake her, but do not give yourself over to a light woman. A fitting wife should be kept, and a light woman must not be allowed to take possession of a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 27. Honour your father with your whole heart, and do not forget the sorrows of your mother. Honour to a father must be wholehearted, and a mother's sorrows must be remembered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 28. Remember that you were begotten of them, and how can you recompense what they have done for you? Your very existence came from your parents, and no repayment can match what they did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 29. Fear the Lord with all your soul, and reverence his priests. Whole-souled fear of God belongs with reverence for those who serve him as priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 30. Love him who made you with all your strength, and do not forsake his ministers. Love for the Maker, with all one's strength, includes not abandoning his ministers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 31. Fear the Lord, honour the priest, and give him his portion as commanded: the firstfruits, the trespass offering, the gift of the shoulders, the sacrifice of sanctification, and the firstfruits of the holy things. The priest's due is itemised because honour must be paid in the commanded portions, not in vague respect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 32. Stretch your hand to the poor, so that your blessing may be perfected. Giving to the poor completes a blessing; without it the blessing is unfinished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 33. A gift has grace in the sight of every man living, and for the dead do not detain it. A gift wins favour among the living, and what is owed for the dead should not be held back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 34. Do not fail to be with those who weep, and mourn with those who mourn. Presence with the weeping and shared mourning are duties, not options. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 35. Do not be slow to visit the sick, for that will make you beloved. Prompt visits to the sick win love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 36. Whatever you take in hand, remember the end, and you will never do amiss. Keeping the end in view governs every undertaking and keeps it from going wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
