The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ecclesiastes 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Keep thy foot when thou goest to the house of God, and be more ready to hear, than to give the sacrifice of fools: for they consider not that they do evil.

2 Be not rash with thy mouth, and let not thine heart be hasty to utter any thing before God: for God is in heaven, and thou upon earth: therefore let thy words be few.

3 For a dream cometh through the multitude of business; and a fool’s voice is known by multitude of words.

4 When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it; for he hath no pleasure in fools: pay that which thou hast vowed.

5 Better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay.

6 Suffer not thy mouth to cause thy flesh to sin; neither say thou before the angel, that it was an error: wherefore should God be angry at thy voice, and destroy the work of thine hands?

7 For in the multitude of dreams and many words there are also divers vanities: but fear thou God.

8 If thou seest the oppression of the poor, and violent perverting of judgment and justice in a province, marvel not at the matter: for he that is higher than the highest regardeth; and there be higher than they.

9 Moreover the profit of the earth is for all: the king himself is served by the field.

10 He that loveth silver shall not be satisfied with silver; nor he that loveth abundance with increase: this is also vanity.

11 When goods increase, they are increased that eat them: and what good is there to the owners thereof, saving the beholding of them with their eyes?

12 The sleep of a labouring man is sweet, whether he eat little or much: but the abundance of the rich will not suffer him to sleep.

13 There is a sore evil which I have seen under the sun, namely, riches kept for the owners thereof to their hurt.

14 But those riches perish by evil travail: and he begetteth a son, and there is nothing in his hand.

15 As he came forth of his mother’s womb, naked shall he return to go as he came, and shall take nothing of his labour, which he may carry away in his hand.

16 And this also is a sore evil, that in all points as he came, so shall he go: and what profit hath he that hath laboured for the wind?

17 All his days also he eateth in darkness, and he hath much sorrow and wrath with his sickness.

18 Behold that which I have seen: it is good and comely for one to eat and to drink, and to enjoy the good of all his labour that he taketh under the sun all the days of his life, which God giveth him: for it is his portion.

19 Every man also to whom God hath given riches and wealth, and hath given him power to eat thereof, and to take his portion, and to rejoice in his labour; this is the gift of God.

20 For he shall not much remember the days of his life; because God answereth him in the joy of his heart.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Reverence comes first: guard your steps in God's house, be slow to speak, and pay your vows. Then wealth is weighed and found wanting: lovers of silver are never satisfied, riches kept to their owner's hurt perish, and the good portion is to eat, drink and enjoy one's labour as God's gift. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Keep thy foot when thou goest to the house of God, and be more ready to hear than to give the sacrifice of fools, because worship must begin with reverent attention, not with careless offerings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Be not rash with thy mouth, nor hasty to utter anything before God, for God is in heaven and thou upon earth, therefore let thy words be few, since reverence suits the distance between God and man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. A dream comes through the multitude of business, and a fool's voice is known by multitude of words, because a mind crowded with affairs and a mouth crowded with words both betray a lack of government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it, for he has no pleasure in fools, so pay what thou hast vowed, since a promise to God is not a thing to be trifled with. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Better it is that thou shouldest not vow than that thou shouldest vow and not pay, because an unmade vow leaves no debt, while a broken vow adds guilt to rashness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Suffer not thy mouth to cause thy flesh to sin, nor say before the angel that it was an error, because an excuse offered after a rash vow cannot undo it, and God will be angry at the voice that made it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. In the multitude of dreams and many words there are also vanities, but fear thou God, because dreams and talk are empty, while the fear of God is the solid ground of worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. If thou seest the oppression of the poor and violent perverting of judgment in a province, marvel not at the matter, for he that is higher than the highest regards it, and there are higher than they, so that no wrong escapes a higher court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Moreover the profit of the earth is for all, and the king himself is served by the field, because even the highest rank depends on the tillage of the ground, which humbles human pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. He that loves silver shall not be satisfied with silver, nor he that loves abundance with increase, because desire grows with possession, and this also is vanity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. When goods increase, they are increased that eat them, so what good is there to the owners but the beholding of them with their eyes, since wealth multiplies dependents faster than enjoyment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The sleep of a labouring man is sweet, whether he eat little or much, but the abundance of the rich will not suffer him to sleep, because a quiet conscience rests while a full estate watches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. There is a sore evil under the sun, riches kept for the owners thereof to their hurt, because wealth hoarded becomes a snare to the very man who hoards it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Those riches perish by evil travail, and the man begets a son and there is nothing in his hand, because one misadventure can strip a lifetime's gathering in a day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. As he came forth of his mother's womb, naked shall he return to go as he came, and he shall take nothing of his labour to carry in his hand, because death strips every man to the state in which he was born. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. This also is a sore evil, that in all points as he came so shall he go, and what profit has he that has laboured for the wind, since all his toil leaves him exactly where he began. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. All his days also he eats in darkness, and has much sorrow and wrath with his sickness, because the life spent grasping at wealth ends in gloom even before it ends in death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Behold what he has seen to be good and comely, to eat and drink and enjoy the good of all his labour all the days of his life which God gives him, for it is his portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Every man to whom God has given riches and wealth, and power to eat thereof and take his portion and rejoice in his labour, has received the gift of God, and enjoyment itself is God's grant, not man's purchase. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He shall not much remember the days of his life, because God answers him in the joy of his heart, so that a heart contented with its portion is kept from brooding on the brevity of its days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
