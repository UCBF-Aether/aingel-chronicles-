The Text — 2 Book of the Covenant 19 (J. Cooper & A. J. Maclean, The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1902), public domain (archive.org cu31924029296170))

1 [OF THE DEACONS WHO GO AND PASS AMONG WOMEN, LEST THERE BE DISORDERLY YOUNG CHILDREN °]

2 Let the readers assist them. Similarly also the subdeacons. Let them not allow them to sleep.’ For that night is a figure of the kingdom, and especially that of the Saturday.

3 Those who labour and work, let them work till midnight.

4 Let the catechumens first be dismissed, having received blessings from the bread which is broken.

5 When the faithful are dismissed, let them go in order’ and knowledge to their houses. In their feasts let them not forget the prayers.

6 Let the priests not abbreviate their ministrations.

7 Let the women go, each one cleaving to her husband.

8 Let the widows stay till dawn in the temple, having food there.

9 Let the virgins abide together in the temple, and let the bishop help and provide for them, and let the deacons minister to them.

10 Let the presbyteresses stay with the bishop till dawn, praying and resting.

11 Similarly also those who were lately baptized.

12 Let virgins who are ready for marriage go, cleaving to their mothers. This is thus fitting.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

On the Paschal night the deacons go and pass among the women, lest there be disorderly young children, with the readers assisting them, and similarly the subdeacons, and they are not to allow them to sleep — for that night is a figure of the kingdom, and especially that of the Saturday. Those who labour and work are to work till midnight. The catechumens are first to be dismissed, having received blessings from the bread which is broken. When the faithful are dismissed, they are to go in order and knowledge to their houses, and in their feasts not forget the prayers. The priests are not to abbreviate their ministrations. The women are to go, each one cleaving to her husband. The widows are to stay till dawn in the temple, having food there. The virgins are to abide together in the temple, and the bishop is to help and provide for them, with the deacons ministering to them. The presbyteresses are to stay with the bishop till dawn, praying and resting, and similarly those lately baptized. Virgins ready for marriage are to go, cleaving to their mothers. This is thus fitting. Interpreted Chapter text above; the study-notes file for this chapter is silent, so no commentary is cited
