The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Peter 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to the strangers scattered throughout Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia,

2 Elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, through sanctification of the Spirit, unto obedience and sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ: Grace unto you, and peace, be multiplied.

3 Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, which according to his abundant mercy hath begotten us again unto a lively hope by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead,

4 To an inheritance incorruptible, and undefiled, and that fadeth not away, reserved in heaven for you,

5 Who are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation ready to be revealed in the last time.

6 Wherein ye greatly rejoice, though now for a season, if need be, ye are in heaviness through manifold temptations:

7 That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ:

8 Whom having not seen, ye love; in whom, though now ye see him not, yet believing, ye rejoice with joy unspeakable and full of glory:

9 Receiving the end of your faith, even the salvation of your souls.

10 Of which salvation the prophets have enquired and searched diligently, who prophesied of the grace that should come unto you:

11 Searching what, or what manner of time the Spirit of Christ which was in them did signify, when it testified beforehand the sufferings of Christ, and the glory that should follow.

12 Unto whom it was revealed, that not unto themselves, but unto us they did minister the things, which are now reported unto you by them that have preached the gospel unto you with the Holy Ghost sent down from heaven; which things the angels desire to look into.

13 Wherefore gird up the loins of your mind, be sober, and hope to the end for the grace that is to be brought unto you at the revelation of Jesus Christ;

14 As obedient children, not fashioning yourselves according to the former lusts in your ignorance:

15 But as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation;

16 Because it is written, Be ye holy; for I am holy.

17 And if ye call on the Father, who without respect of persons judgeth according to every man’s work, pass the time of your sojourning here in fear:

18 Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers;

19 But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:

20 Who verily was foreordained before the foundation of the world, but was manifest in these last times for you,

21 Who by him do believe in God, that raised him up from the dead, and gave him glory; that your faith and hope might be in God.

22 Seeing ye have purified your souls in obeying the truth through the Spirit unto unfeigned love of the brethren, see that ye love one another with a pure heart fervently:

23 Being born again, not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible, by the word of God, which liveth and abideth for ever.

24 For all flesh is as grass, and all the glory of man as the flower of grass. The grass withereth, and the flower thereof falleth away:

25 But the word of the Lord endureth for ever. And this is the word which by the gospel is preached unto you.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Peter writes to strangers scattered across Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia and Bithynia, chosen according to God's foreknowledge, praising God for a living hope and an inheritance incorruptible and unfading, kept in heaven. Trials, though painful for a season, refine faith like gold, and readers, who love Christ without having seen him, are called to holy living, redeemed not with silver or gold but with Christ's precious blood, and born again through the enduring word of God. Interpreted Editorial overview of the chapter text; verse-by-verse study-note summaries follow

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Peter writes as an apostle of Jesus Christ to strangers scattered through Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia. Henry notes the epistle is addressed to believers in general, strangers in every city or country where they live, scattered through the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. These strangers are elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, through sanctification of the Spirit, unto obedience and sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ, with grace and peace multiplied to them. Henry traces salvation to the electing love of the Father, the redemption of the Son, and the sanctification of the Spirit, giving glory to one God in three persons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who according to his abundant mercy has begotten us again to a living hope by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead. Worldly hope is uncertain and built on sand, but this hope is living in both its object and its effect, enlivening and comforting in all distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The hope is set on an inheritance incorruptible, undefiled, and unfading, reserved in heaven for believers. Henry contrasts it with earthly possessions, which are stained, uncertain, and soon pass away like flowers of the field, while this estate cannot be spent and never grows less pleasant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Believers are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation, ready to be revealed in the last time. God not only gives his people grace but preserves them unto glory, so that every believer always has something wherein to greatly rejoice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. In this salvation believers greatly rejoice, though now for a season, if need be, they are in heaviness through manifold temptations. Henry notes the Lord does not willingly afflict, yet his wise love often appoints sharp trials to show his people their hearts and to do them good at the latter end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The trial of faith is much more precious than gold that perishes, though tried with fire, and will be found to praise, honour, and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ. Gold becomes less by trial in the fire, but faith is made firm and multiplied by troubles and afflictions, and this should reconcile believers to present affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Christ, whom believers have not seen, they love, and believing in him they rejoice with joy unspeakable and full of glory. Henry says believing Christ's excellence in himself and his love toward us kindles a fire in the heart that rises up in a sacrifice of love to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Believers receive the end of their faith, even the salvation of their souls. The certainty of this hope is, in Henry's words, as if believers had already received it, since God's glory and their own happiness are so united that seeking the one now secures the other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Of this salvation the prophets enquired and searched diligently, prophesying of the grace that would come to believers. Christ was the main subject of the prophets' studies, and their inquiry into his sufferings and the glories to follow leads to a view of the whole gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The prophets searched what, or what manner of time, the Spirit of Christ in them signified when testifying beforehand of Christ's sufferings and the glory that should follow. The sum of their message, Henry notes, is that Christ Jesus was delivered for our offences and raised again for our justification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. It was revealed to the prophets that they ministered these things not to themselves but to us, as now reported by gospel preachers with the Holy Ghost sent down from heaven, things angels desire to look into. The doctrine of the prophets and of the apostles exactly agree, coming from the same Spirit of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Wherefore gird up the loins of your mind, be sober, and hope to the end for the grace to be brought at the revelation of Jesus Christ. As travellers, racers, warriors, and labourers gathered their long garments to be ready, so Christians should gather their minds and affections, watching against spiritual dangers and trusting fully in God's grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. As obedient children, believers must not fashion themselves according to their former lusts in ignorance. Holiness is the desire and duty of every Christian, and the written word is the surest rule of life, commanding holiness in every way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. As he who called you is holy, so be holy in all manner of conversation. Holiness must extend to all affairs, every condition, and toward all people, and believers must especially watch and pray against the sins to which they are inclined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. It is written, Be holy, for I am holy. God makes those holy whom he saves, so the command is not a bare demand but the pattern of the salvation itself, measured by God's own character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. If you call on the Father, who without respect of persons judges according to every person's work, pass the time of your sojourning here in fear. Holy confidence in God as Father and awful fear of him as Judge agree together: regarding God always as Judge makes him dear to us as Father, and keeps believers from both fearless presumption and enslaving dread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Believers were not redeemed with corruptible things, silver and gold, from the vain conversation received by tradition from their fathers. Henry warns that merely customary religion is dangerous: it is folly to resolve to live and die in a way only because one's forefathers did so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Redemption was with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot. The price paid for man's redemption was nothing less than that blood, given for openly wicked and merely unprofitable lives alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Christ was foreordained before the foundation of the world, but manifested in these last times for believers. God had purposes of special favour toward his people long before he made such grace manifest, and the light, supports, and power of ordinances are all greater since Christ came than before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. By Christ believers trust in God, who raised him from the dead and gave him glory, so that faith and hope are in God. Being by faith made one with Christ, his present glory assures believers that where he is, they shall be also. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Having purified their souls in obeying the truth through the Spirit unto unfeigned love of the brethren, believers are to love one another with a pure heart fervently. The soul must be purified before it can give up its own desires, and the word planted by the Spirit works a total change of dispositions and affections. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Believers are born again, not of corruptible seed but of incorruptible, by the word of God which lives and abides forever. The word of God planted in the heart by the Holy Ghost is the means of spiritual life, stirring up duty and bringing the soul to eternal life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. All flesh is as grass, and all the glory of man as the flower of grass: the grass withers and its flower falls away. In his life and fall, natural man is like grass, soon withering and dying, in contrast with the excellence of the renewed spiritual person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. But the word of the Lord endures forever, and this is the word preached to believers by the gospel. Henry's counsel is to hear, receive, and love this holy, living word, lodging it in the heart as the only treasure here and the certain pledge of the treasure of glory laid up in heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
