The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover Ahithophel said unto Absalom, Let me now choose out twelve thousand men, and I will arise and pursue after David this night:

2 And I will come upon him while he is weary and weak handed, and will make him afraid: and all the people that are with him shall flee; and I will smite the king only:

3 And I will bring back all the people unto thee: the man whom thou seekest is as if all returned: so all the people shall be in peace.

4 And the saying pleased Absalom well, and all the elders of Israel.

5 Then said Absalom, Call now Hushai the Archite also, and let us hear likewise what he saith.

6 And when Hushai was come to Absalom, Absalom spake unto him, saying, Ahithophel hath spoken after this manner: shall we do after his saying? if not; speak thou.

7 And Hushai said unto Absalom, The counsel that Ahithophel hath given is not good at this time.

8 For, said Hushai, thou knowest thy father and his men, that they be mighty men, and they be chafed in their minds, as a bear robbed of her whelps in the field: and thy father is a man of war, and will not lodge with the people.

9 Behold, he is hid now in some pit, or in some other place: and it will come to pass, when some of them be overthrown at the first, that whosoever heareth it will say, There is a slaughter among the people that follow Absalom.

10 And he also that is valiant, whose heart is as the heart of a lion, shall utterly melt: for all Israel knoweth that thy father is a mighty man, and they which be with him are valiant men.

11 Therefore I counsel that all Israel be generally gathered unto thee, from Dan even to Beersheba, as the sand that is by the sea for multitude; and that thou go to battle in thine own person.

12 So shall we come upon him in some place where he shall be found, and we will light upon him as the dew falleth on the ground: and of him and of all the men that are with him there shall not be left so much as one.

13 Moreover, if he be gotten into a city, then shall all Israel bring ropes to that city, and we will draw it into the river, until there be not one small stone found there.

14 And Absalom and all the men of Israel said, The counsel of Hushai the Archite is better than the counsel of Ahithophel. For the LORD had appointed to defeat the good counsel of Ahithophel, to the intent that the LORD might bring evil upon Absalom.

15 Then said Hushai unto Zadok and to Abiathar the priests, Thus and thus did Ahithophel counsel Absalom and the elders of Israel; and thus and thus have I counselled.

16 Now therefore send quickly, and tell David, saying, Lodge not this night in the plains of the wilderness, but speedily pass over; lest the king be swallowed up, and all the people that are with him.

17 Now Jonathan and Ahimaaz stayed by Enrogel; for they might not be seen to come into the city: and a wench went and told them; and they went and told king David.

18 Nevertheless a lad saw them, and told Absalom: but they went both of them away quickly, and came to a man’s house in Bahurim, which had a well in his court; whither they went down.

19 And the woman took and spread a covering over the well’s mouth, and spread ground corn thereon; and the thing was not known.

20 And when Absalom’s servants came to the woman to the house, they said, Where is Ahimaaz and Jonathan? And the woman said unto them, They be gone over the brook of water. And when they had sought and could not find them, they returned to Jerusalem.

21 And it came to pass, after they were departed, that they came up out of the well, and went and told king David, and said unto David, Arise, and pass quickly over the water: for thus hath Ahithophel counselled against you.

22 Then David arose, and all the people that were with him, and they passed over Jordan: by the morning light there lacked not one of them that was not gone over Jordan.

23 And when Ahithophel saw that his counsel was not followed, he saddled his ass, and arose, and gat him home to his house, to his city, and put his household in order, and hanged himself, and died, and was buried in the sepulchre of his father.

24 Then David came to Mahanaim. And Absalom passed over Jordan, he and all the men of Israel with him.

25 And Absalom made Amasa captain of the host instead of Joab: which Amasa was a man’s son, whose name was Ithra an Israelite, that went in to Abigail the daughter of Nahash, sister to Zeruiah Joab’s mother.

26 So Israel and Absalom pitched in the land of Gilead.

27 And it came to pass, when David was come to Mahanaim, that Shobi the son of Nahash of Rabbah of the children of Ammon, and Machir the son of Ammiel of Lodebar, and Barzillai the Gileadite of Rogelim,

28 Brought beds, and basons, and earthen vessels, and wheat, and barley, and flour, and parched corn, and beans, and lentiles, and parched pulse,

29 And honey, and butter, and sheep, and cheese of kine, for David, and for the people that were with him, to eat: for they said, The people is hungry, and weary, and thirsty, in the wilderness.

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ahithophel counsels an immediate night pursuit of David, but Hushai, David's planted friend, argues for a grand muster instead, and Absalom follows Hushai, giving David time to cross the Jordan. Ahithophel, seeing his counsel rejected, goes home and hangs himself, while David gathers at Mahanaim. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Ahithophel asks Absalom to let him choose twelve thousand men and arise to pursue David that very night. Speed is the counsel's strength: strike before the fugitives can regroup. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. He will come upon David while weary and weak-handed, make him afraid, scatter his people, and smite the king only. A single blow at the king would end the war without a general battle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. He will bring back all the people to Absalom, as if all returned, so all shall be in peace. The plan promises a bloodless settlement after one assassination. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The saying pleases Absalom well and all the elders of Israel. The best counsel in the room is recognised at once, and that makes its rejection the more remarkable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Absalom says, Call now Hushai the Archite also, and let us hear likewise what he says. Here is the wonderful effect of Providence, Henry notes, blinding Absalom's mind and influencing his heart, so he cannot rest in Ahithophel's counsel but desires Hushai's advice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. When Hushai comes, Absalom lays Ahithophel's counsel before him and asks whether to follow it, or else speak. Hushai is invited to judge the very counsel he was planted to defeat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Hushai says Ahithophel's counsel is not good at this time. The qualification matters: he does not call it foolish in itself, only ill-timed, which makes his alternative persuasive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Hushai warns that David and his men are mighty men, chafed as a bear robbed of her whelps, and David is a man of war who will not lodge with the people. A cornered, experienced force is the worst target for a night attack. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. David is hid now in some pit or place, and if some of Absalom's men fall at the first, all who hear will say there is a slaughter among Absalom's followers. A small reverse at the start would break the new army's nerve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Even the valiant man whose heart is as a lion's shall utterly melt, for all Israel knows David is mighty and his men valiant. Reputation itself becomes a weapon for the defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Hushai counsels instead that all Israel be gathered from Dan to Beersheba, as the sand by the sea, and that Absalom go to battle in his own person. Delay disguised as thoroughness is the real counsel: time is what David needs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Then they shall come upon David where he is found and light on him as dew falls on the ground, leaving none of his men remaining. The grand army promises total victory, later, instead of a swift stroke now. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. If David gets into a city, all Israel shall bring ropes and draw it into the river until not one small stone is found. The boastful image of dragging a city away flatters Absalom's sense of overwhelming force. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Absalom and all Israel say Hushai's counsel is better than Ahithophel's, for the Lord had appointed to defeat Ahithophel's good counsel, to bring evil upon Absalom. There is no contending with God, Henry says, who can arm a man against himself and destroy him by his own mistakes; Ahithophel's former counsel was followed because God intended to correct David, but this one was not, because God meant not to destroy him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Hushai tells Zadok and Abiathar what Ahithophel counselled and what he counselled. The double agent reports both counsels to David's priests, so the warning carries the full picture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Send quickly, Hushai says, and tell David not to lodge this night in the plains of the wilderness, but speedily pass over, lest the king be swallowed up. Even a rejected counsel is dangerous until David is across the Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Jonathan and Ahimaaz stay by Enrogel, not daring to be seen entering the city, and a wench goes and tells them, and they go to tell David. The messenger chain David set up in chapter 15 now works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. A lad sees them and tells Absalom, but they hurry to a man's house in Bahurim with a well in his court. The escape is narrow, and the warning nearly fails at the last link. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. The woman spreads a covering over the well's mouth and ground corn on it, and the thing is not known. A household's quick wit hides the king's messengers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. When Absalom's servants ask where Ahimaaz and Jonathan are, the woman says they are gone over the brook of water. The misdirection sends the search the wrong way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. After the servants depart, the messengers climb out of the well and tell David to arise and pass quickly over Jordan, for Ahithophel counselled thus against him. The warning arrives in time because Hushai's delay gave the messengers their chance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. David and all his people pass over Jordan, and by morning light not one is left uncrossed. The whole company escapes intact, and whatever wisdom men employ, Henry notes, success is from God alone, who will not let his people perish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. When Ahithophel sees his counsel is not followed, he saddles his ass, goes home to his city, puts his household in order, hangs himself, and dies, buried in his father's sepulchre. He hangs himself for vexation, Henry says: what breaks a proud man's heart will not break a humble man's sleep, and he does justice on himself to prevent a public execution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. David comes to Mahanaim, and Absalom passes over Jordan with all Israel. The pursuit resumes, but now on David's chosen ground and on his timetable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Absalom makes Amasa captain of the host instead of Joab, Amasa being the son of Ithra who went in to Abigail, David's sister. A new commander replaces the general who stayed loyal to David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Israel and Absalom pitch in the land of Gilead. The rebel army masses across the Jordan, preparing for the battle Hushai's counsel made inevitable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. When David comes to Mahanaim, Shobi of Rabbah, Machir of Lodebar, and Barzillai the Gileadite meet him. God, Henry observes, sometimes makes up to his people from strangers the comfort they are disappointed of in their own families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. They bring beds, basins, earthen vessels, wheat, barley, flour, parched corn, beans, and lentils. The fugitive army is furnished for a camp, not merely fed for a night. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. They bring honey, butter, sheep, and cheese of kine for David and his people, saying the people are hungry, weary, and thirsty in the wilderness. What is done for the least of the King's brethren, Henry notes, is accepted and recompensed as if done to himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
