Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 149 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Praise ye the LORD. Sing unto the LORD a new song, and his praise in the congregation of saints.

2 Let Israel rejoice in him that made him: let the children of Zion be joyful in their King.

3 Let them praise his name in the dance: let them sing praises unto him with the timbrel and harp.

4 For the LORD taketh pleasure in his people: he will beautify the meek with salvation.

5 Let the saints be joyful in glory: let them sing aloud upon their beds.

6 Let the high praises of God be in their mouth, and a two-edged sword in their hand;

7 To execute vengeance upon the heathen, and punishments upon the people;

8 To bind their kings with chains, and their nobles with fetters of iron;

9 To execute upon them the judgment written: this honour have all his saints. Praise ye the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A new song in the congregation of saints: let Israel rejoice in its maker, the children of Zion be joyful in their King, praising his name in dance with timbrel and harp, for the LORD takes pleasure in his people and will beautify the meek with salvation, and the saints are joyful in glory, singing aloud upon their beds. The second half is martial and must be reported as written: with the high praises of God in their mouth and a two-edged sword in their hand, they are to execute vengeance on the heathen and punishments on the peoples, bind their kings with chains and nobles with fetters of iron, and execute upon them the judgment written — "this honour have all his saints." Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
