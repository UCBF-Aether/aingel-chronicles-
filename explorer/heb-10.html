The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hebrews 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect.

2 For then would they not have ceased to be offered? because that the worshippers once purged should have had no more conscience of sins.

3 But in those sacrifices there is a remembrance again made of sins every year.

4 For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins.

5 Wherefore when he cometh into the world, he saith, Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not, but a body hast thou prepared me:

6 In burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin thou hast had no pleasure.

7 Then said I, Lo, I come (in the volume of the book it is written of me,) to do thy will, O God.

8 Above when he said, Sacrifice and offering and burnt offerings and offering for sin thou wouldest not, neither hadst pleasure therein; which are offered by the law;

9 Then said he, Lo, I come to do thy will, O God. He taketh away the first, that he may establish the second.

10 By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.

11 And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins:

12 But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;

13 From henceforth expecting till his enemies be made his footstool.

14 For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.

15 Whereof the Holy Ghost also is a witness to us: for after that he had said before,

16 This is the covenant that I will make with them after those days, saith the Lord, I will put my laws into their hearts, and in their minds will I write them;

17 And their sins and iniquities will I remember no more.

18 Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin.

19 Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus,

20 By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;

21 And having an high priest over the house of God;

22 Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.

23 Let us hold fast the profession of our faith without wavering; (for he is faithful that promised;)

24 And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works:

25 Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.

26 For if we sin wilfully after that we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins,

27 But a certain fearful looking for of judgment and fiery indignation, which shall devour the adversaries.

28 He that despised Moses’ law died without mercy under two or three witnesses:

29 Of how much sorer punishment, suppose ye, shall he be thought worthy, who hath trodden under foot the Son of God, and hath counted the blood of the covenant, wherewith he was sanctified, an unholy thing, and hath done despite unto the Spirit of grace?

30 For we know him that hath said, Vengeance belongeth unto me, I will recompense, saith the Lord. And again, The Lord shall judge his people.

31 It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God.

32 But call to remembrance the former days, in which, after ye were illuminated, ye endured a great fight of afflictions;

33 Partly, whilst ye were made a gazingstock both by reproaches and afflictions; and partly, whilst ye became companions of them that were so used.

34 For ye had compassion of me in my bonds, and took joyfully the spoiling of your goods, knowing in yourselves that ye have in heaven a better and an enduring substance.

35 Cast not away therefore your confidence, which hath great recompence of reward.

36 For ye have need of patience, that, after ye have done the will of God, ye might receive the promise.

37 For yet a little while, and he that shall come will come, and will not tarry.

38 Now the just shall live by faith: but if any man draw back, my soul shall have no pleasure in him.

39 But we are not of them who draw back unto perdition; but of them that believe to the saving of the soul.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The law, a shadow of good things to come, could never by yearly sacrifices perfect the worshippers, the writer argues, since sacrifices repeated still left a remembrance of sins. But Christ, offering one sacrifice for sins forever, has perfected those being sanctified, and sins are remembered no more, so no further offering remains. Readers should therefore draw near, hold fast, stir one another to love and good works, and not forsake meeting together, enduring rather than shrinking back. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect — the tabernacle and Sinai ordinances were only emblems and types of the gospel, and sacrifices offered continually could not make worshippers perfect in pardon and purified conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. For then would they not have ceased to be offered? because that the worshippers once purged should have had no more conscience of sins — if the sacrifices had perfected, they would have ceased, for purged worshippers would have no more conscience of sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. But in those sacrifices there is a remembrance again made of sins every year — instead, those sacrifices made a remembrance of sins every year, proving they did not take them away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins — it is not possible that bulls' and goats' blood should take away sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Wherefore when he cometh into the world, he saith, Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not, but a body hast thou prepared me — when Christ came into the world, he declared that God would not have animal sacrifice and offering, but had prepared him a body for the true sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. In burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin thou hast had no pleasure — burnt offerings and sin sacrifices gave God no pleasure as ends in themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Then said I, Lo, I come (in the volume of the book it is written of me,) to do thy will, O God — Christ came, as written of him in the volume of the book, to do God's will; the fountain of all he has done for his people is God's sovereign will and grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Above when he said, Sacrifice and offering and burnt offerings and offering for sin thou wouldest not, neither hadst pleasure therein; which are offered by the law — he first sets aside the legal sacrifices, in which God had no pleasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Then said he, Lo, I come to do thy will, O God. He taketh away the first, that he may establish the second — then he establishes the second: he takes away the first covenant's sacrifices to establish God's will as the ground of salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all — by that will we are sanctified through the offering of Christ's body once for all; the atoning sacrifice had to be one capable of consenting, placing himself of his own will in the sinner's stead, and Christ did so, his free-will sufferings being of infinite value because the Sufferer is of infinite worth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins — every priest stood daily offering the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God — but this Man, after offering one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down at God's right hand, his work finished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. From henceforth expecting till his enemies be made his footstool — from henceforth he expects till his enemies are made his footstool. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified — by one offering he has perfected for ever those who are sanctified; the righteousness brought in and the sacrifice once offered are of eternal power, and his salvation shall never be done away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Whereof the Holy Ghost also is a witness to us: for after that he had said before — the Holy Ghost also witnesses to this new covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. This is the covenant that I will make with them after those days, saith the Lord, I will put my laws into their hearts, and in their minds will I write them — the covenant promise is the law written in hearts and minds, so that by it we may know we are justified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. And their sins and iniquities will I remember no more — and their sins and iniquities God will remember no more; under the new covenant full and final pardon is to be had, a vast difference from the old, where sacrifices were repeated and only pardon as to this world was obtained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin — where remission is, there is no more offering for sin; one Sacrifice suffices for all nations and ages, for spiritual pardon and freedom from punishment in the world to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus — believers therefore have boldness to enter the holiest by Jesus' blood, and having an open way to God's presence, it becomes them to use this privilege. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh — the way is new and living, consecrated through the veil, his flesh; our way to heaven is by a crucified Saviour, his death is to us the way of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. And having an high priest over the house of God — and having a High Priest over God's house, we have both access and an advocate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water — let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and bodies washed with pure water, baptism reminding us our conduct should be pure and holy; it would be contempt of Christ still to keep at a distance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Let us hold fast the profession of our faith without wavering; (for he is faithful that promised;) — let us hold fast our profession without wavering, for he who promised is faithful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works — believers are to consider how they can serve each other, especially stirring one another to more vigorous love and good works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching — not forsaking assembling together, but exhorting one another, so much the more as we see the day approaching; the communion of saints is a great help and means of stedfastness, and we should observe coming times of trial and be quickened to greater diligence, for a trying day is coming on all men, the day of death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. For if we sin wilfully after that we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins — the sin here is a total and final falling away, when men with full and fixed will despise and reject Christ, the only Saviour, resist the Spirit, the only Sanctifier, and renounce the gospel, the only way of salvation; for such wilful apostasy no other sacrifice remains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. But a certain fearful looking for of judgment and fiery indignation, which shall devour the adversaries — but only a fearful looking for judgment and fiery indignation to devour the adversaries; God gives some notorious sinners on earth a fearful foreboding of this in their consciences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. He that despised Moses' law died without mercy under two or three witnesses — under Moses' law, the despiser died without mercy on two or three witnesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Of how much sorer punishment, suppose ye, shall he be thought worthy, who hath trodden under foot the Son of God, and hath counted the blood of the covenant, wherewith he was sanctified, an unholy thing, and hath done despite unto the Spirit of grace? — how much sorer punishment for one who tramples the Son of God, counts the covenant blood unholy, and does despite to the Spirit of grace? What punishment can be sorer than to die without mercy? To die by mercy — by the mercy and grace despised; how dreadful when not only God's justice but his abused grace and mercy call for vengeance! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. For we know him that hath said, Vengeance belongeth unto me, I will recompense, saith the Lord. And again, The Lord shall judge his people — vengeance belongs to the Lord, and the Lord shall judge his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God — it is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God; yet this does not mean any soul sorrowing for sin will be shut out from mercy, or anyone willing to accept Christ's sacrifice refused — him that comes to Christ, he will in no wise cast out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. But call to remembrance the former days, in which, after ye were illuminated, ye endured a great fight of afflictions — call to remembrance former days, when after illumination you endured a great fight of afflictions; many and various afflictions united against the early Christians, and they had a great conflict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. Partly, whilst ye were made a gazingstock both by reproaches and afflictions; and partly, whilst ye became companions of them that were so used — partly made a gazingstock by reproaches and afflictions, partly companions of those so used; the Christian spirit is not selfish, but puts us upon pitying, visiting, helping, and pleading for others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. For ye had compassion of me in my bonds, and took joyfully the spoiling of your goods, knowing in yourselves that ye have in heaven a better and an enduring substance — you had compassion in bonds and took joyfully the spoiling of your goods, knowing you have in heaven a better and enduring substance; all things here are but shadows, but the saints' happiness in heaven lasts for ever, and enemies can never take it away as earthly goods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. Cast not away therefore your confidence, which hath great recompence of reward — cast not away therefore your confidence, which has great recompense of reward; this will make rich amends for all we may lose and suffer here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. For ye have need of patience, that, after ye have done the will of God, ye might receive the promise — you have need of patience, that after doing God's will you might receive the promise; the greatest part of the saints' happiness as yet is in promise, and it is a trial of patience to be content to live after work is done and stay for reward till God's time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. For yet a little while, and he that shall come will come, and will not tarry — for yet a little while, and he who shall come will come and will not tarry; he will soon come at death to end all sufferings and give a crown of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. Now the just shall live by faith: but if any man draw back, my soul shall have no pleasure in him — now the just shall live by faith, but if any draw back, God's soul has no pleasure in him; God is never pleased with formal profession and outward duties from those who do not persevere, but beholds them with great displeasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. But we are not of them who draw back unto perdition; but of them that believe to the saving of the soul — but we are not of those who draw back to perdition, but of those who believe to the saving of the soul; those kept faithful in great trials in the past have reason to hope for the same grace to live by faith still, till they receive the end of faith and patience, the salvation of their souls; living by faith and dying in faith, our souls are safe for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
