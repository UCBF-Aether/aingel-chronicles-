The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ephesians 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And you hath he quickened, who were dead in trespasses and sins;

2 Wherein in time past ye walked according to the course of this world, according to the prince of the power of the air, the spirit that now worketh in the children of disobedience:

3 Among whom also we all had our conversation in times past in the lusts of our flesh, fulfilling the desires of the flesh and of the mind; and were by nature the children of wrath, even as others.

4 But God, who is rich in mercy, for his great love wherewith he loved us,

5 Even when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ, (by grace ye are saved;)

6 And hath raised us up together, and made us sit together in heavenly places in Christ Jesus:

7 That in the ages to come he might shew the exceeding riches of his grace in his kindness toward us through Christ Jesus.

8 For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God:

9 Not of works, lest any man should boast.

10 For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.

11 Wherefore remember, that ye being in time past Gentiles in the flesh, who are called Uncircumcision by that which is called the Circumcision in the flesh made by hands;

12 That at that time ye were without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope, and without God in the world:

13 But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.

14 For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us;

15 Having abolished in his flesh the enmity, even the law of commandments contained in ordinances; for to make in himself of twain one new man, so making peace;

16 And that he might reconcile both unto God in one body by the cross, having slain the enmity thereby:

17 And came and preached peace to you which were afar off, and to them that were nigh.

18 For through him we both have access by one Spirit unto the Father.

19 Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God;

20 And are built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone;

21 In whom all the building fitly framed together groweth unto an holy temple in the Lord:

22 In whom ye also are builded together for an habitation of God through the Spirit.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Readers once dead in sins have been made alive by grace through faith, Paul says, not by works, so no one can boast. Gentiles once far off, strangers to the covenants, have been brought near by Christ's blood, made one people with Jews, and built together into a dwelling for God, on the foundation of the apostles and prophets with Christ as cornerstone. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God has quickened believers who were dead in trespasses and sins, since sin is the death of the soul and a dead soul has no desire for spiritual pleasures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. In time past they walked according to the course of this world and the prince of the power of the air, a state of conformity to the world under Satan's rule in the hearts of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. All, whether prone to sensual or spiritual wickedness, once lived in the lusts of the flesh and were by nature children of disobedience and therefore children of wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. But God, who is rich in mercy, acted from his great love toward them, God's eternal love being the fountain from which all his mercies flow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Even when they were dead in sins, God quickened them together with Christ, and Henry stresses the parenthesis: by grace ye are saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. God has raised them up together and made them sit together in heavenly places in Christ Jesus, sanctified souls raised above this world by his grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He did this so that in ages to come he might show the exceeding riches of his grace in kindness toward them through Christ Jesus, encouraging later sinners to hope in the same grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Salvation is by grace through faith, and that not of themselves: it is the gift of God, faith itself and conversion both given, not produced by man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. It is not of works, lest any man should boast, since nothing done by us brings salvation about and all boasting is shut out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Believers are God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works which God ordained beforehand, that they should glorify God by good conversation and perseverance in holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. They are to remember that they were once Gentiles in the flesh, called Uncircumcision by those called the Circumcision, outsiders to Israel's privileges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. At that time they were without Christ, aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope and without God, a sad description from which no one can remove himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. But now in Christ Jesus those who were far off are made near by the blood of Christ, since salvation comes by his sufferings and death, and God is a help at hand to his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Christ himself is our peace, who has made both Jew and Gentile one and broken down the middle wall of partition between them, being the author, center, and substance of their peace with God and their union in one church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. In his flesh he abolished the enmity, the law of commandments in ordinances, to make in himself of two one new man, so making peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He did this to reconcile both to God in one body by the cross, having slain the enmity thereby, so that the cross ends the hostility it answers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. He came and preached peace to those afar off and to those near, since through his person, sacrifice, and mediation sinners may draw near to God as Father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. For through him both Jew and Gentile have access by one Spirit unto the Father, Christ purchasing leave to come and the Spirit giving a heart and strength to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Therefore Gentile believers are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God, the church being like a city and a house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. They are built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief cornerstone, the church founded on the doctrine of Christ delivered by prophets and apostles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. In Christ the whole building, fitly framed together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord, each converted sinner becoming part of God's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. In him they also are built together for a habitation of God through the Spirit, so believers should ask whether they are devoted as holy temples and habitations of God by the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • quickened. made alive, here raised from being dead in sins (verses 1, 5) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
  • conversation. in this older wording, conduct or way of life (verses 2–3) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
