Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter is best read as a meditation on martyrdom and resurrection rather than a continuous narrative: verse 1 is a fragment, “And as for them —”, and verses 5 and 9 break off mid-sentence. The sons of the Meqabyan, it says, gave their souls to the fire — the text itself glosses this as martyrdom — that they might be saved from the fire of heaven, knowing this world passes away and that God’s wrath is more grievous than any king’s. One day in the joy of Paradise, it says, is reckoned more than a thousand years of life on earth. Trusting in God, they refused the polluted sacrifice of liḥa and gave their flesh as wax for God’s sake, believing the dead shall rise, in soul and flesh, and that judgment follows resurrection — while those who know not and believe not the resurrection are set against them. The martyrs, the chapter says, shall reign in the world to come for generation to generation, where there is no sickness, sorrow, or death, and shall shine sevenfold more than the sun at the resurrection of all.
