The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, when king Hezekiah heard it, that he rent his clothes, and covered himself with sackcloth, and went into the house of the LORD.

2 And he sent Eliakim, which was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and the elders of the priests, covered with sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet the son of Amoz.

3 And they said unto him, Thus saith Hezekiah, This day is a day of trouble, and of rebuke, and blasphemy; for the children are come to the birth, and there is not strength to bring forth.

4 It may be the LORD thy God will hear all the words of Rabshakeh, whom the king of Assyria his master hath sent to reproach the living God; and will reprove the words which the LORD thy God hath heard: wherefore lift up thy prayer for the remnant that are left.

5 So the servants of king Hezekiah came to Isaiah.

6 And Isaiah said unto them, Thus shall ye say to your master, Thus saith the LORD, Be not afraid of the words which thou hast heard, with which the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed me.

7 Behold, I will send a blast upon him, and he shall hear a rumour, and shall return to his own land; and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land.

8 So Rabshakeh returned, and found the king of Assyria warring against Libnah: for he had heard that he was departed from Lachish.

9 And when he heard say of Tirhakah king of Ethiopia, Behold, he is come out to fight against thee: he sent messengers again unto Hezekiah, saying,

10 Thus shall ye speak to Hezekiah king of Judah, saying, Let not thy God in whom thou trustest deceive thee, saying, Jerusalem shall not be delivered into the hand of the king of Assyria.

11 Behold, thou hast heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all lands, by destroying them utterly: and shalt thou be delivered?

12 Have the gods of the nations delivered them which my fathers have destroyed; as Gozan, and Haran, and Rezeph, and the children of Eden which were in Thelasar?

13 Where is the king of Hamath, and the king of Arpad, and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, of Hena, and Ivah?

14 And Hezekiah received the letter of the hand of the messengers, and read it: and Hezekiah went up into the house of the LORD, and spread it before the LORD.

15 And Hezekiah prayed before the LORD, and said, O LORD God of Israel, which dwellest between the cherubims, thou art the God, even thou alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth; thou hast made heaven and earth.

16 LORD, bow down thine ear, and hear: open, LORD, thine eyes, and see: and hear the words of Sennacherib, which hath sent him to reproach the living God.

17 Of a truth, LORD, the kings of Assyria have destroyed the nations and their lands,

18 And have cast their gods into the fire: for they were no gods, but the work of men’s hands, wood and stone: therefore they have destroyed them.

19 Now therefore, O LORD our God, I beseech thee, save thou us out of his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that thou art the LORD God, even thou only.

20 Then Isaiah the son of Amoz sent to Hezekiah, saying, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, That which thou hast prayed to me against Sennacherib king of Assyria I have heard.

21 This is the word that the LORD hath spoken concerning him; The virgin the daughter of Zion hath despised thee, and laughed thee to scorn; the daughter of Jerusalem hath shaken her head at thee.

22 Whom hast thou reproached and blasphemed? and against whom hast thou exalted thy voice, and lifted up thine eyes on high? even against the Holy One of Israel.

23 By thy messengers thou hast reproached the LORD, and hast said, With the multitude of my chariots I am come up to the height of the mountains, to the sides of Lebanon, and will cut down the tall cedar trees thereof, and the choice fir trees thereof: and I will enter into the lodgings of his borders, and into the forest of his Carmel.

24 I have digged and drunk strange waters, and with the sole of my feet have I dried up all the rivers of besieged places.

25 Hast thou not heard long ago how I have done it, and of ancient times that I have formed it? now have I brought it to pass, that thou shouldest be to lay waste fenced cities into ruinous heaps.

26 Therefore their inhabitants were of small power, they were dismayed and confounded; they were as the grass of the field, and as the green herb, as the grass on the house tops, and as corn blasted before it be grown up.

27 But I know thy abode, and thy going out, and thy coming in, and thy rage against me.

28 Because thy rage against me and thy tumult is come up into mine ears, therefore I will put my hook in thy nose, and my bridle in thy lips, and I will turn thee back by the way by which thou camest.

29 And this shall be a sign unto thee, Ye shall eat this year such things as grow of themselves, and in the second year that which springeth of the same; and in the third year sow ye, and reap, and plant vineyards, and eat the fruits thereof.

30 And the remnant that is escaped of the house of Judah shall yet again take root downward, and bear fruit upward.

31 For out of Jerusalem shall go forth a remnant, and they that escape out of mount Zion: the zeal of the LORD of hosts shall do this.

32 Therefore thus saith the LORD concerning the king of Assyria, He shall not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shield, nor cast a bank against it.

33 By the way that he came, by the same shall he return, and shall not come into this city, saith the LORD.

34 For I will defend this city, to save it, for mine own sake, and for my servant David’s sake.

35 And it came to pass that night, that the angel of the LORD went out, and smote in the camp of the Assyrians an hundred fourscore and five thousand: and when they arose early in the morning, behold, they were all dead corpses.

36 So Sennacherib king of Assyria departed, and went and returned, and dwelt at Nineveh.

37 And it came to pass, as he was worshipping in the house of Nisroch his god, that Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons smote him with the sword: and they escaped into the land of Armenia. And Esarhaddon his son reigned in his stead.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Hezekiah takes the Assyrian threats to the temple and spreads the letter before the LORD; Isaiah promises deliverance, and in the night the angel of the LORD strikes a hundred and eighty-five thousand in the Assyrian camp. Sennacherib withdraws to Nineveh, where his sons murder him. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When Hezekiah heard Rabshakeh's words, he rent his clothes, covered himself with sackcloth, and went into the LORD's house. JFB explains the rending expressed horror at the daring blasphemy, the sackcloth his mental distress, and his entrance into the temple the refuge of a pious man in affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Hezekiah sent Eliakim over the household, Shebna the scribe, and the elders of the priests, covered with sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet. JFB notes forwarding an account of the Assyrian's speech to Isaiah was to obtain the prophet's counsel and comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They told Isaiah that Hezekiah said this was a day of trouble, rebuke, and blasphemy, for the children were come to the birth with no strength to bring forth. JFB explains this figure described the kingdom's desperate condition and its inability to help itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They hoped the LORD his God would hear Rabshakeh's words, sent to reproach the living God, and would reprove them, so Isaiah should lift up prayer for the remnant left. JFB notes "the living God" is significant against the senseless deities Rabshakeh boasted could not resist Assyria, and the message intimated hope that this blasphemous defiance might lead to direct interposition for God's honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. So Hezekiah's servants came to Isaiah. JFB's section "Hezekiah in deep affliction" carries this embassy to the prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Isaiah told them to say to their master, "Thus saith the LORD, Be not afraid of the words thou hast heard, with which the servants of Assyria's king have blasphemed me." JFB calls the prophet's answer most cheering, holding out speedy deliverance from the invader. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The LORD would send a blast upon Sennacherib, who would hear a rumour, return to his own land, and fall by the sword there. JFB notes this brief prediction was soon fulfilled in all three particulars: the alarm hastening his retreat, the destruction of his army, and the violent death ending his career. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Rabshakeh returned and found Assyria's king warring against Libnah, having heard he had departed from Lachish. JFB notes whether Lachish had fallen is not said, but Sennacherib had transferred his battering-rams against the neighbouring fortress of Libnah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. When Sennacherib heard that Tirhakah king of Ethiopia had come out to fight him, he sent messengers to Hezekiah. JFB identifies this as the "rumour" Isaiah had foretold, noting Tirhakah reigned in Upper Egypt while So ruled Lower Egypt, and rage at Hezekiah's Egyptian alliance, or hope of meeting the southern attack, moved Sennacherib to send a menacing letter forcing immediate surrender. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The messengers were to tell Hezekiah not to let his God, in whom he trusted, deceive him by saying Jerusalem would not be delivered to Assyria's king. JFB notes the letter, couched in the same vaunting style as Rabshakeh's speech, exceeded it in blasphemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The letter reminded Hezekiah what Assyria's kings had done to all lands, destroying them utterly, and asked how he could be delivered. JFB notes the letter contained a larger enumeration of conquered places, to terrify Hezekiah and show the hopelessness of resistance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. It asked whether the gods of the nations his fathers destroyed — Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and Eden's children in Telassar — had delivered them. JFB presents this catalogue as the letter's terrifying enumeration of conquered places. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. It asked where were the kings of Hamath, Arpad, Sepharvaim's city, Hena, and Ivah. JFB notes this further enumeration aimed to show the utter hopelessness of all attempts at resistance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Hezekiah received the letter, read it, went up into the LORD's house, and spread it before the LORD. JFB explains that after reading it, Hezekiah hastened into the temple and spread it in childlike confidence of faith before the LORD, as containing taunts deeply affecting the divine honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Hezekiah prayed, "O LORD God of Israel, which dwellest between the cherubims, thou art the God, even thou alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth; thou hast made heaven and earth." JFB praises the prayer's recognition of God's majesty, strikingly contrasted with the Assyrian fancy of his merely local power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Hezekiah prayed the LORD to bow down his ear, open his eyes, and hear Sennacherib's words sent to reproach the living God. JFB notes the prayer implored deliverance from this proud defier of God and man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Hezekiah acknowledged that of a truth Assyria's kings had destroyed the nations and their lands. JFB notes the prayer acknowledged the conquests obtained over other lands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Hezekiah noted Assyria had cast the nations' gods into the fire, for they were no gods but the work of men's hands, wood and stone. JFB explains that, according to Assyrian practice, wooden idols were committed to the flames because their tutelary deities were no gods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Hezekiah besought the LORD to save them from Sennacherib's hand, that all earth's kingdoms might know the LORD alone was God. JFB notes the prayer's object was that all kingdoms might know the LORD was the only God, an attitude worthy of a pious theocratic king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Isaiah sent to Hezekiah saying the LORD God of Israel had heard his prayer against Sennacherib. JFB explains that a revelation having been made to Isaiah, the prophet announced the prayer was heard, in a message of three portions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The LORD's word against Sennacherib began: the virgin daughter of Zion had despised him and laughed him to scorn, shaking her head behind him. JFB describes this first portion as a highly poetical apostrophe to Sennacherib, descriptive of his turgid vanity, haughty pretensions, and presumptuous impiety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The LORD asked whom Sennacherib had reproached and blasphemed, and against whom he had exalted his voice and lifted up his eyes on high, even against the Holy One of Israel. JFB's description of the apostrophe covers this charge of impiety against the despot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. By his messengers Sennacherib had reproached the LORD, boasting he had come up with his multitude of chariots to the mountains' sides and Lebanon, cutting down its tall cedars and choice firs, and lodging in its utmost border. JFB presents these boasts as the haughty pretensions the apostrophe answers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Sennacherib boasted he had digged and drunk strange waters, and with his feet's sole dried up all the rivers of besieged places. JFB's account of the vaunting despot covers these claims of effortless conquest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The LORD answered that Sennacherib had surely heard long ago how God himself had done it and formed it in ancient times, and now had brought it to pass to lay waste fenced cities into ruinous heaps. JFB's exposition presents this as God's claim that Assyria was merely his instrument. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Therefore the nations' inhabitants were of small power, dismayed and confounded, like grass on housetops, blasted before it is grown. JFB's section on the apostrophe covers this picture of Assyria's victims as powerless before God's ordained judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The LORD declared he knew Sennacherib's abode, going out, coming in, and rage against him. JFB notes the apostrophe's strain of divine omniscience over the despot's movements. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Because Sennacherib's rage and tumult had come up into God's ears, God would put his hook in his nose, his bridle in his lips, and turn him back by the way he came. JFB's exposition of the apostrophe covers this humbling of the presumptuous invader. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. The sign to Hezekiah was that they would eat this year what grew of itself, the second year what sprang of the same, and the third year they would sow, reap, plant vineyards, and eat their fruit. JFB explains the second portion addressed to Hezekiah gave this sign: for two years the enemy would interrupt husbandry, but in the third year the people would till and reap as formerly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The remnant escaped of Judah's house would again take root downward and bear fruit upward. JFB's second portion covers this promise of the remnant's recovery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Out of Jerusalem would go forth a remnant, and out of Mount Zion those who escape, by the zeal of the LORD of hosts. JFB notes this remnant promise formed part of the sign of deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The LORD declared Assyria's king would not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shield, nor cast a bank against it. JFB's third portion announced the invasion's issue, and its note explains Sennacherib would be permitted none of ancient siege warfare's principal modes: no arrow even from the most powerful engine, no fence or mantelet, no raised bank overtopping the walls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. By the way Sennacherib came, by the same he would return, and not come into the city, said the LORD. JFB notes the army under Rabshakeh encamped at a little distance intending blockade, but delayed laying siege, waiting for the king's detachment, and only divine interposition could have saved Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. The LORD would defend this city to save it, for his own sake and for his servant David's sake. JFB's third portion closes with this promise of defence, confirmed, as Rawlinson noted, in its most important features by Sennacherib's own Annals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. That night the angel of the LORD went out and smote in the Assyrian camp a hundred fourscore and five thousand, and in the morning they were all dead corpses. JFB calls this the miraculous interposition of the Almighty defending Jerusalem, most probably effected by a hot south wind, the simoon, which envelops and destroys whole caravans, the camp's discipline being relaxed in full security. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. Sennacherib king of Assyria departed, went and returned, and dwelt at Nineveh. JFB notes he returned the same way he came, and his dwelling at Nineveh implies a considerable period, his Annals carrying his history at least five years after the disastrous campaign, recording victories alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. As Sennacherib worshipped in the house of Nisroch his god, Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons smote him with the sword, and they escaped to Armenia, while Esarhaddon his son reigned in his stead. JFB identifies Nisroch with Asshur, head of the Assyrian pantheon, represented as a winged figure in a circle, and notes Sennacherib's savage cruelty at last led his two sons, whom he intended to sacrifice, to assassinate him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

JFB explains the second portion addressed to Hezekiah gave this sign: for two years the enemy would interrupt husbandry, but in the third year the people would till and reap as formerly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The remnant escaped of Judah's house would again take root downward and bear fruit upward. JFB's second portion covers this promise of the remnant's recovery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Out of Jerusalem would go forth a remnant, and out of Mount Zion those who escape, by the zeal of the LORD of hosts. JFB notes this remnant promise formed part of the sign of deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The LORD declared Assyria's king would not come into this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with shield, nor cast a bank against it. JFB's third portion announced the invasion's issue, and its note explains Sennacherib would be permitted none of ancient siege warfare's principal modes: no arrow even from the most powerful engine, no fence or mantelet, no raised bank overtopping the walls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. By the way Sennacherib came, by the same he would return, and not come into the city, said the LORD. JFB notes the army under Rabshakeh encamped at a little distance intending blockade, but delayed laying siege, waiting for the king's detachment, and only divine interposition could have saved Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. The LORD would defend this city to save it, for his own sake and for his servant David's sake. JFB's third portion closes with this promise of defence, confirmed, as Rawlinson noted, in its most important features by Sennacherib's own Annals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. That night the angel of the LORD went out and smote in the Assyrian camp a hundred fourscore and five thousand, and in the morning they were all dead corpses. JFB calls this the miraculous interposition of the Almighty defending Jerusalem, most probably effected by a hot south wind, the simoon, which envelops and destroys whole caravans, the camp's discipline being relaxed in full security. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. Sennacherib king of Assyria departed, went and returned, and dwelt at Nineveh. JFB notes he returned the same way he came, and his dwelling at Nineveh implies a considerable period, his Annals carrying his history at least five years after the disastrous campaign, recording victories alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. As Sennacherib worshipped in the house of Nisroch his god, Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons smote him with the sword, and they escaped to Armenia, while Esarhaddon his son reigned in his stead. JFB identifies Nisroch with Asshur, head of the Assyrian pantheon, represented as a winged figure in a circle, and notes Sennacherib's savage cruelty at last led his two sons, whom he intended to sacrifice, to assassinate him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
