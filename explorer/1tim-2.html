The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Timothy 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 I exhort therefore, that, first of all, supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks, be made for all men;

2 For kings, and for all that are in authority; that we may lead a quiet and peaceable life in all godliness and honesty.

3 For this is good and acceptable in the sight of God our Saviour;

4 Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth.

5 For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;

6 Who gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time.

7 Whereunto I am ordained a preacher, and an apostle, (I speak the truth in Christ, and lie not;) a teacher of the Gentiles in faith and verity.

8 I will therefore that men pray every where, lifting up holy hands, without wrath and doubting.

9 In like manner also, that women adorn themselves in modest apparel, with shamefacedness and sobriety; not with broided hair, or gold, or pearls, or costly array;

10 But (which becometh women professing godliness) with good works.

11 Let the woman learn in silence with all subjection.

12 But I suffer not a woman to teach, nor to usurp authority over the man, but to be in silence.

13 For Adam was first formed, then Eve.

14 And Adam was not deceived, but the woman being deceived was in the transgression.

15 Notwithstanding she shall be saved in childbearing, if they continue in faith and charity and holiness with sobriety.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

First of all, Paul says, prayers should be made for all people, including kings and those in authority, so that believers may live quiet and godly lives, for God desires all to be saved and come to knowledge of the truth. There is one God and one mediator, Christ Jesus, who gave himself as a ransom for all, and the chapter gives instructions on prayer and on women's adornment, learning and conduct. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul exhorts that supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings be made for all men, and Henry notes that Christ's disciples must be praying people without distinction of nation, sect, rank, or party. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Prayer is made especially for kings and all in authority, so that believers may lead a quiet and peaceable life in godliness and honesty, which Henry sums up as the whole of Christian duty: right worship of God and good conduct toward men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Such prayer is good and acceptable in the sight of God our Saviour, and Henry urges us to abound in whatever is acceptable to him rather than treating it as optional. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. God will have all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth, and Henry insists that knowledge of the truth is God's appointed way to save sinners, since we cannot be ruled by a truth we do not know. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. There is one God and one Mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, and Henry notes that sin made the quarrel between us and God, and Christ the Mediator makes the peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Christ gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time, and Henry explains that in Old Testament times his sufferings and following glory were spoken of as things to be revealed in the last times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Paul was ordained a preacher, apostle, and teacher of the Gentiles in faith and truth, and Henry notes this appointment was for Jews and Gentiles of every nation, that all who are willing may come to the mercy-seat of a pardoning God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Men must pray everywhere, lifting up holy hands without wrath and doubting, and Henry applies this widely: in closets, families, at meals, on journeys, and in solemn assemblies, always in charity and in faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Women likewise are to adorn themselves in modest apparel with shamefacedness and sobriety, not with braided hair, gold, pearls, or costly array, and Henry notes that modesty and neatness are more to be consulted than elegance and fashion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Their proper ornament is good works, as becomes women professing godliness, and Henry calls good works the best ornament, of great price in God's sight, worth more time and money than decorating selves and children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Women are to learn in silence with all subjection, and Henry explains that learning itself is not excused: they must learn what is necessary to salvation, though not usurp authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Paul does not permit a woman to teach or usurp authority over the man, but to be in silence, and Henry reasons that teaching is an office of authority, while good women may and ought to teach their children at home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The reason given is creation order: Adam was first formed, then Eve, and Henry notes that as woman was last in creation, which is one reason for her subjection, so she was first in transgression. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Adam was not deceived, but the woman being deceived was in the transgression, and Henry keeps both halves of Paul's argument together: order in creation and priority in the fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Yet there is comfort: she shall be saved in childbearing if they continue in faith, charity, holiness, and sobriety, and Henry reads this as salvation with childbearing, by the Messiah who was born of a woman, with the sorrows of that sex calling men to exercise authority with gentleness and affection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
