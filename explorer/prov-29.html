The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 29 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 He, that being often reproved hardeneth his neck, shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy.

2 When the righteous are in authority, the people rejoice: but when the wicked beareth rule, the people mourn.

3 Whoso loveth wisdom rejoiceth his father: but he that keepeth company with harlots spendeth his substance.

4 The king by judgment establisheth the land: but he that receiveth gifts overthroweth it.

5 A man that flattereth his neighbour spreadeth a net for his feet.

6 In the transgression of an evil man there is a snare: but the righteous doth sing and rejoice.

7 The righteous considereth the cause of the poor: but the wicked regardeth not to know it.

8 Scornful men bring a city into a snare: but wise men turn away wrath.

9 If a wise man contendeth with a foolish man, whether he rage or laugh, there is no rest.

10 The bloodthirsty hate the upright: but the just seek his soul.

11 A fool uttereth all his mind: but a wise man keepeth it in till afterwards.

12 If a ruler hearken to lies, all his servants are wicked.

13 The poor and the deceitful man meet together: the LORD lighteneth both their eyes.

14 The king that faithfully judgeth the poor, his throne shall be established for ever.

15 The rod and reproof give wisdom: but a child left to himself bringeth his mother to shame.

16 When the wicked are multiplied, transgression increaseth: but the righteous shall see their fall.

17 Correct thy son, and he shall give thee rest; yea, he shall give delight unto thy soul.

18 Where there is no vision, the people perish: but he that keepeth the law, happy is he.

19 A servant will not be corrected by words: for though he understand he will not answer.

20 Seest thou a man that is hasty in his words? there is more hope of a fool than of him.

21 He that delicately bringeth up his servant from a child shall have him become his son at the length.

22 An angry man stirreth up strife, and a furious man aboundeth in transgression.

23 A man’s pride shall bring him low: but honour shall uphold the humble in spirit.

24 Whoso is partner with a thief hateth his own soul: he heareth cursing, and bewrayeth it not.

25 The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.

26 Many seek the ruler’s favour; but every man’s judgment cometh from the LORD.

27 An unjust man is an abomination to the just: and he that is upright in the way is abomination to the wicked.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Reproof hardened ends suddenly without remedy, and the chapter follows authority through a land: when the righteous are in authority the people rejoice, when the wicked bear rule they mourn. The rod and reproof give wisdom, a child left to himself shames his mother, where there is no vision the people perish, pride brings a person low while honour upholds the humble, and the fear of man is a snare against trust in the LORD. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He who, being often reproved, hardens his neck shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy, for if God wounds, who can heal, and God's word warns all to flee from wrath to come to the hope set before us in Jesus Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. When the righteous are in authority, the people rejoice, but when the wicked bear rule, the people mourn, for the people have cause to rejoice or mourn as their rulers are righteous or wicked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Whoso loves wisdom rejoices his father, but he who keeps company with harlots spends his substance, for divine wisdom best keeps us from ruinous lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The king by judgment establishes the land, but he who receives gifts overthrows it, for the Lord Jesus is the King who will minister true judgment to the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. A man who flatters his neighbour spreads a net for his feet, for flatterers put men off their guard, which betrays them into foolish conduct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. In the transgression of an evil man there is a snare, but the righteous do sing and rejoice, for transgressions always end in vexations, while righteous men walk at liberty and in safety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The righteous consider the cause of the poor, but the wicked regard not to know it, for this verse applies to compassion for the poor's distress and the wicked's unfeeling disregard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Scornful men bring a city into a snare, but wise men turn away wrath, for the scornful mock at things sacred and serious, while men promoting religion, true wisdom, turn away God's wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. If a wise man contends with a foolish man, whether he rage or laugh, there is no rest, for if a wise man disputes with a conceited wrangler, he will be treated with anger or ridicule, and no good is done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The bloodthirsty hate the upright, but the just seek his soul, for Christ told his disciples they should be hated of all men, and the just, whom the bloodthirsty hate, gladly do anything for their salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. A fool utters all his mind, but a wise man keeps it in till afterwards, for he is a fool who tells everything he knows and can keep no counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. If a ruler hearkens to lies, all his servants are wicked, for one who loves flatterers and hearkens to slanderers causes his servants to become liars and false accusers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The poor and the deceitful man meet together, the LORD lightens both their eyes, for some are poor, others have deceitful riches, they meet in this world's business, the Lord gives both life's comforts, and to some of both sorts he gives his grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The king who faithfully judges the poor, his throne shall be established for ever, for the rich will look to themselves, but the poor and needy the prince must defend and plead for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The rod and reproof give wisdom, but a child left to himself brings his mother to shame, for parents must consider the benefit of due correction and the mischief of undue indulgence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. When the wicked are multiplied, transgression increases, but the righteous shall see their fall, for let not the righteous have faith and hope shocked by sin's increase, but let them wait with patience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Correct thy son, and he shall give thee rest, yea, he shall give delight unto thy soul, for children must not be suffered to go without rebuke when they do amiss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Where there is no vision, the people perish, but he who keeps the law, happy is he, for how bare does a place look without Bibles and ministers, and what an easy prey is it to the enemy of souls, while the gospel, an open vision holding forth Christ, humbling the sinner and exalting the Saviour, keeps the soul alive and prevents perishing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. A servant will not be corrected by words, for though he understand he will not answer, for here is an unprofitable, slothful, wicked servant, one serving not from conscience or love, but from fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Seest thou a man hasty in his words, there is more hope of a fool than of him, for when a man is self-conceited, rash, and given to wrangling, there is more hope of the ignorant and profligate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. He who delicately brings up his servant from a child shall have him become his son at the length, for good usage to a servant does not mean indulgence, which would ruin even a child, and the body, a servant to the soul, humoured and over-tenderly treated, will forget its place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. An angry man stirs up strife, and a furious man abounds in transgression, for an angry, passionate disposition makes men provoking to one another and provoking to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. A man's pride shall bring him low, but honour shall uphold the humble in spirit, for only those who humble themselves shall be exalted and established. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Whoso is partner with a thief hates his own soul, he hears cursing and bewrays it not, for the receiver is as bad as the thief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. The fear of man brings a snare, but whoso puts his trust in the LORD shall be safe, for many are ashamed to own Christ now, and he will not own them in judgment, but he who trusts in the Lord will be saved from this snare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Many seek the ruler's favour, but every man's judgment comes from the LORD, for the wisest course is to look to God and seek the favour of the Ruler of rulers, since every creature is to us what God makes it to be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. An unjust man is an abomination to the just, and he who is upright in the way is abomination to the wicked, for the just man abhors the wicked's sins and shuns their company, while all unholy have rooted hatred to godliness, and hatred to sin in ourselves and others is a needful branch of the Christian temper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
