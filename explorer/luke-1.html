The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Forasmuch as many have taken in hand to set forth in order a declaration of those things which are most surely believed among us,

2 Even as they delivered them unto us, which from the beginning were eyewitnesses, and ministers of the word;

3 It seemed good to me also, having had perfect understanding of all things from the very first, to write unto thee in order, most excellent Theophilus,

4 That thou mightest know the certainty of those things, wherein thou hast been instructed.

5 THERE was in the days of Herod, the king of Judaea, a certain priest named Zacharias, of the course of Abia: and his wife was of the daughters of Aaron, and her name was Elisabeth.

6 And they were both righteous before God, walking in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless.

7 And they had no child, because that Elisabeth was barren, and they both were now well stricken in years.

8 And it came to pass, that while he executed the priest’s office before God in the order of his course,

9 According to the custom of the priest’s office, his lot was to burn incense when he went into the temple of the Lord.

10 And the whole multitude of the people were praying without at the time of incense.

11 And there appeared unto him an angel of the Lord standing on the right side of the altar of incense.

12 And when Zacharias saw him, he was troubled, and fear fell upon him.

13 But the angel said unto him, Fear not, Zacharias: for thy prayer is heard; and thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son, and thou shalt call his name John.

14 And thou shalt have joy and gladness; and many shall rejoice at his birth.

15 For he shall be great in the sight of the Lord, and shall drink neither wine nor strong drink; and he shall be filled with the Holy Ghost, even from his mother’s womb.

16 And many of the children of Israel shall he turn to the Lord their God.

17 And he shall go before him in the spirit and power of Elias, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just; to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.

18 And Zacharias said unto the angel, Whereby shall I know this? for I am an old man, and my wife well stricken in years.

19 And the angel answering said unto him, I am Gabriel, that stand in the presence of God; and am sent to speak unto thee, and to shew thee these glad tidings.

20 And, behold, thou shalt be dumb, and not able to speak, until the day that these things shall be performed, because thou believest not my words, which shall be fulfilled in their season.

21 And the people waited for Zacharias, and marvelled that he tarried so long in the temple.

22 And when he came out, he could not speak unto them: and they perceived that he had seen a vision in the temple: for he beckoned unto them, and remained speechless.

23 And it came to pass, that, as soon as the days of his ministration were accomplished, he departed to his own house.

24 And after those days his wife Elisabeth conceived, and hid herself five months, saying,

25 Thus hath the Lord dealt with me in the days wherein he looked on me, to take away my reproach among men.

26 And in the sixth month the angel Gabriel was sent from God unto a city of Galilee, named Nazareth,

27 To a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the virgin’s name was Mary.

28 And the angel came in unto her, and said, Hail, thou that art highly favoured, the Lord is with thee: blessed art thou among women.

29 And when she saw him, she was troubled at his saying, and cast in her mind what manner of salutation this should be.

30 And the angel said unto her, Fear not, Mary: for thou hast found favour with God.

31 And, behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son, and shalt call his name JESUS.

32 He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David:

33 And he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.

34 Then said Mary unto the angel, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?

35 And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God.

36 And, behold, thy cousin Elisabeth, she hath also conceived a son in her old age: and this is the sixth month with her, who was called barren.

37 For with God nothing shall be impossible.

38 And Mary said, Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to thy word. And the angel departed from her.

39 And Mary arose in those days, and went into the hill country with haste, into a city of Juda;

40 And entered into the house of Zacharias, and saluted Elisabeth.

41 And it came to pass, that, when Elisabeth heard the salutation of Mary, the babe leaped in her womb; and Elisabeth was filled with the Holy Ghost:

42 And she spake out with a loud voice, and said, Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb.

43 And whence is this to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?

44 For, lo, as soon as the voice of thy salutation sounded in mine ears, the babe leaped in my womb for joy.

45 And blessed is she that believed: for there shall be a performance of those things which were told her from the Lord.

46 And Mary said, My soul doth magnify the Lord,

47 And my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour.

48 For he hath regarded the low estate of his handmaiden: for, behold, from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed.

49 For he that is mighty hath done to me great things; and holy is his name.

50 And his mercy is on them that fear him from generation to generation.

51 He hath shewed strength with his arm; he hath scattered the proud in the imagination of their hearts.

52 He hath put down the mighty from their seats, and exalted them of low degree.

53 He hath filled the hungry with good things; and the rich he hath sent empty away.

54 He hath holpen his servant Israel, in remembrance of his mercy;

55 As he spake to our fathers, to Abraham, and to his seed for ever.

56 And Mary abode with her about three months, and returned to her own house.

57 Now Elisabeth’s full time came that she should be delivered; and she brought forth a son.

58 And her neighbours and her cousins heard how the Lord had shewed great mercy upon her; and they rejoiced with her.

59 And it came to pass, that on the eighth day they came to circumcise the child; and they called him Zacharias, after the name of his father.

60 And his mother answered and said, Not so; but he shall be called John.

61 And they said unto her, There is none of thy kindred that is called by this name.

62 And they made signs to his father, how he would have him called.

63 And he asked for a writing table, and wrote, saying, His name is John. And they marvelled all.

64 And his mouth was opened immediately, and his tongue loosed, and he spake, and praised God.

65 And fear came on all that dwelt round about them: and all these sayings were noised abroad throughout all the hill country of Judaea.

66 And all they that heard them laid them up in their hearts, saying, What manner of child shall this be! And the hand of the Lord was with him.

67 And his father Zacharias was filled with the Holy Ghost, and prophesied, saying,

68 Blessed be the Lord God of Israel; for he hath visited and redeemed his people,

69 And hath raised up an horn of salvation for us in the house of his servant David;

70 As he spake by the mouth of his holy prophets, which have been since the world began:

71 That we should be saved from our enemies, and from the hand of all that hate us;

72 To perform the mercy promised to our fathers, and to remember his holy covenant;

73 The oath which he sware to our father Abraham,

74 That he would grant unto us, that we being delivered out of the hand of our enemies might serve him without fear,

75 In holiness and righteousness before him, all the days of our life.

76 And thou, child, shalt be called the prophet of the Highest: for thou shalt go before the face of the Lord to prepare his ways;

77 To give knowledge of salvation unto his people by the remission of their sins,

78 Through the tender mercy of our God; whereby the dayspring from on high hath visited us,

79 To give light to them that sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace.

80 And the child grew, and waxed strong in spirit, and was in the deserts till the day of his shewing unto Israel.

How to read the labels

  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Luke opens with a careful prologue: he has traced everything from the first, he says, so that Theophilus may know the certainty of what he has been taught (vv. 1–4). Two births are then announced by the angel Gabriel — John to the aged priest Zechariah and his wife Elisabeth, and Jesus to the virgin Mary — with Zechariah struck dumb for disbelief (vv. 5–38). Mary visits Elisabeth, and her song of praise, the Magnificat, is followed by John’s birth, Zechariah’s recovered speech, and his prophecy over the child who will prepare the Lord’s way (vv. 39–80). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 1

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Luke opens by noting that many had already drawn up accounts of the gospel events. Henry stresses that Luke will not write about matters Christians may safely differ over, but about things surely believed; JFB adds that these earlier narratives rested on eyewitness testimony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The earlier accounts came from those who were eyewitnesses from the beginning and ministers of the word. JFB notes that "from the beginning" here points to the start of Christ's public ministry, as the following narrative makes plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Luke claims exact knowledge traced from the very first, and writes in order to Theophilus. JFB observes that this claim to trace all things from their earliest rise is a virtual claim that his Gospel should supersede the many partial narratives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The purpose is certainty: Theophilus, already instructed, should know the sureness of what he was taught. Henry notes the doctrine of Christ is what the wisest and best have ventured their souls upon with confidence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The story begins under Herod with Zacharias, a priest of the course of Abia, and Elisabeth, herself of Aaron's line. Henry notes both were sinners saved like all others, yet eminent for piety and integrity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Both husband and wife are described as righteous before God, walking blamelessly in his commandments and ordinances. Henry's point is that their piety was real and obedient, not mere reputation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Their blameless life was shadowed by childlessness, and both were now well advanced in years. Henry notes it could not be expected that Elisabeth should bear a child in her old age, which makes what follows pure mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Zacharias was executing the priest's office before God in the order of his course. The setting matters: this is ordinary, faithful temple service, not a staged occasion, when God breaks in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. By lot, according to priestly custom, Zacharias was chosen to burn incense in the temple. Henry connects the incense with the people's prayers offered without, acceptable only through Christ's intercession above. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. While Zacharias burned incense, the whole multitude prayed outside at that hour. Henry draws the lesson that we cannot expect an interest in Christ's intercession if we do not pray earnestly ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. An angel of the Lord appeared standing at the right side of the altar of incense. The place is fitting: the answer to long prayer comes while the priest stands at the very symbol of intercession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Zacharias was troubled and fear fell upon him at the sight. Henry's account treats this as the natural awe of a godly man confronted with a heavenly messenger, not as a rebuke in itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The angel's first word is reassurance: Zacharias's prayer is heard, and Elisabeth shall bear a son to be called John. Henry notes that prayers of faith are filed in heaven and not forgotten, even when answered late. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The child will bring joy and gladness, and many shall rejoice at his birth. Henry observes that mercies given in answer to prayer are doubly sweet, and this mercy will gladden more than one household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. John shall be great in the Lord's sight, abstaining from wine and strong drink, and filled with the Holy Ghost from his mother's womb. Henry sees in him a man set apart from earthly pleasures for a holy work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Many of Israel shall John turn to the Lord their God. Henry stresses that John will be instrumental in converting many souls to God and preparing them to receive the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. John will go before the Lord in the spirit and power of Elijah, turning fathers' hearts to children and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just. Henry explains this as bringing the rebellious to attend to the wisdom of the Just One who was coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Zacharias answers with unbelief, asking how he shall know this, since he and his wife are old. Henry is blunt: his unbelief spoke, objecting against God's word even after hearing the angel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The angel names himself Gabriel, who stands in God's presence and was sent to speak these glad tidings. The name and standing underline that this is no private fancy but a message from God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Because Zacharias did not believe, he shall be dumb until the things are performed in their season. Henry reads this as both just and kind: just, for objecting against God's word, and kind, since it stopped further distrustful words and confirmed his faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The people waited and marvelled that Zacharias tarried so long in the temple. The delay itself becomes a sign that something out of the ordinary has happened within. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Coming out unable to speak, Zacharias beckoned, and the people perceived he had seen a vision. Henry notes that even real believers can dishonour God by unbelief, and then find their mouths stopped in silence and confusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. As soon as the days of his ministration were accomplished, Zacharias departed to his own house. Duty done, he carries the silent sign home with him, waiting for God's word to ripen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. After those days Elisabeth conceived and hid herself five months. Henry's theme is God's gracious regard: he has looked on his people with compassion, and deals with them accordingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Elisabeth reads her conception as the Lord taking away her reproach among men. Henry closes the section with the lesson to observe God's gracious dealings and mark his compassion in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. In the sixth month Gabriel was sent from God to Nazareth in Galilee. The same messenger who announced John now announces one far greater, and the lowly town sets the tone of the whole story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The angel comes to a virgin espoused to Joseph, of the house of David, whose name was Mary. Henry notes that Christ must be born miraculously, and David's line is named to mark the promised King. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Gabriel greets Mary as highly favoured, with the Lord present with her and blessing her among women. Henry explains the address as meaning she is especially chosen and favoured of the Most High, an honour Jewish mothers had long desired. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Mary was troubled at the salutation and pondered what manner of greeting it was. Henry notes the wondrous salutation and appearance troubled her, a modest response to an unlooked-for honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The angel reassures her: fear not, for she has found favour with God. The assurance comes before the announcement, as it did for Zacharias, because fear would otherwise drown the message. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Mary shall conceive and bear a son, and call his name Jesus. Henry dwells on the name: Jesus, a Saviour, sweet to speak and sweet to hear, refreshing the fainting spirits of humbled sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. He shall be great, called the Son of the Highest, and given the throne of his father David. Henry stresses that he is one in nature and perfection with the Lord God, not merely a favoured man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. He shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever, and of his kingdom there shall be no end. The everlasting kingdom answers every earthly throne's rise and fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Mary asks how this shall be, seeing she knows not a man. Henry contrasts this with Zacharias: her reply is the language of faith and humble admiration, and she asks no sign to confirm her faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The Holy Ghost shall come upon her and the power of the Highest overshadow her, so the child shall be called the Son of God. Henry calls this a great mystery, fit for one taken into union with the divine nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Elisabeth, called barren, has also conceived in her old age, now in her sixth month. The sign is given unasked: God is already doing in Mary's own family what nature calls impossible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. With God nothing shall be impossible. Henry makes this the rule for all conflicts: as we read and hear God's promises, we should turn them into prayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Mary answers as the Lord's handmaid: be it unto me according to thy word. Henry holds this up as guiding our desires by God's word, and the angel departs once consent is given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Mary arose and went with haste into the hill country, to a city of Judah. Henry notes it is very good for those in whom grace has begun to communicate with one another, and Mary does so at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Mary entered the house of Zacharias and saluted Elisabeth. The meeting of the two mothers is the meeting of the two promises, John's and Jesus'. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. At Mary's salutation the babe leaped in Elisabeth's womb, and Elisabeth was filled with the Holy Ghost. Henry notes Elisabeth was conscious of the approach of the mother of the great Redeemer, and spoke under the Spirit's influence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Elisabeth cries out that Mary is blessed among women, and blessed is the fruit of her womb. Henry reads this as declaring Mary and her expected child most blessed, as peculiarly honoured of and dear to the Most High. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Elisabeth wonders that the mother of her Lord should come to her. The wonder is humility: the greater honour comes to the lesser house, and she knows it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. As soon as Elisabeth heard Mary's voice, the babe leaped for joy. The unborn John already does his forerunner's work, rejoicing at the presence of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Blessed is she that believed, for there shall be a performance of what was told her from the Lord. Henry's contrast is plain: Mary believed where Zacharias doubted, and faith is here pronounced blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Mary answers, my soul doth magnify the Lord. Henry notes that, animated by Elisabeth's address and under the Holy Ghost, Mary broke out into joy, admiration, and gratitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Her spirit rejoices in God her Saviour. Henry stresses that Mary knew herself a sinner who needed a Saviour, and could rejoice in God only as interested in salvation through the promised Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. God has regarded the low estate of his handmaiden, so all generations shall call her blessed. The blessing rests on God's regard for lowliness, not on any claim of her own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. He that is mighty has done great things for her, and holy is his name. Mary's praise turns from her own case to the might and holiness of the God who acted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. His mercy is on them that fear him from generation to generation. The mercy Mary received is not a private exception but the standing way God deals with the fearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. He has showed strength with his arm and scattered the proud in the imagination of their hearts. God's arm reverses human pride at its very root, in proud imaginations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. He has put down the mighty from their seats and exalted them of low degree. The song's reversals are the gospel's pattern: the self-sufficient are sent away, the lowly lifted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. He has filled the hungry with good things, and sent the rich empty away. Henry applies it spiritually: those who see their need of Christ he fills with the best things, while the self-sufficient go empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. He has helped his servant Israel, in remembrance of his mercy. What God does for Mary is what he promised to his people: mercy remembered, not newly invented. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. As he spoke to the fathers, to Abraham and his seed for ever. The promise to Abraham is the anchor: this birth fulfils an old word, not a new idea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Mary abode with Elisabeth about three months, then returned to her own house. The visit ends quietly, with the two mothers parting to await their appointed days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 57. Elisabeth's full time came, and she brought forth a son. Henry notes the great joy among all the relations of the family at John's birth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 58. Neighbours and cousins heard how the Lord had showed great mercy to Elisabeth, and rejoiced with her. Mercy shared becomes joy shared, and the whole neighbourhood enters into it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 59. On the eighth day they came to circumcise the child, and called him Zacharias after his father. Custom and family expectation point one way; the parents' obedience will point another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 60. His mother answered, not so; he shall be called John. Elisabeth holds to the angel's word against the pressure of custom, naming the child by God's appointment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 61. They object that none of her kindred is called by this name. The objection shows how new God's way is: the name breaks family precedent because the child breaks ordinary expectation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 62. They made signs to his father, asking how he would have the child called. Zacharias, still dumb, is appealed to as the one whose silence has already spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 63. He asked for a writing table and wrote, His name is John, and they marvelled all. Henry notes the name means "Gracious," because John shall bring in the gospel wherein God's grace shines most bright. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 64. Immediately his mouth was opened, his tongue loosed, and he spoke and praised God. Henry's summary is memorable: unbelief closed his mouth, and believing opened it again; when God opens our lips, our mouths must show forth his praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 65. Fear came on all round about, and all these sayings were noised abroad through the hill country of Judaea. The private household wonder becomes public talk, preparing the country for what is coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 66. All who heard laid these things up in their hearts, asking what manner of child this shall be, for the hand of the Lord was with him. Henry urges us to observe God's dealings and wait the event, as they did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 67. Zacharias was filled with the Holy Ghost and prophesied. The dumb priest becomes a prophet: the same Spirit that sealed his mouth for unbelief now opens it for praise and prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 68. Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he has visited and redeemed his people. Henry reads the song as prophecy concerning the kingdom and salvation of the Messiah, now at hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 69. God has raised up a horn of salvation in the house of his servant David. The horn is strength and kingship: salvation comes through David's promised heir, not through a new dynasty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 70. As he spoke by the mouth of his holy prophets since the world began. The new salvation is the old promise, spoken all along by prophets, now arriving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 71. That we should be saved from our enemies and from all that hate us. Salvation here is deliverance with a purpose, not escape alone, as the following verses spell out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 72. To perform the mercy promised to the fathers and to remember his holy covenant. JFB notes there is not a word in this noble burst of song about Zacharias's own child: like Elisabeth, he loses sight of self in the glory of a Greater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 73. The oath which he swore to Abraham. The covenant is grounded in God's oath, so its fulfilment is as sure as God's own word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 74. That we, being delivered from our enemies, might serve him without fear. Deliverance is for service: freedom from enemies frees God's people for fearless worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 75. In holiness and righteousness before him all the days of our life. The service is lifelong and inward as well as outward, holiness and righteousness together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 76. And thou, child, shalt be called prophet of the Highest, going before the Lord to prepare his ways. Only now does the song turn to John, and his whole calling is to prepare the way for another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 77. To give knowledge of salvation to his people by the remission of their sins. John's preparation is not ceremony but knowledge: salvation known as forgiveness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 78. Through the tender mercy of our God, whereby the dayspring from on high has visited us. JFB explains the dayspring as Christ himself, the Sun of righteousness arising on a dark world, or the light he sheds; the sense is one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 79. To give light to them that sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace. Henry calls the gospel discovering, reviving, and directing: it shows what we were dark about, revives condemned prisoners, and guides feet into peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 80. The child grew and waxed strong in spirit, and was in the deserts till the day of his showing unto Israel. JFB pictures a concluding paragraph of grandeur, tracing John's bodily and mental growth in the wilderness, alone with God, until his public appearance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Matthew Henry

  • Verse 5. The story begins under Herod with Zacharias, a priest of the course of Abia, and Elisabeth, herself of Aaron's line. Henry notes both were sinners saved like all others, yet eminent for piety and integrity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Both husband and wife are described as righteous before God, walking blamelessly in his commandments and ordinances. Henry's point is that their piety was real and obedient, not mere reputation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Their blameless life was shadowed by childlessness, and both were now well advanced in years. Henry notes it could not be expected that Elisabeth should bear a child in her old age, which makes what follows pure mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Zacharias was executing the priest's office before God in the order of his course. The setting matters: this is ordinary, faithful temple service, not a staged occasion, when God breaks in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. By lot, according to priestly custom, Zacharias was chosen to burn incense in the temple. Henry connects the incense with the people's prayers offered without, acceptable only through Christ's intercession above. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. While Zacharias burned incense, the whole multitude prayed outside at that hour. Henry draws the lesson that we cannot expect an interest in Christ's intercession if we do not pray earnestly ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. An angel of the Lord appeared standing at the right side of the altar of incense. The place is fitting: the answer to long prayer comes while the priest stands at the very symbol of intercession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Zacharias was troubled and fear fell upon him at the sight. Henry's account treats this as the natural awe of a godly man confronted with a heavenly messenger, not as a rebuke in itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The angel's first word is reassurance: Zacharias's prayer is heard, and Elisabeth shall bear a son to be called John. Henry notes that prayers of faith are filed in heaven and not forgotten, even when answered late. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The child will bring joy and gladness, and many shall rejoice at his birth. Henry observes that mercies given in answer to prayer are doubly sweet, and this mercy will gladden more than one household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. John shall be great in the Lord's sight, abstaining from wine and strong drink, and filled with the Holy Ghost from his mother's womb. Henry sees in him a man set apart from earthly pleasures for a holy work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Many of Israel shall John turn to the Lord their God. Henry stresses that John will be instrumental in converting many souls to God and preparing them to receive the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. John will go before the Lord in the spirit and power of Elijah, turning fathers' hearts to children and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just. Henry explains this as bringing the rebellious to attend to the wisdom of the Just One who was coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Zacharias answers with unbelief, asking how he shall know this, since he and his wife are old. Henry is blunt: his unbelief spoke, objecting against God's word even after hearing the angel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The angel names himself Gabriel, who stands in God's presence and was sent to speak these glad tidings. The name and standing underline that this is no private fancy but a message from God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Because Zacharias did not believe, he shall be dumb until the things are performed in their season. Henry reads this as both just and kind: just, for objecting against God's word, and kind, since it stopped further distrustful words and confirmed his faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The people waited and marvelled that Zacharias tarried so long in the temple. The delay itself becomes a sign that something out of the ordinary has happened within. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Coming out unable to speak, Zacharias beckoned, and the people perceived he had seen a vision. Henry notes that even real believers can dishonour God by unbelief, and then find their mouths stopped in silence and confusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. As soon as the days of his ministration were accomplished, Zacharias departed to his own house. Duty done, he carries the silent sign home with him, waiting for God's word to ripen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. After those days Elisabeth conceived and hid herself five months. Henry's theme is God's gracious regard: he has looked on his people with compassion, and deals with them accordingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Elisabeth reads her conception as the Lord taking away her reproach among men. Henry closes the section with the lesson to observe God's gracious dealings and mark his compassion in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. In the sixth month Gabriel was sent from God to Nazareth in Galilee. The same messenger who announced John now announces one far greater, and the lowly town sets the tone of the whole story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The angel comes to a virgin espoused to Joseph, of the house of David, whose name was Mary. Henry notes that Christ must be born miraculously, and David's line is named to mark the promised King. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Gabriel greets Mary as highly favoured, with the Lord present with her and blessing her among women. Henry explains the address as meaning she is especially chosen and favoured of the Most High, an honour Jewish mothers had long desired. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Mary was troubled at the salutation and pondered what manner of greeting it was. Henry notes the wondrous salutation and appearance troubled her, a modest response to an unlooked-for honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The angel reassures her: fear not, for she has found favour with God. The assurance comes before the announcement, as it did for Zacharias, because fear would otherwise drown the message. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Mary shall conceive and bear a son, and call his name Jesus. Henry dwells on the name: Jesus, a Saviour, sweet to speak and sweet to hear, refreshing the fainting spirits of humbled sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. He shall be great, called the Son of the Highest, and given the throne of his father David. Henry stresses that he is one in nature and perfection with the Lord God, not merely a favoured man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. He shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever, and of his kingdom there shall be no end. The everlasting kingdom answers every earthly throne's rise and fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Mary asks how this shall be, seeing she knows not a man. Henry contrasts this with Zacharias: her reply is the language of faith and humble admiration, and she asks no sign to confirm her faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The Holy Ghost shall come upon her and the power of the Highest overshadow her, so the child shall be called the Son of God. Henry calls this a great mystery, fit for one taken into union with the divine nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Elisabeth, called barren, has also conceived in her old age, now in her sixth month. The sign is given unasked: God is already doing in Mary's own family what nature calls impossible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. With God nothing shall be impossible. Henry makes this the rule for all conflicts: as we read and hear God's promises, we should turn them into prayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Mary answers as the Lord's handmaid: be it unto me according to thy word. Henry holds this up as guiding our desires by God's word, and the angel departs once consent is given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Mary arose and went with haste into the hill country, to a city of Judah. Henry notes it is very good for those in whom grace has begun to communicate with one another, and Mary does so at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Mary entered the house of Zacharias and saluted Elisabeth. The meeting of the two mothers is the meeting of the two promises, John's and Jesus'. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. At Mary's salutation the babe leaped in Elisabeth's womb, and Elisabeth was filled with the Holy Ghost. Henry notes Elisabeth was conscious of the approach of the mother of the great Redeemer, and spoke under the Spirit's influence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Elisabeth cries out that Mary is blessed among women, and blessed is the fruit of her womb. Henry reads this as declaring Mary and her expected child most blessed, as peculiarly honoured of and dear to the Most High. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Elisabeth wonders that the mother of her Lord should come to her. The wonder is humility: the greater honour comes to the lesser house, and she knows it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. As soon as Elisabeth heard Mary's voice, the babe leaped for joy. The unborn John already does his forerunner's work, rejoicing at the presence of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Blessed is she that believed, for there shall be a performance of what was told her from the Lord. Henry's contrast is plain: Mary believed where Zacharias doubted, and faith is here pronounced blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Mary answers, my soul doth magnify the Lord. Henry notes that, animated by Elisabeth's address and under the Holy Ghost, Mary broke out into joy, admiration, and gratitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Her spirit rejoices in God her Saviour. Henry stresses that Mary knew herself a sinner who needed a Saviour, and could rejoice in God only as interested in salvation through the promised Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. God has regarded the low estate of his handmaiden, so all generations shall call her blessed. The blessing rests on God's regard for lowliness, not on any claim of her own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. He that is mighty has done great things for her, and holy is his name. Mary's praise turns from her own case to the might and holiness of the God who acted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. His mercy is on them that fear him from generation to generation. The mercy Mary received is not a private exception but the standing way God deals with the fearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. He has showed strength with his arm and scattered the proud in the imagination of their hearts. God's arm reverses human pride at its very root, in proud imaginations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. He has put down the mighty from their seats and exalted them of low degree. The song's reversals are the gospel's pattern: the self-sufficient are sent away, the lowly lifted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. He has filled the hungry with good things, and sent the rich empty away. Henry applies it spiritually: those who see their need of Christ he fills with the best things, while the self-sufficient go empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. He has helped his servant Israel, in remembrance of his mercy. What God does for Mary is what he promised to his people: mercy remembered, not newly invented. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. As he spoke to the fathers, to Abraham and his seed for ever. The promise to Abraham is the anchor: this birth fulfils an old word, not a new idea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Mary abode with Elisabeth about three months, then returned to her own house. The visit ends quietly, with the two mothers parting to await their appointed days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 57. Elisabeth's full time came, and she brought forth a son. Henry notes the great joy among all the relations of the family at John's birth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 58. Neighbours and cousins heard how the Lord had showed great mercy to Elisabeth, and rejoiced with her. Mercy shared becomes joy shared, and the whole neighbourhood enters into it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 59. On the eighth day they came to circumcise the child, and called him Zacharias after his father. Custom and family expectation point one way; the parents' obedience will point another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 60. His mother answered, not so; he shall be called John. Elisabeth holds to the angel's word against the pressure of custom, naming the child by God's appointment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 61. They object that none of her kindred is called by this name. The objection shows how new God's way is: the name breaks family precedent because the child breaks ordinary expectation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 62. They made signs to his father, asking how he would have the child called. Zacharias, still dumb, is appealed to as the one whose silence has already spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 63. He asked for a writing table and wrote, His name is John, and they marvelled all. Henry notes the name means "Gracious," because John shall bring in the gospel wherein God's grace shines most bright. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 64. Immediately his mouth was opened, his tongue loosed, and he spoke and praised God. Henry's summary is memorable: unbelief closed his mouth, and believing opened it again; when God opens our lips, our mouths must show forth his praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 65. Fear came on all round about, and all these sayings were noised abroad through the hill country of Judaea. The private household wonder becomes public talk, preparing the country for what is coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 66. All who heard laid these things up in their hearts, asking what manner of child this shall be, for the hand of the Lord was with him. Henry urges us to observe God's dealings and wait the event, as they did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 67. Zacharias was filled with the Holy Ghost and prophesied. The dumb priest becomes a prophet: the same Spirit that sealed his mouth for unbelief now opens it for praise and prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 68. Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he has visited and redeemed his people. Henry reads the song as prophecy concerning the kingdom and salvation of the Messiah, now at hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 69. God has raised up a horn of salvation in the house of his servant David. The horn is strength and kingship: salvation comes through David's promised heir, not through a new dynasty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 70. As he spoke by the mouth of his holy prophets since the world began. The new salvation is the old promise, spoken all along by prophets, now arriving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 71. That we should be saved from our enemies and from all that hate us. Salvation here is deliverance with a purpose, not escape alone, as the following verses spell out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 73. The oath which he swore to Abraham. The covenant is grounded in God's oath, so its fulfilment is as sure as God's own word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 74. That we, being delivered from our enemies, might serve him without fear. Deliverance is for service: freedom from enemies frees God's people for fearless worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 75. In holiness and righteousness before him all the days of our life. The service is lifelong and inward as well as outward, holiness and righteousness together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 76. And thou, child, shalt be called prophet of the Highest, going before the Lord to prepare his ways. Only now does the song turn to John, and his whole calling is to prepare the way for another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 77. To give knowledge of salvation to his people by the remission of their sins. John's preparation is not ceremony but knowledge: salvation known as forgiveness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
