Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Sirach 2 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 My son, if thou come to serve the Lord, prepare thy soul for temptation.

2 Set thy heart aright, and constantly endure, and make not haste in time of trouble.

3 Cleave unto him, and depart not away, that thou mayest be increased at thy last end.

4 Whatsoever is brought upon thee take cheerfully, and be patient when thou art changed to a low estate.

5 For gold is tried in the fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity.

6 Believe in him, and he will help thee; order thy way aright, and trust in him.

7 Ye that fear the Lord, wait for his mercy; and go not aside, lest ye fall.

8 Ye that fear the Lord, believe him; and your reward shall not fail.

9 Ye that fear the Lord, hope for good, and for everlasting joy and mercy.

10 Look at the generations of old, and see; did ever any trust in the Lord, and was confounded? or did any abide in his fear, and was forsaken? or whom did he ever despise, that called upon him?

11 For the Lord is full of compassion and mercy, longsuffering, and very pitiful, and forgiveth sins, and saveth in time of affliction.

12 Woe be to fearful hearts, and faint hands, and the sinner that goeth two ways!

13 Woe unto him that is fainthearted! for he believeth not; therefore shall he not be defended.

14 Woe unto you that have lost patience! and what will ye do when the Lord shall visit you?

15 They that fear the Lord will not disobey his Word; and they that love him will keep his ways.

16 They that fear the Lord will seek that which is well, pleasing unto him; and they that love him shall be filled with the law.

17 They that fear the Lord will prepare their hearts, and humble their souls in his sight,

18 Saying, We will fall into the hands of the Lord, and not into the hands of men: for as his majesty is, so is his mercy.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Whoever comes to serve the Lord should expect temptation rather than exemption from it. The chapter urges steadfast trust, looking back to earlier generations who trusted and were not confounded, and counsels falling into God's hands rather than men's, since his mercy matches his majesty. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Whoever comes to serve the Lord should prepare his soul for temptation. Service of God is not an escape from trial but an entry into it, and readiness is part of the service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Set your heart aright, endure constantly, and do not make haste in time of trouble. A steadied heart and patient endurance, not panic, are the right response when trouble comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. Cleave to the Lord and do not depart, so that you may be increased at your last end. Holding fast through trial is what brings growth, and the increase is seen at the end of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. Take cheerfully whatever is brought upon you, and be patient when you are changed to a low estate. Losses of standing are to be accepted without resentment, as part of the discipline. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Gold is tried in fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity. Hardship is the assay that proves whether a person is genuine, as fire proves gold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. Believe in him and he will help you; order your way aright and trust in him. Trust is shown in an ordered life, and God's help meets those who both believe and walk straight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. You who fear the Lord, wait for his mercy and do not go aside, lest you fall. Waiting on God's mercy, rather than turning off the path, is what keeps a person from falling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. You who fear the Lord, believe him, and your reward will not fail. Trust in God's word carries a sure reward; it is not a gamble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. You who fear the Lord, hope for good, and for everlasting joy and mercy. The hope held out to the God-fearing is not temporary relief but lasting joy and mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Look at the generations of old: did anyone trust in the Lord and was confounded, or abide in his fear and was forsaken, or call on him and was despised? History itself is the evidence that trust in God has never yet ended in shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. The Lord is full of compassion and mercy, longsuffering and very pitiful, forgiving sins and saving in time of affliction. God's character is the ground of that history: he is slow to anger, ready to forgive, and present in trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. Woe to fearful hearts, faint hands, and the sinner who goes two ways. A divided, cowardly course earns woe because it is neither trust nor obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. Woe to the fainthearted, for he does not believe and therefore will not be defended. Unbelief leaves a person without the defence that trust would have secured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. Woe to those who have lost patience; what will they do when the Lord visits them? Impatience now leaves nothing to stand on when God comes to examine a life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. Those who fear the Lord will not disobey his word, and those who love him will keep his ways. Fear shows itself in obedience and love in keeping God's ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. Those who fear the Lord will seek what is well-pleasing to him, and those who love him will be filled with the law. Reverence aims at pleasing God, and love fills a life with his law rather than with itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. Those who fear the Lord will prepare their hearts and humble their souls in his sight. True preparation for God is inward: a heart made ready and a soul humbled before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. We will fall into the hands of the Lord and not into the hands of men, for as his majesty is, so is his mercy. God's hands are safer than men's because his mercy matches his greatness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
