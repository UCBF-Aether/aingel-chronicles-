The Text — Psalms 7 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 O LORD my God, in thee do I put my trust: save me from all them that persecute me, and deliver me:

2 Lest he tear my soul like a lion, rending it in pieces, while there is none to deliver.

3 O LORD my God, If I have done this; if there be iniquity in my hands;

4 If I have rewarded evil unto him that was at peace with me; (yea, I have delivered him that without cause is mine enemy:)

5 Let the enemy persecute my soul, and take it; yea, let him tread down my life upon the earth, and lay mine honour in the dust. Selah.

6 Arise, O LORD, in thine anger, lift up thyself because of the rage of mine enemies: and awake for me to the judgment that thou hast commanded.

7 So shall the congregation of the people compass thee about: for their sakes therefore return thou on high.

8 The LORD shall judge the people: judge me, O LORD, according to my righteousness, and according to mine integrity that is in me.

9 Oh let the wickedness of the wicked come to an end; but establish the just: for the righteous God trieth the hearts and reins.

10 My defence is of God, which saveth the upright in heart.

11 God judgeth the righteous, and God is angry with the wicked every day.

12 If he turn not, he will whet his sword; he hath bent his bow, and made it ready.

13 He hath also prepared for him the instruments of death; he ordaineth his arrows against the persecutors.

14 Behold, he travaileth with iniquity, and hath conceived mischief, and brought forth falsehood.

15 He made a pit, and digged it, and is fallen into the ditch which he made.

16 His mischief shall return upon his own head, and his violent dealing shall come down upon his own pate.

17 I will praise the LORD according to his righteousness: and will sing praise to the name of the LORD most high.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A protest of innocence under persecution: the speaker invites judgment on himself if guilty, calls on God the righteous judge who tries hearts to end wickedness and establish the just, and notes that the wicked fall into their own pit. It ends in praise. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Heading note: the text panel prints the verses only, without a heading. The name David in the verse notes comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. Authorship and date remain Unresolved (see Trace). What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David puts his trust in the Lord his God and asks to be saved and delivered from all who persecute him; trust is the psalm's foundation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Without deliverance his soul would be torn like a lion's prey, rent in pieces with none to rescue; the danger is mortal, not slight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He submits to examination: if he has done this wrong, if iniquity is in his hands, let the charge stand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. If he has rewarded evil to a friend at peace with him, or plundered an enemy without cause, he accepts the verdict against himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Then let the enemy pursue, overtake, tread down his life, and lay his honour in the dust: innocence is asserted by inviting its own punishment if false. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He calls on God to arise in anger and lift himself against his enemies' rage, awake for the judgment God has commanded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The congregation of peoples shall compass God about, and for their sakes he returns on high: public vindication will teach the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The Lord judges the peoples; David asks to be judged by his righteousness and integrity in this cause — not a claim of sinlessness, but of innocence toward his accusers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Let the wickedness of the wicked end, but establish the just, for the righteous God tries hearts and reins, the inward thoughts and affections. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. His defence is of God, who saves the upright in heart: protection belongs to God, and its recipients are the sincere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. God judges the righteous — vindicates them — and is angry with the wicked every day: divine anger is not a passing mood but a settled opposition to evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. If the wicked will not turn, God whets his sword, bends his bow, and makes it ready: judgment is prepared, not improvised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He has prepared instruments of death and ordains his arrows against persecutors: the weapons of wrath are aimed, not random. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The wicked travails with iniquity, conceives mischief, and brings forth falsehood: sin is pictured as a painful, productive labour ending in lies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He digs a pit and falls into the ditch he made: his own trap becomes his ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. His mischief returns on his own head, and his violence descends on his own pate: retribution follows the exact path of the wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. David ends in praise to the Lord according to his righteousness, singing to the name of the Lord most high: judgment rightly understood issues in worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
