The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 30 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 I will extol thee, O LORD; for thou hast lifted me up, and hast not made my foes to rejoice over me.

2 O LORD my God, I cried unto thee, and thou hast healed me.

3 O LORD, thou hast brought up my soul from the grave: thou hast kept me alive, that I should not go down to the pit.

4 Sing unto the LORD, O ye saints of his, and give thanks at the remembrance of his holiness.

5 For his anger endureth but a moment; in his favour is life: weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning.

6 And in my prosperity I said, I shall never be moved.

7 LORD, by thy favour thou hast made my mountain to stand strong: thou didst hide thy face, and I was troubled.

8 I cried to thee, O LORD; and unto the LORD I made supplication.

9 What profit is there in my blood, when I go down to the pit? Shall the dust praise thee? shall it declare thy truth?

10 Hear, O LORD, and have mercy upon me: LORD, be thou my helper.

11 Thou hast turned for me my mourning into dancing: thou hast put off my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness;

12 To the end that my glory may sing praise to thee, and not be silent. O LORD my God, I will give thanks unto thee for ever.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

From pit to dancing: lifted up and healed, brought from the grave, the speaker who once said in prosperity, 'I shall never be moved,' learns that strength was God's favour. Weeping for a night gives way to morning joy, mourning is exchanged for dancing, so that his glory may sing praise for ever. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. I will extol you, Lord, for you have lifted me up and not made my foes rejoice over me: exaltation of God follows lifting from the pit, and the foes' disappointed joy is part of the praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Lord my God, I cried to you, and you healed me: prayer and healing are joined as cry and answer, whether the sickness was of body or of soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Lord, you brought up my soul from the grave and kept me alive, that I should not go down to the pit: near-death becomes a resurrection in miniature, credited wholly to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Sing to the Lord, you his saints, and give thanks at the remembrance of his holiness: the saints' song is tuned to God's holiness, the attribute that makes his mercy wonderful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. His anger endures but a moment, in his favour is life; weeping may endure for a night, but joy comes in the morning: God's anger is brief, his favour is life itself, and sorrow has a morning appointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. In my prosperity I said, I shall never be moved: prosperity bred the false security that trouble then exposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Lord, by your favour you made my mountain stand strong; you hid your face, and I was troubled: strength was favour, not nature, and the hiding of God's face troubled him more than any enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. I cried to you, Lord, and made supplication: the troubled man returns to the same prayer that healed him before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. What profit is there in my blood, when I go down to the pit? Shall the dust praise you or declare your truth? He pleads that a dead man cannot praise — let him live, that praise may continue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Hear, Lord, and have mercy on me; Lord, be my helper: hearing, mercy, and help are asked together, as one gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. You have turned my mourning into dancing, put off my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness: the exchange is total — mourning garments replaced by gladness itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. To the end that my glory may sing praise to you and not be silent; Lord my God, I will give thanks to you for ever: the purpose of deliverance is endless praise, and the psalm vows it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
