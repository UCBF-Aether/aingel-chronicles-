The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Esther 7 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–10; King James Version, Apocrypha (the Rest of Esther) for the Greek additions)

1 So the king and Haman came to banquet with Esther the queen.

2 And the king said again unto Esther on the second day at the banquet of wine, What is thy petition, queen Esther? and it shall be granted thee: and what is thy request? and it shall be performed, even to the half of the kingdom.

3 Then Esther the queen answered and said, If I have found favour in thy sight, O king, and if it please the king, let my life be given me at my petition, and my people at my request:

4 For we are sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be slain, and to perish. But if we had been sold for bondmen and bondwomen, I had held my tongue, although the enemy could not countervail the king’s damage.

5 Then the king Ahasuerus answered and said unto Esther the queen, Who is he, and where is he, that durst presume in his heart to do so?

6 And Esther said, The adversary and enemy is this wicked Haman. Then Haman was afraid before the king and the queen.

7 And the king arising from the banquet of wine in his wrath went into the palace garden: and Haman stood up to make request for his life to Esther the queen; for he saw that there was evil determined against him by the king.

8 Then the king returned out of the palace garden into the place of the banquet of wine; and Haman was fallen upon the bed whereon Esther was. Then said the king, Will he force the queen also before me in the house? As the word went out of the king’s mouth, they covered Haman’s face.

9 And Harbonah, one of the chamberlains, said before the king, Behold also, the gallows fifty cubits high, which Haman had made for Mordecai, who had spoken good for the king, standeth in the house of Haman. Then the king said, Hang him thereon.

10 So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then was the king’s wrath pacified.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

At the second banquet Esther at last names her petition: her life and her people, sold to be destroyed, slain and to perish. The king demands the enemy's name, Esther names this wicked Haman, and Haman, pleading for his life to the queen, falls upon the bed where Esther sits as the king returns from the garden, which the king reads as an assault on the queen; his face is covered, and on Harbonah's word he is hanged on the gallows he had prepared for Mordecai. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. So the king and Haman came to banquet with Esther the queen. The second banquet gathers the same three, but the night between has changed everything for Haman. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Henry notes the repeated offer shows the king's impatience to grant, and Esther, having deferred once, can delay no longer at this second banquet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Then Esther the queen answered and said, If I have found favour in thy sight, O king, and if it please the king, let my life be given me at my petition, and my people at my request. JFB notes the petition is at last plain: not half a kingdom, but a life and a people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For we are sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be slain, and to perish. But if we had been sold for bondmen and bondwomen, I had held my tongue, although the enemy could not countervail the king's damage. Henry notes Esther names the sale: Haman's ten thousand talents bought a people, and bondage she could have borne, but extermination she cannot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Then the king Ahasuerus answered and said unto Esther the queen, Who is he, and where is he, that durst presume in his heart to do so? JFB notes the king's anger is genuine surprise: he had signed the decree without knowing it touched his own queen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And Esther said, The adversary and enemy is this wicked Haman. Then Haman was afraid before the king and the queen. Henry notes the naming is the turning point: one sentence makes the favourite the enemy, and fear falls on him at his own feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And the king arising from the banquet of wine in his wrath went into the palace garden: and Haman stood up to make request for his life to Esther the queen; for he saw that there was evil determined against him by the king. JFB notes the king withdraws to master his wrath, and Haman, left alone with the queen, begs for the life he had denied to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Henry notes the king's return misreads a suppliant's fall as an assault on the queen, and the covered face that follows is the sign of a condemned man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And Harbonah, one of the chamberlains, said before the king, Behold also, the gallows fifty cubits high, which Haman had made for Mordecai, who had spoken good for the king, stands in the house of Haman. Then the king said, Hang him thereon. JFB notes a chamberlain supplies the instrument: the gallows built for Mordecai stands ready in Haman's own house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then was the king's wrath pacified. Henry notes the proverb is fulfilled in history: the pit dug for another receives the digger, and the king's wrath rests because justice has been done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Sold, to be destroyed. Esther's own description of her petition: she and her people are sold, not into bondage, which she says she could have borne, but to be destroyed, slain and to perish. Documented Chapter text, verse 4
  • Will he force the queen also. The king's question as Haman falls upon the bed where Esther sits: in this scene the king reads Haman's fall on the queen's couch as an assault on the queen in the king's own house. Interpreted Chapter text, verse 8
