The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when they came nigh to Jerusalem, unto Bethphage and Bethany, at the mount of Olives, he sendeth forth two of his disciples,

2 And saith unto them, Go your way into the village over against you: and as soon as ye be entered into it, ye shall find a colt tied, whereon never man sat; loose him, and bring him.

3 And if any man say unto you, Why do ye this? say ye that the Lord hath need of him; and straightway he will send him hither.

4 And they went their way, and found the colt tied by the door without in a place where two ways met; and they loose him.

5 And certain of them that stood there said unto them, What do ye, loosing the colt?

6 And they said unto them even as Jesus had commanded: and they let them go.

7 And they brought the colt to Jesus, and cast their garments on him; and he sat upon him.

8 And many spread their garments in the way: and others cut down branches off the trees, and strawed them in the way.

9 And they that went before, and they that followed, cried, saying, Hosanna; Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord:

10 Blessed be the kingdom of our father David, that cometh in the name of the Lord: Hosanna in the highest.

11 And Jesus entered into Jerusalem, and into the temple: and when he had looked round about upon all things, and now the eventide was come, he went out unto Bethany with the twelve.

12 And on the morrow, when they were come from Bethany, he was hungry:

13 And seeing a fig tree afar off having leaves, he came, if haply he might find any thing thereon: and when he came to it, he found nothing but leaves; for the time of figs was not yet.

14 And Jesus answered and said unto it, No man eat fruit of thee hereafter for ever. And his disciples heard it.

15 And they come to Jerusalem: and Jesus went into the temple, and began to cast out them that sold and bought in the temple, and overthrew the tables of the moneychangers, and the seats of them that sold doves;

16 And would not suffer that any man should carry any vessel through the temple.

17 And he taught, saying unto them, Is it not written, My house shall be called of all nations the house of prayer? but ye have made it a den of thieves.

18 And the scribes and chief priests heard it, and sought how they might destroy him: for they feared him, because all the people was astonished at his doctrine.

19 And when even was come, he went out of the city.

20 And in the morning, as they passed by, they saw the fig tree dried up from the roots.

21 And Peter calling to remembrance saith unto him, Master, behold, the fig tree which thou cursedst is withered away.

22 And Jesus answering saith unto them, Have faith in God.

23 For verily I say unto you, That whosoever shall say unto this mountain, Be thou removed, and be thou cast into the sea; and shall not doubt in his heart, but shall believe that those things which he saith shall come to pass; he shall have whatsoever he saith.

24 Therefore I say unto you, What things soever ye desire, when ye pray, believe that ye receive them, and ye shall have them.

25 And when ye stand praying, forgive, if ye have ought against any: that your Father also which is in heaven may forgive you your trespasses.

26 But if ye do not forgive, neither will your Father which is in heaven forgive your trespasses.

27 And they come again to Jerusalem: and as he was walking in the temple, there come to him the chief priests, and the scribes, and the elders,

28 And say unto him, By what authority doest thou these things? and who gave thee this authority to do these things?

29 And Jesus answered and said unto them, I will also ask of you one question, and answer me, and I will tell you by what authority I do these things.

30 The baptism of John, was it from heaven, or of men? answer me.

31 And they reasoned with themselves, saying, If we shall say, From heaven; he will say, Why then did ye not believe him?

32 But if we shall say, Of men; they feared the people: for all men counted John, that he was a prophet indeed.

33 And they answered and said unto Jesus, We cannot tell. And Jesus answering saith unto them, Neither do I tell you by what authority I do these things.

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jesus enters Jerusalem on a colt that had never been ridden, while crowds spread garments and cry “Hosanna” (vv. 1–11). The next morning he curses a fruitless fig tree and drives sellers, moneychangers, and dove-sellers from the temple, teaching that his Father’s house is a house of prayer for all nations (vv. 12–19). The withered fig tree becomes a lesson on faith, prayer, and forgiveness (vv. 20–26), and when chief priests, scribes, and elders demand his authority, he counters with a question about John’s baptism that they dare not answer (vv. 27–33). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 11

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When they came near to Jerusalem, to Bethphage and Bethany, at the Mount of Olives, he sent forth two of his disciples. The entry begins from the Mount of Olives, the place of prophecy, and two disciples are sent as his agents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He said, Go into the village over against you, and as soon as you enter, you shall find a colt tied, whereon never man sat; loose him and bring him. The colt, never ridden, is fitted for a king's use, and Christ's knowledge of it proves his foresight in small things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. If any man says to you, Why do you this? Say, The Lord has need of him, and straightway he will send him here. 'The Lord has need' is both reason and warrant: the owner's right yields to the Lord's need, and the promise secures consent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They went their way and found the colt tied by the door without in a place where two ways met, and they loosed him. The finding matched the word exactly, down to the door and the meeting of ways, confirming the disciples' trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Certain of those standing there said, What do you, loosing the colt? The bystanders' challenge was reasonable, for loosing another's colt without leave needed explanation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They said to them even as Jesus had commanded, and they let them go. The Lord's word, repeated exactly, satisfied the owners, and obedience to a command is the best answer to a challenge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They brought the colt to Jesus, cast their garments on him, and he sat upon him. Garments for a saddle show the disciples' homage, and a king riding a colt fulfils Zechariah's prophecy of a lowly king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Many spread their garments in the way, and others cut down branches off the trees and strewed them in the way. Garments and branches were the crowd's carpet, the poor man's tribute to a king without a chariot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They that went before and they that followed cried, Hosanna; Blessed is he that comes in the name of the Lord. Before and behind, the procession cried Hosanna, save now, and blessed the comer in the Lord's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Blessed be the kingdom of our father David, that comes in the name of the Lord: Hosanna in the highest. The kingdom of David was their hope, and Hosanna in the highest asked heaven itself to join the praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Jesus entered into Jerusalem and into the temple, and when he had looked round about upon all things, and now the evening was come, he went out to Bethany with the twelve. The temple, not a palace, was his first visit, and his survey of all things preceded the cleansing, as a judge first views the case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. On the morrow, when they came from Bethany, he was hungry. Hunger on the morning road sets up the fig tree, for Christ's true humanity felt need as men do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Seeing a fig tree afar off having leaves, he came, if perhaps he might find anything thereon, and when he came, he found nothing but leaves, for the time of figs was not yet. Leaves promised fruit and gave none, and the tree's season is noted lest the curse seem unjust: it is judged for profession without fruit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Jesus answered and said to it, No man eat fruit of you hereafter for ever, and his disciples heard it. The curse answers the tree's profession, and the disciples heard it, that the lesson might be theirs as well as Israel's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They came to Jerusalem, and Jesus went into the temple, and began to cast out those that sold and bought in the temple, and overthrew the tables of the moneychangers and the seats of those who sold doves. Buying, selling, changing, and dove-selling filled God's house with trade, and Christ cast them out with authority, overthrowing the furniture of the market. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He would not suffer that any man should carry any vessel through the temple. Even carrying a vessel through the courts as a shortcut profaned the house, and Christ barred the temple from becoming a thoroughfare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. He taught, saying, Is it not written, My house shall be called of all nations the house of prayer? But you have made it a den of thieves. The house for all nations' prayer had become a den of thieves, for traders' gain and priests' collusion robbed worshippers and God together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The scribes and chief priests heard it and sought how they might destroy him, for they feared him, because all the people were astonished at his doctrine. Fear of the astonished people restrained them, but their counsel was already murder, and doctrine that moves crowds moves rulers to destroy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. When evening came, he went out of the city. Evening withdrew him to Bethany again, for Jerusalem at night was no safe lodging for its judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. In the morning, as they passed by, they saw the fig tree dried up from the roots. Dried from the roots, not merely withered in leaf, the tree shows judgment that reaches the source, and morning light revealed what the curse had done overnight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Peter calling to remembrance said, Master, behold, the fig tree which you cursed is withered away. Peter's remembrance links the curse to the sight, and his report invites the lesson Christ now gives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

For verily, whoever shall say to this mountain, Be removed and cast into the sea, and shall not doubt in his heart, but shall believe that those things which he says shall come to pass, he shall have whatever he says. Mountain-moving faith is faith without doubt, trusting God's promise rather than the mountain's size, and it receives what it asks in God's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Therefore, whatever things you desire, when you pray, believe that you receive them, and you shall have them. Prayer joined to believing reception is the rule: believe that you receive, and you shall have, for faith is the hand that takes the gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. When you stand praying, forgive, if you have anything against any, that your Father in heaven may forgive you your trespasses. Forgiveness is the condition of prayer: an unforgiving heart cannot expect a forgiving Father, and standing to pray is the time to release debts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

The warning is plain and mutual: forgiveness withheld from men withholds forgiveness from God, for grace received must be grace given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. They came again to Jerusalem, and as he was walking in the temple, there came to him the chief priests, scribes, and elders. The temple walk was interrupted by the whole Sanhedrin in deputation, come to challenge the cleansing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. They said to him, By what authority do you these things? And who gave you this authority to do these things? Authority was the question, for they denied any authority but their own, and demanded his warrant for judging the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Jesus answered, I will also ask of you one question, and answer me, and I will tell you by what authority I do these things. One question answers theirs, for their answer about John will decide their judgment about Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The baptism of John, was it from heaven, or of men? Answer me. John's baptism is the test case: if from heaven, Christ's authority follows, for John witnessed to him; if of men, they must face the people who counted John a prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a parallel examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a possible parallel. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

Entry and temple — Mark 11, Luke 19, and John 2

Mark 11:1–17 and Luke 19:29–46 agree closely: the colt brought and garments cast on it, praise on the descent of the Mount of Olives, and the cleansing of the temple with the words “My house shall be called the house of prayer” turned into “a den of thieves.” John 2:13–17 tells a temple cleansing too — sellers driven out, money poured out, tables overthrown — but places it at a Passover near the start of Jesus’ ministry, with the words “make not my Father’s house an house of merchandise” and the disciples remembering the zeal of God’s house. Readers have long asked whether John records the same cleansing placed differently, or a separate early cleansing. The sources consulted for this page do not settle that question, and this page claims neither one event nor two.
