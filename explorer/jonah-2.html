The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jonah 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Jonah prayed unto the LORD his God out of the fish’s belly,

2 And said, I cried by reason of mine affliction unto the LORD, and he heard me; out of the belly of hell cried I, and thou heardest my voice.

3 For thou hadst cast me into the deep, in the midst of the seas; and the floods compassed me about: all thy billows and thy waves passed over me.

4 Then I said, I am cast out of thy sight; yet I will look again toward thy holy temple.

5 The waters compassed me about, even to the soul: the depth closed me round about, the weeds were wrapped about my head.

6 I went down to the bottoms of the mountains; the earth with her bars was about me for ever: yet hast thou brought up my life from corruption, O LORD my God.

7 When my soul fainted within me I remembered the LORD: and my prayer came in unto thee, into thine holy temple.

8 They that observe lying vanities forsake their own mercy.

9 But I will sacrifice unto thee with the voice of thanksgiving; I will pay that that I have vowed. Salvation is of the LORD.

10 And the LORD spake unto the fish, and it vomited out Jonah upon the dry land.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Out of the fish's belly Jonah prays, retelling his descent into the deep, floods compassing him, weeds wrapped about his head, the earth barred about him as if for ever, and his resolve to look again toward God's holy temple. Those who observe lying vanities forsake their own mercy, he says, but he will sacrifice with thanksgiving and pay his vows, for salvation is of the LORD. Then the LORD speaks to the fish, and it vomits Jonah onto dry land. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Affliction at last brought Jonah to prayer, under a sense of sin and of God's displeasure. Henry notes he prayed once he was kept alive by miracle, a sign he was reserved for further mercy, and that God's goodwill toward him despite his offence opened lips closed by guilt and dread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jonah cried out of his affliction and God heard him, even from what he calls the belly of hell, the fish as grave and prison for his disobedience. Henry's point is that no depth in this world bars crying to God with hope of being heard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Though sailors cast him in, Jonah saw God's hand casting him into the heart of the seas, with floods compassing him on every side. The billows and waves were God's own, commissioned to afflict and terrify him but not to destroy him, in words borrowed from David's complaint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Jonah sank toward despair, thinking himself cast out of God's sight, which Henry calls the very misery of the lost. Yet faith corrected fear with one resolve: he would look again toward God's holy temple, hoping to live and worship there again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The waters threatened his very life and spirit, tokens of God's displeasure that reached to his soul, and the depths closed round him with seaweed wrapped about his head. Henry reads this helpless entanglement as teaching trust not in oneself but in God who raises the dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Jonah went down to the bottoms of the mountains, with the earth barred about him as if for ever, cut off from any hope of return. Henry sets this utter helplessness against the assurance that follows: God brought his life up from corruption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. When his soul fainted within him, Jonah remembered the Lord, and his prayer came into God's holy temple. Henry calls this hope an anchor of the soul: though perplexed, he was not in despair, because faith had the last word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Those who observe lying vanities forsake their own mercy. Henry's concise warning applies Jonah's own case: those who run away from the work of their place and day run away from the comfort of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Jonah vows sacrifice with thanksgiving and payment of his vows, giving all praise to God. The concise note sums the chapter's encouragement: Jonah's experience encourages others in all ages to trust in God as the God of salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The Lord spoke to the fish and it vomited Jonah onto dry land, safe and sound. Henry frames the verse as the completion of the chapter's arc: God heard him praying, and now Nineveh will hear him preaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Belly of hell. the depths Jonah cries from; the study note reads the fish itself as grave and prison for his disobedience, from which no depth bars crying to God. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
