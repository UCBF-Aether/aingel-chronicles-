The Text — 1 Esdras 5 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 After this were the principal men of the families chosen according to their tribes, to go up with their wives and sons and daughters, with their menservants and maidservants, and their cattle.

2 And Darius sent with them a thousand horsemen, till they had brought them back to Jerusalem safely, and with musical [instruments] tabrets and flutes.

3 And all their brethren played, and he made them go up together with them.

4 And these are the names of the men which went up, according to their families among their tribes, after their several heads.

5 The priests, the sons of Phinees the son of Aaron: Jesus the son of Josedec, the son of Saraias, and Joacim the son of Zorobabel, the son of Salathiel, of the house of David, out of the kindred of Phares, of the tribe of Judah;

6 Who spake wise sentences before Darius the king of Persia in the second year of his reign, in the month Nisan, which is the first month.

7 And these are they of Jewry that came up from the captivity, where they dwelt as strangers, whom Nabuchodonosor the king of Babylon had carried away unto Babylon.

8 And they returned unto Jerusalem, and to the other parts of Jewry, every man to his own city, who came with Zorobabel, with Jesus, Nehemias, and Zacharias, and Reesaias, Enenius, Mardocheus. Beelsarus, Aspharasus, Reelius, Roimus, and Baana, their guides.

9 The number of them of the nation, and their governors, sons of Phoros, two thousand an hundred seventy and two; the sons of Saphat, four hundred seventy and two:

10 The sons of Ares, seven hundred fifty and six:

11 The sons of Phaath Moab, two thousand eight hundred and twelve:

12 The sons of Elam, a thousand two hundred fifty and four: the sons of Zathul, nine hundred forty and five: the sons of Corbe, seven hundred and five: the sons of Bani, six hundred forty and eight:

13 The sons of Bebai, six hundred twenty and three: the sons of Sadas, three thousand two hundred twenty and two:

14 The sons of Adonikam, six hundred sixty and seven: the sons of Bagoi, two thousand sixty and six: the sons of Adin, four hundred fifty and four:

15 The sons of Aterezias, ninety and two: the sons of Ceilan and Azetas threescore and seven: the sons of Azuran, four hundred thirty and two:

16 The sons of Ananias, an hundred and one: the sons of Arom, thirty two: and the sons of Bassa, three hundred twenty and three: the sons of Azephurith, an hundred and two:

17 The sons of Meterus, three thousand and five: the sons of Bethlomon, an hundred twenty and three:

18 They of Netophah, fifty and five: they of Anathoth, an hundred fifty and eight: they of Bethsamos, forty and two:

19 They of Kiriathiarius, twenty and five: they of Caphira and Beroth, seven hundred forty and three: they of Pira, seven hundred:

20 They of Chadias and Ammidoi, four hundred twenty and two: they of Cirama and Gabdes, six hundred twenty and one:

21 They of Macalon, an hundred twenty and two: they of Betolius, fifty and two: the sons of Nephis, an hundred fifty and six:

22 The sons of Calamolalus and Onus, seven hundred twenty and five: the sons of Jerechus, two hundred forty and five:

23 The sons of Annas, three thousand three hundred and thirty.

24 The priests: the sons of Jeddu, the son of Jesus among the sons of Sanasib, nine hundred seventy and two: the sons of Meruth, a thousand fifty and two:

25 The sons of Phassaron, a thousand forty and seven: the sons of Carme, a thousand and seventeen.

26 The Levites: the sons of Jessue, and Cadmiel, and Banuas, and Sudias, seventy and four.

27 The holy singers: the sons of Asaph, an hundred twenty and eight.

28 The porters: the sons of Salum, the sons of Jatal, the sons of Talmon, the sons of Dacobi, the sons of Teta, the sons of Sami, in all an hundred thirty and nine.

29 The servants of the temple: the sons of Esau, the sons of Asipha, the sons of Tabaoth, the sons of Ceras, the sons of Sud, the sons of Phaleas, the sons of Labana, the sons of Graba,

30 The sons of Acua, the sons of Uta, the sons of Cetab, the sons of Agaba, the sons of Subai, the sons of Anan, the sons of Cathua, the sons of Geddur,

31 The sons of Airus, the sons of Daisan, the sons of Noeba, the sons of Chaseba, the sons of Gazera, the sons of Azia, the sons of Phinees, the sons of Azare, the sons of Bastai, the sons of Asana, the sons of Meani, the sons of Naphisi, the sons of Acub, the sons of Acipha, the sons of Assur, the sons of Pharacim, the sons of Basaloth,

32 The sons of Meeda, the sons of Coutha, the sons of Charea, the sons of Charcus, the sons of Aserer, the sons of Thomoi, the sons of Nasith, the sons of Atipha.

33 The sons of the servants of Solomon: the sons of Azaphion, the sons of Pharira, the sons of Jeeli, the sons of Lozon, the sons of Israel, the sons of Sapheth,

34 The sons of Hagia, the sons of Pharacareth, the sons of Sabi, the sons of Sarothie, the sons of Masias, the sons of Gar, the sons of Addus, the sons of Suba, the sons of Apherra, the sons of Barodis, the sons of Sabat, the sons of Allom.

35 All the ministers of the temple, and the sons of the servants of Solomon, were three hundred seventy and two.

36 These came up from Thermeleth and Thelersas, Charaathalar leading them, and Aalar;

37 Neither could they shew their families, nor their stock, how they were of Israel: the sons of Ladan, the son of Ban, the sons of Necodan, six hundred fifty and two.

38 And of the priests that usurped the office of the priesthood, and were not found: the sons of Obdia, the sons of Accoz, the sons of Addus, who married Augia one of the daughters of Barzelus, and was named after his name.

39 And when the description of the kindred of these men was sought in the register, and was not found, they were removed from executing the office of the priesthood:

40 For unto them said Nehemias and Atharias, that they should not be partakers of the holy things, till there arose up an high priest clothed with doctrine and truth.

41 So of Israel, from them of twelve years old and upward, they were all in number forty thousand, beside menservants and womenservants two thousand three hundred and sixty.

42 Their menservants and handmaids were seven thousand three hundred forty and seven: the singing men and singing women, two hundred forty and five:

43 Four hundred thirty and five camels, seven thousand thirty and six horses, two hundred forty and five mules, five thousand five hundred twenty and five beasts used to the yoke.

44 And certain of the chief of their families, when they came to the temple of God that is in Jerusalem, vowed to set up the house again in his own place according to their ability,

45 And to give into the holy treasury of the works a thousand pounds of gold, five thousand of silver, and an hundred priestly vestments.

46 And so dwelt the priests and the Levites and the people in Jerusalem, and in the country, the singers also and the porters; and all Israel in their villages.

47 But when the seventh month was at hand, and when the children of Israel were every man in his own place, they came all together with one consent into the open place of the first gate which is toward the east.

48 Then stood up Jesus the son of Josedec, and his brethren the priests and Zorobabel the son of Salathiel, and his brethren, and made ready the altar of the God of Israel,

49 To offer burnt sacrifices upon it, according as it is expressly commanded in the book of Moses the man of God.

50 And there were gathered unto them out of the other nations of the land, and they erected the altar upon his own place, because all the nations of the land were at enmity with them, and oppressed them; and they offered sacrifices according to the time, and burnt offerings to the Lord both morning and evening.

51 Also they held the feast of tabernacles, as it is commanded in the law, and offered sacrifices daily, as was meet:

52 And after that, the continual oblations, and the sacrifice of the sabbaths, and of the new moons, and of all holy feasts.

53 And all they that had made any vow to God began to offer sacrifices to God from the first day of the seventh month, although the temple of the Lord was not yet built.

54 And they gave unto the masons and carpenters money, meat, and drink, with cheerfulness.

55 Unto them of Zidon also and Tyre they gave carrs, that they should bring cedar trees from Libanus, which should be brought by floats to the haven of Joppa, according as it was commanded them by Cyrus king of the Persians.

56 And in the second year and second month after his coming to the temple of God at Jerusalem began Zorobabel the son of Salathiel, and Jesus the son of Josedec, and their brethren, and the priests, and the Levites, and all they that were come unto Jerusalem out of the captivity:

57 And they laid the foundation of the house of God in the first day of the second month, in the second year after they were come to Jewry and Jerusalem.

58 And they appointed the Levites from twenty years old over the works of the Lord. Then stood up Jesus, and his sons and brethren, and Cadmiel his brother, and the sons of Madiabun, with the sons of Joda the son of Eliadun, with their sons and brethren, all Levites, with one accord setters forward of the business, labouring to advance the works in the house of God. So the workmen built the temple of the Lord.

59 And the priests stood arrayed in their vestments with musical instruments and trumpets; and the Levites the sons of Asaph had cymbals,

60 Singing songs of thanksgiving, and praising the Lord, according as David the king of Israel had ordained.

61 And they sung with loud voices songs to the praise of the Lord, because his mercy and glory is for ever in all Israel.

62 And all the people sounded trumpets, and shouted with a loud voice, singing songs of thanksgiving unto the Lord for the rearing up of the house of the Lord.

63 Also of the priests and Levites, and of the chief of their families, the ancients who had seen the former house came to the building of this with weeping and great crying.

64 But many with trumpets and joy shouted with loud voice,

65 Insomuch that the trumpets might not be heard for the weeping of the people: yet the multitude sounded marvellously, so that it was heard afar off.

66 Wherefore when the enemies of the tribe of Judah and Benjamin heard it, they came to know what that noise of trumpets should mean.

67 And they perceived that they that were of the captivity did build the temple unto the Lord God of Israel.

68 So they went to Zorobabel and Jesus, and to the chief of the families, and said unto them, We will build together with you.

69 For we likewise, as ye, do obey your Lord, and do sacrifice unto him from the days of Azbazareth the king of the Assyrians, who brought us hither.

70 Then Zorobabel and Jesus and the chief of the families of Israel said unto them, It is not for us and you to build together an house unto the Lord our God.

71 We ourselves alone will build unto the Lord of Israel, according as Cyrus the king of the Persians hath commanded us.

72 But the heathen of the land lying heavy upon the inhabitants of Judea, and holding them strait, hindered their building;

73 And by their secret plots, and popular persuasions and commotions, they hindered the finishing of the building all the time that king Cyrus lived: so they were hindered from building for the space of two years, until the reign of Darius.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

The principal men of the families go up with their wives, children, servants, and cattle, and the chapter gives the long register of the returnees, priests, Levites, singers, porters, and temple servants. They rebuild the altar, offer sacrifices as written in the book of Moses, keep the feast of tabernacles, and lay the temple foundations amid joy and weeping; but secret plots and persuasions hinder the finishing of the building for two years, all the time Cyrus lived. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Feast of tabernacles. the feast kept after the altar is rebuilt, with burnt sacrifices according to the book of Moses (verses 47-52). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Temple servants. the servants of the temple listed among the returnees (verses 29-32). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Bissell notes this departure follows the contest victory, in which the third speaker secures the king's consent to the return of the Jews, so that the principal men of the families are now chosen to go up with their households and cattle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing E. C. Bissell
  • Verse 2. Bissell notes the Authorized Version's phrasing here is safely, and with musical instruments, tabrets and flutes, and that he reconstructed the sentence after Fritzsche's text, with the brethren playing as the company went up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing E. C. Bissell
  • Verse 3. Bissell notes all their brethren played with music as the company went up together, the same musical escort described in the preceding verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing E. C. Bissell
  • Verse 4. Bissell flags the displacement in the book's arrangement, since this register of names belongs to the families that returned in the time of Cyrus, though it is placed here after the Darius episode. Interpreted Study note, summarizing E. C. Bissell
  • Verse 5. JFB identifies Jeshua the high priest and Zerubbabel as the leaders of the first band of returning exiles, whose register this list preserves, with the names extracted probably from the Persian archives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Bissell notes the wise sentences spoken before Darius are the contest speeches themselves, and records Fritzsche's supposition that the original hero of that portion was not Zerubbabel but his son Joakim, whose name appears here among the leaders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing E. C. Bissell
  • Verse 7. JFB explains the returned exiles are called children of the province, that is, Judea, now reduced from an illustrious kingdom to an obscure tributary province of Persia, and that each returned to his own city, whether his ancestors' city or one newly rebuilt and allotted to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. JFB notes Zerubbabel was the chief leader of the first band of returning exiles, with Jeshua the high priest and Nehemiah conspicuous among those associated in conducting the caravans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. JFB notes the word children, used throughout this catalogue, means posterity or descendants, so each entry names a family and its number. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. JFB notes it is pleasant to see so many of Anathoth returning, since that Levite city had spurned Jeremiah's warning and been laid waste in the Assyrian conquest, but its people, brought to a better state of mind during captivity, returned and rebuilt their city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. JFB notes each priestly family was ranged under its prince or head, as the whole body was divided into twenty-four courses, and that only four of the courses returned from Babylon, afterwards divided again into twenty-four under the original course names appointed by David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. JFB explains the children of Solomon's servants as either the strangers Solomon enlisted in building the temple or those who lived in his palace, which was deemed a high honor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. JFB notes Barzillai preferred that name to his own family name, deeming connection with so noble a family a greater distinction than the house of Levi, but by this worldly ambition he forfeited the dignity and advantages of the priesthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. JFB notes the forfeiture of priestly dignity followed from preferring a noble secular name to Levitical descent, which is why those whose kindred could not be found in the register were removed from executing the priesthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. JFB explains Tirshatha as a title borne by the Persian governors of Judea, derived from the Persian word for severe, and equivalent to your severity or your awfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. JFB notes the gross total of 42,360 is twelve thousand more than the particular numbers in the catalogue amount to, and explains the deficiency as those who did not belong to Judah and Benjamin or to the priests, but to the other tribes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. JFB notes the servants and singers, male and female, are reckoned separately from the congregation, so that putting all items together the company that went with Zerubbabel amounted to fifty thousand, with eight thousand beasts of burden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. JFB notes the beasts of burden are counted with the whole caravan, amounting to eight thousand, alongside the fifty thousand persons who went up with Zerubbabel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. JFB notes the sight of a place hallowed by sacred associations but now lying in desolation made the wellsprings of the chiefs' piety and patriotism gush out afresh, so that before providing for themselves they raised a large sum by voluntary contributions toward restoring the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. JFB notes the gifts of gold coin, here darics, a Persian coin, and of priests' garments were very appropriate in the circumstances, and that presents of garments or usable commodities accord with established Eastern custom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. JFB notes the exiles came again unto Jerusalem and Judah, every one unto his city, settling in Jerusalem, in the country, and in their villages as the work of restoration began. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. JFB notes the returning exiles, after preliminary work rearing habitations amid the ruins, addressed themselves to rebuilding the altar of burnt offering as the seventh month approached, resolving to celebrate the feast of tabernacles just as if the temple had been fully restored. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. JFB identifies Jeshua as the grandson of Seraiah, the high priest put to death at Riblah, and Zerubbabel as son of Pedaiah by nature but called Salathiel's son because brought up by him, and notes the altar was built of urgent necessity for atonement, blessing, and animating piety and patriotism for the national work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. JFB notes the altar was built to offer burnt offerings for atonement and to obtain divine blessing on the preparations for the temple, animating the people's piety and patriotism for the national work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. JFB notes the altar was reared upon its old foundation, occupying as nearly as possible its former site, and that the people resumed the daily service prescribed by the law without waiting till the temple should be rebuilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. JFB notes the feast of tabernacles was held on the fifteenth day of the seventh month, with the daily oblation revived at that time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. JFB notes the daily oblation and the annual seasons of solemn observance were revived together, including the continual offerings and the sacrifices of sabbaths, new moons, and holy feasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. JFB notes the people did not wait till the temple should be rebuilt, but at the outset resumed the daily service prescribed by the law, offering vows from the first day of the seventh month. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. JFB notes negotiations were opened with the Tyrians for workmen as well as timber, on the same terms and with the same views as Solomon had done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 58. JFB notes the Levites were appointed to set forward the work, that is, to act as overseers of the workmen and to direct and animate the laborers in the various departments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 63. JFB notes the painful emotions of the aged who had seen the first temple were excited by the contrast between its prosperous founding and the desolate, reduced state of the country, the inferior stones and smaller foundation, and above all the absence of the ark, the shekinah, and the Urim and Thummim, though the second temple's glory would outshine the first by receiving the incarnate Savior within its walls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 64. JFB notes that among Eastern people expressions of sorrow are always very loud and vehement, so that the wailing was sometimes not easily distinguishable from joyful acclamations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 65. JFB notes the shout of joy and the noise of weeping mingled so that the people could not discern one from the other, since Eastern expressions of sorrow are loud and vehement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 66. JFB identifies the adversaries of Judah and Benjamin as strangers settled in the land of Israel, whose hearing of the noise prompted their inquiry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 67. JFB identifies the adversaries as the strangers settled in the land, who perceived the returned captives were building the temple unto the Lord God of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 68. JFB explains the offer to build together came from Assyrian settlers whose descendants, a mongrel race called Samaritans, claimed to seek God but served him in a superstitious way of their own, a claim strikingly confirmed by Esar-haddon's annals of deportation and colonization. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 69. JFB notes the settlers' claim rests on Esar-haddon's deportation of Israelites and settlement of Babylonian colonists in their place, as inscribed on a cylinder in the British Museum, and that though instructed in the knowledge of God they served him superstitiously. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 70. JFB notes the refusal to cooperate with the Samaritans, from whatever motives it sprang, was overruled by Providence for ultimate good, since working together would have brought familiar acquaintanceship and intermarriage, a relapse into idolatry, and confusion in the genealogical evidence proving the descent of the Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 71. JFB notes the refusal rested on the commission given to the Jews alone, and that in their hostile and separate condition the two peoples jealously watched each other's proceedings, guarding the purity and honor of Mosaic worship and the integrity of the sacred books. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 72. JFB notes that exasperated by this repulse, the Samaritans endeavored by every means to molest the workmen and obstruct the building, and by bribes and clandestine arts at court labored to frustrate the effects of Cyrus's edict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 73. JFB notes the Samaritans' underhand dealings succeeded greatly, since Cyrus, frequently absent and absorbed in war, left government to Cambyses and then Smerdis, so that for a long period the progress of the work was very slow until the time of Darius. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a question investigated, not concluded)

The return register, told here and in Ezra 2

This section investigates a parallel; it does not conclude that one passage depends on the other, shares its authorship, or records the same event. Parallels and differences are both stated, and the question is left open where the evidence runs out. Interpreted Editorial framing for this section

Parallel. Both 1 Esdras 5 and Ezra 2 give a long register of the families, priests, Levites, singers, porters, and temple servants who went up from captivity, followed by the rebuilding of the altar and the laying of the temple foundations. Interpreted 1 Esdras 5; Ezra 2 (Standard corpus)

Difference. The registers differ in the spelling of names and in several of the numbers given, and 1 Esdras frames the return as the sequel to the contest of the three guardsmen (chapters 3-4), a frame Ezra does not use. Interpreted 1 Esdras 5; Ezra 2 (Standard corpus)

Unresolved. Whether these passages are related beyond the parallel itself is not established by the sources consulted for this page, and no dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty is claimed here. Unresolved No source consulted for this page establishes the relationship

JFB notes the refusal to cooperate with the Samaritans, from whatever motives it sprang, was overruled by Providence for ultimate good, since working together would have brought familiar acquaintanceship and intermarriage, a relapse into idolatry, and confusion in the genealogical evidence proving the descent of the Messiah. JFB notes the refusal rested on the commission given to the Jews alone, and that in their hostile and separate condition the two peoples jealously watched each other's proceedings, guarding the purity and honor of Mosaic worship and the integrity of the sacred books.   • Verse 72. JFB notes that exasperated by this repulse, the Samaritans endeavored by every means to molest the workmen and obstruct the building, and by bribes and clandestine arts at court labored to frustrate the effects of Cyrus's edict. JFB notes the Samaritans' underhand dealings succeeded greatly, since Cyrus, frequently absent and absorbed in war, left government to Cambyses and then Smerdis, so that for a long period the progress of the work was very slow until the time of Darius.
