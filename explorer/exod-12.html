The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt saying,

2 This month shall be unto you the beginning of months: it shall be the first month of the year to you.

3 Speak ye unto all the congregation of Israel, saying, In the tenth day of this month they shall take to them every man a lamb, according to the house of their fathers, a lamb for an house:

4 And if the household be too little for the lamb, let him and his neighbour next unto his house take it according to the number of the souls; every man according to his eating shall make your count for the lamb.

5 Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male of the first year: ye shall take it out from the sheep, or from the goats:

6 And ye shall keep it up until the fourteenth day of the same month: and the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it in the evening.

7 And they shall take of the blood, and strike it on the two side posts and on the upper door post of the houses, wherein they shall eat it.

8 And they shall eat the flesh in that night, roast with fire, and unleavened bread; and with bitter herbs they shall eat it.

9 Eat not of it raw, nor sodden at all with water, but roast with fire; his head with his legs, and with the purtenance thereof.

10 And ye shall let nothing of it remain until the morning; and that which remaineth of it until the morning ye shall burn with fire.

11 And thus shall ye eat it; with your loins girded, your shoes on your feet, and your staff in your hand; and ye shall eat it in haste: it is the LORD’s passover.

12 For I will pass through the land of Egypt this night, and will smite all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; and against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment: I am the LORD.

13 And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt.

14 And this day shall be unto you for a memorial; and ye shall keep it a feast to the LORD throughout your generations; ye shall keep it a feast by an ordinance for ever.

15 Seven days shall ye eat unleavened bread; even the first day ye shall put away leaven out of your houses: for whosoever eateth leavened bread from the first day until the seventh day, that soul shall be cut off from Israel.

16 And in the first day there shall be an holy convocation, and in the seventh day there shall be an holy convocation to you; no manner of work shall be done in them, save that which every man must eat, that only may be done of you.

17 And ye shall observe the feast of unleavened bread; for in this selfsame day have I brought your armies out of the land of Egypt: therefore shall ye observe this day in your generations by an ordinance for ever.

18 In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month at even, ye shall eat unleavened bread, until the one and twentieth day of the month at even.

19 Seven days shall there be no leaven found in your houses: for whosoever eateth that which is leavened, even that soul shall be cut off from the congregation of Israel, whether he be a stranger, or born in the land.

20 Ye shall eat nothing leavened; in all your habitations shall ye eat unleavened bread.

21 Then Moses called for all the elders of Israel, and said unto them, Draw out and take you a lamb according to your families, and kill the passover.

22 And ye shall take a bunch of hyssop, and dip it in the blood that is in the bason, and strike the lintel and the two side posts with the blood that is in the bason; and none of you shall go out at the door of his house until the morning.

23 For the LORD will pass through to smite the Egyptians; and when he seeth the blood upon the lintel, and on the two side posts, the LORD will pass over the door, and will not suffer the destroyer to come in unto your houses to smite you.

24 And ye shall observe this thing for an ordinance to thee and to thy sons for ever.

25 And it shall come to pass, when ye be come to the land which the LORD will give you, according as he hath promised, that ye shall keep this service.

26 And it shall come to pass, when your children shall say unto you, What mean ye by this service?

27 That ye shall say, It is the sacrifice of the LORD’s passover, who passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt, when he smote the Egyptians, and delivered our houses. And the people bowed the head and worshipped.

28 And the children of Israel went away, and did as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron, so did they.

29 And it came to pass, that at midnight the LORD smote all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh that sat on his throne unto the firstborn of the captive that was in the dungeon; and all the firstborn of cattle.

30 And Pharaoh rose up in the night, he, and all his servants, and all the Egyptians; and there was a great cry in Egypt; for there was not a house where there was not one dead.

31 And he called for Moses and Aaron by night, and said, Rise up, and get you forth from among my people, both ye and the children of Israel; and go, serve the LORD, as ye have said.

32 Also take your flocks and your herds, as ye have said, and be gone; and bless me also.

33 And the Egyptians were urgent upon the people, that they might send them out of the land in haste; for they said, We be all dead men.

34 And the people took their dough before it was leavened, their kneadingtroughs being bound up in their clothes upon their shoulders.

35 And the children of Israel did according to the word of Moses; and they borrowed of the Egyptians jewels of silver, and jewels of gold, and raiment:

36 And the LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians, so that they lent unto them such things as they required. And they spoiled the Egyptians.

37 And the children of Israel journeyed from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand on foot that were men, beside children.

38 And a mixed multitude went up also with them; and flocks, and herds, even very much cattle.

39 And they baked unleavened cakes of the dough which they brought forth out of Egypt, for it was not leavened; because they were thrust out of Egypt, and could not tarry, neither had they prepared for themselves any victual.

40 Now the sojourning of the children of Israel, who dwelt in Egypt, was four hundred and thirty years.

41 And it came to pass at the end of the four hundred and thirty years, even the selfsame day it came to pass, that all the hosts of the LORD went out from the land of Egypt.

42 It is a night to be much observed unto the LORD for bringing them out from the land of Egypt: this is that night of the LORD to be observed of all the children of Israel in their generations.

43 And the LORD said unto Moses and Aaron, This is the ordinance of the passover: There shall no stranger eat thereof:

44 But every man’s servant that is bought for money, when thou hast circumcised him, then shall he eat thereof.

45 A foreigner and an hired servant shall not eat thereof.

46 In one house shall it be eaten; thou shalt not carry forth ought of the flesh abroad out of the house; neither shall ye break a bone thereof.

47 All the congregation of Israel shall keep it.

48 And when a stranger shall sojourn with thee, and will keep the passover to the LORD, let all his males be circumcised, and then let him come near and keep it; and he shall be as one that is born in the land: for no uncircumcised person shall eat thereof.

49 One law shall be to him that is homeborn, and unto the stranger that sojourneth among you.

50 Thus did all the children of Israel; as the LORD commanded Moses and Aaron, so did they.

51 And it came to pass the selfsame day, that the LORD did bring the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their armies.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Each household takes a lamb without blemish, kills it at evening, and marks doorposts and lintel with its blood, eating it in haste with unleavened bread. At midnight the LORD strikes Egypt's firstborn and passes over the marked houses, Pharaoh drives Israel out by night, and the chapter sets down the lasting ordinance of Passover and unleavened bread. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD spake to Moses and Aaron in Egypt, giving the passover law before the deliverance itself. Henry observes God makes all things new to those he delivers: the night of departure would begin a new life for his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. This month would be the beginning of months, the first month of the year to Israel. Henry notes the calendar itself was reset: redemption became the starting point from which Israel counted time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. On the tenth day of the month every household was to take a lamb, a lamb for a house. Henry notes the lamb was to be chosen and set apart four days beforehand, denoting the Saviour marked out in God's purpose and promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. If a household was too little for a lamb, it was to join with its neighbour, reckoning according to what each man could eat. Henry observes the provision was communal: no house was left without the sacrifice, and none was to take more than it needed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The lamb was to be without blemish, a male of the first year, taken from the sheep or the goats. Henry notes the lamb was to be in its prime and without spot, as Christ offered himself in the midst of his days, a Lamb without spot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. They were to keep it until the fourteenth day, when the whole assembly of Israel would kill it in the evening. Henry observes the set time taught them to wait upon God's appointment, and the whole congregation acted together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They were to take of the blood and strike it on the two side posts and the upper door post of the houses where they ate it. Henry notes the blood was sprinkled openly on the door, denoting the open profession of faith in Christ, but never upon the threshold, lest the blood of the covenant be trampled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. They were to eat the flesh that night, roast with fire, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. Henry observes the meal was to be fed upon, not merely looked at: believers must by faith make Christ their own food and strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. It was not to be eaten raw or sodden with water, but roast with fire, its head with its legs and its purtenance. Henry notes the roasting denoted Christ's painful sufferings unto death, for the wrath of God is as fire, and Christ was made a curse for us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Nothing was to remain until the morning; what remained was to be burnt with fire. Henry observes the whole lamb was to be eaten at once: today Christ is offered, and is to be accepted while it is called today, before we sleep the sleep of death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. They were to eat it with loins girded, shoes on feet, and staff in hand, eating in haste, for it was the LORD's passover. Henry notes the posture preached readiness: those who feed on Christ must sit loose to the world, ready to depart at his call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. That night God would pass through Egypt and smite all the firstborn of man and beast, and execute judgment against all the gods of Egypt. Henry observes the stroke was both punishment and unmasking: Egypt's gods stood judged by the death of Egypt's sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The blood would be a token upon the houses, and when God saw the blood he would pass over them, and no destroying plague would fall on them. Henry notes the safety was not a reward of their righteousness but the gift of mercy, through the shedding and sprinkling of blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. This day would be a memorial, kept as a feast to the LORD throughout their generations by an ordinance forever. Henry observes the feast looked both ways: backward to deliverance remembered, and forward to the great sacrifice of the Lamb of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Seven days they were to eat unleavened bread, putting away leaven from the first day, on pain of being cut off from Israel. Henry notes the strictness taught sincerity: the feast must be kept without the leaven of malice and hypocrisy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The first day and the seventh day were holy convocations, in which no work was to be done save preparing food. Henry observes the week was hedged with rest, that holy joy might not be crowded out by common care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. They were to observe the feast of unleavened bread, because in that selfsame day God had brought their armies out of Egypt. Henry notes the reason given was the deed itself: the calendar remembered the deliverance that had made them a people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. From the fourteenth day at even until the twenty-first day at even they were to eat unleavened bread. Henry observes the fixed dates bound every household to the same remembrance, year after year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Seven days no leaven was to be found in their houses, and whoever ate leavened bread would be cut off, whether stranger or homeborn. Henry notes the severity showed how seriously God takes sincerity in worship: hidden leaven spoiled the whole feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. They were to eat nothing leavened; in all their habitations they were to eat unleavened bread. Henry observes the rule reached every house: holiness was not a temple matter only, but a household discipline. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Moses called for all the elders of Israel and told them to draw out and take a lamb for each family and kill the passover. Henry notes the elders received the charge first, that the whole people might learn it through their heads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. They were to take hyssop, dip it in the blood in the bason, and strike the lintel and the two side posts, and none was to go out of his house until morning. Henry notes faith was the bunch of hyssop, applying the blood to the soul, and safety lay in staying under its protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The LORD would pass through to smite the Egyptians, and seeing the blood he would pass over the door and not suffer the destroyer to enter. Henry observes the blood was the barrier: where it was, the destroyer had nothing to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. They were to observe this thing for an ordinance to themselves and their sons forever. Henry notes the perpetuity: the ordinance was not for one night but for all generations, until the true Lamb came. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. When they came to the promised land they were still to keep this service. Henry observes the feast would outlive the wilderness, a standing memorial in the land of rest itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. When their children asked, What mean ye by this service? they were to be told. Henry notes it is good for children to ask about the things of God, and good for parents to have an answer ready: they that ask for the way will find it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. They were to answer, It is the sacrifice of the LORD's passover, who passed over Israel's houses, smote the Egyptians, and delivered their own houses, and the people bowed their heads and worshipped. Henry observes instruction ended in worship: the story told became praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The children of Israel went away and did as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron, so did they. Henry notes the simple obedience: the whole people kept the ordinance exactly as given, on the very night of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. At midnight the LORD smote all the firstborn in Egypt, from Pharaoh's firstborn on his throne to the firstborn of the captive in the dungeon, and all the firstborn of cattle. Henry observes the stroke reached from throne to dungeon: prince and peasant stood on the same level before God's judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Pharaoh rose in the night, he and all his servants and all the Egyptians, and there was a great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house where there was not one dead. Henry bids the reader imagine the cry that rang through the land, a long loud shriek of agony from every dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron by night, saying, Rise up and get you forth from among my people, you and the children of Israel, and go serve the LORD as ye have said. Henry notes pride was now abased and he yielded: God's word will stand, and men get nothing by disputing or delaying submission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Take your flocks and herds, as ye have said, and be gone, and bless me also, said Pharaoh. Henry observes the king who had bargained over every hoof now surrendered all, and even begged a blessing from the men he had oppressed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. The Egyptians were urgent upon the people to send them out in haste, for they said, We be all dead men. Henry notes terror made Egypt pay Israel's wages: the people were provided for their journey by those who had enslaved them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The people took their dough before it was leavened, their kneadingtroughs bound up in their clothes upon their shoulders. Henry observes the haste was literal: the bread went unleavened because there was no time to wait. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Israel did according to Moses's word and borrowed of the Egyptians jewels of silver and gold and raiment. Henry notes the borrowing fulfilled the promise of chapter 3: they would not go out empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. The LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians, so that they lent them such things as they required, and they spoiled the Egyptians. Henry observes the Lord took care that hard-earned wages should be paid at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Israel journeyed from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand on foot that were men, besides children. Henry notes the host set forward without delay, a nation born in a night. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. A mixed multitude went up also with them, with flocks and herds, even very much cattle. Henry observes some came out of curiosity or love of Israel's religion, but there were always those among Israel who were not Israelites, as there are still hypocrites in the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. They baked unleavened cakes of the dough brought out of Egypt, for it was not leavened, because they were thrust out and could not tarry, nor had they prepared victuals. Henry notes the cakes themselves became a memorial of haste: deliverance came too swiftly for bread to rise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. The sojourning of Israel in Egypt was four hundred and thirty years. Henry notes the great event came 430 years from the promise to Abraham: though God's promises are not performed quickly, they will be performed in their season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. At the end of the four hundred and thirty years, even the selfsame day, all the hosts of the LORD went out from Egypt. Henry observes the punctuality: the promise kept its very day, after more than four centuries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. It is a night to be much observed unto the LORD for bringing them out of Egypt, a night to be observed by all Israel in their generations. Henry notes the great things God does for his people are to be remembered throughout all ages, especially the work of redemption by Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. This is the ordinance of the passover: no stranger shall eat thereof. Henry notes the feast belonged to the covenant people, and sharing in God's mercies meant joining his covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. But every man's servant bought for money, when circumcised, might eat thereof. Henry observes the door stood open to the household servant who entered the covenant sign, for grace reaches beyond blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. A foreigner and a hired servant shall not eat thereof. Henry notes the distinction: the sojourner who would not join the covenant could not share its feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. In one house shall it be eaten; none of the flesh was to be carried abroad out of the house, neither was a bone to be broken. Henry notes the unbroken bone was fulfilled in Christ, denoting his unbroken strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. All the congregation of Israel shall keep it. Henry notes the universality: the passover was not for priests alone but for the whole congregation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. When a stranger sojourned and would keep the passover, all his males were to be circumcised, and then he might come near and keep it, as one born in the land. Henry notes here an early indication of favour to the Gentiles: privilege rested on covenant, not on descent from Abraham. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. One law shall be to him that is homeborn and unto the stranger that sojourneth among you. Henry observes the single law taught Israel that God's favour was not a matter of birth but of covenant obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Thus did all Israel, as the LORD commanded Moses and Aaron, so did they. Henry notes the obedience was complete and corporate, the whole people acting as one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. The selfsame day the LORD brought Israel out of Egypt by their armies. Henry closes the chapter on the day itself: the night of the passover became the day of the Exodus, and a people went out in order, like an army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 30. Pharaoh rose in the night, he and all his servants and all the Egyptians, and there was a great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house where there was not one dead. Henry bids the reader imagine the cry that rang through the land, a long loud shriek of agony from every dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron by night, saying, Rise up and get you forth from among my people, you and the children of Israel, and go serve the LORD as ye have said. Henry notes pride was now abased and he yielded: God's word will stand, and men get nothing by disputing or delaying submission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Take your flocks and herds, as ye have said, and be gone, and bless me also, said Pharaoh. Henry observes the king who had bargained over every hoof now surrendered all, and even begged a blessing from the men he had oppressed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. The Egyptians were urgent upon the people to send them out in haste, for they said, We be all dead men. Henry notes terror made Egypt pay Israel's wages: the people were provided for their journey by those who had enslaved them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The people took their dough before it was leavened, their kneadingtroughs bound up in their clothes upon their shoulders. Henry observes the haste was literal: the bread went unleavened because there was no time to wait. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Israel did according to Moses's word and borrowed of the Egyptians jewels of silver and gold and raiment. Henry notes the borrowing fulfilled the promise of chapter 3: they would not go out empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. The LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians, so that they lent them such things as they required, and they spoiled the Egyptians. Henry observes the Lord took care that hard-earned wages should be paid at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Israel journeyed from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand on foot that were men, besides children. Henry notes the host set forward without delay, a nation born in a night. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. A mixed multitude went up also with them, with flocks and herds, even very much cattle. Henry observes some came out of curiosity or love of Israel's religion, but there were always those among Israel who were not Israelites, as there are still hypocrites in the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. They baked unleavened cakes of the dough brought out of Egypt, for it was not leavened, because they were thrust out and could not tarry, nor had they prepared victuals. Henry notes the cakes themselves became a memorial of haste: deliverance came too swiftly for bread to rise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. The sojourning of Israel in Egypt was four hundred and thirty years. Henry notes the great event came 430 years from the promise to Abraham: though God's promises are not performed quickly, they will be performed in their season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. At the end of the four hundred and thirty years, even the selfsame day, all the hosts of the LORD went out from Egypt. Henry observes the punctuality: the promise kept its very day, after more than four centuries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. It is a night to be much observed unto the LORD for bringing them out of Egypt, a night to be observed by all Israel in their generations. Henry notes the great things God does for his people are to be remembered throughout all ages, especially the work of redemption by Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. This is the ordinance of the passover: no stranger shall eat thereof. Henry notes the feast belonged to the covenant people, and sharing in God's mercies meant joining his covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. But every man's servant bought for money, when circumcised, might eat thereof. Henry observes the door stood open to the household servant who entered the covenant sign, for grace reaches beyond blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. A foreigner and a hired servant shall not eat thereof. Henry notes the distinction: the sojourner who would not join the covenant could not share its feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. In one house shall it be eaten; none of the flesh was to be carried abroad out of the house, neither was a bone to be broken. Henry notes the unbroken bone was fulfilled in Christ, denoting his unbroken strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. All the congregation of Israel shall keep it. Henry notes the universality: the passover was not for priests alone but for the whole congregation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. When a stranger sojourned and would keep the passover, all his males were to be circumcised, and then he might come near and keep it, as one born in the land. Henry notes here an early indication of favour to the Gentiles: privilege rested on covenant, not on descent from Abraham. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. One law shall be to him that is homeborn and unto the stranger that sojourneth among you. Henry observes the single law taught Israel that God's favour was not a matter of birth but of covenant obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Thus did all Israel, as the LORD commanded Moses and Aaron, so did they. Henry notes the obedience was complete and corporate, the whole people acting as one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. The selfsame day the LORD brought Israel out of Egypt by their armies. Henry closes the chapter on the day itself: the night of the passover became the day of the Exodus, and a people went out in order, like an army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
