The Text — Sirach 6 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Instead of a friend become not an enemy; for [thereby] thou shalt inherit an ill name, shame, and reproach: even so shall a sinner that hath a double tongue.

2 Extol not thyself in the counsel of thine own heart; that thy soul be not torn in pieces as a bull [straying alone.]

3 Thou shalt eat up thy leaves, and lose thy fruit, and leave thyself as a dry tree.

4 A wicked soul shall destroy him that hath it, and shall make him to be laughed to scorn of his enemies.

5 Sweet language will multiply friends: and a fairspeaking tongue will increase kind greetings.

6 Be in peace with many: nevertheless have but one counsellor of a thousand.

7 If thou wouldest get a friend, prove him first and be not hasty to credit him.

8 For some man is a friend for his own occasion, and will not abide in the day of thy trouble.

9 And there is a friend, who being turned to enmity, and strife will discover thy reproach.

10 Again, some friend is a companion at the table, and will not continue in the day of thy affliction.

11 But in thy prosperity he will be as thyself, and will be bold over thy servants.

12 If thou be brought low, he will be against thee, and will hide himself from thy face.

13 Separate thyself from thine enemies, and take heed of thy friends.

14 A faithful friend is a strong defence: and he that hath found such an one hath found a treasure.

15 Nothing doth countervail a faithful friend, and his excellency is invaluable.

16 A faithful friend is the medicine of life; and they that fear the Lord shall find him.

17 Whoso feareth the Lord shall direct his friendship aright: for as he is, so shall his neighbour be also.

18 My son, gather instruction from thy youth up: so shalt thou find wisdom till thine old age.

19 Come unto her as one that ploweth and soweth, and wait for her good fruits: for thou shalt not toil much in labouring about her, but thou shalt eat of her fruits right soon.

20 She is very unpleasant to the unlearned: he that is without understanding will not remain with her.

21 She will lie upon him as a mighty stone of trial; and he will cast her from him ere it be long.

22 For wisdom is according to her name, and she is not manifest unto many.

23 Give ear, my son, receive my advice, and refuse not my counsel,

24 And put thy feet into her fetters, and thy neck into her chain.

25 Bow down thy shoulder, and bear her, and be not grieved with her bonds.

26 Come unto her with thy whole heart, and keep her ways with all thy power.

27 Search, and seek, and she shall be made known unto thee: and when thou hast got hold of her, let her not go.

28 For at the last thou shalt find her rest, and that shall be turned to thy joy.

29 Then shall her fetters be a strong defence for thee, and her chains a robe of glory.

30 For there is a golden ornament upon her, and her bands are purple lace.

31 Thou shalt put her on as a robe of honour, and shalt put her about thee as a crown of joy.

32 My son, if thou wilt, thou shalt be taught: and if thou wilt apply thy mind, thou shalt be prudent.

33 If thou love to hear, thou shalt receive understanding: and if thou bow thine ear, thou shalt be wise,

34 Stand in the multitude of the elders; and cleave unto him that is wise.

35 Be willing to hear every godly discourse; and let not the parables of understanding escape thee.

36 And if thou seest a man of understanding, get thee betimes unto him, and let thy foot wear the steps of his door.

37 Let thy mind be upon the ordinances of the Lord and meditate continually in his commandments: he shall establish thine heart, and give thee wisdom at thine owns desire.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

True friendship is tested, not assumed: a friend can turn enemy, while a faithful friend is a strong defence. The second half turns to wisdom herself, to be approached like ploughing and sowing, her discipline worn as fetters that become a robe of honour, with the mind kept on the Lord's ordinances. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Instead of a friend, do not become an enemy, for thereby you will inherit an ill name, shame, and reproach, as does a sinner with a double tongue. A friendship turned to enmity leaves a bad name, and double-tongued sin produces the same shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Do not extol yourself in the counsel of your own heart, lest your soul be torn in pieces like a bull straying alone. Self-praise in secret counsel isolates a person like a straying bull, and the soul is torn by its own pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. You will eat up your leaves, lose your fruit, and leave yourself as a dry tree. Pride consumes a person's own life: leaves eaten, fruit lost, and only a dry tree left. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. A wicked soul will destroy him who has it, and make him laughed to scorn by his enemies. Wickedness within destroys its owner and hands his enemies their laughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Sweet language multiplies friends, and a fair-speaking tongue increases kind greetings. Gracious speech is fruitful: it grows friendships and draws kind greetings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. Be at peace with many, but have only one counsellor of a thousand. Peaceableness should be broad, but intimate counsel should be narrow and tested. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. If you would get a friend, prove him first, and do not be hasty to credit him. Friendship must be tested before it is trusted; haste in crediting a stranger is folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Some man is a friend for his own occasion and will not abide in the day of your trouble. A friend of convenience disappears when trouble makes friendship costly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. There is a friend who, turned to enmity, will in strife discover your reproach. A false friend turned enemy uses quarrels to expose your shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Again, some friend is a companion at the table and will not continue in the day of your affliction. A table companion shares feasts, not afflictions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. In your prosperity he will be as yourself and will be bold over your servants. The fair-weather friend acts like an equal in good times and presumes over your household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. If you are brought low, he will be against you and will hide himself from your face. When you fall, that same friend turns against you and avoids your face. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. Separate yourself from your enemies, and take heed of your friends. Enemies require distance, and even friends require watchfulness, since not every friend is faithful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. A faithful friend is a strong defence, and he who has found such a one has found a treasure. Faithfulness in friendship is protection, and finding it is finding treasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. Nothing countervails a faithful friend, and his excellency is invaluable. No price balances a faithful friend, and his worth cannot be valued. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. A faithful friend is the medicine of life, and those who fear the Lord will find him. A faithful friend heals life's ills, and it is the God-fearing who find such friends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. Whoever fears the Lord will direct his friendship aright, for as he is, so will his neighbour be also. Reverence orders friendship rightly, and a person's friends tend to mirror what he himself is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. Gather instruction from your youth up, and you will find wisdom till your old age. Instruction gathered early becomes wisdom that lasts into old age. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. Come to wisdom as one who ploughs and sows, and wait for her good fruits; you will not toil much in labouring about her, but you will eat of her fruits right soon. Wisdom repays farming-like labour quickly: the work is light beside the early harvest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 20. Wisdom is very unpleasant to the unlearned, and he who is without understanding will not remain with her. To the untaught, wisdom feels harsh, and the uncomprehending will not stay with her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 21. She will lie upon him as a mighty stone of trial, and he will cast her from him before long. Wisdom weighs on the unlearned like a heavy testing stone, and he soon throws her off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 22. Wisdom is according to her name, and she is not manifest to many. Wisdom lives up to her demanding name, and that is why she is not plain to the many. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 23. Give ear, receive advice, and refuse not counsel. The first posture toward wisdom is listening and accepting counsel rather than refusing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 24. Put your feet into her fetters and your neck into her chain. Wisdom's discipline is pictured as bonds willingly entered, not chains imposed by force. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 25. Bow down your shoulder, bear her, and do not be grieved with her bonds. Carrying wisdom's discipline should be done without resentment at its restraint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 26. Come to her with your whole heart, and keep her ways with all your power. Half-hearted approach will not do; wisdom asks for the whole heart and full strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 27. Search and seek, and she will be made known to you; when you have got hold of her, do not let her go. Diligent seeking is answered by wisdom revealing herself, and the find must then be held fast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 28. At the last you will find her rest, and that will be turned to your joy. The end of wisdom's discipline is rest, and that rest becomes joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 29. Then her fetters will be a strong defence for you, and her chains a robe of glory. The bonds that once restrained become protection and honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 30. There is a golden ornament upon her, and her bands are purple lace. Wisdom's restraints are described as gold and purple, the dress of royalty, not of slavery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 31. You will put her on as a robe of honour and put her about you as a crown of joy. Wisdom, once worn as bonds, is finally worn as honour's robe and joy's crown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 32. If you will, you will be taught, and if you apply your mind, you will be prudent. Willingness opens teaching, and applied attention produces prudence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 33. If you love to hear, you will receive understanding, and if you bow your ear, you will be wise. Love of listening brings understanding, and a bowed ear is the road to wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 34. Stand in the multitude of the elders, and cleave to him who is wise. Company with elders and close attachment to a wise man are the school of wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 35. Be willing to hear every godly discourse, and let not the parables of understanding escape you. Every godly discourse deserves a willing hearing, and pointed sayings of understanding should be held, not lost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 36. If you see a man of understanding, get to him betimes, and let your foot wear the steps of his door. Seek out the understanding man early and often, until your feet have worn a path to his door. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 37. Let your mind be upon the ordinances of the Lord and meditate continually in his commandments; he will establish your heart and give you wisdom at your own desire. Meditation on God's ordinances steadies the heart, and God himself grants the wisdom so desired. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
