The Text — Psalms 5 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Give ear to my words, O LORD, consider my meditation.

2 Hearken unto the voice of my cry, my King, and my God: for unto thee will I pray.

3 My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up.

4 For thou art not a God that hath pleasure in wickedness: neither shall evil dwell with thee.

5 The foolish shall not stand in thy sight: thou hatest all workers of iniquity.

6 Thou shalt destroy them that speak leasing: the LORD will abhor the bloody and deceitful man.

7 But as for me, I will come into thy house in the multitude of thy mercy: and in thy fear will I worship toward thy holy temple.

8 Lead me, O LORD, in thy righteousness because of mine enemies; make thy way straight before my face.

9 For there is no faithfulness in their mouth; their inward part is very wickedness; their throat is an open sepulchre; they flatter with their tongue.

10 Destroy thou them, O God; let them fall by their own counsels; cast them out in the multitude of their transgressions; for they have rebelled against thee.

11 But let all those that put their trust in thee rejoice: let them ever shout for joy, because thou defendest them: let them also that love thy name be joyful in thee.

12 For thou, LORD, wilt bless the righteous; with favour wilt thou compass him as with a shield.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A morning prayer asking God to hear both words and unspoken meditation. God, who hates wickedness and falsehood, is set against enemies whose speech is flattery over corruption, and the psalm ends with joy and shield-like favour for all who trust in him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David asks God to give ear to his words and consider his meditation: prayer includes both spoken words and the unspoken thoughts behind them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He addresses God as his King and his God, and resolves to pray to him alone; dependence is directed, not vague. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Morning prayer orders the day: he directs his prayer to God early and looks up, expecting an answer as a watchman waits for dawn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. God takes no pleasure in wickedness, and evil cannot dwell with him; his holiness sets the character of all who approach him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The foolish cannot stand in his sight, and he hates workers of iniquity: sin is not merely pitied, it is abhorred by a holy God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Liars shall be destroyed, and the bloody and deceitful man is abhorred by the Lord; falsehood and violence are named because they were David's enemies' weapons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. By contrast David enters God's house in the multitude of his mercy, worshipping toward the temple in reverent fear; mercy, not merit, is his entrance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He asks to be led in God's righteousness because of his enemies, with the way made straight before him, so that he neither strays nor stumbles into their traps. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. His enemies' mouths have no faithfulness; inwardly they are wickedness, their throat an open grave, their tongue flattery — corruption that speaks fair and means harm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Their own counsels should bring them down, and their many transgressions cast them out, for they have rebelled against God himself, not merely against David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. All who trust in God shall rejoice and shout for joy, because he defends them; those who love his name find their joy in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The Lord blesses the righteous and compasses him with favour as with a shield: favour surrounds the godly on every side, as a shield covers the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
