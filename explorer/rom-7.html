The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Romans 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Know ye not, brethren, (for I speak to them that know the law,) how that the law hath dominion over a man as long as he liveth?

2 For the woman which hath an husband is bound by the law to her husband so long as he liveth; but if the husband be dead, she is loosed from the law of her husband.

3 So then if, while her husband liveth, she be married to another man, she shall be called an adulteress: but if her husband be dead, she is free from that law; so that she is no adulteress, though she be married to another man.

4 Wherefore, my brethren, ye also are become dead to the law by the body of Christ; that ye should be married to another, even to him who is raised from the dead, that we should bring forth fruit unto God.

5 For when we were in the flesh, the motions of sins, which were by the law, did work in our members to bring forth fruit unto death.

6 But now we are delivered from the law, that being dead wherein we were held; that we should serve in newness of spirit, and not in the oldness of the letter.

7 What shall we say then? Is the law sin? God forbid. Nay, I had not known sin, but by the law: for I had not known lust, except the law had said, Thou shalt not covet.

8 But sin, taking occasion by the commandment, wrought in me all manner of concupiscence. For without the law sin was dead.

9 For I was alive without the law once: but when the commandment came, sin revived, and I died.

10 And the commandment, which was ordained to life, I found to be unto death.

11 For sin, taking occasion by the commandment, deceived me, and by it slew me.

12 Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good.

13 Was then that which is good made death unto me? God forbid. But sin, that it might appear sin, working death in me by that which is good; that sin by the commandment might become exceeding sinful.

14 For we know that the law is spiritual: but I am carnal, sold under sin.

15 For that which I do I allow not: for what I would, that do I not; but what I hate, that do I.

16 If then I do that which I would not, I consent unto the law that it is good.

17 Now then it is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me.

18 For I know that in me (that is, in my flesh,) dwelleth no good thing: for to will is present with me; but how to perform that which is good I find not.

19 For the good that I would I do not: but the evil which I would not, that I do.

20 Now if I do that I would not, it is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me.

21 I find then a law, that, when I would do good, evil is present with me.

22 For I delight in the law of God after the inward man:

23 But I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind, and bringing me into captivity to the law of sin which is in my members.

24 O wretched man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death?

25 I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord. So then with the mind I myself serve the law of God; but with the flesh the law of sin.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Using marriage as an analogy, Paul explains that death frees a person from the law's dominion, so believers, dead to the law through Christ's body, now serve in newness of spirit rather than oldness of letter. The law itself is holy, but it exposes sin, and Paul describes the painful inner conflict of willing the good while doing the evil, ending with thanks to God through Jesus Christ, who delivers him. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The law has dominion over a man as long as he lives; Paul speaks to those who know the law. JFB notes that death ends legal obligation, which is the key to the whole chapter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. A married woman is bound by law to her husband while he lives, but if he dies she is loosed from her husband's law. Henry observes that death dissolves even the strongest legal bond. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. While her husband lives, marriage to another makes her an adulteress, but if he dies she is free, even though married to another. JFB notes the illustration prepares the claim that believers died to the law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Likewise, brethren, you are become dead to the law by Christ's body, that you should be married to another, to him raised from the dead, that we should bring forth fruit to God. Henry stresses that death to the law is not lawlessness but remarriage to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. When we were in the flesh, sinful motions stirred by the law worked in our members to bring forth fruit unto death. JFB notes that the law, by forbidding sin, can stir the very sin it forbids in a fallen heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Now we are delivered from the law, being dead to that in which we were held, that we should serve in newness of spirit, not in oldness of the letter. Henry observes that service continues, but its spring is the Spirit, not the letter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. What shall we say then? Is the law sin? God forbid. Paul would not have known lust except the law had said, you shall not covet. JFB stresses that the law exposes sin; it does not create the sinfulness it exposes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Sin, taking occasion by the commandment, worked in Paul all manner of concupiscence, for without the law sin was dead. Henry notes that a forbidden thing wakens desire in a corrupt heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Paul was alive without the law once, but when the commandment came, sin revived and he died. JFB reads this as the awakening of conscience: the law made him feel his death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The commandment, ordained to life, was found by Paul to be unto death. Henry observes that a law meant to guide to life condemns the one who breaks it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Sin, taking occasion by the commandment, deceived Paul and by it slew him. JFB notes sin's deceit: it promises freedom and delivers death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Therefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, just, and good. Henry stresses that the fault lies in sin, not in the law that condemns it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Was what is good made death to Paul? God forbid; but sin, that it might appear sin, worked death in him by what is good, that sin might become exceedingly sinful. JFB notes that the law's goodness makes sin's evil unmistakable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The law is spiritual, but Paul is carnal, sold under sin. Henry observes that the law's spirituality condemns a nature that is fleshly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. What Paul does he does not allow, for what he would he does not, but what he hates he does. JFB notes the inner war: the renewed will and the remaining corruption contend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. If Paul does what he would not, he consents to the law that it is good. Henry observes that even unwilling sin testifies that the law is right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. It is no more Paul that does it, but sin dwelling in him. JFB stresses that indwelling sin, not the new man, is the author of the evil done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. In Paul's flesh dwells no good thing, for to will is present with him, but how to perform what is good he finds not. Henry notes the painful gap between willing good and doing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The good Paul would he does not, but the evil he would not, that he does. JFB observes that this repeated confession is the experience of a believer, not an excuse for sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. If Paul does what he would not, it is no more he that does it, but sin dwelling in him. Henry stresses the distinction between the man and the corruption that still lodges in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Paul finds a law: when he would do good, evil is present with him. JFB notes that this law of indwelling sin opposes the law of God in the mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Paul delights in God's law after the inward man. Henry observes that delight in the law marks the new nature, whatever the flesh does. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. He sees another law in his members, warring against the law of his mind and bringing him into captivity to sin's law in his members. JFB notes the warfare is real and lifelong, though not hopeless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. O wretched man that I am! Who shall deliver me from the body of this death? Henry notes that the cry of misery is itself the doorway to deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Paul thanks God through Jesus Christ our Lord; so with the mind he serves God's law, but with the flesh sin's law. JFB stresses that deliverance is in Christ, while the conflict itself continues in this life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • oldness of the letter. Service under the written code of the law, contrasted with serving in newness of spirit. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • the law of sin. Paul's name for the pull of sin dwelling in his members, warring against the law of his mind. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • wretched man. Paul's cry in the midst of the conflict, answered by thanks to God through Jesus Christ. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
