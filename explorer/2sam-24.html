The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 24 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And again the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel, and he moved David against them to say, Go, number Israel and Judah.

2 For the king said to Joab the captain of the host, which was with him, Go now through all the tribes of Israel, from Dan even to Beersheba, and number ye the people, that I may know the number of the people.

3 And Joab said unto the king, Now the LORD thy God add unto the people, how many soever they be, an hundredfold, and that the eyes of my lord the king may see it: but why doth my lord the king delight in this thing?

4 Notwithstanding the king’s word prevailed against Joab, and against the captains of the host. And Joab and the captains of the host went out from the presence of the king, to number the people of Israel.

5 And they passed over Jordan, and pitched in Aroer, on the right side of the city that lieth in the midst of the river of Gad, and toward Jazer:

6 Then they came to Gilead, and to the land of Tahtimhodshi; and they came to Danjaan, and about to Zidon,

7 And came to the strong hold of Tyre, and to all the cities of the Hivites, and of the Canaanites: and they went out to the south of Judah, even to Beersheba.

8 So when they had gone through all the land, they came to Jerusalem at the end of nine months and twenty days.

9 And Joab gave up the sum of the number of the people unto the king: and there were in Israel eight hundred thousand valiant men that drew the sword; and the men of Judah were five hundred thousand men.

10 And David’s heart smote him after that he had numbered the people. And David said unto the LORD, I have sinned greatly in that I have done: and now, I beseech thee, O LORD, take away the iniquity of thy servant; for I have done very foolishly.

11 For when David was up in the morning, the word of the LORD came unto the prophet Gad, David’s seer, saying,

12 Go and say unto David, Thus saith the LORD, I offer thee three things; choose thee one of them, that I may do it unto thee.

13 So Gad came to David, and told him, and said unto him, Shall seven years of famine come unto thee in thy land? or wilt thou flee three months before thine enemies, while they pursue thee? or that there be three days’ pestilence in thy land? now advise, and see what answer I shall return to him that sent me.

14 And David said unto Gad, I am in a great strait: let us fall now into the hand of the LORD; for his mercies are great: and let me not fall into the hand of man.

15 So the LORD sent a pestilence upon Israel from the morning even to the time appointed: and there died of the people from Dan even to Beersheba seventy thousand men.

16 And when the angel stretched out his hand upon Jerusalem to destroy it, the LORD repented him of the evil, and said to the angel that destroyed the people, It is enough: stay now thine hand. And the angel of the LORD was by the threshingplace of Araunah the Jebusite.

17 And David spake unto the LORD when he saw the angel that smote the people, and said, Lo, I have sinned, and I have done wickedly: but these sheep, what have they done? let thine hand, I pray thee, be against me, and against my father’s house.

18 And Gad came that day to David, and said unto him, Go up, rear an altar unto the LORD in the threshingfloor of Araunah the Jebusite.

19 And David, according to the saying of Gad, went up as the LORD commanded.

20 And Araunah looked, and saw the king and his servants coming on toward him: and Araunah went out, and bowed himself before the king on his face upon the ground.

21 And Araunah said, Wherefore is my lord the king come to his servant? And David said, To buy the threshingfloor of thee, to build an altar unto the LORD, that the plague may be stayed from the people.

22 And Araunah said unto David, Let my lord the king take and offer up what seemeth good unto him: behold, here be oxen for burnt sacrifice, and threshing instruments and other instruments of the oxen for wood.

23 All these things did Araunah, as a king, give unto the king. And Araunah said unto the king, The LORD thy God accept thee.

24 And the king said unto Araunah, Nay; but I will surely buy it of thee at a price: neither will I offer burnt offerings unto the LORD my God of that which doth cost me nothing. So David bought the threshingfloor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver.

25 And David built there an altar unto the LORD, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. So the LORD was intreated for the land, and the plague was stayed from Israel.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

David orders a census of Israel, against Joab's protest, and when his conscience strikes him the LORD offers three punishments; David chooses pestilence, and seventy thousand die. At Gad's word David buys Araunah's threshingfloor, builds an altar, and the plague is stayed. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Again the anger of the Lord is kindled against Israel, and he moves David against them to say, Go, number Israel and Judah. God is not the author of sin, Henry insists, but as righteous Judge he permits Satan's suggestion to become the occasion for punishing Israel's own sins, as he once brought famine for Saul's sin and now pestilence for David's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The king tells Joab, captain of the host, to go through all Israel's tribes from Dan to Beersheba and number the people, that he may know the number. The worst thing in the numbering, Henry says, is that David does it in the pride of his heart, a proud conceit of his own greatness and a proud confidence in his own strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Joab answers that the Lord his God add to the people a hundredfold, however many they are, and the king's eyes may see it, but why does the king delight in this thing? Even Joab discerns David's folly and vain-glory, and respectfully tries to divert his pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Notwithstanding, the king's word prevails against Joab and the captains, and they go out from the king's presence to number Israel. It is an unhappiness to great men, Henry notes, to have about them those who will aid them in what is evil, and Joab applies himself with reluctancy to the unpleasing task. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. They pass over Jordan and pitch in Aroer, on the right side of the city in the midst of the river of Gad, toward Jazer. The count begins in the most distant places in the east, beyond Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Then they come to Gilead and Tahtimhodshi, to Danjaan and about to Zidon. The circuit moves northward through Gilead to Dan and toward the coast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They come to the stronghold of Tyre and all the cities of the Hivites and Canaanites, and go out to the south of Judah, even to Beersheba. From Tyre in the east to Beersheba in the south, the whole land is traversed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When they have gone through all the land, they come to Jerusalem at the end of nine months and twenty days. Above nine months are spent in the account, with great trouble and amazement occasioned in the country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Joab gives the sum to the king: in Israel eight hundred thousand valiant men drawing the sword, and in Judah five hundred thousand men. Whether the numbers fed David's pride or mortified it we are not told, Henry observes, but the people are very many, evidence of a fruitful land maintaining thousands in a narrow compass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. David's heart smites him after numbering the people, and he says to the Lord, I have sinned greatly; take away the iniquity of thy servant, for I have done very foolishly. During the nine months he was not sensible of his sin, Henry notes, but when the account is laid before him, his conscience awakens just when he promised himself pleasure, and it is a good sign of grace to have a heart that smites for sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. When David is up in the morning, the word of the Lord comes to Gad, David's seer. Gad, always at hand as confessor and counsellor, is directed by God before David can speak to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Go and say to David, the Lord says, I offer thee three things; choose one, that I may do it to thee. David must be corrected, Henry says, for pride is first among the seven things God hates, and the punishment must answer the sin by making fewer the people he was proud of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Gad tells David: shall seven years of famine come, or wilt thou flee three months before thine enemies, or three days' pestilence in thy land? Now advise, and see what answer to return to him that sent me. God gives leave to choose the rod, Henry observes, to humble David more, and that he may bear the chosen rod more patiently. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Sin brings men into straits, Henry notes, and David casts himself on God, whose tender mercies are not cruel as men's are, and God chooses the shortest scourge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 16. When the angel stretches his hand on Jerusalem to destroy it, the Lord repents of the evil and says to the destroying angel, It is enough, stay now thine hand, and the angel is by Araunah the Jebusite's threshingfloor. God repents of the judgment, Henry says, and David is thereby made more penitent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Let thine hand be against me and my father's house. The shepherd-king asks that the blow fall on him, not on the flock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Gad comes that day and tells David to go up and rear an altar to the Lord in Araunah's threshingfloor. The place where the angel stopped becomes the place of reconciliation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. David, according to Gad's word, goes up as the Lord commanded. Obedience follows confession without delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Araunah looks, sees the king and his servants coming, goes out, and bows before the king with his face to the ground. The Jebusite landowner receives his conqueror as a suppliant king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Araunah asks why the king has come to his servant, and David says, To buy the threshingfloor, to build an altar to the Lord, that the plague may be stayed from the people. The purchase has a stated purpose: an altar where judgment stopped. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Araunah tells David to take and offer what seems good, with oxen for burnt sacrifice and threshing instruments and yokes for wood. He generously offers not only the ground but the sacrifices and implements, all gratis. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. All these things Araunah, as a king, gives to the king, and says, The Lord thy God accept thee. He gives as a king, Henry notes, with a prince's spirit, esteeming David highly and desiring the plague stayed, counting the honour of its staying at his floor a valuable consideration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. The king answers, Nay, but I will surely buy it of thee at a price, neither will I offer burnt offerings to the Lord my God of that which costs me nothing. Two generous souls are well met, Henry says: Araunah is willing to give, but David is determined to buy, for those know not what religion is whose chief care is to make it cheap and easy to themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. David builds an altar to the Lord there and offers burnt and peace offerings, so the Lord is entreated for the land and the plague is stayed from Israel. The book that began with a king's house ends with an altar on a purchased floor, and judgment gives way to entreated mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
