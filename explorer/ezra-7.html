The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ezra 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now after these things, in the reign of Artaxerxes king of Persia, Ezra the son of Seraiah, the son of Azariah, the son of Hilkiah,

2 The son of Shallum, the son of Zadok, the son of Ahitub,

3 The son of Amariah, the son of Azariah, the son of Meraioth,

4 The son of Zerahiah, the son of Uzzi, the son of Bukki,

5 The son of Abishua, the son of Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron the chief priest:

6 This Ezra went up from Babylon; and he was a ready scribe in the law of Moses, which the LORD God of Israel had given: and the king granted him all his request, according to the hand of the LORD his God upon him.

7 And there went up some of the children of Israel, and of the priests, and the Levites, and the singers, and the porters, and the Nethinims, unto Jerusalem, in the seventh year of Artaxerxes the king.

8 And he came to Jerusalem in the fifth month, which was in the seventh year of the king.

9 For upon the first day of the first month began he to go up from Babylon, and on the first day of the fifth month came he to Jerusalem, according to the good hand of his God upon him.

10 For Ezra had prepared his heart to seek the law of the LORD, and to do it, and to teach in Israel statutes and judgments.

11 Now this is the copy of the letter that the king Artaxerxes gave unto Ezra the priest, the scribe, even a scribe of the words of the commandments of the LORD, and of his statutes to Israel.

12 Artaxerxes, king of kings, unto Ezra the priest, a scribe of the law of the God of heaven, perfect peace, and at such a time.

13 I make a decree, that all they of the people of Israel, and of his priests and Levites, in my realm, which are minded of their own freewill to go up to Jerusalem, go with thee.

14 Forasmuch as thou art sent of the king, and of his seven counsellors, to enquire concerning Judah and Jerusalem, according to the law of thy God which is in thine hand;

15 And to carry the silver and gold, which the king and his counsellors have freely offered unto the God of Israel, whose habitation is in Jerusalem,

16 And all the silver and gold that thou canst find in all the province of Babylon, with the freewill offering of the people, and of the priests, offering willingly for the house of their God which is in Jerusalem:

17 That thou mayest buy speedily with this money bullocks, rams, lambs, with their meat offerings and their drink offerings, and offer them upon the altar of the house of your God which is in Jerusalem.

18 And whatsoever shall seem good to thee, and to thy brethren, to do with the rest of the silver and the gold, that do after the will of your God.

19 The vessels also that are given thee for the service of the house of thy God, those deliver thou before the God of Jerusalem.

20 And whatsoever more shall be needful for the house of thy God, which thou shalt have occasion to bestow, bestow it out of the king’s treasure house.

21 And I, even I Artaxerxes the king, do make a decree to all the treasurers which are beyond the river, that whatsoever Ezra the priest, the scribe of the law of the God of heaven, shall require of you, it be done speedily,

22 Unto an hundred talents of silver, and to an hundred measures of wheat, and to an hundred baths of wine, and to an hundred baths of oil, and salt without prescribing how much.

23 Whatsoever is commanded by the God of heaven, let it be diligently done for the house of the God of heaven: for why should there be wrath against the realm of the king and his sons?

24 Also we certify you, that touching any of the priests and Levites, singers, porters, Nethinims, or ministers of this house of God, it shall not be lawful to impose toll, tribute, or custom, upon them.

25 And thou, Ezra, after the wisdom of thy God, that is in thine hand, set magistrates and judges, which may judge all the people that are beyond the river, all such as know the laws of thy God; and teach ye them that know them not.

26 And whosoever will not do the law of thy God, and the law of the king, let judgment be executed speedily upon him, whether it be unto death, or to banishment, or to confiscation of goods, or to imprisonment.

27 Blessed be the LORD God of our fathers, which hath put such a thing as this in the king’s heart, to beautify the house of the LORD which is in Jerusalem:

28 And hath extended mercy unto me before the king, and his counsellors, and before all the king’s mighty princes. And I was strengthened as the hand of the LORD my God was upon me, and I gathered together out of Israel chief men to go up with me.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After these things, in the reign of Artaxerxes, Ezra — a ready scribe in the law of Moses, descended from Aaron through Seraiah — goes up from Babylon with the king’s favour, because the hand of the LORD his God is upon him. Artaxerxes’ letter grants Ezra authority to carry silver and gold for the house of God, to appoint magistrates and judges who know God’s laws, and to enforce them; Ezra blesses the LORD who put this in the king’s heart and gathers strength for the journey. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text in the Text panel above

Unfamiliar terms

  • A ready scribe. Interpreted Henry reads this as not a mere copyist but one learned in law and polity. Study note, Ezra 7:6, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After these things, in the reign of Artaxerxes, Ezra son of Seraiah, son of Azariah, son of Hilkiah, comes forward. JFB notes 'son' here means descendant: Seraiah the high priest died at Riblah long before, so Ezra is his grandson or later descendant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. This Ezra went up from Babylon, a ready scribe in the law of Moses which the LORD God of Israel had given, and the king granted him all his request, according to the hand of the LORD his God upon him. Henry notes the title 'ready scribe' means not a mere copyist but a rabbi learned in law and polity, and his success is credited to God's hand, not court favour alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. There went up with him some of Israel, priests, Levites, singers, porters and Nethinim, to Jerusalem, in the seventh year of Artaxerxes. JFB notes this second caravan is smaller and more priestly: Ezra brings teachers and temple servants, not only settlers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He came to Jerusalem in the fifth month, in the seventh year of the king. The arrival date anchors Ezra's mission in a real reign, after the temple had already stood for decades. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. On the first day of the first month he began to go up from Babylon, and on the first day of the fifth month he came to Jerusalem, according to the good hand of his God upon him. Henry notes the four-month journey is summed up in one refrain: God's good hand, repeated as the cause of every success in this book. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For Ezra had prepared his heart to seek the law of the LORD, and to do it, and to teach in Israel statutes and judgments. JFB notes the threefold order: seek, do, teach. Ezra's authority to teach rests on a heart prepared and a life obedient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. This is the copy of the letter Artaxerxes gave to Ezra the priest, the scribe, even a scribe of the words of the LORD's commandments and statutes to Israel. The letter is quoted in full, so the reader can weigh Ezra's commission in the king's own words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. 'Artaxerxes, king of kings, unto Ezra the priest, a scribe of the law of the God of heaven, perfect peace.' Henry notes the heathen king's titles acknowledge the God of heaven by name, a confession wrung from a Persian court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The king decrees that all of Israel, priests and Levites in his realm, who are minded of their own freewill to go up to Jerusalem, may go with Ezra. JFB notes the return remains voluntary: the decree opens the road, it does not drive the people down it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Forasmuch as thou art sent by the king and his seven counsellors, to enquire concerning Judah and Jerusalem according to the law of thy God in thy hand. Henry notes Ezra is sent as an inspector of the law's observance, with the law-book itself as his standard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And to carry the silver and gold which the king and his counsellors have freely offered to the God of Israel, whose habitation is in Jerusalem. The king's own free offering precedes any tax: the court gives before it commands giving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. And all the silver and gold thou canst find in all the province of Babylon, with the freewill offering of the people and priests, willingly offered for the house of their God at Jerusalem. JFB notes two treasuries feed the temple: the king's gift and the people's willing gifts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. That thou mayest buy speedily with this money bullocks, rams, lambs, with their meat and drink offerings, and offer them on the altar of the house of your God at Jerusalem. The money is earmarked for sacrifice, not for Ezra's enrichment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. And whatever seems good to thee and thy brethren to do with the rest of the silver and gold, do after the will of your God. Henry notes the latitude is bounded: discretion is allowed, but only within God's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The vessels given for the service of the house of thy God, deliver thou before the God of Jerusalem. Vessels are not Ezra's to keep; they are to be presented at the house itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. And whatever more is needful for the house of thy God, which thou shalt have occasion to bestow, bestow it out of the king's treasure house. The king underwrites even unforeseen needs, an open-ended grant for God's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. And I, even I Artaxerxes, decree to all treasurers beyond the river, that whatever Ezra the priest, scribe of the law of the God of heaven, requires of you, it be done speedily. JFB notes the decree to the treasurers makes Ezra's word the king's word across the whole province. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Unto a hundred talents of silver, a hundred measures of wheat, a hundred baths of wine, a hundred baths of oil, and salt without prescribing how much. The fixed limits for most goods, with salt unlimited, show a practical court that knows temple needs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Whatever is commanded by the God of heaven, let it be diligently done for the house of the God of heaven: for why should there be wrath against the realm of the king and his sons? Henry notes the king's motive is self-protection: a neglected God of heaven might turn his wrath on the empire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Also we certify you, that touching any priests, Levites, singers, porters, Nethinim or ministers of this house of God, it shall not be lawful to impose toll, tribute or custom upon them. JFB notes the temple staff is made tax-exempt, a privilege that marks the house as under royal protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. And thou, Ezra, after the wisdom of thy God in thy hand, set magistrates and judges to judge all the people beyond the river, all such as know the laws of thy God; and teach them that know them not. Henry notes Ezra's commission joins government and teaching: judges enforce the law, and the ignorant are taught it, not merely punished by it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. And whoever will not do the law of thy God and the law of the king, let judgment be executed speedily upon him, whether unto death, banishment, confiscation of goods, or imprisonment. JFB notes the two laws are joined: disobedience to God's law is now also an offence against the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Blessed be the LORD God of our fathers, who has put such a thing as this in the king's heart, to beautify the house of the LORD at Jerusalem. Henry notes Ezra's first response is praise, crediting God, not the king, for the king's heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. And hath extended mercy unto me before the king, his counsellors and all his mighty princes. And I was strengthened as the hand of the LORD my God was upon me, and I gathered out of Israel chief men to go up with me. JFB notes the refrain returns: God's hand strengthens, and strengthened men then gather others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
