The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 41 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Keep silence before me, O islands; and let the people renew their strength: let them come near; then let them speak: let us come near together to judgment.

2 Who raised up the righteous man from the east, called him to his foot, gave the nations before him, and made him rule over kings? he gave them as the dust to his sword, and as driven stubble to his bow.

3 He pursued them, and passed safely; even by the way that he had not gone with his feet.

4 Who hath wrought and done it, calling the generations from the beginning? I the LORD, the first, and with the last; I am he.

5 The isles saw it, and feared; the ends of the earth were afraid, drew near, and came.

6 They helped every one his neighbour; and every one said to his brother, Be of good courage.

7 So the carpenter encouraged the goldsmith, and he that smootheth with the hammer him that smote the anvil, saying, It is ready for the sodering: and he fastened it with nails, that it should not be moved.

8 But thou, Israel, art my servant, Jacob whom I have chosen, the seed of Abraham my friend.

9 Thou whom I have taken from the ends of the earth, and called thee from the chief men thereof, and said unto thee, Thou art my servant; I have chosen thee, and not cast thee away.

10 Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.

11 Behold, all they that were incensed against thee shall be ashamed and confounded: they shall be as nothing; and they that strive with thee shall perish.

12 Thou shalt seek them, and shalt not find them, even them that contended with thee: they that war against thee shall be as nothing, and as a thing of nought.

13 For I the LORD thy God will hold thy right hand, saying unto thee, Fear not; I will help thee.

14 Fear not, thou worm Jacob, and ye men of Israel; I will help thee, saith the LORD, and thy redeemer, the Holy One of Israel.

15 Behold, I will make thee a new sharp threshing instrument having teeth: thou shalt thresh the mountains, and beat them small, and shalt make the hills as chaff.

16 Thou shalt fan them, and the wind shall carry them away, and the whirlwind shall scatter them: and thou shalt rejoice in the LORD, and shalt glory in the Holy One of Israel.

17 When the poor and needy seek water, and there is none, and their tongue faileth for thirst, I the LORD will hear them, I the God of Israel will not forsake them.

18 I will open rivers in high places, and fountains in the midst of the valleys: I will make the wilderness a pool of water, and the dry land springs of water.

19 I will plant in the wilderness the cedar, the shittah tree, and the myrtle, and the oil tree; I will set in the desert the fir tree, and the pine, and the box tree together:

20 That they may see, and know, and consider, and understand together, that the hand of the LORD hath done this, and the Holy One of Israel hath created it.

21 Produce your cause, saith the LORD; bring forth your strong reasons, saith the King of Jacob.

22 Let them bring them forth, and shew us what shall happen: let them shew the former things, what they be, that we may consider them, and know the latter end of them; or declare us things for to come.

23 Shew the things that are to come hereafter, that we may know that ye are gods: yea, do good, or do evil, that we may be dismayed, and behold it together.

24 Behold, ye are of nothing, and your work of nought: an abomination is he that chooseth you.

25 I have raised up one from the north, and he shall come: from the rising of the sun shall he call upon my name: and he shall come upon princes as upon morter, and as the potter treadeth clay.

26 Who hath declared from the beginning, that we may know? and beforetime, that we may say, He is righteous? yea, there is none that sheweth, yea, there is none that declareth, yea, there is none that heareth your words.

27 The first shall say to Zion, Behold, behold them: and I will give to Jerusalem one that bringeth good tidings.

28 For I beheld, and there was no man; even among them, and there was no counsellor, that, when I asked of them, could answer a word.

29 Behold, they are all vanity; their works are nothing: their molten images are wind and confusion.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God summons the nations and their idols to a courtroom: who raised up the deliverer from the east, and can any idol foretell what is coming? Cyrus is God's instrument, Israel is upheld and not cast off, and the chapter ends by exposing the idols for what they are — wind and confusion, with works that are nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Keep silence before me, O islands, and let the people renew their strength; let them come near, then let them speak; let us come near together to judgment. This chapter gives additional reasons why the Jews should place confidence in God's promises of delivering them: He will raise up a Prince as their deliverer, whereas the idols could not deliver the heathen nations from that Prince. God is about to argue the case, therefore let the nations listen in reverential silence, in the spirit in which we ought to behave before God. Before me is rather, turning, towards me. Islands include all regions beyond sea, maritime regions, not merely isles in the strict sense. Renew strength means let them gather their strength for the argument, let them adduce their strongest arguments; judgment means here, to decide the point at issue between us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Who raised up the righteous man from the east, called him to his foot, gave the nations before him, and made him rule over kings? He gave them as the dust to his sword, and as driven stubble to his bow. Who means else but God? The fact that God raiseth up Cyrus and qualifies him for becoming the conqueror of the nations and deliverer of God's people is a strong argument why they should trust in Him; the future is here prophetically represented as present or past. The righteous man is Cyrus, as chapters 44, 45, and 46 prove, called righteous not so much on account of his own equity as because he fulfilled God's righteous will in restoring the Jews from their unjust captivity; raised him up in righteousness. The Septuagint takes the Hebrew as a noun, righteousness, and some translate, Who raised up him whom salvation, national and temporal, the gift of God's righteousness to the good, meets at his foot, that is, wherever he goes. Cyrus is said to come from the East because Persia is east of Babylon, but in verse 25, from the north, in reference to Media. At the same time the full sense of righteousness, or righteous, and of the whole passage, is realised only in Messiah, Cyrus's antitype, for Cyrus knew not God. He goes forth as the Universal Conqueror of the nations, in righteousness making war; the idols He shall utterly abolish. Righteousness was always raised up from the East: Paradise was east of Eden, the cherubim were at the east of the garden, Abraham was called from the East, and Judea, the birthplace of Messiah, was in the East. Called to his foot means called him to attend His, God's, steps, that is, follow His guidance; in Ezra Cyrus acknowledges Jehovah as the Giver of his victories. He subdued the nations from the Euxine to the Red Sea, and even Egypt. Dust recalls chapters 17 and 29 and Psalm 18; Persia, Cyrus's country, was famed for the use of the bow. Before him means gave them into his power; some translate, Gave his, the enemy's, sword to be dust, and his, the enemy's, bow to be as stubble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He pursued them, and passed safely, even by the way that he had not gone with his feet. Cyrus had not visited the regions of the Euphrates and westward until he visited them for conquest; so the gospel conquests penetrated regions where the name of God was unknown before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Who has wrought and done it, calling the generations from the beginning? I the Lord, the first, and with the last; I am he. Who means else but God? Calling the generations from the beginning means the origin and position of all nations are from God; what is true of Cyrus and his conquests is true of all the movements of history from the first, all are from God. With the last means that is, the last, as in chapters 44 and 48. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The isles saw it, and feared; the ends of the earth were afraid, drew near, and came. Feared means that they would be subdued. Drew near and came means together, for mutual defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They helped every one his neighbour, and every one said to his brother: Be of good courage. Be of good courage means be not alarmed because of Cyrus, but make new images to secure the favour of the gods against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. So the carpenter encouraged the goldsmith, and he that smooths with the hammer him that smote the anvil, saying: It is ready for the sodering, and he fastened it with nails, that it should not be moved. One workman encourages the other to be quick in finishing the idol, so as to avert the impending danger. Nails are to keep it steady in its place; the Wisdom of Solomon gives a similar picture of the folly of idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. But you, Israel, are my servant, Jacob whom I have chosen, the seed of Abraham my friend. This is the contrast between the idolatrous nations whom God will destroy by Cyrus, and Israel whom God will deliver by the same man for their forefathers' sake. Servant is so termed as being chosen by God to worship Him themselves, and to lead other peoples to do the same. Jacob chosen recalls Psalm 135. My friend is literally loving me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. You whom I have taken from the ends of the earth, and called you from the chief men thereof, and said unto you: You are my servant; I have chosen you, and not cast you away. This refers to Abraham, the father of the Jews, taken from the remote Ur of the Chaldees; others take it of Israel, called out of Egypt. From the chief men is literally the elbows, so the joints, hence the root which joins the tree to the earth, figuratively those of ancient and noble stock; but the parallel clause, ends of the earth, favours Gesenius, who translates, the extremities of the earth, so Jerome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Fear you not, for I am with you; be not dismayed, for I am your God; I will strengthen you, yes, I will help you, yes, I will uphold you with the right hand of my righteousness. Be not dismayed is literally, anxiously to look at one another in dismay. Right hand of my righteousness means My right hand prepared in accordance with My righteousness, faithfulness to My promises, to uphold thee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Behold, all they that were incensed against you will be ashamed and confounded; they will be as nothing, and they that strive with you will perish. Ashamed means put to the shame of defeat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. You will seek them, and will not find them, even them that contended with you; they that war against you will be as nothing, and as a thing of nought. And not find is said of one so utterly put out of the way that not a trace of him can be found; thing of naught means shall utterly perish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. For I the Lord your God will hold your right hand, saying unto you: Fear not; I will help you. This recalls Deuteronomy 33, where God rides upon the heaven in thy help and in His excellency on the sky, and is the refuge and everlasting arms beneath His people, driving out the enemy and saying, Israel shall dwell in safety alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Fear not, you worm Jacob, and you men of Israel; I will help you, says the Lord, and your redeemer, the Holy One of Israel. Worm means in a state of contempt and affliction, whom all loathe and tread on, the very expression which Messiah, on the cross, applies to Himself, so completely are the Lord and His people identified and assimilated. God's people are as worms in humble thoughts of themselves, and in their enemies' haughty thoughts of them; worms, but not vipers, or of the serpent's seed. Men means the parallelism requires the word men here to have associated with it the idea of fewness or feebleness; Lowth translates, Ye mortals of Israel, the Septuagint, altogether diminutive. The Lord is in general, and thy redeemer in particular, a still stronger reason why He should help them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Behold, I will make you a new sharp threshing instrument having teeth; you will thresh the mountains, and beat them small, and will make the hills as chaff. God will make Israel to destroy their enemies as the Eastern corn-drag in chapters 28 bruises out the grain with its teeth, and gives the chaff to the winds to scatter. Teeth are serrated, so as to cut up the straw for fodder and separate the grain from the chaff. Mountains and hills are kingdoms more or less powerful that were hostile to Israel, as in chapter 2. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. You will fan them, and the wind will carry them away, and the whirlwind will scatter them, and you will rejoice in the Lord, and will glory in the Holy One of Israel. Fan means winnowed, as in Matthew 3; whirlwind scatter them recalls Job. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. When the poor and needy seek water, and there is none, and their tongue fails for thirst, I the Lord will hear them, I the God of Israel will not forsake them. Poor and needy are primarily the exiles in Babylon. Water is figuratively refreshment, prosperity after their affliction; the language is so constructed as only very partially to apply to the local and temporary event of the restoration from Babylon, but fully to be realised in the waters of life and of the Spirit, under the gospel. God wrought no miracles that we read of, in any wilderness, during the return from Babylon. Faileth is rather is rigid or parched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. I will open rivers in high places, and fountains in the midst of the valleys; I will make the wilderness a pool of water, and the dry land springs of water. This alludes to the waters with which Israel was miraculously supplied in the desert after coming out of Egypt. High places are bare of trees, barren, and unwatered; high places and valleys spiritually express that in all circumstances, whether elevated or depressed, God's people will have refreshment for their souls, however little to be expected it might seem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. I will plant in the wilderness the cedar, the shittah tree, and the myrtle, and the oil tree; I will set in the desert the fir tree, and the pine, and the box tree together. This recalls chapters 32 and 55. Shittah is rather the acacia, or Egyptian thorn, from which gum Arabic is obtained. Oil tree is the olive. Fir tree is rather the cypress, grateful by its shade. Pine is, Gesenius translates, the holm. Box tree is not the shrub used for bordering flower beds, but a kind of cedar, remarkable for the smallness of its cones and the upward direction of its branches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. That they may see, and know, and consider, and understand together, that the hand of the Lord has done this, and the Holy One of Israel has created it. Consider is literally lay it to heart, turn their attention to it. They refers to all lands; the effect on the Gentiles of God's open interposition hereafter in behalf of Israel shall be, they shall seek Israel's God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Produce your cause, says the Lord; bring forth your strong reasons, says the King of Jacob. This is a new challenge to the idolaters to say, can their idols predict future events as Jehovah can? Your strong reasons are the reasons for idol-worship which you think especially strong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Let them bring them forth, and show us what will happen; let them show the former things, what they be, that we may consider them, and know the latter end of them, or declare us things for to come. What shall happen means let them bring near and declare future contingencies. Former things and the latter end of them means show what former predictions the idols have given, that we may compare the event, latter end, with them; or give new prophecies, declare things to come. Some explain it more reconditely, Let them foretell the entire series of events, showing in their order the things which shall first occur as well as those which shall finally happen; the false prophets tried to predict isolated events, having no mutual dependency, not a long series of events mutually and orderly connected and stretching far into futurity. They did not even try to do this. None but God can do it. Or things to come will, in this view, mean, Let them, if they cannot predict the series, even predict plainly any detached events. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Show the things that are to come hereafter, that we may know that you are gods; yes, do good, or do evil, that we may be dismayed, and behold it together. Evil means give any proof at all of your power, either to reward your friends or punish your enemies. That we may be dismayed and behold it together is, some translate, That we, Jehovah and the idols, may look one another in the face, that is, encounter one another, and see our respective powers by a trial; others translate, Then the moment we behold, we shall be dismayed. We thus, and in the English version, refers to Jehovah and His worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Behold, you are of nothing, and your work of nought; an abomination is he that chooses you. Of nothing is as in chapter 40; the Hebrew text is here corrupt, so the English version treats it. Abomination is abstract for concrete: not merely abominable, but the essence of whatever is so. Chooseth you means as an object of worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. I have raised up one from the north, and he will come; from the rising of the sun he will call upon my name, and he will come upon princes as upon mortar, and as the potter treads clay. Raised up is in purpose, not fulfilled till a hundred and fifty years afterwards. North is, in verse 2, from the East; both are true, as in the note there. Call upon my name means acknowledge Me as God, and attribute his success to Me, which he did in the proclamation in Ezra; this does not necessarily imply Cyrus renounced idolatry, but hearing of Isaiah's prophecy given a hundred and fifty years before, so fully realised in his own acts, he recognised God as the true God, but retained his idol, so Naaman and others. Princes are the Babylonian satraps or governors of provinces. Mortar means mire; he shall tread them under foot as dirt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Who has declared from the beginning, that we may know? And beforetime, that we may say: He is righteous? Yes, there is none that shows, yes, there is none that declares, yes, there is none that hears your words. Who means of the idolatrous soothsayers? When this prophecy shall be fulfilled, all shall see that God foretold as to Cyrus, which none of the soothsayers have. Beforetime means before the event occurred. He is righteous is rather, It is true; it was a true prophecy, as the event shows. He is righteous, in the English version, must be interpreted, The fulfilment of the idol's words proves that he is faithful. Showeth, etc., is rather, there was none of the soothsayers that showed or declared, no one has heard your words foretelling the event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The first will say to Zion: Behold, behold them, and I will give to Jerusalem one that brings good tidings. Them, the wished-for event is now present, is inserted in the middle of the sentence as a detached exclamation, by an elegant transposition, the language being framed abruptly, as one would speak in putting vividly as it were, before the eyes of others, some joyous event which he had just learned. None of the idols had foretold these events. Jehovah was the first to do so, as in verse 4. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. For I beheld, and there was no man, even among them, and there was no counsellor, that, when I asked of them, could answer a word. No counsellor means no one of the idolatrous soothsayers who could inform those who consulted them what would take place, as in the counsel of His messenger in chapter 44. When I asked means that is, challenged them, in this chapter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Behold, they are all vanity; their works are nothing; their molten images are wind and confusion. Confusion means emptiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Men means the parallelism requires the word men here to have associated with it the idea of fewness or feebleness; Lowth translates, Ye mortals of Israel, the Septuagint, altogether diminutive. The Lord is in general, and thy redeemer in particular, a still stronger reason why He should help them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Behold, I will make you a new sharp threshing instrument having teeth; you will thresh the mountains, and beat them small, and will make the hills as chaff. God will make Israel to destroy their enemies as the Eastern corn-drag in chapters 28 bruises out the grain with its teeth, and gives the chaff to the winds to scatter. Teeth are serrated, so as to cut up the straw for fodder and separate the grain from the chaff. Mountains and hills are kingdoms more or less powerful that were hostile to Israel, as in chapter 2. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. You will fan them, and the wind will carry them away, and the whirlwind will scatter them, and you will rejoice in the Lord, and will glory in the Holy One of Israel. Fan means winnowed, as in Matthew 3; whirlwind scatter them recalls Job. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. When the poor and needy seek water, and there is none, and their tongue fails for thirst, I the Lord will hear them, I the God of Israel will not forsake them. Poor and needy are primarily the exiles in Babylon. Water is figuratively refreshment, prosperity after their affliction; the language is so constructed as only very partially to apply to the local and temporary event of the restoration from Babylon, but fully to be realised in the waters of life and of the Spirit, under the gospel. God wrought no miracles that we read of, in any wilderness, during the return from Babylon. Faileth is rather is rigid or parched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. I will open rivers in high places, and fountains in the midst of the valleys; I will make the wilderness a pool of water, and the dry land springs of water. This alludes to the waters with which Israel was miraculously supplied in the desert after coming out of Egypt. High places are bare of trees, barren, and unwatered; high places and valleys spiritually express that in all circumstances, whether elevated or depressed, God's people will have refreshment for their souls, however little to be expected it might seem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. I will plant in the wilderness the cedar, the shittah tree, and the myrtle, and the oil tree; I will set in the desert the fir tree, and the pine, and the box tree together. This recalls chapters 32 and 55. Shittah is rather the acacia, or Egyptian thorn, from which gum Arabic is obtained. Oil tree is the olive. Fir tree is rather the cypress, grateful by its shade. Pine is, Gesenius translates, the holm. Box tree is not the shrub used for bordering flower beds, but a kind of cedar, remarkable for the smallness of its cones and the upward direction of its branches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. That they may see, and know, and consider, and understand together, that the hand of the Lord has done this, and the Holy One of Israel has created it. Consider is literally lay it to heart, turn their attention to it. They refers to all lands; the effect on the Gentiles of God's open interposition hereafter in behalf of Israel shall be, they shall seek Israel's God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Produce your cause, says the Lord; bring forth your strong reasons, says the King of Jacob. This is a new challenge to the idolaters to say, can their idols predict future events as Jehovah can? Your strong reasons are the reasons for idol-worship which you think especially strong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Let them bring them forth, and show us what will happen; let them show the former things, what they be, that we may consider them, and know the latter end of them, or declare us things for to come. What shall happen means let them bring near and declare future contingencies. Former things and the latter end of them means show what former predictions the idols have given, that we may compare the event, latter end, with them; or give new prophecies, declare things to come. Some explain it more reconditely, Let them foretell the entire series of events, showing in their order the things which shall first occur as well as those which shall finally happen; the false prophets tried to predict isolated events, having no mutual dependency, not a long series of events mutually and orderly connected and stretching far into futurity. They did not even try to do this. None but God can do it. Or things to come will, in this view, mean, Let them, if they cannot predict the series, even predict plainly any detached events. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Show the things that are to come hereafter, that we may know that you are gods; yes, do good, or do evil, that we may be dismayed, and behold it together. Evil means give any proof at all of your power, either to reward your friends or punish your enemies. That we may be dismayed and behold it together is, some translate, That we, Jehovah and the idols, may look one another in the face, that is, encounter one another, and see our respective powers by a trial; others translate, Then the moment we behold, we shall be dismayed. We thus, and in the English version, refers to Jehovah and His worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Behold, you are of nothing, and your work of nought; an abomination is he that chooses you. Of nothing is as in chapter 40; the Hebrew text is here corrupt, so the English version treats it. Abomination is abstract for concrete: not merely abominable, but the essence of whatever is so. Chooseth you means as an object of worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. I have raised up one from the north, and he will come; from the rising of the sun he will call upon my name, and he will come upon princes as upon mortar, and as the potter treads clay. Raised up is in purpose, not fulfilled till a hundred and fifty years afterwards. North is, in verse 2, from the East; both are true, as in the note there. Call upon my name means acknowledge Me as God, and attribute his success to Me, which he did in the proclamation in Ezra; this does not necessarily imply Cyrus renounced idolatry, but hearing of Isaiah's prophecy given a hundred and fifty years before, so fully realised in his own acts, he recognised God as the true God, but retained his idol, so Naaman and others. Princes are the Babylonian satraps or governors of provinces. Mortar means mire; he shall tread them under foot as dirt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Who has declared from the beginning, that we may know? And beforetime, that we may say: He is righteous? Yes, there is none that shows, yes, there is none that declares, yes, there is none that hears your words. Who means of the idolatrous soothsayers? When this prophecy shall be fulfilled, all shall see that God foretold as to Cyrus, which none of the soothsayers have. Beforetime means before the event occurred. He is righteous is rather, It is true; it was a true prophecy, as the event shows. He is righteous, in the English version, must be interpreted, The fulfilment of the idol's words proves that he is faithful. Showeth, etc., is rather, there was none of the soothsayers that showed or declared, no one has heard your words foretelling the event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The first will say to Zion: Behold, behold them, and I will give to Jerusalem one that brings good tidings. Them, the wished-for event is now present, is inserted in the middle of the sentence as a detached exclamation, by an elegant transposition, the language being framed abruptly, as one would speak in putting vividly as it were, before the eyes of others, some joyous event which he had just learned. None of the idols had foretold these events. Jehovah was the first to do so, as in verse 4. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. For I beheld, and there was no man, even among them, and there was no counsellor, that, when I asked of them, could answer a word. No counsellor means no one of the idolatrous soothsayers who could inform those who consulted them what would take place, as in the counsel of His messenger in chapter 44. When I asked means that is, challenged them, in this chapter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Behold, they are all vanity; their works are nothing; their molten images are wind and confusion. Confusion means emptiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
