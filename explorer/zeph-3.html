The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Zephaniah 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Woe to her that is filthy and polluted, to the oppressing city!

2 She obeyed not the voice; she received not correction; she trusted not in the LORD; she drew not near to her God.

3 Her princes within her are roaring lions; her judges are evening wolves; they gnaw not the bones till the morrow.

4 Her prophets are light and treacherous persons: her priests have polluted the sanctuary, they have done violence to the law.

5 The just LORD is in the midst thereof; he will not do iniquity: every morning doth he bring his judgment to light, he faileth not; but the unjust knoweth no shame.

6 I have cut off the nations: their towers are desolate; I made their streets waste, that none passeth by: their cities are destroyed, so that there is no man, that there is none inhabitant.

7 I said, Surely thou wilt fear me, thou wilt receive instruction; so their dwelling should not be cut off, howsoever I punished them: but they rose early, and corrupted all their doings.

8 Therefore wait ye upon me, saith the LORD, until the day that I rise up to the prey: for my determination is to gather the nations, that I may assemble the kingdoms, to pour upon them mine indignation, even all my fierce anger: for all the earth shall be devoured with the fire of my jealousy.

9 For then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of the LORD, to serve him with one consent.

10 From beyond the rivers of Ethiopia my suppliants, even the daughter of my dispersed, shall bring mine offering.

11 In that day shalt thou not be ashamed for all thy doings, wherein thou hast transgressed against me: for then I will take away out of the midst of thee them that rejoice in thy pride, and thou shalt no more be haughty because of my holy mountain.

12 I will also leave in the midst of thee an afflicted and poor people, and they shall trust in the name of the LORD.

13 The remnant of Israel shall not do iniquity, nor speak lies; neither shall a deceitful tongue be found in their mouth: for they shall feed and lie down, and none shall make them afraid.

14 Sing, O daughter of Zion; shout, O Israel; be glad and rejoice with all the heart, O daughter of Jerusalem.

15 The LORD hath taken away thy judgments, he hath cast out thine enemy: the king of Israel, even the LORD, is in the midst of thee: thou shalt not see evil any more.

16 In that day it shall be said to Jerusalem, Fear thou not: and to Zion, Let not thine hands be slack.

17 The LORD thy God in the midst of thee is mighty; he will save, he will rejoice over thee with joy; he will rest in his love, he will joy over thee with singing.

18 I will gather them that are sorrowful for the solemn assembly, who are of thee, to whom the reproach of it was a burden.

19 Behold, at that time I will undo all that afflict thee: and I will save her that halteth, and gather her that was driven out; and I will get them praise and fame in every land where they have been put to shame.

20 At that time will I bring you again, even in the time that I gather you: for I will make you a name and a praise among all people of the earth, when I turn back your captivity before your eyes, saith the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Woe to the filthy, polluted, oppressing city that obeys no voice, receives no correction, trusts not in the LORD, and draws not near to God, whose princes are roaring lions, judges evening wolves, prophets light and treacherous, and priests polluting the sanctuary. Yet the just LORD is in her midst and fails not, and the chapter turns to waiting, a pure language for all peoples to serve him with one consent, a remnant afflicted and poor that trusts in the name of the LORD and does no iniquity. It ends in song: sing, daughter of Zion, for the LORD has taken away your judgments and cast out your enemy, the King of Israel is in your midst, and the LORD your God will rejoice over you with joy and with singing. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. This chapter resumes the denunciation of Jerusalem, as being unreformed by the punishment of other nations. She is filthy and polluted, her inward moral filth standing in glaring contrast to the holiness of the worship on Moriah, the hill of the temple, whose name her own name plays upon; and she is the oppressing city, wronging the poor, the weak, widows, orphans, and strangers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. She obeyed not the voice, received not correction, trusted not in the Lord, and drew not near to her God. Jerusalem is incurable, obstinately rejecting salutary admonition, and her distrust in the Lord, as if he were insufficient, is the parent of all superstition and wickedness; though God was specially near to her as her God, she gratuitously estranged herself from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Her princes within her are roaring lions, roaring for prey, and her judges are evening wolves, most ravenous at evening after being foodless all day, devouring all at once, bones and flesh, so ragingly ravenous are they. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Her prophets are light and treacherous persons, in whose life and teaching there is no truth, gravity, or steadiness, false to Jehovah whose prophets they profess to be. Her priests have polluted the sanctuary by their profane deeds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The just Lord is in the midst of her, and he will not do iniquity; every morning does he bring his judgment to light, publicly by the teaching of his prophets and by samples of his judgments on the guilty. The Jews regard not God's justice manifested among them, and the unjust know no shame, and are not shamed by his justice into repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. God had cut off the nations and made their towers desolate, and had hoped that his people, seeing his judgments on other nations, would be led to amendment. But they are not, so blinded by sin are they, and the judgment on the ten tribes and the great incursion of the Scythians under Josiah's long and peaceful reign passed without reforming them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God said, surely thou wilt fear me, surely thou wilt receive instruction, for he had set before them life and death in his word and in his providence, and had chastened them that, being chastened, they might not be condemned. Yet they rose early and corrupted all their doings, setting themselves with the greatest earnestness to corruption, more resolute in wickedness than ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Therefore wait ye upon me, saith the Lord, until the day that I rise up to the prey: here Jehovah turns to the pious Jews, bidding them look forward to the glorious time of restoration to be ushered in by his outpouring of wrath on all nations. He will gather the nations against Jerusalem to pour out his indignation upon them there, rising like a warrior leading Israel to certain victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. For then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of the Lord, to serve him with one consent. The impure lips that called on idols shall be changed and given a pure language again, reversing the confusion at Babel, an earnest of which was the gift of tongues at Pentecost; and to serve with one consent is literally to serve with one shoulder, like two bearers of a yoke helping one another with conjoint effort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. From beyond the rivers of Ethiopia my suppliants, even the daughter of my dispersed, shall bring mine offering. The suppliants are the dispersed Israelites, pictured as worshippers bringing incense, here made the representatives of all Israel which is to be restored, and the peoples brought to fear God by his judgments shall bring them as his offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. In that day shalt thou not be ashamed for all thy doings wherein thou hast transgressed against me, for then will I take away out of the midst of thee them that rejoice in thy pride. Those priding themselves on the temple, on election as God's people, and on other grounds of boasting shall be removed, and the converted remnant shall be of a humble spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. I will also leave in the midst of thee an afflicted and poor people, and they shall trust in the name of the Lord. This is the blessed effect of sanctified affliction on the remnant, for entire trust in the Lord cannot be except where all cause for boasting is taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The remnant of Israel shall not do iniquity, nor speak lies, neither shall a deceitful tongue be found in their mouth. Worshipping God in truth and loving without dissimulation, they shall feed and lie down, and none shall make them afraid, neither foreign foe nor unjust prince, prophet, or priest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Sing, O daughter of Zion, shout, O Israel, be glad and rejoice with all the heart, O daughter of Jerusalem: the prophet in mental vision sees the joyful day of Zion present, and bids her rejoice at it. After the promises of taking away sin follow promises of taking away trouble, for when the cause is removed, the effect will cease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The Lord hath taken away thy judgments, he hath cast out thine enemy: the King of Israel, even the Lord, is in the midst of thee, and thou shalt not see evil any more. After the taking away of sin follows the taking away of trouble, and though he seemed to desert her for a time, he is now present as her safeguard, so that she shall not experience evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. In that day it shall be said to Jerusalem, Fear thou not, and to Zion, Let not thine hands be slack. Do not faint in the work of the Lord, for the day of deliverance has come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The Lord thy God in the midst of thee is mighty; he will save, he will rejoice over thee with joy; he will rest in his love, he will joy over thee with singing. To rest in his love is the calm, silent joy of contentment with his love as his supreme delight, too great for words, like God's silent satisfaction in his work after the six days of creation; the singing, by contrast, is joy uttered aloud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. I will gather them that are sorrowful for the solemn assembly, who are of thee, to whom the reproach of it was a burden. They are pining after the solemn assembly which they cannot celebrate in exile, and they are God's true citizens, whom he will therefore restore, taking away the reproach and captivity that burdened them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Behold, at that time I will undo all that afflict thee, and I will save her that halteth, and gather her that was driven out. To undo is to deal with the afflicters as they deserve, and the destruction of Israel's foes precedes Israel's restoration; her that halteth is all that are helpless, whose weakness will be no barrier to restoration, and God will get them praise and fame in every land where they have been put to shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. At that time will I bring you again, even in the time that I gather you, for I will make you a name and a praise among all people of the earth. The Hebrew speaks of captivities in the plural, the captivities of different ages and places, and this gathering shall happen before your eyes, so that your own eyes with delight shall see what you would scarcely believe for joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
