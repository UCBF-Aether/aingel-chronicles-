The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 38 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 O lord, rebuke me not in thy wrath: neither chasten me in thy hot displeasure.

2 For thine arrows stick fast in me, and thy hand presseth me sore.

3 There is no soundness in my flesh because of thine anger; neither is there any rest in my bones because of my sin.

4 For mine iniquities are gone over mine head: as an heavy burden they are too heavy for me.

5 My wounds stink and are corrupt because of my foolishness.

6 I am troubled; I am bowed down greatly; I go mourning all the day long.

7 For my loins are filled with a loathsome disease: and there is no soundness in my flesh.

8 I am feeble and sore broken: I have roared by reason of the disquietness of my heart.

9 Lord, all my desire is before thee; and my groaning is not hid from thee.

10 My heart panteth, my strength faileth me: as for the light of mine eyes, it also is gone from me.

11 My lovers and my friends stand aloof from my sore; and my kinsmen stand afar off.

12 They also that seek after my life lay snares for me: and they that seek my hurt speak mischievous things, and imagine deceits all the day long.

13 But I, as a deaf man, heard not; and I was as a dumb man that openeth not his mouth.

14 Thus I was as a man that heareth not, and in whose mouth are no reproofs.

15 For in thee, O LORD, do I hope: thou wilt hear, O Lord my God.

16 For I said, Hear me, lest otherwise they should rejoice over me: when my foot slippeth, they magnify themselves against me.

17 For I am ready to halt, and my sorrow is continually before me.

18 For I will declare mine iniquity; I will be sorry for my sin.

19 But mine enemies are lively, and they are strong: and they that hate me wrongfully are multiplied.

20 They also that render evil for good are mine adversaries; because I follow the thing that good is.

21 Forsake me not, O LORD: O my God, be not far from me.

22 Make haste to help me, O Lord my salvation.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A penitential psalm, like Psalm 6: arrows stick fast, iniquities over the head like a flood, wounds festering from folly, friends standing aloof, enemies laying snares. Yet the speaker is deaf and dumb before slander, declares his sin, and closes: forsake me not, make haste to help me, O Lord my salvation. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. O Lord, rebuke me not in your wrath, neither chasten me in your hot displeasure: as in Psalm 6, he accepts chastening but prays it be not in wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. For your arrows stick fast in me, and your hand presses me sore: arrows and a pressing hand picture affliction felt as God's own doing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. There is no soundness in my flesh because of your anger, nor rest in my bones because of my sin: flesh and bones both suffer, and sin is named as the cause alongside anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For my iniquities are gone over my head; as a heavy burden they are too heavy for me: sins are a flood over the head and a burden too heavy to bear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. My wounds stink and are corrupt because of my foolishness: wounds left untended by folly fester — sin neglected corrupts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. I am troubled; I am bowed down greatly; I go mourning all the day: trouble, bowing, and all-day mourning describe the body's posture under guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For my loins are filled with a loathsome disease, and there is no soundness in my flesh: disease fills the loins, and soundness is gone from the flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. I am feeble and sore broken; I have roared by reason of the disquietness of my heart: feebleness, brokenness, and roaring from a disquieted heart — the cry is inward before it is outward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Lord, all my desire is before you, and my groaning is not hidden from you: desire and groaning are both open to God, who needs no informing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. My heart pants, my strength fails; as for the light of my eyes, it also is gone from me: panting heart, failing strength, and lost eyesight mark the body's collapse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. My lovers and friends stand aloof from my sore, and my kinsmen stand afar off: friends and kin keep their distance from suffering, as from a plague. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They also who seek my life lay snares for me, and those who seek my hurt speak mischievous things and imagine deceits all the day: snares, mischievous speech, and all-day deceit add enemies to affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But I, as a deaf man, heard not, and was as a dumb man who opens not his mouth: deafness and dumbness are chosen — he will not answer slander with slander. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Thus I was as a man who hears not, and in whose mouth are no reproofs: no reproof is offered, because the case is left to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. For in you, O Lord, do I hope; you will hear, O Lord my God: hope in God and God's hearing are the reason for silence before men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. For I said, Hear me, lest otherwise they should rejoice over me; when my foot slips, they magnify themselves against me: he fears their rejoicing at his slip more than the slip itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For I am ready to halt, and my sorrow is continually before me: readiness to fall and continual sorrow are the honest state confessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. For I will declare my iniquity; I will be sorry for my sin: declaration and sorrow for sin are the penitential answer to affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. But my enemies are lively and strong, and those who hate me wrongfully are multiplied: lively, strong, multiplied, wrongful haters contrast with his own weakness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. They also who render evil for good are my adversaries, because I follow the thing that is good: good repaid with evil, for the very reason of following good, is the adversaries' charge against themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Forsake me not, O Lord; O my God, be not far from me: the plea against forsaking and farness gathers the whole psalm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Make haste to help me, O Lord my salvation: haste is asked because salvation delayed feels like salvation denied — and God is named as his salvation itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
