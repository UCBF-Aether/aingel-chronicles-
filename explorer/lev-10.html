The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, and put fire therein, and put incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not.

2 And there went out fire from the LORD, and devoured them, and they died before the LORD.

3 Then Moses said unto Aaron, This is it that the LORD spake, saying, I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me, and before all the people I will be glorified. And Aaron held his peace.

4 And Moses called Mishael and Elzaphan, the sons of Uzziel the uncle of Aaron, and said unto them, Come near, carry your brethren from before the sanctuary out of the camp.

5 So they went near, and carried them in their coats out of the camp; as Moses had said.

6 And Moses said unto Aaron, and unto Eleazar and unto Ithamar, his sons, Uncover not your heads, neither rend your clothes; lest ye die, and lest wrath come upon all the people: but let your brethren, the whole house of Israel, bewail the burning which the LORD hath kindled.

7 And ye shall not go out from the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, lest ye die: for the anointing oil of the LORD is upon you. And they did according to the word of Moses.

8 And the LORD spake unto Aaron, saying,

9 Do not drink wine nor strong drink, thou, nor thy sons with thee, when ye go into the tabernacle of the congregation, lest ye die: it shall be a statute for ever throughout your generations:

10 And that ye may put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean;

11 And that ye may teach the children of Israel all the statutes which the LORD hath spoken unto them by the hand of Moses.

12 And Moses spake unto Aaron, and unto Eleazar and unto Ithamar, his sons that were left, Take the meat offering that remaineth of the offerings of the LORD made by fire, and eat it without leaven beside the altar: for it is most holy:

13 And ye shall eat it in the holy place, because it is thy due, and thy sons’ due, of the sacrifices of the LORD made by fire: for so I am commanded.

14 And the wave breast and heave shoulder shall ye eat in a clean place; thou, and thy sons, and thy daughters with thee: for they be thy due, and thy sons’ due, which are given out of the sacrifices of peace offerings of the children of Israel.

15 The heave shoulder and the wave breast shall they bring with the offerings made by fire of the fat, to wave it for a wave offering before the LORD; and it shall be thine, and thy sons’ with thee, by a statute for ever; as the LORD hath commanded.

16 And Moses diligently sought the goat of the sin offering, and, behold, it was burnt: and he was angry with Eleazar and Ithamar, the sons of Aaron which were left alive, saying,

17 Wherefore have ye not eaten the sin offering in the holy place, seeing it is most holy, and God hath given it you to bear the iniquity of the congregation, to make atonement for them before the LORD?

18 Behold, the blood of it was not brought in within the holy place: ye should indeed have eaten it in the holy place, as I commanded.

19 And Aaron said unto Moses, Behold, this day have they offered their sin offering and their burnt offering before the LORD; and such things have befallen me: and if I had eaten the sin offering to day, should it have been accepted in the sight of the LORD?

20 And when Moses heard that, he was content.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter records the sudden judgment on Nadab and Abihu, Aaron's sons, who offered strange fire that the LORD had not commanded, and were killed by fire from before the LORD. Aaron holds his peace, and Moses forbids the surviving priests the usual signs of mourning while they remain on duty. The chapter then gives the standing ban on wine or strong drink for priests entering the tabernacle, so they may distinguish holy from unholy, and ends with Moses' challenge over the sin-offering goat that was burnt instead of eaten, and Aaron's explanation, which Moses accepts. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Nadab and Abihu, Aaron's sons, take censers, put fire and incense in them, and offer strange fire, venturing unauthorized into the highest priestly office, together in a work for one man, and intruding where only the high priest might go, with common fire instead of the altar fire they had just seen descend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Fire goes out from the LORD and devours them, rather kills them, for neither bodies nor robes are consumed, the fire issuing from the most holy place as a first-instance judgment repressing contempt for sacred things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The LORD will be sanctified in those who come near Him, a warning given repeatedly to priests about cautious and reverent approach, and Aaron holds his peace, submitting in silent resignation to the righteous judgment of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Moses calls Mishael and Elzaphan, Uzziel's sons, to carry their brethren from before the sanctuary out of the camp, so the painful intelligence spreads through the congregation and strikes salutary fear into priests and people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. They carry the dead in their coats out of the camp as Moses commanded, their priestly vestments being buried with them as a sign of pollution by their wearers' sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Aaron, Eleazar, and Ithamar are forbidden to uncover their heads or rend their garments in mourning, lest they die and wrath come on the congregation, the prohibition applying to their official costume while engaged in sacred duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They shall not go out from the tabernacle door, lest they die, for the anointing oil is upon them, and they do as Moses commanded, remaining at their sacred duty despite bereavement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The LORD speaks to Aaron directly, introducing a prohibition that follows immediately on the catastrophe and has led many to conjecture, though only as conjecture, that the disobedient priests were intoxicated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Aaron and his sons shall not drink wine or strong drink when they go into the tabernacle, lest they die, a perpetual statute in their generations, guarding the clarity required for sacred service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. This sobriety is required so they may distinguish between holy and unholy and between unclean and clean, the very distinction Nadab and Abihu had violated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They are also to teach Israel all the statutes the LORD has spoken by Moses, so that priestly instruction, not only priestly rite, is preserved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Moses rehearses to Aaron and his surviving sons the laws regulating priestly conduct, a timely and considerate reminder amid the distractions of bereavement, lest they forget a duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The remainder of the meat offering is to be eaten without leaven beside the altar in the holy place, for it is most holy, as part of that rehearsed priestly law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The wave breast and heave shoulder are to be eaten in a clean place by Aaron, his sons, and his daughters, as their due from Israel's peace offerings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The heave shoulder and wave breast are brought with the fat offerings made by fire to be waved before the LORD, and they belong to Aaron and his sons by a perpetual statute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Moses diligently seeks the goat of the sin offering and finds it burnt, discovering a departure from ritual, and challenges the surviving sons, not Aaron whose heart is too lacerated, for the irregularity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Moses asks why they have not eaten the sin offering in the holy place, since it is most holy and given to bear the congregation's iniquity and make atonement for them, the priests typically representing the people and bearing their sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Because its blood was not brought into the holy place, they should indeed have eaten it in the holy place as commanded, instead of burning it without the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Aaron answers that such things have befallen him, and if he had eaten the sin offering that day, would it have been accepted, explaining that all presentation duties were done except the festive part, omitted because his heart was too dejected or because he supposed the day's services were vitiated by judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Moses hears Aaron's explanation and is content, sympathising with his afflicted brother after pointing out the error, though Aaron was decidedly in the wrong, since no view of expediency ought to dispense with a positive statute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
