The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And I looked, and, lo, a Lamb stood on the mount Sion, and with him an hundred forty and four thousand, having his Father’s name written in their foreheads.

2 And I heard a voice from heaven, as the voice of many waters, and as the voice of a great thunder: and I heard the voice of harpers harping with their harps:

3 And they sung as it were a new song before the throne, and before the four beasts, and the elders: and no man could learn that song but the hundred and forty and four thousand, which were redeemed from the earth.

4 These are they which were not defiled with women; for they are virgins. These are they which follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth. These were redeemed from among men, being the firstfruits unto God and to the Lamb.

5 And in their mouth was found no guile: for they are without fault before the throne of God.

6 And I saw another angel fly in the midst of heaven, having the everlasting gospel to preach unto them that dwell on the earth, and to every nation, and kindred, and tongue, and people,

7 Saying with a loud voice, Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come: and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters.

8 And there followed another angel, saying, Babylon is fallen, is fallen, that great city, because she made all nations drink of the wine of the wrath of her fornication.

9 And the third angel followed them, saying with a loud voice, If any man worship the beast and his image, and receive his mark in his forehead, or in his hand,

10 The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation; and he shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb:

11 And the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever: and they have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image, and whosoever receiveth the mark of his name.

12 Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.

13 And I heard a voice from heaven saying unto me, Write, Blessed are the dead which die in the Lord from henceforth: Yea, saith the Spirit, that they may rest from their labours; and their works do follow them.

14 And I looked, and behold a white cloud, and upon the cloud one sat like unto the Son of man, having on his head a golden crown, and in his hand a sharp sickle.

15 And another angel came out of the temple, crying with a loud voice to him that sat on the cloud, Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe.

16 And he that sat on the cloud thrust in his sickle on the earth; and the earth was reaped.

17 And another angel came out of the temple which is in heaven, he also having a sharp sickle.

18 And another angel came out from the altar, which had power over fire; and cried with a loud cry to him that had the sharp sickle, saying, Thrust in thy sharp sickle, and gather the clusters of the vine of the earth; for her grapes are fully ripe.

19 And the angel thrust in his sickle into the earth, and gathered the vine of the earth, and cast it into the great winepress of the wrath of God.

20 And the winepress was trodden without the city, and blood came out of the winepress, even unto the horse bridles, by the space of a thousand and six hundred furlongs.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The Lamb stands on Mount Sion with a hundred and forty-four thousand having his Father's name in their foreheads, singing a new song no one else can learn. Three angels follow: one flying with the everlasting gospel to every nation, one announcing Babylon is fallen, and one warning that beast-worshippers shall drink the wine of God's wrath, while the saints' patience is to keep God's commandments and the faith of Jesus, and a voice blesses the dead who die in the Lord. The chapter closes with two harvests: one like the Son of man reaping the earth's harvest with a sharp sickle, and one gathering the vine of the earth into the great winepress of God's wrath. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John looked, and the Lamb stood on Mount Sion with a hundred forty-four thousand, having his Father's name written in their foreheads. JFB notes the Lamb has left his position in the midst of the throne to stand on Sion, and that God's and Christ's name answers to the seal upon their foreheads in chapter 7, the 144,000 being the first-fruits of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. John heard a voice from heaven as the voice of many waters and as great thunder, and the voice of harpers harping with their harps. JFB notes the voice of his people is like his own voice, and that the harpers and singers evidently include the 144,000. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They sang as it were a new song before the throne, before the four beasts and the elders, which no man could learn but the hundred forty-four thousand redeemed from the earth. JFB notes the song is new because such a conflict with the dragon, beast, and false prophet had never been fought before, and that not even angels can learn it, since they have not come out of great tribulation washed white in the Lamb's blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. These are they who were not defiled with women, for they are virgins, who follow the Lamb wherever he goes, redeemed from among men as first-fruits to God and to the Lamb. JFB reads their virginity spiritually, in contrast to apostate Babylon the harlot, and their following as the fitting reward of following him fully on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. In their mouth was found no guile, for they are without fault before the throne of God. JFB notes they are blameless in respect to the sincerity of their fidelity to him, regarded as such on the ground of his righteousness, not absolutely blameless in themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. John saw another angel fly in the midst of heaven, having the everlasting gospel to preach to those dwelling on the earth, to every nation, kindred, tongue, and people. JFB notes this portion concerns the Gentile world: before the end the gospel is preached for a witness to all nations, giving them a last season of grace to decide for or against Christ before judgment descends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The angel said with a loud voice, "Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come: and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters." JFB notes fear of God is the forerunner to embracing his love, and that the judgment here is not the general judgment but that upon Babylon, the beast, and his worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Another angel followed, saying, "Babylon is fallen, is fallen, that great city, because she made all nations drink of the wine of the wrath of her fornication." JFB notes Babylon, first mentioned here, is the harlot, the apostate church, distinct from the beast and judged separately, and that as she made nations drunk with her fornication, so she shall be made drunk with the wine of God's wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. A third angel followed, warning that if any man worship the beast and his image and receive his mark in his forehead or in his hand, he faces the doom the following verses describe. JFB heads this section the doom of the beast worshippers, proclaimed by the third angel, in contrast to the gospel and Babylon's fall announced by the first two. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation, and shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels and of the Lamb. JFB notes the wine is undiluted, with no drop of water to cool its heat and no grace or hope blended with it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The smoke of their torment ascends up for ever and ever, and they have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image and receive the mark of his name. JFB contrasts this with the living creatures, who rest not day and night in praise yet rest from sin, sorrow, weariness, and temptation, while the lost have no rest from sin, Satan, terror, torment, and remorse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus. JFB notes that in the fiery ordeal awaiting all who will not worship the beast, the faith and patient, persevering endurance of God's followers shall be put to the test and proved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. A voice from heaven said, "Write, Blessed are the dead which die in the Lord from henceforth: Yea, saith the Spirit, that they may rest from their labours; and their works do follow them." JFB notes their full blessedness is now from this time, since the judgment on the beast and the harvest gatherings are imminent, and their works follow with them as proof that they are Christ's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. John looked, and a white cloud, and upon the cloud one sat like unto the Son of man, having on his head a golden crown and in his hand a sharp sickle. JFB notes the crown is a garland of victory, not a kingly diadem, and that the victory is described in detail in chapter 19. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Another angel out of the temple cried to him on the cloud, "Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe." JFB notes the angel does not command the Son of man but merely announces the Father's will, in whose hands the times and seasons are kept, and that by the harvest the elect righteous are gathered out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He that sat on the cloud thrust in his sickle on the earth, and the earth was reaped. JFB notes the Son himself, with a golden crown, gathers the elect in the harvest, while a mere angel conducts the vintage that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Another angel came out of the temple which is in heaven, he also having a sharp sickle. JFB connects this temple with the temple opened in heaven in chapter 11, marking the vintage as a distinct act of judgment after the harvest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Another angel came out from the altar, having power over fire, and cried to him with the sharp sickle, "Thrust in thy sharp sickle, and gather the clusters of the vine of the earth; for her grapes are fully ripe." JFB notes the angel comes from the altar of the saints' incense-accompanied prayers, which bring down God's fiery judgment, and that the grapes are ripe for punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The angel thrust in his sickle into the earth, gathered the vine of the earth, and cast it into the great winepress of the wrath of God. JFB notes the vine is judged because its grapes are wild grapes, not what God looked for after careful culture, and that the object is the apostate world of Christendom, not the heathen world that has not heard of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The winepress was trodden without the city, and blood came out of the winepress, even unto the horse bridles, by the space of a thousand and six hundred furlongs. JFB notes the scene of Christ's and his people's blood-shedding shall also be the scene of God's vengeance, and that the sixteen hundred furlongs signify the completeness and universality of the destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Mount Sion. Where the Lamb stands with the hundred and forty-four thousand, having left his place in the midst of the throne, in JFB's note. Interpreted Study note, verse 1, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Winepress of the wrath of God. The vintage judgment in which the vine of the earth is gathered and trodden without the city. Interpreted Study notes, verses 19–20, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
