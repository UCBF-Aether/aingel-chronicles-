Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 29 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And it came to pass when Rachel had borne Joseph, that Laban went to shear his sheep ; for they were distant from him a three days' journey.

2 And Jacob saw that Laban was going to shear his

3 sheep, and Jacob called Leah and Rachel, and spake kindly unto them that they should come with him to the land of Canaan.

4 For he told them how he had seen everything in a dream, even all that He had spoken unto him that he should return to his father s house ; and they said : "To every place whither thou goest we will go with thee." 4.

5 And Jacob blessed the God of Isaac his father, and the God of Abraham his father's father, and he arose and mounted his wives and his children, and took all his possessions and crossed the river, and came to the land of Gilead, and Jacob hid his intention from Laban and told him not.

6 And in the seventh year of the fourth week Jacob turned (his face) toward Gilead in the first month, on the twenty-first thereof.

7 And Laban pursued after him and overtook Jacob in the mountain of Gilead in the third month, on the thirteenth thereof.

8 And the Lord did not suffer him to injure Jacob; for He appeared to him in a dream by night.

9 And Laban spake to Jacob.

10 And on the fifteenth of those days Jacob made a feast for Laban, and for all who came with him, and Jacob sware to Laban that day, and Laban also to Jacob, that neither should cross the mountain of Gilead to the other with evil purpose.

11 And he made there a heap for a witness ; wherefore the name of that place is called : " The Heap of Witness," after this heap.

12 But before they used to call the land of Gilead the land of the Rephaim ; for it was the land of the Rephaim, and the Rephaim were born (there), giants whose height was ten, nine, eight down to seven cubits. 10.

13 And their habitation was from the land of the children of Ammon to Mount Hermon, and the seats of their kingdom were Karnaim and

14 Ashtaroth and Edrei, and Misur and Beon. ii.

15 And the Lord destroyed them because of the evil of their deeds ; for they were very malignant, and the Amorites dwelt in their stead, wicked and sinful, and there is no people to-day which hath wrought to the full all their sins, and they have no longer length of life on the earth.

16 And Jacob sent away Laban, and he departed into Mesopotamia, the land of the East, and Jacob returned to the land of Gilead.

17 And he passed over the Jabbok in the ninth month, on the eleventh thereof.

18 And on that day Esau, his brother, came to him, and he was reconciled to him, and departed from him unto the land of Seir, but Jacob dwelt in tents.

19 And in the first year of the fifth week in this jubilee he crossed the Jordan, and dwelt beyond the Jordan, and he pastured his sheep from the sea of the heap unto Bethshan and unto Dothan and unto the forest of Akrabbim.

20 And he sent to his father Isaac of all his substance, clothing, and food, and meat, and drink, and milk, and butter, and cheese, and some dates of the valley, 16.

21 And to his mother Rebecca also four times a year, between the times of the months, between ploughing and reaping, and between autumn and the rain (season) and between winter

22 and spring, to the tower of Abraham.

23 For Isaac had returned from the Well of the Oath and gone up to the tower of his father Abraham, and he dwelt there apart from his son Esau.

24 For in the days when Jacob went to Mesopotamia, Esau took to himself a wife Mahalath, the daughter of Ishmael, and he gathered together all the flocks of his father and his wives, and went up and dwelt on Mount Seir, and left Isaac his father at the Well of the Oath alone.

25 And Isaac went up from the Well of the Oath and dwelt in the tower of Abraham his father on the mountains of Hebron, 20.

26 And thither Jacob sent all that he did send to his father and his mother from time to time, all they needed, and they blessed Jacob with all their heart and with all their soul.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: When Rachel had borne Joseph, Laban went to shear his sheep three days' journey away. Jacob sent to his father and mother from time to time all they needed, and they blessed Jacob with all their heart and soul. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. When Rachel had borne Joseph, Laban went to shear his sheep three days' journey away. The shearing gave Jacob the opening to depart, since Laban's attention and household were elsewhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jacob saw that Laban was going to shear his sheep, and took the moment to act. Henry notes that in all our removals we should have respect to the command and promise of God, and Jacob's departure followed the dream-command to return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Jacob called Leah and Rachel and spoke kindly to them, asking them to come with him to Canaan. Henry stresses that Jacob's wives were consulted and won over, not carried off without a word, and that the family's affairs are related minutely because the Bible teaches the common duties of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Jacob told his wives how he had seen in a dream all that God had spoken to him about returning to his father's house, and they answered that wherever he went they would go with him. Henry notes that remembering favoured seasons of communion with God refreshes us in difficulties, and that Jacob's household followed the command and promise together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Jacob blessed the God of Isaac and Abraham, mounted his wives and children, took all his possessions, crossed the river, and came to Gilead, hiding his intention from Laban. Henry observes that selfish men like Laban consider themselves robbed of all that goes past them, and that covetousness swallows up natural affection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. In the seventh year of the fourth week Jacob turned his face toward Gilead on the twenty-first of the first month. Jubilees dates the departure exactly, marking it as a fixed point in its chronology of weeks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Laban pursued Jacob and overtook him in the mountain of Gilead on the thirteenth of the third month. Henry notes that God can put a bridle in the mouth of wicked men to restrain their malice, though he does not change their hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The Lord did not suffer Laban to injure Jacob, for he appeared to Laban in a dream by night. Henry's point stands: God restrained Laban's malice, and Jacob's cause was committed to God rather than to his own strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Laban spoke to Jacob, beginning the parley in which he charged Jacob with things he knew not. Henry notes that those who commit their cause to God are not forbidden to plead it themselves with meekness and fear, as Jacob did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. On the fifteenth of those days Jacob made a feast for Laban and all who came with him, and they swore to each other that neither would cross the mountain of Gilead against the other with evil purpose. Henry notes that covenants of friendship in ancient times were ratified by the parties eating and drinking together, and that peace with God puts true comfort into peace with friends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. A heap was made for a witness, and the place was therefore called the Heap of Witness. Henry notes that a heap of stones was raised to keep up the memory of the event, writing being then not known or little used, and that God is judge between contending parties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Jacob sent Laban away, and Laban departed into Mesopotamia while Jacob returned to the land of Gilead. Henry observes that after the angry parley they parted friends, and that God is often better to us than our fears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Jacob passed over the Jabbok on the eleventh of the ninth month. Henry's account of this crossing sets the scene for what followed: Jacob, having committed his case to God by prayer, went on his way, and the angels of God appeared to encourage him with the assurance of divine protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. On that day Esau came to Jacob, was reconciled to him, and departed to Seir, while Jacob dwelt in tents. Henry notes that Esau embraced Jacob, that God has the hearts of all men in his hands and can turn them, and that when a man's ways please the Lord he makes even his enemies to be at peace with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. In the first year of the fifth week of this jubilee Jacob crossed the Jordan, dwelt beyond it, and pastured his sheep from the sea of the heap to Bethshan, Dothan, and the forest of Akrabbim. Henry notes that Jacob did not content himself with words of thanks for God's favour but gave real thanks, keeping up religion and the worship of God in his family wherever he pitched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. In the days when Jacob went to Mesopotamia, Esau took Mahalath, the daughter of Ishmael, as his wife, gathered his father's flocks and his wives, and went to dwell on Mount Seir, leaving Isaac alone at the Well of the Oath. Henry notes that good examples impress even the profane, but Esau thought by pleasing his parents in one thing to atone for other wrongdoings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Isaac went up from the Well of the Oath and dwelt in the tower of Abraham his father on the mountains of Hebron. The move placed Isaac in the seat of his father's sojourn, apart from Esau, awaiting the gifts his son would send. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Jacob sent to his father and mother from time to time all they needed, and they blessed Jacob with all their heart and soul. The gifts were the fruit of Jacob's prosperity in Haran, returned in honour to the parents who had sent him away blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
