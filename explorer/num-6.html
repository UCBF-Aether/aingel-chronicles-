The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When either man or woman shall separate themselves to vow a vow of a Nazarite, to separate themselves unto the LORD:

3 He shall separate himself from wine and strong drink, and shall drink no vinegar of wine, or vinegar of strong drink, neither shall he drink any liquor of grapes, nor eat moist grapes, or dried.

4 All the days of his separation shall he eat nothing that is made of the vine tree, from the kernels even to the husk.

5 All the days of the vow of his separation there shall no razor come upon his head: until the days be fulfilled, in the which he separateth himself unto the LORD, he shall be holy, and shall let the locks of the hair of his head grow.

6 All the days that he separateth himself unto the LORD he shall come at no dead body.

7 He shall not make himself unclean for his father, or for his mother, for his brother, or for his sister, when they die: because the consecration of his God is upon his head.

8 All the days of his separation he is holy unto the LORD.

9 And if any man die very suddenly by him, and he hath defiled the head of his consecration; then he shall shave his head in the day of his cleansing, on the seventh day shall he shave it.

10 And on the eighth day he shall bring two turtles, or two young pigeons, to the priest, to the door of the tabernacle of the congregation:

11 And the priest shall offer the one for a sin offering, and the other for a burnt offering, and make an atonement for him, for that he sinned by the dead, and shall hallow his head that same day.

12 And he shall consecrate unto the LORD the days of his separation, and shall bring a lamb of the first year for a trespass offering: but the days that were before shall be lost, because his separation was defiled.

13 And this is the law of the Nazarite, when the days of his separation are fulfilled: he shall be brought unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation:

14 And he shall offer his offering unto the LORD, one he lamb of the first year without blemish for a burnt offering, and one ewe lamb of the first year without blemish for a sin offering, and one ram without blemish for peace offerings,

15 And a basket of unleavened bread, cakes of fine flour mingled with oil, and wafers of unleavened bread anointed with oil, and their meat offering, and their drink offerings.

16 And the priest shall bring them before the LORD, and shall offer his sin offering, and his burnt offering:

17 And he shall offer the ram for a sacrifice of peace offerings unto the LORD, with the basket of unleavened bread: the priest shall offer also his meat offering, and his drink offering.

18 And the Nazarite shall shave the head of his separation at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and shall take the hair of the head of his separation, and put it in the fire which is under the sacrifice of the peace offerings.

19 And the priest shall take the sodden shoulder of the ram, and one unleavened cake out of the basket, and one unleavened wafer, and shall put them upon the hands of the Nazarite, after the hair of his separation is shaven:

20 And the priest shall wave them for a wave offering before the LORD: this is holy for the priest, with the wave breast and heave shoulder: and after that the Nazarite may drink wine.

21 This is the law of the Nazarite who hath vowed, and of his offering unto the LORD for his separation, beside that that his hand shall get: according to the vow which he vowed, so he must do after the law of his separation.

22 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

23 Speak unto Aaron and unto his sons, saying, On this wise ye shall bless the children of Israel, saying unto them,

24 The LORD bless thee, and keep thee:

25 The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee:

26 The LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace.

27 And they shall put my name upon the children of Israel, and I will bless them.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives two things that belong together: the Nazarite vow and the priestly blessing. A man or woman who vows to separate themselves to the LORD abstains from wine and all grape produce, lets no razor come on the head, and must not defile himself for the dead, even father or mother; defilement or completion of the vow each has its appointed offerings, and at the end the hair is shaved and burnt under the peace offering. The chapter closes with the blessing Aaron and his sons are to put on Israel: the LORD bless thee and keep thee, make his face shine upon thee, and give thee peace. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Nazarite. One who vows to separate himself to the LORD, abstaining from wine and grape produce, letting no razor come on the head, and avoiding defilement by the dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. When a man or woman vows the vow of a Nazarite, to separate themselves to the Lord, the word Nazarite itself signifies separation. Henry notes the vow was in general voluntary, for a limited time, and open to any person who pleased, though some, like Samson and John the Baptist, were appointed Nazarites from before birth for all their days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The Nazarite separated himself from wine and strong drink, drinking no vinegar of wine or of strong drink, nor any liquor of grapes. Henry's rule is that those who separate themselves to God must not gratify the desires of the body but keep it under, and Christians should be very moderate in wine, lest its love get the mastery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. All the days of his separation he ate nothing made of the vine, from kernel to husk. Henry draws the wider lesson: the utmost care to avoid sin, and all that borders on it, leads to it, or may tempt us, is taught by this total abstinence from the vine's fruit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. No razor came upon his head all the days of his separation, until the days were fulfilled. Henry notes the uncut hair was the Nazarite's mark, as with Samson, signifying neglect of the body and of its ease and ornament for God's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. All the days he separated himself to the Lord he came at no dead body. The Nazarite kept his conscience pure from dead works and touched no unclean thing, since contact with death broke the consecration he lived under. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He might not make himself unclean even for father, mother, brother or sister at their death, because his consecration was upon his head. Henry's principle is that devotion to God moderates even natural affections: the vow to the Lord outranked the nearest family claim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. All the days of his separation he was holy to the Lord. Henry insists this was the meaning of all the outward observances, and without inward holiness they were of no account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. If any man died very suddenly beside him, defiling the head of his consecration, he shaved his head on the day of his cleansing, the seventh day. Henry distinguishes this case: no sacrifice was appointed for wilful breach of the vow, but those who did not sin wilfully were relieved, and the defiled Nazarite was restored by cleansing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. On the eighth day he brought two turtles or two young pigeons to the priest at the tabernacle door. The eighth day, after a full week of cleansing, marked his return to the sanctuary he had been cut off from by defilement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The priest offered one bird for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering, making atonement for him, and he hallowed his head that same day. Atonement came first, and only then was the consecrated head hallowed anew, since the old consecration had been broken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. He consecrated to the Lord the days of his separation anew and brought a lamb of the first year for a trespass offering, while the days before were lost. Henry's rule is plain: the interrupted vow did not resume where it stopped, but began again, and the loss of the former days was part of the cost of defilement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. This is the law of the Nazarite when the days of his separation are fulfilled: he was brought to the door of the tabernacle. The completion was a public act at the sanctuary door, not a private decision that the vow was over. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He offered to the Lord a he lamb of the first year without blemish for a burnt offering, a ewe lamb for a sin offering, and a ram without blemish for peace offerings. The threefold offering at completion covered every side of the vow: dedication, sin, and fellowship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. With the offerings came a basket of unleavened bread, cakes of fine flour mingled with oil and wafers anointed with oil, with their meat and drink offerings. The unleavened bread kept the completion feast free from the leaven of corruption, matching the purity of the separation itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The priest brought them before the Lord and offered his sin offering and his burnt offering. The priest, not the Nazarite, presented the offerings, since acceptance with God came through the appointed mediator, not through the vow itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The priest offered the ram for a peace offering to the Lord, with the basket of unleavened bread, and the priest's own meat and drink offerings. The peace offering closed the vow in fellowship: after separation, the Nazarite shared a meal of peace before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The Nazarite shaved the head of his separation at the tabernacle door and put the hair in the fire under the peace offering. The hair, the visible mark of the whole vow, was given up at the end, consumed with the offering that sealed his return to ordinary life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The priest took the sodden shoulder of the ram, one unleavened cake and one wafer, and put them on the Nazarite's hands after he had shaved. The portion placed in his own hands showed the completed vow was now his to carry back into common life as a finished service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The priest waved them as a wave offering before the Lord, and they were holy for the priest, with the wave breast and heave shoulder. The waved portion belonged to the priest, a reminder that even a voluntary vow ended in provision for God's ministers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. This is the law of the Nazarite who vowed, and of his offering for his separation, besides whatever else his hand could get, according to the vow he vowed. Henry's closing lesson is to vow and pay to the Lord our God, for he has no pleasure in fools who vow and do not perform. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Aaron and his sons were told: on this wise you shall bless the children of Israel, saying to them. The blessing was given in a set form, not invented by the priests, so that the people heard God's own words of blessing from his appointed mouths. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. The Lord bless you and keep you: to be under the almighty protection of God our Saviour is the first part of the blessing. Henry calls protection and favour the substance of this blessing and the sum total of all blessings, beside which worldly joys are not worthy to be mentioned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The Lord make his face shine upon you and be gracious unto you. Henry pictures God's favour as the smile of a loving Father, or the cheering beams of the sun, mercifully forgiving sins, supplying wants, and consoling the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The Lord lift up his countenance upon you and give you peace. The lifted countenance is God's attentive, favourable regard, and its gift is peace: not merely the absence of trouble, but the settled favour that prepares us by grace for eternal glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. They shall put my name upon the children of Israel, and I will bless them. The priests put the name on the people, but God himself blesses: Henry notes the name Jehovah is three times repeated, and Christians read in it the blessing from the grace of Christ, the love of the Father, and the communion of the Holy Ghost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
