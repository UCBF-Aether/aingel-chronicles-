The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech.

2 And it came to pass, as they journeyed from the east, that they found a plain in the land of Shinar; and they dwelt there.

3 And they said one to another, Go to, let us make brick, and burn them thoroughly. And they had brick for stone, and slime had they for morter.

4 And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.

5 And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded.

6 And the LORD said, Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do.

7 Go to, let us go down, and there confound their language, that they may not understand one another’s speech.

8 So the LORD scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth: and they left off to build the city.

9 Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.

10 These are the generations of Shem: Shem was an hundred years old, and begat Arphaxad two years after the flood:

11 And Shem lived after he begat Arphaxad five hundred years, and begat sons and daughters.

12 And Arphaxad lived five and thirty years, and begat Salah:

13 And Arphaxad lived after he begat Salah four hundred and three years, and begat sons and daughters.

14 And Salah lived thirty years, and begat Eber:

15 And Salah lived after he begat Eber four hundred and three years, and begat sons and daughters.

16 And Eber lived four and thirty years, and begat Peleg:

17 And Eber lived after he begat Peleg four hundred and thirty years, and begat sons and daughters.

18 And Peleg lived thirty years, and begat Reu:

19 And Peleg lived after he begat Reu two hundred and nine years, and begat sons and daughters.

20 And Reu lived two and thirty years, and begat Serug:

21 And Reu lived after he begat Serug two hundred and seven years, and begat sons and daughters.

22 And Serug lived thirty years, and begat Nahor:

23 And Serug lived after he begat Nahor two hundred years, and begat sons and daughters.

24 And Nahor lived nine and twenty years, and begat Terah:

25 And Nahor lived after he begat Terah an hundred and nineteen years, and begat sons and daughters.

26 And Terah lived seventy years, and begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran.

27 Now these are the generations of Terah: Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran; and Haran begat Lot.

28 And Haran died before his father Terah in the land of his nativity, in Ur of the Chaldees.

29 And Abram and Nahor took them wives: the name of Abram’s wife was Sarai; and the name of Nahor’s wife, Milcah, the daughter of Haran, the father of Milcah, and the father of Iscah.

30 But Sarai was barren; she had no child.

31 And Terah took Abram his son, and Lot the son of Haran his son’s son, and Sarai his daughter in law, his son Abram’s wife; and they went forth with them from Ur of the Chaldees, to go into the land of Canaan; and they came unto Haran, and dwelt there.

32 And the days of Terah were two hundred and five years: and Terah died in Haran.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

With one language, humanity builds a city and a tower at Babel to keep from scattering, and God confuses their speech so the work stops and they spread abroad. The chapter then traces Shem's line down to Terah, whose death in Haran leaves Abram's household ready for the call that follows. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The descendants of Noah were still held together by one common language, and Henry and JFB note that many were unwilling to separate even though God had directed mankind to spread out and fill the earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. They settled in the fertile valley of Shinar, watered by the great rivers, and chose it as the centre of their union and power rather than moving onward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. With no stone in that region, they used brick, some sun-dried and some kiln-burnt, with bitumen as a strong cement, which is the ordinary building material of that country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. A tower reaching to heaven is a figure of speech for great height. The real fault, the commentators stress, was not building a city as such, but building it to defeat God's purpose by preventing their scattering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God's coming down is spoken in human terms: it pictures God taking notice of and inspecting the work, not a literal descent from place to place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. One people with one language could carry their design through, which shows both the strength of united purpose and why God intervened before their rebellion hardened further. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Confounding their language meant a failure in speech, so that people understood only those of their own group. In this way God easily undid their plan and compelled the very dispersion they had tried to prevent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The scattering was God's doing, and once it happened the building stopped, since the united workforce that the project depended on was gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The place was called Babel because there God confused human speech, and the unfinished city stands as a reminder that plans made against God's direction come to nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The record now turns to Shem's line, tracing the family through which God's purposes will run after the flood, beginning with Arphaxad born two years after it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Arphaxad's long life after his son's birth, with other sons and daughters, shows the line continuing and widening even as the years of each generation begin to shorten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The line moves one step forward from Arphaxad to Shelah, keeping the chosen family in view through the generations between the flood and Abram. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Shelah's years after Shelah's son, with further children, continue the steady record of one generation succeeding another on the way to Abram. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Eber is the next named link, and his name is remembered in the line because the family history is being traced person by person, not merely by peoples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Eber's long remaining years, with other sons and daughters, underline that the chosen line was preserved through ordinary family life over a great span of time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Peleg follows Eber, and the commentators connect his lifetime with the division of peoples that followed Babel, as mankind spread into separate nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Peleg's years after his son, with further children, keep the genealogy moving forward in the same measured pattern from father to son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Reu is the next link in the chain from Shem toward Abram, and the record notes his age at his son's birth to fix each generation in its place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Reu's remaining years, with other sons and daughters, show the family branching out while the main line that the history follows stays clearly marked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Serug follows Reu, bringing the line nearer to Abram's own time as the ages at fatherhood continue to fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Serug's years after his son, with further children, continue the pattern of a line preserved generation by generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Nahor is the next father in the line, and his naming here matters because his family will soon meet Abram's own story directly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Nahor's remaining years, with other sons and daughters, show the wider family growing around the single line the history is tracing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Terah, Abram's father, is the next link, so the long genealogy from Shem now arrives at the household from which Abram himself will come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Terah's years after his son, with further children, close the long backward look and set the stage for Abram's own family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Terah's three sons, Abram, Nahor, and Haran, are named together because the story that follows turns on all three households, not on Abram alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The record narrows to Terah's family: Haran fathered Lot, who will travel with Abram and whose story runs alongside his uncle's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Haran died in his homeland, Ur, a city given to fire-worship, and JFB notes that Terah's family shared in that idolatry, which makes Abram's later call out of it all the sharper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Abram married Sarai and Nahor married Milcah, Haran's daughter, so the two brothers' households were closely joined before the journey to Canaan began. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Sarai's childlessness is noted here because it will make God's later promise of a son to Abram a matter of faith against all ordinary expectation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Terah took Abram, Lot, and Sarai out of Ur toward Canaan, but they stopped and settled in Haran, on the road toward the Euphrates crossing into the promised land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Terah died in Haran at 205 years, and the commentators note the puzzle this age raises about the order of his sons' births, which is eased if Abram was not the eldest son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms
