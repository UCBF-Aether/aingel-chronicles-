Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Elisha said, Hear ye the word of the LORD; Thus saith the LORD, To morrow about this time shall a measure of fine flour be sold for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, in the gate of Samaria.

2 Then a lord on whose hand the king leaned answered the man of God, and said, Behold, if the LORD would make windows in heaven, might this thing be? And he said, Behold, thou shalt see it with thine eyes, but shalt not eat thereof.

3 And there were four leprous men at the entering in of the gate: and they said one to another, Why sit we here until we die?

4 If we say, We will enter into the city, then the famine is in the city, and we shall die there: and if we sit still here, we die also. Now therefore come, and let us fall unto the host of the Syrians: if they save us alive, we shall live; and if they kill us, we shall but die.

5 And they rose up in the twilight, to go unto the camp of the Syrians: and when they were come to the uttermost part of the camp of Syria, behold, there was no man there.

6 For the LORD had made the host of the Syrians to hear a noise of chariots, and a noise of horses, even the noise of a great host: and they said one to another, Lo, the king of Israel hath hired against us the kings of the Hittites, and the kings of the Egyptians, to come upon us.

7 Wherefore they arose and fled in the twilight, and left their tents, and their horses, and their asses, even the camp as it was, and fled for their life.

8 And when these lepers came to the uttermost part of the camp, they went into one tent, and did eat and drink, and carried thence silver, and gold, and raiment, and went and hid it; and came again, and entered into another tent, and carried thence also, and went and hid it.

9 Then they said one to another, We do not well: this day is a day of good tidings, and we hold our peace: if we tarry till the morning light, some mischief will come upon us: now therefore come, that we may go and tell the king’s household.

10 So they came and called unto the porter of the city: and they told them, saying, We came to the camp of the Syrians, and, behold, there was no man there, neither voice of man, but horses tied, and asses tied, and the tents as they were.

11 And he called the porters; and they told it to the king’s house within.

12 And the king arose in the night, and said unto his servants, I will now shew you what the Syrians have done to us. They know that we be hungry; therefore are they gone out of the camp to hide themselves in the field, saying, When they come out of the city, we shall catch them alive, and get into the city.

13 And one of his servants answered and said, Let some take, I pray thee, five of the horses that remain, which are left in the city, (behold, they are as all the multitude of Israel that are left in it: behold, I say, they are even as all the multitude of the Israelites that are consumed:) and let us send and see.

14 They took therefore two chariot horses; and the king sent after the host of the Syrians, saying, Go and see.

15 And they went after them unto Jordan: and, lo, all the way was full of garments and vessels, which the Syrians had cast away in their haste. And the messengers returned, and told the king.

16 And the people went out, and spoiled the tents of the Syrians. So a measure of fine flour was sold for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, according to the word of the LORD.

17 And the king appointed the lord on whose hand he leaned to have the charge of the gate: and the people trode upon him in the gate, and he died, as the man of God had said, who spake when the king came down to him.

18 And it came to pass as the man of God had spoken to the king, saying, Two measures of barley for a shekel, and a measure of fine flour for a shekel, shall be to morrow about this time in the gate of Samaria:

19 And that lord answered the man of God, and said, Now, behold, if the LORD should make windows in heaven, might such a thing be? And he said, Behold, thou shalt see it with thine eyes, but shalt not eat thereof.

20 And so it fell out unto him: for the people trode upon him in the gate, and he died.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Elisha promises that by tomorrow food will be cheap in starving Samaria, and four lepers at the gate find the Syrian camp deserted, the LORD having made them hear chariots and flee. The lepers report it, the city plunders the camp, food is cheap as promised, and the doubting officer is trampled at the gate. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Elisha said that by tomorrow about this time a measure of fine flour would sell for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, in Samaria's gate. Henry notes man's extremity is God's opportunity to make his power glorious: his time to appear for his people is when their strength is gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The lord on whose hand the king leaned answered that even if the LORD made windows in heaven, this could not be, and Elisha replied he would see it with his eyes but not eat of it. Henry warns unbelief greatly dishonours God and deprives men of favours he designed for them; such shall see the promise at a distance but never taste it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Four leprous men at the gate's entrance asked one another why they sat there until they died. Henry's passage shows God using the outcasts at the gate as his messengers of deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The lepers reasoned that entering the famine-struck city meant death, sitting still meant death, and going to the Syrians might at least bring life if the Syrians saved them alive. Henry notes providence ordered their coming just as the Syrians fled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. They rose in the twilight to go to the Syrian camp, and when they came to its uttermost part, no man was there. Henry observes God can make the stoutest heart tremble, and those who will not fear God he can make fear at the shaking of a leaf. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The LORD had made the Syrian host hear a noise of chariots and horses, as of a great host, so they said the king of Israel had hired the Hittite and Egyptian kings against them. Henry presents this unseen terror as God's own doing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The Syrians arose and fled in the twilight, leaving their tents, horses, asses, and camp as it was, fleeing for their lives. Henry notes providence ordered that the lepers came as soon as the Syrians were fled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The lepers went into one tent, ate and drank, and carried away silver, gold, and raiment, hiding it, then went into another tent and did likewise. Henry observes their consciences would soon tell them mischief would befall them if they cared only for themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. They said to one another they did not well, for this was a day of good tidings and they held their peace, and if they tarried till morning, mischief would come upon them, so they resolved to tell the king's household. Henry draws the evangelistic lesson: those who have found Christ's unsearchable riches will not long delay to report the good tidings to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. They came and called to the city's porter, telling him they had come to the Syrian camp and found no man there, only horses and asses tied and tents as they were. Henry notes natural humanity and fear of punishment check selfishness, but love to Christ gladly shares good things with brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The porter called the other porters, and they told it to the king's house within. Henry's outline shows the good tidings spreading from the gate to the palace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The king arose in the night and told his servants the Syrians had surely hidden themselves in the field, planning to catch the Israelites alive when they came out of the city. Henry presents the king's suspicion as the foil to God's plain deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. A servant proposed taking five remaining horses — like all the multitude of Israel left, as if already consumed — and sending to see. Henry notes Israel's wants were supplied in a way they little thought of, encouraging dependence on God's power in the greatest straits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. They took two chariot horses, and the king sent after the Syrian host to go and see. Henry's theme is God's promise safely relied on, for no word of his shall fall to the ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The messengers followed the Syrians to Jordan, finding the way full of garments and vessels the Syrians had cast away in their haste. Henry notes this as the evidence of a flight ordered by God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The people went out and spoiled the Syrian tents, so a measure of fine flour sold for a shekel and two measures of barley for a shekel, according to the LORD's word. Henry observes God's promise was fulfilled exactly, supplying Israel's wants unexpectedly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The king appointed the lord on whose hand he leaned to have charge of the gate, and the people trode upon him in the gate, and he died, as the man of God had spoken. Henry notes the unbelieving nobleman saw the plenty, to silence and shame his unbelief, but did not eat of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. All came to pass as Elisha had spoken to the king: two measures of barley for a shekel and a measure of fine flour for a shekel in the gate of Samaria. Henry stresses God's promise may be safely relied on, for no word of his shall fall to the ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. The lord had answered the man of God that even if the LORD made windows in heaven, this could not be, and Elisha had said he would see it with his eyes but not eat thereof. Henry draws the lesson of how deeply God resents distrust of his power, providence, and promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. So it fell out to him: the people trode upon him in the gate, and he died. Henry closes with the certainty of God's threatenings upon the guilty and the uncertainty of life and its enjoyments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms
