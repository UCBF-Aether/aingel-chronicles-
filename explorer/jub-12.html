2 And he said,

4 For there is no spirit in them

5 For they are dumb forms, and a misleading of the

6 heart.

7 Worship them not :

8 Worship the God of heaven,

9 Who causeth the rain and the dew to descend on the

10 earth And doeth everything upon the earth,

11 And hath created everything by His word

12 Why do ye worship things that have no spirit in

13 them?

14 For they are the work of (men's) hands

15 And on your shoulders do ye bear them, And ye have no help from them,

16 But they are a great cause of shame to those who

17 make them, And a misleading of the heart to those who worship

18 them : Worship them not."

19 And his father said unto him, " I also know it, my son, but what shall I do with a people who have made me to serve before them ?

20 And if I tell them the truth, they will slay me ; for their soul cleaveth

21 to them to worship them and honour them.

22 Keep silent my son, lest they slay thee." 8.

23 And these words he spake to his two brothers, and they were angry with him and he kept silent.

24 And in the fortieth jubilee, in the second week, in the seventh year thereof, Abram took to himself a wife, and her name was Sarai, the daughter of his father, and she became his wife.

25 And Haran, his brother, took to himself a wife in the third year of the third week, and she bare him a son in the seventh year of this week and he called his name Lot.

26 And Nahor, his brother, took to himself a wife.

27 And in the sixtieth year of the life of Abram, that is, in the fourth week, in the fourth year thereof, (1936 AM) Abram arose by night, and burned the house of the idols, and he burned all that was in the house, and no man knew it.

28 And they arose in the night and sought to save their gods from the midst of the fire.

29 And Haran hasted to save them, but the fire flamed over him, and he was burnt in the fire, and he died in Ur of the Chaldees before Terah his father, and they buried him in Ur of the Chaldees.

30 The Family of Terah in Haran ; Abram 's Experiences there ; his Journey to Canaan

31 And Terah went forth from Ur of the Chaldees, he and his sons, to go into the land of Lebanon and into the land of Canaan, and he dwelt in the land of Haran, and Abram dwelt with Terah his father in

32 Haran two weeks of years.

33 And in the sixth week, in the fifth year thereof, Abram sat up throughout the night on the new moon of the seventh month to observe the stars from the evening to the morning, in order to see what would be the character of the year with regard to the rains, and he was alone as he sat and observed.

41 And all things that are are the work of Thy hands.

42 Deliver me from the hands of evil spirits who have

43 sway over the thoughts of men's hearts, And let them not lead me astray from Thee, my

44 God.

45 And stablish Thou me and my seed for ever That we go not astray from henceforth and for

46 evermore."

47 And he said, " Shall I return unto Ur of the Chaldees who seek my face that I may return to them, or am I to remain here in this place?

48 The right path before Thee prosper it in the hands of Thy servant that he may fulfil (it) and that I may not walk in the deceitfulness of my heart, O my God." 22.

49 And he made an end of speaking and praying, and behold the word of the Lord was sent to him through me, saying : " Get thee up from thy country, and from thy kindred and from the house of thy father unto a land which I will show thee, and I shall make thee a great and numerous nation."

52 And thou wilt be blessed in the earth,

53 And in thee will all families of the earth be blessed,

59 took the books of his fathers, and these were written in Hebrew, and he transcribed them, and he began from henceforth to study them, and I made known to him that which he could not (understand), and he studied them during the six rainy months.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the sixth week, in the seventh year thereof, Abram said to Terah his father, saying, 'Father,' and he said, 'Behold, here am I, my son.' The confrontation opens in the formal address of son to father, the setting for Abram's argument against idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 2. This verse opens Abram's speech: and he said. The sentence is completed by the idol argument of the following verses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 3. 'What help and profit have we from those idols which thou dost worship, and before which thou dost bow thyself?' Abram's first question is practical: what help or profit comes from the idols at all? Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 4. 'For there is no spirit in them.' The answer begins with the idols' emptiness: no spirit dwells in them, so no help can come from them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 5. 'For they are dumb forms, and a misleading of the heart.' Dumb forms mislead the heart: speechlessness and deception go together in Abram's charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 6. This verse completes the charge: the idols are a misleading of the heart. The heart misled is the seat of the error Abram discerned at fourteen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. 'Worship them not.' The charge becomes a command: the idols are not to be worshipped. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. 'Worship the God of heaven.' Against the idols Abram sets the God of heaven, the true object of worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. The God of heaven causes the rain and the dew to descend on the earth. Rain and dew, the farmer's life, come from God, not from idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. He does everything upon the earth. God's universal agency replaces the idols' pretended local powers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. He has created everything by his word. Creation by word marks the Creator off from creatures made by hands, the contrast sharpened in verse 14. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. 'Why do ye worship things that have no spirit in them?' The question returns with its reason: spiritless things cannot deserve worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. This verse completes the question: why worship things with no spirit in them? The repetition presses the point home to Terah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. 'For they are the work of men's hands.' Idols are artefacts: made by hands, they cannot be makers or helpers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. 'And on your shoulders do ye bear them, and ye have no help from them.' The worshippers carry their gods, reversing true worship: the borne god cannot bear or help its bearers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. 'But they are a great cause of shame to those who make them.' The makers are shamed by their own work: the idol reflects dishonour back on its maker. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. 'And a misleading of the heart to those who worship them: worship them not.' The speech closes where it began: idols mislead the heart, and the command stands, worship them not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. This verse completes the closing command: worship them not. Abram's argument ends in prohibition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. His father said, 'I also know it, my son, but what shall I do with a people who have made me to serve before them?' Terah concedes the truth but pleads compulsion: the people made him serve before the idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. 'And if I tell them the truth, they will slay me; for their soul cleaves to them to worship them and honour them.' Truth-telling means death, because the people's souls cleave to the idols they worship and honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. This verse completes Terah's plea: their souls cleave to the idols to worship and honour them. Cleaving is the people's bondage, matching Terah's own servitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. 'Keep silent, my son, lest they slay thee.' Terah's counsel is silence for safety: speech against idols will get Abram slain as truth would slay Terah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. These words he spoke to his two brothers, and they were angry with him, and he kept silent. Abram carries the argument to his brothers, meets anger, and obeys his father's counsel of silence for a season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. In the fortieth jubilee, in the second week, in the seventh year thereof, Abram took to himself a wife, Sarai, daughter of his father, and she became his wife. Abram's marriage to Sarai, his father's daughter, is dated to the fortieth jubilee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. Haran his brother took to himself a wife in the third year of the third week, and she bare him a son in the seventh year of this week, and he called his name Lot. Haran's son Lot is born, the nephew Abram will later take as his own son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. Nahor his brother took to himself a wife. The brief notice pairs Nahor's marriage with Abram's and Haran's, completing the three brothers' marriages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. In the sixtieth year of Abram's life, that is, in the fourth week, in the fourth year thereof, year 1936 AM, Abram arose by night and burned the house of the idols, and burned all that was in the house, and no man knew it. At sixty Abram moves from speech to act: the idol house burns by night, secretly, fulfilling the silence of verse 23 with fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. They arose in the night and sought to save their gods from the midst of the fire. The idol-savers rush to rescue gods that cannot save themselves, proving Abram's charge that the idols give no help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. Haran hasted to save them, but the fire flamed over him, and he was burnt in the fire, and he died in Ur of the Chaldees before Terah his father, and they buried him in Ur of the Chaldees. Haran dies saving the idols, burnt in Ur before his father, and is buried there; Genesis likewise records Haran's death before Terah in Ur. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the family of Terah in Haran, Abram's experiences there, and his journey to Canaan. It divides the Ur idol-burning from the Haran sojourn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. Terah went forth from Ur of the Chaldees, he and his sons, to go into the land of Lebanon and into the land of Canaan, and he dwelt in the land of Haran. Terah's migration aims at Lebanon and Canaan but halts at Haran, as in Genesis. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. Abram dwelt with Terah his father in Haran two weeks of years. Fourteen years in Haran follow the departure from Ur, the sojourn in which Abram's night observation and call will come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. In the sixth week, in the fifth year thereof, Abram sat up throughout the night on the new moon of the seventh month to observe the stars from evening to morning, in order to see what would be the character of the year with regard to the rains, and he was alone as he sat and observed. Abram the star-observer keeps a lone night vigil on the seventh month's new moon to read the year's rains from the stars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. A word came into his heart, saying: 'All the signs of the stars, and the signs of the moon and of the sun are all in the hand of the Lord.' The vigil turns: star, moon and sun signs are all in the Lord's hand, not independent omens, against the Watcher astrology of chapter 8 and the Chaldean divination of chapter 11. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. 'Why do I search them out?' The rhetorical question rejects the star-search itself: if the signs are in God's hand, searching them apart from him is vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. 'If he desires, he causes it to rain, morning and evening.' Rain comes at God's desire, morning and evening alike, not by stellar compulsion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. 'And if he desires, he withholds it, and all things are in his hand.' Withholding is equally God's act, and all things rest in his hand: the weather's whole course is divine will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. He prayed that night. The night of observation becomes a night of prayer, introducing the petition that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. 'My God, God Most High, thou alone art my God, and thee and thy dominion have I chosen.' Abram chooses God Most High alone and his dominion, the exclusive allegiance his idol argument demanded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. 'And thou hast created all things.' The Creator claim grounds the choice: the God who made all things outranks all made things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. 'And all things that are are the work of thy hands.' All existing things are God's handiwork, the reverse of the idols as the work of men's hands in verse 14. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. 'Deliver me from the hands of evil spirits who have sway over the thoughts of men's hearts.' Abram asks deliverance from the evil spirits, Mastema's tenth, who sway the thoughts of men's hearts toward idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. 'And let them not lead me astray from thee, my God.' The plea continues: the spirits must not lead Abram astray from his chosen God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. This verse completes the plea: let them not lead me astray from thee, my God. Deliverance and non-erring are the prayer's double ask. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. 'And stablish thou me and my seed forever, that we go not astray from henceforth and forevermore.' The prayer ends by asking perpetual establishment for Abram and his seed, never to go astray again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. This verse completes the establishment plea: that Abram and his seed go not astray from henceforth and forevermore. The seed's future fidelity is prayed for in advance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. 'Shall I return unto Ur of the Chaldees, who seek my face that I may return to them, or am I to remain here in this place?' Abram asks his practical question: return to Ur's seekers or remain in Haran? Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 48. 'The right path before thee prosper it in the hands of thy servant that he may fulfil it, and that I may not walk in the deceitfulness of my heart, O my God.' Abram asks God to prosper the right path in his servant's hands and to keep him from his own heart's deceit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 49. He made an end of speaking and praying, and behold the word of the Lord was sent to him through the angel, saying: 'Get thee up from thy country, and from thy kindred and from the house of thy father unto a land which I will show thee, and I shall make thee a great and numerous nation.' JFB on Genesis 12:1 notes the call included two promises, the land of his future posterity and blessing to all the earth in his posterity, and Abraham obeyed, an instance of faith frequently mentioned in the New Testament. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. 'And I shall bless thee.' The first blessing of the call: God himself will bless Abram. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. 'And I shall make thy name great.' The name made great in Chaldea by the ravens in chapter 11 is now promised greatness by God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 52. 'And thou wilt be blessed in the earth.' Abram's blessing extends to his earthly life, not only to his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. 'And in thee will all families of the earth be blessed.' The universal promise: all earth's families are blessed in Abram, the second promise JFB notes in the call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. 'And I shall bless them that bless thee, and curse them that curse thee.' Blessing and curse follow men's treatment of Abram, making him the measure of nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. 'And I shall be a God to thee and thy son, and to thy son's son, and to all thy seed: fear not, from henceforth and unto all generations of the earth I am thy God.' The covenant closes the call: God to Abram, son, son's son and all seed, from henceforth to all generations, with fear not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 56. The Lord God said: 'Open his mouth and his ears, that he may hear and speak with his mouth, with the language which hath been revealed,' for it had ceased from the mouths of all the children of men from the day of the overthrow of Babel. The revealed language, lost since Babel's overthrow, is to be opened again in Abram's mouth and ears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 57. The angel opened his mouth, ears and lips, and began to speak with him in Hebrew, in the tongue of the creation. Hebrew returns as the tongue of creation, the first language taught again by the angel, reversing Babel's confusion for Abram. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 58. This verse opens the books notice: and he took the books of his fathers. The sentence is completed by the transcription and study of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 59. He took the books of his fathers, written in Hebrew, and transcribed them, and began from henceforth to study them, and the angel made known to him what he could not understand, and he studied them during the six rainy months. The fathers' Hebrew books are transcribed and studied for six rainy months, with angelic explanation of the hard places, grounding Abram's knowledge in written tradition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 60. In the seventh year of the sixth week he spoke to his father and informed him that he would leave Haran to go into the land of Canaan to see it and return to him. Abram announces a Canaan reconnaissance, promising to return to his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 61. Terah his father said to him: 'Go in peace.' The father's first word is permission: go in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 62. 'May the eternal God make thy path straight.' Terah's blessing asks the eternal God to straighten Abram's path. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 63. 'And the Lord protect thee from all evil.' Protection from all evil is the blessing's second petition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 64. 'And grant unto thee grace, mercy and favour before those who see thee.' Grace, mercy and favour before onlookers are asked for the traveller. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 65. 'And may none of the children of men have power over thee to harm thee.' No man is to have power to harm Abram, the blessing's guarding petition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 66. 'Go in peace.' The blessing closes as it opened: go in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 67. 'And if thou seest a land pleasant to thy eyes to dwell in, then arise and take me to thee and take Lot with thee, the son of Haran thy brother, as thine own son: the Lord be with thee.' If Canaan pleases Abram's eyes, he is to fetch Terah and take Lot as his own son, with the Lord's presence wished upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 68. 'And Nahor thy brother leave with me till thou returnest in peace, and we go with thee all together.' Nahor stays with Terah until Abram's peaceful return, when all will go together: the father's plan keeps the family joined across the reconnaissance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H.   • Verse 20. 'And if I tell them the truth, they will slay me; for their soul cleaves to them to worship them and honour them.' Truth-telling means death, because the people's souls cleave to the idols they worship and honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. This verse completes Terah's plea: their souls cleave to the idols to worship and honour them. Cleaving is the people's bondage, matching Terah's own servitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. 'Keep silent, my son, lest they slay thee.' Terah's counsel is silence for safety: speech against idols will get Abram slain as truth would slay Terah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. These words he spoke to his two brothers, and they were angry with him, and he kept silent. Abram carries the argument to his brothers, meets anger, and obeys his father's counsel of silence for a season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. In the fortieth jubilee, in the second week, in the seventh year thereof, Abram took to himself a wife, Sarai, daughter of his father, and she became his wife. Abram's marriage to Sarai, his father's daughter, is dated to the fortieth jubilee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. Haran his brother took to himself a wife in the third year of the third week, and she bare him a son in the seventh year of this week, and he called his name Lot. Haran's son Lot is born, the nephew Abram will later take as his own son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. Nahor his brother took to himself a wife. The brief notice pairs Nahor's marriage with Abram's and Haran's, completing the three brothers' marriages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. In the sixtieth year of Abram's life, that is, in the fourth week, in the fourth year thereof, year 1936 AM, Abram arose by night and burned the house of the idols, and burned all that was in the house, and no man knew it. At sixty Abram moves from speech to act: the idol house burns by night, secretly, fulfilling the silence of verse 23 with fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. They arose in the night and sought to save their gods from the midst of the fire. The idol-savers rush to rescue gods that cannot save themselves, proving Abram's charge that the idols give no help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. Haran hasted to save them, but the fire flamed over him, and he was burnt in the fire, and he died in Ur of the Chaldees before Terah his father, and they buried him in Ur of the Chaldees. Haran dies saving the idols, burnt in Ur before his father, and is buried there; Genesis likewise records Haran's death before Terah in Ur. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the family of Terah in Haran, Abram's experiences there, and his journey to Canaan. It divides the Ur idol-burning from the Haran sojourn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. Terah went forth from Ur of the Chaldees, he and his sons, to go into the land of Lebanon and into the land of Canaan, and he dwelt in the land of Haran. Terah's migration aims at Lebanon and Canaan but halts at Haran, as in Genesis. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. Abram dwelt with Terah his father in Haran two weeks of years. Fourteen years in Haran follow the departure from Ur, the sojourn in which Abram's night observation and call will come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. In the sixth week, in the fifth year thereof, Abram sat up throughout the night on the new moon of the seventh month to observe the stars from evening to morning, in order to see what would be the character of the year with regard to the rains, and he was alone as he sat and observed. Abram the star-observer keeps a lone night vigil on the seventh month's new moon to read the year's rains from the stars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. A word came into his heart, saying: 'All the signs of the stars, and the signs of the moon and of the sun are all in the hand of the Lord.' The vigil turns: star, moon and sun signs are all in the Lord's hand, not independent omens, against the Watcher astrology of chapter 8 and the Chaldean divination of chapter 11. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. 'Why do I search them out?' The rhetorical question rejects the star-search itself: if the signs are in God's hand, searching them apart from him is vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. 'If he desires, he causes it to rain, morning and evening.' Rain comes at God's desire, morning and evening alike, not by stellar compulsion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. 'And if he desires, he withholds it, and all things are in his hand.' Withholding is equally God's act, and all things rest in his hand: the weather's whole course is divine will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. He prayed that night. The night of observation becomes a night of prayer, introducing the petition that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. 'My God, God Most High, thou alone art my God, and thee and thy dominion have I chosen.' Abram chooses God Most High alone and his dominion, the exclusive allegiance his idol argument demanded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. 'And thou hast created all things.' The Creator claim grounds the choice: the God who made all things outranks all made things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. 'And all things that are are the work of thy hands.' All existing things are God's handiwork, the reverse of the idols as the work of men's hands in verse 14. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. 'Deliver me from the hands of evil spirits who have sway over the thoughts of men's hearts.' Abram asks deliverance from the evil spirits, Mastema's tenth, who sway the thoughts of men's hearts toward idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. 'And let them not lead me astray from thee, my God.' The plea continues: the spirits must not lead Abram astray from his chosen God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. This verse completes the plea: let them not lead me astray from thee, my God. Deliverance and non-erring are the prayer's double ask. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. 'And stablish thou me and my seed forever, that we go not astray from henceforth and forevermore.' The prayer ends by asking perpetual establishment for Abram and his seed, never to go astray again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. This verse completes the establishment plea: that Abram and his seed go not astray from henceforth and forevermore. The seed's future fidelity is prayed for in advance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. 'Shall I return unto Ur of the Chaldees, who seek my face that I may return to them, or am I to remain here in this place?' Abram asks his practical question: return to Ur's seekers or remain in Haran? Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 48. 'The right path before thee prosper it in the hands of thy servant that he may fulfil it, and that I may not walk in the deceitfulness of my heart, O my God.' Abram asks God to prosper the right path in his servant's hands and to keep him from his own heart's deceit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 51. 'And I shall make thy name great.' The name made great in Chaldea by the ravens in chapter 11 is now promised greatness by God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 56. The Lord God said: 'Open his mouth and his ears, that he may hear and speak with his mouth, with the language which hath been revealed,' for it had ceased from the mouths of all the children of men from the day of the overthrow of Babel. The revealed language, lost since Babel's overthrow, is to be opened again in Abram's mouth and ears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 57. The angel opened his mouth, ears and lips, and began to speak with him in Hebrew, in the tongue of the creation. Hebrew returns as the tongue of creation, the first language taught again by the angel, reversing Babel's confusion for Abram. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 58. This verse opens the books notice: and he took the books of his fathers. The sentence is completed by the transcription and study of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 59. He took the books of his fathers, written in Hebrew, and transcribed them, and began from henceforth to study them, and the angel made known to him what he could not understand, and he studied them during the six rainy months. The fathers' Hebrew books are transcribed and studied for six rainy months, with angelic explanation of the hard places, grounding Abram's knowledge in written tradition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 60. In the seventh year of the sixth week he spoke to his father and informed him that he would leave Haran to go into the land of Canaan to see it and return to him. Abram announces a Canaan reconnaissance, promising to return to his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 61. Terah his father said to him: 'Go in peace.' The father's first word is permission: go in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 62. 'May the eternal God make thy path straight.' Terah's blessing asks the eternal God to straighten Abram's path. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 63. 'And the Lord protect thee from all evil.' Protection from all evil is the blessing's second petition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 64. 'And grant unto thee grace, mercy and favour before those who see thee.' Grace, mercy and favour before onlookers are asked for the traveller. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 65. 'And may none of the children of men have power over thee to harm thee.' No man is to have power to harm Abram, the blessing's guarding petition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 66. 'Go in peace.' The blessing closes as it opened: go in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 67. 'And if thou seest a land pleasant to thy eyes to dwell in, then arise and take me to thee and take Lot with thee, the son of Haran thy brother, as thine own son: the Lord be with thee.' If Canaan pleases Abram's eyes, he is to fetch Terah and take Lot as his own son, with the Lord's presence wished upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 68. 'And Nahor thy brother leave with me till thou returnest in peace, and we go with thee all together.' Nahor stays with Terah until Abram's peaceful return, when all will go together: the father's plan keeps the family joined across the reconnaissance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 49. He made an end of speaking and praying, and behold the word of the Lord was sent to him through the angel, saying: 'Get thee up from thy country, and from thy kindred and from the house of thy father unto a land which I will show thee, and I shall make thee a great and numerous nation.' JFB on Genesis 12:1 notes the call included two promises, the land of his future posterity and blessing to all the earth in his posterity, and Abraham obeyed, an instance of faith frequently mentioned in the New Testament. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 50. 'And I shall bless thee.' The first blessing of the call: God himself will bless Abram. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. 'And thou wilt be blessed in the earth.' Abram's blessing extends to his earthly life, not only to his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. 'And in thee will all families of the earth be blessed.' The universal promise: all earth's families are blessed in Abram, the second promise JFB notes in the call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. 'And I shall bless them that bless thee, and curse them that curse thee.' Blessing and curse follow men's treatment of Abram, making him the measure of nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. 'And I shall be a God to thee and thy son, and to thy son's son, and to all thy seed: fear not, from henceforth and unto all generations of the earth I am thy God.' The covenant closes the call: God to Abram, son, son's son and all seed, from henceforth to all generations, with fear not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
