The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 17 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Hear the right, O LORD, attend unto my cry, give ear unto my prayer, that goeth not out of feigned lips.

2 Let my sentence come forth from thy presence; let thine eyes behold the things that are equal.

3 Thou hast proved mine heart; thou hast visited me in the night; thou hast tried me, and shalt find nothing; I am purposed that my mouth shall not transgress.

4 Concerning the works of men, by the word of thy lips I have kept me from the paths of the destroyer.

5 Hold up my goings in thy paths, that my footsteps slip not.

6 I have called upon thee, for thou wilt hear me, O God: incline thine ear unto me, and hear my speech.

7 Shew thy marvellous lovingkindness, O thou that savest by thy right hand them which put their trust in thee from those that rise up against them.

8 Keep me as the apple of the eye, hide me under the shadow of thy wings,

9 From the wicked that oppress me, from my deadly enemies, who compass me about.

10 They are inclosed in their own fat: with their mouth they speak proudly.

11 They have now compassed us in our steps: they have set their eyes bowing down to the earth;

12 Like as a lion that is greedy of his prey, and as it were a young lion lurking in secret places.

13 Arise, O LORD, disappoint him, cast him down: deliver my soul from the wicked, which is thy sword:

14 From men which are thy hand, O LORD, from men of the world, which have their portion in this life, and whose belly thou fillest with thy hid treasure: they are full of children, and leave the rest of their substance to their babes.

15 As for me, I will behold thy face in righteousness: I shall be satisfied, when I awake, with thy likeness.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A plea for a just hearing from God, who has tried the speaker's heart by night and found it sincere. Kept by God's word from violent paths, he asks to be kept as the apple of the eye under the shadow of wings, while worldly men take their portion in this life, and he looks to behold God's face and wake satisfied with his likeness. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hear the right, Lord, attend to my cry, and give ear to a prayer not from feigned lips: sincerity is pleaded before the merits of the cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Let my sentence come from your presence, and let your eyes behold what is equal: David asks God, not men, to judge, because God's judgment is even-handed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God has proved his heart, visited him in the night, tried him and found nothing; he purposes that his mouth shall not transgress: night thoughts, the truest test, have not condemned him in this cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Concerning men's works, by the word of God's lips he has kept himself from the paths of the destroyer: Scripture, not custom, kept him from violent ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Hold up his goings in God's paths, that his footsteps slip not: past keeping becomes a prayer for continued upholding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He has called on God because God will hear him; incline your ear and hear his speech: confidence in God's hearing encourages prayer, and prayer expresses confidence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Show marvellous lovingkindness, O Saviour by your right hand of those who trust in you from those who rise against them: God's saving power is the refuge of the trusting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Keep me as the apple of the eye, hide me under the shadow of your wings: the tenderest, most guarded part of the body and the shelter of a bird's wings picture God's care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. From the wicked who oppress him, from deadly enemies who compass him about: the danger is encircling and mortal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They are enclosed in their own fat, and speak proudly with their mouth: prosperity has made them insensible and arrogant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They have compassed his steps, setting their eyes bowing down to the earth: they watch for a fall, crouching to spring. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Like a greedy lion and a young lion lurking in secret places: the enemy's patience makes him more, not less, dangerous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Arise, Lord, disappoint him, cast him down, and deliver my soul from the wicked, who is your sword: even the wicked are God's instrument, but God can stay his own sword. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. From men of the world, who have their portion in this life, whose belly is filled with hidden treasure, full of children, leaving substance to their babes: their whole portion is present, earthly, and inherited — and therefore short. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. As for me, I will behold your face in righteousness, and be satisfied when I awake with your likeness: against their earthly portion stands the vision of God, satisfying beyond waking life itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
