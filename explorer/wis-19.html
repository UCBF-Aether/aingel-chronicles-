The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Wisdom of Solomon 19 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 As for the ungodly, wrath came upon them without mercy unto the end: for he knew before what they would do;

2 How that having given them leave to depart, and sent them hastily away, they would repent and pursue them.

3 For whilst they were yet mourning and making lamentation at the graves of the dead, they added another foolish device, and pursued them as fugitives, whom they had intreated to be gone.

4 For the destiny, whereof they were worthy, drew them unto this end, and made them forget the things that had already happened, that they might fulfil the punishment which was wanting to their torments:

5 And that thy people might pass a wonderful way: but they might find a strange death.

6 For the whole creature in his proper kind was fashioned again anew, serving the peculiar commandments that were given unto them, that thy children might be kept without hurt:

7 As namely, a cloud shadowing the camp; and where water stood before, dry land appeared; and out of the Red sea a way without impediment; and out of the violent stream a green field:

8 Wherethrough all the people went that were defended with thy hand, seeing thy marvellous strange wonders.

9 For they went at large like horses, and leaped like lambs, praising thee, O Lord, who hadst delivered them.

10 For they were yet mindful of the things that were done while they sojourned in the strange land, how the ground brought forth flies instead of cattle, and how the river cast up a multitude of frogs instead of fishes.

11 But afterwards they saw a new generation of fowls, when, being led with their appetite, they asked delicate meats.

12 For quails came up unto them from the sea for their contentment.

13 And punishments came upon the sinners not without former signs by the force of thunders: for they suffered justly according to their own wickedness, insomuch as they used a more hard and hateful behaviour toward strangers.

14 For the Sodomites did not receive those, whom they knew not when they came: but these brought friends into bondage, that had well deserved of them.

15 And not only so, but peradventure some respect shall be had of those, because they used strangers not friendly:

16 But these very grievously afflicted them, whom they had received with feastings, and were already made partakers of the same laws with them.

17 Therefore even with blindness were these stricken, as those were at the doors of the righteous man: when, being compassed about with horrible great darkness, every one sought the passage of his own doors.

18 For the elements were changed in themselves by a kind of harmony, like as in a psaltery notes change the name of the tune, and yet are always sounds; which may well be perceived by the sight of the things that have been done.

19 For earthly things were turned into watery, and the things, that before swam in the water, now went upon the ground.

20 The fire had power in the water, forgetting his own virtue: and the water forgat his own quenching nature.

21 On the other side, the flames wasted not the flesh of the corruptible living things, though they walked therein; neither melted they the icy kind of heavenly meat that was of nature apt to melt.

22 For in all things, O Lord, thou didst magnify thy people, and glorify them, neither didst thou lightly regard them: but didst assist them in every time and place. Go back to the Bible

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Egypt's wrath pursued to the end, though the people had been sent away, and the Red Sea passage refashioned creation itself: cloud shadowing the camp, dry land where water stood, the people passing like horses and leaping like lambs. Quails, the blindness of the inhospitable, and elements changed by harmony close the book's argument: in all things God magnified his people and did not despise them. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. As for the ungodly, wrath came upon them without mercy to the end, for he knew before what they would do. Haydock's opening is merciless wrath to the end, grounded in foreknowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. How that, having given them leave to depart and sent them hastily away, they would repent and pursue them. Haydock notes foreknowledge of the pursuit: leave given, yet pursuit follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. While they were yet mourning and making lamentation at the graves of the dead, they added another foolish device and pursued as fugitives those whom they had entreated to be gone. Haydock observes the folly added to mourning: pursuing as fugitives those entreated to leave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. For the destiny whereof they were worthy drew them to this end, and made them forget the things that had already happened, that they might fulfil the punishment wanting to their torments. Haydock notes destiny draws them to forget past plagues, so punishment may be fulfilled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. While your people passed through a wonderful way, they themselves found a strange death. Haydock contrasts the wonderful way with a strange death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. The whole creature in his proper kind was fashioned again anew, serving your commandments, that your children might be kept without hurt. Haydock's point is that creation itself was refashioned to keep the children unhurt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. The cloud shadowed the camp, and where water stood before, dry land appeared, and out of the Red Sea a way without impediment, and out of the violent stream a green field. Haydock pictures the sea road: cloud shadow, dry land, green field from violent stream. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. Through this the whole nation went, guarded by your hand, seeing your marvellous strange wonders. Haydock notes the whole nation passed guarded, seeing wonders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. They went at large like horses, and leaped like lambs, praising you, O Lord, who had delivered them. Haydock's image is joy: horses at large, lambs leaping, praising the Deliverer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. They were mindful of the things done in their sojourn, how the ground brought forth flies instead of cattle's breed, and the river cast up frogs instead of fishes. Haydock notes memory of the sojourn: flies for cattle, frogs for fishes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. Afterward they saw a new generation of birds, when, being led by appetite, they asked delicate meats. Haydock observes the new generation of birds answering appetite's request. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. Quails came up to them from the sea for their contentment. Haydock notes quails from the sea contented them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. Punishments came upon the sinners not without former signs by the force of thunders, for they suffered justly according to their wickedness, inasmuch as they used a more hard and hateful behaviour toward strangers. Haydock notes thunders gave former signs, and punishment fits the hard behaviour toward strangers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. The Sodomites did not receive those whom they knew not when they came, but these brought friends into bondage, who had well deserved of them. Haydock contrasts Sodom's refusal of strangers with Egypt's bondage of benefactors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. Not only so, but peradventure some respect shall be had of those, because they used strangers not friendly. Haydock notes even Sodom's unfriendliness may find some respect beside Egypt's cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. But these very grievously afflicted them, whom they had received with feastings, and who were already made partakers of the same laws with them. Haydock's point is the aggravation: guests received with feasting and made sharers of laws were grievously afflicted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. Therefore even with blindness were these stricken, as those were at the doors of the righteous man, when, compassed with horrible great darkness, every one sought the passage of his own doors. Haydock notes blindness like Sodom's: each seeking his own door in darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. The elements were changed in themselves by a kind of harmony, like as in a psaltery notes change the name of the tune, and yet are always sounds, which may well be perceived by the sight of the things done. Haydock's image is musical: elements change like psaltery notes, keeping the sound. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. Earthly things were turned into watery, and the things that before swam in the water now went upon the ground. Haydock notes the exchange: land to water, water to land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. The fire had power in the water, forgetting his own virtue, and the water forgot its own quenching nature. Haydock observes fire forgetting its virtue in water, and water forgetting to extinguish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. On the other side, the flames wasted not the flesh of the corruptible living things, though they walked therein, neither melted they the icy kind of heavenly meat that was of nature apt to melt. Haydock notes flames sparing flesh and sparing melting manna. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. O Lord, you magnified your people in all things, and did not despise them, but stood by them in every time and place. Haydock's doxology is that God magnified his people and stood by them everywhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
