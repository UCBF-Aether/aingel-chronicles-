Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This long chapter sets out the order of the offering. On Saturday the bishop offers three loaves for a complete symbol of the Trinity, and on the first day of the week four loaves for a complete symbol of the Gospel. Because the ancient people erred, the veil in front of the door is closed while he offers within it with the presbyters, deacons, canonical widows, subdeacons, deaconesses, readers and those who have gifts. The bishop stands first in the middle, presbyters immediately behind him on either side, widows behind the presbyters on the left, deacons behind them on the right, then readers, subdeacons and deaconesses; the bishop places his hand on the loaves on the altar and the presbyters place their hands together with him. The loaf of a catechumen is not to be received, even if he has a believing son or wife and wishes to offer on their behalf, unless he is baptized. Before the bishop or presbyter offers, the people give the Peace to one another; then, a great silence being made, the deacon gives the admonition — lift up your hearts to heaven; let any man with wrath against his companion be reconciled, any man with a conscience without faith confess, any man with a thought foreign to the commandments depart, any man fallen into sin not hide himself, any man defiled or not firm give place, any man a stranger to the commandments of Jesus depart, any man despising the prophets separate himself — and the people are reminded that the Lord, the Father of Lights with the Son, and the visiting angels are onlookers. The bishop and people then exchange the dialogues — Our Lord be with you, And with thy spirit; Lift up your hearts, They are lifted up unto the Lord; Let us give thanks to the Lord; It is meet and right; Holy things in holy persons; In heaven and on earth without ceasing — followed by the long thanksgivings: to God the Holy One, Confirmer of souls, Giver of life, Treasure of incorruptibility and Father of the Only-begotten sent as Saviour and Proclaimer of His purpose; to the Power of the Father, Grace of the nations, true Wisdom, Medicine of souls and Hope of the persecuted, asking courage, might, reliance, wisdom, strength, unlapsing faith, unshaken hope, knowledge of His Spirit, meekness and uprightness; and to the Founder of the heights, King of the treasuries of light, Visitor of the heavenly Sion, who sent His Word, of His counsel and covenants, into a virgin womb — who, made flesh, born of the Holy Ghost and the Virgin, fulfilling His will and preparing a holy people, stretched forth His hands to suffering to loose from sufferings, corruption and death those who hoped in Him, and, betrayed to voluntary suffering to raise up those who had slipped, find the lost, give life to the dead, loose the pains of death, rend the bonds of the Devil, tread down Sheol, open the way of life and reveal the resurrection, taking bread gave it to His disciples saying, Take, eat, this is My Body which is broken for you for the forgiveness of sins, and gave the cup of wine mixed as a type of the Blood He shed for us. Remembering His death and resurrection, bread and cup are offered with thanks; the thanksgiving is offered to the Eternal Trinity, that this drink and food of His Holiness may be to the partakers not for condemnation, reproach or destruction, but for the medicine and support of the spirit, driving away every proud conception and every root of bitterness by His Name, sustaining those with gifts of revelations, healing, tongues and doctrine, caring for those who do His will, visiting widows, helping orphans, remembering those fallen asleep in the faith, granting an inheritance with the saints, feeding the people in uprightness and sanctifying all, so that all who partake may be made one with Him and filled with the Holy Ghost. The people say Amen; the deacon and bishop pray for concord in the Holy Spirit and healing of souls by the offering; the seal of thanksgiving follows — Let the Name of the Lord be blessed for ever; Blessed is He that hath come in the Name of the Lord; Send the grace of the Spirit upon us. If the bishop be polluted he is not to offer, but a presbyter is to offer, and he is not to receive of the mystery — not as though defiled, but because of the honour of the altar — until after fasting and bathing in pure water, and similarly a presbyter, a menstruous widow, a woman, a layman or any of the clergy. The priests receive first — bishops, presbyters, deacons, widows, readers, subdeacons — then those with gifts, the newly baptized and babes, then the people — old men, virgins and the rest — and the women, deaconesses first and then the rest. Each one receiving says Amen before partaking, then prays, Holy, Holy, Holy, Trinity ineffable, grant me to receive unto life this Body, and not unto condemnation, and to bring forth fruits pleasing to Him, calling Him Father and asking for His kingdom and will; taking the cup he says Amen twice, for a complete symbol of the Body and Blood. After all receive, thanks are given for the reception, and the bishop prays for understanding eyes that always look to Him, ears that hear Him only, a clean heart to comprehend His greatness, and thoughts that shall not swerve, for His kingdom is blessed in Father, Son and Holy Ghost, before the worlds, now and always, and for ever and ever.
