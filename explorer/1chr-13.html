The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And David consulted with the captains of thousands and hundreds, and with every leader.

2 And David said unto all the congregation of Israel, If it seem good unto you, and that it be of the LORD our God, let us send abroad unto our brethren every where, that are left in all the land of Israel, and with them also to the priests and Levites which are in their cities and suburbs, that they may gather themselves unto us:

3 And let us bring again the ark of our God to us: for we enquired not at it in the days of Saul.

4 And all the congregation said that they would do so: for the thing was right in the eyes of all the people.

5 So David gathered all Israel together, from Shihor of Egypt even unto the entering of Hemath, to bring the ark of God from Kirjathjearim.

6 And David went up, and all Israel, to Baalah, that is, to Kirjathjearim, which belonged to Judah, to bring up thence the ark of God the LORD, that dwelleth between the cherubims, whose name is called on it.

7 And they carried the ark of God in a new cart out of the house of Abinadab: and Uzza and Ahio drave the cart.

8 And David and all Israel played before God with all their might, and with singing, and with harps, and with psalteries, and with timbrels, and with cymbals, and with trumpets.

9 And when they came unto the threshingfloor of Chidon, Uzza put forth his hand to hold the ark; for the oxen stumbled.

10 And the anger of the LORD was kindled against Uzza, and he smote him, because he put his hand to the ark: and there he died before God.

11 And David was displeased, because the LORD had made a breach upon Uzza: wherefore that place is called Perezuzza to this day.

12 And David was afraid of God that day, saying, How shall I bring the ark of God home to me?

13 So David brought not the ark home to himself to the city of David, but carried it aside into the house of Obededom the Gittite.

14 And the ark of God remained with the family of Obededom in his house three months. And the LORD blessed the house of Obededom, and all that he had.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells David's first attempt to bring the ark to Jerusalem: it is fetched from Kirjath-jearim, Uzza dies when he touches it, and the ark is left for three months in the house of Obed-edom, which God blesses. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David consulted with the captains of thousands and hundreds, and with every leader. Henry notes that though David had authority to command this good work, he chose consultation to honour the great men, gain advice on manner and timing, and make it a national act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. David said to all Israel's assembly, if it seem good and it be of the Lord our God, let us send to our brethren left in Israel and to the priests and Levites in their cities, to gather unto us. Henry notes he calls the people brethren in humility, remembers them as a remnant after scattering wars, and insists priests and Levites be summoned, since the ark was their province. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Let us bring again the ark of our God to us, for we enquired not at it in the days of Saul. Henry notes David's first care on the throne was to honour God with his power and enjoy the sacred oracle, and he humbly shares the guilt of past neglect rather than blaming Saul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The thing was right in the eyes of all the people, and all the congregation said they would do so. Henry notes unanimous agreement: a prudently proposed good work may find readier concurrence than expected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. David gathered all Israel from Shihor of Egypt even to Hamath, to bring the ark from Kirjath-jearim. Henry notes it was worth travelling far, from south to north, to attend God's ark and grace the solemnity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. They went to Baalah, Kirjath-jearim of Judah, to bring up the ark of God, whose name is called by the name of the Lord that dwelleth between the cherubims. Henry frames this as the revival of a neglected ordinance and return of God's presence token, worthy of all joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They carried the ark on a new cart out of Abinadab's house, and Uzza and Ahio drove the cart. Henry warns even wise and good men err when ordinances are revived: David copied the Philistines' cart, but we must walk by rule, not by example varying from the rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. David and all Israel played before God with all their might, with singing, harps, psalteries, timbrels, cymbals, and trumpets. Henry reads this joy as fitting for religion shining out of obscurity, openly professed and countenanced by princes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. When they came to Chidon's threshingfloor, the oxen stumbled, and Uzza put forth his hand to hold the ark. Henry warns Uzza's sin teaches against presumption and irreverence about holy things: a good intention does not justify a bad action, nor does familiarity excuse contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The anger of the Lord was kindled against Uzza, and he smote him because he put his hand to the ark, and he died there before God. Henry compares Uzza to Nadab and Abihu: God will be sanctified in those who come near him, and the nearer they are, the more he resents presumption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. David was displeased because the Lord had made a breach upon Uzza, and he called the place Perez-uzza to this day. Henry cautions against quarrelling with God under rebuke: if God is angry with us, shall we dare be angry with him? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. David was afraid of God that day, saying, how shall I bring the ark of God home to me? Henry notes the damp on Israel's joy should teach rejoicing with trembling and serving the Lord with fear even amid gladness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. So David brought not the ark home to himself to the city of David, but carried it aside into the house of Obed-edom the Gittite. Henry warns the stop should not drive David from duty, only from sin: he should have gone on, and the breach might have been made up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The ark remained with Obed-edom's family three months, and the Lord blessed his house and all that he had. Henry urges welcoming God's ordinances into our houses: the ark is a guest nobody loses by, life to those who receive it in love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
