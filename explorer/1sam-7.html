The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the men of Kirjathjearim came, and fetched up the ark of the LORD, and brought it into the house of Abinadab in the hill, and sanctified Eleazar his son to keep the ark of the LORD.

2 And it came to pass, while the ark abode in Kirjathjearim, that the time was long; for it was twenty years: and all the house of Israel lamented after the LORD.

3 And Samuel spake unto all the house of Israel, saying, If ye do return unto the LORD with all your hearts, then put away the strange gods and Ashtaroth from among you, and prepare your hearts unto the LORD, and serve him only: and he will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines.

4 Then the children of Israel did put away Baalim and Ashtaroth, and served the LORD only.

5 And Samuel said, Gather all Israel to Mizpeh, and I will pray for you unto the LORD.

6 And they gathered together to Mizpeh, and drew water, and poured it out before the LORD, and fasted on that day, and said there, We have sinned against the LORD. And Samuel judged the children of Israel in Mizpeh.

7 And when the Philistines heard that the children of Israel were gathered together to Mizpeh, the lords of the Philistines went up against Israel. And when the children of Israel heard it, they were afraid of the Philistines.

8 And the children of Israel said to Samuel, Cease not to cry unto the LORD our God for us, that he will save us out of the hand of the Philistines.

9 And Samuel took a sucking lamb, and offered it for a burnt offering wholly unto the LORD: and Samuel cried unto the LORD for Israel; and the LORD heard him.

10 And as Samuel was offering up the burnt offering, the Philistines drew near to battle against Israel: but the LORD thundered with a great thunder on that day upon the Philistines, and discomfited them; and they were smitten before Israel.

11 And the men of Israel went out of Mizpeh, and pursued the Philistines, and smote them, until they came under Bethcar.

12 Then Samuel took a stone, and set it between Mizpeh and Shen, and called the name of it Ebenezer, saying, Hitherto hath the LORD helped us.

13 So the Philistines were subdued, and they came no more into the coast of Israel: and the hand of the LORD was against the Philistines all the days of Samuel.

14 And the cities which the Philistines had taken from Israel were restored to Israel, from Ekron even unto Gath; and the coasts thereof did Israel deliver out of the hands of the Philistines. And there was peace between Israel and the Amorites.

15 And Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life.

16 And he went from year to year in circuit to Bethel, and Gilgal, and Mizpeh, and judged Israel in all those places.

17 And his return was to Ramah; for there was his house; and there he judged Israel; and there he built an altar unto the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The men of Kirjathjearim fetch the ark into Abinadab's house on the hill, sanctifying Eleazar to keep it, and there it abides twenty years while all Israel laments after the LORD. Samuel calls for wholehearted return, putting away Baalim and Ashtaroth and serving the LORD only; Israel does so and gathers at Mizpeh, drawing water and pouring it out, fasting and confessing, We have sinned against the LORD. The Philistines attack the gathered, unarmed people, Samuel offers a sucking lamb wholly and cries to the LORD, and the LORD thunders with a great thunder and discomfits them; Samuel sets up a stone between Mizpeh and Shen and calls it Ebenezer, saying, Hitherto hath the LORD helped us. Cities from Ekron to Gath are restored, and Samuel judges Israel all his life on a yearly circuit of Bethel, Gilgal and Mizpeh, returning to Ramah, where he builds an altar. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The men of Kirjathjearim fetched up the ark and brought it into Abinadab's house on the hill, sanctifying his son Eleazar to keep it. God will find a resting-place for his ark, Henry notes; if some thrust it from them, others' hearts shall be inclined to receive it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. While the ark abode in Kirjathjearim the time was long, twenty years, and all the house of Israel lamented after the LORD. Twenty years passed before Israel cared for the want of the ark, and during this time Samuel laboured to revive true religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Samuel spoke to all Israel: if you return to the LORD with all your hearts, put away the strange gods and Ashtaroth, prepare your hearts, and serve him only, and he will deliver you from the Philistines. This was one of the most effectual revivals of religion ever to take place in Israel, Henry observes, told in very few but expressive words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Then the children of Israel put away Baalim and Ashtaroth and served the LORD only. The revival bore fruit in a clean break with idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Samuel said, Gather all Israel to Mizpeh, and I will pray for you unto the LORD. The people gathered for solemn repentance under their judge and intercessor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. They gathered at Mizpeh, drew water and poured it out before the LORD, fasted, and said, We have sinned against the LORD, and Samuel judged Israel there. Pouring out water signified their humiliation and sorrow for sin; they poured out their hearts in repentance, free and full in confession, fixed in resolution to cast away all wrongdoing, giving glory to God and taking shame to themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. When the Philistines heard Israel was gathered at Mizpeh, their lords went up against Israel, and Israel was afraid. When sinners begin to repent and reform, Henry warns, they must expect Satan to muster all his force against them to oppose and discourage them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Israel begged Samuel, Cease not to cry unto the LORD our God for us, that he will save us from the Philistines. What a comfort it is to believers, Henry notes, that our great Intercessor above never ceases and is never silent, always appearing in God's presence for us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Samuel took a sucking lamb and offered it wholly as a burnt offering, and cried to the LORD for Israel, and the LORD heard him. Samuel's sacrifice without his prayer would have been an empty shadow; God gave a gracious answer to the prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. As Samuel offered the burnt offering, the Philistines drew near, but the LORD thundered with a great thunder that day upon them and discomfited them, and they were smitten before Israel. The thunder answered the prayer at the very moment of sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The men of Israel went out of Mizpeh and pursued the Philistines, smiting them until Bethcar. The pursued became the pursuers once God had scattered the enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Samuel took a stone, set it between Mizpeh and Shen, and called it Ebenezer, saying, Hitherto hath the LORD helped us. Samuel erected a memorial of the victory to God's glory and Israel's encouragement, and through successive generations the church has had cause to set up Ebeneezers for renewed deliverances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. So the Philistines were subdued and came no more into Israel's coast, and the hand of the LORD was against them all the days of Samuel. Neither outward persecutions nor inward corruptions have prevailed against God's church, Henry notes, because hitherto the Lord has helped her, and he will help to the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The cities the Philistines had taken, from Ekron to Gath, were restored to Israel, with their coasts delivered, and there was peace between Israel and the Amorites. The revival's victory recovered lost ground on every side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life. His judgeship, born in the revival, lasted to his death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. He went year by year in circuit to Bethel, Gilgal, and Mizpeh, judging Israel in all those places. The circuit brought justice and worship to the people rather than waiting for them to come to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. His return was to Ramah, where his house was, and there he judged Israel and built an altar to the LORD. In this great revival, Henry observes, the ark was neither removed to Shiloh nor placed with the tabernacle elsewhere — a sign that the typical institutions' chief use was their meaning, and that repentance, faith, and love of God and man outweigh a lifeless service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
