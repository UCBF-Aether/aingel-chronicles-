The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word of the LORD came also unto me, saying,

2 Thou shalt not take thee a wife, neither shalt thou have sons or daughters in this place.

3 For thus saith the LORD concerning the sons and concerning the daughters that are born in this place, and concerning their mothers that bare them, and concerning their fathers that begat them in this land;

4 They shall die of grievous deaths; they shall not be lamented; neither shall they be buried; but they shall be as dung upon the face of the earth: and they shall be consumed by the sword, and by famine; and their carcases shall be meat for the fowls of heaven, and for the beasts of the earth.

5 For thus saith the LORD, Enter not into the house of mourning, neither go to lament nor bemoan them: for I have taken away my peace from this people, saith the LORD, even lovingkindness and mercies.

6 Both the great and the small shall die in this land: they shall not be buried, neither shall men lament for them, nor cut themselves, nor make themselves bald for them:

7 Neither shall men tear themselves for them in mourning, to comfort them for the dead; neither shall men give them the cup of consolation to drink for their father or for their mother.

8 Thou shalt not also go into the house of feasting, to sit with them to eat and to drink.

9 For thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Behold, I will cause to cease out of this place in your eyes, and in your days, the voice of mirth, and the voice of gladness, the voice of the bridegroom, and the voice of the bride.

10 And it shall come to pass, when thou shalt shew this people all these words, and they shall say unto thee, Wherefore hath the LORD pronounced all this great evil against us? or what is our iniquity? or what is our sin that we have committed against the LORD our God?

11 Then shalt thou say unto them, Because your fathers have forsaken me, saith the LORD, and have walked after other gods, and have served them, and have worshipped them, and have forsaken me, and have not kept my law;

12 And ye have done worse than your fathers; for, behold, ye walk every one after the imagination of his evil heart, that they may not hearken unto me:

13 Therefore will I cast you out of this land into a land that ye know not, neither ye nor your fathers; and there shall ye serve other gods day and night; where I will not shew you favour.

14 Therefore, behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that it shall no more be said, The LORD liveth, that brought up the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt;

15 But, The LORD liveth, that brought up the children of Israel from the land of the north, and from all the lands whither he had driven them: and I will bring them again into their land that I gave unto their fathers.

16 Behold, I will send for many fishers, saith the LORD, and they shall fish them; and after will I send for many hunters, and they shall hunt them from every mountain, and from every hill, and out of the holes of the rocks.

17 For mine eyes are upon all their ways: they are not hid from my face, neither is their iniquity hid from mine eyes.

18 And first I will recompense their iniquity and their sin double; because they have defiled my land, they have filled mine inheritance with the carcases of their detestable and abominable things.

19 O LORD, my strength, and my fortress, and my refuge in the day of affliction, the Gentiles shall come unto thee from the ends of the earth, and shall say, Surely our fathers have inherited lies, vanity, and things wherein there is no profit.

20 Shall a man make gods unto himself, and they are no gods?

21 Therefore, behold, I will this once cause them to know, I will cause them to know mine hand and my might; and they shall know that my name is The LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jeremiah's own life becomes a sign: he is told not to marry, not to mourn, and not to feast in this place, because death, lamentation and desolation are coming on it. The chapter then looks past exile to a return greater in memory than the exodus, and to nations learning to know the Lord's hand and might. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The word of the Lord came also unto me, saying. Henry's prophet must conduct himself as one who expected to see his country ruined very shortly: the word that comes to him commands a life that preaches ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Thou shalt not take thee a wife, neither shalt thou have sons or daughters in this place. Henry notes that in the prospect of sad times, he is to abstain from marriage, mourning for the dead, and pleasure: those who would convince others of the truths of God must make it appear by their self-denial that they believe it themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. For thus says the Lord concerning the sons and concerning the daughters that are born in this place, and concerning their mothers that bare them, and concerning their fathers that begat them in this land. Henry's abstinence is a sign: sons, daughters, mothers, and fathers born in this place are all under the coming ruin, and the prophet's childlessness preaches it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They shall die of grievous deaths; they shall not be lamented; neither shall they be buried; but they shall be as dung upon the face of the earth: and they shall be consumed by the sword, and by famine; and their carcases shall be meat for the fowls of heaven, and for the beasts of the earth. Henry's prospect is grievous deaths, unlamented, unburied, dung upon the earth, consumed by sword and famine, meat for fowls and beasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. For thus says the Lord: Enter not into the house of mourning, neither go to lament nor bemoan them: for I have taken away my peace from this people, says the Lord, even lovingkindness and mercies. Henry notes peace, inward and outward, family and public, is wholly the work of God, and from his loving-kindness and mercy: when he takes his peace from any people, distress must follow, and the prophet must not enter the house of mourning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Both the great and the small shall die in this land: they shall not be buried, neither shall men lament for them, nor cut themselves, nor make themselves bald for them. Henry's death is indiscriminate: great and small alike die unburied and unlamented, without cutting or baldness, because peace has been taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Neither shall men tear themselves for them in mourning, to comfort them for the dead; neither shall men give them the cup of consolation to drink for their father or for their mother. Henry notes there may be times when it is proper to avoid things otherwise our duty: tearing in mourning and the cup of consolation are withheld, for the dead are under judgement, not comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Thou shalt not also go into the house of feasting, to sit with them to eat and to drink. Henry notes we should always sit loose to the pleasures and concerns of this life: the prophet abstains from feasting as from mourning, loose to pleasure in the prospect of sad times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. For thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel: Behold, I will cause to cease out of this place in your eyes, and in your days, the voice of mirth, and the voice of gladness, the voice of the bridegroom, and the voice of the bride. Henry's cessation is total: mirth, gladness, bridegroom, and bride all cease out of this place, in their eyes and in their days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And it shall come to pass, when thou shalt shew this people all these words, and they shall say unto thee, Wherefore has the Lord pronounced all this great evil against us? Or what is our iniquity? Or what is our sin that we have committed against the Lord our God? Henry notes here seems to be the language of those who quarrel at the word of God, and instead of humbling and condemning themselves, justify themselves, as though God did them wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Then shalt thou say unto them: Because your fathers have forsaken me, says the Lord, and have walked after other gods, and have served them, and have worshipped them, and have forsaken me, and have not kept my law. Henry notes a plain and full answer is given: the fathers forsook God, walked after other gods, served and worshipped them, and kept not his law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And ye have done worse than your fathers; for, behold, ye walk every one after the imagination of his evil heart, that they may not hearken unto me. Henry notes they were more obstinate in sin than their fathers, walking every one after the devices of his heart, that they might not hearken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Therefore will I cast you out of this land into a land that ye know not, neither ye nor your fathers; and there shall ye serve other gods day and night; where I will not shew you favour. Henry warns that since they will not hearken, they shall be hurried away into a far country, a land they know not, to serve other gods day and night without favour; yet if they had God's favour, that would make even the land of their captivity pleasant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Therefore, behold, the days come, says the Lord, that it shall no more be said, The Lord liveth, that brought up the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt. Henry notes the restoration from the Babylonish captivity would be remembered in place of the deliverance from Egypt: the oath by Egypt's deliverance gives way to a greater deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. But, The Lord liveth, that brought up the children of Israel from the land of the north, and from all the lands whither he had driven them: and I will bring them again into their land that I gave unto their fathers. Henry notes the restoration typified spiritual redemption, and the future deliverance of the church from antichristian oppression: brought from the north and all driven lands, back to the land given to their fathers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Behold, I will send for many fishers, says the Lord, and they shall fish them; and after will I send for many hunters, and they shall hunt them from every mountain, and from every hill, and out of the holes of the rocks. Henry notes God will find out and raise up instruments of his wrath, that shall destroy the Jews, by fraud like fishers, by force like hunters: fished, then hunted from mountain, hill, and holes of rocks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. For mine eyes are upon all their ways: they are not hid from my face, neither is their iniquity hid from mine eyes. Henry warns none of the sins of sinners can be hidden from God, or shall be overlooked by him: eyes upon all ways, iniquity not hid from his eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And first I will recompense their iniquity and their sin double; because they have defiled my land, they have filled mine inheritance with the carcases of their detestable and abominable things. Henry's double recompense answers defilement: the land defiled and the inheritance filled with carcases of detestable things shall be recompensed double. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. O Lord, my strength, and my fortress, and my refuge in the day of affliction, the Gentiles shall come unto thee from the ends of the earth, and shall say, Surely our fathers have inherited lies, vanity, and things wherein there is no profit. Henry notes the prophet, rejoicing at the hope of mercy to come, addressed the Lord as his strength and refuge: the Gentiles shall come from the ends of the earth confessing their fathers inherited lies and vanity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Shall a man make gods unto himself, and they are no gods? Henry's question exposes the inherited lies: man-made gods are no gods, and the confession of the Gentiles condemns the idols Israel served. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Therefore, behold, I will this once cause them to know, I will cause them to know mine hand and my might; and they shall know that my name is The Lord. Henry closes that the nations have often known the power of Jehovah in his wrath, but they shall know him as the strength of his people, and their refuge in time of trouble: this once they shall know his hand, his might, and his name, The Lord, and the deliverance out of captivity shall be a figure of the great salvation to be wrought by the Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
