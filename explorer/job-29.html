The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 29 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover Job continued his parable, and said,

2 Oh that I were as in months past, as in the days when God preserved me;

3 When his candle shined upon my head, and when by his light I walked through darkness;

4 As I was in the days of my youth, when the secret of God was upon my tabernacle;

5 When the Almighty was yet with me, when my children were about me;

6 When I washed my steps with butter, and the rock poured me out rivers of oil;

7 When I went out to the gate through the city, when I prepared my seat in the street!

8 The young men saw me, and hid themselves: and the aged arose, and stood up.

9 The princes refrained talking, and laid their hand on their mouth.

10 The nobles held their peace, and their tongue cleaved to the roof of their mouth.

11 When the ear heard me, then it blessed me; and when the eye saw me, it gave witness to me:

12 Because I delivered the poor that cried, and the fatherless, and him that had none to help him.

13 The blessing of him that was ready to perish came upon me: and I caused the widow’s heart to sing for joy.

14 I put on righteousness, and it clothed me: my judgment was as a robe and a diadem.

15 I was eyes to the blind, and feet was I to the lame.

16 I was a father to the poor: and the cause which I knew not I searched out.

17 And I brake the jaws of the wicked, and plucked the spoil out of his teeth.

18 Then I said, I shall die in my nest, and I shall multiply my days as the sand.

19 My root was spread out by the waters, and the dew lay all night upon my branch.

20 My glory was fresh in me, and my bow was renewed in my hand.

21 Unto me men gave ear, and waited, and kept silence at my counsel.

22 After my words they spake not again; and my speech dropped upon them.

23 And they waited for me as for the rain; and they opened their mouth wide as for the latter rain.

24 If I laughed on them, they believed it not; and the light of my countenance they cast not down.

25 I chose out their way, and sat chief, and dwelt as a king in the army, as one that comforteth the mourners.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Job looks back to months past, when God preserved him, his lamp shone on his head, and his children were about him. Then, he says, he washed his steps with butter, sat as chief at the gate where young men hid and princes refrained from talking, and delivered the poor that cried, the fatherless, and him that had none to help. The blessing of him that was ready to perish came upon him, and he was eyes to the blind and feet to the lame. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Job continues his parable, pausing for a reply that does not come. JFB notes that with no reply made, he proceeds to illustrate God's mysterious dealings by his own case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Oh that Job were as in months past, as in the days when God preserved him! JFB explains preserved as from calamity: the past months are remembered as preservation itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. When God's candle shined upon Job's head, and by his light he walked through darkness. JFB explains candle as God's favour shining, and darkness as dangers passed secure by his safeguard, perhaps like lights carried before caravans on nightly desert travels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. As Job was in the days of his youth, when the secret of God was upon his tabernacle. JFB explains youth literally as autumn, the ripe fruits of prosperity, and secret as the intimate friendship of God resting on his tent, a divan for deliberation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. When the Almighty was yet with Job, and his children were about him. The presence of God and the circle of children belong together in the remembered days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. When Job washed his steps with butter, and the rock poured him out rivers of oil. JFB explains butter as cream or thick milk, flowing abundantly wherever he turned, with washing steps as walking near milk and oil, olives among rocks yielding the best oil, used for food, light, anointing, and medicine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. When Job went out to the gate through the city, and prepared his seat in the street. JFB explains going out from his country house up to the elevated city, preparing his judicial seat in the marketplace, the place of judgment at the gate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The young men saw Job and hid themselves, and the aged arose and stood up. JFB explains hid as stepped backwards reverentially, with the aged, already seated, arising and remaining standing until Job seated himself, in Oriental manners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The princes refrained talking, and laid their hand on their mouth. JFB notes they stopped in the middle of their speech at Job's coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The nobles held their peace, and their tongue cleaved to the roof of their mouth. JFB explains the voice hushed, with tongues cleaving as emirs awed into silence by his presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. When the ear heard Job, it blessed him, and when the eye saw him, it gave witness to him. JFB explains blessed as extolling his virtues, whoever heard of him praising him, and gave witness as to honourable character, with eye as face to face against ear as report. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Because Job delivered the poor that cried, and the fatherless, and him that had none to help him. JFB gives the grounds of his praise: helping the afflicted who cried to him as judge and as one with means of charity, including the fatherless with none to help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The blessing of him that was ready to perish came upon Job, and he caused the widow's heart to sing for joy. JFB notes this is the opposite of sending widows away empty: the perishing blessed him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Job put on righteousness, and it clothed him; his judgment was as a robe and a diadem. JFB explains judgment as justice, and diadem as turban, with robe and turban as the prominent dress of an Oriental grandee: righteousness especially characterized him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Job was eyes to the blind, and feet to the lame. JFB explains it literally of the blind and lame, and figuratively of the spiritual support the enlightened give to the less enlightened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Job was a father to the poor, and the cause which he knew not he searched out. JFB notes this is the opposite of breaking the fatherless' arms, as Eliphaz charged, and the unknown cause as the stranger's cause, searched out with judicial conscientiousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Job broke the jaws of the wicked, and plucked the spoil out of his teeth. JFB gives the wild beast combat image: so compassionate to the oppressed, so terrible to the oppressor, breaking his power and tearing away his spoil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Then Job said he would die in his nest, and multiply his days as the sand. JFB explains in his heart, with nest as secure dwelling, expecting to die with his family rather than before them, with sand as multitude or, in the phoenix reading, the long cycle of the phoenix bird. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Job's root was spread out by the waters, and the dew lay all night upon his branch. JFB explains it literally as opened to the waters, opposed to withering, a picture of vigorous health. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Job's glory was fresh in him, and his bow was renewed in his hand. JFB notes renown, like bodily health, continually fresh, with bow as metaphor for strength gaining renown, ever renewed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Unto Job men gave ear, and waited, and kept silence at his counsel. JFB notes Job reverts with peculiar pleasure to his former dignity in assemblies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. After Job's words they spoke not again, and his speech dropped upon them. JFB explains not again as not contradicting him, with speech dropping as genial rain on soil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. And they waited for Job as for the rain, and opened their mouth wide as for the latter rain. JFB continues the rain image: they waited for salutary counsel as dry soil waits for refreshing rain, panting for it, with early rain at sowing and latter rain in March bringing harvest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. If Job laughed on them, they believed it not, and the light of his countenance they cast not down. JFB explains that when he relaxed his wonted gravity and smiled, they could hardly credit it, yet did not cast aside reverence for his gravity, or smiled kindly on the distrustful, whose despondency could not cast down his serene countenance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Job chose out their way, sat chief, and dwelt as a king in the army, as one that comforts the mourners. JFB explains choosing their way as willingly going up to their assembly, dwelling as a king supreme amid his army, and comforting mourners as unconsciously foreshadowing Christ, whose afflictions fitted him for that office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
