The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Galatians 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage.

2 Behold, I Paul say unto you, that if ye be circumcised, Christ shall profit you nothing.

3 For I testify again to every man that is circumcised, that he is a debtor to do the whole law.

4 Christ is become of no effect unto you, whosoever of you are justified by the law; ye are fallen from grace.

5 For we through the Spirit wait for the hope of righteousness by faith.

6 For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision; but faith which worketh by love.

7 Ye did run well; who did hinder you that ye should not obey the truth?

8 This persuasion cometh not of him that calleth you.

9 A little leaven leaveneth the whole lump.

10 I have confidence in you through the Lord, that ye will be none otherwise minded: but he that troubleth you shall bear his judgment, whosoever he be.

11 And I, brethren, if I yet preach circumcision, why do I yet suffer persecution? then is the offence of the cross ceased.

12 I would they were even cut off which trouble you.

13 For, brethren, ye have been called unto liberty; only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another.

14 For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this; Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.

15 But if ye bite and devour one another, take heed that ye be not consumed one of another.

16 This I say then, Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh.

17 For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary the one to the other: so that ye cannot do the things that ye would.

18 But if ye be led of the Spirit, ye are not under the law.

19 Now the works of the flesh are manifest, which are these; Adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness,

20 Idolatry, witchcraft, hatred, variance, emulations, wrath, strife, seditions, heresies,

21 Envyings, murders, drunkenness, revellings, and such like: of the which I tell you before, as I have also told you in time past, that they which do such things shall not inherit the kingdom of God.

22 But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith,

23 Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.

24 And they that are Christ’s have crucified the flesh with the affections and lusts.

25 If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit.

26 Let us not be desirous of vain glory, provoking one another, envying one another.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Stand fast in the freedom Christ gives, Paul urges, and do not take up circumcision as a requirement, which would cut readers off from Christ. Freedom is not licence for the flesh: walking in the Spirit produces love, joy, peace and the rest of the Spirit's fruit, against the listed works of the flesh. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Stand fast therefore in the liberty with which Christ has made them free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage, since Christ will not be the Savior of any who will not own and rely on him as their only Savior. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Behold, Paul says, if they are circumcised as a means of justification, Christ shall profit them nothing, since dependence on ceremonies displaces reliance on Christ alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Paul testifies again to every man circumcised that he is a debtor to do the whole law, having taken up the whole covenant of works, not part of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Christ has become of no effect to those justified by the law, for they are fallen from grace, grace being no grace unless freely given every way, and it cannot stand with man's merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. For through the Spirit believers wait for the hope of righteousness by faith, taught by the Spirit to expect eternal life as God's gift by faith in Christ, not for their own works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision nor uncircumcision avails anything, but faith which works by love, a working grace that loves God and brethren, without which all else is worthless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They did run well in the Christian race, but who hindered them from obeying the truth, since profession alone is not enough and they must run and hold on to obtain the prize. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. This persuasion comes not from him who calls them, the opinion of mixing law-works with faith being owing to none but Satan, as Paul leaves them to judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. A little leaven leavens the whole lump, so the first beginnings of such leaven must be opposed before destructive error spreads through a church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Paul has confidence in them through the Lord that they will be none otherwise minded, but he who troubles them shall bear his judgment, distinguishing between leaders and the led in reproof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And Paul asks, if he still preached circumcision, why does he still suffer persecution, since then the offense of the cross would have ceased, the Jews being offended precisely because Christ is preached as the only salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. He wishes those who trouble them were even cut off, a sharp word against persistent disturbers of Christ's church, who must bear their judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Brethren have been called unto liberty, only not to use liberty as an occasion to the flesh, but by love to serve one another, since the gospel is a doctrine according to godliness and lays the strongest obligation to avoid sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. For all the law is fulfilled in one word, thou shalt love thy neighbor as thyself, love being the summary of the law's duty to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. But if they bite and devour one another, let them take heed they are not consumed by one another, since quarreling Christians invite the Spirit of love to depart and the evil spirit to prevail. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Walk in the Spirit, and they shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh, acting under the Spirit's guidance and power so corrupt nature does not have dominion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. For the flesh lusts against the Spirit and the Spirit against the flesh, contrary to one another, so believers cannot simply do whatever they would, being engaged in a real conflict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. But if they are led by the Spirit, they are not under the law as a covenant of works nor exposed to its awful curse, their hatred of sin and desires after holiness showing their part in gospel salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Now the works of the flesh are manifest: adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness, sins many and plain, which shut men out of heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Idolatry, witchcraft, hatred, variance, emulations, wrath, strife, seditions, heresies continue the list, chiefly sins hurtful to men and tending to set them against one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Envyings, murders, drunkenness, revelings, and such like complete it, and Paul warns beforehand, as before, that those who practice such things shall not inherit God's kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, fruits tending to make Christians agreeable to one another as well as happy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Meekness and temperance complete the fruit, and against such there is no law, since the law condemns the works of the flesh, not the Spirit's fruit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Those who are Christ's have crucified the flesh with its affections and lusts, sin no longer reigning in their mortal bodies so that they obey it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. If they live in the Spirit, let them also walk in the Spirit, conversation always answering to the principle that guides them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Let them not be desirous of vain glory, provoking or envying one another, but seek to bring forth more abundantly the good fruits which are through Jesus Christ to God's praise and glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
