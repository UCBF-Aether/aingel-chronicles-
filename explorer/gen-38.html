The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 38 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass at that time, that Judah went down from his brethren, and turned in to a certain Adullamite, whose name was Hirah.

2 And Judah saw there a daughter of a certain Canaanite, whose name was Shuah; and he took her, and went in unto her.

3 And she conceived, and bare a son; and he called his name Er.

4 And she conceived again, and bare a son; and she called his name Onan.

5 And she yet again conceived, and bare a son; and called his name Shelah: and he was at Chezib, when she bare him.

6 And Judah took a wife for Er his firstborn, whose name was Tamar.

7 And Er, Judah’s firstborn, was wicked in the sight of the LORD; and the LORD slew him.

8 And Judah said unto Onan, Go in unto thy brother’s wife, and marry her, and raise up seed to thy brother.

9 And Onan knew that the seed should not be his; and it came to pass, when he went in unto his brother’s wife, that he spilled it on the ground, lest that he should give seed to his brother.

10 And the thing which he did displeased the LORD: wherefore he slew him also.

11 Then said Judah to Tamar his daughter in law, Remain a widow at thy father’s house, till Shelah my son be grown: for he said, Lest peradventure he die also, as his brethren did. And Tamar went and dwelt in her father’s house.

12 And in process of time the daughter of Shuah Judah’s wife died; and Judah was comforted, and went up unto his sheepshearers to Timnath, he and his friend Hirah the Adullamite.

13 And it was told Tamar, saying, Behold thy father in law goeth up to Timnath to shear his sheep.

14 And she put her widow’s garments off from her, and covered her with a vail, and wrapped herself, and sat in an open place, which is by the way to Timnath; for she saw that Shelah was grown, and she was not given unto him to wife.

15 When Judah saw her, he thought her to be an harlot; because she had covered her face.

16 And he turned unto her by the way, and said, Go to, I pray thee, let me come in unto thee; (for he knew not that she was his daughter in law.) And she said, What wilt thou give me, that thou mayest come in unto me?

17 And he said, I will send thee a kid from the flock. And she said, Wilt thou give me a pledge, till thou send it?

18 And he said, What pledge shall I give thee? And she said, Thy signet, and thy bracelets, and thy staff that is in thine hand. And he gave it her, and came in unto her, and she conceived by him.

19 And she arose, and went away, and laid by her vail from her, and put on the garments of her widowhood.

20 And Judah sent the kid by the hand of his friend the Adullamite, to receive his pledge from the woman’s hand: but he found her not.

21 Then he asked the men of that place, saying, Where is the harlot, that was openly by the way side? And they said, There was no harlot in this place.

22 And he returned to Judah, and said, I cannot find her; and also the men of the place said, that there was no harlot in this place.

23 And Judah said, Let her take it to her, lest we be shamed: behold, I sent this kid, and thou hast not found her.

24 And it came to pass about three months after, that it was told Judah, saying, Tamar thy daughter in law hath played the harlot; and also, behold, she is with child by whoredom. And Judah said, Bring her forth, and let her be burnt.

25 When she was brought forth, she sent to her father in law, saying, By the man, whose these are, am I with child: and she said, Discern, I pray thee, whose are these, the signet, and bracelets, and staff.

26 And Judah acknowledged them, and said, She hath been more righteous than I; because that I gave her not to Shelah my son. And he knew her again no more.

27 And it came to pass in the time of her travail, that, behold, twins were in her womb.

28 And it came to pass, when she travailed, that the one put out his hand: and the midwife took and bound upon his hand a scarlet thread, saying, This came out first.

29 And it came to pass, as he drew back his hand, that, behold, his brother came out: and she said, How hast thou broken forth? this breach be upon thee: therefore his name was called Pharez.

30 And afterward came out his brother, that had the scarlet thread upon his hand: and his name was called Zarah.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Judah leaves his brothers, marries, and loses two sons, Er and Onan, to judgment; his widowed daughter-in-law Tamar, denied his third son, disguises herself, and Judah unknowingly fathers her twins. Confronted with his own seal, cord and staff, Judah admits she has been more righteous than he, and Perez and Zerah are born. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. At that time Judah went down from his brothers and turned in to Hirah the Adullamite. JFB notes the phrase marks an interval near about that time, not a precise date, and Henry frames the whole chapter as proof that God's choice is of grace, not merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Judah saw a Canaanite's daughter, Shuah's daughter, took her, and went in to her. JFB compares Judah to Esau here: casting off religious restraint, he married into a Canaanite family, and the family that sprang from it was infamous for wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. She conceived and bore a son, and he called his name Er. The firstborn's birth begins the line that will carry Judah's name forward, for better and worse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. She conceived again and bore a son, and she called his name Onan. The second son enters a household already marked by an unsuitable marriage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. She conceived yet again and bore Shelah, at Chezib when she bore him. The third son, the youngest, will later become the centre of the dispute with Tamar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Judah took a wife for Er his firstborn, whose name was Tamar. The marriage joins Tamar to Judah's house before Er's character is exposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Er, Judah's firstborn, was wicked in the sight of the Lord, and the Lord slew him. Henry reads this as an awful example of God's utter displeasure at sin, proclaimed in punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Judah told Onan to go in to his brother's wife, marry her, and raise up seed to his brother. JFB notes this was the first instance of the custom later written in Moses' law: a brother marrying a childless widow so the child would count as the dead brother's heir. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Onan knew the seed would not be his, so when he went in to his brother's wife he spilled it on the ground, lest he give seed to his brother. His act was a deliberate refusal of the duty Judah had laid on him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. What Onan did displeased the Lord, and he slew him also. The second death, like the first, is presented as divine judgment, not accident. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Judah told Tamar to remain a widow at her father's house until Shelah grew up, fearing Shelah might die as his brothers had. The instruction kept Tamar waiting on a promise Judah did not intend to keep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. In process of time Shuah's daughter, Judah's wife, died, and Judah was comforted and went up to Timnath to his sheep-shearers with his friend Hirah. JFB notes sheep-shearing, toward the end of March, was a festive season when masters entertained friends and servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Tamar was told that her father-in-law was going up to Timnath to shear his sheep. The news reached her because Judah's movements were public, and it gave her the occasion for her plan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Tamar put off her widow's garments, covered herself with a veil, wrapped herself, and sat in an open place by the way to Timnath, because Shelah was grown and she had not been given to him. Her disguise was a protest against Judah's broken promise, carried out by deception. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. When Judah saw her, he thought her a harlot, because she had covered her face. The veil that hid her identity also created the misunderstanding on which the rest of the chapter turns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Judah turned to her by the way and asked to come in to her, not knowing she was his daughter-in-law, and she asked what he would give her. His ignorance does not excuse him in Henry's reading: the chapter is a record of profligate conduct, not of innocence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Judah promised a kid from the flock, and Tamar asked for a pledge until he sent it. The bargain turned a passing encounter into a transaction with proof attached. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Asked what pledge, Tamar named his signet, bracelets, and staff, and he gave them to her, and she conceived by him. JFB notes the signet alone was worth more than a kid, and the bracelets were likely a ribbon or lace attaching the seal to the staff, making the pledge unmistakably his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Tamar arose, went away, laid aside her veil, and put on her widow's garments again. The return to widow's dress hid the encounter and restored her outward standing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Judah sent the kid by his friend the Adullamite to recover the pledge, but he did not find the woman. The honest attempt to pay the bargain failed because the woman had vanished with the disguise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Hirah asked the men of the place where the harlot by the wayside was, and they said there was no harlot in that place. The local denial shows Tamar was not known there in that character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Hirah returned to Judah saying he could not find her, and the men of the place said there was no harlot there. The failed search left Judah holding an unpaid bargain and an unredeemed pledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Judah said, let her take the pledge, lest we be shamed, since he had sent the kid and Hirah had not found her. Shame, not justice, ended his effort to recover his signet and staff. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. About three months later Judah was told Tamar had played the harlot and was with child, and he ordered her brought forth to be burnt. JFB notes fathers in patriarchal times held power of life and death over their households, and burning was an ancient punishment for adultery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. As she was brought forth, Tamar sent to her father-in-law, saying she was with child by the man whose these were, and asked him to discern whose signet, bracelets, and staff they were. The pledge, kept for this moment, turned accuser into accused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Judah acknowledged the pledges and said Tamar had been more righteous than he, because he had not given her to Shelah, and he knew her again no more. The confession admits his broken promise as the root of the affair. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. In the time of her travail, behold, twins were in her womb. The twins continue Judah's line, which Henry notes with wonder, since Christ himself would spring from this Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. As she travailed, one put out his hand, and the midwife bound a scarlet thread on it, saying this came out first. The thread marked the firstborn claim before the birth was finished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. As he drew back his hand, his brother came out, and she cried, how hast thou broken forth, so his name was called Pharez, meaning breach. The unexpected order gave the younger twin the firstborn place in the story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Afterward came his brother who had the scarlet thread on his hand, and his name was called Zarah. The thread remained as the sign of the hand that had first appeared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
