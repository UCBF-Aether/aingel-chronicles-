The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ruth 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Naomi her mother in law said unto her, My daughter, shall I not seek rest for thee, that it may be well with thee?

2 And now is not Boaz of our kindred, with whose maidens thou wast? Behold, he winnoweth barley to night in the threshingfloor.

3 Wash thyself therefore, and anoint thee, and put thy raiment upon thee, and get thee down to the floor: but make not thyself known unto the man, until he shall have done eating and drinking.

4 And it shall be, when he lieth down, that thou shalt mark the place where he shall lie, and thou shalt go in, and uncover his feet, and lay thee down; and he will tell thee what thou shalt do.

5 And she said unto her, All that thou sayest unto me I will do.

6 And she went down unto the floor, and did according to all that her mother in law bade her.

7 And when Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was merry, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of corn: and she came softly, and uncovered his feet, and laid her down.

8 And it came to pass at midnight, that the man was afraid, and turned himself: and, behold, a woman lay at his feet.

9 And he said, Who art thou? And she answered, I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman.

10 And he said, Blessed be thou of the LORD, my daughter: for thou hast shewed more kindness in the latter end than at the beginning, inasmuch as thou followedst not young men, whether poor or rich.

11 And now, my daughter, fear not; I will do to thee all that thou requirest: for all the city of my people doth know that thou art a virtuous woman.

12 And now it is true that I am thy near kinsman: howbeit there is a kinsman nearer than I.

13 Tarry this night, and it shall be in the morning, that if he will perform unto thee the part of a kinsman, well; let him do the kinsman’s part: but if he will not do the part of a kinsman to thee, then will I do the part of a kinsman to thee, as the LORD liveth: lie down until the morning.

14 And she lay at his feet until the morning: and she rose up before one could know another. And he said, Let it not be known that a woman came into the floor.

15 Also he said, Bring the vail that thou hast upon thee, and hold it. And when she held it, he measured six measures of barley, and laid it on her: and she went into the city.

16 And when she came to her mother in law, she said, Who art thou, my daughter? And she told her all that the man had done to her.

17 And she said, These six measures of barley gave he me; for he said to me, Go not empty unto thy mother in law.

18 Then said she, Sit still, my daughter, until thou know how the matter will fall: for the man will not be in rest, until he have finished the thing this day.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Naomi, seeking rest for Ruth, sends her to the threshingfloor where Boaz winnows barley that night: wash, anoint, put on raiment, mark where he lies, uncover his feet and lie down, and he will tell her what to do. At midnight Boaz wakes afraid, learns who she is, and hears her claim, spread thy skirt over thine handmaid, for thou art a near kinsman. Boaz blesses her later kindness, promises to do all she requires, but honestly conditions it: there is a kinsman nearer than he, and if that kinsman will not redeem her, he will. She lies at his feet until morning, rises before one could know another, receives six measures of barley for Naomi, and is told to sit still, for the man will not rest until he has finished the thing that day. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Naomi said she would seek rest for Ruth, that it might be well with her. Henry notes the married state should be a rest that fixes the affections for life, and should be entered with seriousness and prayer for God's direction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Naomi reminded Ruth that Boaz, with whose maidens she had been, was their kindred, and was winnowing barley that night in the threshingfloor. Henry frames Naomi's advice as a parent's careful counsel in an important concern, seeking what is best for Ruth's soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Naomi directed Ruth to wash, anoint herself, put on her raiment, and go down to the floor, not making herself known until Boaz had eaten and drunk. Henry stresses this course was according to the laws and usages of Israel, not to be judged by modern rules. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Ruth was to mark where Boaz lay down, uncover his feet, and lie down, and he would tell her what to do. Henry notes that law and custom gave Ruth, now proselyted to the true religion, a legal claim upon Boaz, which widows customarily asserted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Ruth answered, "All that thou sayest unto me I will do." Henry observes that if there had been any evil in the proposal, Ruth, a woman of too much virtue and sense, would not have listened to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Ruth went down to the floor and did all her mother-in-law had bidden her. Henry's outline treats this obedience as Ruth doing all that was fit for her to do before patiently waiting the event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. After eating, drinking, and lying down at the end of the heap of corn, Boaz was approached as Ruth came softly and uncovered his feet and lay down. Henry notes that what would be improper in one age or nation is not always so in another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. At midnight Boaz was afraid and turned himself, and behold, a woman lay at his feet. Henry praises Boaz's conduct throughout: he did not suspect Ruth of ill intentions nor take advantage of her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Boaz asked who she was, and Ruth answered, "I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman." Henry notes Boaz, as a judge of Israel, would tell Ruth what she should do and whether he had the right of redemption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Boaz blessed Ruth, saying her latter kindness exceeded the former, in not following young men, poor or rich. Henry observes Boaz did not disdain her as a poor, destitute stranger, but spoke honourably of her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Boaz told Ruth not to fear, for he would do all she required, since all the city knew she was a virtuous woman. Henry calls Boaz's conduct worthy of the highest praise, honouring Ruth's virtue rather than suspecting her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Boaz confirmed it was true that he was a near kinsman, but there was a kinsman nearer than he. Henry notes Boaz made his promise conditional, since the right of redemption belonged first to that nearer kinsman. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Boaz told Ruth to tarry that night, and in the morning, if the nearer kinsman would redeem her, well, but if not, he would do the kinsman's part, swearing by the LORD. Henry stresses Boaz's honest, conditional promise, made without taking advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Ruth lay at his feet until morning, then rose before one could know another, because Boaz said it should not be known that a woman came into the floor. Henry notes Boaz sent her away as soon as morning arrived, protecting her reputation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Boaz told Ruth to bring her veil, hold it, and he measured six measures of barley into it and laid it on her. Henry presents this as Boaz's present to her mother-in-law, given with the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. When Ruth came to her mother-in-law, Naomi asked who she was, and Ruth told her all the man had done for her. Henry's lesson is that having done all fit for her to do, Ruth must now patiently wait the event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Ruth reported Boaz's gift of six measures of barley, with his words, "Go not empty unto thy mother in law." Henry notes Boaz, having undertaken the matter, would be sure to manage it well. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Naomi told Ruth to sit still until she knew how the matter would fall, for the man would not rest until he had finished the thing that day. Henry draws the believer's lesson: our strength is to sit still, casting our care on God who has promised to care for us, and Christ himself is our near Kinsman with the right to redeem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
