The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now Peter and John went up together into the temple at the hour of prayer, being the ninth hour.

2 And a certain man lame from his mother’s womb was carried, whom they laid daily at the gate of the temple which is called Beautiful, to ask alms of them that entered into the temple;

3 Who seeing Peter and John about to go into the temple asked an alms.

4 And Peter, fastening his eyes upon him with John, said, Look on us.

5 And he gave heed unto them, expecting to receive something of them.

6 Then Peter said, Silver and gold have I none; but such as I have give I thee: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk.

7 And he took him by the right hand, and lifted him up: and immediately his feet and ankle bones received strength.

8 And he leaping up stood, and walked, and entered with them into the temple, walking, and leaping, and praising God.

9 And all the people saw him walking and praising God:

10 And they knew that it was he which sat for alms at the Beautiful gate of the temple: and they were filled with wonder and amazement at that which had happened unto him.

11 And as the lame man which was healed held Peter and John, all the people ran together unto them in the porch that is called Solomon’s, greatly wondering.

12 And when Peter saw it, he answered unto the people, Ye men of Israel, why marvel ye at this? or why look ye so earnestly on us, as though by our own power or holiness we had made this man to walk?

13 The God of Abraham, and of Isaac, and of Jacob, the God of our fathers, hath glorified his Son Jesus; whom ye delivered up, and denied him in the presence of Pilate, when he was determined to let him go.

14 But ye denied the Holy One and the Just, and desired a murderer to be granted unto you;

15 And killed the Prince of life, whom God hath raised from the dead; whereof we are witnesses.

16 And his name through faith in his name hath made this man strong, whom ye see and know: yea, the faith which is by him hath given him this perfect soundness in the presence of you all.

17 And now, brethren, I wot that through ignorance ye did it, as did also your rulers.

18 But those things, which God before had shewed by the mouth of all his prophets, that Christ should suffer, he hath so fulfilled.

19 Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord.

20 And he shall send Jesus Christ, which before was preached unto you:

21 Whom the heaven must receive until the times of restitution of all things, which God hath spoken by the mouth of all his holy prophets since the world began.

22 For Moses truly said unto the fathers, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear in all things whatsoever he shall say unto you.

23 And it shall come to pass, that every soul, which will not hear that prophet, shall be destroyed from among the people.

24 Yea, and all the prophets from Samuel and those that follow after, as many as have spoken, have likewise foretold of these days.

25 Ye are the children of the prophets, and of the covenant which God made with our fathers, saying unto Abraham, And in thy seed shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed.

26 Unto you first God, having raised up his Son Jesus, sent him to bless you, in turning away every one of you from his iniquities.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Peter and John heal a man lame from birth at the temple gate, and Peter preaches to the crowd that it was by Jesus' name, not their own power, that the man stands whole. He calls his hearers to repent, points to Moses' promise of a prophet like himself, and to the covenant with Abraham, in which all peoples would be blessed. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Peter and John went up together into the temple at the hour of prayer, the ninth hour. Henry notes that the apostles and first believers attended the temple worship at the hours of prayer, keeping the appointed rhythms of devotion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. A man lame from his mother's womb was carried daily to the temple gate called Beautiful to ask alms. Henry identifies him as a cripple from his birth, above forty years old, on whom Peter and John were led by a divine direction to work a miracle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Peter said, Silver and gold have I none, but in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk. Henry draws the application: to heal men's souls to good purpose we must go forth in the name and power of Jesus Christ, calling helpless sinners to arise and walk in holiness by faith in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Peter took him by the right hand and lifted him up, and immediately his feet and ankle bones received strength. Henry's reflection on the cure is that the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth can make whole all the crippled faculties of our fallen nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Leaping up, the man stood and walked, and entered with them into the temple, walking and leaping and praising God. Henry pictures the holy joy of it: with what rapture shall we tread the holy courts when God the Spirit causes us to enter therein by his strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Peter answered the wondering people, Why marvel ye at this, or why look ye so earnestly on us, as though by our own power or holiness we had made this man to walk? Henry marks the difference from Christ's manner: the Lord spoke as having almighty power, but the apostles referred all to their Lord and refused honour except as his undeserving instruments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob hath glorified his Son Jesus, whom ye delivered up and denied before Pilate. Henry stresses the apostles' humility again: every crown must be cast at the feet of Christ, and useful men must be very humble, giving glory to his name, not their own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Peter showed the Jews the greatness of their crime in denying the Holy One and the Just and desiring a murderer. Henry adds the pastoral guard: the apostle would not anger them or drive them to despair, and though rejection of Christ is done through ignorance, ignorance can in no case be an excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And now, brethren, I wot that through ignorance ye did it, as did also your rulers. Henry allows the ignorance but removes the excuse: those who reject, refuse, or deny Christ do it through ignorance, but this can in no case be an excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord. Henry charges the absolute necessity of repentance upon every conscience that desires sins blotted out and the refreshment that only a sense of Christ's pardoning love can afford. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And he shall send Jesus Christ, which before was preached unto you. Henry places this sending within the times of refreshing promised to the penitent: to the converted and believing, refreshment comes from the presence of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Whom the heaven must receive until the times of restitution of all things. Henry notes it was not needful for the Spirit to make known the times and seasons of these dispensations, which are still left obscure, and that in this state of trial the glorified Redeemer is out of sight because we must live by faith in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Moses said a prophet shall the Lord raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me, and him shall ye hear in all things. Henry calls this a powerful warning to the Jews in the very words of Moses, their favourite prophet, out of pretended zeal for whom they were ready to reject Christianity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Every soul which will not hear that prophet shall be destroyed from among the people. Henry reads the passage as warning of the dreadful consequences of unbelief, spoken in Moses' own words to those who claimed Moses against Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. All the prophets from Samuel and those that follow after have likewise foretold of these days. Henry's summary of the address is that Christ came into the world to bring a blessing with him, and sent his Spirit to be the great blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Ye are the children of the prophets and of the covenant God made with Abraham, in whose seed all kindreds of the earth shall be blessed. Henry closes the chapter on the blessing itself: Christ came to bless us by turning us from our iniquities and saving us from our sins, and none can be happy continuing in sin when God declares the blessing lies in being turned from all iniquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Unto you first God, having raised up his Son Jesus, sent him to bless you, in turning away every one of you from his iniquities. Henry closes on this blessing: Christ came to bless us by turning us from our iniquities and saving us from our sins, and none should expect to be turned from sin except by believing in and receiving Christ the Son of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.
