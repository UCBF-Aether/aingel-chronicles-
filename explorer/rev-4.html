The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 After this I looked, and, behold, a door was opened in heaven: and the first voice which I heard was as it were of a trumpet talking with me; which said, Come up hither, and I will shew thee things which must be hereafter.

2 And immediately I was in the spirit: and, behold, a throne was set in heaven, and one sat on the throne.

3 And he that sat was to look upon like a jasper and a sardine stone: and there was a rainbow round about the throne, in sight like unto an emerald.

4 And round about the throne were four and twenty seats: and upon the seats I saw four and twenty elders sitting, clothed in white raiment; and they had on their heads crowns of gold.

5 And out of the throne proceeded lightnings and thunderings and voices: and there were seven lamps of fire burning before the throne, which are the seven Spirits of God.

6 And before the throne there was a sea of glass like unto crystal: and in the midst of the throne, and round about the throne, were four beasts full of eyes before and behind.

7 And the first beast was like a lion, and the second beast like a calf, and the third beast had a face as a man, and the fourth beast was like a flying eagle.

8 And the four beasts had each of them six wings about him; and they were full of eyes within: and they rest not day and night, saying, Holy, holy, holy, LORD God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come.

9 And when those beasts give glory and honour and thanks to him that sat on the throne, who liveth for ever and ever,

10 The four and twenty elders fall down before him that sat on the throne, and worship him that liveth for ever and ever, and cast their crowns before the throne, saying,

11 Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power: for thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After the letters, John is called up through a door opened in heaven to a throne vision: one seated on the throne, ringed by four and twenty elders, seven lamps of fire, a sea of glass like crystal, and four living creatures full of eyes who rest not from crying, Holy, holy, holy. The elders answer by falling down and casting their crowns before the throne, confessing God worthy of glory, honour and power because he created all things. Henry reads the scene as heaven's government over church and world, with worship led by ministers and followed by the people. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After the messages to the churches, John looked and saw a door opened in heaven, and the first voice, as of a trumpet, said, "Come up hither, and I will shew thee things which must be hereafter." Henry notes whatever is transacted on earth is first designed in heaven, and we know future events only as God opens the door to reveal them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Immediately John was in the Spirit, and behold, a throne was set in heaven, and one sat on the throne. Henry notes heaven is God's throne, from which he gives laws to church and world, and that bodily sensations were suspended while John's spirit was wholly under the Spirit of prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He who sat was to look upon like a jasper and a sardine stone, with a rainbow round the throne, in sight like an emerald. Henry reads the jasper's vivid colours as God's glorious perfections, the red sardine as his justice displayed in pardoning and punishing, and the emerald rainbow as the everlasting covenant of promise, reviving and refreshing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Round the throne were four and twenty seats, with four and twenty elders sitting, clothed in white raiment, with crowns of gold on their heads. Henry takes the elders as representatives of the whole church, Old and New Testament, their sitting denoting honour, rest, and nearness to God, their white robes the saints' righteousness, and their crowns the glory given them by God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Out of the throne proceeded lightnings, thunderings, and voices, and seven lamps of fire burned before the throne, which are the seven Spirits of God. Henry compares the awful declarations to Sinai, noting the gospel has no less glory and authority than the law, and that the Spirit's gifts and operations in the churches are dispensed by him on the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Before the throne was a sea of glass like crystal, and in the midst of the throne and round about it were four beasts full of eyes before and behind. Henry likens the sea to the temple laver, fulfilled in Christ's blood, in which all admitted to God's presence must be washed, and the living creatures to gospel ministers standing between God and the people, watchful with many eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The first beast was like a lion, the second like a calf, the third had a face as a man, and the fourth was like a flying eagle. Henry reads the four forms as ministers' qualities: lion-like courage, ox-like labour and diligence, human prudence and discretion, and eagle-like sublime affections mounting toward heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Each of the four beasts had six wings and was full of eyes within, and they rest not day and night, saying, "Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come." Henry notes their continual employment is praise, and that the threefold holy adores one God, Father, Son, and Spirit, unchangeable and everlasting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. When the living creatures give glory, honour, and thanks to him who sat on the throne and lives for ever and ever, the elders answer. Henry notes the ministers lead and the people follow in praise, with one and the same object of worship, since two objects would divide the worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The four and twenty elders fall down before him who sits on the throne, worship him who lives for ever and ever, and cast their crowns before the throne. Henry notes the profound humility of the fall and the acknowledgement in the cast crowns: all graces and glories are owed to God, whose crown is infinitely more glorious than theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. They say, "Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power: for thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created." Henry notes they do not claim to give God glory but confess him worthy to receive it, on three grounds: he is Creator, Preserver, and final cause of all things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Sea of glass. Henry likens it to the temple laver, fulfilled in Christ's blood, in which all admitted to God's presence must be washed. Interpreted Study note, verse 6, Matthew Henry
  • Four beasts, or living creatures. Lion, calf, man and flying eagle: Henry reads their forms as ministers' qualities — courage, labour, prudence and sublimity. Interpreted Study note, verse 7, Matthew Henry
