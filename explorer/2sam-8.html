The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And after this it came to pass that David smote the Philistines, and subdued them: and David took Methegammah out of the hand of the Philistines.

2 And he smote Moab, and measured them with a line, casting them down to the ground; even with two lines measured he to put to death, and with one full line to keep alive. And so the Moabites became David’s servants, and brought gifts.

3 David smote also Hadadezer, the son of Rehob, king of Zobah, as he went to recover his border at the river Euphrates.

4 And David took from him a thousand chariots, and seven hundred horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen: and David houghed all the chariot horses, but reserved of them for an hundred chariots.

5 And when the Syrians of Damascus came to succour Hadadezer king of Zobah, David slew of the Syrians two and twenty thousand men.

6 Then David put garrisons in Syria of Damascus: and the Syrians became servants to David, and brought gifts. And the LORD preserved David whithersoever he went.

7 And David took the shields of gold that were on the servants of Hadadezer, and brought them to Jerusalem.

8 And from Betah, and from Berothai, cities of Hadadezer, king David took exceeding much brass.

9 When Toi king of Hamath heard that David had smitten all the host of Hadadezer,

10 Then Toi sent Joram his son unto king David, to salute him, and to bless him, because he had fought against Hadadezer, and smitten him: for Hadadezer had wars with Toi. And Joram brought with him vessels of silver, and vessels of gold, and vessels of brass:

11 Which also king David did dedicate unto the LORD, with the silver and gold that he had dedicated of all nations which he subdued;

12 Of Syria, and of Moab, and of the children of Ammon, and of the Philistines, and of Amalek, and of the spoil of Hadadezer, son of Rehob, king of Zobah.

13 And David gat him a name when he returned from smiting of the Syrians in the valley of salt, being eighteen thousand men.

14 And he put garrisons in Edom; throughout all Edom put he garrisons, and all they of Edom became David’s servants. And the LORD preserved David whithersoever he went.

15 And David reigned over all Israel; and David executed judgment and justice unto all his people.

16 And Joab the son of Zeruiah was over the host; and Jehoshaphat the son of Ahilud was recorder;

17 And Zadok the son of Ahitub, and Ahimelech the son of Abiathar, were the priests; and Seraiah was the scribe;

18 And Benaiah the son of Jehoiada was over both the Cherethites and the Pelethites; and David’s sons were chief rulers.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

David's wars extend his rule over the Philistines, Moab, Zobah, Syria and Edom, and the chapter records the tribute and spoil he dedicates to the LORD. It closes with the list of the officials who run his kingdom, from Joab over the army to the priests. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After this David smites the Philistines, subdues them, and takes Methegammah from their hand. They had long been troublesome to Israel, and Henry sees in their subduing a type of the Son of David treading the powers of darkness underfoot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. He smites Moab and measures them with a line, putting two lines to death and keeping one full line alive, so the Moabites become servants bringing gifts. Henry notes the line kept alive, though but one, is ordered to be a full line: let the line of mercy be stretched to the utmost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. David smites Hadadezer king of Zobah as he goes to recover his border at the Euphrates. The campaign pushes Israel's frontier to the river promised long before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. David takes a thousand chariots, seven hundred horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen, houghing the chariot horses but reserving a hundred chariots. In all these wars, Henry notes, David was protected, and in his psalms he often gives glory to God for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. When the Syrians of Damascus come to succour Hadadezer, David slays twenty-two thousand of them. An ally's rescue attempt only widens the defeat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. David puts garrisons in Syria of Damascus, the Syrians become his servants bringing gifts, and the Lord preserves David wherever he goes. Preservation in every campaign is the refrain of the chapter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. David takes the shields of gold from Hadadezer's servants and brings them to Jerusalem. The precious spoil of a defeated king becomes treasure in the capital. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. From Betah and Berothai, cities of Hadadezer, David takes exceeding much brass. The raw material of war is gathered for a purpose beyond war. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Toi king of Hamath hears David smote all Hadadezer's host. News of the victory travels to neighbouring kings, who must now reckon with David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Toi sends his son Joram to salute and bless David, because he had fought and smitten Hadadezer, who had wars with Toi, bringing vessels of silver, gold, and brass. An old enemy of David's enemy becomes his congratulator. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. All these things David dedicates to the Lord, with the silver and gold he had dedicated from all nations he subdued. Henry notes that in the conquest of a soul, what opposes God must be destroyed, but what may glorify him must be dedicated, its property altered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The dedication gathers spoil of Syria, Moab, Ammon, the Philistines, Amalek, and Hadadezer son of Rehob. Every conquered nation's tribute is redirected to the temple David's son will build. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. David gains a name when he returns from smiting the Syrians in the valley of salt, eighteen thousand men. God employs his servants variously, Henry observes: some, as David, in spiritual battles, and others, as Solomon, in spiritual buildings, one preparing work for the other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. He puts garrisons throughout Edom, and all Edom becomes his servants, and the Lord preserves David wherever he goes. The southern frontier is secured as the northern one was. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. David reigns over all Israel and executes judgment and justice to all his people. He neither did wrong nor denied or delayed right to any, Henry notes, showing close application to business and readiness to hear every appeal, with no respect of persons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Joab is over the host, and Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud is recorder. The kingdom's wars and records each have a named officer, marking an ordered government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Zadok and Ahimelech are priests, and Seraiah is scribe. Worship and writing stand beside warfare in David's administration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Benaiah is over the Cherethites and Pelethites, and David's sons are chief rulers. Henry notes David made his sons chief rulers, but all believers, Christ's spiritual seed, are better preferred, made kings and priests to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
