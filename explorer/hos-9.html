The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hosea 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Rejoice not, O Israel, for joy, as other people: for thou hast gone a whoring from thy God, thou hast loved a reward upon every cornfloor.

2 The floor and the winepress shall not feed them, and the new wine shall fail in her.

3 They shall not dwell in the LORD’s land; but Ephraim shall return to Egypt, and they shall eat unclean things in Assyria.

4 They shall not offer wine offerings to the LORD, neither shall they be pleasing unto him: their sacrifices shall be unto them as the bread of mourners; all that eat thereof shall be polluted: for their bread for their soul shall not come into the house of the LORD.

5 What will ye do in the solemn day, and in the day of the feast of the LORD?

6 For, lo, they are gone because of destruction: Egypt shall gather them up, Memphis shall bury them: the pleasant places for their silver, nettles shall possess them: thorns shall be in their tabernacles.

7 The days of visitation are come, the days of recompence are come; Israel shall know it: the prophet is a fool, the spiritual man is mad, for the multitude of thine iniquity, and the great hatred.

8 The watchman of Ephraim was with my God: but the prophet is a snare of a fowler in all his ways, and hatred in the house of his God.

9 They have deeply corrupted themselves, as in the days of Gibeah: therefore he will remember their iniquity, he will visit their sins.

10 I found Israel like grapes in the wilderness; I saw your fathers as the firstripe in the fig tree at her first time: but they went to Baalpeor, and separated themselves unto that shame; and their abominations were according as they loved.

11 As for Ephraim, their glory shall fly away like a bird, from the birth, and from the womb, and from the conception.

12 Though they bring up their children, yet will I bereave them, that there shall not be a man left: yea, woe also to them when I depart from them!

13 Ephraim, as I saw Tyrus, is planted in a pleasant place: but Ephraim shall bring forth his children to the murderer.

14 Give them, O LORD: what wilt thou give? give them a miscarrying womb and dry breasts.

15 All their wickedness is in Gilgal: for there I hated them: for the wickedness of their doings I will drive them out of mine house, I will love them no more: all their princes are revolters.

16 Ephraim is smitten, their root is dried up, they shall bear no fruit: yea, though they bring forth, yet will I slay even the beloved fruit of their womb.

17 My God will cast them away, because they did not hearken unto him: and they shall be wanderers among the nations.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Israel is told not to rejoice like other peoples, for the harvest will fail, the people will eat unclean things in Assyria, and their sacrifices will be like the bread of mourners that cannot come into the house of the LORD. The days of visitation have come, the corruption is as deep as in the days of Gibeah, and Ephraim's glory will fly away like a bird until the people are wanderers among the nations. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Rejoice not, O Israel, for joy, as other people, for thou hast gone a whoring from thy God, thou hast loved a reward upon every cornfloor, because Israel's joy is forbidden, since its harvest joy was paid as a harlot's wage upon every threshing floor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The floor and the winepress shall not feed them, and the new wine shall fail in her, because the harvest that was loved as a reward will itself fail, and floor and press will feed no one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They shall not dwell in the Lord's land, but Ephraim shall return to Egypt, and they shall eat unclean things in Assyria, because exile removes them from the holy land to lands where even their food is unclean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They shall not offer wine offerings to the Lord, neither shall they be pleasing unto him, their sacrifices shall be unto them as the bread of mourners, all that eat thereof shall be polluted, for their bread for their soul shall not come into the house of the Lord, because in exile there is no acceptable worship, and their bread is mourners' bread, unfit for the house of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. What will ye do in the solemn day, and in the day of the feast of the Lord, because the question exposes their helplessness, since the feasts cannot be kept in a land of exile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For, lo, they are gone because of destruction, Egypt shall gather them up, Memphis shall bury them, the pleasant places for their silver, nettles shall possess them, thorns shall be in their tabernacles, because flight from destruction ends in Egyptian graves, and their pleasant silver houses will be overrun with nettles and thorns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The days of visitation are come, the days of recompense are come, Israel shall know it, the prophet is a fool, the spiritual man is mad, for the multitude of thine iniquity, and the great hatred, because when judgment arrives, the people mock the prophet as a fool, and the hatred that caused their iniquity turns upon the messenger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The watchman of Ephraim was with my God, but the prophet is a snare of a fowler in all his ways, and hatred in the house of his God, because the prophet, meant as a watchman with God, is treated as a fowler's snare, and hatred meets him even in God's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They have deeply corrupted themselves, as in the days of Gibeah, therefore he will remember their iniquity, he will visit their sins, because the corruption of Gibeah, the outrage of the Benjamite days, is repeated, and God will remember and visit as he did then. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. I found Israel like grapes in the wilderness, I saw your fathers as the firstripe in the fig tree at her first time, but they went to Baalpeor, and separated themselves unto that shame, and their abominations were according as they loved, because Israel was once as delightful as grapes in a desert, but at Baalpeor they joined themselves to shame and became abominable like the thing they loved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. As for Ephraim, their glory shall fly away like a bird, from the birth, and from the womb, and from the conception, because the glory of children will depart at every stage, so that there is no birth, no womb, no conception left to boast in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Though they bring up their children, yet will I bereave them, that there shall not be a man left, yea, woe also to them when I depart from them, because even children reared to maturity will be bereaved, and the crowning woe is God's departure itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Ephraim, as I saw Tyrus, is planted in a pleasant place, but Ephraim shall bring forth his children to the murderer, because Ephraim, planted pleasantly like Tyre, will itself hand its children over to the murderer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Give them, O Lord, what wilt thou give, give them a miscarrying womb and dry breasts, because the prophet, shrinking from a heavier curse, prays for barrenness itself as a mercy compared with children given to the murderer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. All their wickedness is in Gilgal, for there I hated them, for the wickedness of their doings I will drive them out of mine house, I will love them no more, all their princes are revolters, because Gilgal, the shrine of their wickedness, is where hatred was fixed, and expulsion from God's house follows, since every prince has revolted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Ephraim is smitten, their root is dried up, they shall bear no fruit, yea, though they bring forth, yet will I slay even the beloved fruit of their womb, because the root itself is dried, so no fruit can follow, and even fruit already brought forth will be slain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. My God will cast them away, because they did not hearken unto him, and they shall be wanderers among the nations, because rejection for not hearkening ends in wandering, a people without a home among the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Baal and Baalim. the Canaanite god, and the plural form for the Baals, whose worship the prophets charge God's people with, crediting idols with gifts God himself gave. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Ephraim. the leading tribe of the northern kingdom, used in the prophets for the northern kingdom of Israel itself, as distinct from Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
