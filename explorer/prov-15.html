The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger.

2 The tongue of the wise useth knowledge aright: but the mouth of fools poureth out foolishness.

3 The eyes of the LORD are in every place, beholding the evil and the good.

4 A wholesome tongue is a tree of life: but perverseness therein is a breach in the spirit.

5 A fool despiseth his father’s instruction: but he that regardeth reproof is prudent.

6 In the house of the righteous is much treasure: but in the revenues of the wicked is trouble.

7 The lips of the wise disperse knowledge: but the heart of the foolish doeth not so.

8 The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination to the LORD: but the prayer of the upright is his delight.

9 The way of the wicked is an abomination unto the LORD: but he loveth him that followeth after righteousness.

10 Correction is grievous unto him that forsaketh the way: and he that hateth reproof shall die.

11 Hell and destruction are before the LORD: how much more then the hearts of the children of men?

12 A scorner loveth not one that reproveth him: neither will he go unto the wise.

13 A merry heart maketh a cheerful countenance: but by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken.

14 The heart of him that hath understanding seeketh knowledge: but the mouth of fools feedeth on foolishness.

15 All the days of the afflicted are evil: but he that is of a merry heart hath a continual feast.

16 Better is little with the fear of the LORD than great treasure and trouble therewith.

17 Better is a dinner of herbs where love is, than a stalled ox and hatred therewith.

18 A wrathful man stirreth up strife: but he that is slow to anger appeaseth strife.

19 The way of the slothful man is as an hedge of thorns: but the way of the righteous is made plain.

20 A wise son maketh a glad father: but a foolish man despiseth his mother.

21 Folly is joy to him that is destitute of wisdom: but a man of understanding walketh uprightly.

22 Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established.

23 A man hath joy by the answer of his mouth: and a word spoken in due season, how good is it!

24 The way of life is above to the wise, that he may depart from hell beneath.

25 The LORD will destroy the house of the proud: but he will establish the border of the widow.

26 The thoughts of the wicked are an abomination to the LORD: but the words of the pure are pleasant words.

27 He that is greedy of gain troubleth his own house; but he that hateth gifts shall live.

28 The heart of the righteous studieth to answer: but the mouth of the wicked poureth out evil things.

29 The LORD is far from the wicked: but he heareth the prayer of the righteous.

30 The light of the eyes rejoiceth the heart: and a good report maketh the bones fat.

31 The ear that heareth the reproof of life abideth among the wise.

32 He that refuseth instruction despiseth his own soul: but he that heareth reproof getteth understanding.

33 The fear of the LORD is the instruction of wisdom; and before honour is humility.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A soft answer turns away wrath, and the chapter stays with the tongue, the heart and the eyes of the LORD that are in every place. Better a dinner of herbs where love is than a stalled ox with hatred, better little with the fear of the LORD than great treasure with trouble. Counsel, timely words and humility close it: before honour is humility, and the fear of the LORD is the instruction of wisdom. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. A soft answer turns away wrath, but grievous words stir up anger, for a right cause is better pleaded with meekness than passion, and nothing stirs anger like grievous words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The tongue of the wise uses knowledge aright, but the mouth of fools pours out foolishness, for he who has knowledge is to use it aright for the good of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The eyes of the LORD are in every place, beholding evil and good, for secret sins, services, and sorrows are under God's eye, speaking comfort to saints and terror to sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. A wholesome tongue is a tree of life, but perverseness therein is a breach in the spirit, for a good tongue heals wounded consciences by comforting them, convinces sin-sick souls, and reconciles parties at variance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. A fool despises his father's instruction, but he who regards reproof is prudent, for if instruction is despised, men should be reproved rather than suffered to go undisturbed toward ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. In the house of the righteous is much treasure, but in the revenues of the wicked is trouble, for worldly wealth increases fears and suspicions, strengthens passions, and renders the fear of death more distressing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The lips of the wise disperse knowledge, but the heart of the foolish does not so, for we use knowledge aright when we disperse it, while the foolish heart has nothing good to disperse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The sacrifice of the wicked is abomination to the LORD, but the prayer of the upright is his delight, for the wicked put other things in place of Christ's atonement, while praying graces are his gift and his Spirit's work, with which he is well pleased. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The way of the wicked is abomination to the LORD, but he loves him who follows after righteousness, for the wicked put other things in place of holy obedience, while God delights in praying graces wrought by his Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Correction is grievous to him who forsakes the way, and he who hates reproof shall die, for he who hates reproof shall perish in his sins, since he would not be parted from them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Hell and destruction are before the LORD, how much more the hearts of the children of men, for nothing can be hid from God's eyes, not even man's thoughts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. A scorner loves not one who reproves him, neither will he go to the wise, for a scorner cannot bear to reflect seriously within his own heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. A merry heart makes a cheerful countenance, but by sorrow of heart the spirit is broken, for a gloomy, impatient, unthankful spirit, springing from pride and attachment to worldly objects, renders a man uneasy to himself and others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The heart of him who has understanding seeks knowledge, but the mouth of fools feeds on foolishness, for a wise man seeks more wisdom, growing in grace and in knowledge of Christ, while a carnal mind rests contented, flattering itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. All the days of the afflicted are evil, but he of merry heart has a continual feast, for some are much in affliction and sorrowful spirit, to be pitied, prayed for, and comforted, while others serve God with gladness, yet should rejoice with trembling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Better is little with the fear of the LORD than great treasure and trouble therewith, for believers often have enough when worldly eyes see little, the Lord being with them without the cares and temptations attending wicked wealth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Better is a dinner of herbs where love is, than a stalled ox and hatred therewith, for believers often have enough when worldly eyes see little, the Lord with them without the troubles attending the wealth of the wicked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. A wrathful man stirs up strife, but he slow to anger appeases strife, for he who is slow to anger not only prevents strife but appeases it if kindled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. The way of the slothful is as a hedge of thorns, but the way of the righteous is made plain, for those with no heart to their work pretend they cannot do it without hardship and danger, and so live in doubt through neglect of duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. A wise son makes a glad father, but a foolish man despises his mother, for those treating an aged mother or father with contempt or neglect show their own folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Folly is joy to him destitute of wisdom, but a man of understanding walks uprightly, for the truly wise study that thoughts, words, and actions should be regular, sincere, and holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Without counsel purposes are disappointed, but in the multitude of counsellors they are established, for if men will not take time and pains to deliberate, they are not likely to bring anything to pass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. A man has joy by the answer of his mouth, and a word spoken in due season, how good, for wisdom is needed to suit discourse to the occasions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. The way of life is above to the wise, that he may depart from hell beneath, for a good man sets his affections on things above, and his way leads directly thither. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. The LORD will destroy the house of the proud, but he will establish the border of the widow, for pride is the ruin of multitudes, while those in affliction God will support. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. The thoughts of the wicked are abomination to the LORD, but the words of the pure are pleasant words, for the thoughts of wicked men offend him who knows the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. He greedy of gain troubles his own house, but he who hates gifts shall live, for the covetous man lets none of his family have rest or enjoyment, and greediness often tempts to projects that bring ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. The heart of the righteous studies to answer, but the mouth of the wicked pours out evil things, for a good man is proved wise by governing his tongue well. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. The LORD is far from the wicked, but he hears the prayer of the righteous, for God sets himself at a distance from those who set him at defiance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. The light of the eyes rejoices the heart, and a good report makes the bones fat, for how delightful to the humbled soul to hear the good report of salvation by the Lord Jesus Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. The ear that hears the reproof of life abides among the wise, for faithful, friendly reproofs help spiritual life and lead to eternal life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. He who refuses instruction despises his own soul, but he who hears reproof gets understanding, for sinners undervalue their own souls, preferring body before soul and wronging the soul to please the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. The fear of the LORD is the instruction of wisdom, and before honour is humility, for the fear of the Lord disposes us to search the Scriptures reverently and follow the Holy Spirit, and humbly depending on God's grace we are exalted in Christ's righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
