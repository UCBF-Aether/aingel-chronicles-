The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ephesians 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 I therefore, the prisoner of the Lord, beseech you that ye walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called,

2 With all lowliness and meekness, with longsuffering, forbearing one another in love;

3 Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

4 There is one body, and one Spirit, even as ye are called in one hope of your calling;

5 One Lord, one faith, one baptism,

6 One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in you all.

7 But unto every one of us is given grace according to the measure of the gift of Christ.

8 Wherefore he saith, When he ascended up on high, he led captivity captive, and gave gifts unto men.

9 (Now that he ascended, what is it but that he also descended first into the lower parts of the earth?

10 He that descended is the same also that ascended up far above all heavens, that he might fill all things.)

11 And he gave some, apostles; and some, prophets; and some, evangelists; and some, pastors and teachers;

12 For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ:

13 Till we all come in the unity of the faith, and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fulness of Christ:

14 That we henceforth be no more children, tossed to and fro, and carried about with every wind of doctrine, by the sleight of men, and cunning craftiness, whereby they lie in wait to deceive;

15 But speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ:

16 From whom the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth, according to the effectual working in the measure of every part, maketh increase of the body unto the edifying of itself in love.

17 This I say therefore, and testify in the Lord, that ye henceforth walk not as other Gentiles walk, in the vanity of their mind,

18 Having the understanding darkened, being alienated from the life of God through the ignorance that is in them, because of the blindness of their heart:

19 Who being past feeling have given themselves over unto lasciviousness, to work all uncleanness with greediness.

20 But ye have not so learned Christ;

21 If so be that ye have heard him, and have been taught by him, as the truth is in Jesus:

22 That ye put off concerning the former conversation the old man, which is corrupt according to the deceitful lusts;

23 And be renewed in the spirit of your mind;

24 And that ye put on the new man, which after God is created in righteousness and true holiness.

25 Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another.

26 Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath:

27 Neither give place to the devil.

28 Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.

29 Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.

30 And grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption.

31 Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice:

32 And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

One body, one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism: Paul urges a walk worthy of that unity, with humility, gentleness and patience. Christ gives gifts, apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers, to build the body up. The old self is put off and the new put on, in truthful, honest, kind and forgiving conduct. Interpreted Editorial overview of the chapter text; verse-by-verse study-note summaries follow

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul, the prisoner of the Lord, urges them to walk worthy of their calling, since nothing is pressed more earnestly in Scripture than a life becoming their profession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. That worthy walk is marked by lowliness, meekness, longsuffering, and forbearing one another in love, humility being the opposite of pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Believers are to endeavor to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace, one heart because they share one hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. There is one body and one Spirit, even as they are called in one hope of their calling, so division contradicts the very nature of their calling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. One Lord, one faith, one baptism bind them together, since they believe the same great truths and were admitted by the same sign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. One God and Father of all, above all, through all, and in them all, dwells in all believers as in his holy temple by his Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. To every believer grace is given according to the measure of Christ's gift, each receiving what Christ sees best to bestow for mutual help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When Christ ascended on high he led captivity captive and gave gifts to men, receiving that he might give, particularly the gift of the Holy Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. That he ascended implies he first descended into the lower parts of the earth, the same Lord humbling himself before he was exalted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. He who descended is the same who ascended far above all heavens, that he might fill all things, so his exaltation is universal, not partial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers, distributing gifts for the church's good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. These gifts are for perfecting the saints, for the work of ministry, and for building up the body of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The goal is that all come to unity of faith and knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of Christ's fullness, a perfection reached only in heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Then they will no longer be children tossed to and fro and carried about by every wind of doctrine, through the trickery of men lying in wait to deceive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Instead, speaking truth in love, they grow up into him in all things, who is the head, even Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. From Christ the whole body, fitly joined and compacted by every joint, grows as each part works, and that growth tends to Christ's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Paul testifies in the Lord that they must no longer walk as other Gentiles walk, in the vanity of their minds, since the gospel draws a real line between nominal and true Christians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The unconverted Gentiles have darkened understanding and are alienated from God's life through ignorance and blindness of heart, sitting in darkness and loving it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Being past feeling, they have given themselves over to lasciviousness, working all uncleanness with greediness, sinful desires promising happiness but bringing misery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Believers have not so learned Christ, whose truth appears in beauty and power when seen as it is in Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. If they have truly heard him and been taught by him, as truth is in Jesus, their lives must show the difference. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. They are to put off the old man, the corrupt former way of life, like a filthy garment, since deceitful lusts must be subdued and mortified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. They are to be renewed in the spirit of their minds, since shaking off corrupt principles is not enough without gracious ones in their place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. They are to put on the new man, created after God in righteousness and true holiness, a new nature directed by regenerating grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Putting away lying, each must speak truth with his neighbor, since God's people are children who dare not lie, and believers are members of one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. They may be angry yet not sin, and must not let the sun go down on their wrath, since ungoverned passion gives place to the devil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Neither give place to the devil: sin yielded to lets the enemy in, so the first motions of sin must be resisted, not consented to. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The thief must steal no more but labor honestly with his hands, not only to live honestly but to have something to give to those in need, since alms gained by robbery are hateful to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. No corrupt communication should proceed from their mouths, but only what is good for edifying, ministering grace to hearers, since filthy words corrupt both speaker and hearer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. They must not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, by whom they are sealed unto the day of redemption, since the Spirit dwelling in them is the earnest of that day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. All bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor, evil speaking, and malice must be put away, since such corrupt passions grieve the Holy Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Instead they are to be kind, tenderhearted, and forgiving, even as God for Christ's sake has forgiven them, God's forgiveness being the pattern and motive for theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
