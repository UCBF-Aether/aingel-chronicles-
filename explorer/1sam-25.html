The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 25 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Samuel died; and all the Israelites were gathered together, and lamented him, and buried him in his house at Ramah. And David arose, and went down to the wilderness of Paran.

2 And there was a man in Maon, whose possessions were in Carmel; and the man was very great, and he had three thousand sheep, and a thousand goats: and he was shearing his sheep in Carmel.

3 Now the name of the man was Nabal; and the name of his wife Abigail: and she was a woman of good understanding, and of a beautiful countenance: but the man was churlish and evil in his doings; and he was of the house of Caleb.

4 And David heard in the wilderness that Nabal did shear his sheep.

5 And David sent out ten young men, and David said unto the young men, Get you up to Carmel, and go to Nabal, and greet him in my name:

6 And thus shall ye say to him that liveth in prosperity, Peace be both to thee, and peace be to thine house, and peace be unto all that thou hast.

7 And now I have heard that thou hast shearers: now thy shepherds which were with us, we hurt them not, neither was there ought missing unto them, all the while they were in Carmel.

8 Ask thy young men, and they will shew thee. Wherefore let the young men find favour in thine eyes: for we come in a good day: give, I pray thee, whatsoever cometh to thine hand unto thy servants, and to thy son David.

9 And when David’s young men came, they spake to Nabal according to all those words in the name of David, and ceased.

10 And Nabal answered David’s servants, and said, Who is David? and who is the son of Jesse? there be many servants now a days that break away every man from his master.

11 Shall I then take my bread, and my water, and my flesh that I have killed for my shearers, and give it unto men, whom I know not whence they be?

12 So David’s young men turned their way, and went again, and came and told him all those sayings.

13 And David said unto his men, Gird ye on every man his sword. And they girded on every man his sword; and David also girded on his sword: and there went up after David about four hundred men; and two hundred abode by the stuff.

14 But one of the young men told Abigail, Nabal’s wife, saying, Behold, David sent messengers out of the wilderness to salute our master; and he railed on them.

15 But the men were very good unto us, and we were not hurt, neither missed we any thing, as long as we were conversant with them, when we were in the fields:

16 They were a wall unto us both by night and day, all the while we were with them keeping the sheep.

17 Now therefore know and consider what thou wilt do; for evil is determined against our master, and against all his household: for he is such a son of Belial, that a man cannot speak to him.

18 Then Abigail made haste, and took two hundred loaves, and two bottles of wine, and five sheep ready dressed, and five measures of parched corn, and an hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs, and laid them on asses.

19 And she said unto her servants, Go on before me; behold, I come after you. But she told not her husband Nabal.

20 And it was so, as she rode on the ass, that she came down by the covert on the hill, and, behold, David and his men came down against her; and she met them.

21 Now David had said, Surely in vain have I kept all that this fellow hath in the wilderness, so that nothing was missed of all that pertained unto him: and he hath requited me evil for good.

22 So and more also do God unto the enemies of David, if I leave of all that pertain to him by the morning light any that pisseth against the wall.

23 And when Abigail saw David, she hasted, and lighted off the ass, and fell before David on her face, and bowed herself to the ground,

24 And fell at his feet, and said, Upon me, my lord, upon me let this iniquity be: and let thine handmaid, I pray thee, speak in thine audience, and hear the words of thine handmaid.

25 Let not my lord, I pray thee, regard this man of Belial, even Nabal: for as his name is, so is he; Nabal is his name, and folly is with him: but I thine handmaid saw not the young men of my lord, whom thou didst send.

26 Now therefore, my lord, as the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, seeing the LORD hath withholden thee from coming to shed blood, and from avenging thyself with thine own hand, now let thine enemies, and they that seek evil to my lord, be as Nabal.

27 And now this blessing which thine handmaid hath brought unto my lord, let it even be given unto the young men that follow my lord.

28 I pray thee, forgive the trespass of thine handmaid: for the LORD will certainly make my lord a sure house; because my lord fighteth the battles of the LORD, and evil hath not been found in thee all thy days.

29 Yet a man is risen to pursue thee, and to seek thy soul: but the soul of my lord shall be bound in the bundle of life with the LORD thy God; and the souls of thine enemies, them shall he sling out, as out of the middle of a sling.

30 And it shall come to pass, when the LORD shall have done to my lord according to all the good that he hath spoken concerning thee, and shall have appointed thee ruler over Israel;

31 That this shall be no grief unto thee, nor offence of heart unto my lord, either that thou hast shed blood causeless, or that my lord hath avenged himself: but when the LORD shall have dealt well with my lord, then remember thine handmaid.

32 And David said to Abigail, Blessed be the LORD God of Israel, which sent thee this day to meet me:

33 And blessed be thy advice, and blessed be thou, which hast kept me this day from coming to shed blood, and from avenging myself with mine own hand.

34 For in very deed, as the LORD God of Israel liveth, which hath kept me back from hurting thee, except thou hadst hasted and come to meet me, surely there had not been left unto Nabal by the morning light any that pisseth against the wall.

35 So David received of her hand that which she had brought him, and said unto her, Go up in peace to thine house; see, I have hearkened to thy voice, and have accepted thy person.

36 And Abigail came to Nabal; and, behold, he held a feast in his house, like the feast of a king; and Nabal’s heart was merry within him, for he was very drunken: wherefore she told him nothing, less or more, until the morning light.

37 But it came to pass in the morning, when the wine was gone out of Nabal, and his wife had told him these things, that his heart died within him, and he became as a stone.

38 And it came to pass about ten days after, that the LORD smote Nabal, that he died.

39 And when David heard that Nabal was dead, he said, Blessed be the LORD, that hath pleaded the cause of my reproach from the hand of Nabal, and hath kept his servant from evil: for the LORD hath returned the wickedness of Nabal upon his own head. And David sent and communed with Abigail, to take her to him to wife.

40 And when the servants of David were come to Abigail to Carmel, they spake unto her, saying, David sent us unto thee, to take thee to him to wife.

41 And she arose, and bowed herself on her face to the earth, and said, Behold, let thine handmaid be a servant to wash the feet of the servants of my lord.

42 And Abigail hasted, and arose and rode upon an ass, with five damsels of hers that went after her; and she went after the messengers of David, and became his wife.

43 David also took Ahinoam of Jezreel; and they were also both of them his wives.

44 But Saul had given Michal his daughter, David’s wife, to Phalti the son of Laish, which was of Gallim.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Samuel dies, and all Israel gathers, laments him and buries him in his house at Ramah, and David goes down to the wilderness of Paran. There a very great man of Maon, whose possessions are in Carmel, three thousand sheep and a thousand goats, is shearing: Nabal, churlish and evil in his doings, and his wife Abigail, a woman of good understanding and beautiful countenance. David sends ten young men with a peaceful greeting and a just claim, his men have hurt nothing and nothing is missing from Nabal's shepherds all the while they were in Carmel, and asks for whatever comes to hand in the good day of shearing; Nabal answers with contempt, Who is David, and who is the son of Jesse, shall I take my bread, my water and my flesh and give it to men whom I know not whence they be. David girds on his sword with four hundred men to destroy Nabal's household by morning, but a servant warns Abigail, who hastens with two hundred loaves, wine, sheep, corn, raisins and fig cakes, meets David on the covert of the hill, falls at his feet, takes the iniquity on herself, and pleads with the image of the soul bound in the bundle of life with the LORD and the promise of a sure house, until David blesses the LORD who sent her, blesses her advice, and receives her gift in peace. Nabal, feasting like a king and very drunken, hears it in the morning, his heart dies within him and becomes as a stone, and about ten days after the LORD smites him and he dies; David sends and takes Abigail to wife, and also Ahinoam of Jezreel, while Saul gives Michal, David's wife, to Phalti son of Laish. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Samuel died, and all Israel gathered, lamented him, and buried him in his house at Ramah, and David arose and went down to the wilderness of Paran. All Israel lamented Samuel, and they had reason, Henry says: he prayed daily for them, and those have hard hearts who can bury faithful ministers without grief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. There was a man in Maon, whose possessions were in Carmel, very great, with three thousand sheep and a thousand goats, shearing his sheep in Carmel. We should not have heard of Nabal, Henry notes, if nothing had passed between him and David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The man's name was Nabal, and his wife Abigail, a woman of good understanding and beautiful countenance, but the man was churlish and evil in his doings, of the house of Caleb. Observe his name, Nabal, meaning a fool, Henry says; riches make men look great in the world's eye, but to one who takes right views, Nabal looked very mean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. David heard in the wilderness that Nabal did shear his sheep. Shearing time was feast time, and the moment for the request. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. David sent ten young men, saying, Get you up to Carmel, go to Nabal, and greet him in my name. The greeting was courteous and the errand peaceful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Thus shall ye say to him that liveth in prosperity, Peace be both to thee, and peace to thine house, and peace unto all that thou hast. The blessing wished him peace in every direction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Now I have heard thou hast shearers; thy shepherds which were with us, we hurt them not, neither was there ought missing unto them, all the while they were in Carmel. David pleaded the kindness Nabal's shepherds had received, Henry notes; considering David's men were in distress, debt, and discontent, and provisions scarce, it was by good management they were kept from plundering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Ask thy young men, and they will show thee; wherefore let the young men find favour in thine eyes, for we come in a good day; give, I pray thee, whatsoever cometh to thine hand unto thy servants and thy son David. The request appealed to witnesses and to the good day of shearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. When David's young men came, they spoke to Nabal according to all those words in David's name, and ceased. The message was delivered exactly, and the messengers waited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Nabal answered with contempt, asking who David and the son of Jesse were, and saying many servants nowadays break away from their masters. He had no honour or honesty, Henry says, and was churlish, cross, ill-humoured, evil in his doings, hard and oppressive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Shall I then take my bread, my water, and my flesh killed for my shearers, and give it unto men whom I know not whence they be? Nabal went into a passion, Henry observes, as covetous men are apt to do when asked for anything, thinking thus to cover one sin with another, and by abusing the poor to excuse themselves from relieving them. But God will not thus be mocked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. So David's young men turned their way, went again, and came and told David all those sayings. The refusal was reported word for word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. David said unto his men, Gird ye on every man his sword, and they girded on, and David also girded on his sword, and about four hundred men went up after David, and two hundred abode by the stuff. David determined to destroy Nabal and all that belonged to him, Henry notes: is this thy voice, O David? Has he been so long in the school of affliction, where he should have learned patience, and yet is so passionate? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. But one of the young men told Abigail, Nabal's wife, saying, Behold, David sent messengers to salute our master, and he railed on them. A servant, not the master, saw the danger and spoke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. But the men were very good unto us, and we were not hurt, neither missed we anything, as long as we were conversant with them in the fields. The servant's testimony confirmed David's claim in full. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. They were a wall unto us both by night and day, all the while we were with them keeping the sheep. David's men had been a living wall around Nabal's flocks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Now therefore know and consider what thou wilt do, for evil is determined against our master and all his household, for he is such a son of Belial that a man cannot speak to him. The household's only hope lay with Abigail. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Then Abigail made haste and took two hundred loaves, two bottles of wine, five sheep ready dressed, five measures of parched corn, a hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs, and laid them on asses. By a present Abigail atoned for Nabal's denial, Henry says; her behaviour was very submissive, and yielding pacifies great offences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. She said to her servants, Go on before me; behold, I come after you. But she told not her husband Nabal. The gift went ahead, and the wife followed, without the husband's knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. As she rode on the ass and came down by the covert of the hill, behold, David and his men came down against her, and she met them. Wife and avenger met on the mountain road. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Now David had said, Surely in vain have I kept all that this fellow hath in the wilderness, so that nothing was missed of all that pertained unto him, and he hath requited me evil for good. God is kind to the evil and unthankful, Henry asks, and why may not we be so? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. So and more also do God unto the enemies of David, if I leave of all that pertain to him by the morning light any that pisseth against the wall. The oath vowed the whole household's destruction by morning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. When Abigail saw David, she hasted, lighted off the ass, fell before David on her face, and bowed herself to the ground. She put herself in the place of a penitent and a petitioner, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. She fell at his feet and said, Upon me, my lord, upon me let this iniquity be, and let thine handmaid speak in thine audience, and hear the words of thine handmaid. She took the iniquity on herself before she argued it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Let not my lord regard this man of Belial, even Nabal, for as his name is, so is he; Nabal is his name, and folly is with him; but I thine handmaid saw not the young men of my lord. She could not excuse her husband's conduct, Henry observes, and she depended not on her own reasonings but on God's grace to soften David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Now therefore, my lord, as the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, seeing the LORD hath withholden thee from coming to shed blood and from avenging thyself with thine own hand, now let thine enemies, and they that seek evil to my lord, be as Nabal. She says it was below him to take vengeance on so weak and despicable an enemy as Nabal, Henry notes, who could do him no hurt as he had done him no kindness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. And now this blessing which thine handmaid hath brought unto my lord, let it even be given unto the young men that follow my lord. The present was offered to the men, not to David's pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. I pray thee, forgive the trespass of thine handmaid, for the LORD will certainly make my lord a sure house, because my lord fighteth the battles of the LORD, and evil hath not been found in thee all thy days. She foretold the glorious end of David's troubles, Henry says: God will preserve thy life, therefore it becomes not thee unjustly to take away the lives of any, especially of the people of thy God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Yet a man is risen to pursue thee and seek thy soul, but the soul of my lord shall be bound in the bundle of life with the LORD thy God, and the souls of thine enemies, them shall he sling out, as out of the middle of a sling. The pursued king's life was bound up with God; his enemies' lives were sling-stones to be cast away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. It shall come to pass, when the LORD shall have done to my lord according to all the good he hath spoken concerning thee, and shall have appointed thee ruler over Israel — Abigail looked past the present flight to the promised throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. That this shall be no grief unto thee, nor offence of heart unto my lord, either that thou hast shed blood causeless, or that my lord hath avenged himself; but when the LORD shall have dealt well with my lord, then remember thine handmaid. Abigail kept this argument for the last, Henry notes, as very powerful with so good a man: the less he indulged his passion, the more he consulted his peace and his conscience's repose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. David said to Abigail, Blessed be the LORD God of Israel, which sent thee this day to meet me. David gave God thanks for sending him this happy check in a sinful way, Henry observes; whoever meets us with counsel, direction, comfort, caution, or seasonable reproof, we must see God sending them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. And blessed be thy advice, and blessed be thou, which hast kept me this day from coming to shed blood and from avenging myself with mine own hand. Most people think it enough to take reproof patiently, Henry says, but few will take it thankfully, commend those who give it, and accept it as a favour; the nearer we are to committing sin, the greater the mercy of a seasonable restraint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. For in very deed, as the LORD God of Israel liveth, which hath kept me back from hurting thee, except thou hadst hasted and come to meet me, surely there had not been left unto Nabal by the morning light any that pisseth against the wall. David owned how close he had come to the slaughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. So David received of her hand what she had brought him, and said, Go up in peace to thine house; see, I have hearkened to thy voice, and have accepted thy person. The gift was accepted, and the avenger became the protector. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. Abigail came to Nabal, and behold, he held a feast like the feast of a king, his heart merry, for he was very drunken, wherefore she told him nothing until the morning light. Sinners are often most secure when most in danger, Henry notes; he was very drunk, a sign he was Nabal, a fool who could not use plenty without abusing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. In the morning, when the wine was gone out of Nabal, his wife told him these things, and his heart died within him, and he became as a stone. His heart overnight merry with wine, next morning heavy as a stone, Henry reflects: so deceitful are carnal pleasures, so soon passes the laughter of the fool, and the end of that mirth is heaviness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. About ten days after, the LORD smote Nabal, that he died. Worldly sorrow, mortified pride, and an affrighted conscience, Henry says, sometimes end the joys of the sensualist, but whatever the weapon, the Lord smites men with death when it pleases him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. When David heard Nabal was dead, he said, Blessed be the LORD, that hath pleaded the cause of my reproach from Nabal's hand, and hath kept his servant from evil, for the LORD hath returned Nabal's wickedness upon his own head. And David sent and communed with Abigail, to take her to him to wife. David blessed God that he had been kept from killing Nabal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. When David's servants came to Abigail to Carmel, saying, David sent us unto thee, to take thee to him to wife — the proposal followed the deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 41. She arose, bowed herself on her face to the earth, and said, Behold, let thine handmaid be a servant to wash the feet of the servants of my lord. Abigail believed David would be king, Henry notes, and greatly esteemed his pious and excellent character; she deemed the proposal honourable and advantageous, notwithstanding his present difficulties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 42. Abigail hasted, arose, rode upon an ass, with five damsels after her, went after David's messengers, and became his wife. With great humility she consented, willing to share his trials; thus those who join themselves to Christ, Henry says, must be willing now to suffer with him, believing that hereafter they shall reign with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 43. David also took Ahinoam of Jezreel, and they were both of them his wives. The fugitive's household grew even in exile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 44. But Saul had given Michal his daughter, David's wife, to Phalti son of Laish of Gallim. The king undid his own daughter's marriage while David married anew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 15. But the men were very good unto us, and we were not hurt, neither missed we anything, as long as we were conversant with them in the fields. The servant's testimony confirmed David's claim in full. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. They were a wall unto us both by night and day, all the while we were with them keeping the sheep. David's men had been a living wall around Nabal's flocks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Now therefore know and consider what thou wilt do, for evil is determined against our master and all his household, for he is such a son of Belial that a man cannot speak to him. The household's only hope lay with Abigail. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Then Abigail made haste and took two hundred loaves, two bottles of wine, five sheep ready dressed, five measures of parched corn, a hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs, and laid them on asses. By a present Abigail atoned for Nabal's denial, Henry says; her behaviour was very submissive, and yielding pacifies great offences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. She said to her servants, Go on before me; behold, I come after you. But she told not her husband Nabal. The gift went ahead, and the wife followed, without the husband's knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. As she rode on the ass and came down by the covert of the hill, behold, David and his men came down against her, and she met them. Wife and avenger met on the mountain road. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Now David had said, Surely in vain have I kept all that this fellow hath in the wilderness, so that nothing was missed of all that pertained unto him, and he hath requited me evil for good. God is kind to the evil and unthankful, Henry asks, and why may not we be so? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. So and more also do God unto the enemies of David, if I leave of all that pertain to him by the morning light any that pisseth against the wall. The oath vowed the whole household's destruction by morning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. When Abigail saw David, she hasted, lighted off the ass, fell before David on her face, and bowed herself to the ground. She put herself in the place of a penitent and a petitioner, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. She fell at his feet and said, Upon me, my lord, upon me let this iniquity be, and let thine handmaid speak in thine audience, and hear the words of thine handmaid. She took the iniquity on herself before she argued it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Let not my lord regard this man of Belial, even Nabal, for as his name is, so is he; Nabal is his name, and folly is with him; but I thine handmaid saw not the young men of my lord. She could not excuse her husband's conduct, Henry observes, and she depended not on her own reasonings but on God's grace to soften David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Now therefore, my lord, as the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, seeing the LORD hath withholden thee from coming to shed blood and from avenging thyself with thine own hand, now let thine enemies, and they that seek evil to my lord, be as Nabal. She says it was below him to take vengeance on so weak and despicable an enemy as Nabal, Henry notes, who could do him no hurt as he had done him no kindness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. And now this blessing which thine handmaid hath brought unto my lord, let it even be given unto the young men that follow my lord. The present was offered to the men, not to David's pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. I pray thee, forgive the trespass of thine handmaid, for the LORD will certainly make my lord a sure house, because my lord fighteth the battles of the LORD, and evil hath not been found in thee all thy days. She foretold the glorious end of David's troubles, Henry says: God will preserve thy life, therefore it becomes not thee unjustly to take away the lives of any, especially of the people of thy God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Yet a man is risen to pursue thee and seek thy soul, but the soul of my lord shall be bound in the bundle of life with the LORD thy God, and the souls of thine enemies, them shall he sling out, as out of the middle of a sling. The pursued king's life was bound up with God; his enemies' lives were sling-stones to be cast away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. It shall come to pass, when the LORD shall have done to my lord according to all the good he hath spoken concerning thee, and shall have appointed thee ruler over Israel — Abigail looked past the present flight to the promised throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. That this shall be no grief unto thee, nor offence of heart unto my lord, either that thou hast shed blood causeless, or that my lord hath avenged himself; but when the LORD shall have dealt well with my lord, then remember thine handmaid. Abigail kept this argument for the last, Henry notes, as very powerful with so good a man: the less he indulged his passion, the more he consulted his peace and his conscience's repose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. David said to Abigail, Blessed be the LORD God of Israel, which sent thee this day to meet me. David gave God thanks for sending him this happy check in a sinful way, Henry observes; whoever meets us with counsel, direction, comfort, caution, or seasonable reproof, we must see God sending them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. And blessed be thy advice, and blessed be thou, which hast kept me this day from coming to shed blood and from avenging myself with mine own hand. Most people think it enough to take reproof patiently, Henry says, but few will take it thankfully, commend those who give it, and accept it as a favour; the nearer we are to committing sin, the greater the mercy of a seasonable restraint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. For in very deed, as the LORD God of Israel liveth, which hath kept me back from hurting thee, except thou hadst hasted and come to meet me, surely there had not been left unto Nabal by the morning light any that pisseth against the wall. David owned how close he had come to the slaughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. So David received of her hand what she had brought him, and said, Go up in peace to thine house; see, I have hearkened to thy voice, and have accepted thy person. The gift was accepted, and the avenger became the protector. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. Abigail came to Nabal, and behold, he held a feast like the feast of a king, his heart merry, for he was very drunken, wherefore she told him nothing until the morning light. Sinners are often most secure when most in danger, Henry notes; he was very drunk, a sign he was Nabal, a fool who could not use plenty without abusing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. In the morning, when the wine was gone out of Nabal, his wife told him these things, and his heart died within him, and he became as a stone. His heart overnight merry with wine, next morning heavy as a stone, Henry reflects: so deceitful are carnal pleasures, so soon passes the laughter of the fool, and the end of that mirth is heaviness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. About ten days after, the LORD smote Nabal, that he died. Worldly sorrow, mortified pride, and an affrighted conscience, Henry says, sometimes end the joys of the sensualist, but whatever the weapon, the Lord smites men with death when it pleases him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. When David heard Nabal was dead, he said, Blessed be the LORD, that hath pleaded the cause of my reproach from Nabal's hand, and hath kept his servant from evil, for the LORD hath returned Nabal's wickedness upon his own head. And David sent and communed with Abigail, to take her to him to wife. David blessed God that he had been kept from killing Nabal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. When David's servants came to Abigail to Carmel, saying, David sent us unto thee, to take thee to him to wife — the proposal followed the deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 41. She arose, bowed herself on her face to the earth, and said, Behold, let thine handmaid be a servant to wash the feet of the servants of my lord. Abigail believed David would be king, Henry notes, and greatly esteemed his pious and excellent character; she deemed the proposal honourable and advantageous, notwithstanding his present difficulties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 42. Abigail hasted, arose, rode upon an ass, with five damsels after her, went after David's messengers, and became his wife. With great humility she consented, willing to share his trials; thus those who join themselves to Christ, Henry says, must be willing now to suffer with him, believing that hereafter they shall reign with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
