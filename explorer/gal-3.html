The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Galatians 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 O foolish Galatians, who hath bewitched you, that ye should not obey the truth, before whose eyes Jesus Christ hath been evidently set forth, crucified among you?

2 This only would I learn of you, Received ye the Spirit by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?

3 Are ye so foolish? having begun in the Spirit, are ye now made perfect by the flesh?

4 Have ye suffered so many things in vain? if it be yet in vain.

5 He therefore that ministereth to you the Spirit, and worketh miracles among you, doeth he it by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?

6 Even as Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness.

7 Know ye therefore that they which are of faith, the same are the children of Abraham.

8 And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through faith, preached before the gospel unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall all nations be blessed.

9 So then they which be of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham.

10 For as many as are of the works of the law are under the curse: for it is written, Cursed is every one that continueth not in all things which are written in the book of the law to do them.

11 But that no man is justified by the law in the sight of God, it is evident: for, The just shall live by faith.

12 And the law is not of faith: but, The man that doeth them shall live in them.

13 Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:

14 That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ; that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.

15 Brethren, I speak after the manner of men; Though it be but a man’s covenant, yet if it be confirmed, no man disannulleth, or addeth thereto.

16 Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.

17 And this I say, that the covenant, that was confirmed before of God in Christ, the law, which was four hundred and thirty years after, cannot disannul, that it should make the promise of none effect.

18 For if the inheritance be of the law, it is no more of promise: but God gave it to Abraham by promise.

19 Wherefore then serveth the law? It was added because of transgressions, till the seed should come to whom the promise was made; and it was ordained by angels in the hand of a mediator.

20 Now a mediator is not a mediator of one, but God is one.

21 Is the law then against the promises of God? God forbid: for if there had been a law given which could have given life, verily righteousness should have been by the law.

22 But the scripture hath concluded all under sin, that the promise by faith of Jesus Christ might be given to them that believe.

23 But before faith came, we were kept under the law, shut up unto the faith which should afterwards be revealed.

24 Wherefore the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ, that we might be justified by faith.

25 But after that faith is come, we are no longer under a schoolmaster.

26 For ye are all the children of God by faith in Christ Jesus.

27 For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ.

28 There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus.

29 And if ye be Christ’s, then are ye Abraham’s seed, and heirs according to the promise.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul argues from the Galatians' own experience: they received the Spirit by hearing with faith, not by works of law. Abraham believed God and was counted righteous, so those of faith are Abraham's children; Christ bore the law's curse, and the law served meanwhile as a schoolmaster leading to Christ, after which there is neither Jew nor Greek, slave nor free, male nor female in Christ. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul calls them foolish Galatians, bewitched from obeying the truth, though Jesus Christ had been evidently set forth crucified before their eyes in preaching and in the Lord's Supper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He asks only this: did they receive the Spirit by works of the law or by hearing of faith, since God owned not the ministry of the law but the doctrine of faith with tokens of favor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Are they so foolish, having begun in the Spirit, to now be made perfect by the flesh, turning from the ministry blessed to their spiritual advantage to useless distinctions? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Have they suffered so many things in vain, if it be yet in vain, a loss that would make their past sufferings pointless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He who ministers the Spirit and works miracles among them does so not by works of the law but by hearing of faith, as the fruits of the Spirit appear most evidently among preachers of faith, not of law-works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Even as Abraham believed God and it was accounted to him for righteousness, his faith fastening on God's word and promise, so justification by faith is no new doctrine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Therefore those of faith are the children of Abraham, blessed in the same way he was blessed, through faith in God's promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Scripture, foreseeing God would justify the heathen through faith, preached the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying all nations would be blessed in him, the Holy Spirit who indited Scripture foreseeing this. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. So then those of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham, sharing his blessing by the same means. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. As many as are of works of the law are under the curse, for it is written that cursed is everyone who does not continue in all things written in the law, and all have sinned and stand guilty before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. That no man is justified by the law in God's sight is evident, for the just shall live by faith, and only those freed from death and wrath and restored to God's favor are righteous, which happens only through faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The law is not of faith, but the man who does them shall live in them, so law and faith offer different terms, and mingling promise with law makes it nothing but law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us, made sin, a sin-offering, laid for a time under divine punishment, not separated from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ, and that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith, Christ from the cross freely invites sinners to take refuge in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Paul speaks after the manner of men: though it be but a man's covenant, once confirmed no man disannuls or adds to it, so much less can the law disannul God's covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. To Abraham and his seed were the promises made, not to seeds as of many, but as of one, to thy seed, which is Christ, who abides forever with his spiritual seed, his by faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The covenant confirmed before by God in Christ cannot be disannulled by the law given four hundred and thirty years afterward, so as to make the promise of no effect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. For if the inheritance is of the law, it is no more of promise, but God gave it to Abraham by promise, the gospel promises being first made to Christ and then by him to those ingrafted into Christ by faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Wherefore then serves the law? It was added because of transgressions, till the seed should come, to show the sinfulness of sin and lead men to see their need of the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Now a mediator is not a mediator of one, but God is one, the law being given by angels and Moses as mediator, while the promise was given by God himself, so the law could not be designed to set the promise aside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Is the law then against God's promises? God forbid, for if a law had been given which could give life, righteousness would indeed have come by law, but the holy, just, good law tends every way to promote the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Scripture has concluded all under sin, that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe, convincing them of guilt and of the law's insufficiency. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Before faith came, they were kept under the law, shut up unto the faith afterwards revealed, like children led to school by servants who have charge of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Wherefore the law was their schoolmaster to bring them to Christ, that they might be justified by faith, pointing by its rites and sacrifices to the one through whom alone pardon comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. But after faith has come, they are no longer under a schoolmaster, enjoying a clearer discovery of divine grace than the Jews of old. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. For all are children of God by faith in Christ Jesus, no longer servants kept at a distance under restraints, but sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. As many as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ, professing to be his disciples, baptized into his death to die to sin and walk in newness of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. There is neither Jew nor Greek, bond nor free, male nor female, for all are one in Christ Jesus, the putting on of Christ consisting not in outward imitation but in a new birth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And if they are Christ's, then they are Abraham's seed and heirs according to the promise, and he who makes believers heirs will provide for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • schoolmaster. a tutor or guardian guiding a child, here the law guiding its hearers toward Christ (verse 24) Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
