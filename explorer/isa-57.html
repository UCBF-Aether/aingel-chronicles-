The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 57 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The righteous perisheth, and no man layeth it to heart: and merciful men are taken away, none considering that the righteous is taken away from the evil to come.

2 He shall enter into peace: they shall rest in their beds, each one walking in his uprightness.

3 But draw near hither, ye sons of the sorceress, the seed of the adulterer and the whore.

4 Against whom do ye sport yourselves? against whom make ye a wide mouth, and draw out the tongue? are ye not children of transgression, a seed of falsehood,

5 Enflaming yourselves with idols under every green tree, slaying the children in the valleys under the clifts of the rocks?

6 Among the smooth stones of the stream is thy portion; they, they are thy lot: even to them hast thou poured a drink offering, thou hast offered a meat offering. Should I receive comfort in these?

7 Upon a lofty and high mountain hast thou set thy bed: even thither wentest thou up to offer sacrifice.

8 Behind the doors also and the posts hast thou set up thy remembrance: for thou hast discovered thyself to another than me, and art gone up; thou hast enlarged thy bed, and made thee a covenant with them; thou lovedst their bed where thou sawest it.

9 And thou wentest to the king with ointment, and didst increase thy perfumes, and didst send thy messengers far off, and didst debase thyself even unto hell.

10 Thou art wearied in the greatness of thy way; yet saidst thou not, There is no hope: thou hast found the life of thine hand; therefore thou wast not grieved.

11 And of whom hast thou been afraid or feared, that thou hast lied, and hast not remembered me, nor laid it to thy heart? have not I held my peace even of old, and thou fearest me not?

12 I will declare thy righteousness, and thy works; for they shall not profit thee.

13 When thou criest, let thy companies deliver thee; but the wind shall carry them all away; vanity shall take them: but he that putteth his trust in me shall possess the land, and shall inherit my holy mountain;

14 And shall say, Cast ye up, cast ye up, prepare the way, take up the stumblingblock out of the way of my people.

15 For thus saith the high and lofty One that inhabiteth eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place, with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the spirit of the humble, and to revive the heart of the contrite ones.

16 For I will not contend for ever, neither will I be always wroth: for the spirit should fail before me, and the souls which I have made.

17 For the iniquity of his covetousness was I wroth, and smote him: I hid me, and was wroth, and he went on frowardly in the way of his heart.

18 I have seen his ways, and will heal him: I will lead him also, and restore comforts unto him and to his mourners.

19 I create the fruit of the lips; Peace, peace to him that is far off, and to him that is near, saith the LORD; and I will heal him.

20 But the wicked are like the troubled sea, when it cannot rest, whose waters cast up mire and dirt.

21 There is no peace, saith my God, to the wicked.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The righteous perish and no one lays it to heart, taken away from the evil to come, while the many run to idolatry on every high hill. God dwells with the contrite and humble, healing and creating peace for those far off and near — but there is no peace, says God, to the wicked, who are like the troubled sea that cannot rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The righteous perishes, and no man lays it to heart, and merciful men are taken away, none considering that the righteous is taken away from the evil to come. This chapter treats the peaceful death of the righteous few, the ungodliness of the many, and how a believing remnant shall survive the general judgments of the nation, and be restored by Him Who creates peace. In the midst of the excesses of the unfaithful watchmen in chapter 56 verses 10 to 12, most of the few that are godly perish: partly by vexation at the prevailing ungodliness, partly by violent death in persecution, prophetical of the persecuting times of Manasseh, before God's judgments in causing the captivity in Babylon, and again those in the last age of the church, before the final judgments on the apostasy, as in 2 Kings, Matthew, and Revelation. The Hebrew for perisheth, and is taken away, expresses a violent death, as in Micah. No man layeth it to heart means as a public calamity. Merciful men are rather, godly men, the subjects of mercy. None considering means namely, what was the design of Providence in removing the godly. From the evil is Hebrew, from the face of the evil, that is, both from the moral evil on every side in chapter 56 verses 10 to 12, and from the evils about to come in punishment of the national sins, foreign invasions, etc., as in chapter 56 verse 9 and verse 13. So Ahijah's death is represented as a blessing conferred on him by God for his piety, as in 1 Kings and 2 Kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He will enter into peace; they will rest in their beds, each one walking in his uprightness. Or, he entereth into peace, in contrast to the persecutions which he suffered in this world, as in Job. The margin not so well translates, he shall go in peace, as in Psalm 37 and Luke. Rest means the calm rest of their bodies in their graves, called beds, as in 2 Chronicles, compare chapter 14, because they sleep in them, with the certainty of awakening at the resurrection, as in 1 Thessalonians, is the emblem of the eternal rest, as in Hebrews and Revelation. Each one walking in uprightness means this clause defines the character of those who at death rest in their beds, namely, all who walk uprightly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. But draw near hither, you sons of the sorceress, the seed of the adulterer and the whore. Ye means in contrast to the righteous and their end, he announces to the unbelieving Jews their doom. Sons of the sorceress means that is, ye that are addicted to sorcery: this was connected with the worship of false gods, as in 2 Kings. No insult is greater to an Oriental than any slur cast on his mother, as in 1 Samuel and Job. Seed of the adulterer means spiritual adultery is meant: idolatry and apostasy, as in Matthew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Against whom do you sport yourselves? Against whom do you make a wide mouth, and draw out the tongue? Are you not children of transgression, a seed of falsehood? Sport yourselves means make a mock, as in chapter 66. Are ye aware of the glory of Him whom you mock, by mocking His servants, the righteous in verse 1, as in 2 Chronicles? Make wide mouth recalls Psalms and Lamentations. Children of transgression, etc., means not merely children of transgressors, and a seed of false parents, but of transgression and falsehood itself, utterly unfaithful to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Enflaming yourselves with idols under every green tree, slaying the children in the valleys under the clifts of the rocks. Enflaming yourselves means burning with lust towards idols, or else, compare the margin, in the terebinth groves, which the Hebrew and the parallelism favour, as in chapter 1. Under tree recalls 2 Kings: the tree, as in the Assyrian sculptures, was probably made an idolatrous symbol of the heavenly hosts. Slaying children means as a sacrifice to Molech, etc., as in 2 Kings and 2 Chronicles. In valleys means the valley of the son of Hinnom. Fire was put within a hollow brazen statue, and the child was put in his heated arms; kettle drums, Hebrew, toph, were beaten to drown the child's cries; whence the valley was called Tophet, as in 2 Chronicles and Jeremiah. Under clifts means the gloom of caverns suiting their dark superstitions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Among the smooth stones of the stream is your portion; they, they are your lot; even to them have you poured a drink offering, you have offered a meat offering. Should I receive comfort in these? The smooth stones, shaped as idols, are the gods chosen by thee as thy portion, as in Psalm 16. Meat offering means not a bloody sacrifice, but one of meal and flour mingled with oil. Meat in Old English meant food, not flesh, as it means now, as in Leviticus. Should I receive comfort is rather, Shall I bear these things with patience? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Upon a lofty and high mountain have you set your bed; even thither you went up to offer sacrifice. High mountain, bed is an image from adultery, open and shameless, as in Ezekiel; the bed answers to the idolatrous altar, the scene of their spiritual unfaithfulness to their divine husband, as in Ezekiel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Behind the doors also and the posts have you set up your remembrance, for you have discovered yourself to another than me, and are gone up; you have enlarged your bed, and made you a covenant with them; you loved their bed where you saw it. Remembrance means that is, memorials of thy idolatry: the objects which thou holdest in remembrance. They hung up household tutelary gods behind the doors, the very place where God has directed them to write His laws on the posts and gates, as in Deuteronomy; a curse, too, was pronounced on putting up an image in a secret place, as in Deuteronomy. Discovered thyself is an image from an adulteress. Enlarged bed means so as to receive the more paramours. Made covenant means with idols: in open violation of thy covenant with God, as in Exodus. Or, hast made assignations with them for thyself. Thy bed, their bed means the Jews' sin was twofold; they resorted to places of idolatry, their bed, and they received idols into the temple of God, thy bed. Where is rather, ever since that. The Hebrew for where means room, margin, a place; therefore, translate, thou hast provided a place for it, for their bed, namely, by admitting idolatrous altars in thy land; or thou choosest a convenient place for thyself in their bed, as in chapter 56. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. You went to the king with ointment, and did increase your perfumes, and did send your messengers far off, and did debase yourself even unto hell. The king means the idol which they came to worship, perfumed with oil, like harlots, as in Jeremiah and Ezekiel. So king means idol, as in Amos and Zephaniah, malcham meaning king. Rather, the king of Assyria or Egypt, and other foreign princes, on whom Israel relied, instead of on God; the ointment will thus refer to the presents, as in Hosea, and perhaps the compliances with foreigners' idolatries, whereby Israel sought to gain their favour, as in chapters 30 and Ezekiel and Hosea. Send messengers far off means not merely to neighbouring nations, but to those far off, in search of new idols, or else alliances. Even unto hell means the lowest possible degradation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. You are wearied in the greatness of your way, yet you said not: There is no hope; you have found the life of your hand; therefore you were not grieved. Way means the length of thy journey in seeking strange gods, or else foreign aid, as in Jeremiah. Notwithstanding thy deriving no good from these long journeys, so, send far off in verse 9, thou dost not still give up hope, as in Jeremiah. Hast found life of hand means for thou still findest life, that is, vigour, enough in thy hand to make new idols, or to seek new alliance, hand being then taken for strength in general. Grieved is rather, therefore thou art not weak, inasmuch as having life in thy hand, thou art still strong in hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And of whom have you been afraid or feared, that you have lied, and have not remembered me, nor laid it to your heart? Have not I held my peace even of old, and you fear me not? Israel wished not to seem altogether to have denied God. Therefore they lied to Him. God asks, Why dost thou do so? Whom dost thou fear? Certainly not Me, for thou hast not remembered Me. Translate, seeing that thou hast not remembered Me. Laid it to heart is rather, nor hast Me at heart; hast no regard for Me; and that, because I have been long silent and have not punished thee. Literally, Have I not held My peace, and that for long? and so thou fearest Me not, as in Psalm 50 and Ecclesiastes. It would be better openly to renounce God, than to flatter Him with lies of false professions, as in Psalm 78. However, chapters 51 verses 12 and 13 favour the English version of the whole verse; God's silent long-suffering, which was intended to lead them to repentance, caused them not to fear Him, as in Romans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. I will declare your righteousness, and your works, for they will not profit you. Declare means I will expose publicly thy hypocritical righteousness. I will show openly how vain thy works, in having recourse to idols, or foreign alliances, shall prove, as in verse 3. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. When you cry, let your companies deliver you, but the wind will carry them all away; vanity will take them, but he that puts his trust in me will possess the land, and will inherit my holy mountain. When thou criest means in the time of thy trouble. Companies means namely, of idols, collected by thee from every quarter; or else, of foreigners, summoned to thy aid. Wind carry away recalls Job and Matthew. Vanity is rather, a breath. Possess land, inherit means that is, the literal land of Judea and Mount Zion; the believing remnant of Israel shall return and inherit the land. Secondarily, the heavenly inheritance, and the spiritual Zion, as in chapters 49 and Psalms and Matthew and Hebrews. He that putteth his trust in Me, of whatever extraction, shall succeed to the spiritual patrimony of the apostate Jew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. And will say: Cast up, cast up, prepare the way, take up the stumblingblock out of the way of my people. Shall say means the nominative is, He that trusteth in Me in verse 13. The believing remnant shall have every obstacle to their return cleared out of the way, at the coming restoration of Israel, the antitype to the return from Babylon, as in chapters 35, 40, and 62. Cast up means a high road before the returning Jews. Stumbling-block means Jesus had been so to the Jews, but will not be so then any longer, as in 1 Corinthians; their prejudices shall then be taken out of the way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. For thus says the high and lofty One that inhabits eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place, with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the spirit of the humble, and to revive the heart of the contrite ones. The pride and self-righteousness of the Jews were the stumbling block in the way of their acknowledging Christ. The contrition of Israel in the last days shall be attended with God's interposition in their behalf. So their self-humiliation, as in chapter 66, precedes their final prosperity, as in Zechariah; there will, probably, be a previous period of unbelief even after their return, as in Zechariah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. For I will not contend for ever, neither will I be always angry, for the spirit should fail before me, and the souls which I have made. For refers to the promise in verses 14 and 15, of restoring Israel when contrite, as in Genesis and Psalms and Micah. God will not contend for ever with His people, for their human spirit would thereby be utterly crushed, whereas God's object is to chasten, not to destroy them, as in Lamentations and Micah. With the ungodly He is angry every day, as in Psalm 7 and Revelation. Spirit before me means that is, the human spirit which went forth from Me, as in Numbers, answering to which I have made in the parallel clause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For the iniquity of his covetousness was I angry, and smote him; I hid me, and was angry, and he went on frowardly in the way of his heart. Covetousness means akin to idolatry; and, like it, having drawn off Israel's heart from God, as in chapters 2, 56, and 58, Jeremiah, and Colossians. Hid me recalls chapters 8 and 45. Went on frowardly means the result of God's hiding His face, as in Psalm 81 and Romans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. I have seen his ways, and will heal him; I will lead him also, and restore comforts unto him and to his mourners. Rather, I have seen his ways in sin, yet will I heal him, that is, restore Israel spiritually and temporally, as in Jeremiah and Hosea. I will restore comforts unto him and to his mourners means however, the phrase, his mourners, favours the English version; his ways will thus be his ways of repentance; and God's pardon on seeing them answers to the like promise, as in chapters 61 and Jeremiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. I create the fruit of the lips; Peace, peace to him that is far off, and to him that is near, says the Lord, and I will heal him. Lips means that is, thanksgivings which flow from the lips. I make men to return thanks to Me, as in Hosea and Hebrews. Peace, peace means perfect peace, as in chapter 26 and John. Primarily, the cessation of the troubles now afflicting the Jews, as formerly, under the Babylonian exile. More generally, the peace which the Gospel proclaims both to Israel that is near, and to the Gentiles who are far off, as in Acts and Ephesians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. But the wicked are like the troubled sea, when it cannot rest, whose waters cast up mire and dirt. When it cannot rest is rather, for it can have no rest, as in Job and Proverbs. The English version represents the sea as occasionally agitated; but the Hebrew expresses that it can never be at rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. There is no peace, says my God, to the wicked. This recalls chapters 48 and 2 Kings. My God means the prophet, having God as his God, speaks in the person of Israel, prophetically regarded as having now appropriated God and His peace, as in chapter 11, warning the impenitent that, while they continue so, they can have no peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 14. And will say: Cast up, cast up, prepare the way, take up the stumblingblock out of the way of my people. Shall say means the nominative is, He that trusteth in Me in verse 13. The believing remnant shall have every obstacle to their return cleared out of the way, at the coming restoration of Israel, the antitype to the return from Babylon, as in chapters 35, 40, and 62. Cast up means a high road before the returning Jews. Stumbling-block means Jesus had been so to the Jews, but will not be so then any longer, as in 1 Corinthians; their prejudices shall then be taken out of the way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. For thus says the high and lofty One that inhabits eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place, with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the spirit of the humble, and to revive the heart of the contrite ones. The pride and self-righteousness of the Jews were the stumbling block in the way of their acknowledging Christ. The contrition of Israel in the last days shall be attended with God's interposition in their behalf. So their self-humiliation, as in chapter 66, precedes their final prosperity, as in Zechariah; there will, probably, be a previous period of unbelief even after their return, as in Zechariah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. For I will not contend for ever, neither will I be always angry, for the spirit should fail before me, and the souls which I have made. For refers to the promise in verses 14 and 15, of restoring Israel when contrite, as in Genesis and Psalms and Micah. God will not contend for ever with His people, for their human spirit would thereby be utterly crushed, whereas God's object is to chasten, not to destroy them, as in Lamentations and Micah. With the ungodly He is angry every day, as in Psalm 7 and Revelation. Spirit before me means that is, the human spirit which went forth from Me, as in Numbers, answering to which I have made in the parallel clause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For the iniquity of his covetousness was I angry, and smote him; I hid me, and was angry, and he went on frowardly in the way of his heart. Covetousness means akin to idolatry; and, like it, having drawn off Israel's heart from God, as in chapters 2, 56, and 58, Jeremiah, and Colossians. Hid me recalls chapters 8 and 45. Went on frowardly means the result of God's hiding His face, as in Psalm 81 and Romans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. I have seen his ways, and will heal him; I will lead him also, and restore comforts unto him and to his mourners. Rather, I have seen his ways in sin, yet will I heal him, that is, restore Israel spiritually and temporally, as in Jeremiah and Hosea. I will restore comforts unto him and to his mourners means however, the phrase, his mourners, favours the English version; his ways will thus be his ways of repentance; and God's pardon on seeing them answers to the like promise, as in chapters 61 and Jeremiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. I create the fruit of the lips; Peace, peace to him that is far off, and to him that is near, says the Lord, and I will heal him. Lips means that is, thanksgivings which flow from the lips. I make men to return thanks to Me, as in Hosea and Hebrews. Peace, peace means perfect peace, as in chapter 26 and John. Primarily, the cessation of the troubles now afflicting the Jews, as formerly, under the Babylonian exile. More generally, the peace which the Gospel proclaims both to Israel that is near, and to the Gentiles who are far off, as in Acts and Ephesians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. But the wicked are like the troubled sea, when it cannot rest, whose waters cast up mire and dirt. When it cannot rest is rather, for it can have no rest, as in Job and Proverbs. The English version represents the sea as occasionally agitated; but the Hebrew expresses that it can never be at rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. There is no peace, says my God, to the wicked. This recalls chapters 48 and 2 Kings. My God means the prophet, having God as his God, speaks in the person of Israel, prophetically regarded as having now appropriated God and His peace, as in chapter 11, warning the impenitent that, while they continue so, they can have no peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
