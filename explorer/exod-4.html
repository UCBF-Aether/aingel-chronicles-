The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Moses answered and said, But, behold, they will not believe me, nor hearken unto my voice: for they will say, The LORD hath not appeared unto thee.

2 And the LORD said unto him, What is that in thine hand? And he said, A rod.

3 And he said, Cast it on the ground. And he cast it on the ground, and it became a serpent; and Moses fled from before it.

4 And the LORD said unto Moses, Put forth thine hand, and take it by the tail. And he put forth his hand, and caught it, and it became a rod in his hand:

5 That they may believe that the LORD God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, hath appeared unto thee.

6 And the LORD said furthermore unto him, Put now thine hand into thy bosom. And he put his hand into his bosom: and when he took it out, behold, his hand was leprous as snow.

7 And he said, Put thine hand into thy bosom again. And he put his hand into his bosom again; and plucked it out of his bosom, and, behold, it was turned again as his other flesh.

8 And it shall come to pass, if they will not believe thee, neither hearken to the voice of the first sign, that they will believe the voice of the latter sign.

9 And it shall come to pass, if they will not believe also these two signs, neither hearken unto thy voice, that thou shalt take of the water of the river, and pour it upon the dry land: and the water which thou takest out of the river shall become blood upon the dry land.

10 And Moses said unto the LORD, O my LORD, I am not eloquent, neither heretofore, nor since thou hast spoken unto thy servant: but I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue.

11 And the LORD said unto him, Who hath made man’s mouth? or who maketh the dumb, or deaf, or the seeing, or the blind? have not I the LORD?

12 Now therefore go, and I will be with thy mouth, and teach thee what thou shalt say.

13 And he said, O my LORD, send, I pray thee, by the hand of him whom thou wilt send.

14 And the anger of the LORD was kindled against Moses, and he said, Is not Aaron the Levite thy brother? I know that he can speak well. And also, behold, he cometh forth to meet thee: and when he seeth thee, he will be glad in his heart.

15 And thou shalt speak unto him, and put words in his mouth: and I will be with thy mouth, and with his mouth, and will teach you what ye shall do.

16 And he shall be thy spokesman unto the people: and he shall be, even he shall be to thee instead of a mouth, and thou shalt be to him instead of God.

17 And thou shalt take this rod in thine hand, wherewith thou shalt do signs.

18 And Moses went and returned to Jethro his father in law, and said unto him, Let me go, I pray thee, and return unto my brethren which are in Egypt, and see whether they be yet alive. And Jethro said to Moses, Go in peace.

19 And the LORD said unto Moses in Midian, Go, return into Egypt: for all the men are dead which sought thy life.

20 And Moses took his wife and his sons, and set them upon an ass, and he returned to the land of Egypt: and Moses took the rod of God in his hand.

21 And the LORD said unto Moses, When thou goest to return into Egypt, see that thou do all those wonders before Pharaoh, which I have put in thine hand: but I will harden his heart, that he shall not let the people go.

22 And thou shalt say unto Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD, Israel is my son, even my firstborn:

23 And I say unto thee, Let my son go, that he may serve me: and if thou refuse to let him go, behold, I will slay thy son, even thy firstborn.

24 And it came to pass by the way in the inn, that the LORD met him, and sought to kill him.

25 Then Zipporah took a sharp stone, and cut off the foreskin of her son, and cast it at his feet, and said, Surely a bloody husband art thou to me.

26 So he let him go: then she said, A bloody husband thou art, because of the circumcision.

27 And the LORD said to Aaron, Go into the wilderness to meet Moses. And he went, and met him in the mount of God, and kissed him.

28 And Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD who had sent him, and all the signs which he had commanded him.

29 And Moses and Aaron went and gathered together all the elders of the children of Israel:

30 And Aaron spake all the words which the LORD had spoken unto Moses, and did the signs in the sight of the people.

31 And the people believed: and when they heard that the LORD had visited the children of Israel, and that he had looked upon their affliction, then they bowed their heads and worshipped.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God gives Moses signs to carry back to Israel: a rod that becomes a serpent, a hand made leprous and restored, and water turned to blood. Moses still pleads slow speech, so Aaron is appointed to speak for him. On the way back, after a dangerous encounter at an inn, Aaron meets Moses at the mount of God, and the elders of Israel believe the signs. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Moses objected that Israel would not believe him or hearken to his voice, saying the LORD had not appeared to him. Henry notes this distrust followed hard upon God's promise, showing how weak faith clings to difficulties even after clear assurances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The LORD asked what was in Moses's hand, and he answered, A rod, the shepherd's staff of his ordinary calling. Henry observes God commonly works through the mean things in our hands rather than through what we do not have. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Cast on the ground, the rod became a serpent, and Moses fled from before it. Henry reads the sign as proof that God could turn Moses's own staff into an instrument of power, and that even the chosen messenger feared his first wonder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Bidden to take the serpent by the tail, Moses caught it and it became a rod again in his hand. Henry notes obedience in the face of fear was the condition of the wonder, and the restored rod showed God's power to govern what he had made fearful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The sign was given that Israel might believe the LORD God of their fathers had appeared unto Moses. Henry stresses the signs were not for display but to confirm the messenger's commission to a doubting people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Put into his bosom, Moses's hand came out leprous as snow. Henry reads the leprosy as an emblem of sin's defilement and of the power that would both smite Egypt and heal Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Put into his bosom again, the hand came out restored as his other flesh. Henry notes the second half of the sign: the same hand that smites can heal, and God's judgments carry mercy within them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. If Israel would not believe the first sign, they would believe the voice of the latter sign. Henry observes God gives line upon line to unbelief, multiplying evidence rather than abandoning his people to their doubts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. If neither sign persuaded them, Moses was to take Nile water and pour it on dry land, where it would become blood. Henry notes the third sign struck at Egypt's river-god in advance, a warning of the plagues to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Moses pleaded he was not eloquent, but slow of speech and of a slow tongue, neither before nor since God spoke to him. Henry calls this a modest but unbelieving excuse: God had not promised eloquence, but his presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The LORD answered, Who hath made man's mouth, or maketh the dumb, the deaf, the seeing, or the blind? Henry notes the Creator of speech can supply speech, and infirmity is no bar to a divine commission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Go, said God, and I will be with thy mouth and teach thee what thou shalt say. Henry observes this promise answers every excuse at once: God's teaching, not man's readiness, makes the messenger sufficient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Moses still prayed, Send by the hand of him whom thou wilt send, in effect declining the work. Henry marks this as the excuse that crossed from humility into unbelief, and it kindled the LORD's anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The LORD's anger was kindled, yet he gave Aaron the Levite as spokesman, who was coming to meet Moses and would be glad in his heart. Henry notes mercy still mingled with rebuke: Moses lost some honour, but gained a helper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Moses was to speak to Aaron and put words in his mouth, while God would be with both their mouths and teach them what to do. Henry observes the partnership: Moses received the word, Aaron uttered it, and God owned them both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Aaron would be Moses's spokesman to the people, instead of a mouth, while Moses would be to him instead of God. Henry notes the order preserved: the prophet hears from God, and the people hear from the prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Moses was to take the rod in his hand, wherewith he would do signs. Henry observes the shepherd's staff, once a sign of his lowly calling, was now consecrated as the instrument of wonders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Moses returned to Jethro and asked leave to go back to his brethren in Egypt to see whether they were yet alive. Henry notes he sought his father-in-law's consent with courtesy, and concealed nothing by fraud, though he did not yet declare the whole commission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The LORD told Moses in Midian to return to Egypt, for all the men who sought his life were dead. Henry observes providence had cleared the way: the old king and his court were gone before the deliverer set out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Moses took his wife and sons upon an ass and returned to Egypt, carrying the rod of God in his hand. Henry notes he went in the same humble manner as he had fled, but now bearing the token of divine authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. God warned Moses to do all the wonders before Pharaoh, yet said he would harden Pharaoh's heart so he would not let the people go. Henry explains the hardening as God leaving Pharaoh to the hardness he had chosen, and overruling it for greater glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Moses was to tell Pharaoh, Israel is my son, even my firstborn. Henry notes the claim grounded the demand: Israel was God's adopted firstborn, dear to him above other nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Let my son go that he may serve me, said the LORD, and if Pharaoh refused, God would slay his firstborn son. Henry observes the warning matched the crime: Pharaoh who slew Israel's sons would lose his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. By the way at the inn, the LORD met Moses and sought to kill him, because his son was uncircumcised. Henry reads this as a sharp rebuke: the lawgiver himself must keep the covenant sign before he could carry God's message. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Zipporah took a sharp stone, circumcised her son, and cast the foreskin at Moses's feet, saying, A bloody husband thou art to me. Henry notes her reluctant act removed the offence, and the danger passed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Then the LORD let him go, and Zipporah repeated, A bloody husband thou art, because of the circumcision. Henry observes the sharp saying marked her distaste for the rite, though she had performed it to save her husband's life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The LORD told Aaron to go into the wilderness to meet Moses, and he met him in the mount of God and kissed him. Henry notes the brothers' meeting was itself a providence, joining the prophet and the spokesman at the very place of the call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD and all the signs he had commanded. Henry observes the commission was shared in full, so Aaron spoke not his own words but the words given to Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Moses and Aaron gathered all the elders of Israel together. Henry notes the orderly course: the elders first, as representatives, before the whole congregation was addressed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Aaron spoke all the words the LORD had spoken to Moses and did the signs in the sight of the people. Henry observes the signs did their appointed work, confirming the word to those who heard it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. The people believed, and when they heard the LORD had visited Israel and looked upon their affliction, they bowed their heads and worshipped. Henry closes on the happy answer to Moses's fear: the people who he thought would not believe, believed and worshipped. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
