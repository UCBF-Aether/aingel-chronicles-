The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 75 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Unto thee, O God, do we give thanks, unto thee do we give thanks: for that thy name is near thy wondrous works declare.

2 When I shall receive the congregation I will judge uprightly.

3 The earth and all the inhabitants thereof are dissolved: I bear up the pillars of it. Selah.

4 I said unto the fools, Deal not foolishly: and to the wicked, Lift not up the horn:

5 Lift not up your horn on high: speak not with a stiff neck.

6 For promotion cometh neither from the east, nor from the west, nor from the south.

7 But God is the judge: he putteth down one, and setteth up another.

8 For in the hand of the LORD there is a cup, and the wine is red; it is full of mixture; and he poureth out of the same: but the dregs thereof, all the wicked of the earth shall wring them out, and drink them.

9 But I will declare for ever; I will sing praises to the God of Jacob.

10 All the horns of the wicked also will I cut off; but the horns of the righteous shall be exalted.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Thanks, and God's cup: name near, wondrous works declared. God speaks, when he receives the congregation he will judge uprightly, bearing earth's pillars though all dissolve. Fools are bidden not lift the horn, for promotion comes not from east, west, or south, but God the judge puts down one and sets up another. In his hand a cup of red, mixed wine poured out for the wicked to drain, while the speaker declares for ever and sings to Jacob's God, cutting off wicked horns and exalting the righteous. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Unto you, O God, do we give thanks, unto you do we give thanks, for that your name is near, your wondrous works declare: thanks twice given, name near, wondrous works declaring — God's nearness is proved by his works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When I shall receive the congregation I will judge uprightly: God speaks — when he receives the congregation, he will judge uprightly; judgment waits for God's appointed time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The earth and all its inhabitants are dissolved; I bear up the pillars of it: earth dissolved, inhabitants dissolved, yet God bears up its pillars — the Judge upholds the world he judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. I said unto the fools, Deal not foolishly, and to the wicked, Lift not up the horn: fools bidden not deal foolishly, wicked bidden not lift the horn — the horn is power proudly raised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Lift not up your horn on high; speak not with a stiff neck: horn not lifted on high, no stiff-necked speech — pride's posture and pride's speech are both forbidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For promotion comes neither from the east, nor from the west, nor from the south: promotion from neither east, west, nor south — the compass is searched and promotion is found in none of its quarters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But God is the judge; he puts down one, and sets up another: God the judge puts down and sets up — promotion's source is judgment, not geography. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For in the hand of the Lord there is a cup, and the wine is red; it is full of mixture; and he pours out of the same; but the dregs thereof, all the wicked of the earth shall wring them out, and drink them: the Lord's hand holds a cup, red wine full of mixture, poured out — and the dregs, wrung out and drunk by all the earth's wicked, are judgment's bitter end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. But I will declare for ever; I will sing praises to the God of Jacob: declaring for ever, singing praises to Jacob's God — the psalmist's answer to God's judgment is endless declaration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. All the horns of the wicked also will I cut off, but the horns of the righteous shall be exalted: wicked horns cut off, righteous horns exalted — God's judgment, promised in verse 7, is completed in the cutting and exalting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
