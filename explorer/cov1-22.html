The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Book of the Covenant 22 (J. Cooper & A. J. Maclean, The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1902), public domain (archive.org cu31924029296170))

1 Let him be constant at the altar; in prayers let him be persistent day and night, especially at the obligatory times of night; at the first hour, at midnight, and at early twilight when the star of the dawn riseth. Then also in the morning,’ at the third, sixth, ninth [hours, and the] twelfth hour at the lamp [lighting].

2 If also at every hour he offer prayers without ceasing for the people and for himself, he doeth well. Let him abide in the house of the Church alone. If he have one or two likeminded with himself, it is good that he should be with them for united supplication in unison?

3 For where two or three are gathered together in My Name, ye know that I have said unto you that I am in the midst of them. But if he cannot abide all night long, yet let him remain these hours that I have said. For then the angels visit the Church.

4 Let him fast three days each [week] all the year. But for three weeks after his appointment let him maintain the fast according to the number of the eighteen Exalted Entrances by which the Only-begotten passed when He came to the passion.

5 But on the first day of the week only let him feed on bread with oil and honey and salt, and all fruits of trees; but let him in no wise taste wine, except only the cup of the Offering. This let him use whether ill or well.

6 For it is good that this be for the priests only.’ And so after these weeks all the year, let him fast three days each [week]; and for the rest of the time let him fast according to his strength.

7 But in no wise let him eat meat, not because if he taste or eat [meat] he is to be blamed, but because when he loveth infirmity these strong meats are not fitting, and in order that he may watch.

8 Let the Offering only be on Saturday, or on the first day of the week, and on a fast-day.

9 On the eve let him instruct and teach these things in the manner of a mystery to those whom he hath tested as having ears to hear’ But if he be sick in body, let him quickly take care to heal himself, feeding on fish, and constantly [taking] a little wine of the Holy thing, that the Church may not also come to an end because he is lying sick ; but [that] those who learn may receive joy.

10 But when teaching in the Church, let him speak thus carefully, as a man who knoweth that he is speaking for a testimony the doctrine of all the ministry of the Father of all, that [doctrine] which is accurately written. Let him say all these things—all those which he accurately knoweth and remembereth of old. For if he knoweth what he saith, he may have hope that his hearers also [will] have known these things.

11 And with all his labour, let him beseech the Lord, so that his word may bring forth the fruits’ of the Holy Spirit in them that hear.

12 Let him do everything in order, and with knowledge. Let him dismiss the catechumens after he hath admonished them with meditations and admonitions of the Prophets and Apostles with instructive words, so that they may know Him whom they confess.

13 But let him teach the faithful after the manner of a mystery, having first dismissed the catechumens ; and after the instruction in the mysteries let him offer, so that knowing in what mystery they are taking part, they may offer with fear.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The bishop is to be constant at the altar and persistent in prayer day and night, especially at the obligatory night hours — the first hour, midnight, early twilight when the dawn star rises — and in the morning, at the third, sixth, ninth and twelfth hours at lamp-lighting; if he offers prayer at every hour without ceasing for the people and himself, he does well. He is to abide in the house of the Church alone, or with one or two likeminded for united supplication, for where two or three are gathered in His Name, He is in the midst of them; if he cannot abide all night, he is to remain at the hours named, for then the angels visit the Church. He is to fast three days each week all the year, and for three weeks after his appointment according to the number of the eighteen Exalted Entrances by which the Only-begotten passed when He came to the passion. On the first day of the week only he is to feed on bread with oil and honey and salt and all fruits of trees, tasting no wine except the cup of the Offering, whether ill or well; and in no wise is he to eat meat — not because tasting it is blameworthy, but because strong meats are not fitting when he loves infirmity, and that he may watch. The Offering is only on Saturday, the first day of the week, or a fast-day. On the eve he is to instruct in the manner of a mystery those tested as having ears to hear; if sick in body, he is quickly to heal himself, feeding on fish and constantly taking a little wine of the Holy thing, lest the Church come to an end through his sickness. When teaching, he is to speak carefully, as one speaking for a testimony the doctrine of all the ministry of the Father, all that is accurately written and all he accurately knows and remembers of old, and to beseech the Lord that his word may bring forth the fruits of the Holy Spirit in his hearers. He is to do everything in order and with knowledge, dismissing the catechumens after admonishing them with meditations and admonitions of the Prophets and Apostles, and teaching the faithful after the manner of a mystery only after the catechumens are dismissed, then offering, so that knowing in what mystery they take part, they may offer with fear.
