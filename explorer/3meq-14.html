Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The speaker opens grieved over four groups who believe not the resurrection of the dead — Judahites, Samaritans, Pharisees, and Sadducees — and reports each position in turn: the Judahites, “Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die”; the Samaritans, flesh shall become dust and not rise; the Pharisees, resurrection yes, but souls clothed in heaven with another flesh than on earth; the Sadducees, no resurrection at all, neither soul nor flesh. The speaker answers that they err exceedingly and have no hope of salvation, and turns to address a “blind Judahite”: God, who made soul and flesh in His likeness and image, will raise him whether he desires it or not, and judge him by his deeds. A long, fragmentary passage about the cord of Sheol (verses 11–15) and the gathering of spirit, dust, and flame (verses 19–21) is too broken — sentences ending in “...” and many untranslated words — to paraphrase with confidence, and this page does not try. The argument then steadies into images from nature: as the earth brings forth grass when rain falls, as the tree brings forth shoot and fruit, as the womb cannot restrain conception, so the earth cannot restrain the dead when it hears God’s voice and His dew comes down. Bodies shall be gathered into their places, souls shall enter the bodies from which they went forth, and at the blowing of the horn the dead shall rise in the twinkling of an eye, stand before God, and be requited according to their deeds — and the denier, seeing his deeds written before his eyes, shall know he shall rise and be judged.
