The Text — Psalms 151 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

*This Psalm on David was written by himself in prose, when he fought in single combat with Goliath.*

1 I was little among my brethren, and the youngest of my father's family. I fed my father's flocks.

2 My hands had made an organ; and my fingers had tuned a psaltery.

3 But who will tell my Lord. My Lord himself heareth.

4 He sent his messenger and took me from my father's flocks and anointed me with anointing oil.

5 My brothers were comely and great; but the Lord did not delight in them.

6 I went out to meet the Philistine, and he cursed me by his idols.

7 But I drew his own sword and cut off his head, and took away reproach from the children of Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter stands apart from Psalms 1–150: its text is not from the King James Version but from Charles Thomson's 1808 English Septuagint, as the Trace panel records, and it has no study-note commentary on this page. Its superscription, printed as part of the text above, presents the psalm as written by David himself in prose, when he fought in single combat with Goliath. In the psalm's own first-person voice, the speaker was little among his brethren and the youngest of his father's family, feeding his father's flocks, his hands making an organ and his fingers tuning a psaltery. God's messenger took him from the flocks and anointed him with oil, passing over brothers who were comely and great, in whom the Lord did not delight. He went out to meet the Philistine, who cursed him by his idols; he drew the Philistine's own sword, cut off his head, and took away reproach from the children of Israel. Authorship beyond what the superscription itself presents is not independently established by the sources consulted for this page.
