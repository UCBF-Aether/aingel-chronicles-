The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now these are they that came to David to Ziklag, while he yet kept himself close because of Saul the son of Kish: and they were among the mighty men, helpers of the war.

2 They were armed with bows, and could use both the right hand and the left in hurling stones and shooting arrows out of a bow, even of Saul’s brethren of Benjamin.

3 The chief was Ahiezer, then Joash, the sons of Shemaah the Gibeathite; and Jeziel, and Pelet, the sons of Azmaveth; and Berachah, and Jehu the Antothite.

4 And Ismaiah the Gibeonite, a mighty man among the thirty, and over the thirty; and Jeremiah, and Jahaziel, and Johanan, and Josabad the Gederathite,

5 Eluzai, and Jerimoth, and Bealiah, and Shemariah, and Shephatiah the Haruphite,

6 Elkanah, and Jesiah, and Azareel, and Joezer, and Jashobeam, the Korhites,

7 And Joelah, and Zebadiah, the sons of Jeroham of Gedor.

8 And of the Gadites there separated themselves unto David into the hold to the wilderness men of might, and men of war fit for the battle, that could handle shield and buckler, whose faces were like the faces of lions, and were as swift as the roes upon the mountains;

9 Ezer the first, Obadiah the second, Eliab the third,

10 Mishmannah the fourth, Jeremiah the fifth,

11 Attai the sixth, Eliel the seventh,

12 Johanan the eighth, Elzabad the ninth,

13 Jeremiah the tenth, Machbanai the eleventh.

14 These were of the sons of Gad, captains of the host: one of the least was over an hundred, and the greatest over a thousand.

15 These are they that went over Jordan in the first month, when it had overflown all his banks; and they put to flight all them of the valleys, both toward the east, and toward the west.

16 And there came of the children of Benjamin and Judah to the hold unto David.

17 And David went out to meet them, and answered and said unto them, If ye be come peaceably unto me to help me, mine heart shall be knit unto you: but if ye be come to betray me to mine enemies, seeing there is no wrong in mine hands, the God of our fathers look thereon, and rebuke it.

18 Then the spirit came upon Amasai, who was chief of the captains, and he said, Thine are we, David, and on thy side, thou son of Jesse: peace, peace be unto thee, and peace be to thine helpers; for thy God helpeth thee. Then David received them, and made them captains of the band.

19 And there fell some of Manasseh to David, when he came with the Philistines against Saul to battle: but they helped them not: for the lords of the Philistines upon advisement sent him away, saying, He will fall to his master Saul to the jeopardy of our heads.

20 As he went to Ziklag, there fell to him of Manasseh, Adnah, and Jozabad, and Jediael, and Michael, and Jozabad, and Elihu, and Zilthai, captains of the thousands that were of Manasseh.

21 And they helped David against the band of the rovers: for they were all mighty men of valour, and were captains in the host.

22 For at that time day by day there came to David to help him, until it was a great host, like the host of God.

23 And these are the numbers of the bands that were ready armed to the war, and came to David to Hebron, to turn the kingdom of Saul to him, according to the word of the LORD.

24 The children of Judah that bare shield and spear were six thousand and eight hundred, ready armed to the war.

25 Of the children of Simeon, mighty men of valour for the war, seven thousand and one hundred.

26 Of the children of Levi four thousand and six hundred.

27 And Jehoiada was the leader of the Aaronites, and with him were three thousand and seven hundred;

28 And Zadok, a young man mighty of valour, and of his father’s house twenty and two captains.

29 And of the children of Benjamin, the kindred of Saul, three thousand: for hitherto the greatest part of them had kept the ward of the house of Saul.

30 And of the children of Ephraim twenty thousand and eight hundred, mighty men of valour, famous throughout the house of their fathers.

31 And of the half tribe of Manasseh eighteen thousand, which were expressed by name, to come and make David king.

32 And of the children of Issachar, which were men that had understanding of the times, to know what Israel ought to do; the heads of them were two hundred; and all their brethren were at their commandment.

33 Of Zebulun, such as went forth to battle, expert in war, with all instruments of war, fifty thousand, which could keep rank: they were not of double heart.

34 And of Naphtali a thousand captains, and with them with shield and spear thirty and seven thousand.

35 And of the Danites expert in war twenty and eight thousand and six hundred.

36 And of Asher, such as went forth to battle, expert in war, forty thousand.

37 And on the other side of Jordan, of the Reubenites, and the Gadites, and of the half tribe of Manasseh, with all manner of instruments of war for the battle, an hundred and twenty thousand.

38 All these men of war, that could keep rank, came with a perfect heart to Hebron, to make David king over all Israel: and all the rest also of Israel were of one heart to make David king.

39 And there they were with David three days, eating and drinking: for their brethren had prepared for them.

40 Moreover they that were nigh them, even unto Issachar and Zebulun and Naphtali, brought bread on asses, and on camels, and on mules, and on oxen, and meat, meal, cakes of figs, and bunches of raisins, and wine, and oil, and oxen, and sheep abundantly: for there was joy in Israel.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter lists the men who came to David while Saul still reigned, at Ziklag, and then the far larger host from every tribe that came to Hebron to make him king, bringing provisions with them. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These are they that came to David to Ziklag, while he yet kept himself close because of Saul. Henry notes David's kingdom ripened slowly, and God inclined helpers to come even while David did not invite them, foreseeing Saul's death was near. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. They were of Saul's brethren of Benjamin, armed with bows, using right and left hand in hurling stones and shooting arrows. Henry suggests generous indignation at Saul's base treatment of David moved his own tribesmen to act vigorously for him, and their ambidextrous dexterity made a few of great service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Of the Gadites there separated themselves unto David into the hold to the wilderness men of might, fit for battle, with shield and buckler, faces like lions, swift as roes. Henry calls this a laudable separation: though few, eleven, they were brave, disciplined, and daring, unlike David's earlier broken-fortune followers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. These Gadites were captains of the host: the least was over a hundred, the greatest over a thousand. Henry notes they were officers of their tribe's militia, so though they brought no soldiers, they had hundreds and thousands at command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. These swam over Jordan when it overflowed its banks, and put to flight all in the valleys east and west. Henry praises men fit for God's cause who venture in dependence on divine protection and go through with their business, following their blow both ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. There came of Benjamin and Judah to the hold unto David. Henry introduces Amasai's company as Judah and Benjamin men coming to David, whose leader's speech follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. David went out to meet them and said, if ye come peaceably to help me, my heart shall be knit unto you, but if to betray me, the God of our fathers look and rebuke it. Henry praises David's prudent, fair treaty: he accepts cordial goodwill thankfully, and leaves treachery to God, refusing to judge or avenge his own cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Then the Spirit came upon Amasai, chief of the captains, and he said, thine are we, David, and on thy side, thou son of Jesse; peace be unto thee, and peace to thy helpers, for thy God helpeth thee. Henry notes the Spirit here is wisdom and resolution, and the speech professes adherence, wishes peace, and assures help from heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Of Manasseh also there fell some to David when he went with the Philistines against Saul. Henry notes providence gave them the opportunity as David marched through their country, and though not many, they were mighty men who helped against the Amalekites who plundered Ziklag. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. For at that time day by day there came to David to help him, until it was a great host, like the host of God. Henry marvels how David's interest grew strangely just when he needed it: when the promise comes to birth, God finds strength to bring it forth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. These are the numbers of the bands ready armed to the war that came to David to Hebron, to turn Saul's kingdom to him according to the Lord's word. Henry frames the Hebron muster as the tribes perfecting David's settlement after Ishbosheth's death, each tribe bringing its armed quota. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. The children of Judah, bearing shield and spear, were 6,800 ready armed to the war. Henry notes near tribes brought fewest, not from coldness but prudence, since the rest lay near within call, while Judah entertained those from afar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Of Simeon, mighty men of valour for the war, 7,100. Henry counts Simeon among the near tribes bringing few, yet still mighty men of valour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Of the Levites 4,600. Henry notes Levites and priests appeared hearty for David, ready to fight for him as well as pray, because they knew he was called of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Jehoiada, leader of the Aaronites, and with him 3,700. Henry includes the Aaronite leader in the priestly support, hearty in a cause they knew was God's calling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Zadok, a young man mighty of valour, and of his father's house twenty-two captains. Henry names Zadok among the mighty men of valour who came armed and governable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Of Benjamin, Saul's brethren, 3,000, for hitherto the greatest part of them had kept Saul's ward. Henry notes even Saul's kindred came over, though not many, because foolish tribal affection kept many long in Saul's sinking interest, and kindred must never overrule conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Of Ephraim 20,800, mighty men of valour, famous throughout their fathers' house. Henry cites Ephraim among the mighty men of valour in the Hebron host. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Of Issachar, men that had understanding of the times, to know what Israel ought to do, 200 chiefs, and all their brethren at their commandment. Henry calls them the fewest yet as serviceable as the greatest numbers: statesmen who knew the political times and judged Israel ought to make David king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Of Zebulun, such as went forth to battle, expert in war, with all instruments of war, 50,000, who could keep rank, and were not of double heart. Henry singles out Zebulun for singleness: Israelites indeed, in whom was no guile, of one heart for the public good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Of Dan expert in war 28,600. Henry cites Dan among those expert in war, bringing arms and instruments, since David could not furnish them all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Of Asher, such as went forth to battle, expert in war, 40,000. Henry pairs Asher with Dan as expert in war, from the remote tribes bringing large numbers out of zeal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Of the other side Jordan, Reuben, Gad, and half Manasseh, with all manner of instruments of war, 120,000. Henry notes the trans-Jordan tribes brought the largest host, showing zeal matching their distance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. All these men of war, who could keep rank, came with a perfect heart to Hebron to make David king over all Israel, and all the rest of Israel were of one heart. Henry stresses their perfection was unity: none had separate interests, all for the public good, warm hearts but cool heads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. There they were with David three days, eating and drinking, for their brethren had prepared for them. Henry notes adjacent tribes, at least pains in travelling, bore the charge of victualling the camps, that there might be equality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Moreover they that were nigh brought bread, meat, meal, cakes, wine, oil, oxen, and sheep abundantly, for there was joy in Israel. Henry closes with the noble feast: when the righteous bear rule the city rejoices, a foretaste of the joy when Christ's throne is set up in a soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
