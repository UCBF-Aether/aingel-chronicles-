The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The Revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave unto him, to shew unto his servants things which must shortly come to pass; and he sent and signified it by his angel unto his servant John:

2 Who bare record of the word of God, and of the testimony of Jesus Christ, and of all things that he saw.

3 Blessed is he that readeth, and they that hear the words of this prophecy, and keep those things which are written therein: for the time is at hand.

4 John to the seven churches which are in Asia: Grace be unto you, and peace, from him which is, and which was, and which is to come; and from the seven Spirits which are before his throne;

5 And from Jesus Christ, who is the faithful witness, and the first begotten of the dead, and the prince of the kings of the earth. Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood,

6 And hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father; to him be glory and dominion for ever and ever. Amen.

7 Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him: and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him. Even so, Amen.

8 I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the ending, saith the Lord, which is, and which was, and which is to come, the Almighty.

9 I John, who also am your brother, and companion in tribulation, and in the kingdom and patience of Jesus Christ, was in the isle that is called Patmos, for the word of God, and for the testimony of Jesus Christ.

10 I was in the Spirit on the Lord’s day, and heard behind me a great voice, as of a trumpet,

11 Saying, I am Alpha and Omega, the first and the last: and, What thou seest, write in a book, and send it unto the seven churches which are in Asia; unto Ephesus, and unto Smyrna, and unto Pergamos, and unto Thyatira, and unto Sardis, and unto Philadelphia, and unto Laodicea.

12 And I turned to see the voice that spake with me. And being turned, I saw seven golden candlesticks;

13 And in the midst of the seven candlesticks one like unto the Son of man, clothed with a garment down to the foot, and girt about the paps with a golden girdle.

14 His head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow; and his eyes were as a flame of fire;

15 And his feet like unto fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace; and his voice as the sound of many waters.

16 And he had in his right hand seven stars: and out of his mouth went a sharp twoedged sword: and his countenance was as the sun shineth in his strength.

17 And when I saw him, I fell at his feet as dead. And he laid his right hand upon me, saying unto me, Fear not; I am the first and the last:

18 I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.

19 Write the things which thou hast seen, and the things which are, and the things which shall be hereafter;

20 The mystery of the seven stars which thou sawest in my right hand, and the seven golden candlesticks. The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches: and the seven candlesticks which thou sawest are the seven churches.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The book opens as a revelation given through Christ by an angel to John, addressed to seven churches in Asia, and the chapter's centre is John's vision on Patmos of the risen Christ standing among seven golden lampstands, holding seven stars. Henry's notes trace the chain of giving — God to Christ, Christ by angel to John — and read the vision's details as picturing Christ's presence, purity, piercing knowledge and authority over his churches. The chapter closes with Christ's commission: write what you have seen, what is, and what is to come. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. This is the revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave him to show his servants things which must shortly come to pass, sent and signified by his angel to his servant John. Henry traces the order: God gave it to Christ, Christ employed an angel, and the angel signified it to John, who was to deliver it to all the church's servants, not ministers only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. John bore record of the word of God, the testimony of Jesus Christ, and all things that he saw. Henry notes John, like the prophets, prefixes his name and office, ready to vouch for the truth: he was an eye-witness who added nothing and kept back no part of God's counsels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Blessed is he who reads, and they who hear the words of this prophecy and keep what is written, for the time is at hand. Henry urges study of this book despite its obscurity, since it repays careful readers, but insists reading and hearing are not enough: the words must be kept in memory, mind, affection, and practice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. John writes to the seven churches in Asia, wishing grace and peace from him who is, was, and is to come, and from the seven Spirits before his throne. Henry explains the blessing as holiness and comfort, grace going before and peace following, and the seven Spirits as the one infinite perfect Spirit in diversity of gifts and operations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The blessing also comes from Jesus Christ, the faithful witness, the first begotten of the dead, and the prince of the kings of the earth, who loved us and washed us from our sins in his own blood. Henry notes Christ cannot be deceived and cannot deceive us, raised himself by his own power, and limits and overrules the powers of earthly kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Christ has made us kings and priests to God and his Father, to whom be glory and dominion for ever and ever. Henry explains the double honour: as kings they govern their spirits, conquer Satan, and prevail in prayer, and as priests they have access into the holiest to offer spiritual sacrifices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Behold, he comes with clouds, and every eye shall see him, including those who pierced him, and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him. Henry notes the book begins and ends with the second coming, and that Christ comes publicly, to the terror of the impenitent and the astonishment of the pagan world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Christ himself ratifies the account: "I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the ending, saith the Lord, which is, and which was, and which is to come, the Almighty." Henry notes he justly claims the same honour and power ascribed to the Father, being the eternal, unchangeable one from whom and for whom all things are. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. John describes himself as the churches' brother and companion in tribulation, in the kingdom and patience of Jesus Christ, who was on the isle of Patmos for the word of God and the testimony of Jesus. Henry notes he values his relation to the church above his apostolic authority, and that he suffered not as an evil-doer but for bearing witness to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. John was in the Spirit on the Lord's day and heard behind him a great voice, as of a trumpet. Henry identifies the Lord's day as the Christian sabbath, the first day of the week kept in remembrance of the resurrection, and notes God prepares souls for uncommon manifestations by the Spirit's quickening influence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The voice said, "I am Alpha and Omega, the first and the last," and commanded John to write what he saw in a book and send it to the seven churches: Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamos, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, and Laodicea. Henry notes Christ, the captain of salvation, gave notice of his appearance as with a trumpet blast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. John turned to see the voice that spoke with him, and saw seven golden candlesticks. Henry explains the emblem: churches hold forth the light of the gospel, receiving their light from Christ and his gospel, and as golden candlesticks they should be precious and pure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. In the midst of the candlesticks he saw one like the Son of man, clothed with a garment down to the foot and girt about the paps with a golden girdle. Henry reads the princely and priestly robe as righteousness and honour, and the golden girdle as the high priest's breastplate bearing his people's names, ready girt for the Redeemer's work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. His head and hairs were white like wool, as white as snow, and his eyes were as a flame of fire. Henry notes the hoary head is no sign of decay but a crown of glory, marking him the Ancient of days, while the fiery eyes pierce into hearts and scatter terror among adversaries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. His feet were like fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace, and his voice was as the sound of many waters. Henry reads the burning brass as strength and steadfastness, supporting his own interest and treading enemies to powder, and the voice as a mighty gospel stream heard by those afar off as well as near. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He had in his right hand seven stars, and out of his mouth went a sharp two-edged sword, and his countenance was as the sun shining in his strength. Henry explains the stars as the churches' ministers, held, lit, and preserved by Christ, and the sword as his word, wounding and healing, striking at sin on either hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. When John saw him, he fell at his feet as dead, and Christ laid his right hand on him, saying, "Fear not; I am the first and the last." Henry notes the apostle was overpowered by the lustre and glory, though he had been familiar with Christ before, and draws the comfort that God speaks to us by men like ourselves, whose terrors do not make us afraid, while Christ's touch put strength into his disciple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Christ said, "I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death." Henry notes the two claims together: by resurrection he conquered death, opened the grave, and partakes of an endless life, and by office he holds sovereign dominion over the invisible world, opening the gates of death and of the eternal world of happiness or misery as Judge of all, from whose sentence there lies no appeal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Christ commanded John to write the things he had seen, the things which are, and the things which shall be hereafter. Henry notes this threefold commission covers the vision itself, the present state of the churches, and the prophecy of things to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The mystery of the seven stars is that they are the angels of the seven churches, and the seven golden candlesticks are the seven churches. Henry closes the chapter with this key: Christ stands in the midst of his churches, filling them with light, life, and love, as their animating soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Alpha and Omega. The first and last letters of the Greek alphabet — Christ's title in verses 8 and 11 for the eternal, unchangeable one, as Henry's note reads it. Interpreted Study note, verse 8, Matthew Henry
  • Golden candlesticks. Lampstands holding forth light: Henry's emblem for the churches, receiving their light from Christ and his gospel. Interpreted Study note, verse 12, Matthew Henry
  • Seven stars. The angels, or ministers, of the seven churches, held in Christ's right hand, as the chapter itself explains in verse 20. Interpreted Study note, verse 20, Matthew Henry
