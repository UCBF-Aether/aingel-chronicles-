The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And in those days, when the number of the disciples was multiplied, there arose a murmuring of the Grecians against the Hebrews, because their widows were neglected in the daily ministration.

2 Then the twelve called the multitude of the disciples unto them, and said, It is not reason that we should leave the word of God, and serve tables.

3 Wherefore, brethren, look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business.

4 But we will give ourselves continually to prayer, and to the ministry of the word.

5 And the saying pleased the whole multitude: and they chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Ghost, and Philip, and Prochorus, and Nicanor, and Timon, and Parmenas, and Nicolas a proselyte of Antioch:

6 Whom they set before the apostles: and when they had prayed, they laid their hands on them.

7 And the word of God increased; and the number of the disciples multiplied in Jerusalem greatly; and a great company of the priests were obedient to the faith.

8 And Stephen, full of faith and power, did great wonders and miracles among the people.

9 Then there arose certain of the synagogue, which is called the synagogue of the Libertines, and Cyrenians, and Alexandrians, and of them of Cilicia and of Asia, disputing with Stephen.

10 And they were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit by which he spake.

11 Then they suborned men, which said, We have heard him speak blasphemous words against Moses, and against God.

12 And they stirred up the people, and the elders, and the scribes, and came upon him, and caught him, and brought him to the council,

13 And set up false witnesses, which said, This man ceaseth not to speak blasphemous words against this holy place, and the law:

14 For we have heard him say, that this Jesus of Nazareth shall destroy this place, and shall change the customs which Moses delivered us.

15 And all that sat in the council, looking stedfastly on him, saw his face as it had been the face of an angel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

As the disciples multiply, a complaint arises that Greek-speaking widows are neglected in the daily distribution, so seven men of good report, full of the Spirit and wisdom, are appointed to serve tables while the apostles give themselves to prayer and the word. Stephen, full of faith and power, is then seized on false charges, and his face before the council looks like the face of an angel. Interpreted Overview of this chapter's text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When the number of the disciples was multiplied, there arose a murmuring of the Grecians against the Hebrews because their widows were neglected in the daily ministration. Henry notes the contrast: hitherto the disciples had been of one accord, often noticed to their honour, but now, multiplied, they began to murmur. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. It is not reason that we should leave the word of God and serve tables. Henry's principle is that the word of God was enough to take up all the thoughts, cares and time of the apostles, so the ministry of the word must not be displaced by the care of tables. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business. Henry sets the qualification: those chosen to serve tables must be duly qualified, filled with gifts and graces of the Holy Ghost necessary to rightly managing the trust, men of truth and hating covetousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. But we will give ourselves continually to prayer and to the ministry of the word. Henry presents this division of labour as the apostles' proper work: prayer and the ministry of the word claim their whole time, while the daily ministration is committed to qualified men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Whom they set before the apostles, and when they had prayed, they laid their hands on them. Henry's rule for church appointments fits here: all who are employed in the service of the church ought to be commended to the divine grace by the prayers of the church, and they blessed them in the name of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The word of God increased, and the number of the disciples multiplied in Jerusalem greatly, and a great company of the priests were obedient to the faith. Henry marvels at the reach of it: the word and grace of God are greatly magnified when those are wrought upon by it who were least likely. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Stephen, full of faith and power, did great wonders and miracles among the people. Henry's account of the opposition that follows begins here: when they could not answer Stephen's arguments as a disputant, they prosecuted him as a criminal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit by which he spake. Henry's point is that argument failed first: it was only when they could not answer Stephen's arguments that they turned to prosecution and false witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Then they suborned men, which said, We have heard him speak blasphemous words against Moses and against God. Henry is blunt about the method: it is next to a miracle of providence that no greater number of religious persons have been murdered in the world by perjury and pretence of law, when so many thousands hate them who make no conscience of false oaths. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And set up false witnesses, which said, This man ceaseth not to speak blasphemous words against this holy place and the law. Henry asks what to say of man, a rational being, yet attempting to uphold a religious system by false witness and murder, and answers that the blame rests not so much upon the understanding as upon the heart of a fallen creature, deceitful above all things and desperately wicked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. For we have heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth shall destroy this place and shall change the customs which Moses delivered us. Henry treats the charge as the same false-witness prosecution: unable to answer Stephen, his enemies manufactured a criminal case out of his preaching about Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. All that sat in the council, looking stedfastly on him, saw his face as it had been the face of an angel. Henry closes with the contrast: wisdom and holiness make a man's face to shine, yet will not secure men from being treated badly, but the servant of the Lord, possessing a clear conscience, cheerful hope and divine consolations, may smile in the midst of danger and death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • Grecians. Greek-speaking Jews, distinguished in the chapter from the Hebrew-speaking Jews, whose widows were said to be neglected. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • suborned men. Men secretly induced to give false testimony against Stephen. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • synagogue of the Libertines. One of the synagogues whose members disputed with Stephen; the chapter names it alongside those of Cyrene, Alexandria, Cilicia, and Asia. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
