The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now it came to pass after this, that Nahash the king of the children of Ammon died, and his son reigned in his stead.

2 And David said, I will shew kindness unto Hanun the son of Nahash, because his father shewed kindness to me. And David sent messengers to comfort him concerning his father. So the servants of David came into the land of the children of Ammon to Hanun, to comfort him.

3 But the princes of the children of Ammon said to Hanun, Thinkest thou that David doth honour thy father, that he hath sent comforters unto thee? are not his servants come unto thee for to search, and to overthrow, and to spy out the land?

4 Wherefore Hanun took David’s servants, and shaved them, and cut off their garments in the midst hard by their buttocks, and sent them away.

5 Then there went certain, and told David how the men were served. And he sent to meet them: for the men were greatly ashamed. And the king said, Tarry at Jericho until your beards be grown, and then return.

6 And when the children of Ammon saw that they had made themselves odious to David, Hanun and the children of Ammon sent a thousand talents of silver to hire them chariots and horsemen out of Mesopotamia, and out of Syriamaachah, and out of Zobah.

7 So they hired thirty and two thousand chariots, and the king of Maachah and his people; who came and pitched before Medeba. And the children of Ammon gathered themselves together from their cities, and came to battle.

8 And when David heard of it, he sent Joab, and all the host of the mighty men.

9 And the children of Ammon came out, and put the battle in array before the gate of the city: and the kings that were come were by themselves in the field.

10 Now when Joab saw that the battle was set against him before and behind, he chose out of all the choice of Israel, and put them in array against the Syrians.

11 And the rest of the people he delivered unto the hand of Abishai his brother, and they set themselves in array against the children of Ammon.

12 And he said, If the Syrians be too strong for me, then thou shalt help me: but if the children of Ammon be too strong for thee, then I will help thee.

13 Be of good courage, and let us behave ourselves valiantly for our people, and for the cities of our God: and let the LORD do that which is good in his sight.

14 So Joab and the people that were with him drew nigh before the Syrians unto the battle; and they fled before him.

15 And when the children of Ammon saw that the Syrians were fled, they likewise fled before Abishai his brother, and entered into the city. Then Joab came to Jerusalem.

16 And when the Syrians saw that they were put to the worse before Israel, they sent messengers, and drew forth the Syrians that were beyond the river: and Shophach the captain of the host of Hadarezer went before them.

17 And it was told David; and he gathered all Israel, and passed over Jordan, and came upon them, and set the battle in array against them. So when David had put the battle in array against the Syrians, they fought with him.

18 But the Syrians fled before Israel; and David slew of the Syrians seven thousand men which fought in chariots, and forty thousand footmen, and killed Shophach the captain of the host.

19 And when the servants of Hadarezer saw that they were put to the worse before Israel, they made peace with David, and became his servants: neither would the Syrians help the children of Ammon any more.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the war with Ammon: David's kindness is repaid with insult to his messengers, Ammon hires Syrian allies, and Joab and Abishai defeat both armies, with Hadarezer's servants making peace with David. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After Nahash king of Ammon dies, his son reigns in his stead, setting up the chapter's story of David's war with the Ammonites and their Syrian allies. Henry notes the chapter repeats the history already told in 2 Samuel 10, now in Chronicles' own telling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. David resolves to show kindness to Hanun, because Hanun's father showed kindness to him, and sends messengers to comfort him. Henry observes that good people should be neighbourly and grateful, returning kindness received from parents to their children when the parents are gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The Ammonite princes tell Hanun that David's comforters have really come to search, overthrow, and spy out the land. Henry applies the ancient proverb that wickedness proceeds from the wicked: those base and designing ill themselves are apt to suspect ill of others without cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Hanun takes David's servants, shaves them, cuts off their garments in the middle, and sends them away. Henry notes Hanun hearkened to the groundless suggestion and, against the law of nations, treated David's ambassadors villainously. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. When David hears how his men were served, he sends to meet them, for they are greatly ashamed, and bids them tarry at Jericho until their beards grow. Henry draws the lesson that masters ought to protect their servants with tenderness when they suffer loss or damage in their service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Seeing they have made themselves odious to David, Hanun and the Ammonites send a thousand talents of silver to hire chariots and horsemen from Mesopotamia, Syrian Maacah, and Zobah. Henry observes how hearts marked for ruin harden to their destruction: wisdom would have sought peace and offered satisfaction, but instead they prepared for war and brought on desolations David never intended. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They hire thirty-two thousand chariots, with the king of Maacah and his people, who encamp before Medeba, while the Ammonites gather from their cities to battle. Henry notes the Ammonites did their utmost to make the best of their position, bringing as good a force into the field as policy could dispose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When David hears of it, he sends Joab and all the host of the mighty men. Henry's second section frames the campaign: against this hired array stands Israel's army under a commander whose courage difficulties will heighten rather than break. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The Ammonites put themselves in battle array before the gate of the city, while the kings who came to help are by themselves in the field. Henry notes the Ammonites disposed their force with as much policy as possible, yet, having a bad cause and acting in defence of wrong, it would not do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Joab sees the battle set against him before and behind, and chooses the choice men of Israel to array against the Syrians. Henry observes that the courage of brave men is heightened by difficulties: instead of meditating retreat, Joab doubles his resolution and, though he cannot double his army, divides it with great presence of mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The rest of the people Joab delivers to his brother Abishai, who arrays them against the Ammonites. Henry notes Joab not only speaks but acts like a gallant man when surrounded, dividing his forces so each front is met. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Joab covenants mutual help with Abishai: if the Syrians are too strong, Abishai will help him, and if the Ammonites are too strong, Joab will help Abishai. Henry highlights this engagement for mutual assistance as the mark of a commander acting with presence of mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Joab charges them to be of good courage and behave valiantly for their people and the cities of their God, leaving the issue to the Lord to do what is good in his sight. Henry notes Joab excites himself and the officers to act vigorously in their posts, with an eye to God's glory and their country's good, not their own honour, and then leaves the issue to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Joab and his men draw near to the Syrians in battle, and the Syrians flee before him. Henry's lesson is that the greatest art and strength are vain against justice and equity: right will prevail and triumph at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. When the Ammonites see the Syrians fled, they likewise flee before Abishai and enter the city, and Joab comes to Jerusalem. Henry notes the Ammonites, for all their policy and force, are put to the worst because they defend a wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. When the Syrians see they are put to the worse, they send messengers and draw forth the Syrians beyond the river, with Shophach captain of Hadarezer's host. Henry observes how little purpose there is in rallying again without God on one's side: beaten mercenaries call in the Syrians from beyond Euphrates only to retrieve their honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. David hears, gathers all Israel, passes over Jordan, and sets the battle in array against them. Henry's account of the second campaign turns on the same point: the reinforced Syrians face not only Joab now, but David and all Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The Syrians flee before Israel, and David slays seven thousand men who fought in chariots and forty thousand footmen, and kills Shophach. Henry notes the rally comes to nothing: they still flee before Israel, losing seven thousand men, reckoned as the men of seven hundred chariots. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. When Hadarezer's servants see they are put to the worse, they make peace with David and become his servants, and the Syrians will help Ammon no more. Henry closes that those who meddled with strife not their own do well to learn wit at length and meddle no further, agreeing quickly while they are in the way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
