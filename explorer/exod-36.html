The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 36 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then wrought Bezaleel and Aholiab, and every wise hearted man, in whom the LORD put wisdom and understanding to know how to work all manner of work for the service of the sanctuary, according to all that the LORD had commanded.

2 And Moses called Bezaleel and Aholiab, and every wise hearted man, in whose heart the LORD had put wisdom, even every one whose heart stirred him up to come unto the work to do it:

3 And they received of Moses all the offering, which the children of Israel had brought for the work of the service of the sanctuary, to make it withal. And they brought yet unto him free offerings every morning.

4 And all the wise men, that wrought all the work of the sanctuary, came every man from his work which they made;

5 And they spake unto Moses, saying, The people bring much more than enough for the service of the work, which the LORD commanded to make.

6 And Moses gave commandment, and they caused it to be proclaimed throughout the camp, saying, Let neither man nor woman make any more work for the offering of the sanctuary. So the people were restrained from bringing.

7 For the stuff they had was sufficient for all the work to make it, and too much.

8 And every wise hearted man among them that wrought the work of the tabernacle made ten curtains of fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet: with cherubims of cunning work made he them.

9 The length of one curtain was twenty and eight cubits, and the breadth of one curtain four cubits: the curtains were all of one size.

10 And he coupled the five curtains one unto another: and the other five curtains he coupled one unto another.

11 And he made loops of blue on the edge of one curtain from the selvedge in the coupling: likewise he made in the uttermost side of another curtain, in the coupling of the second.

12 Fifty loops made he in one curtain, and fifty loops made he in the edge of the curtain which was in the coupling of the second: the loops held one curtain to another.

13 And he made fifty taches of gold, and coupled the curtains one unto another with the taches: so it became one tabernacle.

14 And he made curtains of goats’ hair for the tent over the tabernacle: eleven curtains he made them.

15 The length of one curtain was thirty cubits, and four cubits was the breadth of one curtain: the eleven curtains were of one size.

16 And he coupled five curtains by themselves, and six curtains by themselves.

17 And he made fifty loops upon the uttermost edge of the curtain in the coupling, and fifty loops made he upon the edge of the curtain which coupleth the second.

18 And he made fifty taches of brass to couple the tent together, that it might be one.

19 And he made a covering for the tent of rams’ skins dyed red, and a covering of badgers’ skins above that.

20 And he made boards for the tabernacle of shittim wood, standing up.

21 The length of a board was ten cubits, and the breadth of a board one cubit and a half.

22 One board had two tenons, equally distant one from another: thus did he make for all the boards of the tabernacle.

23 And he made boards for the tabernacle; twenty boards for the south side southward:

24 And forty sockets of silver he made under the twenty boards; two sockets under one board for his two tenons, and two sockets under another board for his two tenons.

25 And for the other side of the tabernacle, which is toward the north corner, he made twenty boards,

26 And their forty sockets of silver; two sockets under one board, and two sockets under another board.

27 And for the sides of the tabernacle westward he made six boards.

28 And two boards made he for the corners of the tabernacle in the two sides.

29 And they were coupled beneath, and coupled together at the head thereof, to one ring: thus he did to both of them in both the corners.

30 And there were eight boards; and their sockets were sixteen sockets of silver, under every board two sockets.

31 And he made bars of shittim wood; five for the boards of the one side of the tabernacle,

32 And five bars for the boards of the other side of the tabernacle, and five bars for the boards of the tabernacle for the sides westward.

33 And he made the middle bar to shoot through the boards from the one end to the other.

34 And he overlaid the boards with gold, and made their rings of gold to be places for the bars, and overlaid the bars with gold.

35 And he made a vail of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen: with cherubims made he it of cunning work.

36 And he made thereunto four pillars of shittim wood, and overlaid them with gold: their hooks were of gold; and he cast for them four sockets of silver.

37 And he made an hanging for the tabernacle door of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, of needlework;

38 And the five pillars of it with their hooks: and he overlaid their chapiters and their fillets with gold: but their five sockets were of brass.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Bezaleel, Aholiab, and every wise-hearted man begin the work, and the people's offerings prove so abundant that Moses must restrain them from bringing more. The chapter then describes the making of the tabernacle itself: the curtains joined, the goats' hair covering, the boards and bars, the veil, and the hanging for the door. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then wrought Bezaleel and Aholiab, and every wise hearted man, in whom the LORD put wisdom and understanding to know how to work all manner of work for the service of the sanctuary, according to all that the LORD had commanded. Henry notes the readiness and zeal with which these builders set about their work, and the exactness with which they performed it, are worthy of our imitation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And Moses called Bezaleel and Aholiab, and every wise hearted man, in whose heart the LORD had put wisdom, even every one whose heart stirred him up to come unto the work to do it. Henry observes the call and the stirring went together: Moses summoned, and the stirred heart answered, for God gives both the gift and the willingness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And they received of Moses all the offering, which Israel had brought for the work of the service of the sanctuary, to make it withal, and they brought yet unto him free offerings every morning. Henry notes the offerings came every morning, free and continual, until the workmen had more than they could use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And all the wise men, that wrought all the work of the sanctuary, came every man from his work which they made. Henry observes the workmen left their work to speak, for the matter was urgent: the people's giving had outrun the building. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And they spake unto Moses, saying, The people bring much more than enough for the service of the work, which the LORD commanded to make. Henry notes the faithfulness with which they objected to receive more contributions: those in public trusts must abhor filthy lucre and avoid all occasions of covetousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And Moses gave commandment, and they caused it to be proclaimed throughout the camp, saying, Let neither man nor woman make any more work for the offering of the sanctuary. So the people were restrained from bringing. Henry observes the proclamation restrained giving itself, a rare command in a willing camp, for the stuff was sufficient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. For the stuff they had was sufficient for all the work to make it, and too much. Henry notes the sufficiency was itself a testimony: a people lately enslaved had given too much for God's house, and the surplus proved their willing hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And every wise hearted man among them that wrought the work of the tabernacle made ten curtains of fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet, with cherubims of cunning work made he them. Henry notes the tabernacle of witness was a visible testimony of God's love to fallen men, and the cherubim curtains were its inner glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The length of one curtain was twenty and eight cubits, and the breadth of one curtain four cubits, and the curtains were all of one size. Henry observes the work matched the pattern exactly: one size for all ten, as commanded on the mount. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And he coupled the five curtains one unto another, and the other five curtains he coupled one unto another. Henry notes the coupling was done as commanded: five and five, joined into one tabernacle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And he made loops of blue on the edge of one curtain from the selvedge in the coupling, and likewise made he in the uttermost edge of another curtain, in the coupling of the second. Henry observes the blue loops were made as commanded, matching edge to edge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Fifty loops made he in one curtain, and fifty loops made he in the edge of the curtain which was in the coupling of the second, and the loops held one curtain to another. Henry notes the fifty and fifty held together, loop to loop, that the curtains might be one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And he made fifty taches of gold, and coupled the curtains one unto another with the taches, so it became one tabernacle. Henry observes the gold taches completed the inner curtains, and the ten became one tabernacle, as the pattern required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And he made curtains of goats' hair for the tent over the tabernacle, and eleven curtains he made them. Henry notes the goats' hair tent covered the rich curtains, outwardly mean, inwardly glorious, as Christ and his church are. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The length of one curtain was thirty cubits, and four cubits was the breadth of one curtain, and the eleven curtains were of one size. Henry observes the outer curtains were longer than the inner, thirty cubits, that they might cover the house fully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And he coupled five curtains by themselves, and six curtains by themselves. Henry notes the outer coupling was five and six, with the sixth doubled at the front, as commanded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And he made fifty loops upon the uttermost edge of the curtain in the coupling, and fifty loops upon the edge of the curtain which coupleth the second. Henry observes the outer loops matched the inner in number, fifty and fifty, for both coverings were coupled alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And he made fifty taches of brass to couple the tent together, that it might be one. Henry notes the brass taches joined the outer tent, brass without and gold within, fitting the meaner material. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And he made a covering for the tent of rams' skins dyed red, and a covering of badgers' skins above that. Henry observes the skin coverings completed the tent: red rams' skins beneath and badgers' skins above, weatherproof layers over all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And he made boards for the tabernacle of shittim wood, standing up. Henry notes the boards were the walls, shittim wood standing upright, the frame of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The length of a board was ten cubits, and the breadth of a board one cubit and a half. Henry observes the boards were ten cubits high, uniform, that the house might stand to its full height. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. One board had two tenons, equally distant one from another, and thus did he make for all the boards of the tabernacle. Henry notes the two tenons per board fitted the sockets, making walls firm though movable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And he made boards for the tabernacle, twenty boards for the south side southward. Henry observes the south wall was twenty boards, a full side of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And forty sockets of silver he made under the twenty boards, two sockets under one board for his two tenons, and two sockets under another board for his two tenons. Henry notes the silver sockets, about 115 pounds each, stood in rows on the ground, and each board was fitted into its pair. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And for the other side of the tabernacle, which is toward the north corner, he made twenty boards. Henry observes the north side matched the south, twenty boards again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And their forty sockets of silver, two sockets under one board, and two sockets under another board. Henry notes the north sockets matched the south, forty more of silver. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And for the sides of the tabernacle westward he made six boards. Henry observes the west end was six boards, closing the house behind the most holy place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And two boards made he for the corners of the tabernacle in the two sides. Henry notes the corner boards bound sides to rear, strengthening the angles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And they were coupled beneath, and coupled together at the head thereof, to one ring, and thus he did to both of them in both the corners. Henry observes the corner boards were coupled below and above, double-joined, that the corners might be strongest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And there were eight boards, and their sockets were sixteen sockets of silver, under every board two sockets. Henry notes the west end counted eight boards on sixteen sockets, six plus two corners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And he made bars of shittim wood, five for the boards of the one side of the tabernacle. Henry notes the bars passed through gold rings and held the walls together, five for one side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And five bars for the boards of the other side of the tabernacle, and five bars for the boards of the tabernacle for the sides westward. Henry observes the bars ran on every side, binding the whole frame into one house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And he made the middle bar to shoot through the boards from the one end to the other. Henry notes the middle bar ran the whole length, a single spine through the wall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. And he overlaid the boards with gold, and made their rings of gold to be places for the bars, and overlaid the bars with gold. Henry observes the boards, rings, and bars were all overlaid with gold, for the house was a type of the church built on apostles and prophets, Christ himself the chief Corner-stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And he made a vail of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, with cherubims made he it of cunning work. Henry notes the vail separated holy from most holy, woven richly with cherubim guarding the way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And he made thereunto four pillars of shittim wood, and overlaid them with gold, their hooks were of gold, and he cast for them four sockets of silver. Henry observes the vail hung on four gold pillars on silver sockets, a rich partition for a holy separation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. And he made a hanging for the tabernacle door of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, of needlework. Henry notes the door hanging was the outer vail, all the defence the tabernacle had, yet God takes care of his church, and a curtain, if God please, is as strong a defence as gates of brass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. And the five pillars of it with their hooks, and he overlaid their chapiters and their fillets with gold, but their five sockets were of brass. Henry closes on the door's five pillars, gold above and brass below, for the entrance stood between the court and the holy place, and this love of God was shown by Christ's taking up his abode on earth, for the Word was made flesh and tabernacled among us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
