The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he arose from thence, and cometh into the coasts of Judaea by the farther side of Jordan: and the people resort unto him again; and, as he was wont, he taught them again.

2 And the Pharisees came to him, and asked him, Is it lawful for a man to put away his wife? tempting him.

3 And he answered and said unto them, What did Moses command you?

4 And they said, Moses suffered to write a bill of divorcement, and to put her away.

5 And Jesus answered and said unto them, For the hardness of your heart he wrote you this precept.

6 But from the beginning of the creation God made them male and female.

7 For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and cleave to his wife;

8 And they twain shall be one flesh: so then they are no more twain, but one flesh.

9 What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder.

10 And in the house his disciples asked him again of the same matter.

11 And he saith unto them, Whosoever shall put away his wife, and marry another, committeth adultery against her.

12 And if a woman shall put away her husband, and be married to another, she committeth adultery.

13 And they brought young children to him, that he should touch them: and his disciples rebuked those that brought them.

14 But when Jesus saw it, he was much displeased, and said unto them, Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.

15 Verily I say unto you, Whosoever shall not receive the kingdom of God as a little child, he shall not enter therein.

16 And he took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them.

17 And when he was gone forth into the way, there came one running, and kneeled to him, and asked him, Good Master, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life?

18 And Jesus said unto him, Why callest thou me good? there is none good but one, that is, God.

19 Thou knowest the commandments, Do not commit adultery, Do not kill, Do not steal, Do not bear false witness, Defraud not, Honour thy father and mother.

20 And he answered and said unto him, Master, all these have I observed from my youth.

21 Then Jesus beholding him loved him, and said unto him, One thing thou lackest: go thy way, sell whatsoever thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven: and come, take up the cross, and follow me.

22 And he was sad at that saying, and went away grieved: for he had great possessions.

23 And Jesus looked round about, and saith unto his disciples, How hardly shall they that have riches enter into the kingdom of God!

24 And the disciples were astonished at his words. But Jesus answereth again, and saith unto them, Children, how hard is it for them that trust in riches to enter into the kingdom of God!

25 It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God.

26 And they were astonished out of measure, saying among themselves, Who then can be saved?

27 And Jesus looking upon them saith, With men it is impossible, but not with God: for with God all things are possible.

28 Then Peter began to say unto him, Lo, we have left all, and have followed thee.

29 And Jesus answered and said, Verily I say unto you, There is no man that hath left house, or brethren, or sisters, or father, or mother, or wife, or children, or lands, for my sake, and the gospel’s,

30 But he shall receive an hundredfold now in this time, houses, and brethren, and sisters, and mothers, and children, and lands, with persecutions; and in the world to come eternal life.

31 But many that are first shall be last; and the last first.

32 And they were in the way going up to Jerusalem; and Jesus went before them: and they were amazed; and as they followed, they were afraid. And he took again the twelve, and began to tell them what things should happen unto him,

33 Saying, Behold, we go up to Jerusalem; and the Son of man shall be delivered unto the chief priests, and unto the scribes; and they shall condemn him to death, and shall deliver him to the Gentiles:

34 And they shall mock him, and shall scourge him, and shall spit upon him, and shall kill him: and the third day he shall rise again.

35 And James and John, the sons of Zebedee, come unto him, saying, Master, we would that thou shouldest do for us whatsoever we shall desire.

36 And he said unto them, What would ye that I should do for you?

37 They said unto him, Grant unto us that we may sit, one on thy right hand, and the other on thy left hand, in thy glory.

38 But Jesus said unto them, Ye know not what ye ask: can ye drink of the cup that I drink of? and be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with?

39 And they said unto him, We can. And Jesus said unto them, Ye shall indeed drink of the cup that I drink of; and with the baptism that I am baptized withal shall ye be baptized:

40 But to sit on my right hand and on my left hand is not mine to give; but it shall be given to them for whom it is prepared.

41 And when the ten heard it, they began to be much displeased with James and John.

42 But Jesus called them to him, and saith unto them, Ye know that they which are accounted to rule over the Gentiles exercise lordship over them; and their great ones exercise authority upon them.

43 But so shall it not be among you: but whosoever will be great among you, shall be your minister:

44 And whosoever of you will be the chiefest, shall be servant of all.

45 For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.

46 And they came to Jericho: and as he went out of Jericho with his disciples and a great number of people, blind Bartimaeus, the son of Timaeus, sat by the highway side begging.

47 And when he heard that it was Jesus of Nazareth, he began to cry out, and say, Jesus, thou son of David, have mercy on me.

48 And many charged him that he should hold his peace: but he cried the more a great deal, Thou son of David, have mercy on me.

49 And Jesus stood still, and commanded him to be called. And they call the blind man, saying unto him, Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee.

50 And he, casting away his garment, rose, and came to Jesus.

51 And Jesus answered and said unto him, What wilt thou that I should do unto thee? The blind man said unto him, Lord, that I might receive my sight.

52 And Jesus said unto him, Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole. And immediately he received his sight, and followed Jesus in the way.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Pharisees test Jesus on divorce; he appeals to Moses’ permission and to creation, that husband and wife are one flesh (vv. 1–12). He blesses little children and says the kingdom must be received as a little child receives (vv. 13–16). A rich ruler who has kept the commandments goes away sorrowful when told to sell all and follow (vv. 17–31). On the road Jesus predicts his death a third time, James and John ask for the chief seats, and he teaches that greatness is service, before healing blind Bartimaeus outside Jericho (vv. 32–52). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 10

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He arose and came into the coasts of Judea by the farther side of Jordan, and the people resorted to him again, and as he was wont, he taught them again. His custom was teaching, wherever the people resorted, and Judea heard the same gospel Galilee had heard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The Pharisees came and asked, Is it lawful for a man to put away his wife? tempting him. The question was a trap, for any answer would offend either Herod's party or the strict school, and they sought his words, not his wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He answered, What did Moses command you? Christ sends them back to Moses, for the law itself, rightly read, answers the tempter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They said, Moses suffered to write a bill of divorcement and to put her away. They turned a permission, given to restrain worse evil, into a licence, as if suffering meant approving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Jesus answered, For the hardness of your heart he wrote you this precept. Divorce was permitted because of hard hearts, not because it was good; a concession to sin is no rule for godliness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But from the beginning of the creation God made them male and female. Creation, not custom, is the standard: the first institution defines marriage before any later permission qualified it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and cleave to his wife. Marriage creates a new bond stronger than the old, for leaving and cleaving belong to its very nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They two shall be one flesh, so then they are no more two but one flesh. One flesh means one life, one interest, and one indissoluble union, not a partnership at will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. What therefore God has joined together, let not man put asunder. Man must not sunder what God has joined, for the bond is God's making, not man's convenience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. In the house his disciples asked him again of the same matter. Private inquiry followed public controversy, and the disciples sought the plain rule for their own conduct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. He said, Whoever shall put away his wife and marry another commits adultery against her. Putting away and remarrying, except where God allows, is adultery, for the first bond still stands before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. If a woman shall put away her husband and be married to another, she commits adultery. The rule binds both sexes equally, a rare equality in that age, for the marriage bond is mutual. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They brought young children to him, that he should touch them, and his disciples rebuked those that brought them. Parents brought children for a touch of blessing, and the disciples, thinking children too small for Christ, turned them away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. But when Jesus saw it, he was much displeased, and said, Suffer the little children to come to me, and forbid them not, for of such is the kingdom of God. His displeasure at hindering children is the strongest rebuke in the passage: the kingdom belongs to such as these. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Verily, whoever shall not receive the kingdom of God as a little child shall not enter therein. Childlike reception means humble, trusting, and unpretending acceptance, without the bargains adults make with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them. Arms, hands, and blessing together show Christ's tenderness to children, and his blessing is more than a touch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. When he had gone forth into the way, there came one running and kneeled to him, and asked, Good Master, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life? Running and kneeling showed earnestness, and his question sought the way to life, though it still trusted in doing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Jesus said, Why do you call me good? There is none good but one, that is, God. The answer tests his title: if Christ is truly good, he is God, and the young man must reckon with who stands before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. You know the commandments: Do not commit adultery, do not kill, do not steal, do not bear false witness, defraud not, honour your father and mother. Christ names the second table, the duties to neighbours, as the test the man claimed to have kept. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He answered, Master, all these have I observed from my youth. His claim was sincere as far as outward keeping went, yet it showed he did not know his own heart's failure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Then Jesus beholding him loved him, and said, One thing you lack: go, sell whatever you have, and give to the poor, and you shall have treasure in heaven, and come, take up the cross, and follow me. Love, not anger, gave the hard command: one thing lacked, his wealth, and selling it would prove whether he loved God or goods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. He was sad at that saying, and went away grieved, for he had great possessions. Sadness and grief, not anger, mark his departure, for he loved his possessions more than the treasure in heaven, and sorrow without surrender is the rich man's tragedy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Jesus looked round and said to his disciples, How hardly shall they that have riches enter into the kingdom of God! Riches are a hard road to heaven, for they promise security, breed self-sufficiency, and hold the heart fast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The disciples were astonished at his words, but Jesus answered again, Children, how hard is it for them that trust in riches to enter into the kingdom of God! The hardness lies not in having riches but in trusting them, and trust in wealth shuts the kingdom's door. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God. The proverb's impossibility is deliberate: humanly, the rich man's entrance is as impossible as a camel through a needle's eye. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. They were astonished out of measure, saying among themselves, Who then can be saved? If the rich, who seem most blessed, cannot enter, the disciples fear no one can, and their astonishment is honest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Jesus looking upon them said, With men it is impossible, but not with God, for with God all things are possible. What is impossible to men is possible to God, and grace, not wealth or poverty, opens the kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Then Peter began to say, Lo, we have left all and have followed you. Peter's claim, though true in measure, seeks a reckoning, and Christ answers it with promise, not reproof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Jesus answered, Verily, there is no man that has left house, brethren, sisters, father, mother, wife, children, or lands, for my sake and the gospel's. Every loss for Christ and the gospel is named, that no sacrifice may think itself forgotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. But he shall receive a hundredfold now in this time, houses, brethren, sisters, mothers, children, and lands, with persecutions, and in the world to come eternal life. A hundredfold in this life comes in the church's fellowship, with persecutions mixed in, and eternal life crowns it in the world to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. But many that are first shall be last, and the last first. The reversal warns Peter's reckoning: first in sacrifice may be last in reward, and God orders the kingdom by grace, not by seniority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. They were in the way going up to Jerusalem, and Jesus went before them, and they were amazed, and as they followed, they were afraid, and he took again the twelve and began to tell them what things should happen to him. Going before them to danger amazed them, and fear followed, yet he again foretold what awaited him, that they might not be surprised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. Saying, Behold, we go up to Jerusalem, and the Son of man shall be delivered to the chief priests and scribes, and they shall condemn him to death, and shall deliver him to the Gentiles. The third prediction names the betrayers and the judges: priests and scribes condemn, Gentiles execute, and Jerusalem is the place of the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. They shall mock him, scourge him, spit upon him, kill him, and the third day he shall rise again. Mocking, scourging, and spitting precede killing, and the third day's rising closes the prediction as its only comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. James and John, the sons of Zebedee, came to him, saying, Master, we would that you should do for us whatever we shall desire. Their request, made in the shadow of the cross, asks a blank favour, and ambition blinds them to the hour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. He said to them, What would you that I should do for you? Christ asks them to name their desire, that its unfitness may be plain to themselves and to the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. They said, Grant to us that we may sit, one on your right hand and the other on your left hand, in your glory. Right and left hand in glory are the places of highest honour, and they ask them before the cup, mistaking glory for privilege. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. But Jesus said, You know not what you ask: can you drink of the cup that I drink of, and be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with? The cup and baptism are his suffering and death, and they do not know what they ask, for glory's road runs through both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. They said, We can, and Jesus said, You shall indeed drink of the cup that I drink of, and with the baptism that I am baptized with shall you be baptized. Their confident 'we can' is accepted as prophecy: both would share his suffering, James by the sword and John by long persecution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. But to sit on my right hand and on my left hand is not mine to give, but it shall be given to them for whom it is prepared. Places in glory are the Father's preparation, not the Son's favouritism, and ambition cannot claim what grace prepares. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. When the ten heard it, they began to be much displeased with James and John. The ten's displeasure was not holy zeal but rival ambition, for each wanted the places the two had asked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. But Jesus called them to him and said, You know that they who are accounted to rule over the Gentiles exercise lordship over them, and their great ones exercise authority upon them. Gentile rule is lordship and authority exercised downward, and Christ names it that his disciples may reject it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. But so shall it not be among you: but whoever will be great among you shall be your minister. Greatness in his kingdom is ministry, and the measure of honour is the measure of service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. Whoever of you will be the chiefest shall be servant of all. Chief place means lowest service, for the servant of all is the chiefest in Christ's reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many. The Son of man is the pattern: he came to serve, and his service ended in a ransom, his life given for many. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. They came to Jericho, and as he went out of Jericho with his disciples and a great number of people, blind Bartimaeus, son of Timaeus, sat by the highway side begging. A named beggar by the highway is the last healing before Jerusalem, and his name preserves the witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. When he heard that it was Jesus of Nazareth, he began to cry out, Jesus, son of David, have mercy on me. Hearing became faith: he named Jesus son of David, the Messiah's title, and cried for mercy, not for wages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. Many charged him that he should hold his peace, but he cried the more a great deal, Son of David, have mercy on me. Rebuke increased his cry, for earnest prayer grows louder under opposition, and he would not be silenced from mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. Jesus stood still and commanded him to be called, and they called the blind man, saying, Be of good comfort, rise; he calls you. Christ's standing still is his attention to one cry in a crowd, and the bystanders' comfort repeats his call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. He, casting away his garment, rose and came to Jesus. The garment cast away shows haste and expectation: he left his beggar's cloak to come to his healer, as if he would not need it again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. Jesus answered and said, What will you that I should do to you? The blind man said, Lord, that I might receive my sight. Christ asks, though he knows, that faith may speak its desire plainly, and the man's answer is simply sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. Jesus said, Go your way; your faith has made you whole, and immediately he received his sight, and followed Jesus in the way. Faith is credited with the cure, sight came immediately, and the healed beggar's first use of sight was to follow Jesus on the road to Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Then Jesus beholding him loved him, and said, One thing you lack: go, sell whatever you have, and give to the poor, and you shall have treasure in heaven, and come, take up the cross, and follow me. Love, not anger, gave the hard command: one thing lacked, his wealth, and selling it would prove whether he loved God or goods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. He was sad at that saying, and went away grieved, for he had great possessions. Sadness and grief, not anger, mark his departure, for he loved his possessions more than the treasure in heaven, and sorrow without surrender is the rich man's tragedy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Jesus looked round and said to his disciples, How hardly shall they that have riches enter into the kingdom of God! Riches are a hard road to heaven, for they promise security, breed self-sufficiency, and hold the heart fast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The disciples were astonished at his words, but Jesus answered again, Children, how hard is it for them that trust in riches to enter into the kingdom of God! The hardness lies not in having riches but in trusting them, and trust in wealth shuts the kingdom's door. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God. The proverb's impossibility is deliberate: humanly, the rich man's entrance is as impossible as a camel through a needle's eye. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. They were astonished out of measure, saying among themselves, Who then can be saved? If the rich, who seem most blessed, cannot enter, the disciples fear no one can, and their astonishment is honest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Jesus looking upon them said, With men it is impossible, but not with God, for with God all things are possible. What is impossible to men is possible to God, and grace, not wealth or poverty, opens the kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Then Peter began to say, Lo, we have left all and have followed you. Peter's claim, though true in measure, seeks a reckoning, and Christ answers it with promise, not reproof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Jesus answered, Verily, there is no man that has left house, brethren, sisters, father, mother, wife, children, or lands, for my sake and the gospel's. Every loss for Christ and the gospel is named, that no sacrifice may think itself forgotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. But he shall receive a hundredfold now in this time, houses, brethren, sisters, mothers, children, and lands, with persecutions, and in the world to come eternal life. A hundredfold in this life comes in the church's fellowship, with persecutions mixed in, and eternal life crowns it in the world to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. But many that are first shall be last, and the last first. The reversal warns Peter's reckoning: first in sacrifice may be last in reward, and God orders the kingdom by grace, not by seniority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. They were in the way going up to Jerusalem, and Jesus went before them, and they were amazed, and as they followed, they were afraid, and he took again the twelve and began to tell them what things should happen to him. Going before them to danger amazed them, and fear followed, yet he again foretold what awaited him, that they might not be surprised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. Saying, Behold, we go up to Jerusalem, and the Son of man shall be delivered to the chief priests and scribes, and they shall condemn him to death, and shall deliver him to the Gentiles. The third prediction names the betrayers and the judges: priests and scribes condemn, Gentiles execute, and Jerusalem is the place of the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. They shall mock him, scourge him, spit upon him, kill him, and the third day he shall rise again. Mocking, scourging, and spitting precede killing, and the third day's rising closes the prediction as its only comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. James and John, the sons of Zebedee, came to him, saying, Master, we would that you should do for us whatever we shall desire. Their request, made in the shadow of the cross, asks a blank favour, and ambition blinds them to the hour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. He said to them, What would you that I should do for you? Christ asks them to name their desire, that its unfitness may be plain to themselves and to the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. They said, Grant to us that we may sit, one on your right hand and the other on your left hand, in your glory. Right and left hand in glory are the places of highest honour, and they ask them before the cup, mistaking glory for privilege. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. But Jesus said, You know not what you ask: can you drink of the cup that I drink of, and be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with? The cup and baptism are his suffering and death, and they do not know what they ask, for glory's road runs through both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. They said, We can, and Jesus said, You shall indeed drink of the cup that I drink of, and with the baptism that I am baptized with shall you be baptized. Their confident 'we can' is accepted as prophecy: both would share his suffering, James by the sword and John by long persecution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. But to sit on my right hand and on my left hand is not mine to give, but it shall be given to them for whom it is prepared. Places in glory are the Father's preparation, not the Son's favouritism, and ambition cannot claim what grace prepares. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. When the ten heard it, they began to be much displeased with James and John. The ten's displeasure was not holy zeal but rival ambition, for each wanted the places the two had asked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. But Jesus called them to him and said, You know that they who are accounted to rule over the Gentiles exercise lordship over them, and their great ones exercise authority upon them. Gentile rule is lordship and authority exercised downward, and Christ names it that his disciples may reject it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. But so shall it not be among you: but whoever will be great among you shall be your minister. Greatness in his kingdom is ministry, and the measure of honour is the measure of service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. Whoever of you will be the chiefest shall be servant of all. Chief place means lowest service, for the servant of all is the chiefest in Christ's reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many. The Son of man is the pattern: he came to serve, and his service ended in a ransom, his life given for many. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. They came to Jericho, and as he went out of Jericho with his disciples and a great number of people, blind Bartimaeus, son of Timaeus, sat by the highway side begging. A named beggar by the highway is the last healing before Jerusalem, and his name preserves the witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. When he heard that it was Jesus of Nazareth, he began to cry out, Jesus, son of David, have mercy on me. Hearing became faith: he named Jesus son of David, the Messiah's title, and cried for mercy, not for wages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. Many charged him that he should hold his peace, but he cried the more a great deal, Son of David, have mercy on me. Rebuke increased his cry, for earnest prayer grows louder under opposition, and he would not be silenced from mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. Jesus stood still and commanded him to be called, and they called the blind man, saying, Be of good comfort, rise; he calls you. Christ's standing still is his attention to one cry in a crowd, and the bystanders' comfort repeats his call. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. He, casting away his garment, rose and came to Jesus. The garment cast away shows haste and expectation: he left his beggar's cloak to come to his healer, as if he would not need it again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. Jesus answered and said, What will you that I should do to you? The blind man said, Lord, that I might receive my sight. Christ asks, though he knows, that faith may speak its desire plainly, and the man's answer is simply sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. Jesus said, Go your way; your faith has made you whole, and immediately he received his sight, and followed Jesus in the way. Faith is credited with the cure, sight came immediately, and the healed beggar's first use of sight was to follow Jesus on the road to Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
