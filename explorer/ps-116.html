The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 116 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 I love the LORD, because he hath heard my voice and my supplications.

2 Because he hath inclined his ear unto me, therefore will I call upon him as long as I live.

3 The sorrows of death compassed me, and the pains of hell gat hold upon me: I found trouble and sorrow.

4 Then called I upon the name of the LORD; O LORD, I beseech thee, deliver my soul.

5 Gracious is the LORD, and righteous; yea, our God is merciful.

6 The LORD preserveth the simple: I was brought low, and he helped me.

7 Return unto thy rest, O my soul; for the LORD hath dealt bountifully with thee.

8 For thou hast delivered my soul from death, mine eyes from tears, and my feet from falling.

9 I will walk before the LORD in the land of the living.

10 I believed, therefore have I spoken: I was greatly afflicted:

11 I said in my haste, All men are liars.

12 What shall I render unto the LORD for all his benefits toward me?

13 I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the LORD.

14 I will pay my vows unto the LORD now in the presence of all his people.

15 Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of his saints.

16 O LORD, truly I am thy servant; I am thy servant, and the son of thine handmaid: thou hast loosed my bonds.

17 I will offer to thee the sacrifice of thanksgiving, and will call upon the name of the LORD.

18 I will pay my vows unto the LORD now in the presence of all his people.

19 In the courts of the LORD’s house, in the midst of thee, O Jerusalem. Praise ye the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A personal thanksgiving: "I love the LORD, because he hath heard my voice and my supplications." The sorrows of death compassed the speaker and the pains of hell took hold, trouble and sorrow were found — then he called on the name of the LORD, "deliver my soul," and the gracious, righteous, merciful LORD preserved the simple, helped him when brought low, delivered his soul from death, eyes from tears, feet from falling. The question follows — what shall I render for all his benefits? — answered by taking the cup of salvation, calling on the LORD's name, paying vows in the presence of all his people, and offering the sacrifice of thanksgiving in the courts of the LORD's house in Jerusalem. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry opens with love born of answered prayer: David had humbly and earnestly begged mercy, and God graciously accepted it, took cognizance of his case and granted an answer of peace, so love of complacency answers God's love of compassion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry reads the inclined ear as wonderful condescension — God laying his ear, as it were, to whispered groanings — and as encouragement to pray for life: having sped well despite unworthiness, why glean in any other field, but call upon him every day to the last day? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry pictures sorrows likely to be his death compassing him on every side, pains of hell as terror of conscience from guilt, arresting him so he could not escape; without were fightings, within were fears, and melancholy sometimes creates its own trouble by indulging fancy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry calls this prayer the old and only remedy, not a last one, short but to the purpose and beggar-like: deliver my soul — save from death and from sin, which is what kills the soul — pleaded on God's graciousness itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry testifies God is both righteous and gracious: righteous, doing no wrong in afflicting, and gracious in supporting and delivering; speak of God as we have found him, and have we ever found him otherwise than just and good? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry says God preserves the simple — sincere and upright without guile, and also weak and helpless, unable to shift for themselves — because they commit themselves to him with no confidence in their own sufficiency; those under his protection shall be safe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry bids the soul return to its rest in God alone, not agitated by distrustful fears: God has dealt bountifully, providing for comfort and refreshment, so be satisfied — a word fit to close the eyes at night and at death, return to thy Noah, the ark of rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry counts a threefold deliverance: soul from death, a life often forfeited yet lengthened as a miracle of mercy; eyes from tears, kept from being swallowed by over-much sorrow until God wipes all tears away; feet from falling into sin, misery or the grave, held by God's right hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry makes walking before the Lord the devoted life: doing all in his presence and under his eye, in conformity and cheerful confidence, quickened by being spared in the land of the living — a land of mercy and opportunity, where a new life must be a new life indeed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry notes faith confessed with the mouth: David believed God's being, providence and promise — Samuel's assurance of sceptre for crook — and therefore spoke to God in prayer and praise, to his own soul, and to friends, though speaking cost him great affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry is honest about haste: under sore, long affliction David spoke rashly in consternation and flight, calling all men liars as friends deserted him, yet the best saints' faith is imperfect; what is spoken amiss in haste must by repentance be unsaid, and in the apostle's sense, let God be true and every man a liar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry hears a man sensible of many mercies and studious how to express gratitude: one benefit brings many to mind, and he asks what he shall render, not as if recompense were possible, but seeking something acceptable as acknowledgment of a grateful mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry takes the cup of salvation as drink-offering in memory of deliverance, and also as whatever cup God puts in his hand — affliction sanctified to salvation, as Christ's own cup, or consolation tasted as God's love — together with calling on the Lord again, for God counts his people's prayers an honour, not a trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry insists vows are debts to be paid presently, not delayed like sorry debtors, and publicly in the presence of all his people — not for ostentation, but unashamed, inviting others to join. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry says saints' deaths are precious in God's sight: he will not gratify Saul or Absalom with David's death, often prevents it when but a step away, orders its circumstances for the best, and will make killers pay dearly when inquisition is made for saints' blood — a comfort that should make us willing to die for Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry glories in servanthood over kingship: truly I am thy servant, by birth as son of God's handmaid and by redemption, since God loosed the bonds of death's sorrows — and the very bonds loosed shall tie him faster to his Master. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry repeats the thank-offering and the call: thankful hearts express gratitude in sacrifices of piety and charity, first giving themselves as living sacrifices, and God is well pleased with doing good and communicating as accompaniment to thanks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry repeats the public payment of vows, now in the presence of all his people, showing he was not ashamed of God's service and that others might be invited to join him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry places that public praise in the courts of the Lord's house, in the midst of Jerusalem, amid a crowd of Israelites, that devotion might be brought into more reputation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
