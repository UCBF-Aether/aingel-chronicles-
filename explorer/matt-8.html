The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Matthew 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 When he was come down from the mountain, great multitudes followed him.

2 And, behold, there came a leper and worshipped him, saying, Lord, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean.

3 And Jesus put forth his hand, and touched him, saying, I will; be thou clean. And immediately his leprosy was cleansed.

4 And Jesus saith unto him, See thou tell no man; but go thy way, shew thyself to the priest, and offer the gift that Moses commanded, for a testimony unto them.

5 And when Jesus was entered into Capernaum, there came unto him a centurion, beseeching him,

6 And saying, Lord, my servant lieth at home sick of the palsy, grievously tormented.

7 And Jesus saith unto him, I will come and heal him.

8 The centurion answered and said, Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldest come under my roof: but speak the word only, and my servant shall be healed.

9 For I am a man under authority, having soldiers under me: and I say to this man, Go, and he goeth; and to another, Come, and he cometh; and to my servant, Do this, and he doeth it.

10 When Jesus heard it, he marvelled, and said to them that followed, Verily I say unto you, I have not found so great faith, no, not in Israel.

11 And I say unto you, That many shall come from the east and west, and shall sit down with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, in the kingdom of heaven.

12 But the children of the kingdom shall be cast out into outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.

13 And Jesus said unto the centurion, Go thy way; and as thou hast believed, so be it done unto thee. And his servant was healed in the selfsame hour.

14 And when Jesus was come into Peter’s house, he saw his wife’s mother laid, and sick of a fever.

15 And he touched her hand, and the fever left her: and she arose, and ministered unto them.

16 When the even was come, they brought unto him many that were possessed with devils: and he cast out the spirits with his word, and healed all that were sick:

17 That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet, saying, Himself took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses.

18 Now when Jesus saw great multitudes about him, he gave commandment to depart unto the other side.

19 And a certain scribe came, and said unto him, Master, I will follow thee whithersoever thou goest.

20 And Jesus saith unto him, The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air have nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head.

21 And another of his disciples said unto him, Lord, suffer me first to go and bury my father.

22 But Jesus said unto him, Follow me; and let the dead bury their dead.

23 And when he was entered into a ship, his disciples followed him.

24 And, behold, there arose a great tempest in the sea, insomuch that the ship was covered with the waves: but he was asleep.

25 And his disciples came to him, and awoke him, saying, Lord, save us: we perish.

26 And he saith unto them, Why are ye fearful, O ye of little faith? Then he arose, and rebuked the winds and the sea; and there was a great calm.

27 But the men marvelled, saying, What manner of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey him!

28 And when he was come to the other side into the country of the Gergesenes, there met him two possessed with devils, coming out of the tombs, exceeding fierce, so that no man might pass by that way.

29 And, behold, they cried out, saying, What have we to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of God? art thou come hither to torment us before the time?

30 And there was a good way off from them an herd of many swine feeding.

31 So the devils besought him, saying, If thou cast us out, suffer us to go away into the herd of swine.

32 And he said unto them, Go. And when they were come out, they went into the herd of swine: and, behold, the whole herd of swine ran violently down a steep place into the sea, and perished in the waters.

33 And they that kept them fled, and went their ways into the city, and told every thing, and what was befallen to the possessed of the devils.

34 And, behold, the whole city came out to meet Jesus: and when they saw him, they besought him that he would depart out of their coasts.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A chapter of healings and authority: a leper cleansed, the centurion's servant healed by a word from afar, Peter's mother-in-law raised from fever, and many healed in the evening, fulfilling Isaiah's “Himself took our infirmities.” Jesus then speaks sharply to would-be followers about the cost of following, stills a storm on the sea at his disciples' fear, and casts devils from two men in the country of the Gergesenes into swine, after which the whole city asks him to depart. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When he was come down from the mountain, great multitudes followed him. This verse closes the foregoing sermon, and those to whom Christ has made himself known desire to know more of him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. There came a leper — Luke says a man full of leprosy, a loathsome, diffusive and, when thoroughly pronounced, incurable disease, furnishing the nation a familiar symbol of sin as loathsome, spreading and incurable. He worshipped him, beseeching and kneeling, saying, “Lord, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean”: sure of his power with a confidence that knew no doubt, unsure only of his willingness, a faith in the germ of that which now casts its crown before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand and touched him — a touch occasioning ceremonial defilement, and the leper's nearness itself against Levitical regulation, but he who had healing in his wings transcended such statutes. “I will; be thou clean”: by not assuring him of power he seals the man's confession of it, and by assuring him of the one thing doubted, his will, he makes a claim as divine as the cure; immediately the leprosy was cleansed, perfectly and instantaneously. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Jesus straitly charged him and sent him away: see thou tell no man, but go show thyself to the priest and offer the gift Moses commanded, for a testimony unto them — a palpable witness the Great Healer had come and God had visited his people. Mark records the sequel: the man blazed it abroad, so Jesus could no more openly enter the city, and withdrew into the wilderness and prayed, retreating from popular excitement into the secret place of the Most High. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. When Jesus was entered into Capernaum, there came unto him a centurion beseeching him. This centurion was a heathen, a Roman soldier, yet a godly man; no man's calling or place will be an excuse for unbelief and sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Lord, my servant lieth at home sick of the palsy, grievously tormented. We should concern ourselves for the souls of our children and servants, who are spiritually sick, feel not spiritual evils, and know not what is spiritually good, and bring them to Christ by faith and prayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Jesus saith unto him, I will come and heal him. The servant got a cure of his disease in the end, and the master the approval of his faith; what was said to him is said to all: Believe, and ye shall receive, only believe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The centurion answered, Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldest come under my roof, but speak the word only, and my servant shall be healed. Observe his self-abasement, made more humble by Christ's gracious dealings, and his great faith: the more diffident we are of ourselves, the stronger our confidence in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. For I am a man under authority, having soldiers under me: and I say to this man, Go, and he goeth; and to another, Come, and he cometh; and to my servant, Do this, and he doeth it. Herein the centurion owns Christ to have divine power and full command of all creatures and powers of nature, as a master over his servants; such servants we all should be to God, going and coming by his word and providence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. When Jesus heard it, he marvelled, and said to them that followed, Verily I say unto you, I have not found so great faith, no, not in Israel. But when the Son of man comes he finds little faith, therefore he finds little fruit; see the power of Christ, and the power of faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Many shall come from the east and west, and shall sit down with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, in the kingdom of heaven. An outward profession may cause us to be called children of the kingdom, but if we rest in that and have nothing else to show, we shall be cast out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. But the children of the kingdom shall be cast out into outer darkness: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth. The healing of our souls is at once the effect and evidence of our interest in the blood of Christ, not of bare profession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Jesus said unto the centurion, Go thy way; and as thou hast believed, so be it done unto thee. And his servant was healed in the selfsame hour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. When Jesus was come into Peter's house, he saw his wife's mother laid, and sick of a fever. Peter had a wife, yet was an apostle, Christ showing he approved the married state by kindness to Peter's wife's relations; the church forbidding ministers to marry goes contrary to that apostle. He had his wife's mother in his family, an example of kindness to relations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. He touched her hand, and the fever left her: and she arose, and ministered unto them. In spiritual healing the Scripture speaks the word and the Spirit gives the touch; those recovering from fevers are commonly weak some time after, but to show this cure above nature, the woman was at once well enough to go about the business of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. When the even was come, they brought unto him many that were possessed with devils: and he cast out the spirits with his word, and healed all that were sick. The miracles being noised abroad, many thronged to him, and he healed all, though the patient ever so mean and the case ever so bad. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet, saying, Himself took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses. There is more in those words to support and comfort us under bodily disease than in all the writings of the philosophers; let us not grudge labour, trouble or expense in doing good to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. When Jesus saw great multitudes about him, he gave commandment to depart unto the other side. The multitudes following after the sermon now press him to cross the lake, and the incidents of discipleship that follow test what following him will cost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. A certain scribe came, and said unto him, Master, I will follow thee whithersoever thou goest. One scribe might do more credit and service than twelve fishermen, but Christ saw his heart: his resolve seems from sudden conviction taken up without due consideration, and from a worldly, covetous principle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Jesus saith unto him, The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air have nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head. The scribe's speech shows transient emotion and temporary impulse rather than intelligent principle; Christ rejects not his offer, but will have him count the cost and weigh whether his attachment will abide in trial — the Rash or Precipitate Disciple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Another of his disciples said unto him, Lord, suffer me first to go and bury my father. This man was too slow, where the scribe was too hasty; delay in doing is as bad as hastiness in resolving. His father was most likely frail and aged, not yet dead, since burial took place the day of death; burying the dead is a good work, but it is not thy work at this time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. But Jesus said unto him, Follow me; and let the dead bury their dead — Luke adds, but go thou and preach the kingdom of God. The paradox turns on “dead” in two senses: leave nature's claims to those dead to the higher claims of the kingdom of grace, for the kingdom lies neglected and needy, and thou art called to preach it. This is the Procrastinating or Entangled Disciple; and power went with the word, for he did follow Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. When he was entered into a ship, his disciples followed him. It is a comfort to those who go down to the sea in ships and are often in perils there, to reflect they have a Saviour to trust in who knows what it is to be on the water and in storms there; those passing with Christ over the ocean of this world must expect storms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Behold, there arose a great tempest in the sea, insomuch that the ship was covered with the waves: but he was asleep. His human nature, like ours in everything but sin, was wearied, and he slept at this time to try the faith of his disciples; so in a soul, when lusts and temptations swell and God seems asleep to it, it is brought to the brink of despair and cries for a word from his mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. His disciples came to him, and awoke him, saying, Lord, save us: we perish. Many that have true faith are weak in it; Christ's disciples are apt to be disquieted with fears in a stormy day, tormenting themselves that things are bad and with dismal thoughts that they will be worse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. He saith unto them, Why are ye fearful, O ye of little faith? Then he arose, and rebuked the winds and the sea; and there was a great calm. Great storms of doubt and fear in the soul, under the power of the spirit of bondage, sometimes end in a wonderful calm, created and spoken by the Spirit of adoption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. But the men marvelled, saying, What manner of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey him! They never saw a storm so turned at once into a perfect calm; he that can do this can do anything, which encourages confidence and comfort in him in the most stormy day, within or without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. When he was come to the other side into the country of the Gergesenes, there met him two possessed with devils, coming out of the tombs, exceeding fierce, so that no man might pass by that way. The devils have nothing to do with Christ as a Saviour; they neither have, nor hope for, any benefit from him — the depth of divine love, that fallen man has so much to do with Christ when fallen angels have nothing to do with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Behold, they cried out, saying, What have we to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of God? art thou come hither to torment us before the time? The devils desire nothing to do with Christ as a Ruler, but it is not true they have nothing to do with him as a Judge, for they have, and they know it, and thus it is with all the children of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. There was a good way off from them an herd of many swine feeding. Satan and his instruments can go no further than Christ permits; they cannot break his hedge of protection about his people, and they cannot enter even a swine without his leave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. So the devils besought him, saying, If thou cast us out, suffer us to go away into the herd of swine. God often, for wise and holy ends, permits the efforts of Satan's rage; thus the devil hurries people to sin, to what they resolved against and know will be shame and grief to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. He said unto them, Go. And when they were come out, they went into the herd of swine: and, behold, the whole herd of swine ran violently down a steep place into the sea, and perished in the waters. They had leave, and the destruction that followed showed whose power held even the demons in check. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. They that kept them fled, and went their ways into the city, and told every thing, and what was befallen to the possessed of the devils. Miserable is the condition of those led captive by the devil at his will, hurried to sin against resolution and knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Behold, the whole city came out to meet Jesus: and when they saw him, they besought him that he would depart out of their coasts. There are a great many who prefer their swine before the Saviour, and so come short of Christ and salvation, desiring him to depart because he and his word would destroy their brutish lusts, those swine they give themselves up to feed; and justly will Christ forsake all that are weary of him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Satan and his instruments can go no further than Christ permits; they cannot break his hedge of protection about his people, and they cannot enter even a swine without his leave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. So the devils besought him, saying, If thou cast us out, suffer us to go away into the herd of swine. God often, for wise and holy ends, permits the efforts of Satan's rage; thus the devil hurries people to sin, to what they resolved against and know will be shame and grief to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. He said unto them, Go. And when they were come out, they went into the herd of swine: and, behold, the whole herd of swine ran violently down a steep place into the sea, and perished in the waters. They had leave, and the destruction that followed showed whose power held even the demons in check. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. They that kept them fled, and went their ways into the city, and told every thing, and what was befallen to the possessed of the devils. Miserable is the condition of those led captive by the devil at his will, hurried to sin against resolution and knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Behold, the whole city came out to meet Jesus: and when they saw him, they besought him that he would depart out of their coasts. There are a great many who prefer their swine before the Saviour, and so come short of Christ and salvation, desiring him to depart because he and his word would destroy their brutish lusts, those swine they give themselves up to feed; and justly will Christ forsake all that are weary of him. Investigation (editorial — a parallel question, not a conclusion)

Parallel. The leper's healing and the charge to tell no man appear in Matthew and, in the notes' terms, in Mark and Luke as well, and the “let the dead bury their dead” saying appears in Luke too. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Difference. Luke describes the leper as a man full of leprosy; Mark records the sequel, that the man blazed the matter abroad; and Luke adds to the discipleship saying, “but go thou and preach the kingdom of God”. Matthew alone, in these notes, gives the saying in its shorter form. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
