The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Job answered and said,

2 How long will ye vex my soul, and break me in pieces with words?

3 These ten times have ye reproached me: ye are not ashamed that ye make yourselves strange to me.

4 And be it indeed that I have erred, mine error remaineth with myself.

5 If indeed ye will magnify yourselves against me, and plead against me my reproach:

6 Know now that God hath overthrown me, and hath compassed me with his net.

7 Behold, I cry out of wrong, but I am not heard: I cry aloud, but there is no judgment.

8 He hath fenced up my way that I cannot pass, and he hath set darkness in my paths.

9 He hath stripped me of my glory, and taken the crown from my head.

10 He hath destroyed me on every side, and I am gone: and mine hope hath he removed like a tree.

11 He hath also kindled his wrath against me, and he counteth me unto him as one of his enemies.

12 His troops come together, and raise up their way against me, and encamp round about my tabernacle.

13 He hath put my brethren far from me, and mine acquaintance are verily estranged from me.

14 My kinsfolk have failed, and my familiar friends have forgotten me.

15 They that dwell in mine house, and my maids, count me for a stranger: I am an alien in their sight.

16 I called my servant, and he gave me no answer; I intreated him with my mouth.

17 My breath is strange to my wife, though I intreated for the children’s sake of mine own body.

18 Yea, young children despised me; I arose, and they spake against me.

19 All my inward friends abhorred me: and they whom I loved are turned against me.

20 My bone cleaveth to my skin and to my flesh, and I am escaped with the skin of my teeth.

21 Have pity upon me, have pity upon me, O ye my friends; for the hand of God hath touched me.

22 Why do ye persecute me as God, and are not satisfied with my flesh?

23 Oh that my words were now written! oh that they were printed in a book!

24 That they were graven with an iron pen and lead in the rock for ever!

25 For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth:

26 And though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God:

27 Whom I shall see for myself, and mine eyes shall behold, and not another; though my reins be consumed within me.

28 But ye should say, Why persecute we him, seeing the root of the matter is found in me?

29 Be ye afraid of the sword: for wrath bringeth the punishments of the sword, that ye may know there is a judgment.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Job answers Bildad by counting the cost of his friends’ words: these ten times they have reproached him and made themselves strange to him, persecuting him as God does. Yet in the middle of the reproach he declares the hope the chapter is remembered for: I know that my redeemer liveth, and shall stand at the latter day upon the earth, and though worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God — with his own eyes, and not another’s. He closes by warning his friends that wrath brings the punishments of the sword, and there is a judgment. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Job answers Bildad, protesting his friends' unkind usage. Henry notes the friends blamed him as wicked because he was afflicted, and Job shows their condemnation was capable of excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. How long will the friends vex Job's soul and break him in pieces with words? Henry observes harsh language from friends greatly adds to the weight of afflictions, yet it is best not to lay it to heart lest resentment be harboured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. These ten times the friends have reproached Job, and are not ashamed to make themselves strange to him. The repeated reproaches have become estrangement, not comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. If Job has truly erred, his error remains with himself. The friends' case fails even on its own terms: an error of Job's would not justify their treatment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. If indeed the friends magnify themselves against Job and plead his reproach against him, they use his affliction as their argument. Henry notes what they condemned was capable of excuse, yet they turned it into accusation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Know now that God has overthrown Job and compassed him with his net. Job attributes the net to God himself, not merely to men: his affliction is divinely encompassed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Job cries out of wrong, but is not heard; he cries aloud, but there is no judgment. The complaint is not only suffering but unheard appeal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. God has fenced up Job's way so he cannot pass, and set darkness in his paths. Henry places this in the doleful complaints of verses 8-22, where Job traces his afflictions to God as their author. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. God has stripped Job of his glory and taken the crown from his head. Honour as well as wealth is removed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. God has destroyed Job on every side and he is gone, uprooting his hope like a tree. The image is total: every side attacked, hope itself uprooted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. God's wrath is kindled against Job, and he counts him as one of his enemies. Henry warns it is a common mistake to think those whom God afflicts he treats as enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. God's troops come together, raise up their way against Job, and encamp round about his tabernacle. Affliction arrives as an army besieging his house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. God has put Job's brethren far from him, and his acquaintance are verily estranged from him. Henry notes how uncertain is the friendship of men, though God as friend will not fail in need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Job's kinsfolk have failed, and his familiar friends have forgotten him. Kinship and familiarity alike prove hollow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Those who dwell in Job's house and his maids count him a stranger, and he is an alien in their sight. Even his household treats him as an outsider. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Job calls his servant, and he gives no answer, though he entreats him with his mouth. The master who once commanded now begs and is ignored. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Job's wife finds his breath strange, though he entreats for the children's sake of his own body. Henry notes the body, after all care, is consumed by diseases it has in itself, and even the nearest bond is strained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Young children despise Job, and when he arises they speak against him. Contempt reaches down to the youngest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. All Job's inward friends abhor him, and those he loved are turned against him. The inner circle completes the desertion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Job's bone cleaves to his skin and flesh, and he has escaped with the skin of his teeth. Wasting has left almost nothing between him and death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Have pity upon Job, O his friends, for the hand of God has touched him. Henry notes Job recommends himself to his friends' compassion and justly blames their harshness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Why do the friends persecute Job as God does, and are not satisfied with his flesh? To add human persecution to divine affliction is to do God's work of wounding without God's right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Oh that Job's words were now written, printed in a book, graven with iron and lead in rock forever! Henry notes the Spirit powerfully wrought on Job here: he witnessed a good confession, declaring sound faith and assured hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Oh that his words were graven with an iron pen and lead in the rock forever! The permanence he seeks for his testimony matches the permanence of the hope it declares. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. For Job knows his Redeemer lives, and shall stand at the latter day upon the earth. Henry explains Job was taught to believe in a living Redeemer, his Redeemer, though not yet come in the flesh, who would appear as Judge to raise the dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And though after his skin worms destroy this body, yet in his flesh shall Job see God. Henry notes Job comforted himself with expectation of the resurrection of the dead and the life of the world to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Whom Job shall see for himself, and his eyes shall behold, and not another, though his reins are consumed within him. The seeing is personal and bodily: his own eyes, not another's, shall behold God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. But the friends should say, why persecute we him, seeing the root of the matter is found in him? Henry explains the root as a living, quickening, commanding principle of grace in the heart, as necessary to religion as a root is to a tree's fixedness and fruitfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Be afraid of the sword, for wrath brings the punishments of the sword, that you may know there is a judgment. Henry notes Job and his friends agreed in the root of the matter, the belief of another world, and the warning is that judgment will test persecutors too. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Where the speakers disagree

The friends, Job says, have reproached him ten times and treat affliction itself as proof of wickedness, persecuting him as God does. Job answers with a different ground of hope: his redeemer lives and he shall see God in his flesh, with his own eyes. He also turns their judgment back on them, warning that wrath brings the punishments of the sword and there is a judgment. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
