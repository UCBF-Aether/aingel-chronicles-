The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Hear, ye children, the instruction of a father, and attend to know understanding.

2 For I give you good doctrine, forsake ye not my law.

3 For I was my father’s son, tender and only beloved in the sight of my mother.

4 He taught me also, and said unto me, Let thine heart retain my words: keep my commandments, and live.

5 Get wisdom, get understanding: forget it not; neither decline from the words of my mouth.

6 Forsake her not, and she shall preserve thee: love her, and she shall keep thee.

7 Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom: and with all thy getting get understanding.

8 Exalt her, and she shall promote thee: she shall bring thee to honour, when thou dost embrace her.

9 She shall give to thine head an ornament of grace: a crown of glory shall she deliver to thee.

10 Hear, O my son, and receive my sayings; and the years of thy life shall be many.

11 I have taught thee in the way of wisdom; I have led thee in right paths.

12 When thou goest, thy steps shall not be straitened; and when thou runnest, thou shalt not stumble.

13 Take fast hold of instruction; let her not go: keep her; for she is thy life.

14 Enter not into the path of the wicked, and go not in the way of evil men.

15 Avoid it, pass not by it, turn from it, and pass away.

16 For they sleep not, except they have done mischief; and their sleep is taken away, unless they cause some to fall.

17 For they eat the bread of wickedness, and drink the wine of violence.

18 But the path of the just is as the shining light, that shineth more and more unto the perfect day.

19 The way of the wicked is as darkness: they know not at what they stumble.

20 My son, attend to my words; incline thine ear unto my sayings.

21 Let them not depart from thine eyes; keep them in the midst of thine heart.

22 For they are life unto those that find them, and health to all their flesh.

23 Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.

24 Put away from thee a froward mouth, and perverse lips put far from thee.

25 Let thine eyes look right on, and let thine eyelids look straight before thee.

26 Ponder the path of thy feet, and let all thy ways be established.

27 Turn not to the right hand nor to the left: remove thy foot from evil.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A father passes on what his own father taught him: get wisdom, get understanding, exalt her and she will crown you. Two paths are set against each other, the shining path of the just that grows brighter, and the darkness of the wicked who do not know what they stumble over. The counsel narrows to the heart: keep it with all diligence, put away perverse speech, and look straight ahead. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hear, ye children, the instruction of a father, and attend to know understanding, welcoming instruction even when it carries reproof and correction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The doctrine given is good, so forsake not the law, for wise and godly men in every age agree that true wisdom consists in obedience united to happiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Solomon was his father's son, tender and only beloved, and his parents loved him and therefore taught him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. His father taught him to let his heart retain words and keep commandments and live, taking pains for wisdom above the wealth of this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Get wisdom, then hold it: the section makes wisdom the one thing needful, to be taken pains for above the wealth of this world, neither forgotten nor declined from. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Forsake her not and she shall preserve thee; love her and she shall keep thee, for an interest in Christ's salvation is necessary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Wisdom is the principal thing, therefore get wisdom, and with all thy getting get understanding, taking more pains for it than for worldly wealth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Exalt wisdom and she repays the honour: promotion and honour come to the pupil who embraces her, as the section's rule that esteem for wisdom is answered with standing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Henry's section crowns wisdom's pupil with grace now and glory at last: the ornament and the crown are one wisdom's two gifts, not two masters' wages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Hear and receive these sayings and the years of thy life shall be many, for a soul without true wisdom and grace is a dead soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The father has taught in the way of wisdom and led in right paths, and by taking and keeping fast hold of instruction our path will be plain before us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Taking and keeping fast hold of instruction is what keeps the path plain: by that hold the walker avoids being straitened or stumbling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Holding instruction fast is the exhortation of vv 1-13: get wisdom and take pains for it above the wealth of the world, for a soul without true wisdom and grace is a dead soul, and keeping hold keeps the path plain and free from stumbling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Enter not into the path of the wicked nor go in the way of evil men, for though it may seem pleasant and the nearest way, it is evil and will end ill. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Avoid it, pass not by it, turn from it and pass away: keep not at a due distance but at a great distance, never thinking you can get far enough from it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The wicked way ends ill because it is pursued without rest: mischief must be done and someone made to fall before its travellers can sleep, sin as a nightly task. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Their way is darkness, so they feed on darkness: bread of wickedness and wine of violence are the daily fare of a path that ends ill, however pleasant it seems. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. The righteous way is light that grows: Christ is their Way and he is the Light, and the saints shine brighter until they reach heaven and shine as the sun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. The way of the wicked is as darkness: they know not at what they stumble, falling into sin without knowing how to avoid it and into trouble without seeking why God contends with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Attend to the words and incline the ear to the sayings, for attentive hearing of God's word is a sign of grace begun and a means of carrying it on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Let them not depart from thine eyes but keep them in the midst of thine heart, for in God's word there is a remedy for all diseases of the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. The sayings are not information but medicine: in God's word lies a proper remedy for all diseases of the soul, and that remedy is life and health to the whole man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Keep thy heart with all diligence, setting a strict guard upon the soul to keep it from doing hurt and getting hurt, for out of it are the issues of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Put away a froward mouth and perverse lips far from thee, seeking from the Lord Jesus the living water of the sanctifying Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Let thine eyes look right on and thine eyelids straight before thee, turned from beholding vanity and walking by the rule of God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. Ponder the path of thy feet and let all thy ways be established, treading in the steps of the Lord and Master. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Turn not to the right hand nor to the left, and remove thy foot from evil, following Christ more closely for the time to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
