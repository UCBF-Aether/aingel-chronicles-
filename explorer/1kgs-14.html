The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Kings 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 At that time Abijah the son of Jeroboam fell sick.

2 And Jeroboam said to his wife, Arise, I pray thee, and disguise thyself, that thou be not known to be the wife of Jeroboam; and get thee to Shiloh: behold, there is Ahijah the prophet, which told me that I should be king over this people.

3 And take with thee ten loaves, and cracknels, and a cruse of honey, and go to him: he shall tell thee what shall become of the child.

4 And Jeroboam’s wife did so, and arose, and went to Shiloh, and came to the house of Ahijah. But Ahijah could not see; for his eyes were set by reason of his age.

5 And the LORD said unto Ahijah, Behold, the wife of Jeroboam cometh to ask a thing of thee for her son; for he is sick: thus and thus shalt thou say unto her: for it shall be, when she cometh in, that she shall feign herself to be another woman.

6 And it was so, when Ahijah heard the sound of her feet, as she came in at the door, that he said, Come in, thou wife of Jeroboam; why feignest thou thyself to be another? for I am sent to thee with heavy tidings.

7 Go, tell Jeroboam, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Forasmuch as I exalted thee from among the people, and made thee prince over my people Israel,

8 And rent the kingdom away from the house of David, and gave it thee: and yet thou hast not been as my servant David, who kept my commandments, and who followed me with all his heart, to do that only which was right in mine eyes;

9 But hast done evil above all that were before thee: for thou hast gone and made thee other gods, and molten images, to provoke me to anger, and hast cast me behind thy back:

10 Therefore, behold, I will bring evil upon the house of Jeroboam, and will cut off from Jeroboam him that pisseth against the wall, and him that is shut up and left in Israel, and will take away the remnant of the house of Jeroboam, as a man taketh away dung, till it be all gone.

11 Him that dieth of Jeroboam in the city shall the dogs eat; and him that dieth in the field shall the fowls of the air eat: for the LORD hath spoken it.

12 Arise thou therefore, get thee to thine own house: and when thy feet enter into the city, the child shall die.

13 And all Israel shall mourn for him, and bury him: for he only of Jeroboam shall come to the grave, because in him there is found some good thing toward the LORD God of Israel in the house of Jeroboam.

14 Moreover the LORD shall raise him up a king over Israel, who shall cut off the house of Jeroboam that day: but what? even now.

15 For the LORD shall smite Israel, as a reed is shaken in the water, and he shall root up Israel out of this good land, which he gave to their fathers, and shall scatter them beyond the river, because they have made their groves, provoking the LORD to anger.

16 And he shall give Israel up because of the sins of Jeroboam, who did sin, and who made Israel to sin.

17 And Jeroboam’s wife arose, and departed, and came to Tirzah: and when she came to the threshold of the door, the child died;

18 And they buried him; and all Israel mourned for him, according to the word of the LORD, which he spake by the hand of his servant Ahijah the prophet.

19 And the rest of the acts of Jeroboam, how he warred, and how he reigned, behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.

20 And the days which Jeroboam reigned were two and twenty years: and he slept with his fathers, and Nadab his son reigned in his stead.

21 And Rehoboam the son of Solomon reigned in Judah. Rehoboam was forty and one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city which the LORD did choose out of all the tribes of Israel, to put his name there. And his mother’s name was Naamah an Ammonitess.

22 And Judah did evil in the sight of the LORD, and they provoked him to jealousy with their sins which they had committed, above all that their fathers had done.

23 For they also built them high places, and images, and groves, on every high hill, and under every green tree.

24 And there were also sodomites in the land: and they did according to all the abominations of the nations which the LORD cast out before the children of Israel.

25 And it came to pass in the fifth year of king Rehoboam, that Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem:

26 And he took away the treasures of the house of the LORD, and the treasures of the king’s house; he even took away all: and he took away all the shields of gold which Solomon had made.

27 And king Rehoboam made in their stead brasen shields, and committed them unto the hands of the chief of the guard, which kept the door of the king’s house.

28 And it was so, when the king went into the house of the LORD, that the guard bare them, and brought them back into the guard chamber.

29 Now the rest of the acts of Rehoboam, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

30 And there was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all their days.

31 And Rehoboam slept with his fathers, and was buried with his fathers in the city of David. And his mother’s name was Naamah an Ammonitess. And Abijam his son reigned in his stead.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jeroboam's sick son sends his disguised wife to Ahijah, who sees through the disguise and pronounces judgment on Jeroboam's house; the child dies as she crosses the threshold. The chapter closes with the short records of Jeroboam and Rehoboam, and Shishak of Egypt plundering the temple. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. At that time Abijah, Jeroboam's son, fell sick, and the king who had made gods of gold now faced a trouble no idol could heal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jeroboam told his wife to disguise herself so she would not be known as his wife, and go to Shiloh to Ahijah, who had told him he would be king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. She was to take ten loaves, cracknels, and a cruse of honey, a peasant's present for a prophet, and ask what would become of the child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Jeroboam's wife reached Ahijah's house at Shiloh, but the prophet could not see for age — Henry notes the paradox: blind through age, he was still blessed with visions of the Almighty, which need no bodily eyes and are rather favoured by the want of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The Lord told Ahijah that Jeroboam's wife was coming, feigning to be another woman, to ask about her son, so disguise was useless before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. At the sound of her feet Ahijah named her at the door, wife of Jeroboam, why feign to be another — Henry's point is plain dealing: the prophet was no courtier, and those who hide from God in disguise will be confounded on the day of discovery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Go tell Jeroboam, said the Lord, that though God exalted him from among the people and made him prince over Israel, he had not kept covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. God had rent the kingdom from David's house and given it to Jeroboam, yet he had not been as David, who followed God with all his heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Jeroboam's evil stood above all before him, Henry argues, because Saul never worshipped idols and Solomon did so only in his dotage without making Israel sin — while Jeroboam's gilded calves changed the truth of God into a lie and provoked him to anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Therefore God would bring evil on Jeroboam's house and cut off every male, him that is shut up and left in Israel, leaving none to continue his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Him that died in the city the dogs would eat, and him that died in the field the fowls would eat, for the Lord had spoken it, a burial of dishonour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. She was to arise and go home, and when her feet entered the city the child would die, the prophecy giving her a sign she could not miss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. All Israel would mourn this child because in him alone was found some good thing toward God — Henry calls a good child in Jeroboam's house a miracle of grace, and notes God often takes soonest those he loves best. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Moreover the Lord would raise up a king over Israel who would cut off Jeroboam's house that day, and what day it would be, judgment was already on its way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 16. He would give Israel up because of Jeroboam's sins, who sinned and made Israel to sin, the king's sin becoming the nation's ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Jeroboam's wife arose and came to Tirzah, and when she came to the threshold of the door, the child died, exactly as Ahijah had said. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. They buried him and all Israel mourned for him, according to the Lord's word spoken by Ahijah, the one good son lamented by the whole nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The rest of Jeroboam's acts, how he warred and reigned, are written in the chronicles of Israel's kings, a fuller record beyond this account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Jeroboam reigned twenty-two years, slept with his fathers, and Nadab his son reigned in his stead, the house continuing only to be cut off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Rehoboam son of Solomon reigned in Judah, forty-one years old at his accession, reigning seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city God had chosen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 23. Judah built high places, images and groves on every high hill and under every green tree — Henry notes the folly: they fancied they exalted God on the hills and pleased him in the pleasant shade, while profaning his name and ordinances by serving idols with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Sodomites were in the land, following the abominations of nations God had cast out — Henry's sequence is stark: they dishonoured God by one sin, and God left them to dishonour themselves by another, so that doing like the cast-out, they could expect to be cast out like them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. In Rehoboam's fifth year Shishak of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, the first foreign invasion of the divided kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. He took away the treasures of the Lord's house and the king's house, even all, including Solomon's shields of gold, wealth gathered in peace now carried off in war. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Rehoboam made brass shields in their stead and committed them to the chief of the guard who kept the king's house door, gold replaced by brass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. When the king went into the Lord's house, the guard bore the shields and brought them back to the guard chamber, a diminished glory kept up by ceremony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. The rest of Rehoboam's acts are written in the chronicles of Judah's kings, the record of a reign that began with a lost kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all their days, the division hardening into perpetual border war. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Rehoboam slept with his fathers and was buried in Jerusalem, his mother Naamah an Ammonitess — Henry's summary is bleak: no good is said of him, and where his father, grandfather and grandson reigned forty years apiece, sin shortened his reign to seventeen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
