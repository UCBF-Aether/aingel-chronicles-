The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Deuteronomy 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 When the LORD thy God hath cut off the nations, whose land the LORD thy God giveth thee, and thou succeedest them, and dwellest in their cities, and in their houses;

2 Thou shalt separate three cities for thee in the midst of thy land, which the LORD thy God giveth thee to possess it.

3 Thou shalt prepare thee a way, and divide the coasts of thy land, which the LORD thy God giveth thee to inherit, into three parts, that every slayer may flee thither.

4 And this is the case of the slayer, which shall flee thither, that he may live: Whoso killeth his neighbour ignorantly, whom he hated not in time past;

5 As when a man goeth into the wood with his neighbour to hew wood, and his hand fetcheth a stroke with the axe to cut down the tree, and the head slippeth from the helve, and lighteth upon his neighbour, that he die; he shall flee unto one of those cities, and live:

6 Lest the avenger of the blood pursue the slayer, while his heart is hot, and overtake him, because the way is long, and slay him; whereas he was not worthy of death, inasmuch as he hated him not in time past.

7 Wherefore I command thee, saying, Thou shalt separate three cities for thee.

8 And if the LORD thy God enlarge thy coast, as he hath sworn unto thy fathers, and give thee all the land which he promised to give unto thy fathers;

9 If thou shalt keep all these commandments to do them, which I command thee this day, to love the LORD thy God, and to walk ever in his ways; then shalt thou add three cities more for thee, beside these three:

10 That innocent blood be not shed in thy land, which the LORD thy God giveth thee for an inheritance, and so blood be upon thee.

11 But if any man hate his neighbour, and lie in wait for him, and rise up against him, and smite him mortally that he die, and fleeth into one of these cities:

12 Then the elders of his city shall send and fetch him thence, and deliver him into the hand of the avenger of blood, that he may die.

13 Thine eye shall not pity him, but thou shalt put away the guilt of innocent blood from Israel, that it may go well with thee.

14 Thou shalt not remove thy neighbour’s landmark, which they of old time have set in thine inheritance, which thou shalt inherit in the land that the LORD thy God giveth thee to possess it.

15 One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity, or for any sin, in any sin that he sinneth: at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established.

16 If a false witness rise up against any man to testify against him that which is wrong;

17 Then both the men, between whom the controversy is, shall stand before the LORD, before the priests and the judges, which shall be in those days;

18 And the judges shall make diligent inquisition: and, behold, if the witness be a false witness, and hath testified falsely against his brother;

19 Then shall ye do unto him, as he had thought to have done unto his brother: so shalt thou put the evil away from among you.

20 And those which remain shall hear, and fear, and shall henceforth commit no more any such evil among you.

21 And thine eye shall not pity; but life shall go for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Once settled, Israel must have cities of refuge ready: three west of the Jordan to add to the three already set aside east of it, so that someone who kills a neighbour by accident can flee there and live until trial. Deliberate murder is excluded from that protection. The chapter also forbids moving a neighbour's landmark, requires two or three witnesses for any charge, and punishes a false witness with the penalty he sought for his brother. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Possession brings obligation: once Israel dwells in the conquered cities and houses, the refuge law must already be in place, so that bloodguilt does not take root in the new inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Three more cities west of Jordan complete the pattern Henry traces through the chapter: refuge must lie within reach on both sides of the river, so that no slayer need outrun the avenger to find safety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The way must be prepared and the land divided in three, so that refuge lies within reach wherever the slayer stands: Henry's section makes the whole provision turn on a man not dying for what was not his willing act, and a refuge too far away would defeat it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The case turns on ignorance and absence of hatred: the slayer may live not because killing is light, but because the law distinguishes a death without malice from murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The axe-head case is Henry's pattern of unknowing manslaughter: no prior hatred, no intent, only a slipped tool, and that absence of malice is exactly what separates a man who may live in refuge from a murderer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The cities of refuge exist because a slayer pursued in hot blood might be killed for what was not his willing act; Henry reads that refuge as Christ, to whom those who flee from their sins are safe, though never as shelter for continuing in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Wherefore I command you, saying, You shall separate three cities for yourself, the provision being that a man should not die for what was not his willing act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Enlargement is promise, not blank grant: the extra land comes as God swore to the fathers, and the next verse ties three added cities to keeping the commandments, so blessing expands only with obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. If you keep all these commandments to do them, commanded this day, to love the Lord and walk ever in his ways, then you shall add three cities more for yourself, beside these three. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Innocent blood shed without refuge would leave bloodguilt on the whole land: the cities exist so that an unknowing slayer's death does not become Israel's corporate guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The marks are premeditation, hatred, ambush and rising up, so the law can tell murder from mischance before the man ever reaches a city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Then the elders of his city shall send and fetch him from there, and deliver him into the hand of the avenger of blood, that he may die; there is no refuge in Christ for presumptuous sinners who go on in their trespasses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Pity must not spare the murderer: sparing him would leave innocent blood unpurged from Israel, and the nation's welfare turns on purging it rather than on mercy to the guilty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. You shall not remove your neighbour's landmark, which they of old time have set in your inheritance, which you shall inherit in the land the Lord gives you to possess; it is God's will that everyone should know his own, and this moral precept is still binding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The rule guards both ways: it protects the accused from a lone accuser and gives the court a test, agreement of independent witnesses, before any matter is treated as established. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. A witness who rises to testify falsely faces, in Henry's judgment, the justest of laws: he must suffer the very punishment he sought to have inflicted on the man he accused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The trial is held as before the Lord himself: priests and judges sit, but the controversy is judged in God's presence, which is why a false oath there carries the weight it does. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Judges must inquire diligently before condemning: a witness is not presumed true because he swore, and the court must test whether his testimony against his brother is false. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Then shall you do to him as he had thought to have done to his brother, so you shall put the evil away from among you; a false witness suffers the punishment he sought for the accused, and no law could be more just. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Public punishment of the false witness is meant to be heard and feared, so that the rest commit no more such evil; Henry adds the private warning not to join in slander even outside the courtroom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The talion measures punishment to the injury: life for life, eye for eye, so that vengeance cannot exceed the wrong, and the false witness in particular suffers exactly what he sought for his brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

Glosses below are for words a first-time reader may stumble on in this chapter; they explain the chapter's own usage and add nothing to it.

  • Cities of refuge. Cities set apart so that a person who kills a neighbour unawares — without prior hatred — can flee there, stand before the congregation, and live, rather than be killed by the avenger of blood before trial. Interpreted Chapter text, Deuteronomy 19:4–7, and study note
