The Text — Jubilees 47 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the seventh week, in the seventh year, in the forty-seventh jubilee, thy father went forth from the land of Canaan, and thou wast born in the fourth week, in the sixth year thereof, in the forty-eighth jubilee ; this was the time of tribulation on the children of Israel.

2 And Pharaoh, king of Egypt, issued a command regarding them that they

3 , pp. 321-352.

4 should cast all their male children which were born into the river.

5 And they cast them in for seven months until the day that thou wast born.

6 And thy mother hid thee for three months, and they told regarding her.

7 And she made an ark for thee, and covered it with pitch and asphalt, and placed it in the flags on the bank of the river, and she placed thee in it seven days, and thy mother came by night and suckled thee, and by day Miriam, thy sister, guarded thee from the birds.

8 And in those days Tharmuth the daughter of Pharaoh, came to bathe in the river, and she heard thy voice crying, and she told her maidens to bring thee forth, and they brought thee unto her.

9 And she took thee out of the ark, and she had compassion on thee.

10 And thy sister said unto her : " Shall I go and call unto thee one of the Hebrew women to nurse and suckle this babe for thee?" And she said (unto her) : " Go." 8.

11 And she went and called thy mother Jochebed and she gave her wages, and she nursed thee.

12 And afterwards, when thou wast grown up, they brought thee unto the daughter of Pharaoh, and thou didst become her son, and Amram thy father taught thee writing and after thou hadst completed three weeks they brought thee into the royal court.

13 And thou wast three weeks of years at court until the time when thou didst go forth from the royal court and didst see an Egyptian smiting thy friend who was of the children of Israel, and thou didst slay him and hide him in the sand.

14 And on the second day thou didst find two of the children of Israel striving together, and thou didst say to him who was doing the wrong : " Why dost thou smite thy brother? ' 12.

15 And he was angry and indignant, and said : " Who made thee a prince and a judge over us?

16 Thinkest thou to kill me as thou killedst the Egyptian yesterday ?

17 " And thou didst fear and flee on account of these words.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: In the seventh week, in the seventh year, in the forty-seventh jubilee, Moses' father went forth from Canaan, and Moses was born in the fourth week, in the sixth year thereof, in the forty-eighth jubilee, in the time of tribulation on Israel. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the seventh week, in the seventh year, in the forty-seventh jubilee, Moses' father went forth from Canaan, and Moses was born in the fourth week, in the sixth year thereof, in the forty-eighth jubilee, in the time of tribulation on Israel. Henry notes the order of providence: just when Pharaoh's cruelty rose to its height by ordering the Hebrew children drowned, the deliverer was born, for when men contrive the ruin of the church, God prepares its salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Pharaoh, king of Egypt, issued a command regarding the Israelites, that their male children should be dealt with. Henry notes that Pharaoh gave orders to drown all the male children of the Hebrews, the enemy attempting to destroy the church in its infant state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The command was that all male children born should be cast into the river. Henry observes that the murdering of the infants was Pharaoh's cruelty at its height, and that the deliverer was born at that very time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The Israelites cast their children into the river for seven months, until the day Moses was born. The seven months of drowning precede the birth that would end the drowning, the tribulation reaching its height as the deliverer arrived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Moses' mother hid him for three months, and word was told regarding her. Henry notes that Moses' parents hid him by faith, relying on the promise that Israel should be preserved, and that faith quickens the use of lawful means for obtaining mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. His mother made an ark, covered it with pitch and asphalt, placed it in the flags on the river's bank, placed Moses in it seven days, and came by night to suckle him, while by day Miriam his sister guarded him from the birds. Henry notes that at three months' end, when they could hide the infant no longer, they put him in an ark of bulrushes by the river's brink and set his sister to watch, and that Moses never had stronger protection than when he lay alone, a helpless babe upon the waves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. In those days Tharmuth, Pharaoh's daughter, came to bathe in the river, heard the child's voice crying, and told her maidens to bring him forth, and they brought him to her. Henry notes that providence brought Pharaoh's daughter to the place where the forlorn infant lay, inclining her heart to pity it, which she dared do when none else durst. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Pharaoh's daughter took Moses out of the ark and had compassion on him. Henry observes that Pharaoh cruelly sought to destroy Israel, but his own daughter had pity on a Hebrew child, and without knowing it preserved Israel's deliverer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Moses' sister asked Pharaoh's daughter whether she should go and call one of the Hebrew women to nurse and suckle the babe, and she said go. Henry notes that the sister brought the mother into the place of a nurse, so that Moses was provided with a good nurse, even his own mother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The sister went and called Moses' mother Jochebed, and Pharaoh's daughter gave her wages, and she nursed him. Henry notes God's care of Moses in his infancy, and that his own mother, hired as nurse, could teach him the faith of his people under the princess's protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Afterwards, when Moses was grown up, they brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son, and Amram his father taught him writing, and after he had completed three weeks they brought him into the royal court. Henry notes that Moses was treated as the son of Pharaoh's daughter, and that many obscure and poor by birth are raised high by surprising providences, to make men know that God rules. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Moses was three weeks of years at court, until he went forth and saw an Egyptian smiting his friend of the children of Israel, and slew him and hid him in the sand. Henry notes that Moses boldly owned the cause of God's people, in faith, with the full purpose of leaving the honours, wealth, and pleasures of his rank among the Egyptians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. On the second day Moses found two Israelites striving together, and said to the wrongdoer, why dost thou smite thy brother? Henry notes that Moses tried to end a dispute between two Hebrews, and that the reproof he gave may still be of use to disputants who divide and weaken the church, forgetting they are brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The wrongdoer was angry and indignant, asking who had made Moses a prince and a judge over them. Henry observes that it is a sign of guilt to be angry at reproof, and that men know not what enemies they are to themselves when they resist faithful reprovers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He asked whether Moses thought to kill him as he had killed the Egyptian yesterday. The taunt exposed the secret deed, and the knowledge that the killing was known changed Moses' standing at court in a moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Moses feared and fled on account of these words. Henry notes that Moses was obliged to flee into Midian, and that God ordered this for wise and holy ends, the flight beginning the exile that would end at the burning bush. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
