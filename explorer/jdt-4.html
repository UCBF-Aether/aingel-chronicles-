The Text — Judith 4 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Now the children of Israel, that dwelt in Judea, heard all that Holofernes the chief captain of Nabuchodonosor king of the Assyrians had done to the nations, and after what manner he had spoiled all their temples, and brought them to nought.

2 Therefore they were exceedingly afraid of him, and were troubled for Jerusalem, and for the temple of the Lord their God:

3 For they were newly returned from the captivity, and all the people of Judea were lately gathered together: and the vessels, and the altar, and the house, were sanctified after the profanation.

4 Therefore they sent into all the coasts of Samaria, and the villages and to Bethoron, and Belmen, and Jericho, and to Choba, and Esora, and to the valley of Salem:

5 And possessed themselves beforehand of all the tops of the high mountains, and fortified the villages that were in them, and laid up victuals for the provision of war: for their fields were of late reaped.

6 Also Joacim the high priest, which was in those days in Jerusalem, wrote to them that dwelt in Bethulia, and Betomestham, which is over against Esdraelon toward the open country, near to Dothaim,

7 Charging them to keep the passages of the hill country: for by them there was an entrance into Judea, and it was easy to stop them that would come up, because the passage was straight, for two men at the most.

8 And the children of Israel did as Joacim the high priest had commanded them, with the ancients of all the people of Israel, which dwelt at Jerusalem.

9 Then every man of Israel cried to God with great fervency, and with great vehemency did they humble their souls:

10 Both they, and their wives and their children, and their cattle, and every stranger and hireling, and their servants bought with money, put sackcloth upon their loins.

11 Thus every man and women, and the little children, and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, fell before the temple, and cast ashes upon their heads, and spread out their sackcloth before the face of the Lord: also they put sackcloth about the altar,

12 And cried to the God of Israel all with one consent earnestly, that he would not give their children for a prey, and their wives for a spoil, and the cities of their inheritance to destruction, and the sanctuary to profanation and reproach, and for the nations to rejoice at.

13 So God heard their prayers, and looked upon their afflictions: for the people fasted many days in all Judea and Jerusalem before the sanctuary of the Lord Almighty.

14 And Joacim the high priest, and all the priests that stood before the Lord, and they which ministered unto the Lord, had their loins girt with sackcloth, and offered the daily burnt offerings, with the vows and free gifts of the people,

15 And had ashes on their mitres, and cried unto the Lord with all their power, that he would look upon all the house of Israel graciously.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Hearing what Holofernes has done to the nations and their temples, the children of Israel in Judea prepare for war: Joacim the high priest writes to Bethulia and Betomestham to fortify the passages of the hill country, and the whole people, men, women, and little children, fall before the temple, cast ashes on their heads, spread sackcloth before the Lord, and cry with all their power that he would look graciously on the house of Israel. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Bethulia. the hill town whose passes Joacim orders fortified against Holofernes (verse 6). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Sackcloth and ashes. the garments and sign of mourning and repentance worn in the fast (verses 11-15). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Joacim. the high priest in Jerusalem in those days, who directs the defence (verse 6). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The children of Israel in Judea heard what Holofernes had done; Judea is whither those had retreated who had escaped the fury of the Assyrians under Tiglath-pileser and others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. They were exceedingly afraid for Jerusalem and for the temple of the Lord their God, because surrendering would not secure these things, so that their very life and religion were at stake; resistance in other circumstances would have been rashness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. They were newly returned from captivity, and all the people of Judea lately gathered together, with vessels, altar, and house purified from profanation — the Greek adds that they were "recently come up from captivity, and lately all the people had been collected out of Judea, and the vessels, and the altar, and the house, had been purified from profanation" to which they had been exposed under Manasses, who with several subjects had been made prisoner and was now at Babylon, or, if returned, was wholly taken up with his soul's concerns and committed affairs to Eliachim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. They sent into all the coasts of Samaria and the villages, though Samaria was an enemy's country, as the danger was common; Ezechias and Josias seemed to claim a right over the country (2 Chronicles 30:1; 34:6, 33). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. They possessed themselves beforehand of the tops of the high mountains, fortified the villages, and laid up victuals; the Greek adds, "as the harvest was just ended" — the timing of their preparation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. Joacim the high priest in Jerusalem wrote to them that dwelt in Bethulia and Betomestham, over against Esdrelon and the plain near Dothaim, giving orders to seize the mountainous ascents by which there was a passage into Judea; it was easy to stop their progress, as the passage was narrow and would not admit above two men at once — recalling Leonidas with his 300 Spartans resisting Xerxes' millions at Thermopylae. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. He charged them to keep the passages of the hill country, for by them there was an entrance into Judea and it was easy to stop those who would come up; the Jews attempted to defend these defiles, having received the orders before the enemy encamped near Scythopolis. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. The children of Israel did as Joacim commanded, with the ancients of all the people; the Greek adds "and the senate of all the people of Israel, which sat at Jerusalem," and Serarius thinks Manasses, busy fortifying the city, had committed the rest of the kingdom to Eliachim, though it is more probable he was at Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. Both they and their wives, children, cattle, strangers, hirelings, and bought servants put on sackcloth; the Greek adds "and their little ones, and beasts, and every sojourner, and hired servant and slave. They put sackcloth on their loins" — the very beasts were covered with sackcloth to denote the general sorrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. Thus every man and woman, the little children, and the inhabitants of Jerusalem fell before the temple and cast ashes upon their heads; the Greek adds that "every man, woman, and child, and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, fell prostrate before the temple, and sprinkled ashes on their heads." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. They cried to the God of Israel with one consent earnestly that he would not give their children for a prey and their wives for a spoil; "off" is literally "divided" from their husbands, or among the victors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. So God heard their prayers and looked upon their afflictions; the Greek adds, "and God heard their petition, and rescued them from their affliction; and all the people fasted many days in all Judea and Jerusalem, before the sanctuary of the Lord almighty." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. Joacim the high priest and all the priests that stood before the Lord and ministered unto him had their loins girt with sackcloth; the Greek adds that they "spread their sackclothes before the Lord, and arrayed the altar with sackcloth," and the high priest Joachim and all the priests, having loins girded with sackcloth, offered the accustomed holocaust and the vows and presents of the people, with ashes upon their caps — a spectacle that must have made an impression on the most obdurate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. They had ashes on their mitres and cried unto the Lord with all their power that he would look upon all the house of Israel graciously; the sackcloth dress follows the advice of Joel 1:13. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock

Connections to other passages

  • The study note for verse 4 connects this passage to 2 Chronicles 30:1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
