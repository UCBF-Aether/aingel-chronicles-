The Text — Sinodos 7 (Ge'ez text of G. Horner, The Statutes of the Apostles; or, Canones Ecclesiastici (London: Williams & Norgate, 1904), public domain)

1 The twenty-third canon: concerning the death of priests. If it come to pass that a priest die, let a priest read [the prayer], with his hand upon his head.

2 And let all the priests surround[?] him, and pray over him, after the manner of the first that prayeth, while he said: My God, Father of our Lord and our Saviour Jesus Christ, look upon this Thy servant, and divide unto him the spirit of grace and the honour of holiness, that he may be able, and may tend[?] Thy people with a pure heart, as Thou didst look upon Thy chosen people.

3 And Thou didst command Moses to choose priests from among them …[much lost]… the Holy Spirit, which Thou gavest[?] unto Thy servant, and Thou didst anoint[?] Moses. And now, O my God, give unto this Thy servant grace that faileth not, while Thou keepest for us the spirit of Thy favour.

4 And apportion[?] unto us — fill us[?] — with Thy worship in our hearts continually[?], while we praise Thee.

5 Through Thy Son Jesus Christ, to whom be glory and power; to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, in Thy holy church, now and always, and for ever and ever. Amen.

6 And all the people say: Amen.

8 The twenty-fourth canon: concerning the death of deacons. If it come to pass that one priest[?] die, let a deacon choose, as we commanded before — we have spoken — and let him read his hand upon his head: one priest alone.

9 For this reason: one priest alone shall read his hand upon him.

10 For as touching the rite[?], this is the manner of it: for he was not appointed unto the priesthood save by the laying on of hands[?] — one priest — that he may do the commandments which were commanded him by him[?].

11 And he was not appointed to be a teacher unto them all; but[?] let him be he that is chosen[?] as he willeth[?], and let him speak unto one priest.

12 And as for him that was not appointed to receive the great Spirit — these[?] that join themselves[?] unto the priests — but let him be admonished[?] as he willeth[?], that the priest may acknowledge him, and [?] as is fitting.

13 For one priest alone shall read his hand upon him.

14 And as for the priests: for one priest[?] — and let all the priests join[?] with him.

15 And let them lay[?] their hands upon him, for the Spirit [?] which descendeth upon him.

16 And as for one priest alone, there is none other that doeth it[?] …[much lost]… that they should die[?]; and for this cause are they not appointed[?] …[?] … the deacons[?] … from one priest alone; but let one priest alone read his hand upon him.

17 The prayer for the deacons that die[?].

18 O Lord God, who hast created all[?], and by Thy word hast established them[?].

19 Father of our Lord and our Saviour Jesus Christ, who didst send Him [?] by Thy will, and reveal unto us Thy hidden counsel.

20 Give the Spirit and grace, and [?] upon this Thy servant whom Thou hast chosen, that he may become a deacon in [?] the church, and may offer in Thy holy sanctuary that which is Thine and is offered[?], from the appointed[?] chief of Thy priesthood[?], unto the glory of Thy name!

22 …[the opening is lost to severe corruption — stray numerals and marks]… through Thy Son[?] Jesus Christ our Lord, to whom be glory unto Thee[?], with Him glory and [?] and power and praise, with the Holy Spirit, now and always, and for ever and ever. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Canon 23 gives the rite on the death of a priest: a priest reads the prayer with his hand on the dead man's head, all priests surrounding him, asking God to divide to him the spirit of grace and the honour of holiness, recalling that God commanded Moses to choose priests. Canon 24 gives the rite on the death of deacons, insisting repeatedly that one priest alone shall read his hand upon the dead deacon, since he was appointed not to the priesthood but by that single laying on of hands — and the chapter ends with a prayer for deacons who die.
