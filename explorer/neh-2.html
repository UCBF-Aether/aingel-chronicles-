Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Nehemiah 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass in the month Nisan, in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes the king, that wine was before him: and I took up the wine, and gave it unto the king. Now I had not been beforetime sad in his presence.

2 Wherefore the king said unto me, Why is thy countenance sad, seeing thou art not sick? this is nothing else but sorrow of heart. Then I was very sore afraid,

3 And said unto the king, Let the king live for ever: why should not my countenance be sad, when the city, the place of my fathers’ sepulchres, lieth waste, and the gates thereof are consumed with fire?

4 Then the king said unto me, For what dost thou make request? So I prayed to the God of heaven.

5 And I said unto the king, If it please the king, and if thy servant have found favour in thy sight, that thou wouldest send me unto Judah, unto the city of my fathers’ sepulchres, that I may build it.

6 And the king said unto me, (the queen also sitting by him,) For how long shall thy journey be? and when wilt thou return? So it pleased the king to send me; and I set him a time.

7 Moreover I said unto the king, If it please the king, let letters be given me to the governors beyond the river, that they may convey me over till I come into Judah;

8 And a letter unto Asaph the keeper of the king’s forest, that he may give me timber to make beams for the gates of the palace which appertained to the house, and for the wall of the city, and for the house that I shall enter into. And the king granted me, according to the good hand of my God upon me.

9 Then I came to the governors beyond the river, and gave them the king’s letters. Now the king had sent captains of the army and horsemen with me.

10 When Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, heard of it, it grieved them exceedingly that there was come a man to seek the welfare of the children of Israel.

11 So I came to Jerusalem, and was there three days.

12 And I arose in the night, I and some few men with me; neither told I any man what my God had put in my heart to do at Jerusalem: neither was there any beast with me, save the beast that I rode upon.

13 And I went out by night by the gate of the valley, even before the dragon well, and to the dung port, and viewed the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down, and the gates thereof were consumed with fire.

14 Then I went on to the gate of the fountain, and to the king’s pool: but there was no place for the beast that was under me to pass.

15 Then went I up in the night by the brook, and viewed the wall, and turned back, and entered by the gate of the valley, and so returned.

16 And the rulers knew not whither I went, or what I did; neither had I as yet told it to the Jews, nor to the priests, nor to the nobles, nor to the rulers, nor to the rest that did the work.

17 Then said I unto them, Ye see the distress that we are in, how Jerusalem lieth waste, and the gates thereof are burned with fire: come, and let us build up the wall of Jerusalem, that we be no more a reproach.

18 Then I told them of the hand of my God which was good upon me; as also the king’s words that he had spoken unto me. And they said, Let us rise up and build. So they strengthened their hands for this good work.

19 But when Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the servant, the Ammonite, and Geshem the Arabian, heard it, they laughed us to scorn, and despised us, and said, What is this thing that ye do? will ye rebel against the king?

20 Then answered I them, and said unto them, The God of heaven, he will prosper us; therefore we his servants will arise and build: but ye have no portion, nor right, nor memorial, in Jerusalem.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Seeing Nehemiah sad, King Artaxerxes asks what he wants; Nehemiah prays, then asks to be sent to Judah to rebuild the city of his fathers' graves, with letters for safe passage and timber from the king's forest. On arrival he inspects the ruined walls by night, then rallies the people to build. Sanballat and Tobiah mock and oppose the work, but Nehemiah answers that the God of heaven will prosper them, while their opponents have no portion, right, or memorial in Jerusalem. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Beyond the river. the Persian province west of the Euphrates, through which Nehemiah had to travel to reach Judah. Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Nisan. the month of Nehemiah's audience with the king, nearly four months after Chisleu (study note on verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In Nisan, nearly four months after Chisleu, Nehemiah took up the wine and gave it to the king, having waited for a fitting moment to press his cause. Henry stresses that his prayers were seconded by serious endeavour: he did not sit still and expect God to do the work alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The king noticed his sad countenance, though he was not sick, and asked why. Henry draws a lesson in sympathy: masters should concern themselves with their servants' sorrows, and sorrow for the church's calamities may well sadden a face even without sickness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Nehemiah answered with meekness and fear, "Let the king live for ever," then asked why he should not be sad when the city of his fathers' sepulchres lay waste and its gates were consumed with fire. Henry notes he could give a good reason for his grief, appealing even to the king himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Asked what he requested, Nehemiah first prayed silently to the God of heaven for wisdom and favour. Henry calls this a secret, sudden ejaculation: those who would find favour with kings must first secure the favour of the King of kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He then petitioned modestly but explicitly: if he had found favour, let the king send him to Judah, to the city of his fathers' sepulchres, to build it. Henry reads this as a request for a governor's commission to rebuild Jerusalem's wall, for a set time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The king, with the queen sitting by, asked how long the journey would be and when he would return, then granted leave and set a time. Henry sees God's hand here: Nehemiah gained his point not by merit or management but according to the good hand of his God upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Nehemiah further asked for letters to the governors beyond the river, that they would convey him safely and supply what he needed. Henry notes he asked not merely for passage but for provision from each province he crossed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He also asked for a letter to Asaph, keeper of the king's forest, for timber for the gates of the palace by the temple, for the city wall, and for his own house. Henry observes the king let him have whatever clauses he pleased inserted in his commission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Coming to the governors beyond the river, Nehemiah delivered the king's letters, escorted by captains of the army and horsemen. Henry reads the escort as both guard and honour: those whom the King of kings sends, he protects and dignifies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite heard of his coming, they were exceedingly grieved that a man had come to seek the welfare of Israel. Henry notes their vexation: their arts to weaken Israel were baffled by a generous royal project to strengthen it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Nehemiah came to Jerusalem and stayed three days, apparently unnoticed by the city's great men. Henry admires the humility: he came with great blessings yet without show or noise, lying hid until his work began. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. He arose in the night with a few men, telling no one what God had put in his heart to do, with no beast except the one he rode. Henry notes he would first see the ruins with his own eyes, quietly, before proposing anything. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. By night he went out by the valley gate, past the dragon well to the dung port, viewing the broken walls of Jerusalem. Henry's rule: good work is likely well done when first well considered, and those who would build the church's walls must first notice their ruins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He went on to the fountain gate and the king's pool, but there was no place for his beast to pass. Henry treats this night survey as careful preparation: he examined what remained of the old foundations and materials before choosing his method. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. He then went up by the brook in the night, viewed the wall, turned back, and entered again by the valley gate. Henry notes the wisdom of proceeding on his own inspection rather than on reports alone, and of doing it unobserved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The rulers did not know where he had gone or what he did, for he had told neither Jews, priests, nobles, nor rulers. Henry explains the silence: he would not trumpet his purpose before he knew it was practicable, and upright men do not sound a trumpet before their good offices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. At last he told them plainly: they saw the distress, Jerusalem lying waste with gates burned by fire, so let them rise and build the wall to end their reproach. Henry notes he did not command imperiously despite his commission, but exhorted them as a brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. He told them of the good hand of his God upon him and of the king's words, and they answered, "Let us rise up and build," strengthening their hands for the good work. Henry observes that many a good work only waits for one good head to lead in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Sanballat, Tobiah, and Geshem the Arabian laughed them to scorn, despised them, and charged rebellion against the king. Henry notes the double slander: the work was mocked as foolish and condemned as treason, though it stood under the king's own commission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Nehemiah answered that the God of heaven would prosper them, so his servants would arise and build, but their enemies had no portion, right, or memorial in Jerusalem. Henry's ground: they served the only true God, and their maligners envied a right they did not share. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
