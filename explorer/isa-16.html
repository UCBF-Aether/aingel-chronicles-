The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Send ye the lamb to the ruler of the land from Sela to the wilderness, unto the mount of the daughter of Zion.

2 For it shall be, that, as a wandering bird cast out of the nest, so the daughters of Moab shall be at the fords of Arnon.

3 Take counsel, execute judgment; make thy shadow as the night in the midst of the noonday; hide the outcasts; bewray not him that wandereth.

4 Let mine outcasts dwell with thee, Moab; be thou a covert to them from the face of the spoiler: for the extortioner is at an end, the spoiler ceaseth, the oppressors are consumed out of the land.

5 And in mercy shall the throne be established: and he shall sit upon it in truth in the tabernacle of David, judging, and seeking judgment, and hasting righteousness.

6 We have heard of the pride of Moab; he is very proud: even of his haughtiness, and his pride, and his wrath: but his lies shall not be so.

7 Therefore shall Moab howl for Moab, every one shall howl: for the foundations of Kirhareseth shall ye mourn; surely they are stricken.

8 For the fields of Heshbon languish, and the vine of Sibmah: the lords of the heathen have broken down the principal plants thereof, they are come even unto Jazer, they wandered through the wilderness: her branches are stretched out, they are gone over the sea.

9 Therefore I will bewail with the weeping of Jazer the vine of Sibmah: I will water thee with my tears, O Heshbon, and Elealeh: for the shouting for thy summer fruits and for thy harvest is fallen.

10 And gladness is taken away, and joy out of the plentiful field; and in the vineyards there shall be no singing, neither shall there be shouting: the treaders shall tread out no wine in their presses; I have made their vintage shouting to cease.

11 Wherefore my bowels shall sound like an harp for Moab, and mine inward parts for Kirharesh.

12 And it shall come to pass, when it is seen that Moab is weary on the high place, that he shall come to his sanctuary to pray; but he shall not prevail.

13 This is the word that the LORD hath spoken concerning Moab since that time.

14 But now the LORD hath spoken, saying, Within three years, as the years of an hireling, and the glory of Moab shall be contemned, with all that great multitude; and the remnant shall be very small and feeble.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Moab, fled south, is advised to send the tribute lamb to Judah's king and to seek shelter under Zion's shadow — but Moab's pride and arrogance block the plea, and the lament continues over its ruined vineyards. The chapter ends with a fixed deadline: within three years, as the years of a hireling, Moab's glory will be brought to contempt and only a small, feeble remnant left. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Send the lamb to the ruler of the land from Sela to the wilderness, unto the mount of the daughter of Zion. The prophet advises the Moabites fled south to Idumea to send to Judah's king the tribute of lambs they formerly paid to Israel but had given up, for David probably imposed it before Judah and Israel split; Sela means a rock, Petra in Greek, capital of Idumea whose dwellings are hewn from rock, and the ruler is Judah's king, since Amaziah had mastered Idumea and Sela. The tribute is a type of submitting to Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. For it will be as a wandering bird cast out of the nest, so the daughters of Moab will be at the fords of Arnon. As a brood just fledged and expelled from its nest, the daughters of Moab — the inhabitants of Moab — stand at the fords trying to cross the boundary river to escape the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Take counsel, execute judgment, make your shadow as the night in the midst of the noonday, hide the outcasts, and betray not him that wanders. These verses are the fugitive Moabites' address to the Jews for protection, or the prophet's further advice to Moab; the shadow as night at noonday is a thick shelter from glaring heat, and the wanderer must not be betrayed to his pursuer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Let my outcasts dwell with you, Moab — rather, let the outcasts of Moab dwell with you, Judah — be a covert to them from the face of the spoiler, for the extortioner is at an end, the spoiler ceases, and the oppressors are consumed out of the land. By the time Moab begs Judah for shelter, Judah will be able to afford it, for the Assyrian oppressor will have been consumed out of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. In mercy the throne will be established, and he will sit upon it in truth in the tabernacle of David, judging and seeking judgment and hasting righteousness. If Judah shelters suppliant Moab, blessing will redound to Judah and its throne, and the language is divinely framed to apply to the latter days under King Messiah, when the Lord brings again Moab's captivity, with a king prompt in executing righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. We have heard of the pride of Moab; he is very proud, even of his haughtiness, pride, and wrath, but his lies will not be so. The Jews answer: we reject Moab's supplication for his pride, and his false boasts will prove vain, not right, and will not effect what he claims. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Therefore Moab will howl for Moab, everyone will howl, and for the foundations of Kirhareseth you will mourn, surely stricken. All hope of shelter from the Jews being cut off, Moab howls, and the foundations — that is, the ruins, since when houses are pulled down only foundations are left — of Kirhareseth, literally a citadel of brick, are mourned as utterly stricken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For the fields of Heshbon languish, and the vine of Sibmah; the lords of the heathen have broken down its principal plants, which reached even to Jazer, wandered through the wilderness, and went over the sea. The vine-fields of Heshbon and the vine of Sibmah near Heshbon languish because the heathen princes, the Assyrians who invaded Moab, destroyed the vines, whose shoots once reached fifteen miles to Jazer, overran the wilderness of Arabia, and went over the Dead Sea or the sea of Jazer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Therefore Isaiah will bewail with the weeping of Jazer the vine of Sibmah, watering it with tears, for the shouting is fallen upon its summer fruits and upon its harvest. Though belonging to another nation, the prophet bewails Moab's desolation, weeping as Jazer weeps, because upon the summer fruits and luxuriant vines has fallen the battle shout instead of the joyous shout of grape-gatherers at vintage — the spoiler fallen upon the harvest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Gladness is taken away, and joy out of the plentiful field, and in the vineyards there will be no singing, neither joyful noise, and no treader will tread out wine in their presses, for God has made their vintage shouting to cease. There will be no harvest or vintage owing to the desolation, and therefore none of the gladness felt in gathering a rich harvest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Therefore Isaiah's bowels will sound like a harp for Moab, and his inward parts for Kirharesh. The bowels are in Scripture the seat of yearning compassion, the inward seat of emotion, and they sound like a harp as its strings vibrate when beaten with the hand, echoing the prophet's pity for Moab already expressed in chapter 15. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. When Moab is weary on the high place, he will come to his sanctuary to pray, but he will not prevail. When Moab has appeared before his gods and fatigued himself with burdensome rites on the high place, and comes to the sanctuary of Chemosh on Mount Nebo to pray, he will effect nothing by his prayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. This is the word that the Lord has spoken concerning Moab since that time — rather, respecting that time. Former prophecies have been given as to Moab, of which Isaiah has given the substance, but now a definite and steady time is also fixed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. But now the Lord has spoken, saying: Within three years, as the years of a hireling, the glory of Moab will be contemned, with all that great multitude, and the remnant will be very small and feeble. Just as a hireling's fixed term of engagement can neither be added to nor taken from, so Moab's fall is unalterably fixed within three years, fulfilled about the time the Assyrians led Israel captive, and the ruins of Elealeh, Heshbon, Medeba, and Dibon still confirm the prophecy's particularity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
