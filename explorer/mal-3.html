The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Malachi 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me: and the LORD, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in: behold, he shall come, saith the LORD of hosts.

2 But who may abide the day of his coming? and who shall stand when he appeareth? for he is like a refiner’s fire, and like fullers’ soap:

3 And he shall sit as a refiner and purifier of silver: and he shall purify the sons of Levi, and purge them as gold and silver, that they may offer unto the LORD an offering in righteousness.

4 Then shall the offering of Judah and Jerusalem be pleasant unto the LORD, as in the days of old, and as in former years.

5 And I will come near to you to judgment; and I will be a swift witness against the sorcerers, and against the adulterers, and against false swearers, and against those that oppress the hireling in his wages, the widow, and the fatherless, and that turn aside the stranger from his right, and fear not me, saith the LORD of hosts.

6 For I am the LORD, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed.

7 Even from the days of your fathers ye are gone away from mine ordinances, and have not kept them. Return unto me, and I will return unto you, saith the LORD of hosts. But ye said, Wherein shall we return?

8 Will a man rob God? Yet ye have robbed me. But ye say, Wherein have we robbed thee? In tithes and offerings.

9 Ye are cursed with a curse: for ye have robbed me, even this whole nation.

10 Bring ye all the tithes into the storehouse, that there may be meat in mine house, and prove me now herewith, saith the LORD of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it.

11 And I will rebuke the devourer for your sakes, and he shall not destroy the fruits of your ground; neither shall your vine cast her fruit before the time in the field, saith the LORD of hosts.

12 And all nations shall call you blessed: for ye shall be a delightsome land, saith the LORD of hosts.

13 Your words have been stout against me, saith the LORD. Yet ye say, What have we spoken so much against thee?

14 Ye have said, It is vain to serve God: and what profit is it that we have kept his ordinance, and that we have walked mournfully before the LORD of hosts?

15 And now we call the proud happy; yea, they that work wickedness are set up; yea, they that tempt God are even delivered.

16 Then they that feared the LORD spake often one to another: and the LORD hearkened, and heard it, and a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared the LORD, and that thought upon his name.

17 And they shall be mine, saith the LORD of hosts, in that day when I make up my jewels; and I will spare them, as a man spareth his own son that serveth him.

18 Then shall ye return, and discern between the righteous and the wicked, between him that serveth God and him that serveth him not.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Behold, I will send my messenger, and he will prepare the way before me, and the LORD whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, but who may abide the day of his coming? He is like a refiner's fire and fullers' soap, and he will sit as a refiner of silver to purify the sons of Levi until they offer an offering in righteousness. A swift witness comes against sorcerers, adulterers, false swearers, and oppressors of hireling, widow, fatherless, and stranger, yet the LORD changes not, so the sons of Jacob are not consumed. Return to me, he says, and stop robbing God in tithes and offerings; bring all the tithes into the storehouse and prove him, and he will open the windows of heaven. Against those who call it vain to serve God stands a book of remembrance for those who fear the LORD, who will be his jewels in the day he makes up, spared as a son who serves his father, until all discern between the righteous and the wicked. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me, he said, and the LORD, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in, behold, he shall come, saith the LORD of hosts, for this is a prophecy of John the Baptist, Christ's harbinger, who shall prepare the way by calling men to repentance, and the Messiah, long called He that should come, will now shortly come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. But who may abide the day of his coming, he asked, and who shall stand when he appeareth, for he is like a refiner's fire, and like fullers' soap, for no hypocrite or formalist can endure his doctrine or stand before his tribunal, and Christ came to distinguish men, to separate the precious from the vile. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And he shall sit as a refiner and purifier of silver, he said, and he shall purify the sons of Levi, and purge them as gold and silver, that they may offer unto the LORD an offering in righteousness, for by his gospel Christ shall purify and reform his church, and by his Spirit shall regenerate and cleanse souls, taking away the dross that renders their faculties worthless, and the believer need not fear the fiery trial, for the Saviour will not make it more intense or longer than is needful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Then shall the offering of Judah and Jerusalem be pleasant unto the LORD, as in the days of old, and as in former years, for Christ, by interceding for them, will make them accepted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And I will come near to you to judgment, he said, and I will be a swift witness against the sorcerers, and against the adulterers, and against false swearers, and against those that oppress the hireling in his wages, the widow, and the fatherless, and that turn aside the stranger from his right, and fear not me, saith the LORD of hosts, for where no fear of God is, no good is to be expected, and evil pursues sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. For I am the LORD, I change not, he said, therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed, for God is unchangeable, and though sentence against evil works be not executed speedily, yet it will be executed, and because his compassions fail not, we are not consumed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Even from the days of your fathers ye are gone away from mine ordinances, and have not kept them, he said; return unto me, and I will return unto you, saith the LORD of hosts, but ye said, Wherein shall we return, for that generation had turned away from God, yet God gave them a gracious call, and God notices what returns our hearts make to the calls of his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Will a man rob God, he asked, yet ye have robbed me, but ye say, Wherein have we robbed thee, in tithes and offerings, for it shows great perverseness in sin, when men make afflictions excuses for sin, which are sent to part between them and their sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Ye are cursed with a curse, he said, for ye have robbed me, even this whole nation, for the curse followed the withholding of what was God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Bring ye all the tithes into the storehouse, he said, that there may be meat in mine house, and prove me now herewith, saith the LORD of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it, for God must be served in the first place, and the interest of our souls preferred before that of our bodies, and he who makes trial will find nothing is lost by honouring the Lord with his substance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And I will rebuke the devourer for your sakes, he said, and he shall not destroy the fruits of your ground, neither shall your vine cast her fruit before the time in the field, saith the LORD of hosts, for God has blessings ready for us, but through the weakness of our faith and narrowness of our desires, we have not room to receive them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And all nations shall call you blessed, he said, for ye shall be a delightsome land, saith the LORD of hosts, for obedience would make Israel a blessing among the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Your words have been stout against me, saith the LORD, he said, yet ye say, What have we spoken so much against thee, for among the Jews at this time some plainly discovered themselves to be children of the wicked one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Ye have said, It is vain to serve God, he said, and what profit is it that we have kept his ordinance, and that we have walked mournfully before the LORD of hosts, for the yoke of Christ is easy, but those who work wickedness tempt God by presumptuous sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And now we call the proud happy, he said, yea, they that work wickedness are set up, yea, they that tempt God are even delivered, for they judged things by present prosperity, not as they will appear when the doom of proud sinners is executed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Then they that feared the LORD spake often one to another, he said, and the LORD hearkened, and heard it, and a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared the LORD, and that thought upon his name, for those who feared the Lord spoke kindly, preserving mutual love when sin abounded, and as evil communications corrupt good minds, so good communications confirm them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And they shall be mine, saith the LORD of hosts, in that day when I make up my jewels, he said, and I will spare them, as a man spareth his own son that serveth him, for the saints are God's jewels, dear to him, and he will preserve them as his jewels when the earth is burned up like dross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Then shall ye return, and discern between the righteous and the wicked, he said, between him that serveth God and him that serveth him not, for all are righteous or wicked, such as serve God or serve him not, and at the bar of Christ every man's character will be known, though we are often deceived in our opinions now, and must judge nothing before the time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Refiner's fire and fullers' soap. purifying images in Malachi 3: fire that refines silver and soap that cleanses cloth, for the coming that will purify the sons of Levi. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Book of remembrance. the record written in Malachi 3 for those who feared the LORD and thought upon his name, who will be his jewels when he makes them up. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
