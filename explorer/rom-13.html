The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Romans 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Let every soul be subject unto the higher powers. For there is no power but of God: the powers that be are ordained of God.

2 Whosoever therefore resisteth the power, resisteth the ordinance of God: and they that resist shall receive to themselves damnation.

3 For rulers are not a terror to good works, but to the evil. Wilt thou then not be afraid of the power? do that which is good, and thou shalt have praise of the same:

4 For he is the minister of God to thee for good. But if thou do that which is evil, be afraid; for he beareth not the sword in vain: for he is the minister of God, a revenger to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil.

5 Wherefore ye must needs be subject, not only for wrath, but also for conscience sake.

6 For for this cause pay ye tribute also: for they are God’s ministers, attending continually upon this very thing.

7 Render therefore to all their dues: tribute to whom tribute is due; custom to whom custom; fear to whom fear; honour to whom honour.

8 Owe no man any thing, but to love one another: for he that loveth another hath fulfilled the law.

9 For this, Thou shalt not commit adultery, Thou shalt not kill, Thou shalt not steal, Thou shalt not bear false witness, Thou shalt not covet; and if there be any other commandment, it is briefly comprehended in this saying, namely, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.

10 Love worketh no ill to his neighbour: therefore love is the fulfilling of the law.

11 And that, knowing the time, that now it is high time to awake out of sleep: for now is our salvation nearer than when we believed.

12 The night is far spent, the day is at hand: let us therefore cast off the works of darkness, and let us put on the armour of light.

13 Let us walk honestly, as in the day; not in rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness, not in strife and envying.

14 But put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh, to fulfil the lusts thereof.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Every soul is to be subject to the governing authorities, Paul says, for authority is ordained by God, and rulers are God's ministers for good and for punishment of evil; love of neighbour fulfils the law, and, since the night is far spent and the day at hand, believers are to cast off works of darkness, walk honestly, and put on the Lord Jesus Christ, making no provision for the flesh. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Let every soul be subject to the higher powers, for there is no power but of God, and the powers that are ordained of God. Henry notes that civil authority itself is God's ordinance, whatever the ruler's character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Whoever resists the power resists God's ordinance, and those who resist shall receive damnation to themselves. JFB stresses that resistance to lawful authority is resistance to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Rulers are not a terror to good works but to evil; if you do good you shall have praise from the power. Henry observes that government is meant to encourage good, not to frighten it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The ruler is God's minister to you for good, but if you do evil, be afraid, for he bears not the sword in vain, being God's minister and avenger of wrath on evildoers. JFB notes that the sword is God's instrument of justice, not mere force. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Therefore you must be subject, not only for wrath but also for conscience' sake. Henry stresses that conscience, not fear of punishment alone, grounds obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For this cause you pay tribute also, for rulers are God's ministers attending continually to this very thing. JFB notes that taxes support the ministry of government God ordained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Render to all their dues: tribute to whom tribute is due, custom to whom custom, fear to whom fear, honour to whom honour. Henry observes that every due, fiscal or personal, is owed in its proper place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Owe no man anything except to love one another, for he who loves another has fulfilled the law. JFB notes that love is the one debt that is never discharged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The commandments against adultery, murder, theft, false witness, and covetousness, and every other, are summed up in this: you shall love your neighbour as yourself. Henry observes that love gathers all the second table into one word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Love works no ill to its neighbour, therefore love is the fulfilling of the law. JFB stresses that love does not abolish the law; it performs it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Knowing the time, that now it is high time to awake out of sleep, for our salvation is nearer than when we believed. Henry notes that nearness of salvation is a reason for wakefulness, not slumber. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The night is far spent, the day is at hand; let us cast off works of darkness and put on the armour of light. JFB pictures the Christian as a soldier dressing for dawn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Let us walk honestly, as in the day, not in rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness, not in strife and envying. Henry observes that daylight conduct has nothing to hide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to fulfil its lusts. JFB notes that putting on Christ leaves no room for planning sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • higher powers. The governing authorities, to whom Paul says every soul should be subject, since there is no power but of God. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • tribute. Tax or revenue owed to rulers; Paul says to render tribute and custom to whom they are due. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • put on Christ. Paul's clothing image for the Christian life: to be clothed with Christ rather than provision for fleshly lusts. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
