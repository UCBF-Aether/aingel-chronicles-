The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judith 15 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 And when they that were in the tents heard, they were astonished at the thing that was done.

2 And fear and trembling fell upon them, so that there was no man that durst abide in the sight of his neighbour, but rushing out all together, they fled into every way of the plain, and of the hill country.

3 They also that had camped in the mountains round about Bethulia fled away. Then the children of Israel, every one that was a warrior among them, rushed out upon them.

4 Then sent Ozias to Betomasthem, and to Bebai, and Chobai, and Cola and to all the coasts of Israel, such as should tell the things that were done, and that all should rush forth upon their enemies to destroy them.

5 Now when the children of Israel heard it, they all fell upon them with one consent, and slew them unto Chobai: likewise also they that came from Jerusalem, and from all the hill country, (for men had told them what things were done in the camp of their enemies) and they that were in Galaad, and in Galilee, chased them with a great slaughter, until they were past Damascus and the borders thereof.

6 And the residue that dwelt at Bethulia, fell upon the camp of Assur, and spoiled them, and were greatly enriched.

7 And the children of Israel that returned from the slaughter had that which remained; and the villages and the cities, that were in the mountains and in the plain, gat many spoils: for the multitude was very great.

8 Then Joacim the high priest, and the ancients of the children of Israel that dwelt in Jerusalem, came to behold the good things that God had shewed to Israel, and to see Judith, and to salute her.

9 And when they came unto her, they blessed her with one accord, and said unto her, Thou art the exaltation of Jerusalem, thou art the great glory of Israel, thou art the great rejoicing of our nation:

10 Thou hast done all these things by thine hand: thou hast done much good to Israel, and God is pleased therewith: blessed be thou of the Almighty Lord for evermore. And all the people said, So be it.

11 And the people spoiled the camp the space of thirty days: and they gave unto Judith Holofernes his tent, and all his plate, and beds, and vessels, and all his stuff: and she took it and laid it on her mule; and made ready her carts, and laid them thereon.

12 Then all the women of Israel ran together to see her, and blessed her, and made a dance among them for her: and she took branches in her hand, and gave also to the women that were with her.

13 And they put a garland of olive upon her and her maid that was with her, and she went before all the people in the dance, leading all the women: and all the men of Israel followed in their armour with garlands, and with songs in their mouths.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Hearing what is done, those in the Assyrian tents are astonished, fear and trembling fall on them, and they flee. The children of Israel fall on them with one consent and pursue the slaughter to Chobai, while the villages and cities of mountain and plain gather the spoils, which are very great. Joacim and the elders come from Jerusalem to bless Judith, and she is given a garland of olive, leading the women in the dance while the men follow in armour with garlands and songs. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Spoils. the plunder of the fled camp, gathered by Israel and the villages round about (verse 7). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Garland of olive. the crown given to Judith and her maid for the victory dance (verse 13). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And when they that were in the tents heard, they were astonished at the thing that was done; the Greek reads, "Those who were in the tents heard, they were astonished at the fact; and fear and trembling fell upon them." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. And fear and trembling fell upon them, so that there was no man that durst abide in the sight of his neighbour, but rushing out all together, they fled into every way of the plain, and of the mountains; the Greek continues, "and there was not a man remaining before his neighbour; but rushing out, with one accord, they fled through every path, in the plains and in the mountains." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. They also that had camped in the mountains round about Bethulia fled away. Then the children of Israel, every one that was a warrior among them, rushed out upon them; the Greek continues, "and those who were encamped in the high places around Bethulia (the Moabites, &c., chapter 7:8) fled. Then every warrior of Israel rushed out upon them, and Ozias," and so on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. Then sent Ozias to Betomasthem, and to Bebai, and Chobai, and Cola, and to all the coasts of Israel, such as should tell the things that were done, and that they should pursue after the enemies, and destroy them; the Greek adds, "informing them of what had been accomplished, and that all might pour upon the enemies, to cut them off." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. Now when the children of Israel heard it, they all fell upon them with one consent, and slew them unto Chobai: likewise also they that came from Jerusalem, and from all the mountainous country; the Greek continues, "But when the Israelites heard this, they all, unanimously, fell upon them, as far as Chobai (Hoba); in like manner those from Jerusalem came up, and from all the mountainous country; for they also were informed of what had happened in the camp of their enemies; and the men of Galaad and of Galilee scattered them with great slaughter, till they had passed Damascus and its boundaries." As the enemy fled without a leader, we may easily imagine what carnage would ensue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. And the residue that dwelt at Bethulia, fell upon the camp of Assur, and spoiled them, and were greatly enriched; the Greek reads, "their spoils, and were greatly enriched" — Mariana suggests we should read honestati, which is used in this sense (Ecclesiasticus 11:23), instead of onustati, loaded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. And the children of Israel that returned from the slaughter had that which remained; and the villages and the cities, that were in the mountains and in the plain, got many spoils: for the multitude of them was very great; the Greek reads, "The Israelites, returning from the slaughter, took possession of the remainder, and the villages and cities, both in the mountains and champaign country, took many spoils; for these was great abundance." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. Then Joacim the high priest, and the ancients of the children of Israel that dwelt in Jerusalem, came to behold the good things that God had showed to Israel, and to see Judith, and to speak friendly unto her; the Greek adds, "and the senate of Israel, residing at Jerusalem, came" — this alludes to the sanhedrim, though its institution seems posterior to the captivity, and we may understand the principal men of the city or of the priests. "To see" is in Greek, "the good things (Complutensian; to confirm or lay a foundation for, the good things) which God had done to Israel, and to see Judith, and speak peace with her" — Protestants read "to salute her." Joachim is the same with Eliacim, El being only prefixed; he is the Sadoc II or Odeas of Josephus, and succeeded Sobna both in spiritual and temporal power (Isaiah 22). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. And when they came unto her, they blessed her with one accord, and said unto her, "Thou art the exaltation of Jerusalem, thou art the great glory of Israel, thou art the great rejoicing of our nation"; the Greek adds, "great joy, and the great boast of our race." Judith was a figure of the blessed Virgin Mary, to whom these praises chiefly belong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. Thou hast done all these things by thine hand: thou hast done much good to Israel, and God is pleased therewith: blessed be thou of the Almighty Lord for evermore; the Greek continues, "because thou hast done all these things with thy hand, thou hast procured the good of Israel, and God hath been pleased with them. Be thou blessed by the omnipotent Lord for ever." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. And the people spoiled the camp the space of thirty days: and they gave unto Judith Holofernes his tent, and all his plate, and beds, and vessels, and all his household stuff; the Greek reads, "And the people plundered the camp thirty (Syriac; three) days." The camp of the Assyrians was in various places, and the people waited a month before they began to divide the spoils among all, according to their laws (Numbers 31:27; 1 Samuel 30:24). The Greek continues, "And they gave to Judith the tent of Holofernes, and all the silver plate, and beds and basins, and all his furniture, which she taking, placed upon her mule, and put to her chariots, and heaped them thereon." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. Then all the women of Israel ran together to see her, and blessed her, and made a dance among them for her: and she took branches in her hand, and gave also to the women that were with her; the Greek continues, "and every woman of Israel ran to see her, and they blessed her, and danced in her honour. Then she took some thyrsus (or branches entwined with ivy) in her hands, and gave to those women who accompanied her." The bearing of green branches on such occasions was very common (Leviticus 23:40; 2 Maccabees 10:7; Matthew 21:8); hence Tacitus supposed that the Jews adored Bacchus, but there was nothing here of the immodesty which generally attended the pagan festivals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. And they put a garland of olive upon her and her maid that was with her, and she went before all the people in the dance, leading all the women: and all the men of Israel followed in their armour with garlands, and with songs in their mouths; the Greek continues, "and they crowned with olive both her and her attendant (maid, or Alexandrian Septuagint, 'and she was crowned with olive, and her mule'; Grabe substitutes 'female companions'), and she went before all the people, leading the dance of all the women; and every man of Israel followed in arms, with crowns, and with hymns in their mouth." We only find this instance of women being crowned with olive, but this tree was very proper, as it was used in rejoicings — "Ramo felicis olivae," on which Servius remarks, "olivae, arboris festae" — and at certain races in Greece, women who gained the victory were thus crowned, and it was used by the Roman cavalry on the ides of July, and in ovations. The processions of the Church often admit of similar ornaments in honour of God, to promote piety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
