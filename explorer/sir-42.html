The Text — Sirach 42 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Of these things be not thou ashamed, and accept no person to sin thereby:

2 Of the law of the most High, and his covenant; and of judgment to justify the ungodly;

3 Of reckoning with thy partners and travellers; or of the gift of the heritage of friends;

4 Of exactness of balance and weights; or of getting much or little;

5 And of merchants' indifferent selling; of much correction of children; and to make the side of an evil servant to bleed.

6 Sure keeping is good, where an evil wife is; and shut up, where many hands are.

7 Deliver all things in number and weight; and put all in writing that thou givest out, or receivest in.

8 Be not ashamed to inform the unwise and foolish, and the extreme aged that contendeth with those that are young: thus shalt thou be truly learned, and approved of all men living.

9 The father waketh for the daughter, when no man knoweth; and the care for her taketh away sleep: when she is young, lest she pass away the flower of her age; and being married, lest she should be hated:

10 In her virginity, lest she should be defiled and gotten with child in her father's house; and having an husband, lest she should misbehave herself; and when she is married, lest she should be barren.

11 Keep a sure watch over a shameless daughter, lest she make thee a laughingstock to thine enemies, and a byword in the city, and a reproach among the people, and make thee ashamed before the multitude.

12 Behold not every body's beauty, and sit not in the midst of women.

13 For from garments cometh a moth, and from women wickedness.

14 Better is the churlishness of a man than a courteous woman, a woman, I say, which bringeth shame and reproach.

15 I will now remember the works of the Lord, and declare the things that I have seen: In the words of the Lord are his works.

16 The sun that giveth light looketh upon all things, and the work thereof is full of the glory of the Lord.

17 The Lord hath not given power to the saints to declare all his marvellous works, which the Almighty Lord firmly settled, that whatsoever is might be established for his glory.

18 He seeketh out the deep, and the heart, and considereth their crafty devices: for the Lord knoweth all that may be known, and he beholdeth the signs of the world.

19 He declareth the things that are past, and for to come, and revealeth the steps of hidden things.

20 No thought escapeth him, neither any word is hidden from him.

21 He hath garnished the excellent works of his wisdom, and he is from everlasting to everlasting: unto him may nothing be added, neither can he be diminished, and he hath no need of any counsellor.

22 Oh how desirable are all his works! and that a man may see even to a spark.

23 All these things live and remain for ever for all uses, and they are all obedient.

24 All things are double one against another: and he hath made nothing imperfect.

25 One thing establisheth the good or another: and who shall be filled with beholding his glory?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter lists things not to be ashamed of, warning against accepting any person to sin thereby. It includes the saying that as moths come from garments, so wickedness is said to come from women, and closes by marvelling that one thing establishes the good of another, and no one can be filled with beholding God's glory. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

  • Verse 3. Of reckoning with your partners and travellers, or of the gift of the heritage of friends. Do not be ashamed of honest reckoning with partners and travellers, or of gifts from friends' heritage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. Of exactness of balance and weights, or of getting much or little. Exact balances and weights, and getting much or little honestly, are nothing to be ashamed of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. And of merchants' indifferent selling; of much correction of children; and to make the side of an evil servant bleed. Impartial merchant selling, much correction of children, and severe correction of an evil servant are not shames. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. Sure keeping is good, where an evil wife is, and shut up, where many hands are. Sure keeping is good with an evil wife, and locking up where many hands are about. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. Deliver all things in number and weight, and put all in writing that you give out or receive. All things should be delivered by number and weight, and all givings and receivings put in writing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Do not be ashamed to inform the unwise and foolish, and the extreme aged who contend with those who are young; thus you will be truly learned, and approved of all men living. Informing the unwise, foolish, and aged contenders with the young is no shame; it proves true learning and wins approval. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

The father wakes for the daughter, when no man knows, and care for her takes away sleep: when she is young, lest she pass away the flower of her age; and being married, lest she should be hated. He has garnished the excellent works of his wisdom, and he is from everlasting to everlasting; to him nothing may be added, nor can he be diminished, and he has no need of any counsellor. God's excellent wisdom-works are adorned, he is eternal, nothing can be added to or taken from him, and he needs no counsellor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 22. O how desirable are all his works, and that a man may see even to a spark! God's works are altogether desirable, even to the smallest spark a man may see. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 23. All these things live and remain for ever for all uses, and they are all obedient. All these things live and remain for ever, for all uses, and all obey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

  • Verse 25. One thing establishes the good of another, and who will be filled with beholding his glory? One thing supports another's good, and no one can be filled with beholding God's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
