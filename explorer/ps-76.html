The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 76 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 In Judah is God known: his name is great in Israel.

2 In Salem also is his tabernacle, and his dwelling place in Zion.

3 There brake he the arrows of the bow, the shield, and the sword, and the battle. Selah.

4 Thou art more glorious and excellent than the mountains of prey.

5 The stouthearted are spoiled, they have slept their sleep: and none of the men of might have found their hands.

6 At thy rebuke, O God of Jacob, both the chariot and horse are cast into a dead sleep.

7 Thou, even thou, art to be feared: and who may stand in thy sight when once thou art angry?

8 Thou didst cause judgment to be heard from heaven; the earth feared, and was still,

9 When God arose to judgment, to save all the meek of the earth. Selah.

10 Surely the wrath of man shall praise thee: the remainder of wrath shalt thou restrain.

11 Vow, and pay unto the LORD your God: let all that be round about him bring presents unto him that ought to be feared.

12 He shall cut off the spirit of princes: he is terrible to the kings of the earth.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God known in Judah, terrible to kings: in Salem and Zion he kept tabernacle and dwelling-place, and there he broke bow, shield, sword, and battle itself. More glorious than mountains of prey, stouthearted invaders spoiled into dead sleep at the God of Jacob's rebuke, chariot and horse alike. Judgment heard from heaven stilled the earth, God arising to save earth's meek, even man's wrath made to praise him, the rest restrained. Vow and pay, for he cuts off princes' spirit and is terrible to earth's kings. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God made himself known in Judah as he was not known among other nations, so that where he is known his name is great. Henry calls this distinguishing favour the honour and privilege of his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. In Salem and Zion God kept his tabernacle and dwelling-place, receiving his people's homage and issuing his orders by his oracles. His presence by his ordinances was the glory of the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. There, in answer to prayer and in fulfilment of promise, God broke every weapon of war — bow, shield, sword and battle itself. Public success owes as much to what is done in the church as to what is done in the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. God is more glorious than the mountains of prey — the proud, oppressive powers that live by destroying. Their glory is to destroy; his is to deliver. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The stouthearted invaders were spoiled and left helpless, sleeping the sleep of sinners that wakes to shame. Bold men were cowed, and strong men could not so much as find their hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. At the rebuke of the God of Jacob chariot and horse alike were cast into a dead sleep. God did but speak the word, and the destroying angel left their war-gear unformidable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. God alone is to be feared, for who can stand before him once he is angry? His majesty is to be reverenced, his sovereignty submitted to, and his justice dreaded by offenders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. God caused judgment to be heard from heaven, and the earth feared and was still, as silence is made by proclamation when the court sits. When God speaks judgment, reverent silence is the fitting response. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. God arose to judgment to save all the meek of the earth — the quiet in the land who bear wrongs but do none. Though meekness exposes them to injury, God will appear and plead their cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Even the wrath of man is made to praise God, as far as he permits it to break forth, and what will not turn to his praise he restrains. He sets bounds to human rage as he does to the raging sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. All who approach God are to vow and pay — to bind themselves to him as loyal subjects and live up to the bond. Their presents are their prayers, praises, and above all their hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. God cuts off the spirit of princes, slipping it off as easily as a flower from its stalk, and is terrible to the kings of the earth. Since there is no contending with God, submission is both wisdom and duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
