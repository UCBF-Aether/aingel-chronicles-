The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the seventeenth year of Pekah the son of Remaliah Ahaz the son of Jotham king of Judah began to reign.

2 Twenty years old was Ahaz when he began to reign, and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem, and did not that which was right in the sight of the LORD his God, like David his father.

3 But he walked in the way of the kings of Israel, yea, and made his son to pass through the fire, according to the abominations of the heathen, whom the LORD cast out from before the children of Israel.

4 And he sacrificed and burnt incense in the high places, and on the hills, and under every green tree.

5 Then Rezin king of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah king of Israel came up to Jerusalem to war: and they besieged Ahaz, but could not overcome him.

6 At that time Rezin king of Syria recovered Elath to Syria, and drave the Jews from Elath: and the Syrians came to Elath, and dwelt there unto this day.

7 So Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglathpileser king of Assyria, saying, I am thy servant and thy son: come up, and save me out of the hand of the king of Syria, and out of the hand of the king of Israel, which rise up against me.

8 And Ahaz took the silver and gold that was found in the house of the LORD, and in the treasures of the king’s house, and sent it for a present to the king of Assyria.

9 And the king of Assyria hearkened unto him: for the king of Assyria went up against Damascus, and took it, and carried the people of it captive to Kir, and slew Rezin.

10 And king Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglathpileser king of Assyria, and saw an altar that was at Damascus: and king Ahaz sent to Urijah the priest the fashion of the altar, and the pattern of it, according to all the workmanship thereof.

11 And Urijah the priest built an altar according to all that king Ahaz had sent from Damascus: so Urijah the priest made it against king Ahaz came from Damascus.

12 And when the king was come from Damascus, the king saw the altar: and the king approached to the altar, and offered thereon.

13 And he burnt his burnt offering and his meat offering, and poured his drink offering, and sprinkled the blood of his peace offerings, upon the altar.

14 And he brought also the brasen altar, which was before the LORD, from the forefront of the house, from between the altar and the house of the LORD, and put it on the north side of the altar.

15 And king Ahaz commanded Urijah the priest, saying, Upon the great altar burn the morning burnt offering, and the evening meat offering, and the king’s burnt sacrifice, and his meat offering, with the burnt offering of all the people of the land, and their meat offering, and their drink offerings; and sprinkle upon it all the blood of the burnt offering, and all the blood of the sacrifice: and the brasen altar shall be for me to enquire by.

16 Thus did Urijah the priest, according to all that king Ahaz commanded.

17 And king Ahaz cut off the borders of the bases, and removed the laver from off them; and took down the sea from off the brasen oxen that were under it, and put it upon the pavement of stones.

18 And the covert for the sabbath that they had built in the house, and the king’s entry without, turned he from the house of the LORD for the king of Assyria.

19 Now the rest of the acts of Ahaz which he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

20 And Ahaz slept with his fathers, and was buried with his fathers in the city of David: and Hezekiah his son reigned in his stead.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ahaz of Judah, pressed by Syria and Israel, buys Assyria's help with temple treasure and copies a Damascus altar for the temple, remodelling God's house to a foreign pattern. He walks in the ways of Israel's kings, and his son Hezekiah succeeds. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of Jotham king of Judah began to reign. JFB's section "Ahaz' wicked reign over Judah" opens with this accession, noting Isaiah prophesied in this period of religious degeneracy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Ahaz was twenty when he began to reign, reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem, and did not what was right in the LORD's sight like David his father. JFB notes the voluptuousness and religious degeneracy of all classes under Ahaz, as worldly wealth and luxury from Azariah's and Jotham's reigns bore fruit in idolatry throughout the kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Ahaz walked in the way of Israel's kings, even making his son pass through the fire, after the abominations of the heathen the LORD cast out before Israel. JFB explains this describes his early reign, when he patronized symbolic worship by images before going further into gross idolatry, and the fire-passing was a lustration between Moloch's red-hot hands, sometimes burning children to death, strongly prohibited in the law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Ahaz sacrificed and burnt incense in the high places, on the hills, and under every green tree. JFB notes idolatrous practices of every kind prevailed in all parts of the kingdom under his influence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Rezin king of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah king of Israel came up to Jerusalem to war but could not prevail against it. JFB notes that despite great efforts and military preparations they failed to take the city, and, disappointed, raised the siege and returned home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. At that time Rezin recovered Elath to Syria, driving the Jews from Elath, and the Syrians came and dwelt there to this day. JFB notes Elath was what Azariah had gained, and prefers the Septuagint's "Edomites" here, following judicious commentators and travellers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglathpileser king of Assyria, saying, "I am thy servant and thy son: come up, and save me out of the hand of Syria's king and Israel's king." JFB notes Ahaz did this despite Isaiah's assurances by two signs that the confederate kings would not prevail, seeking Assyrian aid instead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Ahaz took the silver and gold found in the LORD's house and the king's treasures and sent it as a present to Assyria's king. JFB notes he purchased Assyrian aid with the treasures of palace and temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Assyria's king hearkened, went up against Damascus, took it, slew Rezin, and carried Damascus captive to Kir. JFB notes Tiglathpileser marched against Damascus, slew its king, and carried its people to Kir, thought to be Karine in Media. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglathpileser, saw an altar there, and sent its fashion and pattern to Urijah the priest. JFB explains this was a visit of respect and perhaps gratitude, during which Ahaz, captivated by a heathen altar, sent a sketch to Jerusalem ordering Urijah to build one on the Damascus model to supersede the old temple altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Urijah the priest built an altar according to all King Ahaz had sent from Damascus, before the king came from Damascus. JFB notes Urijah, with culpable complaisance, acted according to his instructions, and the sin lay in meddling with temple altars whose patterns had been furnished by divine authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. When the king came from Damascus and saw the altar, he approached it and offered upon it. JFB's account of the Damascus altar covers this first use by the king himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Ahaz burnt his burnt offering, meat offering, and drink offering, sprinkling the blood of his peace offerings upon the altar. JFB presents this as the king's adoption of the heathen-modelled altar for regular worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He also brought the brasen altar before the LORD from the forefront of the house, between the new altar and the LORD's house, and put it on the north side of his altar. JFB notes the new altar was to supersede the old one in the temple, displacing the divinely patterned altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Ahaz commanded Urijah that upon the great altar should be burnt the morning and evening offerings, the king's offerings, and all the people's offerings, with the brasen altar reserved for his inquiry. JFB's exposition of the altar's replacement covers this transfer of all regular worship to the Damascus model. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Urijah the priest did according to all King Ahaz commanded. JFB notes Urijah, one of Isaiah's witnesses against Syria and Israel, acted with culpable complaisance throughout. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Ahaz cut off the borders of the bases, removed the laver from them, and took down the sea from the brasen oxen, setting it on a pavement of stones. JFB's section "He spoils the temple" explains he did this, it is thought, to use the elaborate sculpture in adorning his palace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Ahaz took away the covert for the sabbath built in the house and the king's entry without, turning them to the king of Assyria. JFB explains the covert was the portico through which priests entered on the sabbath, and the king's entry a private external entrance, both removed into the temple from fear of Assyria's king, to secure the temple entrance in case of siege. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Ahaz slept with his fathers, was buried with them in the city of David, and Hezekiah his son reigned in his stead. JFB's chapter closes with this succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
