The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass in the days of Ahaz the son of Jotham, the son of Uzziah, king of Judah, that Rezin the king of Syria, and Pekah the son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up toward Jerusalem to war against it, but could not prevail against it.

2 And it was told the house of David, saying, Syria is confederate with Ephraim. And his heart was moved, and the heart of his people, as the trees of the wood are moved with the wind.

3 Then said the LORD unto Isaiah, Go forth now to meet Ahaz, thou, and Shearjashub thy son, at the end of the conduit of the upper pool in the highway of the fuller’s field;

4 And say unto him, Take heed, and be quiet; fear not, neither be fainthearted for the two tails of these smoking firebrands, for the fierce anger of Rezin with Syria, and of the son of Remaliah.

5 Because Syria, Ephraim, and the son of Remaliah, have taken evil counsel against thee, saying,

6 Let us go up against Judah, and vex it, and let us make a breach therein for us, and set a king in the midst of it, even the son of Tabeal:

7 Thus saith the Lord GOD, It shall not stand, neither shall it come to pass.

8 For the head of Syria is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin; and within threescore and five years shall Ephraim be broken, that it be not a people.

9 And the head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is Remaliah’s son. If ye will not believe, surely ye shall not be established.

10 Moreover the LORD spake again unto Ahaz, saying,

11 Ask thee a sign of the LORD thy God; ask it either in the depth, or in the height above.

12 But Ahaz said, I will not ask, neither will I tempt the LORD.

13 And he said, Hear ye now, O house of David; Is it a small thing for you to weary men, but will ye weary my God also?

14 Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.

15 Butter and honey shall he eat, that he may know to refuse the evil, and choose the good.

16 For before the child shall know to refuse the evil, and choose the good, the land that thou abhorrest shall be forsaken of both her kings.

17 The LORD shall bring upon thee, and upon thy people, and upon thy father’s house, days that have not come, from the day that Ephraim departed from Judah; even the king of Assyria.

18 And it shall come to pass in that day, that the LORD shall hiss for the fly that is in the uttermost part of the rivers of Egypt, and for the bee that is in the land of Assyria.

19 And they shall come, and shall rest all of them in the desolate valleys, and in the holes of the rocks, and upon all thorns, and upon all bushes.

20 In the same day shall the Lord shave with a razor that is hired, namely, by them beyond the river, by the king of Assyria, the head, and the hair of the feet: and it shall also consume the beard.

21 And it shall come to pass in that day, that a man shall nourish a young cow, and two sheep;

22 And it shall come to pass, for the abundance of milk that they shall give he shall eat butter: for butter and honey shall every one eat that is left in the land.

23 And it shall come to pass in that day, that every place shall be, where there were a thousand vines at a thousand silverlings, it shall even be for briers and thorns.

24 With arrows and with bows shall men come thither; because all the land shall become briers and thorns.

25 And on all hills that shall be digged with the mattock, there shall not come thither the fear of briers and thorns: but it shall be for the sending forth of oxen, and for the treading of lesser cattle.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

When Syria and Israel march on Jerusalem to replace King Ahaz, God sends Isaiah to tell him not to fear: the two kings are smouldering firebrands whose plan will fail. Ahaz refuses a sign, so God gives one anyway — a child called Immanuel — and warns that Assyria, the power Ahaz trusted, will flood the land until vineyards lie under briers and thorns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the days of Ahaz son of Jotham, Rezin king of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah king of Israel went up toward Jerusalem to war, but could not prevail against it. Their design, formed in Jotham's reign, was probably to unite the three kingdoms against Assyria, and an actual siege of Jerusalem took place but was foiled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When it was told the house of David that Syria was confederate with Ephraim — encamped upon Ephraim's territory, or supported by Ephraim, whose land lay between Syria and Judah — the heart of Ahaz and his people was moved as trees of the wood are moved by wind. The mention of David contrasts sadly with the time when David made Syria subject to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The Lord told Isaiah to go forth to meet Ahaz, with his son Shearjashub, at the end of the conduit of the upper pool in the highway of the fuller's field. Ahaz was there superintending works of defence and securing the water supply, and Shearjashub's very name, "A remnant shall return," was a standing memorial that the nation would not be utterly destroyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Isaiah was to say: Take heed and be quiet, fear not, neither be fainthearted for the two tails of smoking firebrands — Rezin and the son of Remaliah. The two kings are mere ends of firebrands, almost consumed themselves and about to go out, therefore harmless, and Ahaz must not seek Assyrian aid in a fit of panic. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Because Syria, Ephraim, and the son of Remaliah have taken evil counsel against Judah, God answers their plot with the assurance that follows. Their counsel is the scheme spelled out in the next verse: to break Judah apart and set up a vassal king of their own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Their plan was to go up against Judah, throw it into consternation, cleave it asunder, and set a king in its midst, even the son of Tabeal. The scheme was to divide a large portion of the territory between themselves and set up a vassal king over the rest, Tabeal being an unknown Syrian-sounding name perhaps favoured by a party in Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Thus says the Lord God: It shall not stand, neither shall it come to pass. The confederates' counsel against Judah is overruled by God's word, as Proverbs says that no counsel stands against the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For the head of Syria is Damascus and the head of Damascus is Rezin, and within sixty-five years Ephraim will be broken so as to be no people. Each capital will remain only its own dominion and not conquer Judah, while Israel's final breaking — under Esarhaddon, with Samaria colonised by foreigners, sixty-five years from the prophecy — assures Judah that God will ultimately destroy his people's enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The head of Ephraim is Samaria and the head of Samaria is Remaliah's son, and if you will not believe, surely you shall not be established. There is a play on words in Hebrew — if you will not confide, you shall not abide — and Ahaz brought distress on himself by distrust in the Lord and trust in Assyria. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Since Ahaz does not credit the prophet's words, the Lord offers a sign: ask it either in the depth or in the height above. A sign is a miraculous token assuring that God will fulfil his promise of saving Jerusalem, and Ahaz is offered the widest limits — depth of earth or Hades, or height of heaven — within which to choose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. But Ahaz said, I will not ask, neither will I tempt the Lord, using a hypocritical pretext of keeping the law. To ask a sign when thus offered by God's prophet would not have been tempting God; Ahaz's true reason was his resolve to negotiate with Assyria and persevere in idolatry, excusing distrust in God by professed reverence for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Isaiah answers: Hear now, O house of David; is it a small thing for you to weary men, but will you weary my God also? The allusion to David contrasts his trust in God with his degenerate descendant's distrust, and since God has now offered a sign which Ahaz publicly rejects, the sin is openly against God, not merely against men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Therefore the Lord himself will give a sign: Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel. The sign is given for the sake of the believing house of David, not for unbelieving Ahaz; "virgin" in its simplest sense applies to the Virgin Mary, and "Immanuel," God with us, cannot in a strict sense apply to Isaiah's son, but only to him expressly called the mighty God in chapter 9 — while the language is also partially applicable to the immediate event, as a type foreshadowing the great central end of prophecy, Jesus Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Butter and honey shall he eat, that he may know to refuse the evil and choose the good. Butter is curdled milk, grateful in the heat of the East, and honey was abundant in Palestine; at about three years of age moral consciousness begins, and the child's ordinary infant food also implies the real humanity of Immanuel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. For before the child shall know to refuse the evil and choose the good, the land that you dread will be forsaken by both her kings. Desolate shall be the land before whose two kings Ahaz is alarmed — Syria and Samaria regarded as one — just two years after this prophecy, as Tiglath-pileser subdued them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The Lord will bring upon Ahaz, his people, and his father's house days that have not come since Ephraim departed from Judah: the king of Assyria. Though temporary deliverance was given then and final deliverance through Messiah, sore punishment follows Ahaz's Assyrian policy, and after subduing Syria and Israel the Assyrians will make Judah the battlefield against Egypt and tributary to Assyria itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. In that day the Lord will hiss — whistle, as to bring bees to settle — for the fly in the uttermost part of the rivers of Egypt and for the bee in the land of Assyria. The fly figures numerous, troublesome foes from the remotest parts of Egypt, such as Pharaoh-necho, and the bee figures Assyria's hosts, fulfilled by Sennacherib, Esarhaddon, and Nebuchadnezzar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. They will come and rest in the desolate valleys, in the holes of the rocks, upon all thorns, and upon all bushes, the image of flies and bees kept up. The enemy will overspread the land everywhere, even in desolate valleys, among wild thorns as well as valued bushes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. In the same day the Lord will shave with a razor that is hired, by them beyond the river, by the king of Assyria, the head and the hair of the feet. The Assyrians are God's instrument for devastating Judea, as a razor sweeps away all hair, and "hired" alludes to Ahaz hiring Tiglath-pileser; to cut the beard is the greatest indignity to an Easterner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. In that day a man will nourish a young cow and two sheep, for agriculture will cease and the land become one great pasturage. With so few inhabitants left, a single heifer and two sheep will be all a man owns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Because of the abundance of milk they give, he will eat butter — thick milk or cream — for butter and honey will everyone eat who is left in the land. Food of spontaneous growth will be the resource of the few inhabitants left, with honey abundant as bees find wild flowers everywhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. In that day every place where there were a thousand vines worth a silverling each will become briers and thorns. Vineyards were estimated by the number and goodness of their vines, and Judea, which admits of a high state of cultivation, will fall barren through neglect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. With arrows and bows men will come there, because all the land will become briers and thorns. The once-cultivated land will become a vast hunting ground abounding in wild beasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. On all hills that were digged with the mattock for planting vines, no one will come for fear of briers and thorns; it will be for the sending forth of oxen and the treading of lesser cattle, sheep and goats. Only cattle will be able to penetrate the briery ground where vine-dressers once worked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms
