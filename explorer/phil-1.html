The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Philippians 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Paul and Timotheus, the servants of Jesus Christ, to all the saints in Christ Jesus which are at Philippi, with the bishops and deacons:

2 Grace be unto you, and peace, from God our Father, and from the Lord Jesus Christ.

3 I thank my God upon every remembrance of you,

4 Always in every prayer of mine for you all making request with joy,

5 For your fellowship in the gospel from the first day until now;

6 Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:

7 Even as it is meet for me to think this of you all, because I have you in my heart; inasmuch as both in my bonds, and in the defence and confirmation of the gospel, ye all are partakers of my grace.

8 For God is my record, how greatly I long after you all in the bowels of Jesus Christ.

9 And this I pray, that your love may abound yet more and more in knowledge and in all judgment;

10 That ye may approve things that are excellent; that ye may be sincere and without offence till the day of Christ.

11 Being filled with the fruits of righteousness, which are by Jesus Christ, unto the glory and praise of God.

12 But I would ye should understand, brethren, that the things which happened unto me have fallen out rather unto the furtherance of the gospel;

13 So that my bonds in Christ are manifest in all the palace, and in all other places;

14 And many of the brethren in the Lord, waxing confident by my bonds, are much more bold to speak the word without fear.

15 Some indeed preach Christ even of envy and strife; and some also of good will:

16 The one preach Christ of contention, not sincerely, supposing to add affliction to my bonds:

17 But the other of love, knowing that I am set for the defence of the gospel.

18 What then? notwithstanding, every way, whether in pretence, or in truth, Christ is preached; and I therein do rejoice, yea, and will rejoice.

19 For I know that this shall turn to my salvation through your prayer, and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ,

20 According to my earnest expectation and my hope, that in nothing I shall be ashamed, but that with all boldness, as always, so now also Christ shall be magnified in my body, whether it be by life, or by death.

21 For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.

22 But if I live in the flesh, this is the fruit of my labour: yet what I shall choose I wot not.

23 For I am in a strait betwixt two, having a desire to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better:

24 Nevertheless to abide in the flesh is more needful for you.

25 And having this confidence, I know that I shall abide and continue with you all for your furtherance and joy of faith;

26 That your rejoicing may be more abundant in Jesus Christ for me by my coming to you again.

27 Only let your conversation be as it becometh the gospel of Christ: that whether I come and see you, or else be absent, I may hear of your affairs, that ye stand fast in one spirit, with one mind striving together for the faith of the gospel;

28 And in nothing terrified by your adversaries: which is to them an evident token of perdition, but to you of salvation, and that of God.

29 For unto you it is given in the behalf of Christ, not only to believe on him, but also to suffer for his sake;

30 Having the same conflict which ye saw in me, and now hear to be in me.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul thanks God at every remembrance of the Philippians and prays their love will abound in knowledge and discernment. His imprisonment, he reports, has advanced the gospel, even emboldening others to preach, and he is torn between living on, which serves them, and departing to be with Christ, which he calls far better: for him, to live is Christ and to die is gain. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul and Timothy, servants of Jesus Christ, write to all the saints in Christ Jesus at Philippi, with the bishops and deacons. Servants, not lords, address saints with their officers, for the church's order is named without exalting any office above servanthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Grace be to you, and peace, from God our Father and from the Lord Jesus Christ. Grace and peace, from Father and Son together, are the apostolic blessing, for grace is the fountain and peace is the stream. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. I thank my God upon every remembrance of you. Every remembrance of the Philippians turned to thanksgiving, for their faithfulness made memory itself a prayer of gratitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Always in every prayer of mine for you all making request with joy. Every prayer for them all was made with joy, for intercession for a faithful church is delight, not burden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For your fellowship in the gospel from the first day until now. Their fellowship in the gospel, unbroken from the first day, is the ground of joy, for constancy in the gospel is rarer than a good beginning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Being confident of this very thing, that he who has begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ. Confidence rests not on their constancy but on God's beginning: he who began the work will perform it until Christ's day, for God's work is never left half-done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Even as it is right for me to think this of you all, because I have you in my heart, inasmuch as both in my bonds and in the defence and confirmation of the gospel, you all are partakers of my grace. He holds them in his heart because they shared both his bonds and his defence of the gospel, partakers of the same grace in suffering and in witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For God is my record, how greatly I long after you all in the bowels of Jesus Christ. God witnesses his longing, which is not mere affection but the bowels of Christ himself, for Paul's love for them is Christ's love working in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. This I pray, that your love may abound yet more and more in knowledge and in all judgment. His prayer is that love abound, but in knowledge and judgment, for love without discernment is blind, and abounding love must grow in wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. That you may approve things that are excellent, that you may be sincere and without offence till the day of Christ. Approving the excellent, being sincere and without offence, is love's fruit: a conscience clear and a life that stumbles none, until Christ's day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Being filled with the fruits of righteousness, which are by Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God. Fruits of righteousness, produced by Christ, fill the believer, and their end is God's glory and praise, not man's reputation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. But I would you should understand, brethren, that the things which happened to me have fallen out rather to the furtherance of the gospel. His imprisonment, which seemed a hindrance, furthered the gospel, for God's purposes turn chains into channels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. So that my bonds in Christ are manifest in all the palace, and in all other places. His bonds in Christ became manifest in the palace itself, for the praetorium heard the gospel from a prisoner, and chains preached where a free apostle could not enter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Many of the brethren in the Lord, waxing confident by my bonds, are much more bold to speak the word without fear. His bonds emboldened others, for seeing a prisoner preach without fear taught brethren to speak without fear, and courage is contagious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Some indeed preach Christ even of envy and strife, and some also of good will. Preachers divide into two sorts: some preach from envy and strife, others from good will, and the same Christ is preached from opposite motives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The one preach Christ of contention, not sincerely, supposing to add affliction to my bonds. Contention's preachers are insincere, hoping to add affliction to his bonds, for they would make his prison heavier by their rivalry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. But the other of love, knowing that I am set for the defence of the gospel. Love's preachers know he is set for the gospel's defence, and their preaching supports, not supplants, his appointed work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. What then? Notwithstanding, every way, whether in pretence or in truth, Christ is preached, and I therein do rejoice, yea, and will rejoice. What then? If Christ is preached, whether in pretence or truth, Paul rejoices and will rejoice, for the message matters more than the messenger's motive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. For I know that this shall turn to my salvation through your prayer, and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ. His deliverance, whether from prison or through death, will come through their prayer and the Spirit's supply, for prayer and Spirit together sustain the suffering apostle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. According to my earnest expectation and hope, that in nothing I shall be ashamed, but that with all boldness, as always, so now also Christ shall be magnified in my body, whether by life or by death. His hope is to be ashamed in nothing, but with boldness to magnify Christ in his body, by life or death alike, for either outcome can glorify Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain. Living is Christ, for Christ is the life's business and end, and dying is gain, for death brings the life's reward, and both sides of the balance favour the believer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. But if I live in the flesh, this is the fruit of my labour, yet what I shall choose I know not. Living in the flesh means fruitful labour, yet choice between life and death baffles him, for both have their gain and he cannot choose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. For I am in a strait between two, having a desire to depart and to be with Christ, which is far better. Strait between two, he desires to depart and be with Christ, which is far better, for presence with Christ outweighs every earthly good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Nevertheless to abide in the flesh is more needful for you. Yet abiding in the flesh is more needful for them, and love for the church outweighs desire for heaven, for his life is theirs as well as his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Having this confidence, I know that I shall abide and continue with you all for your furtherance and joy of faith. Confidence that he shall abide is grounded in their need: his continuance will further their faith and joy, and God's servant stays while his work remains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. That your rejoicing may be more abundant in Jesus Christ for me by my coming to you again. His coming again will make their rejoicing more abundant in Christ, for his presence will be a fresh cause of joy in the Lord, not in himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Only let your conversation be as it becomes the gospel of Christ, that whether I come and see you, or else be absent, I may hear of your affairs, that you stand fast in one spirit, with one mind striving together for the faith of the gospel. Their conduct must become the gospel, standing fast in one spirit and striving together for its faith, whether he comes or is absent, for unity in the gospel is the church's witness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. And in nothing terrified by your adversaries, which is to them an evident token of perdition, but to you of salvation, and that of God. Unterrified steadfastness is a token: to adversaries it evidences perdition, to believers salvation, and both tokens are of God, who marks his own by their courage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. For to you it is given in behalf of Christ, not only to believe on him, but also to suffer for his sake. Believing and suffering are both gifts in Christ's behalf, for suffering for him is not a misfortune but a grant, given as surely as faith itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Having the same conflict which you saw in me, and now hear to be in me. Their conflict is the same as his, seen in his former labours and heard in his present bonds, for the church's warfare is one, whether in Philippi or in Rome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
