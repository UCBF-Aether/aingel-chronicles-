The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now in the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar, Pontius Pilate being governor of Judaea, and Herod being tetrarch of Galilee, and his brother Philip tetrarch of Ituraea and of the region of Trachonitis, and Lysanias the tetrarch of Abilene,

2 Annas and Caiaphas being the high priests, the word of God came unto John the son of Zacharias in the wilderness.

3 And he came into all the country about Jordan, preaching the baptism of repentance for the remission of sins;

4 As it is written in the book of the words of Esaias the prophet, saying, The voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make his paths straight.

5 Every valley shall be filled, and every mountain and hill shall be brought low; and the crooked shall be made straight, and the rough ways shall be made smooth;

6 And all flesh shall see the salvation of God.

7 Then said he to the multitude that came forth to be baptized of him, O generation of vipers, who hath warned you to flee from the wrath to come?

8 Bring forth therefore fruits worthy of repentance, and begin not to say within yourselves, We have Abraham to our father: for I say unto you, That God is able of these stones to raise up children unto Abraham.

9 And now also the axe is laid unto the root of the trees: every tree therefore which bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire.

10 And the people asked him, saying, What shall we do then?

11 He answereth and saith unto them, He that hath two coats, let him impart to him that hath none; and he that hath meat, let him do likewise.

12 Then came also publicans to be baptized, and said unto him, Master, what shall we do?

13 And he said unto them, Exact no more than that which is appointed you.

14 And the soldiers likewise demanded of him, saying, And what shall we do? And he said unto them, Do violence to no man, neither accuse any falsely; and be content with your wages.

15 And as the people were in expectation, and all men mused in their hearts of John, whether he were the Christ, or not;

16 John answered, saying unto them all, I indeed baptize you with water; but one mightier than I cometh, the latchet of whose shoes I am not worthy to unloose: he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost and with fire:

17 Whose fan is in his hand, and he will throughly purge his floor, and will gather the wheat into his garner; but the chaff he will burn with fire unquenchable.

18 And many other things in his exhortation preached he unto the people.

19 But Herod the tetrarch, being reproved by him for Herodias his brother Philip’s wife, and for all the evils which Herod had done,

20 Added yet this above all, that he shut up John in prison.

21 Now when all the people were baptized, it came to pass, that Jesus also being baptized, and praying, the heaven was opened,

22 And the Holy Ghost descended in a bodily shape like a dove upon him, and a voice came from heaven, which said, Thou art my beloved Son; in thee I am well pleased.

23 And Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli,

24 Which was the son of Matthat, which was the son of Levi, which was the son of Melchi, which was the son of Janna, which was the son of Joseph,

25 Which was the son of Mattathias, which was the son of Amos, which was the son of Naum, which was the son of Esli, which was the son of Nagge,

26 Which was the son of Maath, which was the son of Mattathias, which was the son of Semei, which was the son of Joseph, which was the son of Juda,

27 Which was the son of Joanna, which was the son of Rhesa, which was the son of Zorobabel, which was the son of Salathiel, which was the son of Neri,

28 Which was the son of Melchi, which was the son of Addi, which was the son of Cosam, which was the son of Elmodam, which was the son of Er,

29 Which was the son of Jose, which was the son of Eliezer, which was the son of Jorim, which was the son of Matthat, which was the son of Levi,

30 Which was the son of Simeon, which was the son of Juda, which was the son of Joseph, which was the son of Jonan, which was the son of Eliakim,

31 Which was the son of Melea, which was the son of Menan, which was the son of Mattatha, which was the son of Nathan, which was the son of David,

32 Which was the son of Jesse, which was the son of Obed, which was the son of Booz, which was the son of Salmon, which was the son of Naasson,

33 Which was the son of Aminadab, which was the son of Aram, which was the son of Esrom, which was the son of Phares, which was the son of Juda,

34 Which was the son of Jacob, which was the son of Isaac, which was the son of Abraham, which was the son of Thara, which was the son of Nachor,

35 Which was the son of Saruch, which was the son of Ragau, which was the son of Phalec, which was the son of Heber, which was the son of Sala,

36 Which was the son of Cainan, which was the son of Arphaxad, which was the son of Sem, which was the son of Noe, which was the son of Lamech,

37 Which was the son of Mathusala, which was the son of Enoch, which was the son of Jared, which was the son of Maleleel, which was the son of Cainan,

38 Which was the son of Enos, which was the son of Seth, which was the son of Adam, which was the son of God.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Luke dates John’s ministry precisely — in the fifteenth year of Tiberius, under Pilate, Herod, Philip, Lysanias, Annas, and Caiaphas — and John preaches a baptism of repentance in the Jordan country, quoting Isaiah’s voice in the wilderness (vv. 1–6). Crowds, publicans, and soldiers are told what repentance looks like in practice, and John points beyond his water baptism to one mightier who will baptize with the Holy Ghost and fire; Herod shuts John in prison (vv. 7–20). Jesus is then baptized, the Spirit descends like a dove, the Father’s voice speaks, and a genealogy traces him back through David and Abraham to Adam and to God (vv. 21–38). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 3

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the fifteenth year of Tiberius, with Pilate governing Judaea, Herod tetrarch of Galilee, Philip of Ituraea and Trachonitis, and Lysanias of Abilene, the scene is dated by rulers. John's ministry begins inside real imperial and local history, not outside it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Annas and Caiaphas being high priests, the word of God came to John, son of Zacharias, in the wilderness. The word bypasses the temple establishment and comes to a prophet in the desert. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. John came into all the country about Jordan, preaching the baptism of repentance for the remission of sins. Henry stresses the scope of his ministry: to bring people from their sins to their Saviour, with water baptism as the outward sign of inward cleansing and renewal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. As written in Isaiah, the voice crying in the wilderness says, prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight. Henry sees Scripture fulfilled in John's ministry: way is made for the gospel by levelling high thoughts into obedience to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Every valley shall be filled, every mountain and hill brought low, the crooked made straight, the rough ways smooth. The levelling is inward: whatever hinders Christ and his grace in the soul must be removed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And all flesh shall see the salvation of God. The preparation ends in a universal sight: salvation is not for one party but shown to all flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. To the multitude coming to be baptized, John says, O generation of vipers, who warned you to flee from the wrath to come? Henry calls the guilty, corrupted race a generation of vipers, hateful to God and hating one another, with no flight from wrath but by repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Bring forth fruits worthy of repentance, and do not say, we have Abraham to our father, for God can raise up children to Abraham from stones. Henry warns that profession and relation to God's church stand us in no stead without real holiness in heart and life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The axe is laid to the root of the trees: every tree not bringing forth good fruit is hewn down and cast into the fire. Fruit, not foliage, is the test John sets, and judgment follows barrenness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The people asked, what shall we do then? John's warnings produce the right question: not debate about doctrine, but what repentance looks like in practice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He who has two coats, let him impart to him who has none; and he who has meat, let him do likewise. Henry notes the gospel requires mercy, not sacrifice, and repentance must show itself by reformation according to each person's place and condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Publicans also came to be baptized, asking, Master, what shall we do? Even tax collectors, despised as collaborators, are welcomed to the same question and the same repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Exact no more than that which is appointed you. John's instruction to publicans is exact justice: forego unjust gain, and the same principle leads to restoring what was gained by wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Soldiers asked what they should do, and John answered: do violence to no man, accuse none falsely, and be content with wages. Henry notes men must be cautioned against the temptations of their own employments, and these answers form a test of sincerity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The people were in expectation, musing whether John were the Christ. Expectation is high because John's preaching has awakened Israel to look for the promised one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. John answered all: I baptize with water, but one mightier than I comes, whose shoe latchet I am not worthy to loose; he shall baptize with the Holy Ghost and with fire. Henry stresses John could only exhort and assure forgiveness on repentance, but Christ can give the Spirit to cleanse within, as fire clears dross and casts metal anew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. His fan is in his hand; he will thoroughly purge his floor, gather the wheat into his garner, and burn the chaff with unquenchable fire. The mightier one's baptism is also a separation, and John does not soften it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. With many other things John exhorted and preached to the people. Henry describes him as affectionate, practical, popular, and evangelical: in all exhortations directing people to Christ, for righteousness and strength, and shunning not the whole counsel of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Herod the tetrarch, reproved by John for Herodias, his brother Philip's wife, and for all his evils, heard the same plain preaching in his own court. John's boldness did not stop at the crowds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Herod added above all that he shut up John in prison. Henry notes a full stop was put to John's preaching in the midst of his usefulness, and those who injure God's faithful servants add greater guilt to their other sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. When all the people were baptized, Jesus also being baptized and praying, the heaven was opened. Henry notes Christ had no sin to confess, but he prayed as others did, keeping communion with his Father, and the witness from heaven came while he was praying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The Holy Ghost descended in bodily shape like a dove upon him, and a voice from heaven said, Thou art my beloved Son; in thee I am well pleased. Henry sees here a proof of the Holy Trinity, the Three Persons in the Godhead, given at Christ's baptism. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Jesus began to be about thirty years of age, being, as was supposed, the son of Joseph, who was the son of Heli. Henry notes Luke traces the line beginning with Heli, the father not of Joseph but of Mary, showing Jesus as the Seed of the woman. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Heli's line continues through Matthat, Levi, Melchi, Janna, and Joseph. Each link carries the line one step further from Jesus toward its ancient root. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The line continues through Mattathias, Amos, Naum, Esli, and Nagge. Luke's ascent passes through names otherwise little known, because the line itself, not each man's fame, is the point. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The line continues through Maath, Mattathias, Semei, Joseph, and Juda. The chain is unbroken as it climbs toward the royal house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The line continues through Joanna, Rhesa, Zorobabel, Salathiel, and Neri. Zorobabel stands in the line at the return from exile, tying Jesus to the post-exilic hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The line continues through Melchi, Addi, Cosam, Elmodam, and Er. The ascent moves steadily backward through generations that bridge exile and kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. The line continues through Jose, Eliezer, Jorim, Matthat, and Levi. Each named father is another step in Luke's backward trace toward David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The line continues through Simeon, Juda, Joseph, Jonan, and Eliakim. The repeated familiar names show an ordinary human lineage, not a list of only the famous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. The line continues through Melea, Menan, Mattatha, and Nathan, who was the son of David. Henry's contrast with Matthew matters here: Luke follows Nathan's branch, yet still arrives at David, heir to the throne and promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. David was the son of Jesse, of Obed, of Booz, of Salmon, of Naasson. The royal line is now traced into the Bethlehem household of Ruth's story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. The line continues through Aminadab, Aram, Esrom, Phares, and Juda. From David the ascent passes back through Judah, the tribe of the promised ruler. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. The line continues through Jacob, Isaac, Abraham, Thara, and Nachor. With Abraham the line reaches the father of the promise, in whom all families of the earth are blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The line continues through Saruch, Ragau, Phalec, Heber, and Sala. Beyond Abraham the trace runs into the generations after the flood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. The line continues through Cainan, Arphaxad, Sem, Noe, and Lamech. Sem and Noe carry the line through the flood itself to the new beginning of mankind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. The line continues through Mathusala, Enoch, Jared, Maleleel, and Cainan. The antediluvian names take the line back toward the first man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. The line ends: Enos, Seth, Adam, who was the son of God. Henry's conclusion is the point of the whole list: Christ was both the son of Adam and the Son of God, a proper Mediator between God and the sons of Adam, bringing them to be sons of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
