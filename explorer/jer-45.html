The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 45 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word that Jeremiah the prophet spake unto Baruch the son of Neriah, when he had written these words in a book at the mouth of Jeremiah, in the fourth year of Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah, saying,

2 Thus saith the LORD, the God of Israel, unto thee, O Baruch:

3 Thou didst say, Woe is me now! for the LORD hath added grief to my sorrow; I fainted in my sighing, and I find no rest.

4 Thus shalt thou say unto him, The LORD saith thus; Behold, that which I have built will I break down, and that which I have planted I will pluck up, even this whole land.

5 And seekest thou great things for thyself? seek them not: for, behold, I will bring evil upon all flesh, saith the LORD: but thy life will I give unto thee for a prey in all places whither thou goest.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This short chapter is a personal word to Baruch, spoken when he had written Jeremiah's words in a book in the fourth year of Jehoiakim. Baruch is told not to seek great things for himself while God is breaking down what he built and plucking up what he planted, but is promised that his life will be given him for a prey wherever he goes. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry explains the placement: though this prophecy concerns Baruch only and stands after the destruction of Jerusalem and the dispersion, it was delivered long before, in the fourth year of Jehoiakim, and belongs with the story of Baruch writing and reading Jeremiah's roll in chapter 36, but was thrown to the latter end of the book as a private person's matter, as Philemon stands after Paul's other epistles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry stresses the comfort in the address itself: though Baruch was only Jeremiah's scribe, God despises not any of his servants but graciously concerns himself for the meanest and weakest, for Baruch the scribe as well as for Jeremiah the prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry describes Baruch as a young man well affected to God, willing to serve God and his prophet, who hoped reading the roll publicly would gain him reputation and preferment, but instead found himself driven into a corner in danger of prison or worse, crying that the LORD had added grief to his sorrow after the grief of writing prophecies of his country's ruin, and Henry calls this his infirmity, noting that young beginners in religion are apt to faint at first difficulties and that even dear saints may disquiet themselves with melancholy apprehensions beyond cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry reads the reproof as searching the wound to its root: Baruch's complaints came from raised expectations too high in this world, and God shows him the ship was sinking, since what God had built as a house for himself he was breaking down and what he had planted as a vineyard he was plucking up, even this whole land, so it was folly to desire abundance of wealth and honour at such a time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry calls it absurd to paint one's own cabin while the ship sinks, and unbecoming to seek great things for oneself when the public is in danger, yet gives the encouragement that though Baruch should not be great he should be safe: his life would be given him for a prey in all places whither he goes, escaped often very narrowly and saved as by fire, and Henry notes that preservation of life at such times is a signal favour, since life is more than meat and prolongs the opportunity to glorify God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
