The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 30 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Hezekiah sent to all Israel and Judah, and wrote letters also to Ephraim and Manasseh, that they should come to the house of the LORD at Jerusalem, to keep the passover unto the LORD God of Israel.

2 For the king had taken counsel, and his princes, and all the congregation in Jerusalem, to keep the passover in the second month.

3 For they could not keep it at that time, because the priests had not sanctified themselves sufficiently, neither had the people gathered themselves together to Jerusalem.

4 And the thing pleased the king and all the congregation.

5 So they established a decree to make proclamation throughout all Israel, from Beersheba even to Dan, that they should come to keep the passover unto the LORD God of Israel at Jerusalem: for they had not done it of a long time in such sort as it was written.

6 So the posts went with the letters from the king and his princes throughout all Israel and Judah, and according to the commandment of the king, saying, Ye children of Israel, turn again unto the LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, and he will return to the remnant of you, that are escaped out of the hand of the kings of Assyria.

7 And be not ye like your fathers, and like your brethren, which trespassed against the LORD God of their fathers, who therefore gave them up to desolation, as ye see.

8 Now be ye not stiffnecked, as your fathers were, but yield yourselves unto the LORD, and enter into his sanctuary, which he hath sanctified for ever: and serve the LORD your God, that the fierceness of his wrath may turn away from you.

9 For if ye turn again unto the LORD, your brethren and your children shall find compassion before them that lead them captive, so that they shall come again into this land: for the LORD your God is gracious and merciful, and will not turn away his face from you, if ye return unto him.

10 So the posts passed from city to city through the country of Ephraim and Manasseh even unto Zebulun: but they laughed them to scorn, and mocked them.

11 Nevertheless divers of Asher and Manasseh and of Zebulun humbled themselves, and came to Jerusalem.

12 Also in Judah the hand of God was to give them one heart to do the commandment of the king and of the princes, by the word of the LORD.

13 And there assembled at Jerusalem much people to keep the feast of unleavened bread in the second month, a very great congregation.

14 And they arose and took away the altars that were in Jerusalem, and all the altars for incense took they away, and cast them into the brook Kidron.

15 Then they killed the passover on the fourteenth day of the second month: and the priests and the Levites were ashamed, and sanctified themselves, and brought in the burnt offerings into the house of the LORD.

16 And they stood in their place after their manner, according to the law of Moses the man of God: the priests sprinkled the blood, which they received of the hand of the Levites.

17 For there were many in the congregation that were not sanctified: therefore the Levites had the charge of the killing of the passovers for every one that was not clean, to sanctify them unto the LORD.

18 For a multitude of the people, even many of Ephraim, and Manasseh, Issachar, and Zebulun, had not cleansed themselves, yet did they eat the passover otherwise than it was written. But Hezekiah prayed for them, saying, The good LORD pardon every one

19 That prepareth his heart to seek God, the LORD God of his fathers, though he be not cleansed according to the purification of the sanctuary.

20 And the LORD hearkened to Hezekiah, and healed the people.

21 And the children of Israel that were present at Jerusalem kept the feast of unleavened bread seven days with great gladness: and the Levites and the priests praised the LORD day by day, singing with loud instruments unto the LORD.

22 And Hezekiah spake comfortably unto all the Levites that taught the good knowledge of the LORD: and they did eat throughout the feast seven days, offering peace offerings, and making confession to the LORD God of their fathers.

23 And the whole assembly took counsel to keep other seven days: and they kept other seven days with gladness.

24 For Hezekiah king of Judah did give to the congregation a thousand bullocks and seven thousand sheep; and the princes gave to the congregation a thousand bullocks and ten thousand sheep: and a great number of priests sanctified themselves.

25 And all the congregation of Judah, with the priests and the Levites, and all the congregation that came out of Israel, and the strangers that came out of the land of Israel, and that dwelt in Judah, rejoiced.

26 So there was great joy in Jerusalem: for since the time of Solomon the son of David king of Israel there was not the like in Jerusalem.

27 Then the priests the Levites arose and blessed the people: and their voice was heard, and their prayer came up to his holy dwelling place, even unto heaven.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Hezekiah's invitation to all Israel and Judah to keep the passover at Jerusalem, its keeping in the second month by a great multitude, and the joy that followed. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And Hezekiah sent to all Israel and Judah, and wrote letters also to Ephraim and Manasseh, that they should come to the house of the Lord at Jerusalem, to keep the passover unto the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes an invitation was sent to the ten revolted tribes, not with political design to bring them back to David's house, but with pious design to bring them back to the Lord God of Israel, for let them take whom they will for king, so they will but take him for their God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. For the king had taken counsel, and his princes, and all the congregation in Jerusalem, to keep the passover in the second month. Henry notes a passover was resolved upon: the reviving temple service fell within the feast's appointed days, bringing the forgotten solemnity to mind, and Hezekiah, finding a proviso in Moses' law that particular persons unclean in the first month might keep it the fourteenth of the second, doubted not it might be extended to the congregation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. For they could not keep it at that time, because the priests had not sanctified themselves sufficiently, neither had the people gathered themselves together to Jerusalem. Henry notes the time had elapsed for this year, the congregation was thin, people had not notice, priests were not prepared, and many were for deferring it, but Hezekiah considered affections would cool by twelve-month and it would be too long to want the ordinance's benefit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And the thing pleased the king and all the congregation. Henry notes the resolution to keep the passover in the second month pleased king and congregation, for let the circumstance give way to the substance, and let not the thing itself be lost upon a nicety about time, and it is good striking while the iron is hot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. So they established a decree to make proclamation throughout all Israel, from Beersheba even to Dan, that they should come to keep the passover unto the Lord God of Israel at Jerusalem, for they had not done it of a long time in such sort as it was written. Henry notes expresses were sent post throughout all Israel's tribes with memorials earnestly pressing the people to take this opportunity of returning to the God from whom they had revolted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. So the posts went with the letters from the king and his princes throughout all Israel and Judah, and according to the king's commandment, saying, Ye children of Israel, turn again unto the Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, and he will return to the remnant of you, that are escaped out of the hand of the kings of Assyria. Henry notes Hezekiah's circular letters show great concern for God's honour and the neighbouring kingdom's welfare, rendering good for evil, and press returning to God, beginning and concluding that if you return to God in duty, he will return to you in mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And be not ye like your fathers, and like your brethren, which trespassed against the Lord God of their fathers, who therefore gave them up to desolation, as ye see. Henry notes the letters argue from fathers: late fathers who forsook God and trespassed were given up to desolation, their apostasy and idolatry their ruin, so let their harms be your warnings, and be not like them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Now be ye not stiffnecked, as your fathers were, but yield yourselves unto the Lord, and enter into his sanctuary, which he hath sanctified for ever, and serve the Lord your God, that the fierceness of his wrath may turn away from you. Henry notes he presses them to yield themselves to the Lord, give the hand in covenant, consent to take him for God, be not stiff-necked as fathers were, and enter his sanctuary now opened, for this is the only way of turning away God's fierce anger, which will consume them if they continue stiff-necked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. For if ye turn again unto the Lord, your brethren and your children shall find compassion before them that lead them captive, so that they shall come again into this land, for the Lord your God is gracious and merciful, and will not turn away his face from you, if ye return unto him. Henry notes the letters promise that returning to God, you will find him gracious and merciful, not turning away his face if you seek him, and may hope he will turn again the captivity of brethren carried away and bring them back to their land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. So the posts passed from city to city through the country of Ephraim and Manasseh even unto Zebulun, but they laughed them to scorn, and mocked them. Henry notes the generality slighted the call: messengers went city to city with pressing entreaties, but the people abused them, laughed them to scorn and mocked them, knowing not the God of Abraham, having other gods, and neither dreading his wrath nor desiring his mercy, and their contempt hastened and aggravated their ruin, leaving them inexcusable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Nevertheless divers of Asher and Manasseh and of Zebulun humbled themselves, and came to Jerusalem. Henry notes yet some few accepted: in the worst times God has had a remnant, many of Asher, Manasseh and Zebulun humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem, sorry for sins and submitted to God, for pride keeps men from yielding to the Lord, and when it is brought down the work is done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Also in Judah the hand of God was to give them one heart to do the commandment of the king and of the princes, by the word of the Lord. Henry notes Judah universally obeyed with one heart, all of a mind, and God's hand gave them that one heart, for it is in the day of power that Christ's subjects are made willing, and God works both to will and to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And there assembled at Jerusalem much people to keep the feast of unleavened bread in the second month, a very great congregation. Henry notes at the appointed time a very great congregation came together, and by this the reformation set on foot in the foregoing chapter was greatly advanced and established. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And they arose and took away the altars that were in Jerusalem, and all the altars for incense took they away, and cast them into the brook Kidron. Henry notes good preparation was made: they took away all idolatrous altars, not only in the temple but in Jerusalem, casting them into Kidron, for the best preparation for the gospel passover is to cast away our iniquities, our spiritual idolatries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Then they killed the passover on the fourteenth day of the second month, and the priests and the Levites were ashamed, and sanctified themselves, and brought in the burnt offerings into the house of the Lord. Henry notes the people were so forward and zealous that priests and Levites blushed to see themselves outdone by the commonalty, more ready to bring sacrifices than they to offer them, and this put them upon sanctifying themselves, for the zeal of others should make us ashamed of coldness and quicken us to duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And they stood in their place after their manner, according to the law of Moses the man of God: the priests sprinkled the blood, which they received of the hand of the Levites. Henry notes they did according to their place's duty, sprinkling blood upon the altar, a type of Christ our passover sacrificed for us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. For there were many in the congregation that were not sanctified, therefore the Levites had the charge of the killing of the passovers for every one that was not clean, to sanctify them unto the Lord. Henry notes an irregularity: Levites killed the passover, which should have been done by priests only, and assisted more than the law ordinarily allowed, particularly for those under ceremonial pollution, for ordinarily every man killed his lamb, but now for the unclean the Levites killed it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. For a multitude of the people, even many of Ephraim, and Manasseh, Issachar, and Zebulun, had not cleansed themselves, yet did they eat the passover otherwise than it was written. But Hezekiah prayed for them, saying, The good Lord pardon every one. Henry notes many were permitted to eat who were not purified according to the law's strictness, for this was the second month and there was no warrant to put them off to a third, and they were loth to forbid communicating lest they discourage new converts, and Hezekiah thought himself concerned to intercede for those eating otherwise than written. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. That prepareth his heart to seek God, the Lord God of his fathers, though he be not cleansed according to the purification of the sanctuary. Henry notes his short prayer was to the purpose: the good Lord pardon every one who has fixed, engaged or prepared his heart to seek God, though ceremonial preparation be wanting, for the great thing required is that we prepare hearts to seek him, sincere and upright, making heart-work of it, for God desires truth in the inward part. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And the Lord hearkened to Hezekiah, and healed the people. Henry notes a successful prayer: the Lord hearkened to Hezekiah, well pleased with his pious concern, and healed the people, not only not laying sin to their charge but graciously accepting services notwithstanding, for healing denotes forgiveness, comfort and peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And Israel's children present at Jerusalem kept the feast of unleavened bread seven days with great gladness, and the Levites and the priests praised the Lord day by day, singing with loud instruments unto the Lord. Henry notes after the passover followed unleavened bread seven days, with abundance of sacrifices, good prayers, good preaching and psalms every day, for Levites and priests praised the Lord day by day with songs and instruments, expressing and exciting joy in God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And Hezekiah spake comfortably unto all the Levites that taught the good knowledge of the Lord, and they did eat throughout the feast seven days, offering peace offerings, and making confession to the Lord God of their fathers. Henry notes many good prayers were put up with peace-offerings, making confession to the God of their fathers, religious, penitent, thankful and supplicatory, and there was good preaching, for Levites taught the people the good knowledge of the Lord, and Hezekiah spoke comfortably to them, commending diligence and assuring protection, encouraging faithful preachers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And the whole assembly took counsel to keep other seven days, and they kept other seven days with gladness. Henry notes having kept seven days religiously, they had so much comfort that they kept other seven days, not instituting new modes but repeating the old, for they had been long without the ordinance, were in a devout serious frame, and knew not when they might have such opportunity again, and the zeal of God's house made them forget secular affairs even in harvest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. For Hezekiah king of Judah did give to the congregation a thousand bullocks and seven thousand sheep, and the princes gave to the congregation a thousand bullocks and ten thousand sheep, and a great number of priests sanctified themselves. Henry notes to keep up the service Hezekiah gave out of his own stock 1,000 bullocks and 7,000 sheep, and the princes, excited by his example, gave the same bullocks and a greater number of sheep, all for peace-offerings, by which God was honoured, the festival's joy kept up, and strangers encouraged to come again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And all the congregation of Judah, with the priests and the Levites, and all the congregation that came out of Israel, and the strangers that came out of the land of Israel, and that dwelt in Judah, rejoiced. Henry notes all rejoiced, particularly the strangers, for holy duties should be performed with holy gladness, forwardly and with pleasure in communion with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. So there was great joy in Jerusalem, for since the time of Solomon son of David king of Israel there was not the like in Jerusalem. Henry notes there was great joy in Jerusalem, never the like since Solomon's temple dedication, for holy gladness is matter of unspeakable joy and comfort, with earnests of everlasting joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Then the priests the Levites arose and blessed the people, and their voice was heard, and their prayer came up to his holy dwelling place, even unto heaven. Henry notes the congregation was dismissed with a solemn blessing, which priests pronounced as part of their office, both the people's mouth to God by prayer and God's mouth to the people by promise, and God said Amen to it, for their voice was heard in heaven and came up to God's holy habitation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
