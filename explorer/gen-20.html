The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Abraham journeyed from thence toward the south country, and dwelled between Kadesh and Shur, and sojourned in Gerar.

2 And Abraham said of Sarah his wife, She is my sister: and Abimelech king of Gerar sent, and took Sarah.

3 But God came to Abimelech in a dream by night, and said to him, Behold, thou art but a dead man, for the woman which thou hast taken; for she is a man’s wife.

4 But Abimelech had not come near her: and he said, LORD, wilt thou slay also a righteous nation?

5 Said he not unto me, She is my sister? and she, even she herself said, He is my brother: in the integrity of my heart and innocency of my hands have I done this.

6 And God said unto him in a dream, Yea, I know that thou didst this in the integrity of thy heart; for I also withheld thee from sinning against me: therefore suffered I thee not to touch her.

7 Now therefore restore the man his wife; for he is a prophet, and he shall pray for thee, and thou shalt live: and if thou restore her not, know thou that thou shalt surely die, thou, and all that are thine.

8 Therefore Abimelech rose early in the morning, and called all his servants, and told all these things in their ears: and the men were sore afraid.

9 Then Abimelech called Abraham, and said unto him, What hast thou done unto us? and what have I offended thee, that thou hast brought on me and on my kingdom a great sin? thou hast done deeds unto me that ought not to be done.

10 And Abimelech said unto Abraham, What sawest thou, that thou hast done this thing?

11 And Abraham said, Because I thought, Surely the fear of God is not in this place; and they will slay me for my wife’s sake.

12 And yet indeed she is my sister; she is the daughter of my father, but not the daughter of my mother; and she became my wife.

13 And it came to pass, when God caused me to wander from my father’s house, that I said unto her, This is thy kindness which thou shalt shew unto me; at every place whither we shall come, say of me, He is my brother.

14 And Abimelech took sheep, and oxen, and menservants, and womenservants, and gave them unto Abraham, and restored him Sarah his wife.

15 And Abimelech said, Behold, my land is before thee: dwell where it pleaseth thee.

16 And unto Sarah he said, Behold, I have given thy brother a thousand pieces of silver: behold, he is to thee a covering of the eyes, unto all that are with thee, and with all other: thus she was reproved.

17 So Abraham prayed unto God: and God healed Abimelech, and his wife, and his maidservants; and they bare children.

18 For the LORD had fast closed up all the wombs of the house of Abimelech, because of Sarah Abraham’s wife.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Abraham sojourns in Gerar and again calls Sarah his sister, so Abimelech takes her; God warns Abimelech in a dream, and the king restores Sarah with gifts and reproves Abraham. Abraham prays, and God heals Abimelech's household, whose wombs had been closed because of Sarah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Abraham journeyed south into the Negeb, living between Kadesh and Shur and sojourning in Gerar, still a pilgrim moving from place to place in the promised region. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. In Gerar, Abraham again called Sarah his sister, repeating his old Egyptian device, and Abimelech, king of Gerar, sent and took her, so the old fault endangered the promise a second time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God came to Abimelech in a dream by night and told him he was a dead man for taking another man's wife, warning the king before any wrong was done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Abimelech had not come near Sarah, and he pleaded that he had acted in the integrity of his heart, asking whether God would slay even a righteous nation for a wrong done unknowingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He explained that Abraham himself had said she was his sister, and Sarah had said he was her brother, so his deed rested on their own words, and his conscience was clear in the matter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. God answered that he knew Abimelech's integrity, and had himself kept the king from sinning by not allowing him to touch Sarah, showing God's hand guarding the promise even through a pagan king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God told Abimelech to restore Sarah, because Abraham was a prophet who would pray for him, and warned that refusal would mean death for him and all his household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Abimelech rose early in the morning, told all his servants what God had said, and they were greatly afraid, since the dream left no doubt about the danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Abimelech called Abraham and demanded what he had done to bring such great sin on his kingdom, for Abraham's deception had nearly made an innocent king guilty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Abimelech pressed Abraham to explain what he had seen or feared that made him do this, giving Abraham a full hearing before judging him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Abraham answered that he thought there was no fear of God in that place and that men would kill him for his wife, an excuse Henry notes as both a slander on Gerar and a confession of distrust in God's protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Abraham added that Sarah truly was his sister, his father's daughter though not his mother's, and had become his wife, so his statement was a half-truth used to hide the whole truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He explained that when God caused him to wander from his father's house, he had asked Sarah to say he was her brother wherever they went, a standing agreement of fear made long before Gerar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Abimelech restored Sarah to Abraham with sheep, oxen, and servants, making generous amends and honouring the prophet he had nearly wronged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Abimelech told Abraham the whole land was before him to dwell wherever he pleased, turning a dangerous sojourn into a free and welcome stay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Abimelech gave Sarah a thousand pieces of silver as a covering of the eyes, a public vindication before her brother and all who were with her, so that her honour was cleared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Abraham prayed to God, and God healed Abimelech, his wife, and his maidservants, so that they could bear children again, showing Abraham's restored role as intercessor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The barrenness of Abimelech's household had come because God had closed every womb on Sarah's account, and its lifting proved that God had guarded Sarah throughout the affair. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
