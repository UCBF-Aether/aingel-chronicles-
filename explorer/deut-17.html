The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Deuteronomy 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Thou shalt not sacrifice unto the LORD thy God any bullock, or sheep, wherein is blemish, or any evilfavouredness: for that is an abomination unto the LORD thy God.

2 If there be found among you, within any of thy gates which the LORD thy God giveth thee, man or woman, that hath wrought wickedness in the sight of the LORD thy God, in transgressing his covenant,

3 And hath gone and served other gods, and worshipped them, either the sun, or moon, or any of the host of heaven, which I have not commanded;

4 And it be told thee, and thou hast heard of it, and enquired diligently, and, behold, it be true, and the thing certain, that such abomination is wrought in Israel:

5 Then shalt thou bring forth that man or that woman, which have committed that wicked thing, unto thy gates, even that man or that woman, and shalt stone them with stones, till they die.

6 At the mouth of two witnesses, or three witnesses, shall he that is worthy of death be put to death; but at the mouth of one witness he shall not be put to death.

7 The hands of the witnesses shall be first upon him to put him to death, and afterward the hands of all the people. So thou shalt put the evil away from among you.

8 If there arise a matter too hard for thee in judgment, between blood and blood, between plea and plea, and between stroke and stroke, being matters of controversy within thy gates: then shalt thou arise, and get thee up into the place which the LORD thy God shall choose;

9 And thou shalt come unto the priests the Levites, and unto the judge that shall be in those days, and enquire; and they shall shew thee the sentence of judgment:

10 And thou shalt do according to the sentence, which they of that place which the LORD shall choose shall shew thee; and thou shalt observe to do according to all that they inform thee:

11 According to the sentence of the law which they shall teach thee, and according to the judgment which they shall tell thee, thou shalt do: thou shalt not decline from the sentence which they shall shew thee, to the right hand, nor to the left.

12 And the man that will do presumptuously, and will not hearken unto the priest that standeth to minister there before the LORD thy God, or unto the judge, even that man shall die: and thou shalt put away the evil from Israel.

13 And all the people shall hear, and fear, and do no more presumptuously.

14 When thou art come unto the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee, and shalt possess it, and shalt dwell therein, and shalt say, I will set a king over me, like as all the nations that are about me;

15 Thou shalt in any wise set him king over thee, whom the LORD thy God shall choose: one from among thy brethren shalt thou set king over thee: thou mayest not set a stranger over thee, which is not thy brother.

16 But he shall not multiply horses to himself, nor cause the people to return to Egypt, to the end that he should multiply horses: forasmuch as the LORD hath said unto you, Ye shall henceforth return no more that way.

17 Neither shall he multiply wives to himself, that his heart turn not away: neither shall he greatly multiply to himself silver and gold.

18 And it shall be, when he sitteth upon the throne of his kingdom, that he shall write him a copy of this law in a book out of that which is before the priests the Levites:

19 And it shall be with him, and he shall read therein all the days of his life: that he may learn to fear the LORD his God, to keep all the words of this law and these statutes, to do them:

20 That his heart be not lifted up above his brethren, and that he turn not aside from the commandment, to the right hand, or to the left: to the end that he may prolong his days in his kingdom, he, and his children, in the midst of Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Sacrifices must be without blemish, idolatry is tried on the evidence of two or three witnesses and never on one alone, and hard cases go up to the priests and judge at the central place. The chapter then lays down the law for a future king: he must not multiply horses, wives or wealth, and must write out his own copy of the law and read it all his life, so that his heart is not lifted above his brothers. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. You shall not sacrifice to the Lord any bullock or sheep with blemish or any evilfavouredness, for that is an abomination; no creature with any blemish was to be offered, reminding Israel of the perfect, pure, spotless sacrifice of Christ, and that God must be served with our best. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Henry treats a case found among you as no private sin: worship of other gods breaks the covenant itself, which is why the law that follows deals with it as a public crime punishable by death rather than a matter of conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The forbidden worship is named by its objects, sun, moon and the host of heaven, and Henry's section reads all such worship as setting up idols in the heart: the crime is not the rite's strangeness but its displacement of God, punished in Israel as a warning to later idolaters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Diligent inquiry must precede any sentence because, in Henry's reading, the penalty here is death by stoning: a charge this grave must be proved true and certain before the community acts, not taken on report alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Then you shall bring forth that man or woman who has committed that wicked thing, to your gates, and stone them with stones till they die; so great a punishment, and so remarkable a death, was inflicted on the Jewish idolater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. For so grave a sentence as death by stoning, Henry insists the safeguard is the number of witnesses: a capital charge for idolatry may rest on two or three witnesses, never on one alone, lest a single accuser take a life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The witnesses' hands go first: Henry's section makes the idolater's death a communal sentence, and the order puts the accusers' hands on the execution before the people's, so the charge is owned by those who proved it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Hard cases go up, not around: blood, plea and stroke too hard for local judgment are carried to the place God chooses, where priests and judge, not the parties, give the sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And you shall come to the priests the Levites, and to the judge that shall be in those days, and enquire, and they shall show you the sentence of judgment; courts were to be set up in every city, but hard cases went to this central court of wise, prudent, experienced men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The sentence binds in full: what the court of the chosen place informs must be observed in all, with no declining to either side, for Henry's section grounds that obedience in the court's wisdom, not in the litigant's agreement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. According to the sentence of the law which they shall teach you, and according to the judgment which they shall tell you, you shall do; you shall not decline from the sentence shown you, to the right hand nor to the left. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And the man who will do presumptuously, and will not hearken to the priest standing to minister there before the Lord, or to the judge, even that man shall die, and you shall put away the evil from Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The sentence on the presumptuous man is heard by all Israel: Henry's court section makes the judgment public precisely so that hearing breeds fear and presumption stops, and the verdict educates the nation, not only the offender. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The desire for a king is framed as imitation, like all the nations around them, and Henry's section answers it by insisting that God's choice must direct, determine and overrule the people's own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. You shall in any wise set him king over you, whom the Lord shall choose; one from among your brethren shall you set king over you, and you may not set a stranger over you who is not your brother; God himself was Israel's King in a particular manner, so if they had another, he must choose the person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Horses mean Egypt: multiplying them would send the people back down the road God closed, trading dependence on God for cavalry strength and a return to the house of bondage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Neither shall he multiply wives to himself, that his heart turn not away, neither shall he greatly multiply to himself silver and gold; riches, honours, and pleasures are three great hindrances of godliness, especially to those in high stations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The king writes his own copy from the priests' book: the law is not merely received but copied by the royal hand, so that the throne's first work is transcription, not legislation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Reading is lifelong and daily: the copy stays with the king all his life so that fear of God and keeping of the law are learned by constant use, for Henry's point is that having Bibles is nothing without using them daily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. That his heart be not lifted up above his brethren, and that he turn not aside from the commandment, to the right hand or to the left, to the end that he may prolong his days in his kingdom, he, and his children, in the midst of Israel; writing and reading were as nothing if he did not practise what he wrote and read. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
