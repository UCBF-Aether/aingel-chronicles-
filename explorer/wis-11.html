The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Wisdom of Solomon 11 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 She prospered their works in the hand of the holy prophet.

2 They went through the wilderness that was not inhabited, and pitched tents in places where there lay no way.

3 They stood against their enemies, and were avenged of their adversaries.

4 When they were thirsty, they called upon thee, and water was given them out of the flinty rock, and their thirst was quenched out of the hard stone.

5 For by what things their enemies were punished, by the same they in their need were benefited.

6 For instead of of a perpetual running river troubled with foul blood,

7 For a manifest reproof of that commandment, whereby the infants were slain, thou gavest unto them abundance of water by a means which they hoped not for:

8 Declaring by that thirst then how thou hadst punished their adversaries.

9 For when they were tried albeit but in mercy chastised, they knew how the ungodly were judged in wrath and tormented, thirsting in another manner than the just.

10 For these thou didst admonish and try, as a father: but the other, as a severe king, thou didst condemn and punish.

11 Whether they were absent or present, they were vexed alike.

12 For a double grief came upon them, and a groaning for the remembrance of things past.

13 For when they heard by their own punishments the other to be benefited, they had some feeling of the Lord.

14 For whom they respected with scorn, when he was long before thrown out at the casting forth of the infants, him in the end, when they saw what came to pass, they admired.

15 But for the foolish devices of their wickedness, wherewith being deceived they worshipped serpents void of reason, and vile beasts, thou didst send a multitude of unreasonable beasts upon them for vengeance;

16 That they might know, that wherewithal a man sinneth, by the same also shall he be punished.

17 For thy Almighty hand, that made the world of matter without form, wanted not means to send among them a multitude of bears or fierce lions,

18 Or unknown wild beasts, full of rage, newly created, breathing out either a fiery vapour, or filthy scents of scattered smoke, or shooting horrible sparkles out of their eyes:

19 Whereof not only the harm might dispatch them at once, but also the terrible sight utterly destroy them.

20 Yea, and without these might they have fallen down with one blast, being persecuted of vengeance, and scattered abroad through the breath of thy power: but thou hast ordered all things in measure and number and weight.

21 For thou canst shew thy great strength at all times when thou wilt; and who may withstand the power of thine arm?

22 For the whole world before thee is as a little grain of the balance, yea, as a drop of the morning dew that falleth down upon the earth.

23 But thou hast mercy upon all; for thou canst do all things, and winkest at the sins of men, because they should amend.

24 For thou lovest all the things that are, and abhorrest nothing which thou hast made: for never wouldest thou have made any thing, if thou hadst hated it.

25 And how could any thing have endured, if it had not been thy will? or been preserved, if not called by thee?

26 But thou sparest all: for they are thine, O Lord, thou lover of souls.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the wilderness the same pattern holds: by the very things their enemies were punished, Israel in need was benefited, water from the flinty rock against rivers of foul blood. God chastens Israel as a father and the ungodly as a severe king, yet spares all, because the whole world before him is as a grain of the balance and a drop of dew, and he is the lover of souls. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. She prospered their works in the hand of the holy prophet. Haydock notes wisdom prospered Israel's works through Moses, the holy prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. They went through a wilderness not inhabited, and pitched tents where there lay no way. Haydock observes the wilderness had no road, yet they were led through it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. They stood against their enemies and were avenged of their adversaries. Haydock's point is that wisdom gave them standing against enemies, not mere flight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. When they were thirsty, they called upon you, and water was given from the flinty rock, and thirst quenched out of the hard stone. Haydock notes the rock's water answers thirst with a miracle of provision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. For by the things by which their enemies were punished, by the same they were benefited in their need. Haydock's key to chapters 11-19 is this correspondence: the same instrument punishes Egypt and benefits Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. Instead of a perpetual running river troubled with foul blood, their enemies were punished, yet Israel was given water in abundance. Haydock contrasts the Nile turned to blood with Israel's abundant water. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. For a manifest reproof of the command to kill infants, you gave them abundant water unlooked for. Haydock reads the water as reproof: Egypt, which killed infants, is judged by water, while Israel is refreshed by it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. Declaring by thirst at that time how you had punished their adversaries. Haydock notes thirst itself declared the punishment: Egypt's need exposed its judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. When they were tried, though chastened in mercy, they understood how the ungodly were judged in wrath and tormented. Haydock observes Israel's trial was merciful chastening, teaching them the difference from wrathful judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. You tried the one as a father admonishing, and the other as a severe king condemning. Haydock's contrast is father against king: admonition against condemnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. Whether absent or present, they were vexed alike. Haydock notes Egypt's torment followed them whether Israel was near or far. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. For a double grief came upon them, and groaning for remembrance of things past. Haydock's point is double grief: present pain plus remembrance of what they had done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. When they heard by their own punishments that others were benefited, they perceived the Lord. Haydock observes that seeing Israel benefited by their punishments taught Egypt to perceive the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. For whom they had before in derision, and rejected with scorn, him in the end they admired, after suffering thirst unlike the righteous'. Haydock notes the reversal: the derided Moses is admired in the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. For the foolish devices of their wickedness, by which they were deceived to worship brute beasts and creeping things, you sent a multitude of brute beasts to punish them. Haydock's correspondence is exact: beast-worshippers are punished by beasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. That they might know that by the things by which a man sins, by the same also he shall be punished. Haydock states the book's rule of judgment: the instrument of sin becomes the instrument of punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. Your Almighty hand, which made the world from formless matter, did not lack means to send a multitude of bears or fierce lions, or unknown wild beasts full of rage, or breathing fire, or sending smoke, or shooting terrible sparks from their eyes. Haydock stresses God's hand lacks no means, and lists the beasts he could have sent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. Even without these they might have fallen with one blast, being persecuted by your judgment and scattered by your spirit's breath. Haydock notes God needed no army; one blast of judgment would suffice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. But you ordered all things in measure, number, and weight. Haydock's famous line is that even judgment is ordered, measured, numbered, and weighed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. You can always show great strength when you will; who can withstand your arm's power? Haydock observes that power is always at hand, yet exercised in measure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. The whole world before you is as a little grain of the balance, as a drop of morning dew falling on earth. Haydock's image is littleness: the world weighs as a grain and falls as a dew drop. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. You have mercy on all, for you can do all things, and wink at men's sins, that they should amend. Haydock notes mercy is grounded in power: because God can do all, he can afford to wait for amendment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 23. You love all things that are, and abhor nothing you made, for you would never have made anything if you had hated it. Haydock stresses that existence itself is a sign of love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 24. How could anything have endured if it had not been your will, or been preserved if not called by you? Haydock's argument is that continuance depends on God's will alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 25. You spare all, for they are yours, O Lord, lover of souls. Haydock closes with the name: Lord, lover of souls, who spares what is his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 26. You spare all, for they are yours, O Lord, lover of souls. Haydock's close to the chapter is the name itself: the Lord who spares is the lover of souls, and what is his he does not destroy lightly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
