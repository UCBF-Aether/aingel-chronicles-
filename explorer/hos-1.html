The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hosea 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word of the LORD that came unto Hosea, the son of Beeri, in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah, and in the days of Jeroboam the son of Joash, king of Israel.

2 The beginning of the word of the LORD by Hosea. And the LORD said to Hosea, Go, take unto thee a wife of whoredoms and children of whoredoms: for the land hath committed great whoredom, departing from the LORD.

3 So he went and took Gomer the daughter of Diblaim; which conceived, and bare him a son.

4 And the LORD said unto him, Call his name Jezreel; for yet a little while, and I will avenge the blood of Jezreel upon the house of Jehu, and will cause to cease the kingdom of the house of Israel.

5 And it shall come to pass at that day, that I will break the bow of Israel, in the valley of Jezreel.

6 And she conceived again, and bare a daughter. And God said unto him, Call her name Loruhamah: for I will no more have mercy upon the house of Israel; but I will utterly take them away.

7 But I will have mercy upon the house of Judah, and will save them by the LORD their God, and will not save them by bow, nor by sword, nor by battle, by horses, nor by horsemen.

8 Now when she had weaned Loruhamah, she conceived, and bare a son.

9 Then said God, Call his name Loammi: for ye are not my people, and I will not be your God.

10 Yet the number of the children of Israel shall be as the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured nor numbered; and it shall come to pass, that in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living God.

11 Then shall the children of Judah and the children of Israel be gathered together, and appoint themselves one head, and they shall come up out of the land: for great shall be the day of Jezreel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God tells Hosea to marry Gomer, and their three children are given names that spell out judgment on Israel: Jezreel, Lo-ruhamah (no more mercy), and Lo-ammi (not my people). Yet the chapter ends by reversing the sentence: the people once called 'not my people' will be called sons of the living God, and Judah and Israel will be gathered together under one head. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The word of the Lord came to Hosea the son of Beeri, in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, kings of Judah, and in the days of Jeroboam the son of Joash, king of Israel, because Hosea's ministry spans the last flourishing and the decline of both kingdoms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The beginning of the word of the Lord by Hosea commands him to take a wife of whoredoms and children of whoredoms, because the land has committed great whoredom, departing from the Lord, and the prophet's own house is made a living parable of Israel's unfaithfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. So he went and took Gomer the daughter of Diblaim, who conceived and bare him a son, because the prophet obeys the hard sign, and the marriage itself becomes the sermon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. God said, call his name Jezreel, for yet a little while and I will avenge the blood of Jezreel upon the house of Jehu, and will cause the kingdom of the house of Israel to cease, because Jehu's massacre at Jezreel, though used as judgment, was itself bloodguilt that would be repaid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. At that day I will break the bow of Israel in the valley of Jezreel, because the valley that gave Israel its military strength will become the place where that strength is broken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. She conceived again and bare a daughter, and God said, call her name Lo-ruhamah, for I will no more have mercy upon the house of Israel, but I will utterly take them away, because the name declares that the season of sparing mercy is ended. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But I will have mercy upon the house of Judah, and will save them by the Lord their God, and will not save them by bow, nor by sword, nor by battle, by horses, nor by horsemen, because Judah's deliverance will be God's own work, not the work of armies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Now when she had weaned Lo-ruhamah, she conceived and bare a son, because the signs follow in order, each child adding a further sentence upon the nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Then said God, call his name Lo-ammi, for ye are not my people, and I will not be your God, because the covenant bond is declared broken from Israel's side, and the name pronounces the divorce. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Yet the number of the children of Israel shall be as the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured nor numbered, and it shall come to pass that in the place where it was said unto them, ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, ye are the sons of the living God, because judgment does not cancel the promise, and restoration reverses the very sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Then shall the children of Judah and the children of Israel be gathered together, and appoint themselves one head, and they shall come up out of the land, for great shall be the day of Jezreel, because the divided kingdoms are reunited under one head, and Jezreel, the name of scattering, becomes the day of sowing again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Jezreel. the valley and town in Israel where Jehu's bloodshed took place; in Hosea the name is turned both ways, scattering in judgment and sowing again in restoration. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Lo-ruhamah. the daughter's name in Hosea 1, explained in the chapter itself: God will no more have mercy upon the house of Israel. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Lo-ammi. the son's name in Hosea 1, explained in the chapter itself: ye are not my people, and I will not be your God. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
