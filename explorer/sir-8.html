Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Sirach 8 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Strive not with a mighty man' lest thou fall into his hands.

2 Be not at variance with a rich man, lest he overweigh thee: for gold hath destroyed many, and perverted the hearts of kings.

3 Strive not with a man that is full of tongue, and heap not wood upon his fire.

4 Jest not with a rude man, lest thy ancestors be disgraced.

5 Reproach not a man that turneth from sin, but remember that we are all worthy of punishment.

6 Dishonour not a man in his old age: for even some of us wax old.

7 Rejoice not over thy greatest enemy being dead, but remember that we die all.

8 Despise not the discourse of the wise, but acquaint thyself with their proverbs: for of them thou shalt learn instruction, and how to serve great men with ease.

9 Miss not the discourse of the elders: for they also learned of their fathers, and of them thou shalt learn understanding, and to give answer as need requireth.

10 Kindle not the coals of a sinner, lest thou be burnt with the flame of his fire.

11 Rise not up [in anger] at the presence of an injurious person, lest he lie in wait to entrap thee in thy words

12 Lend not unto him that is mightier than thyself; for if thou lendest him, count it but lost.

13 Be not surety above thy power: for if thou be surety, take care to pay it.

14 Go not to law with a judge; for they will judge for him according to his honour.

15 Travel not by the way with a bold fellow, lest he become grievous unto thee: for he will do according to his own will, and thou shalt perish with him through his folly.

16 Strive not with an angry man, and go not with him into a solitary place: for blood is as nothing in his sight, and where there is no help, he will overthrow thee.

17 Consult not with a fool; for he cannot keep counsel.

18 Do no secret thing before a stranger; for thou knowest not what he will bring forth.

19 Open not thine heart to every man, lest he requite thee with a shrewd turn.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter is a prudence manual for dealing with power: do not strive with a mighty or rich man, do not kindle a sinner's coals, and do not open your heart to every man, lest a cunning return follow. Guarded speech and careful company are its steady counsels. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Do not strive with a mighty man, lest you fall into his hands. Contention with power ends in being at its mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Do not be at variance with a rich man, lest he overweigh you, for gold has destroyed many and perverted the hearts of kings. Wealth can outweigh right, and it has corrupted even kings, so quarrelling with the rich is perilous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. Do not strive with a man full of tongue, and do not heap wood upon his fire. Arguing with a talkative man only feeds his fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. Do not jest with a rude man, lest your ancestors be disgraced. Jesting with the rude drags disgrace beyond yourself, onto your family name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Do not reproach a man who turns from sin, but remember that we are all worthy of punishment. A repentant man should not be reproached, since all stand deserving of punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. Do not dishonour a man in his old age, for even some of us wax old. Old age deserves honour, if only because we ourselves grow old. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. Do not rejoice over your greatest enemy being dead, but remember that we all die. Death is common to all, so even an enemy's death is no occasion for rejoicing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Do not despise the discourse of the wise, but acquaint yourself with their proverbs, for from them you will learn instruction and how to serve great men with ease. The wise men's sayings teach both instruction and the ease of serving the great. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. Do not miss the discourse of the elders, for they also learned from their fathers, and from them you will learn understanding and how to give answer as need requires. Elders pass on what they received, and their discourse teaches understanding and timely answers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Do not kindle the coals of a sinner, lest you be burnt with the flame of his fire. Stirring a sinner's passions burns the stirrer himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. Do not rise up in anger at the presence of an injurious person, lest he lie in wait to entrap you in your words. Angry rising against an injurious man gives him the chance to trap you by your own words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. Do not lend to him who is mightier than yourself, for if you lend to him, count it but lost. A loan to the mightier cannot be enforced, so it should be counted as lost from the start. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. Do not be surety above your power, for if you are surety, take care to pay it. Standing surety beyond your means is folly, and once bound, payment must be seen to. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. Do not go to law with a judge, for they will judge for him according to his honour. Litigation against a judge is hopeless, since his peers judge in his favour out of regard for his office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. Do not travel by the way with a bold fellow, lest he become grievous to you, for he will do according to his own will, and you will perish with him through his folly. A bold companion acts on his own will, and his folly can drag a fellow-traveller to ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. Do not strive with an angry man, and do not go with him into a solitary place, for blood is as nothing in his sight, and where there is no help he will overthrow you. An angry man sets no value on blood, and a lonely place leaves no help against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. Do not consult with a fool, for he cannot keep counsel. A fool is useless as a counsellor because he cannot keep what is entrusted to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. Do no secret thing before a stranger, for you do not know what he will bring forth. A stranger's use of a secret cannot be predicted, so secrets should not be done before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. Do not open your heart to every man, lest he requite you with a shrewd turn. An open heart to everyone invites a cunning return for trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
