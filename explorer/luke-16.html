The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he said also unto his disciples, There was a certain rich man, which had a steward; and the same was accused unto him that he had wasted his goods.

2 And he called him, and said unto him, How is it that I hear this of thee? give an account of thy stewardship; for thou mayest be no longer steward.

3 Then the steward said within himself, What shall I do? for my lord taketh away from me the stewardship: I cannot dig; to beg I am ashamed.

4 I am resolved what to do, that, when I am put out of the stewardship, they may receive me into their houses.

5 So he called every one of his lord’s debtors unto him, and said unto the first, How much owest thou unto my lord?

6 And he said, An hundred measures of oil. And he said unto him, Take thy bill, and sit down quickly, and write fifty.

7 Then said he to another, And how much owest thou? And he said, An hundred measures of wheat. And he said unto him, Take thy bill, and write fourscore.

8 And the lord commended the unjust steward, because he had done wisely: for the children of this world are in their generation wiser than the children of light.

9 And I say unto you, Make to yourselves friends of the mammon of unrighteousness; that, when ye fail, they may receive you into everlasting habitations.

10 He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much.

11 If therefore ye have not been faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches?

12 And if ye have not been faithful in that which is another man’s, who shall give you that which is your own?

13 No servant can serve two masters: for either he will hate the one, and love the other; or else he will hold to the one, and despise the other. Ye cannot serve God and mammon.

14 And the Pharisees also, who were covetous, heard all these things: and they derided him.

15 And he said unto them, Ye are they which justify yourselves before men; but God knoweth your hearts: for that which is highly esteemed among men is abomination in the sight of God.

16 The law and the prophets were until John: since that time the kingdom of God is preached, and every man presseth into it.

17 And it is easier for heaven and earth to pass, than one tittle of the law to fail.

18 Whosoever putteth away his wife, and marrieth another, committeth adultery: and whosoever marrieth her that is put away from her husband committeth adultery.

19 There was a certain rich man, which was clothed in purple and fine linen, and fared sumptuously every day:

20 And there was a certain beggar named Lazarus, which was laid at his gate, full of sores,

21 And desiring to be fed with the crumbs which fell from the rich man’s table: moreover the dogs came and licked his sores.

22 And it came to pass, that the beggar died, and was carried by the angels into Abraham’s bosom: the rich man also died, and was buried;

23 And in hell he lift up his eyes, being in torments, and seeth Abraham afar off, and Lazarus in his bosom.

24 And he cried and said, Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water, and cool my tongue; for I am tormented in this flame.

25 But Abraham said, Son, remember that thou in thy lifetime receivedst thy good things, and likewise Lazarus evil things: but now he is comforted, and thou art tormented.

26 And beside all this, between us and you there is a great gulf fixed: so that they which would pass from hence to you cannot; neither can they pass to us, that would come from thence.

27 Then he said, I pray thee therefore, father, that thou wouldest send him to my father’s house:

28 For I have five brethren; that he may testify unto them, lest they also come into this place of torment.

29 Abraham saith unto him, They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them.

30 And he said, Nay, father Abraham: but if one went unto them from the dead, they will repent.

31 And he said unto him, If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The parable of the unjust steward, who reduces his master’s debtors’ bills to secure a welcome when he is dismissed, teaches that children of this world are wiser in their generation than the children of light, and that faithfulness in the least — in unrighteous mammon — is the test for true riches (vv. 1–13). Pharisees who are covetous deride him, and he answers on the law, on divorce, and then with the rich man and Lazarus: the beggar carried to Abraham’s bosom, the rich man in torment asking for a drop of water, and Abraham’s answer that if they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded by one risen from the dead (vv. 14–31). What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He said also unto his disciples, There was a certain rich man, which had a steward, accused that he had wasted his goods. Henry notes whatever we have, the property is God's; we have only its use, according to our great Lord's direction and for his honour, and we are all liable to the same charge of not improving what God trusted us with. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He called him, and said, How is it that I hear this of thee? Give an account of thy stewardship, for thou mayest be no longer steward. The steward cannot deny it; he must make up accounts and be gone, Henry notes, teaching that death will come and deprive us of present opportunities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Then the steward said within himself, What shall I do? For my lord takes away my stewardship: I cannot dig; to beg I am ashamed. Dismissal forces a plan, and both honest alternatives are closed to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. I am resolved what to do, that, when I am put out of the stewardship, they may receive me into their houses. The plan is to buy future welcome with present discounts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. So he called every one of his lord's debtors, and said to the first, How much owest thou unto my lord? The steward works through the debtors one by one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He said, A hundred measures of oil. And he said, Take thy bill, sit down quickly, and write fifty. Half the oil debt is struck off, quickly, before the stewardship ends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Then said he to another, And how much owest thou? He said, A hundred measures of wheat. And he said, Take thy bill, and write fourscore. A fifth of the wheat debt is struck off, the same policy at a different rate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And the lord commended the unjust steward, because he had done wisely, for the children of this world are in their generation wiser than the children of light. Henry stresses the lord commended not the fraud, but the policy; the steward is not an example in cheating, but worldly men in activity and perseverance are often wiser than believers, and children of light should as earnestly pursue their better object. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And I say unto you, Make to yourselves friends of the mammon of unrighteousness, that, when ye fail, they may receive you into everlasting habitations. Wealth, used for others now, becomes welcome hereafter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much, and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much. Faithfulness is a character, not a scale; it shows in the least. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. If therefore ye have not been faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches? Henry notes true riches signify spiritual blessings, and if a man spends on himself or hoards what God trusted him with, what evidence has he of being an heir of God through Christ? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And if ye have not been faithful in that which is another man's, who shall give you that which is your own? Stewardship of another's is the test for receiving one's own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. No servant can serve two masters, for either he will hate the one and love the other, or hold to the one and despise the other. Ye cannot serve God and mammon. Henry adds the solemn warning: ye cannot serve God and the world, so divided are the two interests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And the Pharisees also, who were covetous, heard all these things, and they derided him. Covetous hearers answer the mammon teaching with derision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Ye are they which justify yourselves before men, but God knows your hearts, for that which is highly esteemed among men is abomination in the sight of God. Henry notes many covetous sticklers for forms of godliness are the bitterest enemies to its power, and try to set others against the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The law and the prophets were until John; since that time the kingdom of God is preached, and every man presses into it. John marks the turn from law's era to the kingdom's preaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And it is easier for heaven and earth to pass, than one tittle of the law to fail. The kingdom's newness does not abolish the law's smallest letter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Whosoever puts away his wife, and marries another, commits adultery, and whosoever marries her put away from her husband commits adultery. Henry notes the Lord showed, in a case respecting divorce, that what the Pharisees contended for as law was a wresting of its meaning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. There was a certain rich man, clothed in purple and fine linen, and fared sumptuously every day. Henry notes we are not told he got his estate by fraud or oppression, yet a man may have wealth, pomp, and pleasure, yet perish under God's wrath; his sin was providing for himself only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And there was a certain beggar named Lazarus, laid at his gate, full of sores. The named beggar lies at the unnamed rich man's gate, a contrast Henry stresses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Desiring to be fed with crumbs from the rich man's table; moreover dogs came and licked his sores. Henry notes we are not told the rich man harmed him, but we do not find he had any care for him; dogs show more attention than the rich man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. It came to pass, the beggar died, and was carried by angels into Abraham's bosom; the rich man also died, and was buried. Henry notes the godly poor man, in depth of adversity here, is carried by angels, while the rich man is merely buried. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And in hell he lifted up his eyes, being in torments, and sees Abraham afar off, and Lazarus in his bosom. The reversal is now visible across an unbridgeable distance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. He cried, Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water, and cool my tongue, for I am tormented in this flame. The man who would not give crumbs now begs a fingertip of water. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. But Abraham said, Son, remember that thou in thy lifetime receivedst thy good things, and likewise Lazarus evil things; but now he is comforted, and thou art tormented. Henry notes wicked people have good things only in this life, and at death are for ever separated from all good, while godly people have evil things only in this life, and at death are for ever put from them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And beside all this, between us and you there is a great gulf fixed, so that they which would pass from hence to you cannot, neither can they pass to us, that would come from thence. Henry notes in this world there is no gulf between nature and grace, we may pass from sin to God, but if we die in our sins, there is no coming out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Then he said, I pray thee therefore, father, that thou wouldest send him to my father's house. The tormented man turns advocate for his family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. For I have five brethren, that he may testify unto them, lest they also come into this place of torment. Henry notes the rich man would have his brethren stopped in their course, since their coming would make his misery worse, having helped show them the way thither. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Abraham said, They have Moses and the prophets; let them hear them. Scripture already given is the sufficient testimony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And he said, Nay, father Abraham, but if one went unto them from the dead, they will repent. A resurrection witness is pleaded as stronger than Scripture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And he said, If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead. Henry concludes a messenger from the dead could say no more than Scripture says, and the same corruption that breaks through the written word would triumph over a witness from the dead; let us seek the law and testimony, the sure word of prophecy, for no terrors can give true repentance without God's special grace renewing the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
