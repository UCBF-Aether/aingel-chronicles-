The Text — Judges 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then sang Deborah and Barak the son of Abinoam on that day, saying,

2 Praise ye the LORD for the avenging of Israel, when the people willingly offered themselves.

3 Hear, O ye kings; give ear, O ye princes; I, even I, will sing unto the LORD; I will sing praise to the LORD God of Israel.

4 LORD, when thou wentest out of Seir, when thou marchedst out of the field of Edom, the earth trembled, and the heavens dropped, the clouds also dropped water.

5 The mountains melted from before the LORD, even that Sinai from before the LORD God of Israel.

6 In the days of Shamgar the son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the highways were unoccupied, and the travellers walked through byways.

7 The inhabitants of the villages ceased, they ceased in Israel, until that I Deborah arose, that I arose a mother in Israel.

8 They chose new gods; then was war in the gates: was there a shield or spear seen among forty thousand in Israel?

9 My heart is toward the governors of Israel, that offered themselves willingly among the people. Bless ye the LORD.

10 Speak, ye that ride on white asses, ye that sit in judgment, and walk by the way.

11 They that are delivered from the noise of archers in the places of drawing water, there shall they rehearse the righteous acts of the LORD, even the righteous acts toward the inhabitants of his villages in Israel: then shall the people of the LORD go down to the gates.

12 Awake, awake, Deborah: awake, awake, utter a song: arise, Barak, and lead thy captivity captive, thou son of Abinoam.

13 Then he made him that remaineth have dominion over the nobles among the people: the LORD made me have dominion over the mighty.

14 Out of Ephraim was there a root of them against Amalek; after thee, Benjamin, among thy people; out of Machir came down governors, and out of Zebulun they that handle the pen of the writer.

15 And the princes of Issachar were with Deborah; even Issachar, and also Barak: he was sent on foot into the valley. For the divisions of Reuben there were great thoughts of heart.

16 Why abodest thou among the sheepfolds, to hear the bleatings of the flocks? For the divisions of Reuben there were great searchings of heart.

17 Gilead abode beyond Jordan: and why did Dan remain in ships? Asher continued on the sea shore, and abode in his breaches.

18 Zebulun and Naphtali were a people that jeoparded their lives unto the death in the high places of the field.

19 The kings came and fought, then fought the kings of Canaan in Taanach by the waters of Megiddo; they took no gain of money.

20 They fought from heaven; the stars in their courses fought against Sisera.

21 The river of Kishon swept them away, that ancient river, the river Kishon. O my soul, thou hast trodden down strength.

22 Then were the horsehoofs broken by the means of the pransings, the pransings of their mighty ones.

23 Curse ye Meroz, said the angel of the LORD, curse ye bitterly the inhabitants thereof; because they came not to the help of the LORD, to the help of the LORD against the mighty.

24 Blessed above women shall Jael the wife of Heber the Kenite be, blessed shall she be above women in the tent.

25 He asked water, and she gave him milk; she brought forth butter in a lordly dish.

26 She put her hand to the nail, and her right hand to the workmen’s hammer; and with the hammer she smote Sisera, she smote off his head, when she had pierced and stricken through his temples.

27 At her feet he bowed, he fell, he lay down: at her feet he bowed, he fell: where he bowed, there he fell down dead.

28 The mother of Sisera looked out at a window, and cried through the lattice, Why is his chariot so long in coming? why tarry the wheels of his chariots?

29 Her wise ladies answered her, yea, she returned answer to herself,

30 Have they not sped? have they not divided the prey; to every man a damsel or two; to Sisera a prey of divers colours, a prey of divers colours of needlework, of divers colours of needlework on both sides, meet for the necks of them that take the spoil?

31 So let all thine enemies perish, O LORD: but let them that love him be as the sun when he goeth forth in his might. And the land had rest forty years.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Deborah and Barak sing the victory, praising the LORD and the people who willingly offered themselves, and recalling the LORD's march from Seir when earth trembled and heavens dropped water. The song names who came and who stayed away: Zebulun and Naphtali risked their lives on the high places, while Reuben, Gilead, Dan and Asher kept to sheepfolds, ships and seashore; the stars and the swollen Kishon fight for Israel, Meroz is cursed for not helping, Jael is blessed, and Sisera's mother waits in vain for spoil. Then the land has rest forty years. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Deborah and Barak sang on the day of victory, and Henry notes that no time should be lost in returning thanks, since praises are most acceptable when they flow from a full heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The song begins with praise for the avenging of Israel and for a people who willingly offered themselves, so that both God's vengeance and human willingness are honoured together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Kings and princes are summoned to hear, for Deborah will sing to the Lord God of Israel, making the victory a public testimony rather than a private boast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The song recalls the Lord marching from Seir and Edom, when the earth trembled and the heavens dropped water, so that the recent battle is set within God's ancient, awesome marches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The mountains melted before the Lord, the God of Israel, and Henry insists that whatever Deborah, Barak, or the army had done, the Lord must have all the praise, since will, power, and success were all from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. In the days of Shamgar and Jael the highways were unoccupied and travellers took byways, a picture of how thoroughly Jabin's tyranny had broken ordinary life and travel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The villages ceased until Deborah arose, a mother in Israel, and Henry notes she was a mother by diligently promoting the salvation of souls, not merely by holding office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Israel chose new gods with new names, and war came to the gates, while among forty thousand there was neither shield nor spear, showing that idolatry had disarmed the nation it promised to enrich. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Deborah's heart turns to the governors who offered themselves willingly, and she calls on all who shared the deliverance to bless the Lord for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Riders on white asses, judges, and travellers on the way are all called to speak of the victory, since public peace, especially for villagers, is itself a gift to be praised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. By the places of drawing water they rehearse the Lord's righteous acts toward his villages, and the people go down to the gates, so that the song becomes the nation's memory kept alive at its wells. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Deborah calls on her own soul to awake and on Barak to lead captivity captive, and Henry notes that one who would set others' hearts on fire must burn himself, and that praising God is work to awake to. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. A remnant of the people gained dominion over the nobles, and the Lord gained dominion for Deborah over the mighty, reversing the hierarchy that oppression had imposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Ephraim, Benjamin, Machir, and Zebulun are named among those who came down to the battle, each tribe honoured by name for taking its part. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The princes of Issachar stood with Deborah and Barak, but Reuben's divisions brought great searchings of heart, honour and hesitation set side by side in the same roll. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Deborah asks why Reuben abode among the sheepfolds to hear the bleatings of the flocks, since the love of ease and of worldly business kept many from duty when difficulty and danger appeared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Gilead stayed beyond Jordan, Dan remained in ships, and Asher continued on the seashore, and Henry groups them with Reuben as those kept away by fear of trouble and undue affection for their own advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Zebulun and Naphtali, by contrast, jeoparded their lives on the high places of the field, and are praised precisely for risking what the others guarded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The kings of Canaan fought at Taanach by the waters of Megiddo but took no gain of money, so that the enemy's hired warfare profited nothing against the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The stars in their courses fought against Sisera, and Henry reads this as the whole creation at war with those to whom God is an enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The river Kishon swept the enemy away, that ancient river, and Henry explains that a usually shallow stream, swollen by great rain, drowned those who tried to cross it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The horsehoofs were broken by the pransings of the mighty, a vivid close-up of chariot horses floundering in the very flood that undid their army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The angel's curse falls on Meroz, because its people came not to the help of the Lord, and Henry warns that neutrality in the contest between the Lord and his enemies falls under the curse against workers of iniquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Jael is blessed above women in the tent, and Henry notes that those in a low and narrow sphere who serve God with the powers given them shall not lose their reward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Sisera asked water and she gave milk in a lordly dish, hospitality turned, in the song's telling, into the prelude of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. She put her hand to the nail and her right hand to the workman's hammer, and smote Sisera through the head, the song lingering on the deed that ended the oppressor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. At her feet he bowed, fell, and lay dead, the conqueror brought low in the tent of a woman he had thought a refuge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Sisera's mother looked through the lattice and cried over his delayed chariot, expecting triumph and dreading delay in the same breath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Her wise ladies answered, and she answered herself, rehearsing the spoil she expected, and Henry calls this a picture of an ungodly and sensual heart doting on vain glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The imagined spoil is counted out: a damsel or two for each man, dyed needlework for Sisera, embroidered spoil for his neck, the very finery that reveals what his house loved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. The song ends by praying that all the Lord's enemies perish as Sisera did, while those who love him shine as the sun in its strength, and then the land had rest forty years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
