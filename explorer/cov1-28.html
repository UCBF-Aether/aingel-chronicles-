1 After that let the bishop teach the mysteries to the people. But if he be not present, let a presbyter speak so that the faithful may know to whom they are approaching and who is their’ God and Father. Then let the teaching of the mysteries be said thus:

2 [MYSTAGOGIA oR] INITIATION INTO THE MYSTERIES WHICH IS SAID BEFORE THE OFFERING TO THE FAITHFUL [We confess] Him who is pre-existent, and was present,

*MYSTAGOGIA*

3 As we believe, we confess Him who is our Light, Salvation, Saviour, Protector, Helper, Teacher, Deliverer, Rewarder, Assister, Strength, Wall; our Shepherd, Entrance, Door, Way,

4 Life, Medicine, Provision, Drink, [and] Judge. We confess Him passible [yet] not passible, Son?

5 [yet] not created,’ dead [yet] alive, the Son of the Father, incomprehensible [yet] comprehensible; who, [Himself] sinless, hath borne our sins when He left the Father’s heaven; whose Body being broken becometh our salvation, and [His] Blood and Spirit [our] life and holiness, and the water our cleansing ; who giveth light to the hearts of those who fear Him, being with them in all things; who hath made us strangers to the whole way of the Devil; the Renewer of souls, in whom we all have put our trust.

6 He, being God, and before the worlds with the Father, eternal God, when He saw the world perishing in the bonds of sin, and trodden down by the power of a crafty wild beast, and made subject to death through ignorance and error, determining to heal the race of mankind, came to a virgin womb, though hidden from all the camps of the heavenly ones, and cast into ignorance [the] opposing hosts.

7 But when [He], the Incorruptible, clothed Himself with corruptible fiesh, making flesh which was under death to be incorruptible, He thus showed in the flesh of dead Adam, wherewith He clothed Himself, an example’ of incorruptibility, by which example the things of corruption were abolished.

8 He, then, having become Man, the Son of God, the Lord, who took [on Him] the dead race of Adam in all its kinds, by emptying [Himself !], He who is the First, came to birth, as Man, though He is God; He who was foreknown by the prophets,” and preached by the apostles, and lauded by angels, and glorified by the Father of all’ He was crucified for us; and His cross is our life, our strength, [our] salvation, for it is the hidden mystery,‘ the ineffable joy, and through it the whole nature of mankind, always bearing it, is made inseparable from God, for it is the virtue benign’ and inseparable & from God, that cannot be spoken as is meet by these lips, [and] that was hidden from the beginning; but now the mystery which is revealed, which is for the faithful, shall be, not as it seemeth to be, but as it is.

9 This cross in which we boast, so that we may be glorified, [and] the bearers whereof, the faithful and perfect, separate their souls from everything that can be felt, from everything that is seen, as from a thing which is not true? —by this ask for yourselves, O ye who quit you like men’; make deaf your visible ears"; make blind your bodily eyes ; so that ye may know the will of Christ and all the mystery of your salvation.

10 Holy men and women, whose property it is to make your boast in the Lord, listen to the inward man.

11 Our Lord, when He taught us and appointed to us a covenant, and made us of [His] household, and came, after His passion, into Sheol, made captive all the earth—He who made the nature of death captive to life, and Death when it saw Him descending in His soul’ to Sheol, was deceived, and hoped that He was food for him, as was his custom.

12 But when he saw in Him the beauty of the Godhead, he cried out with [his] voice, saying: Who is this that hath clothed Himself with Man who [was] under me, and hath conquered me? Who is this that snatcheth from destruction flesh which was bound by me? Who is this! that hath clothed Himself with earth but [Himself] is heaven?

13 Who is this that was born in corruptibility, but suffereth no corruption Who is this [that is] a stranger to my laws? Who is this that maketh captive those that are mine? Who is this that striveth with the power of burning Death, and conquereth darkness? What is this new glory which [is] in this vision that preventeth me from doing the things which I would? Who is this new dead One without sin?

14 Who is this that by the abundance of light extinguisheth darkness, and doth not allow me to have rule over those that are mine, but draweth to heaven the souls which were given unto me? What is this glory which preventeth the body from being corruptible? Who is this whom I cannot touch? What is this glory unsearchable to its surroundings? Woe’ isme!

15 I am put to flight’ by Him and by those things which are His, for I cannot injure them.

16 He, being the Christ, who was crucified, by whom the [things] that were on the left hand were’ [placed] on the right hand, and those which were beneath [were] as those which [were] above, and those which [were] behind as those which [were] before, when He rose from the dead, and trod down Sheol, and by death slew Death"; after He rose on the third day He gave thanks to the Father, saying: I give thanks to Thee, O My Father, not with these lips which are fixed together, nor yet with a corporeal tongue through which truth and lying go out, nor with this created and material word ; but I give thanks to Thee, the King, with that Voice which through Thee understandeth all [things], which cometh not by a bodily organ, which falleth not on carnal ears, which

17 is not in the world and is not left on earth, but with this Voice, the Spirit who! is in Us, only speaking to Thee, O Father, loving Thee, praising Thee, through whom also the whole choir of perfect saints calleth Thee beloved, [calleth] Thee Father, [calleth] Thee Sustainer, [calleth] Thee Helper ; for Thou art all, and all [are] in Thee; for whatever is, is Thine and not another’s, but is Thine alone, who art for ever and ever.

18 Amen:

19 Let the shepherd know the mysteries of all nature. After I have prayed to the Father, as ye know and see, I am taken up, saith Jesus.

20 Therefore it is right that the shepherd should speak the teaching of the Initiation into the mysteries, so that they may know of whom in the holy things they are partaking, and what memorial they are making’ through the Eucharist.

21 And at the end, after this, let him say thus: As then we also have taken refuge in Him, and have learnt that it is in Him alone to give, let us beg from Him those things which He said that He would give us, which eye hath not seen and ear hath not heard, and [which] have not entered” into the heart of man, the things which God hath prepared for them that love Him, as Moses and some of the saints have said.

22 As then we have hoped in Him, let us give to Him praise; and to Him be glory and might for ever and ever. Amen.

23 Let the people say: Amen.

24 Cor. ii. (almost exactly); not as in Isa. lxiv. . The reference is perhaps to Deut. xxix. ; but it is more probably a blunder.

25 After the people are taught the Initiation’ into the mysteries, let the Eucharist be offered ; but let not the Initiation into the mysteries be said each time, but only at Pascha, on Saturday, and on the first day of the week, and on the days of the Epiphany and of Pentecost.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After the bishop teaches the mysteries to the people — or, if he is not present, a presbyter speaks so that the faithful may know to whom they are approaching and who is their God and Father — the teaching of the mysteries is given as the Mystagogia, or Initiation into the Mysteries, said before the offering to the faithful. It confesses Him who is pre-existent and present, Light, Salvation, Saviour, Protector, Helper, Teacher, Deliverer, Rewarder, Assister, Strength, Wall, Shepherd, Entrance, Door, Way, Life, Medicine, Provision, Drink and Judge — passible yet not passible, Son yet not created, dead yet alive, Son of the Father, incomprehensible yet comprehensible, who, Himself sinless, bore our sins when He left the Father's heaven, whose Body broken becomes our salvation, whose Blood and Spirit are our life and holiness, and the water our cleansing, who gives light to the hearts of those who fear Him and has made us strangers to the whole way of the Devil, the Renewer of souls. He, being God and before the worlds with the Father, eternal God, seeing the world perishing in the bonds of sin, trodden down by a crafty wild beast and made subject to death through ignorance and error, determined to heal the race of mankind and came to a virgin womb, hidden from all the camps of the heavenly ones, casting the opposing hosts into ignorance. The Incorruptible, clothing Himself with corruptible flesh, made flesh under death incorruptible, showing in the flesh of dead Adam, wherewith He clothed Himself, an example of incorruptibility by which the things of corruption were abolished. Having become Man, the Son of God, the Lord, who took the dead race of Adam in all its kinds by emptying Himself, the First, came to birth as Man though He is God — foreknown by the prophets, preached by the apostles, lauded by angels, glorified by the Father of all — and was crucified for us; His cross is our life, strength and salvation, the hidden mystery, the ineffable joy, through which the whole nature of mankind, always bearing it, is made inseparable from God. By this cross the faithful are to separate their souls from everything that can be felt and seen, make deaf their visible ears and blind their bodily eyes, so that they may know the will of Christ and all the mystery of their salvation; holy men and women whose property it is to boast in the Lord are to listen to the inward man. The Lord, when He taught and appointed a covenant and made us of His household, came after His passion into Sheol and made captive all the earth — He who made the nature of death captive to life; and Death, seeing Him descending in His soul to Sheol, was deceived, hoping He was food for him as was his custom, but seeing in Him the beauty of the Godhead cried out in a long series of questions — Who is this that clothed Himself with Man under me and conquered me, that snatches from destruction flesh bound by me, that was born in corruptibility but suffers no corruption, that is a stranger to my laws, that makes captive those that are mine, that strives with burning Death and conquers darkness, this new dead One without sin, who by abundance of light extinguishes darkness and draws to heaven the souls given to me? — and confessed, Woe is me, I am put to flight by Him and by the things which are His, for I cannot injure them. He, the Christ who was crucified, by whom things on the left were placed on the right, beneath as above, behind as before, when He rose from the dead trod down Sheol and by death slew Death, and after He rose on the third day gave thanks to the Father — not with lips fixed together, nor a corporeal tongue, nor a created material word, but with that Voice which through Him understands all things, which comes not by a bodily organ and falls not on carnal ears, is not in the world and is not left on earth, but with the Spirit who is in Us, speaking to the Father, loving and praising Him, through whom the whole choir of perfect saints calls Him beloved, Father, Sustainer and Helper, for He is all and all are in Him. The shepherd is to know the mysteries of all nature, and after praying to the Father, as they know and see, Jesus says He is taken up; therefore it is right that the shepherd speak the teaching of the Initiation, so that they may know of whom in the holy things they are partaking and what memorial they are making through the Eucharist. The chapter closes with prayer for the things God has prepared for those who love Him, which eye has not seen and ear has not heard and have not entered the heart of man, as Moses and some of the saints have said, and with the direction that after the people are taught the Initiation, the Eucharist is to be offered — but the Initiation is not to be said each time, only at Pascha, on Saturday, on the first day of the week, and on the days of the Epiphany and of Pentecost.
