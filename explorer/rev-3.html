Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And unto the angel of the church in Sardis write; These things saith he that hath the seven Spirits of God, and the seven stars; I know thy works, that thou hast a name that thou livest, and art dead.

2 Be watchful, and strengthen the things which remain, that are ready to die: for I have not found thy works perfect before God.

3 Remember therefore how thou hast received and heard, and hold fast, and repent. If therefore thou shalt not watch, I will come on thee as a thief, and thou shalt not know what hour I will come upon thee.

4 Thou hast a few names even in Sardis which have not defiled their garments; and they shall walk with me in white: for they are worthy.

5 He that overcometh, the same shall be clothed in white raiment; and I will not blot out his name out of the book of life, but I will confess his name before my Father, and before his angels.

6 He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.

7 And to the angel of the church in Philadelphia write; These things saith he that is holy, he that is true, he that hath the key of David, he that openeth, and no man shutteth; and shutteth, and no man openeth;

8 I know thy works: behold, I have set before thee an open door, and no man can shut it: for thou hast a little strength, and hast kept my word, and hast not denied my name.

9 Behold, I will make them of the synagogue of Satan, which say they are Jews, and are not, but do lie; behold, I will make them to come and worship before thy feet, and to know that I have loved thee.

10 Because thou hast kept the word of my patience, I also will keep thee from the hour of temptation, which shall come upon all the world, to try them that dwell upon the earth.

11 Behold, I come quickly: hold that fast which thou hast, that no man take thy crown.

12 Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God, and he shall go no more out: and I will write upon him the name of my God, and the name of the city of my God, which is new Jerusalem, which cometh down out of heaven from my God: and I will write upon him my new name.

13 He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.

14 And unto the angel of the church of the Laodiceans write; These things saith the Amen, the faithful and true witness, the beginning of the creation of God;

15 I know thy works, that thou art neither cold nor hot: I would thou wert cold or hot.

16 So then because thou art lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will spue thee out of my mouth.

17 Because thou sayest, I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing; and knowest not that thou art wretched, and miserable, and poor, and blind, and naked:

18 I counsel thee to buy of me gold tried in the fire, that thou mayest be rich; and white raiment, that thou mayest be clothed, and that the shame of thy nakedness do not appear; and anoint thine eyes with eyesalve, that thou mayest see.

19 As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten: be zealous therefore, and repent.

20 Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me.

21 To him that overcometh will I grant to sit with me in my throne, even as I also overcame, and am set down with my Father in his throne.

22 He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Three more letters complete the seven. Sardis has a name that it lives yet is dead, and must strengthen what remains, though a faithful remnant has not defiled its garments. Philadelphia, with little strength, has kept Christ's word and not denied his name, and is given an open door no one can shut. Laodicea, neither cold nor hot and calling itself rich while wretched, poor, blind and naked, is counselled to buy tried gold, white raiment and eye-salve, and is met by Christ standing at the door and knocking. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The message to Sardis comes from him who has the seven Spirits of God and the seven stars. Henry notes Christ holds the Spirit without measure for a languishing church to apply to for revival, and holds ministers accountable to himself, since the Spirit usually works by the ministry and the ministry is nothing without the Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Sardis is told to be watchful and strengthen the things that remain and are ready to die, for Christ has not found their works perfect before God. Henry traces their deadness to a dropped watch, and their works as shells without kernels: prayers unfilled with holy desire, alms without true charity, sabbaths without devotion of soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They must remember how they received and heard, hold fast, and repent, or Christ will come on them as a thief in an hour they do not know. Henry stresses remembering not only what they received but how, with what first affections, and warns that a dead people's security keeps them from discerning Christ's judicial visit until it strips their remaining mercies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Yet Sardis has a few names who have not defiled their garments, and they shall walk with Christ in white, for they are worthy. Henry notes God marks the smallest faithful remnant, and the white robes are those of justification, adoption, and glory, a suitable reward for keeping unspotted from the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The overcomer shall be clothed in white raiment, and Christ will not blot his name out of the book of life, but confess his name before his Father and his angels. Henry explains the book as the register of eternal election and remembrance, and notes church rolls may lose mere names, but the faithful overcome shall never be blotted out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. Henry notes every word from God deserves attention, and what seems directed to one body instructs all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The message to Philadelphia comes from him who is holy, who is true, and who has the key of David, opening and no man shutting, shutting and no man opening. Henry notes holiness makes Christ true to his word, and the key is absolute sovereignty over the church's government: doors of opportunity, utterance, entrance, and heaven itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Christ has set before Philadelphia an open door no man can shut, and commends them: though they have a little strength, they have kept his word and not denied his name. Henry notes the open door is Christ's own gift, kept open despite adversaries, and that weak true grace, though it calls for growth, still keeps Christ's word and confesses his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Christ will make those of the synagogue of Satan, who say they are Jews and are not, come and worship before Philadelphia's feet, and know that Christ has loved her. Henry explains the subjection is not divine honour paid to the church but conviction: enemies forced to acknowledge the church is right and beloved, and desiring communion with her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Because Philadelphia kept the word of Christ's patience, he will keep them from the hour of temptation coming on all the world to try earth's dwellers. Henry notes the gospel is the word of God's patience and Christ's exemplary patience, and that those who keep it in peace are prepared by the same grace to be faithful in persecution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Christ comes quickly, so they must hold fast what they have, that no man take their crown. Henry urges perseverance: the crown once hoped for can be lost by backsliding professors, while the persevering Christian wins the prize. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The overcomer shall be made a pillar in the temple of God, never to go out, with the name of God, the name of the city of God, the new Jerusalem, and Christ's new name written upon him. Henry explains the pillar is a monument of free and powerful grace, never defaced, inscribed with the believer's service to the church and the Captain under whose banner he fought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches, how Christ loves, values, commends, and will crown his faithful people. Henry closes the Philadelphia letter on Christ's love for fidelity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The message to Laodicea comes from the Amen, the faithful and true witness, the beginning of the creation of God. Henry notes Christ is steady and unchangeable in purposes and promises, a swift true witness against lukewarm professors, and the first cause of the first creation and head of the second, the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Christ knows Laodicea's works: they are neither cold nor hot, and he wishes they were cold or hot. Henry calls lukewarmness the worst temper in the world: if religion is real it deserves all earnestness, and if not, earnest opposition, leaving no room for neutrality between God and Baal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Because they are lukewarm, Christ will spue them out of his mouth. Henry compares lukewarm water turning the stomach: lukewarm professors turn Christ's heart against them, and what they call charity or moderation is nauseous to him, ending in final rejection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Laodicea says, "I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing," yet Christ says they are wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked. Henry notes the self-deception: learning taken for religion, gifts for grace, wit for wisdom, ordinances for the God of ordinances, while their souls starved in abundance, blind to their state and naked of justification and sanctification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Christ counsels them to buy from him gold tried in the fire to be rich, white raiment to cover their nakedness, and eye-salve to see. Henry explains the purchase is without money and without price, parting only with sin and self-sufficiency, receiving Christ's imputed righteousness and holiness, and resigning blind human wisdom to his word and Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. As many as Christ loves, he rebukes and chastens, so Laodicea is to be zealous and repent. Henry notes rebuke is a token of love: had Christ hated them he would have left them to ruin, and sinners should take the wounds of a friend over the flattering smiles of an enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Behold, Christ stands at the door and knocks; if anyone hears his voice and opens, he will come in and sup with him, and he with Christ. Henry pictures Christ coming by word and Spirit to a heart shut by ignorance, unbelief, and prejudice, waiting patiently, and bringing the best part of the entertainment himself, so communion is his gift as well as theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. To the overcomer Christ will grant to sit with him on his throne, as he overcame and sat down with his Father on his throne. Henry notes Christ met temptations and overcame them all, reassuming the glory he had veiled on earth, and that those conformed to him in trials and victories shall be conformed to him in glory and judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. Henry closes the seven letters by noting they are patterns to all ages: succeeding churches resembling these in graces or sins may expect God to deal with them as he dealt with these. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Lukewarm. Neither cold nor hot: Henry calls it the worst temper in religion, since if religion is real it deserves all earnestness, and lukewarm water turns the stomach. Interpreted Study note, verse 15, Matthew Henry
  • Book of life. The register of eternal election and remembrance, from which the overcomer's name shall not be blotted, in Henry's note. Interpreted Study note, verse 5, Matthew Henry
