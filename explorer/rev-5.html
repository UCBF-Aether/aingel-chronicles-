The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And I saw in the right hand of him that sat on the throne a book written within and on the backside, sealed with seven seals.

2 And I saw a strong angel proclaiming with a loud voice, Who is worthy to open the book, and to loose the seals thereof?

3 And no man in heaven, nor in earth, neither under the earth, was able to open the book, neither to look thereon.

4 And I wept much, because no man was found worthy to open and to read the book, neither to look thereon.

5 And one of the elders saith unto me, Weep not: behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof.

6 And I beheld, and, lo, in the midst of the throne and of the four beasts, and in the midst of the elders, stood a Lamb as it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth.

7 And he came and took the book out of the right hand of him that sat upon the throne.

8 And when he had taken the book, the four beasts and four and twenty elders fell down before the Lamb, having every one of them harps, and golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints.

9 And they sung a new song, saying, Thou art worthy to take the book, and to open the seals thereof: for thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation;

10 And hast made us unto our God kings and priests: and we shall reign on the earth.

11 And I beheld, and I heard the voice of many angels round about the throne and the beasts and the elders: and the number of them was ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands;

12 Saying with a loud voice, Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power, and riches, and wisdom, and strength, and honour, and glory, and blessing.

13 And every creature which is in heaven, and on the earth, and under the earth, and such as are in the sea, and all that are in them, heard I saying, Blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb for ever and ever.

14 And the four beasts said, Amen. And the four and twenty elders fell down and worshipped him that liveth for ever and ever.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

No creature in heaven, on earth or under the earth is found worthy to open the sealed book of God's decrees, and John weeps — until an elder points to the Lion of the tribe of Judah, who appears as a Lamb as it had been slain, with seven horns and seven eyes. The Lamb takes the book by merit and appointment, and heaven answers in widening circles: living creatures and elders with harps and prayers, myriads of angels, and every creature, all ascribing worth to the Lamb that was slain and redeemed people out of every kindred, tongue, people and nation. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John saw in the right hand of him who sat on the throne a book written within and on the backside, sealed with seven seals. Henry explains the book as God's decrees: the design is laid, every part adjusted, and passed into record, yet known to none but God until he allows it to be opened, with each of seven parts sealed until its turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. A strong angel proclaimed with a loud voice, "Who is worthy to open the book, and to loose the seals thereof?" Henry notes the angel comes as a champion, challenging any creature to try its wisdom on the counsels of God, loudly enough for every creature to hear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. No man in heaven, nor in earth, neither under the earth, was able to open the book or even to look thereon. Henry stresses the point: angels before the throne, the wisest and best of men, prophets beyond what God reveals, fallen angels, and departed spirits all alike cannot dive into God's decrees; God only can open them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. John wept much because no man was found worthy to open and read the book, nor to look thereon. Henry notes those who have seen most of God desire to see more, and that eager desires not presently answered turn to grief, since hope deferred makes the heart sick. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. One of the elders told John not to weep: behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has prevailed to open the book and loose its seven seals. Henry explains the titles: Lion by his human nature after Jacob's prophecy, Root of David by his divine nature, and as God and man, Mediator, he alone is fit and worthy to open and execute God's counsels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. In the midst of the throne, the living creatures, and the elders stood a Lamb as it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth. Henry notes the paradox: a lion to conquer Satan, a lamb to satisfy God's justice, bearing his sufferings' marks, with perfect power and wisdom from the Spirit received without measure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The Lamb came and took the book out of the right hand of him who sat on the throne. Henry stresses he took it not by violence or fraud but by merit and worthiness, by authority and the Father's appointment, and that God willingly put his eternal counsels into Christ's hand, who gladly took them to reveal and do his Father's will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When he took the book, the four living creatures and twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb, each with harps and golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints. Henry notes the church leads the worship, with profound adoration, and that prayer and praise should always go together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. They sang a new song: "Thou art worthy to take the book, and to open the seals thereof: for thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation." Henry notes the song suits the gospel state, grounding Christ's worthiness chiefly in his sufferings and their fruit: redemption from sin, guilt, and Satan to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Christ has made the redeemed kings and priests to God, and they shall reign on the earth. Henry notes the height of the preferment: ransomed slaves are not merely freed but made kings to rule their spirits and overcome the world, and priests with access to offer spiritual sacrifices, judging the world with Christ at the great day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. John heard the voice of many angels round the throne, the living creatures, and the elders, numbering ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands. Henry notes the angels, though needing no Saviour themselves, rejoice in sinners' redemption and join the church's doxology. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The angels said with a loud voice, "Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power, and riches, and wisdom, and strength, and honour, and glory, and blessing." Henry notes Christ is worthy both of the office requiring the greatest power and wisdom and of all honour for being sufficient and faithful in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth, in the sea, and all in them said, "Blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb for ever and ever." Henry notes the same honour is ascribed in the same words to the Lamb and to him on the throne, since their essence is one, though their parts in salvation are distinct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The four living creatures said, Amen, and the twenty-four elders fell down and worshipped him who lives for ever and ever. Henry closes the scene as it began: the church that started the anthem ends it with low prostration, as the sealed book passes with great solemnity from the Creator's hand into the Redeemer's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • The sealed book. God's decrees, laid out and recorded yet known to none but God until he allows it to be opened, in Henry's note. Interpreted Study note, verse 1, Matthew Henry
  • Lion of Judah, Root of David. Christ's titles in verse 5: Lion by his human nature, Root of David by his divine nature, in Henry's note. Interpreted Study note, verse 5, Matthew Henry
