Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the children of Israel again did evil in the sight of the LORD, when Ehud was dead.

2 And the LORD sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, that reigned in Hazor; the captain of whose host was Sisera, which dwelt in Harosheth of the Gentiles.

3 And the children of Israel cried unto the LORD: for he had nine hundred chariots of iron; and twenty years he mightily oppressed the children of Israel.

4 And Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lapidoth, she judged Israel at that time.

5 And she dwelt under the palm tree of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in mount Ephraim: and the children of Israel came up to her for judgment.

6 And she sent and called Barak the son of Abinoam out of Kedeshnaphtali, and said unto him, Hath not the LORD God of Israel commanded, saying, Go and draw toward mount Tabor, and take with thee ten thousand men of the children of Naphtali and of the children of Zebulun?

7 And I will draw unto thee to the river Kishon Sisera, the captain of Jabin’s army, with his chariots and his multitude; and I will deliver him into thine hand.

8 And Barak said unto her, If thou wilt go with me, then I will go: but if thou wilt not go with me, then I will not go.

9 And she said, I will surely go with thee: notwithstanding the journey that thou takest shall not be for thine honour; for the LORD shall sell Sisera into the hand of a woman. And Deborah arose, and went with Barak to Kedesh.

10 And Barak called Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh; and he went up with ten thousand men at his feet: and Deborah went up with him.

11 Now Heber the Kenite, which was of the children of Hobab the father in law of Moses, had severed himself from the Kenites, and pitched his tent unto the plain of Zaanaim, which is by Kedesh.

12 And they shewed Sisera that Barak the son of Abinoam was gone up to mount Tabor.

13 And Sisera gathered together all his chariots, even nine hundred chariots of iron, and all the people that were with him, from Harosheth of the Gentiles unto the river of Kishon.

14 And Deborah said unto Barak, Up; for this is the day in which the LORD hath delivered Sisera into thine hand: is not the LORD gone out before thee? So Barak went down from mount Tabor, and ten thousand men after him.

15 And the LORD discomfited Sisera, and all his chariots, and all his host, with the edge of the sword before Barak; so that Sisera lighted down off his chariot, and fled away on his feet.

16 But Barak pursued after the chariots, and after the host, unto Harosheth of the Gentiles: and all the host of Sisera fell upon the edge of the sword; and there was not a man left.

17 Howbeit Sisera fled away on his feet to the tent of Jael the wife of Heber the Kenite: for there was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite.

18 And Jael went out to meet Sisera, and said unto him, Turn in, my lord, turn in to me; fear not. And when he had turned in unto her into the tent, she covered him with a mantle.

19 And he said unto her, Give me, I pray thee, a little water to drink; for I am thirsty. And she opened a bottle of milk, and gave him drink, and covered him.

20 Again he said unto her, Stand in the door of the tent, and it shall be, when any man doth come and enquire of thee, and say, Is there any man here? that thou shalt say, No.

21 Then Jael Heber’s wife took a nail of the tent, and took an hammer in her hand, and went softly unto him, and smote the nail into his temples, and fastened it into the ground: for he was fast asleep and weary. So he died.

22 And, behold, as Barak pursued Sisera, Jael came out to meet him, and said unto him, Come, and I will shew thee the man whom thou seekest. And when he came into her tent, behold, Sisera lay dead, and the nail was in his temples.

23 So God subdued on that day Jabin the king of Canaan before the children of Israel.

24 And the hand of the children of Israel prospered, and prevailed against Jabin the king of Canaan, until they had destroyed Jabin king of Canaan.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After Ehud's death Israel again does evil and is sold to Jabin of Hazor, whose captain Sisera, with nine hundred iron chariots, oppresses them for twenty years. Deborah, prophetess and judge under her palm tree, sends Barak to Mount Tabor with ten thousand men; the LORD discomfits Sisera at the Kishon, and the fleeing general is killed in the tent of Jael, who drives a tent nail through his temples as he sleeps. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After Ehud's death Israel again did evil, and Henry observes that eighty years of rest had made them secure and indulgent rather than confirming them in religion, so that the prosperity of fools destroyed them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The Lord sold them to Jabin of Hazor, whose captain Sisera commanded nine hundred iron chariots, and this nearer enemy mightily oppressed Israel twenty years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Israel cried to the Lord because of the iron chariots, and Henry notes the pattern: those who slight God in prosperity find themselves under necessity of seeking him in trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Deborah, a prophetess and wife of Lapidoth, judged Israel, instructed in divine knowledge by the Spirit, and acting as God's mouth to correct abuses and redress grievances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. She dwelt under the palm tree of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel, and Israel came to her for judgment, a court held in the open under a tree rather than in a palace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Deborah sent for Barak of Kedesh-naphtali and gave him God's command to draw toward Mount Tabor with ten thousand men of Naphtali and Zebulun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. God promised to draw Sisera with his chariots and multitude to the river Kishon and deliver him into Barak's hand, so that the battle's place and outcome were appointed before the army marched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Barak answered that he would go only if Deborah went with him, valuing her presence and the assurance of God's word more than mere honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Deborah promised to go, but warned that the honour would not be Barak's, for the Lord would sell Sisera into the hand of a woman, and she would not send him where she would not go herself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Barak called Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh, ten thousand men went up at his feet, and Deborah went up with him, so that the prophetess kept her word on the field. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Heber the Kenite had separated from his kin and pitched his tent by the oak in Zaanaim near Kedesh, a detail that sets the stage for where Sisera would flee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Sisera was told that Barak had gone up to Mount Tabor, and the report drew the Canaanite army exactly where God had said it would be drawn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Sisera gathered all his nine hundred iron chariots and all his people to the river Kishon, trusting, as Henry notes, chiefly in his chariots. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Deborah told Barak to arise, for the Lord had gone before him, and Barak went down from Tabor with ten thousand men, quitting the mountain in dependence on divine power though the plain favoured chariots. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The Lord discomfited Sisera and all his chariots and host with the edge of the sword, and Sisera himself fled away on foot, his pride in iron undone by the God who goes before his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Barak pursued the chariots and the host to Harosheth, and all Sisera's host fell by the sword until not a man was left. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Sisera fled on foot to the tent of Jael, wife of Heber the Kenite, because there was peace between Jabin and Heber's house, and the fugitive sought shelter where he expected alliance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Jael came out to meet Sisera, invited him in, and covered him with a mantle, receiving the enemy general with the outward signs of hospitality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. When he asked for water she gave him milk and covered him again, and Henry allows that Jael may at first have intended kindness before a divine impulse led her to see him as the determined enemy of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Sisera told her to stand at the tent door and deny he was there, asking her to lie for his life and making her tent his last defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Jael took a tent nail and hammer, went softly to the sleeping Sisera, and smote the nail into his temples until it fastened into the ground, so that he died, and Henry notes that the weak things of the world thus confound the mighty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. When Barak pursued Sisera, Jael came out and showed him the man lying dead with the nail in his temples, so that the pursuer found his enemy already judged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. So God subdued Jabin king of Canaan before Israel that day, and the victory is credited to God rather than to Barak or Jael alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Israel's hand prevailed more and more against Jabin until he was destroyed, and Henry adds that Israel would have prevented much mischief had they destroyed the Canaanites sooner, though better to be wise late than never. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
