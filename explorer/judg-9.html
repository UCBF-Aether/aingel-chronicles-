The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Abimelech the son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem unto his mother’s brethren, and communed with them, and with all the family of the house of his mother’s father, saying,

2 Speak, I pray you, in the ears of all the men of Shechem, Whether is better for you, either that all the sons of Jerubbaal, which are threescore and ten persons, reign over you, or that one reign over you? remember also that I am your bone and your flesh.

3 And his mother’s brethren spake of him in the ears of all the men of Shechem all these words: and their hearts inclined to follow Abimelech; for they said, He is our brother.

4 And they gave him threescore and ten pieces of silver out of the house of Baalberith, wherewith Abimelech hired vain and light persons, which followed him.

5 And he went unto his father’s house at Ophrah, and slew his brethren the sons of Jerubbaal, being threescore and ten persons, upon one stone: notwithstanding yet Jotham the youngest son of Jerubbaal was left; for he hid himself.

6 And all the men of Shechem gathered together, and all the house of Millo, and went, and made Abimelech king, by the plain of the pillar that was in Shechem.

7 And when they told it to Jotham, he went and stood in the top of mount Gerizim, and lifted up his voice, and cried, and said unto them, Hearken unto me, ye men of Shechem, that God may hearken unto you.

8 The trees went forth on a time to anoint a king over them; and they said unto the olive tree, Reign thou over us.

9 But the olive tree said unto them, Should I leave my fatness, wherewith by me they honour God and man, and go to be promoted over the trees?

10 And the trees said to the fig tree, Come thou, and reign over us.

11 But the fig tree said unto them, Should I forsake my sweetness, and my good fruit, and go to be promoted over the trees?

12 Then said the trees unto the vine, Come thou, and reign over us.

13 And the vine said unto them, Should I leave my wine, which cheereth God and man, and go to be promoted over the trees?

14 Then said all the trees unto the bramble, Come thou, and reign over us.

15 And the bramble said unto the trees, If in truth ye anoint me king over you, then come and put your trust in my shadow: and if not, let fire come out of the bramble, and devour the cedars of Lebanon.

16 Now therefore, if ye have done truly and sincerely, in that ye have made Abimelech king, and if ye have dealt well with Jerubbaal and his house, and have done unto him according to the deserving of his hands;

17 (For my father fought for you, and adventured his life far, and delivered you out of the hand of Midian:

18 And ye are risen up against my father’s house this day, and have slain his sons, threescore and ten persons, upon one stone, and have made Abimelech, the son of his maidservant, king over the men of Shechem, because he is your brother;)

19 If ye then have dealt truly and sincerely with Jerubbaal and with his house this day, then rejoice ye in Abimelech, and let him also rejoice in you:

20 But if not, let fire come out from Abimelech, and devour the men of Shechem, and the house of Millo; and let fire come out from the men of Shechem, and from the house of Millo, and devour Abimelech.

21 And Jotham ran away, and fled, and went to Beer, and dwelt there, for fear of Abimelech his brother.

22 When Abimelech had reigned three years over Israel,

23 Then God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem; and the men of Shechem dealt treacherously with Abimelech:

24 That the cruelty done to the threescore and ten sons of Jerubbaal might come, and their blood be laid upon Abimelech their brother, which slew them; and upon the men of Shechem, which aided him in the killing of his brethren.

25 And the men of Shechem set liers in wait for him in the top of the mountains, and they robbed all that came along that way by them: and it was told Abimelech.

26 And Gaal the son of Ebed came with his brethren, and went over to Shechem: and the men of Shechem put their confidence in him.

27 And they went out into the fields, and gathered their vineyards, and trode the grapes, and made merry, and went into the house of their god, and did eat and drink, and cursed Abimelech.

28 And Gaal the son of Ebed said, Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we should serve him? is not he the son of Jerubbaal? and Zebul his officer? serve the men of Hamor the father of Shechem: for why should we serve him?

29 And would to God this people were under my hand! then would I remove Abimelech. And he said to Abimelech, Increase thine army, and come out.

30 And when Zebul the ruler of the city heard the words of Gaal the son of Ebed, his anger was kindled.

31 And he sent messengers unto Abimelech privily, saying, Behold, Gaal the son of Ebed and his brethren be come to Shechem; and, behold, they fortify the city against thee.

32 Now therefore up by night, thou and the people that is with thee, and lie in wait in the field:

33 And it shall be, that in the morning, as soon as the sun is up, thou shalt rise early, and set upon the city: and, behold, when he and the people that is with him come out against thee, then mayest thou do to them as thou shalt find occasion.

34 And Abimelech rose up, and all the people that were with him, by night, and they laid wait against Shechem in four companies.

35 And Gaal the son of Ebed went out, and stood in the entering of the gate of the city: and Abimelech rose up, and the people that were with him, from lying in wait.

36 And when Gaal saw the people, he said to Zebul, Behold, there come people down from the top of the mountains. And Zebul said unto him, Thou seest the shadow of the mountains as if they were men.

37 And Gaal spake again, and said, See there come people down by the middle of the land, and another company come along by the plain of Meonenim.

38 Then said Zebul unto him, Where is now thy mouth, wherewith thou saidst, Who is Abimelech, that we should serve him? is not this the people that thou hast despised? go out, I pray now, and fight with them.

39 And Gaal went out before the men of Shechem, and fought with Abimelech.

40 And Abimelech chased him, and he fled before him, and many were overthrown and wounded, even unto the entering of the gate.

41 And Abimelech dwelt at Arumah: and Zebul thrust out Gaal and his brethren, that they should not dwell in Shechem.

42 And it came to pass on the morrow, that the people went out into the field; and they told Abimelech.

43 And he took the people, and divided them into three companies, and laid wait in the field, and looked, and, behold, the people were come forth out of the city; and he rose up against them, and smote them.

44 And Abimelech, and the company that was with him, rushed forward, and stood in the entering of the gate of the city: and the two other companies ran upon all the people that were in the fields, and slew them.

45 And Abimelech fought against the city all that day; and he took the city, and slew the people that was therein, and beat down the city, and sowed it with salt.

46 And when all the men of the tower of Shechem heard that, they entered into an hold of the house of the god Berith.

47 And it was told Abimelech, that all the men of the tower of Shechem were gathered together.

48 And Abimelech gat him up to mount Zalmon, he and all the people that were with him; and Abimelech took an axe in his hand, and cut down a bough from the trees, and took it, and laid it on his shoulder, and said unto the people that were with him, What ye have seen me do, make haste, and do as I have done.

49 And all the people likewise cut down every man his bough, and followed Abimelech, and put them to the hold, and set the hold on fire upon them; so that all the men of the tower of Shechem died also, about a thousand men and women.

50 Then went Abimelech to Thebez, and encamped against Thebez, and took it.

51 But there was a strong tower within the city, and thither fled all the men and women, and all they of the city, and shut it to them, and gat them up to the top of the tower.

52 And Abimelech came unto the tower, and fought against it, and went hard unto the door of the tower to burn it with fire.

53 And a certain woman cast a piece of a millstone upon Abimelech’s head, and all to brake his skull.

54 Then he called hastily unto the young man his armourbearer, and said unto him, Draw thy sword, and slay me, that men say not of me, A women slew him. And his young man thrust him through, and he died.

55 And when the men of Israel saw that Abimelech was dead, they departed every man unto his place.

56 Thus God rendered the wickedness of Abimelech, which he did unto his father, in slaying his seventy brethren:

57 And all the evil of the men of Shechem did God render upon their heads: and upon them came the curse of Jotham the son of Jerubbaal.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Abimelech, Gideon's son by a Shechem concubine, hires vain men with silver from the house of Baal-berith and murders his seventy brothers on one stone at Ophrah; only Jotham escapes, and from Mount Gerizim he tells the parable of the trees, in which the fruitful olive, fig and vine refuse the crown and only the worthless bramble accepts it. God sends an evil spirit between Abimelech and Shechem: Gaal's revolt fails, Shechem is taken and sown with salt, its tower is burned, and at Thebez a woman drops a millstone on Abimelech's head, so that he has his armourbearer kill him lest men say a woman slew him. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Abimelech son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem, where idolatry once creeping in was now openly professed, and JFB notes he plied the arts of a demagogue among his maternal relatives to raise himself to a throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He asked whether seventy sons of Jerubbaal reigning or one man reigning was better, and JFB calls this a false insinuation, since Gideon had rejected kingship with abhorrence and no other son is shown coveting the title. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. His mother's brethren spoke for him to all Shechem, and their hearts inclined to follow him because he was their brother, so that kinship, not merit, carried the demagogue's case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They gave him seventy pieces of silver from the house of Baal-berith, god of the covenant by whom the league of cities was formed, and with it he hired vain and light persons, whom JFB calls idle vagabonds, the scum of society, with nothing to lose and much to gain from revolution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. At Ophrah he slew his brethren, seventy persons, upon one stone, and JFB notes this first recorded Eastern massacre of rivals, probably a sacrifice on a stone altar in revenge for his father's demolition of Baal's altar, with two wanting to complete the round number. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. All Shechem and the house of Millo, that is, the priests of Baal in the temple, made Abimelech king by the plain of the pillar, or rather by the oak near a raised mound, so the coronation might be conspicuous to a crowd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Jotham stood on Mount Gerizim and lifted up his voice, and JFB notes the spot was Shechem's public place, the parable fitted to the valley's foliage, and the overhanging hill carried his voice distinctly to listeners below. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The trees went forth to anoint a king and said to the olive, Reign thou over us, opening Jotham's parable of reproach against a people choosing a king from among the worthless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The olive refused, asking whether it should leave its fatness, by which God and man are honoured, to be promoted over the trees, and JFB notes the oil honoured God in accepted sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The trees then said to the fig tree, Come and reign over us, turning from the olive's refusal to the next fruitful tree. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The fig tree likewise refused, asking whether it should forsake its sweetness and good fruit to be promoted over the trees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The trees then said to the vine, Come and reign over us, pressing the crown on a third fruitful tree. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The vine refused, asking whether it should leave its wine which cheers God and man, and JFB explains God is said to be cheered when sacrifices are accepted, as he is honoured by oil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Then all the trees said to the bramble, Come and reign over us, and the parable's sting lands: only the worthless thorn accepts the crown the fruitful trees refused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The bramble answered that if they truly anointed it king they must trust in its shadow, and if not, fire would come out of the bramble and devour the cedars of Lebanon, a threat as empty as its shade. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Jotham applied the parable, asking whether they had acted in truth and sincerity in making Abimelech king and in dealing well with Jerubbaal and his house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. He reminded them his father had fought for them, adventured his life, and delivered them from Midian, the service Abimelech's kingship repaid with murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They had risen against his father's house that day, slain his sons on one stone, and made the son of a maidservant king because he was their brother, the very kinship argument of verse 3 turned into an indictment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. If they had acted truly, Jotham said, let them rejoice in Abimelech and let him rejoice in them, an ironic blessing that leaves the curse to follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. But if not, let fire come out from Abimelech and devour Shechem and Millo, and fire from Shechem and Millo devour Abimelech, so that king and makers would destroy each other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Jotham then ran away and went to Beer and dwelt there for fear of Abimelech, and JFB identifies Beer as the modern El-Bireh on the ridge north of Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Abimelech reigned three years over Israel, and JFB notes his reign at first extended little beyond Shechem, growing by stealthy encroachments, and that the word used signifies despotism, not the divinely authorised rule of a judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem, and they dealt treacherously with him, and JFB explains this in providence as jealousy, distrust, and smothered rebellion permitted to punish the fratricide and idolatrous usurper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. This came that the cruelty to Jerubbaal's seventy sons might come, and their blood be laid on Abimelech who slew them and on Shechem's men who strengthened his hands, so that both murderer and accomplices share the guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Shechem's men set ambushers in the mountain tops who robbed all who passed by, and the conspiracy against their own king began with highway robbery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Gaal son of Ebed came with his brethren, and Shechem put confidence in him, and JFB calls this an insurrection of the original Canaanites breaking out in Shechem under a new head. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The Shechemites went into the fields, gathered grapes, trod them, made merry, and went into their god's house to eat, drink, and curse Abimelech, turning harvest joy into open treason. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Gaal boasted, Who is Abimelech and who is Shechem, that we should serve him, and JFB calls him boastful, impudent, and cowardly, totally unfit to lead a revolution, whose end would be ambush, defeat, a city destroyed and sown with salt, and a stronghold burned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Gaal wished the people were under his hand so he could remove Abimelech, and told Abimelech to increase his army and come out, a challenge issued before any army existed to back it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Zebul, ruler of the city, heard Gaal's words and his anger was kindled, so that the conspiracy now had an enemy inside the walls as well as outside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Zebul sent messengers secretly to Abimelech, warning that Gaal and his brethren had come to Shechem and were fortifying the city against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. He advised Abimelech to rise by night with his people and lie in wait in the field, turning Gaal's open boast into a trap. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. In the morning at sunrise Abimelech was to rise early and fall upon the city, and when Gaal came out, do to him as occasion served. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Abimelech rose by night with four companies and lay in wait against Shechem, following Zebul's counsel exactly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Gaal went out and stood in the gate's entrance while Abimelech and his people rose from their ambush, so that the challenger found himself already outmanoeuvred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. Gaal saw the people and told Zebul they were coming down from the mountain tops, and Zebul answered that he saw only the shadow of the mountains as men, stalling Gaal until the ambush was unmistakable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Gaal insisted the people were coming down by the middle of the land and another company by the plain of Meonenim, and his alarm came too late to save him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. Zebul then taunted him, asking where his mouth was now, and whether this was not the people he had despised, and sent him out to fight them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. Gaal went out before Shechem's men and fought with Abimelech, the boaster now forced to back his words with battle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. Abimelech chased Gaal, who fled, and many fell wounded even to the gate's entrance, so that the insurrection collapsed at the city gate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Abimelech dwelt at Arumah while Zebul thrust Gaal and his brethren out of Shechem, expelling the Canaanite rising from the city it had seized. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. The next day the people went out into the field, and it was told to Abimelech, so that the city's punishment, not merely Gaal's expulsion, followed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. Abimelech took his people, divided them into three companies, laid wait in the field, and when the people came out of the city, rose up and smote them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. Abimelech and his company rushed forward and stood in the gate's entrance while the other two companies slew those in the field, cutting off both escape and return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. He fought against the city all day, took it, slew its people, beat down the city, and sowed it with salt, fulfilling Jotham's curse in the very terms of desolation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. The men of the tower of Shechem, hearing this, entered the hold of the house of the god Berith, fleeing from the sacked city to their god's stronghold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. It was told to Abimelech that all the men of the tower were gathered together, so that the last refuge became his next target. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. Abimelech went to Mount Zalmon, took an axe, cut a bough, laid it on his shoulder, and told his people to do as they had seen him do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. All the people cut boughs, followed him, put them to the hold, and set the hold on fire, so that about a thousand men and women of the tower died, the fire fulfilling the bramble's threat from the parable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. Abimelech then went to Thebez and encamped against it, and JFB identifies Thebez as the modern Tubas, not far from Shechem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. All the men and women of Thebez fled to the tower, shut themselves in, and went up to its top, and JFB notes Canaanite forts often had such a strong tower as a last refuge, a scene matched in Assyrian bas-reliefs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. Abimelech came to the tower, fought against it, and drew near to its door to burn it with fire, repeating at Thebez the very tactic that had destroyed Shechem's tower. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. A certain woman cast a piece of a millstone on Abimelech's head and broke his skull, and JFB's description of huge stone fragments dropping from the battlements on assailants is exactly this stroke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. Abimelech called his armourbearer to draw his sword and slay him, lest men say a woman slew him, and the young man thrust him through and he died, pride outliving the king to his last breath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. When Israel saw Abimelech was dead, they departed every man to his place, and the usurper's army dissolved with its head. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 56. Thus God rendered Abimelech's wickedness, which he did to his father in slaying his seventy brethren, returning the fratricide's blood on his own head. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 57. And all the evil of Shechem's men God rendered upon their heads, and upon them came the curse of Jotham son of Jerubbaal, so that parable, conspiracy, and judgment close together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 22. Abimelech reigned three years over Israel, and JFB notes his reign at first extended little beyond Shechem, growing by stealthy encroachments, and that the word used signifies despotism, not the divinely authorised rule of a judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem, and they dealt treacherously with him, and JFB explains this in providence as jealousy, distrust, and smothered rebellion permitted to punish the fratricide and idolatrous usurper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. This came that the cruelty to Jerubbaal's seventy sons might come, and their blood be laid on Abimelech who slew them and on Shechem's men who strengthened his hands, so that both murderer and accomplices share the guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Shechem's men set ambushers in the mountain tops who robbed all who passed by, and the conspiracy against their own king began with highway robbery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 27. The Shechemites went into the fields, gathered grapes, trod them, made merry, and went into their god's house to eat, drink, and curse Abimelech, turning harvest joy into open treason. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Gaal boasted, Who is Abimelech and who is Shechem, that we should serve him, and JFB calls him boastful, impudent, and cowardly, totally unfit to lead a revolution, whose end would be ambush, defeat, a city destroyed and sown with salt, and a stronghold burned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Gaal wished the people were under his hand so he could remove Abimelech, and told Abimelech to increase his army and come out, a challenge issued before any army existed to back it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Zebul, ruler of the city, heard Gaal's words and his anger was kindled, so that the conspiracy now had an enemy inside the walls as well as outside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Zebul sent messengers secretly to Abimelech, warning that Gaal and his brethren had come to Shechem and were fortifying the city against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. He advised Abimelech to rise by night with his people and lie in wait in the field, turning Gaal's open boast into a trap. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. In the morning at sunrise Abimelech was to rise early and fall upon the city, and when Gaal came out, do to him as occasion served. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Abimelech rose by night with four companies and lay in wait against Shechem, following Zebul's counsel exactly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Gaal went out and stood in the gate's entrance while Abimelech and his people rose from their ambush, so that the challenger found himself already outmanoeuvred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. Gaal saw the people and told Zebul they were coming down from the mountain tops, and Zebul answered that he saw only the shadow of the mountains as men, stalling Gaal until the ambush was unmistakable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Gaal insisted the people were coming down by the middle of the land and another company by the plain of Meonenim, and his alarm came too late to save him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 41. Abimelech dwelt at Arumah while Zebul thrust Gaal and his brethren out of Shechem, expelling the Canaanite rising from the city it had seized. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 50. Abimelech then went to Thebez and encamped against it, and JFB identifies Thebez as the modern Tubas, not far from Shechem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
