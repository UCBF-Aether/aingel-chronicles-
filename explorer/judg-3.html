The Text — Judges 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now these are the nations which the LORD left, to prove Israel by them, even as many of Israel as had not known all the wars of Canaan;

2 Only that the generations of the children of Israel might know, to teach them war, at the least such as before knew nothing thereof;

3 Namely, five lords of the Philistines, and all the Canaanites, and the Sidonians, and the Hivites that dwelt in mount Lebanon, from mount Baalhermon unto the entering in of Hamath.

4 And they were to prove Israel by them, to know whether they would hearken unto the commandments of the LORD, which he commanded their fathers by the hand of Moses.

5 And the children of Israel dwelt among the Canaanites, Hittites, and Amorites, and Perizzites, and Hivites, and Jebusites:

6 And they took their daughters to be their wives, and gave their daughters to their sons, and served their gods.

7 And the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the LORD, and forgat the LORD their God, and served Baalim and the groves.

8 Therefore the anger of the LORD was hot against Israel, and he sold them into the hand of Chushanrishathaim king of Mesopotamia: and the children of Israel served Chushanrishathaim eight years.

9 And when the children of Israel cried unto the LORD, the LORD raised up a deliverer to the children of Israel, who delivered them, even Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother.

10 And the Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he judged Israel, and went out to war: and the LORD delivered Chushanrishathaim king of Mesopotamia into his hand; and his hand prevailed against Chushanrishathaim.

11 And the land had rest forty years. And Othniel the son of Kenaz died.

12 And the children of Israel did evil again in the sight of the LORD: and the LORD strengthened Eglon the king of Moab against Israel, because they had done evil in the sight of the LORD.

13 And he gathered unto him the children of Ammon and Amalek, and went and smote Israel, and possessed the city of palm trees.

14 So the children of Israel served Eglon the king of Moab eighteen years.

15 But when the children of Israel cried unto the LORD, the LORD raised them up a deliverer, Ehud the son of Gera, a Benjamite, a man lefthanded: and by him the children of Israel sent a present unto Eglon the king of Moab.

16 But Ehud made him a dagger which had two edges, of a cubit length; and he did gird it under his raiment upon his right thigh.

17 And he brought the present unto Eglon king of Moab: and Eglon was a very fat man.

18 And when he had made an end to offer the present, he sent away the people that bare the present.

19 But he himself turned again from the quarries that were by Gilgal, and said, I have a secret errand unto thee, O king: who said, Keep silence. And all that stood by him went out from him.

20 And Ehud came unto him; and he was sitting in a summer parlour, which he had for himself alone. And Ehud said, I have a message from God unto thee. And he arose out of his seat.

21 And Ehud put forth his left hand, and took the dagger from his right thigh, and thrust it into his belly:

22 And the haft also went in after the blade; and the fat closed upon the blade, so that he could not draw the dagger out of his belly; and the dirt came out.

23 Then Ehud went forth through the porch, and shut the doors of the parlour upon him, and locked them.

24 When he was gone out, his servants came; and when they saw that, behold, the doors of the parlour were locked, they said, Surely he covereth his feet in his summer chamber.

25 And they tarried till they were ashamed: and, behold, he opened not the doors of the parlour; therefore they took a key, and opened them: and, behold, their lord was fallen down dead on the earth.

26 And Ehud escaped while they tarried, and passed beyond the quarries, and escaped unto Seirath.

27 And it came to pass, when he was come, that he blew a trumpet in the mountain of Ephraim, and the children of Israel went down with him from the mount, and he before them.

28 And he said unto them, Follow after me: for the LORD hath delivered your enemies the Moabites into your hand. And they went down after him, and took the fords of Jordan toward Moab, and suffered not a man to pass over.

29 And they slew of Moab at that time about ten thousand men, all lusty, and all men of valour; and there escaped not a man.

30 So Moab was subdued that day under the hand of Israel. And the land had rest fourscore years.

31 And after him was Shamgar the son of Anath, which slew of the Philistines six hundred men with an ox goad: and he also delivered Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The nations left in the land are left to prove Israel, and Israel instead dwells among them, intermarries and serves their gods. Three deliverances follow the same shape: Othniel, raised after eight years under Mesopotamia, gives forty years of rest; Ehud, the left-handed Benjamite, kills the fat king Eglon with a hidden dagger and holds the fords of Jordan; and Shamgar, with an ox-goad, slays six hundred Philistines. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The nations the Lord left were left to prove Israel, and JFB explains the double design: to discipline Israel morally through their inroads and to teach the young, who had never known war, the use of weapons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The new generations were to learn war, since Israel was not to be idle and slothful, and Henry notes that trials both detect the wickedness of sinners' hearts and strengthen believers' graces in daily conflict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The nations left included five Philistine lords, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites of Mount Lebanon, a deliberate remnant left on every border of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They remained to prove whether Israel would hearken to the Lord's commandments, so that the test was obedience, not merely courage in battle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Israel dwelt among the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites, and JFB observes that the two classes gradually came into habits of contact until Israel relaxed its principles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They intermarried with them and served their gods, so that the friendship of the world proved more fatal than its enmity, murdering souls where war could only kill the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Israel did evil, forgot the Lord their God, and served Baalim and the groves, exchanging the memory of deliverance for the worship of the land's idols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The Lord's anger was hot, and he sold them into the hand of Chushan-rishathaim of Mesopotamia for eight years, a distant oppressor for a people who had looked to distant gods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. When Israel cried to the Lord he raised up Othniel, and Henry notes that affliction makes those cry to God who before would scarcely speak to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The Spirit of the Lord came upon Othniel, giving wisdom, courage, and power, and he first judged Israel, reproving and reforming them, before he went to war, since sin at home must be conquered first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The land had rest forty years until Othniel died, a long quiet that showed what reformation followed by deliverance could give, and how much depended on one faithful judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Israel again did evil, and God strengthened Eglon of Moab against them, for though the Moabites did worse, God punishes his own people's sins in this world, weakening Israel and strengthening its enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Eglon gathered Ammon and Amalek and took the city of palm trees, so that Israel's old enemies combined against a people weakened by their own sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Israel served Eglon eighteen years, a longer servitude than before, since lesser troubles had not done their work and God sent a greater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. When Israel cried again, the Lord raised up Ehud, a Benjamite, a man left-handed, an unlikely deliverer chosen precisely so that the power would appear to be God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Ehud made himself a double-edged dagger a cubit long and girded it under his raiment on his right thigh, preparing in secret the instrument of Moab's judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. He brought the present to Eglon, who was a very fat man, and the tribute itself became the occasion for gaining access to the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. After presenting the present Ehud sent away the bearers, clearing the chamber so that what followed would be between the judge and the oppressor alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Ehud returned from the quarries by Gilgal with a secret errand, and Henry notes his bold address: what message from God but vengeance can a proud rebel expect, though ministers now bear a message of mercy to those who accept it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Eglon rose from his seat in the summer parlour to hear the message, and his deference to a word from God became the moment of his judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Ehud put forth his left hand, took the dagger, and thrust it into Eglon's belly, executing as a minister of divine justice the sentence on an enemy of God and Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The haft went in after the blade and the fat closed over it, so that Ehud could not draw the dagger out, and the wound itself bore witness to the completeness of the stroke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Ehud went out through the porch, shut the parlour doors and locked them, concealing the deed long enough to make his escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. The servants, finding the doors locked, supposed the king was covering his feet in his chamber, and their respectful delay gave Ehud the time he needed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Ashamed at the long wait, they took a key and opened the doors to find their lord fallen dead, and the court that had trusted in its king discovered its own helplessness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Ehud escaped past the quarries to Seirath while the servants lingered, so that God's deliverer slipped away as quietly as he had come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. In the hill country of Ephraim Ehud blew a trumpet and led Israel down from the hills, turning a single assassination into a national rising. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. He ordered the taking of the fords of Jordan, so that no Moabite could escape back across the river, and the victory was made complete by holding the crossings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Israel slew about ten thousand Moabites, all lusty men of valour, and not a man escaped, showing that the deliverance was thorough, not partial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Moab was subdued that day under Israel, and the land had rest eighty years, a rest Henry sets against the saints' everlasting rest in the heavenly Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. After Ehud came Shamgar, who with an ox-goad slew six hundred Philistines and also delivered Israel, and Henry notes that God makes serviceable those whose birth and employment are mean, since it is no matter what the weapon is if God directs the arm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
