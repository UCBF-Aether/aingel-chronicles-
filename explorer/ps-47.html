The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 47 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 O clap your hands, all ye people; shout unto God with the voice of triumph.

2 For the LORD most high is terrible; he is a great King over all the earth.

3 He shall subdue the people under us, and the nations under our feet.

4 He shall choose our inheritance for us, the excellency of Jacob whom he loved. Selah.

5 God is gone up with a shout, the LORD with the sound of a trumpet.

6 Sing praises to God, sing praises: sing praises unto our King, sing praises.

7 For God is the King of all the earth: sing ye praises with understanding.

8 God reigneth over the heathen: God sitteth upon the throne of his holiness.

9 The princes of the people are gathered together, even the people of the God of Abraham: for the shields of the earth belong unto God: he is greatly exalted.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Clap and shout for the great King over all the earth: peoples subdued, inheritance chosen, God gone up with shout and trumpet, which the study note says Christians read as Christ's ascension. Sing praises four times over, with understanding, for God reigns over the nations from his holy throne, and earth's shields belong to him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. O clap your hands, all you people; shout to God with the voice of triumph: clapping and shouting with triumph are the people's part in the King's victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. For the Lord Most High is terrible; he is a great King over all the earth: terribleness and universal kingship are the reasons for the triumph. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He shall subdue the people under us, and the nations under our feet: subjugation of peoples and nations under Israel's feet is God's work for his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He shall choose our inheritance for us, the excellency of Jacob whom he loved: the inheritance is chosen by God, and its excellency is Jacob, whom God loved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God is gone up with a shout, the Lord with the sound of a trumpet: the ascension — God going up amid shouts and trumpets — is celebrated, and Christians read here Christ's ascension. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Sing praises to God, sing praises; sing praises to our King, sing praises: the command is repeated four times, because praise for such a King cannot be said once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For God is the King of all the earth; sing praises with understanding: universal kingship calls for praise with understanding, not mere noise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. God reigns over the heathen; God sits upon the throne of his holiness: reign over the heathen from a holy throne — power and holiness enthroned together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The princes of the people are gathered together, even the people of the God of Abraham; for the shields of the earth belong to God; he is greatly exalted: princes and peoples gather to Abraham's God, for earth's shields are his, and he is greatly exalted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
