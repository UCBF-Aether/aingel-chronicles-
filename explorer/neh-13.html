The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Nehemiah 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 On that day they read in the book of Moses in the audience of the people; and therein was found written, that the Ammonite and the Moabite should not come into the congregation of God for ever;

2 Because they met not the children of Israel with bread and with water, but hired Balaam against them, that he should curse them: howbeit our God turned the curse into a blessing.

3 Now it came to pass, when they had heard the law, that they separated from Israel all the mixed multitude.

4 And before this, Eliashib the priest, having the oversight of the chamber of the house of our God, was allied unto Tobiah:

5 And he had prepared for him a great chamber, where aforetime they laid the meat offerings, the frankincense, and the vessels, and the tithes of the corn, the new wine, and the oil, which was commanded to be given to the Levites, and the singers, and the porters; and the offerings of the priests.

6 But in all this time was not I at Jerusalem: for in the two and thirtieth year of Artaxerxes king of Babylon came I unto the king, and after certain days obtained I leave of the king:

7 And I came to Jerusalem, and understood of the evil that Eliashib did for Tobiah, in preparing him a chamber in the courts of the house of God.

8 And it grieved me sore: therefore I cast forth all the household stuff to Tobiah out of the chamber.

9 Then I commanded, and they cleansed the chambers: and thither brought I again the vessels of the house of God, with the meat offering and the frankincense.

10 And I perceived that the portions of the Levites had not been given them: for the Levites and the singers, that did the work, were fled every one to his field.

11 Then contended I with the rulers, and said, Why is the house of God forsaken? And I gathered them together, and set them in their place.

12 Then brought all Judah the tithe of the corn and the new wine and the oil unto the treasuries.

13 And I made treasurers over the treasuries, Shelemiah the priest, and Zadok the scribe, and of the Levites, Pedaiah: and next to them was Hanan the son of Zaccur, the son of Mattaniah: for they were counted faithful, and their office was to distribute unto their brethren.

14 Remember me, O my God, concerning this, and wipe not out my good deeds that I have done for the house of my God, and for the offices thereof.

15 In those days saw I in Judah some treading wine presses on the sabbath, and bringing in sheaves, and lading asses; as also wine, grapes, and figs, and all manner of burdens, which they brought into Jerusalem on the sabbath day: and I testified against them in the day wherein they sold victuals.

16 There dwelt men of Tyre also therein, which brought fish, and all manner of ware, and sold on the sabbath unto the children of Judah, and in Jerusalem.

17 Then I contended with the nobles of Judah, and said unto them, What evil thing is this that ye do, and profane the sabbath day?

18 Did not your fathers thus, and did not our God bring all this evil upon us, and upon this city? yet ye bring more wrath upon Israel by profaning the sabbath.

19 And it came to pass, that when the gates of Jerusalem began to be dark before the sabbath, I commanded that the gates should be shut, and charged that they should not be opened till after the sabbath: and some of my servants set I at the gates, that there should no burden be brought in on the sabbath day.

20 So the merchants and sellers of all kind of ware lodged without Jerusalem once or twice.

21 Then I testified against them, and said unto them, Why lodge ye about the wall? if ye do so again, I will lay hands on you. From that time forth came they no more on the sabbath.

22 And I commanded the Levites that they should cleanse themselves, and that they should come and keep the gates, to sanctify the sabbath day. Remember me, O my God, concerning this also, and spare me according to the greatness of thy mercy.

23 In those days also saw I Jews that had married wives of Ashdod, of Ammon, and of Moab:

24 And their children spake half in the speech of Ashdod, and could not speak in the Jews’ language, but according to the language of each people.

25 And I contended with them, and cursed them, and smote certain of them, and plucked off their hair, and made them swear by God, saying, Ye shall not give your daughters unto their sons, nor take their daughters unto your sons, or for yourselves.

26 Did not Solomon king of Israel sin by these things? yet among many nations was there no king like him, who was beloved of his God, and God made him king over all Israel: nevertheless even him did outlandish women cause to sin.

27 Shall we then hearken unto you to do all this great evil, to transgress against our God in marrying strange wives?

28 And one of the sons of Joiada, the son of Eliashib the high priest, was son in law to Sanballat the Horonite: therefore I chased him from me.

29 Remember them, O my God, because they have defiled the priesthood, and the covenant of the priesthood, and of the Levites.

30 Thus cleansed I them from all strangers, and appointed the wards of the priests and the Levites, every one in his business;

31 And for the wood offering, at times appointed, and for the firstfruits. Remember me, O my God, for good.

  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Reading the book of Moses, the people find that Ammonites and Moabites are not to enter the congregation of God, and they separate the mixed multitude. Nehemiah, returning from the king, finds Tobiah lodged in a temple chamber, casts out his stuff, and restores the chambers; he contends with the rulers for the forsaken house of God, restores the tithes, shuts the gates against sabbath trading, and confronts marriages with foreign wives, closing with the prayer, 'Remember me, O my God, for good.' Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Mixed multitude. the strangers separated from Israel after the reading of the law (verse 3). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Wood offering. the offering of wood for the altar, appointed by lot at set times (verse 31). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. On the day of the dedication reading, they found written in the book of Moses that the Ammonite and Moabite should not come into the congregation of God for ever. Henry notes the public reading of the word discovers sin and duty, and profits us when it moves us to separate from the evil we had practised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The reason was that they had not met Israel with bread and water, but hired Balaam against them to curse them, though God turned the curse into a blessing. Henry notes their injury was the greater because Israel had done them no harm and was expressly forbidden to harm them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. When they heard the law, they separated from Israel all the mixed multitude. Henry observes the mixed multitude had of old been a snare, falling a lusting, and these inmates were expelled as usurpers and dangerous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Eliashib the priest, who had oversight of the chamber of God's house, was allied to Tobiah. Henry traces the base alliance: the high priest's grandson had married Sanballat's daughter, and Eliashib counted the alliance an honour, though it was his greatest disgrace and a contempt of his consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Eliashib prepared a great chamber for Tobiah where formerly lay the meat offerings, frankincense, vessels, and tithes of corn, wine, and oil for Levites, singers, and porters, with the priests' offerings. Henry calls it wretched: the temple stores were thrown out to make a state-room for an Ammonite in the courts of God's house, next to setting up an idol there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. All this happened while Nehemiah was not at Jerusalem, for in the thirty-second year of Artaxerxes he had gone to the king, and after certain days obtained leave to return. Henry notes the enemy sows tares when the servants sleep or are absent, as the calf was made while Moses was on the mount. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. When Nehemiah came to Jerusalem and understood the evil Eliashib had done for Tobiah in preparing him a chamber in the courts of God's house, it grieved him sorely. Henry notes nothing grieves a good magistrate more than seeing the ministers of God's house do wicked things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He cast forth all Tobiah's household stuff out of the chamber. Henry compares this to Christ cleansing the temple: those who would expel sin from their hearts must throw out its household stuff and all provision made for it, starving it of food and fuel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. He commanded the chambers to be cleansed, then brought back the vessels of God's house with the meat offering and frankincense. Henry notes the chambers, having been profaned, were first sprinkled with the water of purification, then furnished again for God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Nehemiah perceived the Levites' portions had not been given them, so the Levites and singers had fled each to his field. Henry suggests Tobiah, seizing the store-chambers with Eliashib's connivance, converted the stores, and notes a scandalous maintenance makes a scandalous ministry, since the work is neglected because the workmen are. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He contended with the rulers, asking why the house of God was forsaken, then gathered the Levites and set them in their place. Henry notes both sides failed: the people forsook the Levites, forbidden by the law, and then the Levites forsook their post, while magistrates had not kept either to duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. All Judah brought the tithe of corn, new wine, and oil to the treasuries. Henry observes his zeal provoked theirs: when they saw the Levites at their work, they could not for shame withhold their wages, for the better church-work is done the better church-dues are paid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. He appointed treasurers over the treasuries: Shelemiah the priest, Zadok the scribe, Pedaiah the Levite, and Hanan son of Zaccur son of Mattaniah, counted faithful, with the office of distributing to their brethren. Henry's rule: let men be tried first and then trusted, tried in the less and then trusted with more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He prayed, "Remember me, O my God, concerning this, and wipe not out my good deeds that I have done for the house of my God, and for the offices thereof." Henry notes he asked only to be remembered, not rewarded or published, yet God did more than he asked, recording his deeds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. In Judah Nehemiah saw some treading winepresses on the sabbath, bringing in sheaves, loading asses with wine, grapes, figs, and all burdens into Jerusalem, and he testified against them in the day they sold victuals. Henry notes the law expressly required rest even in harvest time, when temptation to take liberty was greatest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Men of Tyre dwelling there brought fish and all manner of ware and sold them on the sabbath to the children of Judah in Jerusalem. Henry laments that Judah's children had so little grace as to buy, encouraging traders to make the Father's day a day of merchandise against the fourth commandment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Nehemiah contended with the nobles of Judah, asking what evil thing they did in profaning the sabbath day. Henry notes the nobles were guilty by connivance, bearing the sword in vain, and by bad example, since their own sabbath sports and idle visits led businessmen to profane it by worldly employment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. He reasoned that their fathers had done likewise, and God had brought all this evil upon them and the city, yet they brought more wrath on Israel by profaning the sabbath. Henry notes sabbath-breaking was among the sins for which judgments had come, and returning to it invited further judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. He commanded the gates to be shut when it began to be dark before the sabbath, not to be opened till after the sabbath, setting his own servants at the gates so no burden would be brought in on the sabbath day. Henry praises the method: aim at reformation first, using the bit and bridle so the lash is not needed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The merchants and sellers of all kinds of ware lodged outside Jerusalem once or twice. Henry notes they were forced to lodge without the city, no doubt wishing the sabbath were gone so they might sell corn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Nehemiah testified against them, asking why they lodged about the wall, and warning that if they did so again he would lay hands on them, after which they came no more on the sabbath. Henry's lesson: if reformers put on resolution, more may be done to break bad customs than they imagine, for vice connived at grows daring but cowers before magistrates who make themselves a terror to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. He commanded the Levites to cleanse themselves, come and keep the gates, and sanctify the sabbath, praying God to remember him and spare him according to the greatness of his mercy. Henry notes magistrates and ministers joining forces is what makes reformation likely to last, and that the best saints still stand in need of sparing mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Nehemiah saw Jews who had married wives of Ashdod, Ammon, and Moab. Henry notes the corruption had returned after Ezra's reform, since an unclean spirit re-enters when no watchful eye is kept, and men still lean toward what is forbidden, whether from fondness for the far-fetched or hope of strengthening alliances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Their children spoke half in the speech of Ashdod and could not speak the Jews' language, but spoke according to the language of each people's mothers. Henry observes children learn much from their mothers and imitate them, which is why the corrupt side inclines them and why Christians should not be unequally yoked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Nehemiah contended with them, cursed them, smote certain of them, plucked off their hair, and made them swear not to give their daughters to their sons nor take their daughters for their sons or themselves. Henry contrasts Ezra, who plucked his own hair in sorrow, with Nehemiah, who plucked theirs in holy indignation, showing the divers graces of the same Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. He argued from Solomon: did not Solomon king of Israel sin by these things, though no king was like him for wisdom, though he was beloved of God and king over all Israel, yet outlandish women caused him to sin? Henry's warning: the falls of great and good men are recorded so we take heed lest we fall on the same precipice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. He asked whether they should hearken to doing all this great evil, transgressing against God by marrying strange wives. Henry notes he showed the sin in the glass of the commandment, quoting Deuteronomy's precept, and would not connive at an iniquity to be punished by the judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. One of the sons of Joiada son of Eliashib the high priest was son-in-law to Sanballat the Horonite, so Nehemiah chased him away. Henry notes the young priest would not put away his wife, so he was deprived and degraded, and Josephus says he went to Sanballat, who built him a temple on Mount Gerizim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Nehemiah prayed, "Remember them, O my God, because they have defiled the priesthood, and the covenant of the priesthood, and of the Levites." Henry reads the prayer two ways: that God would convince and convert them, or that he would reckon with them, since those who defile the priesthood despise God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. He cleansed them from all strangers and appointed the wards of the priests and Levites, each in his business. Henry notes it was no loss to part with the scandal of the cloth, and the work would be done better without him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. He also appointed the wood offering at appointed times and the firstfruits, closing with prayer: "Remember me, O my God, for good." Henry notes the best public services are sometimes forgotten by men, so Nehemiah took God for his paymaster, and this summary of petitions suffices for happiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
