The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Saul spake to Jonathan his son, and to all his servants, that they should kill David.

2 But Jonathan Saul’s son delighted much in David: and Jonathan told David, saying, Saul my father seeketh to kill thee: now therefore, I pray thee, take heed to thyself until the morning, and abide in a secret place, and hide thyself:

3 And I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where thou art, and I will commune with my father of thee; and what I see, that I will tell thee.

4 And Jonathan spake good of David unto Saul his father, and said unto him, Let not the king sin against his servant, against David; because he hath not sinned against thee, and because his works have been to thee-ward very good:

5 For he did put his life in his hand, and slew the Philistine, and the LORD wrought a great salvation for all Israel: thou sawest it, and didst rejoice: wherefore then wilt thou sin against innocent blood, to slay David without a cause?

6 And Saul hearkened unto the voice of Jonathan: and Saul sware, As the LORD liveth, he shall not be slain.

7 And Jonathan called David, and Jonathan shewed him all those things. And Jonathan brought David to Saul, and he was in his presence, as in times past.

8 And there was war again: and David went out, and fought with the Philistines, and slew them with a great slaughter; and they fled from him.

9 And the evil spirit from the LORD was upon Saul, as he sat in his house with his javelin in his hand: and David played with his hand.

10 And Saul sought to smite David even to the wall with the javelin: but he slipped away out of Saul’s presence, and he smote the javelin into the wall: and David fled, and escaped that night.

11 Saul also sent messengers unto David’s house, to watch him, and to slay him in the morning: and Michal David’s wife told him, saying, If thou save not thy life to night, to morrow thou shalt be slain.

12 So Michal let David down through a window: and he went, and fled, and escaped.

13 And Michal took an image, and laid it in the bed, and put a pillow of goats’ hair for his bolster, and covered it with a cloth.

14 And when Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, He is sick.

15 And Saul sent the messengers again to see David, saying, Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may slay him.

16 And when the messengers were come in, behold, there was an image in the bed, with a pillow of goats’ hair for his bolster.

17 And Saul said unto Michal, Why hast thou deceived me so, and sent away mine enemy, that he is escaped? And Michal answered Saul, He said unto me, Let me go; why should I kill thee?

18 So David fled, and escaped, and came to Samuel to Ramah, and told him all that Saul had done to him. And he and Samuel went and dwelt in Naioth.

19 And it was told Saul, saying, Behold, David is at Naioth in Ramah.

20 And Saul sent messengers to take David: and when they saw the company of the prophets prophesying, and Samuel standing as appointed over them, the Spirit of God was upon the messengers of Saul, and they also prophesied.

21 And when it was told Saul, he sent other messengers, and they prophesied likewise. And Saul sent messengers again the third time, and they prophesied also.

22 Then went he also to Ramah, and came to a great well that is in Sechu: and he asked and said, Where are Samuel and David? And one said, Behold, they be at Naioth in Ramah.

23 And he went thither to Naioth in Ramah: and the Spirit of God was upon him also, and he went on, and prophesied, until he came to Naioth in Ramah.

24 And he stripped off his clothes also, and prophesied before Samuel in like manner, and lay down naked all that day and all that night. Wherefore they say, Is Saul also among the prophets?

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Saul speaks openly to Jonathan and all his servants that they should kill David, and Jonathan, who delights much in David, warns him, pleads his cause in the field, recalling how David put his life in his hand and slew the Philistine, and wins Saul's oath that David shall not be slain, so that David is for a season in Saul's presence as in times past. War comes again, David wins a great slaughter, the evil spirit returns, and Saul seeks to smite David to the wall with his javelin; David slips away that night, and Michal lets him down through a window, lays an image in the bed with a pillow of goats' hair, and answers the messengers, He is sick, then, pressed, that he threatened her. David flees to Samuel at Naioth in Ramah, and company after company of Saul's messengers, and at last Saul himself, are overtaken by the Spirit and prophesy instead of seizing him, until Saul lies down naked all that day and night and men say again, Is Saul also among the prophets. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Saul spake to Jonathan his son and to all his servants, that they should kill David. The king's envy, once private, became a standing order to his whole court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. But Jonathan delighted much in David, and told him, Saul my father seeketh to kill thee; take heed until the morning, abide in a secret place, and hide thyself. The covenant friend became the warning friend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where thou art, and I will commune with my father of thee, and what I see, that I will tell thee. Jonathan would plead David's cause in person before reporting back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Jonathan spake good of David unto Saul, saying, Let not the king sin against his servant David, because he hath not sinned against thee, and his works have been to thee-ward very good. How forcible are right words, Henry observes — Saul was for a time convinced of the unreasonableness of his enmity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. For he did put his life in his hand, and slew the Philistine, and the LORD wrought a great salvation for all Israel; thou sawest it and didst rejoice; wherefore then wilt thou sin against innocent blood, to slay David without a cause? Jonathan's argument recalled Saul's own joy at the victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Saul hearkened unto Jonathan's voice, and sware, As the LORD liveth, he shall not be slain. The oath gave David a reprieve, though not a lasting peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Jonathan called David, showed him all those things, and brought David to Saul, and he was in his presence as in times past. The reconciliation restored David to court, for a season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. There was war again, and David went out, fought the Philistines, slew them with a great slaughter, and they fled from him. Fresh victory renewed the old pattern: triumph, then trouble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The evil spirit from the LORD was upon Saul as he sat in his house with his javelin in his hand, and David played with his hand. The harpist served the king even as the king's hand closed on the javelin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Saul sought to smite David even to the wall with the javelin, but David slipped away out of his presence, and the javelin smote the wall, and David fled and escaped that night. But Saul continued his malice against David, Henry notes; so incurable is the hatred of the serpent's seed, and so deceitful and desperately wicked is the heart of man without God's grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Saul also sent messengers to David's house, to watch him and slay him in the morning, and Michal his wife told him, If thou save not thy life tonight, tomorrow thou shalt be slain. The king's daughter warned the husband her father hunted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. So Michal let David down through a window, and he went and fled and escaped. The escape was by night, through a window, like a fugitive's flight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Michal took an image, laid it in the bed, put a pillow of goats' hair for his bolster, and covered it with a cloth. The household image in the bed kept the watchers deceived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. When Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, He is sick. Michal's stratagem gained time for David to get to a distance, Henry notes, and was allowable in its purpose, but her falsehood had not even the plea of necessity to excuse it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Saul sent the messengers again to see David, saying, Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may slay him. The king would kill even a sick man in his bed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. When the messengers came in, behold, there was an image in the bed, with a pillow of goats' hair for his bolster. The deception was exposed, and the prey was gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Saul said to Michal, Why hast thou deceived me so, and sent away mine enemy, that he is escaped? And Michal answered, He said unto me, Let me go; why should I kill thee? Her falsehood, Henry observes, manifests that she was not influenced by the same spirit of piety which had dictated Jonathan's language to Saul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. So David fled and escaped, and came to Samuel to Ramah, and told him all Saul had done to him, and he and Samuel dwelt in Naioth. In flying to Samuel, Henry says, David made God his refuge; Samuel as prophet was best able to advise him in this day of distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. It was told Saul, Behold, David is at Naioth in Ramah. The fugitive's refuge was soon reported to the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Saul sent messengers to take David, and when they saw the company of prophets prophesying, with Samuel standing as appointed over them, the Spirit of God was upon Saul's messengers, and they also prophesied. The arresting party was arrested by the Spirit instead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. When it was told Saul, he sent other messengers, and they prophesied likewise, and Saul sent messengers a third time, and they prophesied also. Three companies in succession were turned from hunters into prophets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Then Saul himself went to Ramah, and came to a great well in Sechu, and asked, Where are Samuel and David? And one said, Behold, they be at Naioth in Ramah. So impatient was Saul after David's blood, Henry notes, so restless against him, that although baffled by one providence after another, he could not see that David was under God's special protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. He went to Naioth in Ramah, and the Spirit of God was upon him also, and he went on and prophesied until he came to Naioth. The hunter himself was overtaken by the Spirit on the road. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. He stripped off his clothes also, and prophesied before Samuel, and lay down naked all that day and night. Wherefore they say, Is Saul also among the prophets? When God will protect David this way, Henry observes, even Saul prophesies; many have great gifts yet no grace, and may prophesy in Christ's name yet be disowned by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
