The Text — Judges 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the children of Israel did evil again in the sight of the LORD; and the LORD delivered them into the hand of the Philistines forty years.

2 And there was a certain man of Zorah, of the family of the Danites, whose name was Manoah; and his wife was barren, and bare not.

3 And the angel of the LORD appeared unto the woman, and said unto her, Behold now, thou art barren, and bearest not: but thou shalt conceive, and bear a son.

4 Now therefore beware, I pray thee, and drink not wine nor strong drink, and eat not any unclean thing:

5 For, lo, thou shalt conceive, and bear a son; and no razor shall come on his head: for the child shall be a Nazarite unto God from the womb: and he shall begin to deliver Israel out of the hand of the Philistines.

6 Then the woman came and told her husband, saying, A man of God came unto me, and his countenance was like the countenance of an angel of God, very terrible: but I asked him not whence he was, neither told he me his name:

7 But he said unto me, Behold, thou shalt conceive, and bear a son; and now drink no wine nor strong drink, neither eat any unclean thing: for the child shall be a Nazarite to God from the womb to the day of his death.

8 Then Manoah intreated the LORD, and said, O my Lord, let the man of God which thou didst send come again unto us, and teach us what we shall do unto the child that shall be born.

9 And God hearkened to the voice of Manoah; and the angel of God came again unto the woman as she sat in the field: but Manoah her husband was not with her.

10 And the woman made haste, and ran, and shewed her husband, and said unto him, Behold, the man hath appeared unto me, that came unto me the other day.

11 And Manoah arose, and went after his wife, and came to the man, and said unto him, Art thou the man that spakest unto the woman? And he said, I am.

12 And Manoah said, Now let thy words come to pass. How shall we order the child, and how shall we do unto him?

13 And the angel of the LORD said unto Manoah, Of all that I said unto the woman let her beware.

14 She may not eat of any thing that cometh of the vine, neither let her drink wine or strong drink, nor eat any unclean thing: all that I commanded her let her observe.

15 And Manoah said unto the angel of the LORD, I pray thee, let us detain thee, until we shall have made ready a kid for thee.

16 And the angel of the LORD said unto Manoah, Though thou detain me, I will not eat of thy bread: and if thou wilt offer a burnt offering, thou must offer it unto the LORD. For Manoah knew not that he was an angel of the LORD.

17 And Manoah said unto the angel of the LORD, What is thy name, that when thy sayings come to pass we may do thee honour?

18 And the angel of the LORD said unto him, Why askest thou thus after my name, seeing it is secret?

19 So Manoah took a kid with a meat offering, and offered it upon a rock unto the LORD: and the angel did wonderously; and Manoah and his wife looked on.

20 For it came to pass, when the flame went up toward heaven from off the altar, that the angel of the LORD ascended in the flame of the altar. And Manoah and his wife looked on it, and fell on their faces to the ground.

21 But the angel of the LORD did no more appear to Manoah and to his wife. Then Manoah knew that he was an angel of the LORD.

22 And Manoah said unto his wife, We shall surely die, because we have seen God.

23 But his wife said unto him, If the LORD were pleased to kill us, he would not have received a burnt offering and a meat offering at our hands, neither would he have shewed us all these things, nor would as at this time have told us such things as these.

24 And the woman bare a son, and called his name Samson: and the child grew, and the LORD blessed him.

25 And the Spirit of the LORD began to move him at times in the camp of Dan between Zorah and Eshtaol.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Israel again does evil and is delivered to the Philistines for forty years. At Zorah, the angel of the LORD appears to Manoah's barren wife and promises a son who shall be a Nazarite from the womb, his mother keeping the same abstinence, no razor ever coming on his head, and he only beginning to deliver Israel. Manoah prays for a second visit, asks the visitor's name and is told it is secret, or wonderful; the angel ascends in the flame of the offering, and Samson is born and blessed, the Spirit beginning to move him in the camp of Dan. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Israel again did evil, and the Lord delivered them to the Philistines forty years, and JFB notes this Philistine ascendency, reckoned from Elon's time to Samson's death, chastised the gross and confirmed idolatry described in chapter 10. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. There was a man of Zorah, of the Danites, named Manoah, whose wife was barren, and JFB notes Zorah lay on Judah and Dan's common boundary, near the Philistine border, where the deliverer was most needed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The angel of the Lord appeared to the woman and promised a son, and JFB identifies this messenger as the messenger of the covenant, the divine personage of so many earlier appearances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. She was told to drink no wine or strong drink and eat no unclean thing, since the mother must practise the Nazarite abstinence for the sake of her promised offspring. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. She would conceive and bear a son, no razor coming on his head, for the child would be a Nazarite from the womb, and JFB notes he would only begin to deliver Israel, indicating the deliverance was still distant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The woman told her husband a man of God had come, whose countenance was like an angel's, very terrible, and she had not asked whence he was nor had he told his name, and JFB notes Manoah on hearing this made the promise a subject of earnest prayer, a remarkable instance of the connection between prayer and the fulfilment of promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. She repeated the angel's words, that she would bear a son who would be a Nazarite to God from the womb to his death, fixing the lifelong character of the promised child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Manoah entreated the Lord that the man of God would come again and teach them what to do for the child, and JFB traces this desire not to doubt but to lively faith and anxiety to follow the instructions exactly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. God hearkened to Manoah, and the angel came again to the woman as she sat in the field, though Manoah was not with her, so that the answer came to the same person who had first received the promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The woman made haste and ran to tell her husband that the man who had come before had appeared again, carrying the second visit to the husband who had prayed for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Manoah arose, followed his wife, and asked the man whether he was the one who had spoken to the woman, and JFB notes his intense desire for the repetition was the fruit of faith, blessed was he who had not seen, yet had believed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Manoah asked how they should order the child and what they should do to him, seeking the practical rule for a Nazarite's upbringing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The angel answered that of all he had said to the woman she must beware, referring Manoah back to the original charge rather than adding a new one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. She might eat nothing of the vine, drink no wine or strong drink, eat no unclean thing, and observe all that was commanded, the Nazarite discipline restated in full. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Manoah asked the angel to stay until they had made ready a kid, but the stranger declined hospitality, saying if it were an offering it must be presented to the Lord, and JFB notes Manoah had meant to offer the viands to him as a prophet or human messenger, not yet knowing him as an angel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The angel added that though Manoah detained him he would not eat his bread, but if he offered a burnt offering he must offer it to the Lord, for Manoah did not know he was an angel of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Manoah asked the angel's name, so that they might honour him when his sayings came to pass, and JFB notes the request drew unequivocal proofs of divinity, in the name secret, or wonderful, and in the miraculous flame of the accepted sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The angel asked why Manoah asked after his name, seeing it is secret, or wonderful, and the unanswerable name itself became the proof Manoah sought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Manoah took a kid and a meat offering and offered it on a rock to the Lord, and the angel did wondrously while Manoah and his wife looked on, the offering becoming the occasion of revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. As the flame went up from the altar toward heaven, the angel ascended in the flame, and Manoah and his wife fell on their faces to the ground, the miraculous flame betokening acceptance and revealing the visitor's nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The angel did no more appear, and then Manoah knew he was an angel of the Lord, the departure itself completing the recognition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Manoah said they would surely die because they had seen God, expressing the ancient fear that a vision of the divine brings death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. His wife answered that if the Lord were pleased to kill them he would not have received their offering, nor shown them all these things, nor told them such things as these, and her calm reasoning answered his fear with the logic of grace received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The woman bore a son and called his name Samson, and the child grew and the Lord blessed him, and JFB notes the child of promise, with his national services foretold, must from the first have been an object of peculiar interest and careful instruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The Spirit of the Lord began to move Samson at times in the camp of Dan between Zorah and Eshtaol, and JFB explains this not as prophetic inspiration but as a spirit of high and devoted patriotism kindled in his youthful bosom, at places on the Judah-Dan border. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words in this chapter that a modern reader may stumble on. They explain the chapter text; they are not Scripture.

  • Nazarite. one consecrated to God from the womb, marked here by no razor on the head and by abstinence from wine, strong drink and unclean things Interpreted Editorial gloss, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes
  • meat offering. an offering of flour and food, here a kid with its offering, presented on the rock Interpreted Editorial gloss, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes
