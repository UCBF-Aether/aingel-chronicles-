The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 12 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Help, LORD; for the godly man ceaseth; for the faithful fail from among the children of men.

2 They speak vanity every one with his neighbour: with flattering lips and with a double heart do they speak.

3 The LORD shall cut off all flattering lips, and the tongue that speaketh proud things:

4 Who have said, With our tongue will we prevail; our lips are our own: who is lord over us?

5 For the oppression of the poor, for the sighing of the needy, now will I arise, saith the LORD; I will set him in safety from him that puffeth at him.

6 The words of the LORD are pure words: as silver tried in a furnace of earth, purified seven times.

7 Thou shalt keep them, O LORD, thou shalt preserve them from this generation for ever.

8 The wicked walk on every side, when the vilest men are exalted.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Lament over flattering, double-hearted speech at a time when the godly seem to fail. God will cut off proud tongues, arise for the oppressed poor, and his pure, seven-times-tried words, matched by preserving care, stand against a generation in which the vilest are exalted. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Help, Lord, for the godly cease and the faithful fail among men: David laments a time when true religion seems almost extinct. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Neighbours speak vanity, with flattering lips and a double heart: smooth speech hides divided, deceitful intentions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The Lord shall cut off flattering lips and the tongue that speaks proud things: God will silence speech used as a weapon of pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They boast that with their tongue they will prevail, their lips are their own, and no one is lord over them: the claim to absolute freedom of speech is rebellion against God's lordship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For the oppression of the poor and the sighing of the needy, now God will arise and set the needy in safety from him that puffs at him: the cry of the oppressed moves God to action. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The words of the Lord are pure, like silver tried in a furnace of earth, purified seven times: unlike flattering human words, God's words are wholly without dross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God shall keep his people and preserve them from this generation for ever: his pure word is matched by his preserving care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The wicked walk on every side when the vilest men are exalted: when base men rise to honour, evil grows bold everywhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
