The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Amos 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Hear ye this word which I take up against you, even a lamentation, O house of Israel.

2 The virgin of Israel is fallen; she shall no more rise: she is forsaken upon her land; there is none to raise her up.

3 For thus saith the Lord GOD; The city that went out by a thousand shall leave an hundred, and that which went forth by an hundred shall leave ten, to the house of Israel.

4 For thus saith the LORD unto the house of Israel, Seek ye me, and ye shall live:

5 But seek not Bethel, nor enter into Gilgal, and pass not to Beersheba: for Gilgal shall surely go into captivity, and Bethel shall come to nought.

6 Seek the LORD, and ye shall live; lest he break out like fire in the house of Joseph, and devour it, and there be none to quench it in Bethel.

7 Ye who turn judgment to wormwood, and leave off righteousness in the earth,

8 Seek him that maketh the seven stars and Orion, and turneth the shadow of death into the morning, and maketh the day dark with night: that calleth for the waters of the sea, and poureth them out upon the face of the earth: The LORD is his name:

9 That strengtheneth the spoiled against the strong, so that the spoiled shall come against the fortress.

10 They hate him that rebuketh in the gate, and they abhor him that speaketh uprightly.

11 Forasmuch therefore as your treading is upon the poor, and ye take from him burdens of wheat: ye have built houses of hewn stone, but ye shall not dwell in them; ye have planted pleasant vineyards, but ye shall not drink wine of them.

12 For I know your manifold transgressions and your mighty sins: they afflict the just, they take a bribe, and they turn aside the poor in the gate from their right.

13 Therefore the prudent shall keep silence in that time; for it is an evil time.

14 Seek good, and not evil, that ye may live: and so the LORD, the God of hosts, shall be with you, as ye have spoken.

15 Hate the evil, and love the good, and establish judgment in the gate: it may be that the LORD God of hosts will be gracious unto the remnant of Joseph.

16 Therefore the LORD, the God of hosts, the LORD, saith thus; Wailing shall be in all streets; and they shall say in all the highways, Alas! alas! and they shall call the husbandman to mourning, and such as are skilful of lamentation to wailing.

17 And in all vineyards shall be wailing: for I will pass through thee, saith the LORD.

18 Woe unto you that desire the day of the LORD! to what end is it for you? the day of the LORD is darkness, and not light.

19 As if a man did flee from a lion, and a bear met him; or went into the house, and leaned his hand on the wall, and a serpent bit him.

20 Shall not the day of the LORD be darkness, and not light? even very dark, and no brightness in it?

21 I hate, I despise your feast days, and I will not smell in your solemn assemblies.

22 Though ye offer me burnt offerings and your meat offerings, I will not accept them: neither will I regard the peace offerings of your fat beasts.

23 Take thou away from me the noise of thy songs; for I will not hear the melody of thy viols.

24 But let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream.

25 Have ye offered unto me sacrifices and offerings in the wilderness forty years, O house of Israel?

26 But ye have borne the tabernacle of your Moloch and Chiun your images, the star of your god, which ye made to yourselves.

27 Therefore will I cause you to go into captivity beyond Damascus, saith the LORD, whose name is The God of hosts.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A lamentation is taken up over the virgin of Israel, fallen and forsaken with none to raise her up. Seek the LORD and live, not Bethel, Gilgal, or Beersheba; seek good and not evil, hate evil and love good, for the day of the LORD will be darkness and not light to those who trust in feasts and songs while justice is trampled. God says he hates their feast days and will take away their songs, but let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 1. Hear this word which the prophet takes up against them, even a lamentation, O house of Israel, the convincing, awakening word needing to be heard as well as words of comfort, since whether heard or not, God's word shall take effect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The virgin of Israel is fallen, she shall no more rise, forsaken upon her land with none to raise her up, the nation pictured as a fallen virgin abandoned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. For thus says the Lord God: the city that went out by a thousand shall leave a hundred, and that by a hundred shall leave ten, to the house of Israel, a remnant of a remnant surviving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. For thus says the Lord to Israel: seek me, and you shall live, the Lord still proclaiming mercy, though men often expect deliverance from self-invented forms that make condemnation sure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. But seek not Bethel, nor enter Gilgal, nor pass to Beersheba, for Gilgal shall surely go into captivity and Bethel shall come to nought, the idolatrous sanctuaries themselves doomed, unable to protect their worshippers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Seek the Lord, and you shall live, lest he break out like fire in Joseph's house and devour it, with none to quench it in Bethel, since while they refuse to come to Christ for mercy, divine wrath breaks forth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. You who turn judgment to wormwood and leave off righteousness in the earth are addressed next, justice itself made bitter by corruption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Seek him who makes the seven stars and Orion, turns death's shadow into morning, darkens day with night, calls the sea's waters and pours them on the earth: the Lord is his name, the same almighty power able to turn affliction into joy for the repentant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. He strengthens the spoiled against the strong, so the spoiled shall come against the fortress, God's power reversing human odds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They hate him who rebukes in the gate and abhor him who speaks uprightly, evil times not bearing plain dealing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Forasmuch as their treading is upon the poor and they take from him burdens of wheat, though they built houses of hewn stone they shall not dwell in them, and though they planted pleasant vineyards they shall not drink their wine, gains from oppression not enjoyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. For God knows their manifold transgressions and mighty sins: they afflict the just, take a bribe, and turn aside the poor in the gate from his right, so that judgments that often passed by them shall now pass through them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Therefore the prudent shall keep silence in that time, for it is an evil time, wise and good men seeing it vain even to speak to such evil men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Seek good and not evil, that you may live, and so the Lord God of hosts shall be with you as you have spoken, those who seek and love good helping to save a land from ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Hate the evil, love the good, and establish judgment in the gate, that it may be the Lord God of hosts will be gracious to Joseph's remnant, piety and duty attended to by souls that seek God, who is ever ready to be gracious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Therefore the Lord God of hosts says: wailing shall be in all streets, and in highways they shall say, alas, alas, calling the husbandman to mourning and skillful lamenters to wailing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And in all vineyards shall be wailing, for God will pass through them, says the Lord, judgment reaching every place of work and joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Woe to you who desire the day of the Lord, to what end is it for you, the day of the Lord being darkness and not light, woe to those who long for war and confusion hoping to rise on their country's ruins, since the desolation will profit nobody. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. As if a man fled from a lion and a bear met him, or went into a house, leaned his hand on the wall, and a serpent bit him, so judgments pursue those not reformed by them, escaping one only to meet another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Shall not the day of the Lord be darkness and not light, even very dark with no brightness in it, since when God makes a day dark, all the world cannot make it light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. God hates and despises their feast days and will not smell their solemn assemblies, a pretense of piety being double iniquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Though they offer burnt offerings and meat offerings, God will not accept them, nor regard the peace offerings of fat beasts, sacrifices without communion with God thriving little. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Take away from God the noise of their songs, for he will not hear the melody of their viols, worship's music rejected when the heart is idolatrous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. But let judgment run down as waters and righteousness as a mighty stream, the demand being justice and righteousness in life, not ritual noise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Have they offered God sacrifices and offerings in the wilderness forty years, O house of Israel, the question exposing that their worship was never truly his alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. But they bore the tabernacle of Moloch and Chiun their images, the star of their god, which they made for themselves, copying their forefathers' crimes and led captive by Satan into idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Therefore God will cause them to go into captivity beyond Damascus, says the Lord, whose name is the God of hosts, captivity among idolaters being the fit end of idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • The day of the LORD. the day of God's visitation and judgment, pictured in the prophets as darkness, trumpet alarm, and distress, and also as deliverance for those who call on his name. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
