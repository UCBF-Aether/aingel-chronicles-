The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Peter 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Forasmuch then as Christ hath suffered for us in the flesh, arm yourselves likewise with the same mind: for he that hath suffered in the flesh hath ceased from sin;

2 That he no longer should live the rest of his time in the flesh to the lusts of men, but to the will of God.

3 For the time past of our life may suffice us to have wrought the will of the Gentiles, when we walked in lasciviousness, lusts, excess of wine, revellings, banquetings, and abominable idolatries:

4 Wherein they think it strange that ye run not with them to the same excess of riot, speaking evil of you:

5 Who shall give account to him that is ready to judge the quick and the dead.

6 For for this cause was the gospel preached also to them that are dead, that they might be judged according to men in the flesh, but live according to God in the spirit.

7 But the end of all things is at hand: be ye therefore sober, and watch unto prayer.

8 And above all things have fervent charity among yourselves: for charity shall cover the multitude of sins.

9 Use hospitality one to another without grudging.

10 As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God.

11 If any man speak, let him speak as the oracles of God; if any man minister, let him do it as of the ability which God giveth: that God in all things may be glorified through Jesus Christ, to whom be praise and dominion for ever and ever. Amen.

12 Beloved, think it not strange concerning the fiery trial which is to try you, as though some strange thing happened unto you:

13 But rejoice, inasmuch as ye are partakers of Christ’s sufferings; that, when his glory shall be revealed, ye may be glad also with exceeding joy.

14 If ye be reproached for the name of Christ, happy are ye; for the spirit of glory and of God resteth upon you: on their part he is evil spoken of, but on your part he is glorified.

15 But let none of you suffer as a murderer, or as a thief, or as an evildoer, or as a busybody in other men’s matters.

16 Yet if any man suffer as a Christian, let him not be ashamed; but let him glorify God on this behalf.

17 For the time is come that judgment must begin at the house of God: and if it first begin at us, what shall the end be of them that obey not the gospel of God?

18 And if the righteous scarcely be saved, where shall the ungodly and the sinner appear?

19 Wherefore let them that suffer according to the will of God commit the keeping of their souls to him in well doing, as unto a faithful Creator.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Since Christ suffered in the flesh, arm yourselves with the same mind, Peter says: the past life of excess is enough, and former companions will think it strange that readers no longer run with them. The end of all things is at hand, so be sober for prayer, keep fervent love, offer hospitality without grudging, and steward every gift as from God. Suffering as a Christian is no shame, and judgment beginning at God's house calls for committing souls to a faithful Creator in doing good. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Since Christ suffered for us in the flesh, arm yourselves with the same mind, for whoever has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin. The strongest arguments against sin are taken from Christ's sufferings: he died to destroy sin, submitted to the worst sufferings, yet never gave way to the least sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The aim is to live the rest of one's time in the flesh no longer to the lusts of people but to the will of God. True Christians make God's will, not their own lust or desires, the rule of their lives and actions, and conversion alters mind, judgment, affections, and conversation together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The time past may suffice to have worked the will of the Gentiles, walking in lasciviousness, lusts, excess of wine, revellings, banquetings, and abominable idolatries. When a person is truly converted, it is grievous to think how the past time of life was spent, since one sin draws on another, and six sins are here listed depending on one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Former companions think it strange that believers do not run with them to the same excess of riot, and speak evil of them. The world's scorn at a changed life is expected, and believers are not to care though the world reproaches them for refusing its excesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Those who speak evil will give account to him who is ready to judge the living and the dead. The judgment awaiting scoffers is the sober answer to their scorn, and it belongs to Christ, not to the believer's retaliation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. For this cause the gospel was preached also to those who are dead, that they might be judged according to people in the flesh but live according to God in the spirit. Henry explains these as people since dead who, by proud and carnal judgment, were condemned as evildoers and some even suffered death, yet being quickened to divine life by the Spirit they lived to God as his devoted servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The end of all things is at hand, so be sober and watchful unto prayer. The destruction of the Jewish church and nation was very near, and the speedy approach of death and judgment concerns everyone, making sobriety in worldly matters and earnestness in religion a powerful argument. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Above all things have fervent charity among yourselves, for charity covers a multitude of sins. Unless love covers, excuses, and forgives in others the mistakes and faults for which everyone needs forbearance, Satan will stir up divisions, though charity does not itself make amends for the sins of those who exercise it before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Use hospitality to one another without grudging. Hospitality is the practical, ungrudging form of the fervent charity just commanded, and it must be offered freely rather than with complaint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. As everyone has received a gift, so minister the same to one another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God. Christian work is high work and hard work, and the goodness of the Master and the excellence of the reward require serious and earnest endeavour in stewardship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. If anyone speaks, let him speak as the oracles of God, and if anyone ministers, let him do it as of the ability God gives, that God in all things may be glorified through Jesus Christ, to whom be praise and dominion forever. In every duty and service, God's glory is to be the chief end, and the person who gives himself wholly to God may say confidently the Lord is his portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Beloved, do not think it strange concerning the fiery trial which tries you, as though some strange thing happened to you. Suffering for Christ is not an anomaly in the Christian life, and even the best people need warning against the worst sins, while their enemies falsely charge them with foul crimes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Rejoice, inasmuch as you are partakers of Christ's sufferings, that when his glory is revealed you may be glad also with exceeding joy. By patience and fortitude in suffering, dependence on God's promises, and keeping to the word the Spirit has revealed, the Spirit is glorified, and present sharing in suffering prepares for future sharing in glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. If you are reproached for the name of Christ, happy are you, for the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you: on their part he is evil spoken of, but on your part he is glorified. Reproach for Christ's name, borne patiently, becomes a mark of the Spirit's presence rather than of shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Let none of you suffer as a murderer, thief, evildoer, or busybody in other people's matters. There is no comfort in sufferings brought on by one's own sin and folly, and even the best need warning against the worst sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Yet if anyone suffers as a Christian, let him not be ashamed, but let him glorify God on this behalf. Suffering under the name Christian, as distinct from suffering for crime, is a cause for glorifying God rather than shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The time has come for judgment to begin at the house of God, and if it first begins with us, what shall the end be of those who do not obey the gospel of God? A time of universal calamity was at hand, and if such things befall believers in this life, how awful will the day of judgment be for the disobedient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. If the righteous are scarcely saved, where shall the ungodly and the sinner appear? This does not mean God's purpose is uncertain, but only that the way is hard, with temptations, tribulations, fightings without and fears within, and lusts and corruptions within as the worst clogs; if the righteous way is so hard, how hard shall the end of the ungodly be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Therefore let those who suffer according to God's will commit the keeping of their souls to him in well-doing, as to a faithful Creator. The only way to keep the soul well is to commit it to God by prayer and patient perseverance in well-doing, trusting he will overrule all to the believer's final advantage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
