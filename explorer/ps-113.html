The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 113 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Praise ye the LORD. Praise, O ye servants of the LORD, praise the name of the LORD.

2 Blessed be the name of the LORD from this time forth and for evermore.

3 From the rising of the sun unto the going down of the same the LORD’s name is to be praised.

4 The LORD is high above all nations, and his glory above the heavens.

5 Who is like unto the LORD our God, who dwelleth on high,

6 Who humbleth himself to behold the things that are in heaven, and in the earth!

7 He raiseth up the poor out of the dust, and lifteth the needy out of the dunghill;

8 That he may set him with princes, even with the princes of his people.

9 He maketh the barren woman to keep house, and to be a joyful mother of children. Praise ye the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A call to the servants of the LORD to praise his name from this time forth and for evermore, from the rising of the sun to its going down. The LORD is high above all nations, his glory above the heavens, and none is like him, who dwells on high yet humbles himself to behold things in heaven and earth. The psalm's picture of that condescension is concrete: he raises the poor out of the dust and lifts the needy out of the dunghill to set them with princes, and makes the barren woman keep house as a joyful mother of children. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry stresses the pressing, repeated call: praise is a necessary and excellent duty, pleasing to God, yet one we are backward to, so precept is added to precept — and those who taste its pleasure court others to share the work, for there is employment for all hearts and hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry reads the blessing of the name as without end: not to die with us, but to run through all generations of time and on into the better world, each age taking up what the last laid down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry takes sunrise to sunset as the whole habitable world: God's name ought to be praised in every place where his wisdom, power and goodness appear, and he glimpses here the gospel age when the church spreads beyond Israel to all countries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry exalts God above every nation and its pomp: put all nations together and he is above them all, as the drop of the bucket, and his glory sits above the heavens themselves, above the angels who serve under his command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry turns the question into holy admiration: no prince, potentate or blessed spirit can equal or compare with him, for he dwells on high, sees all, rules all, and justly draws all praise to himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry marvels that the God who needs nothing humbles himself to behold heaven and earth — to support angels, direct their motions and accept their praise, and much more to visit men and order their affairs; greatest of all, the Son came from heaven to earth to seek and save the lost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry sees God magnifying his own wisdom by raising the despicable on a sudden: Gideon from threshing, Saul from seeking asses, David from sheep, apostles from fishing — and Joseph from prison-dust — so that the excellency of the power is seen to be of God, and in Christ poor fallen men are lifted from the dunghill of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry reads the seating with princes as promotion that comes from God alone, sometimes rewarding long-tried piety and patience under poverty and disgrace, and wisely observed as a return of providence showing the loving-kindness of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry recalls Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, Hannah and Elizabeth: God looks on the affliction of his handmaids and takes away reproach, giving the long-childless particular joy and particular thanksgiving — yet rejoice with trembling — and applies the figure to the Gentile gospel church, children of the desolate, who have reason to say, Praise ye the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
