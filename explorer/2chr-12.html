The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, when Rehoboam had established the kingdom, and had strengthened himself, he forsook the law of the LORD, and all Israel with him.

2 And it came to pass, that in the fifth year of king Rehoboam Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, because they had transgressed against the LORD,

3 With twelve hundred chariots, and threescore thousand horsemen: and the people were without number that came with him out of Egypt; the Lubims, the Sukkiims, and the Ethiopians.

4 And he took the fenced cities which pertained to Judah, and came to Jerusalem.

5 Then came Shemaiah the prophet to Rehoboam, and to the princes of Judah, that were gathered together to Jerusalem because of Shishak, and said unto them, Thus saith the LORD, Ye have forsaken me, and therefore have I also left you in the hand of Shishak.

6 Whereupon the princes of Israel and the king humbled themselves; and they said, The LORD is righteous.

7 And when the LORD saw that they humbled themselves, the word of the LORD came to Shemaiah, saying, They have humbled themselves; therefore I will not destroy them, but I will grant them some deliverance; and my wrath shall not be poured out upon Jerusalem by the hand of Shishak.

8 Nevertheless they shall be his servants; that they may know my service, and the service of the kingdoms of the countries.

9 So Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, and took away the treasures of the house of the LORD, and the treasures of the king’s house; he took all: he carried away also the shields of gold which Solomon had made.

10 Instead of which king Rehoboam made shields of brass, and committed them to the hands of the chief of the guard, that kept the entrance of the king’s house.

11 And when the king entered into the house of the LORD, the guard came and fetched them, and brought them again into the guard chamber.

12 And when he humbled himself, the wrath of the LORD turned from him, that he would not destroy him altogether: and also in Judah things went well.

13 So king Rehoboam strengthened himself in Jerusalem, and reigned: for Rehoboam was one and forty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city which the LORD had chosen out of all the tribes of Israel, to put his name there. And his mother’s name was Naamah an Ammonitess.

14 And he did evil, because he prepared not his heart to seek the LORD.

15 Now the acts of Rehoboam, first and last, are they not written in the book of Shemaiah the prophet, and of Iddo the seer concerning genealogies? And there were wars between Rehoboam and Jeroboam continually.

16 And Rehoboam slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David: and Abijah his son reigned in his stead.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Rehoboam's forsaking of God's law once established, Shishak of Egypt's invasion and plundering of Jerusalem, the king's humbling, and his death, with Abijah succeeding him. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And it came to pass, when Rehoboam had established the kingdom and strengthened himself, he forsook the law of the Lord, and all Israel with him. Henry notes his happy three years walking in David and Solomon's way expired, and he grew remiss; as long as his throne tottered he kept duty to make God his friend, but once it stood firm he thought he had no more occasion for religion, for the prosperity of fools destroys them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And it came to pass, in the fifth year of King Rehoboam, Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, because they had transgressed against the Lord. Henry notes God quickly brought troubles to awaken them before hearts hardened: in the fourth year they began to corrupt themselves, and in the fifth Egypt came, by a hand they had little reason to suspect after friendly correspondence in the last reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. With twelve hundred chariots and threescore thousand horsemen, and people without number out of Egypt, Lubims, Sukkiims and Ethiopians. Henry notes Shishak came with a vast army, so violent that all Judah's fenced cities fell immediately, plainly showing it was from the Lord because they had transgressed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And he took the fenced cities which pertained to Judah, and came to Jerusalem. Henry notes the cities Rehoboam had lately fortified and garrisoned, on which he relied much for safety, fell without resistance, showing the calamity was from the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Then came Shemaiah the prophet to Rehoboam and the princes of Judah gathered at Jerusalem because of Shishak, and said, Thus saith the Lord, Ye have forsaken me, and therefore have I also left you in the hand of Shishak. Henry notes God by the word explained the rod: the reason Shishak prevailed was not impolitic management but forsaking God, for God never leaves any till they first leave him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Whereupon the princes of Israel and the king humbled themselves, and they said, The Lord is righteous. Henry notes the rebukes of word and rod joined, king and princes penitently acknowledged sin and patiently accepted punishment, saying none but themselves were to blame and God is clear when he judges; even kings must either bend or break before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And when the Lord saw that they humbled themselves, the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah, saying, They have humbled themselves, therefore I will not destroy them, but I will grant them some deliverance, and my wrath shall not be poured out upon Jerusalem by Shishak's hand. Henry notes God in mercy prevented destruction on the brink: the destroying angel at Jerusalem was forbidden, for those who acknowledge God righteous in afflicting them shall find him gracious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Nevertheless they shall be his servants, that they may know my service, and the service of the kingdoms of the countries. Henry notes God left them to smart in liberty: they who thought God's law too hard a yoke would try neighbouring princes' rule awhile, and learn that God's service, compared with others, is reasonable and easy, while disobedience's punishment is far harder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. So Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, and took away the treasures of the house of the Lord and of the king's house; he took all, and carried away also the shields of gold Solomon had made. Henry notes Egypt plundered temple and exchequer, treasuries Solomon left very full; David and Solomon, walking with God, filled them by war and merchandise, but Rehoboam, forsaking God's law, emptied them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Instead of which King Rehoboam made shields of brass, and committed them to the chief of the guard, that kept the entrance of the king's house. Henry notes the golden shields were taken and brazen ones substituted, the visible mark of diminished glory after forsaking God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And when the king entered into the house of the Lord, the guard came and fetched them, and brought them again into the guard chamber. Henry's account of the substituted brazen shields applies: the guard fetched them for the king's entry and returned them to the guard chamber, ceremony now guarded by brass instead of gold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And when he humbled himself, the wrath of the Lord turned from him, that he would not destroy him altogether, and also in Judah things went well. Henry notes deliverance was partial, matching partial reformation, yet in Judah things went well and looked better: good things remained, good ministers, people and families made better by calamity, and affairs altered for the better on repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. So King Rehoboam strengthened himself in Jerusalem, and reigned, for Rehoboam was forty-one when he began to reign, and reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city the Lord chose out of all tribes to put his name there, and his mother's name was Naamah an Ammonitess. Henry's general character of Rehoboam applies: his reign in the chosen city is summed up as melancholy, a Judges-like account of evil, judgment and partial mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And he did evil, because he prepared not his heart to seek the Lord. Henry's whole chapter verdict applies: Rehoboam did evil in forsaking the law when established, growing remiss in worship, and preparing not his heart to seek God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Now the acts of Rehoboam, first and last, are they not written in the book of Shemaiah the prophet, and of Iddo the seer concerning genealogies? And there were wars between Rehoboam and Jeroboam continually. Henry notes the chapter gives a fuller account than Kings, recorded by Shemaiah and Iddo, with continual war between the divided kingdoms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And Rehoboam slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David, and Abijah his son reigned in his stead. Henry's conclusion of Rehoboam's reign applies: the melancholy account closes with his burial in David's city and Abijah's succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Connections to other passages
