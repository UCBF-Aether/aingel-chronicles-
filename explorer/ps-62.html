The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 62 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Truly my soul waiteth upon God: from him cometh my salvation.

2 He only is my rock and my salvation; he is my defence; I shall not be greatly moved.

3 How long will ye imagine mischief against a man? ye shall be slain all of you: as a bowing wall shall ye be, and as a tottering fence.

4 They only consult to cast him down from his excellency: they delight in lies: they bless with their mouth, but they curse inwardly. Selah.

5 My soul, wait thou only upon God; for my expectation is from him.

6 He only is my rock and my salvation: he is my defence; I shall not be moved.

7 In God is my salvation and my glory: the rock of my strength, and my refuge, is in God.

8 Trust in him at all times; ye people, pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us. Selah.

9 Surely men of low degree are vanity, and men of high degree are a lie: to be laid in the balance, they are altogether lighter than vanity.

10 Trust not in oppression, and become not vain in robbery: if riches increase, set not your heart upon them.

11 God hath spoken once; twice have I heard this; that power belongeth unto God.

12 Also unto thee, O Lord, belongeth mercy: for thou renderest to every man according to his work.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Waiting only on God: truly the soul waits, he only is rock, salvation, defence, not greatly moved, then not moved at all. Plotters consult to cast him from excellency, blessing with mouth while cursing inwardly. Men low and high are lighter than vanity in the balance, riches are not to be trusted, for power belongs to God, and mercy too, rendering to each according to his work. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Truly my soul waits upon God; from him comes my salvation: waiting truly — silently, solely — on God, whose salvation is the soul's supply. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He only is my rock and my salvation; he is my defence; I shall not be greatly moved: only rock, only salvation, defence itself — and therefore not greatly moved, though shaken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. How long will you imagine mischief against a man? You shall be slain, all of you, as a bowing wall and a tottering fence: the plotters, who imagine mischief against one man, are themselves like a bowing wall and tottering fence — ready to fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. They only consult to cast him down from his excellency; they delight in lies; they bless with their mouth but curse inwardly: consultation to cast down, delight in lies, blessing mouth with cursing heart — the conspiracy is defined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. My soul, wait only upon God, for my expectation is from him: the soul is bidden again, now only upon God, for expectation is from him alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He only is my rock and my salvation; he is my defence; I shall not be moved: the refrain returns stronger — now not greatly moved, but not moved at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. In God is my salvation and my glory; the rock of my strength, and my refuge, is in God: salvation and glory both in God, with rock of strength and refuge in him — glory is not in the excellency men would cast down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Trust in him at all times, you people; pour out your heart before him; God is a refuge for us: trust at all times, poured-out hearts, God as refuge for us — the personal psalm becomes the people's counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Surely men of low degree are vanity, and men of high degree are a lie; laid in the balance, they are altogether lighter than vanity: low men vanity, high men a lie, together lighter than vanity itself — human help weighs nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Trust not in oppression, and become not vain in robbery; if riches increase, set not your heart upon them: oppression, robbery, and increased riches are three false trusts, each forbidden to the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. God has spoken once; twice have I heard this, that power belongs unto God: once spoken, twice heard — power belongs to God, heard and re-heard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Also unto you, O Lord, belongs mercy, for you render to every man according to his work: mercy also belongs to God, who renders to every man according to his work — power and mercy together govern the rendering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
