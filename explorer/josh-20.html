The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Joshua 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The LORD also spake unto Joshua, saying,

2 Speak to the children of Israel, saying, Appoint out for you cities of refuge, whereof I spake unto you by the hand of Moses:

3 That the slayer that killeth any person unawares and unwittingly may flee thither: and they shall be your refuge from the avenger of blood.

4 And when he that doth flee unto one of those cities shall stand at the entering of the gate of the city, and shall declare his cause in the ears of the elders of that city, they shall take him into the city unto them, and give him a place, that he may dwell among them.

5 And if the avenger of blood pursue after him, then they shall not deliver the slayer up into his hand; because he smote his neighbour unwittingly, and hated him not beforetime.

6 And he shall dwell in that city, until he stand before the congregation for judgment, and until the death of the high priest that shall be in those days: then shall the slayer return, and come unto his own city, and unto his own house, unto the city from whence he fled.

7 And they appointed Kedesh in Galilee in mount Naphtali, and Shechem in mount Ephraim, and Kirjatharba, which is Hebron, in the mountain of Judah.

8 And on the other side Jordan by Jericho eastward, they assigned Bezer in the wilderness upon the plain out of the tribe of Reuben, and Ramoth in Gilead out of the tribe of Gad, and Golan in Bashan out of the tribe of Manasseh.

9 These were the cities appointed for all the children of Israel, and for the stranger that sojourneth among them, that whosoever killeth any person at unawares might flee thither, and not die by the hand of the avenger of blood, until he stood before the congregation.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Six cities of refuge are appointed — Kedesh, Shechem and Hebron west of the Jordan, Bezer, Ramoth and Golan east of it — so that a person who kills a neighbour unintentionally can flee there and stand trial before the congregation, safe from the avenger of blood. If the killing proves deliberate, there is no refuge. The manslayer stays in the city until the death of the high priest, and only then may return home. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Lord also spoke to Joshua, giving the law concerning the cities of refuge now that Israel was settled in their inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Joshua was to speak to Israel to appoint cities of refuge, of which the Lord had spoken by the hand of Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The slayer who killed any person unawares and unwittingly might flee thither, and those cities would be his refuge from the avenger of blood, typifying the relief the gospel provides for penitent sinners in Christ, to whom believers flee for refuge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. When the fugitive stood at the entering of the gate of the city and declared his cause in the elders' ears, they were to take him into the city and give him a place to dwell among them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. If the avenger of blood pursued him, they were not to deliver the slayer into his hand, because he smote his neighbour unwittingly and hated him not before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He was to dwell in that city until he stood before the congregation for judgment, and until the death of the high priest then in office, and then return to his own city and house from whence he fled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They appointed Kedesh in Galilee in mount Naphtali, Shechem in mount Ephraim, and Kirjatharba, which is Hebron, in the mountain of Judah, and Henry notes the names' significance for Christ our Refuge: Kedesh signifies holy, Shechem a shoulder, the government being upon his shoulder, and Hebron fellowship, believers being called into fellowship with Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. On the other side of Jordan eastward they assigned Bezer in the wilderness on the plain from Reuben, Ramoth in Gilead from Gad, and Golan in Bashan from Manasseh, and Henry notes Bezer is a fortification, God our stronghold, Ramoth height or exaltation, to which we are called in Christ, and Golan joy or exultation, for in him the saints are justified and shall glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. These were the cities appointed for all Israel and for the stranger sojourning among them, that whoever killed any person unawares might flee thither and not die by the avenger of blood until he stood before the congregation, and Henry notes they stood so that a man might reach one in half a day from any part of the country, for God is ever a Refuge at hand, and they were all Levites' cities, so the fugitive had God's servants with him to instruct and pray for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

Glosses below are for words a first-time reader may stumble on in this chapter; they explain the chapter's own usage and add nothing to it.

  • Avenger of blood. The kinsman entitled to pursue a killer. The cities of refuge exist to hold the two apart until trial: an unintentional killer is safe in the city, but a deliberate murderer finds no refuge there. Interpreted Chapter text, Joshua 20:3–6, and study note
