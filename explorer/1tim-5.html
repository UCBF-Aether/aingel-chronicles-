The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Timothy 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Rebuke not an elder, but intreat him as a father; and the younger men as brethren;

2 The elder women as mothers; the younger as sisters, with all purity.

3 Honour widows that are widows indeed.

4 But if any widow have children or nephews, let them learn first to shew piety at home, and to requite their parents: for that is good and acceptable before God.

5 Now she that is a widow indeed, and desolate, trusteth in God, and continueth in supplications and prayers night and day.

6 But she that liveth in pleasure is dead while she liveth.

7 And these things give in charge, that they may be blameless.

8 But if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel.

9 Let not a widow be taken into the number under threescore years old, having been the wife of one man.

10 Well reported of for good works; if she have brought up children, if she have lodged strangers, if she have washed the saints’ feet, if she have relieved the afflicted, if she have diligently followed every good work.

11 But the younger widows refuse: for when they have begun to wax wanton against Christ, they will marry;

12 Having damnation, because they have cast off their first faith.

13 And withal they learn to be idle, wandering about from house to house; and not only idle, but tattlers also and busybodies, speaking things which they ought not.

14 I will therefore that the younger women marry, bear children, guide the house, give none occasion to the adversary to speak reproachfully.

15 For some are already turned aside after Satan.

16 If any man or woman that believeth have widows, let them relieve them, and let not the church be charged; that it may relieve them that are widows indeed.

17 Let the elders that rule well be counted worthy of double honour, especially they who labour in the word and doctrine.

18 For the scripture saith, Thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn. And, The labourer is worthy of his reward.

19 Against an elder receive not an accusation, but before two or three witnesses.

20 Them that sin rebuke before all, that others also may fear.

21 I charge thee before God, and the Lord Jesus Christ, and the elect angels, that thou observe these things without preferring one before another, doing nothing by partiality.

22 Lay hands suddenly on no man, neither be partaker of other men’s sins: keep thyself pure.

23 Drink no longer water, but use a little wine for thy stomach’s sake and thine often infirmities.

24 Some men’s sins are open beforehand, going before to judgment; and some men they follow after.

25 Likewise also the good works of some are manifest beforehand; and they that are otherwise cannot be hid.

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Treat older men as fathers and younger men as brothers, older women as mothers and younger women as sisters, Paul instructs, then turns to widows: honour those truly alone, let families care for their own, and enrol only older widows of proven good works. Elders who rule well deserve double honour, accusations need two or three witnesses, public sin is rebuked publicly, and hands are not to be laid on anyone hastily. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. An elder must not be rebuked sharply but entreated as a father, and Henry notes that respect must be paid to the dignity of years and place, with much meekness and care in reproving those who deserve reproof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Younger men are to be treated as brethren, elder women as mothers, and younger women as sisters with all purity, so that every correction keeps the manners of a family, not of a court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Widows that are widows indeed are to be honoured, that is, relieved and maintained, and Henry notes that widowhood is a desolate state in which widows must trust in the Lord and continue in prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. If a widow has children or nephews, they must first learn to show piety at home and requite their parents, and Henry warns that those who do not maintain their poor relations in effect deny the faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The widow indeed, desolate and trusting in God, continues in supplications and prayers night and day, making prayer the work of her solitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. She that lives in pleasure is dead while she lives, spiritually dead in trespasses and sins, and Henry laments how many nominal Christians answer this description even to the latest period of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. These things Timothy must command, so that the widows and their families may be blameless, and the church's care does not become a cover for disorder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Whoever provides not for his own, especially his own house, has denied the faith and is worse than an infidel, and Henry applies it sharply: spending on lusts and pleasures what should maintain a family is a denial of the gospel professed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. A widow is not to be taken into the church's number under threescore years, having been the wife of one man, since office in the church requires freedom from just censure, though many proper objects of charity ought not to be employed in public services. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. She must be well reported of for good works: bringing up children, lodging strangers, washing saints' feet, relieving the afflicted, and following every good work, so that a life of mercy is the qualification for the church's trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Younger widows are to be refused for that number, since when they wax wanton against Christ they will marry, and Henry notes that those who would find mercy in distress must show mercy in prosperity, while idleness seldom stays idle but makes mischief among neighbours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Having damnation because they cast off their first faith, they fall under judgment for breaking the pledge their enrolment implied. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. With idleness they learn to wander from house to house, becoming tattlers and busybodies speaking things they ought not, and Henry warns that the idle very seldom are only idle, but sow discord among brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Paul wills therefore that younger women marry, bear children, guide the house, and give the adversary no occasion to speak reproachfully, turning domestic faithfulness into the answer to gossip. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. For some are already turned aside after Satan, showing that the danger Paul guards against is not hypothetical but already at work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. If any believing man or woman has widows, they must relieve them and not burden the church, so that the church may relieve those who are widows indeed, entirely destitute and friendless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Elders that rule well are worthy of double honour, especially those who labour in word and doctrine, and Henry notes that care must be taken that ministers are maintained, since it is their just due as much as the labourer's reward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Scripture says, Thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn, and, The labourer is worthy of his reward, grounding ministerial maintenance in both Testaments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. An accusation against an elder is not to be received except before two or three witnesses, guarding a public office against a single malicious tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Those who sin are to be rebuked before all, that others also may fear, so that public sin receives public correction for the sake of the whole church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Timothy is charged before God, Christ, and the elect angels to observe these things without preferring one before another and doing nothing by partiality, and Henry notes the solemn need to watch at all times against partaking of other men's sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Hands must not be laid suddenly on any man, nor must Timothy partake of other men's sins, but keep himself pure, and Henry explains purity here as refusing not only to do the like himself but to countenance or help it in others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Timothy is told to drink no longer water only, but use a little wine for his stomach's sake and often infirmities, and Henry notes the balance: bodies are to be neither masters nor slaves, but used so as to be most helpful in God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Some men's sins are open beforehand, going before to judgment, and some follow after, and Henry reminds us that God will bring to light the hidden things of darkness and make known the counsels of hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Likewise good works are manifest beforehand, and those that are otherwise cannot be hid, so that both sin and goodness come to light at the judgment, and Timothy must act with that day in view. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
