The Text — Psalms 122 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 I was glad when they said unto me, Let us go into the house of the LORD.

2 Our feet shall stand within thy gates, O Jerusalem.

3 Jerusalem is builded as a city that is compact together:

4 Whither the tribes go up, the tribes of the LORD, unto the testimony of Israel, to give thanks unto the name of the LORD.

5 For there are set thrones of judgment, the thrones of the house of David.

6 Pray for the peace of Jerusalem: they shall prosper that love thee.

7 Peace be within thy walls, and prosperity within thy palaces.

8 For my brethren and companions’ sakes, I will now say, Peace be within thee.

9 Because of the house of the LORD our God I will seek thy good.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A song of gladness at going up to Jerusalem: "I was glad when they said unto me, Let us go into the house of the LORD," and "our feet shall stand within thy gates, O Jerusalem." Jerusalem is built as a city compact together, where the tribes go up to give thanks, and where thrones of judgment stand, the thrones of the house of David. The psalm's prayer is for the peace of Jerusalem — peace within her walls, prosperity within her palaces — spoken for brethren and companions' sakes, and, because of the house of the LORD our God, a resolve to seek her good. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry welcomes the invitation as kindness, not affront: God wills concert worship, many joining in public ordinances beyond house worship, not forsaking assembling — and we must stir one another, let us go together, not do you go and pray for us, for slow and backward souls sharpen one another as iron sharpens iron, and rejoicers in God rejoice in calls to wait on him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry triumphs in the prospect: feet standing within Jerusalem's gates makes amends for tedious journey fatigue, desirable even as servants standing by or doorkeepers — for now there is a resting-place for the ark, and where it is there will we be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry praises the beautiful, compact city: houses contiguous, streets fair and spacious, uniform, strengthening one another, Jebusites driven out and wholly God's people's — a type of the gospel church compact in holy love and communion, all as one city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry gathers tribes as one man to the holy city: in obedience they go up to receive instruction from the testimony of Israel, consulting God's oracle, and to ascribe glory by thanksgiving — for if God speaks by his word, we answer by thanksgivings, and that is our errand in public worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry loves the royal city for thrones of judgment: justice administered by a man after God's own heart secured civil interests as well as ecclesiastical, happy courts like Westminster Hall — and goodly neighbours, testimony directing thrones and thrones protecting testimony, befriending one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry calls all to pray for Jerusalem's peace out of love, not custom: welfare, uniting inhabitants and preservation from enemies, desired because in its peace we have peace, earnestly prayed as God's gift to be enquired of — and lovers prosper, for God loves Zion's gates and will love those loving them, souls prospering by ordinances dearly loved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry puts words in mouths for all within walls and for palaces: peace in fortifications never attacked or never taken, effectual security — and prosperity for princes at the helm, for if they prosper it is well for public, and poorer sort must pray for palaces rather than envy them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry resolves personal prayer for brethren and companions: now tribes cheerfully resort and Jerusalem settled as God's name-place, he will say peace be within thee, not leaving prayer to priests, prophets or people while he fights and rules — for brethren in worship have knit his heart to them as true-hearted Israelites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry seconds prayer with endeavour for God's house: to utmost power seek thy good, for activity for public good proves sincerity of prayer — not merely because Jerusalem was his own city and family interests lodged there, but from warm regard to saints' communion and ordinances, affections set to God's house, which makes concern for public welfare right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
