The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 26 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In that day shall this song be sung in the land of Judah; We have a strong city; salvation will God appoint for walls and bulwarks.

2 Open ye the gates, that the righteous nation which keepeth the truth may enter in.

3 Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.

4 Trust ye in the LORD for ever: for in the LORD JEHOVAH is everlasting strength:

5 For he bringeth down them that dwell on high; the lofty city, he layeth it low; he layeth it low, even to the ground; he bringeth it even to the dust.

6 The foot shall tread it down, even the feet of the poor, and the steps of the needy.

7 The way of the just is uprightness: thou, most upright, dost weigh the path of the just.

8 Yea, in the way of thy judgments, O LORD, have we waited for thee; the desire of our soul is to thy name, and to the remembrance of thee.

9 With my soul have I desired thee in the night; yea, with my spirit within me will I seek thee early: for when thy judgments are in the earth, the inhabitants of the world will learn righteousness.

10 Let favour be shewed to the wicked, yet will he not learn righteousness: in the land of uprightness will he deal unjustly, and will not behold the majesty of the LORD.

11 LORD, when thy hand is lifted up, they will not see: but they shall see, and be ashamed for their envy at the people; yea, the fire of thine enemies shall devour them.

12 LORD, thou wilt ordain peace for us: for thou also hast wrought all our works in us.

13 O LORD our God, other lords beside thee have had dominion over us: but by thee only will we make mention of thy name.

14 They are dead, they shall not live; they are deceased, they shall not rise: therefore hast thou visited and destroyed them, and made all their memory to perish.

15 Thou hast increased the nation, O LORD, thou hast increased the nation: thou art glorified: thou hadst removed it far unto all the ends of the earth.

16 LORD, in trouble have they visited thee, they poured out a prayer when thy chastening was upon them.

17 Like as a woman with child, that draweth near the time of her delivery, is in pain, and crieth out in her pangs; so have we been in thy sight, O LORD.

18 We have been with child, we have been in pain, we have as it were brought forth wind; we have not wrought any deliverance in the earth; neither have the inhabitants of the world fallen.

19 Thy dead men shall live, together with my dead body shall they arise. Awake and sing, ye that dwell in dust: for thy dew is as the dew of herbs, and the earth shall cast out the dead.

20 Come, my people, enter thou into thy chambers, and shut thy doors about thee: hide thyself as it were for a little moment, until the indignation be overpast.

21 For, behold, the LORD cometh out of his place to punish the inhabitants of the earth for their iniquity: the earth also shall disclose her blood, and shall no more cover her slain.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Judah sings of a strong city whose walls and bulwarks are salvation itself, where the righteous nation that keeps truth enters and the mind stayed on God is kept in perfect peace. The song moves from trust to judgment: the proud are brought low, God's people hide briefly until indignation passes, and the Lord comes out of his place to punish the earth and disclose its blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In that day this song will be sung in the land of Judah: We have a strong city; salvation will God appoint for walls and bulwarks. This is the song of praise of Israel after being restored to their own land, and as the overthrow of the apostate faction is described in chapter 25, so the peace of the faithful is described under the image of a well-fortified city. The strong city is Jerusalem, strong in Jehovah's protection and type of the new Jerusalem, contrasted with the overthrow of the ungodly foe, and salvation itself serves as walls, with the trench and antemural earthworks exterior to the wall as bulwarks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Open the gates, that the righteous nation which keeps the truth may enter in. This is the address of the returning people to the gates of Jerusalem, type of the heavenly city, and the righteous nation is that which had not apostatized during the captivity, or the nation of the Just One, the Jews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. You will keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on you, because he trusts in you. Stayed recalls the Psalms, and Jesus can create perfect peace within the mind though storms of trial rage without, as a city kept securely by a strong garrison within though besieged without; keep literally means guard as with a garrison, and the mind is rather a thing formed, God's workmanship, constantly supported and preserved in perpetual peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Trust in the Lord forever, for in the Lord Jehovah is everlasting strength. Lord Jehovah is in Hebrew Jah, Jehovah, and the union of the two names expresses in the highest degree God's unchanging love and power; this is one of the four passages where the English version retains Jehovah, and Jah, the eternal unchangeable One, is Jehovah, the rock of ages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For he brings down those who dwell on high, and the lofty city he lays low, he lays it low even to the ground, and brings it even to the dust. The lofty city is Babylon, representative of the stronghold of God's people's foes in all ages, as in chapters 25 and 13. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The foot will tread it down, even the feet of the poor, and the steps of the needy. The poor are the once-afflicted Jewish captives, and the foot treading is figurative for exulting in the fall of God's enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The way of the just is uprightness: you, most upright, do weigh the path of the just. Uprightness is rather direct, directed by God to a prosperous issue however many afflictions intervene, as with the Jewish exiles, so the way means God's dealings with the righteous; the most upright is God himself, who weighs — rather, makes plain and level — the path, removing all obstacles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Yes, in the way of your judgments, O Lord, we have waited for you; the desire of our soul is to your name, and to the remembrance of you. The way of judgments is waiting for God's proceeding to punish the enemy, and the name and remembrance are the manifested character of God by which he would be remembered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. With my soul I have desired you in the night, yes, with my spirit within me I will seek you early, for when your judgments are in the earth, the inhabitants of the world will learn righteousness. The faithful Jews speak individually, and the overthrow of the foe and restoration of the Jews follow upon prayer on their part and on all God's people's part; in the night recalls the Psalms and Song, and when God's judgments are in the earth, the remnant left after judgments will learn righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Let favour be shown to the wicked, yet he will not learn righteousness; in the land of uprightness he will deal unjustly, and will not behold the majesty of the Lord. Uprightness here is rather prosperity, answering to favour and in antithesis to judgments in the earth: where prosperity attends the wicked as well as the just, he will not learn righteousness, therefore judgments must be sent that he may learn it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Lord, when your hand is lifted up, they will not see, but they will see and be ashamed for their envy at the people; yes, the fire of your enemies will devour them. God's hand is lifted up to punish his people's foes, and they who will not see will be made to see to their cost; their envy at the people is God's zeal for his people, seen with confusion, and the fire of his enemies is the fire to which his enemies are doomed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Lord, you will ordain peace for us, for you also have wrought all our works in us. Peace is God's favour, including all blessings temporal and spiritual, opposed to their previous trials, and God himself has wrought all their works in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. O Lord our God, other lords besides you have had dominion over us, but by you only will we make mention of your name. The other lords are temporal heathen kings such as Nebuchadnezzar, and spiritually idols and lusts, and it is due to God alone that his people again worship him as their Lord — they are his only, and will celebrate his name — showing the sanctifying effect of affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. They are dead, they will not live; they are deceased, they will not rise; therefore you have visited and destroyed them, and made all their memory to perish. They are the other lords or tyrants, who will not live again; the deceased are in Hebrew Rephaim, powerless in the land of shades, and therefore — inasmuch as — God has visited and destroyed them and made their memory perish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. You have increased the nation, O Lord, you have increased the nation, and are glorified; you had removed it far unto all the ends of the earth. Hast is a prophetical preterite, and to have removed far unto the ends of the earth is rather to have extended far all the borders of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Lord, in trouble they have visited you, they poured out a prayer when your chastening was upon them. Visited means sought, and poured out is as a vessel emptying all its contents; the prayer is literally a whispered prayer, a secret sighing to God for help in chastening. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Like as a woman with child, that draws near the time of her delivery, is in pain and cries out in her pangs, so we have been in your sight, O Lord. This is an image of anguish accompanied with expectation, to be followed by joy that will cause the anguish utterly to be forgotten: Zion, looking for deliverance seemingly in vain but really about to be gloriously saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. We have been with child, we have been in pain, we have as it were brought forth wind, we have not wrought any deliverance in the earth, neither have the inhabitants of the world fallen. Wind is a figure for an abortive effort, and the "we" stands against "thy" and "my" in verse 19: what we vainly attempt, God will accomplish. The land of Judea is not made a place of security from enemies, and the inhabitants of the world — Israel, not the world in general — are not yet born, for the country still lies desolate and unpopulated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Your dead men will live, together with my dead body they will arise. Awake and sing, you that dwell in dust, for your dew is as the dew of herbs, and the earth will cast out the dead. In antithesis to verse 14, where Israel's foes will not live, Jehovah's dead men, the Jews, will live, primarily restored spiritually, civilly, and nationally, and ultimately and in the fullest scope restored to life literally. Together with my dead body is rather my dead body or bodies, the Jewish nation personified as spiritually and civilly dead, or the nation as a parent speaking of her children's bodies, and as Jesus is the antitype to Israel, Christ's dead body raised again is the source of his people's raising. Awake is spiritual, those in dust are prostrate and dead spiritually and nationally and literally, the dew falls copiously in the East and supplies the lack of rain, and the earth will bring the dead forth to life again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Come, my people, enter into your chambers, and shut your doors about you; hide yourself as it were for a little moment, until the indignation be overpast. The chambers are a place of safety where God shuts in the saints when he is about to take vengeance on the ungodly, as Noah and his family were shut in during the flood and as Israel was commanded not to go out on the night of the first-born's slaying; the saints are calmly and confidently to await the issue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. For behold, the Lord comes out of his place to punish the inhabitants of the earth for their iniquity, and the earth will disclose her blood, and will no more cover her slain. The Lord comes out of his place as in Micah, and the earth disclosing her blood means all innocent blood shed and all wrongs done, so long seemingly with impunity, will then be avenged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
