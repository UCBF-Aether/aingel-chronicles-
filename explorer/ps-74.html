The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 74 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 O God, why hast thou cast us off for ever? why doth thine anger smoke against the sheep of thy pasture?

2 Remember thy congregation, which thou hast purchased of old; the rod of thine inheritance, which thou hast redeemed; this mount Zion, wherein thou hast dwelt.

3 Lift up thy feet unto the perpetual desolations; even all that the enemy hath done wickedly in the sanctuary.

4 Thine enemies roar in the midst of thy congregations; they set up their ensigns for signs.

5 A man was famous according as he had lifted up axes upon the thick trees.

6 But now they break down the carved work thereof at once with axes and hammers.

7 They have cast fire into thy sanctuary, they have defiled by casting down the dwelling place of thy name to the ground.

8 They said in their hearts, Let us destroy them together: they have burned up all the synagogues of God in the land.

9 We see not our signs: there is no more any prophet: neither is there among us any that knoweth how long.

10 O God, how long shall the adversary reproach? shall the enemy blaspheme thy name for ever?

11 Why withdrawest thou thy hand, even thy right hand? pluck it out of thy bosom.

12 For God is my King of old, working salvation in the midst of the earth.

13 Thou didst divide the sea by thy strength: thou brakest the heads of the dragons in the waters.

14 Thou brakest the heads of leviathan in pieces, and gavest him to be meat to the people inhabiting the wilderness.

15 Thou didst cleave the fountain and the flood: thou driedst up mighty rivers.

16 The day is thine, the night also is thine: thou hast prepared the light and the sun.

17 Thou hast set all the borders of the earth: thou hast made summer and winter.

18 Remember this, that the enemy hath reproached, O LORD, and that the foolish people have blasphemed thy name.

19 O deliver not the soul of thy turtledove unto the multitude of the wicked: forget not the congregation of thy poor for ever.

20 Have respect unto the covenant: for the dark places of the earth are full of the habitations of cruelty.

21 O let not the oppressed return ashamed: let the poor and needy praise thy name.

22 Arise, O God, plead thine own cause: remember how the foolish man reproacheth thee daily.

23 Forget not the voice of thine enemies: the tumult of those that rise up against thee increaseth continually.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Sanctuary in ruins: why cast off for ever, anger smoking against pasture sheep? Remember the congregation purchased of old, Mount Zion where you dwelt. Enemies roar, set ensigns, break carved work with axes, cast fire into the sanctuary, burn the synagogues, no signs, no prophet, none knowing how long. Yet God is King of old, sea divided, dragons' heads broken, leviathan given as meat to wilderness people, fountains cleft, day and night and seasons his. So: arise, plead your own cause, have respect to the covenant, let not the oppressed return ashamed. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

  • Verse 3. Lift up your feet unto the perpetual desolations, even all that the enemy has done wickedly in the sanctuary: feet lifted to perpetual desolations, all the enemy's wicked doing in the sanctuary — God is bidden come and see the ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Your enemies roar in the midst of your congregations; they set up their ensigns for signs: roaring enemies in the congregations, ensigns set up for signs — the sanctuary is occupied by hostile standards. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. A man was famous according as he had lifted up axes upon the thick trees: fame for axe-work on thick trees — the destroyers' earlier boasting is recalled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But now they break down the carved work thereof at once with axes and hammers: carved work broken at once with axes and hammers — the sanctuary's art is smashed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They have cast fire into your sanctuary, they have defiled by casting down the dwelling place of your name to the ground: fire cast into the sanctuary, the Name's dwelling cast to the ground and defiled — the fire completes the desecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They said in their hearts, Let us destroy them together; they have burned up all the synagogues of God in the land: 'destroy them together' in their hearts, all God's synagogues burned in the land — the destruction is total and intended. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. We see not our signs; there is no more any prophet; neither is there among us any that knows how long: no signs seen, no prophet, none knowing how long — the silence of heaven matches the ruin on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. O God, how long shall the adversary reproach, and shall the enemy blaspheme your name for ever? The question turns from ruin to God's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Why do you withdraw your hand, even your right hand? Pluck it out of your bosom: withdrawn hand, right hand in the bosom — God is bidden pluck out the hand he seems to hide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. For God is my King of old, working salvation in the midst of the earth: God as King of old, working salvation in earth's midst — ancient kingship grounds present hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. You did divide the sea by your strength; you broke the heads of the dragons in the waters: sea divided by strength, dragons' heads broken in waters — the Exodus victory over chaos-monsters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. You broke the heads of leviathan in pieces, and gave him to be meat to the people inhabiting the wilderness: leviathan's heads broken, given as meat to wilderness people — the sea-monster becomes food for God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. You did cleave the fountain and the flood; you dried up mighty rivers: fountain and flood cleft, mighty rivers dried — water miracles in wilderness and Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The day is yours, the night also is yours; you have prepared the light and the sun: day and night God's, light and sun prepared by him — creation's order is his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. You have set all the borders of the earth; you have made summer and winter: earth's borders set, summer and winter made — seasons and boundaries are God's appointments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Remember this, that the enemy has reproached, O Lord, and that the foolish people have blasphemed your name: remember the enemy's reproach and the foolish people's blasphemy of God's name — the plea returns to the name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. O deliver not the soul of your turtledove unto the multitude of the wicked; forget not the congregation of your poor for ever: turtledove soul not delivered to the wicked multitude, poor congregation not forgotten for ever — the defenceless dove is God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Have respect unto the covenant, for the dark places of the earth are full of the habitations of cruelty: respect to the covenant is prayed, for dark places are full of cruelty's habitations — covenant is the argument against universal cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. O let not the oppressed return ashamed; let the poor and needy praise your name: oppressed not returning ashamed, poor and needy praising God's name — shame turned to praise is the prayed outcome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Arise, O God, plead your own cause; remember how the foolish man reproaches you daily: arising, pleading his own cause, remembering daily reproach by the foolish — God's own cause is now the plea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Forget not the voice of your enemies; the tumult of those who rise up against you increases continually: enemies' voice unforgotten, tumult of risers increasing continually — the psalm ends with rising tumult still unanswered, and the prayer still standing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
