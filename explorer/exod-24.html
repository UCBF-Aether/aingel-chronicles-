The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 24 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he said unto Moses, Come up unto the LORD, thou, and Aaron, Nadab, and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel; and worship ye afar off.

2 And Moses alone shall come near the LORD: but they shall not come nigh; neither shall the people go up with him.

3 And Moses came and told the people all the words of the LORD, and all the judgments: and all the people answered with one voice, and said, All the words which the LORD hath said will we do.

4 And Moses wrote all the words of the LORD, and rose up early in the morning, and builded an altar under the hill, and twelve pillars, according to the twelve tribes of Israel.

5 And he sent young men of the children of Israel, which offered burnt offerings, and sacrificed peace offerings of oxen unto the LORD.

6 And Moses took half of the blood, and put it in basons; and half of the blood he sprinkled on the altar.

7 And he took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient.

8 And Moses took the blood, and sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you concerning all these words.

9 Then went up Moses, and Aaron, Nadab, and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel:

10 And they saw the God of Israel: and there was under his feet as it were a paved work of a sapphire stone, and as it were the body of heaven in his clearness.

11 And upon the nobles of the children of Israel he laid not his hand: also they saw God, and did eat and drink.

12 And the LORD said unto Moses, Come up to me into the mount, and be there: and I will give thee tables of stone, and a law, and commandments which I have written; that thou mayest teach them.

13 And Moses rose up, and his minister Joshua: and Moses went up into the mount of God.

14 And he said unto the elders, Tarry ye here for us, until we come again unto you: and, behold, Aaron and Hur are with you: if any man have any matters to do, let him come unto them.

15 And Moses went up into the mount, and a cloud covered the mount.

16 And the glory of the LORD abode upon mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days: and the seventh day he called unto Moses out of the midst of the cloud.

17 And the sight of the glory of the LORD was like devouring fire on the top of the mount in the eyes of the children of Israel.

18 And Moses went into the midst of the cloud, and gat him up into the mount: and Moses was in the mount forty days and forty nights.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Moses reads the book of the covenant to the people, who answer that all the LORD has said they will do, and he sprinkles half the blood on the altar and half on the people to seal it. Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders go up and see the God of Israel, and then Moses alone goes into the cloud on the mountain for forty days and forty nights. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God said unto Moses, Come up unto the LORD, thou, and Aaron, Nadab, and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel, and worship ye afar off. Henry notes a solemn covenant was now to be made between God and Israel, typifying the covenant of grace between God and believers through Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And Moses alone shall come near the LORD, but they shall not come nigh, neither shall the people go up with him. Henry observes the degrees of approach: the people below, the elders afar off, and Moses alone near, for even in grace, access has its order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Moses came and told the people all the words of the LORD and all the judgments, and all the people answered with one voice, All the words which the LORD hath said will we do. Henry notes God's covenants and commands are so just in themselves and so much for our good, that the more plainly they are set before us, the more reason we may see to comply with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Moses wrote all the words of the LORD, and rose up early in the morning, and built an altar under the hill, and twelve pillars according to the twelve tribes of Israel. Henry observes that as soon as God separated a peculiar people to himself, he governed them by a written word, as he has done ever since. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He sent young men of Israel, which offered burnt offerings and sacrificed peace offerings of oxen unto the LORD. Henry notes the sacrifices sealed the covenant: burnt offerings of whole surrender, and peace offerings of communion, both offered before the law was yet written on stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Moses took half of the blood and put it in basons, and half of the blood he sprinkled on the altar. Henry observes the blood was divided between altar and people, for the covenant bound both sides, God and Israel, in one sprinkling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He took the book of the covenant and read in the audience of the people, and they said, All that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient. Henry notes the reading came before the second sprinkling: the people consented to words they had heard, not to a blank covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Moses took the blood and sprinkled it on the people, saying, Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you concerning all these words. Henry observes the blood was sprinkled on the altar, on the book, and on the people, for neither their persons, their moral obedience, nor their religious services would meet acceptance from a holy God except through the shedding and sprinkling of blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Then went up Moses, and Aaron, Nadab, and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel. Henry notes the ascent followed the covenant: those who had been sprinkled were now permitted to go up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And they saw the God of Israel, and there was under his feet as it were a paved work of a sapphire stone, and as it were the body of heaven in his clearness. Henry observes the elders had some glimpse of his glory, though whatever they saw, it was something of which no image could be made, yet enough to satisfy them that God was with them of a truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And upon the nobles of Israel he laid not his hand, and also they saw God, and did eat and drink. Henry notes the wonder: they saw God and lived, and ate and drank in his presence, for the sapphires under his feet teach us to put all the wealth of this world under our feet, and not in our hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And the LORD said unto Moses, Come up to me into the mount, and be there, and I will give thee tables of stone, and a law, and commandments which I have written, that thou mayest teach them. Henry observes the written word was now to be written by God himself on stone, that Moses might teach what God had written. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And Moses rose up, and his minister Joshua, and Moses went up into the mount of God. Henry notes Joshua went as minister, to accompany Moses part of the way, for even the mediator needed a fellow-traveller. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And he said unto the elders, Tarry ye here for us, until we come again unto you, and behold, Aaron and Hur are with you, and if any man have any matters to do, let him come unto them. Henry observes order was left behind: Aaron and Hur to judge causes while Moses was on the mount, so the camp was not lawless in his absence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And Moses went up into the mount, and a cloud covered the mount. Henry notes the cloud was a token of God's special presence there, and Moses was sure that he who called him up would protect him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And the glory of the LORD abode upon mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days, and the seventh day he called unto Moses out of the midst of the cloud. Henry observes the six days of waiting preceded the call: glory abode, and Moses waited, before the voice came. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And the sight of the glory of the LORD was like devouring fire on the top of the mount in the eyes of the children of Israel. Henry notes even those glorious attributes of God which are most terrible to the wicked, the saints with humble reverence rejoice in, for the fire that devours enemies is the glory that dwells with his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And Moses went into the midst of the cloud, and gat him up into the mount, and Moses was in the mount forty days and forty nights. Henry closes on the wonder: Moses entered the cloud and remained forty days, and through faith in the atoning Sacrifice we hope for greater honour than Moses ever enjoyed on earth, for now we see through a glass darkly, but then face to face. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
