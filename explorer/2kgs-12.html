Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the seventh year of Jehu Jehoash began to reign; and forty years reigned he in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Zibiah of Beersheba.

2 And Jehoash did that which was right in the sight of the LORD all his days wherein Jehoiada the priest instructed him.

3 But the high places were not taken away: the people still sacrificed and burnt incense in the high places.

4 And Jehoash said to the priests, All the money of the dedicated things that is brought into the house of the LORD, even the money of every one that passeth the account, the money that every man is set at, and all the money that cometh into any man’s heart to bring into the house of the LORD,

5 Let the priests take it to them, every man of his acquaintance: and let them repair the breaches of the house, wheresoever any breach shall be found.

6 But it was so, that in the three and twentieth year of king Jehoash the priests had not repaired the breaches of the house.

7 Then king Jehoash called for Jehoiada the priest, and the other priests, and said unto them, Why repair ye not the breaches of the house? now therefore receive no more money of your acquaintance, but deliver it for the breaches of the house.

8 And the priests consented to receive no more money of the people, neither to repair the breaches of the house.

9 But Jehoiada the priest took a chest, and bored a hole in the lid of it, and set it beside the altar, on the right side as one cometh into the house of the LORD: and the priests that kept the door put therein all the money that was brought into the house of the LORD.

10 And it was so, when they saw that there was much money in the chest, that the king’s scribe and the high priest came up, and they put up in bags, and told the money that was found in the house of the LORD.

11 And they gave the money, being told, into the hands of them that did the work, that had the oversight of the house of the LORD: and they laid it out to the carpenters and builders, that wrought upon the house of the LORD,

12 And to masons, and hewers of stone, and to buy timber and hewed stone to repair the breaches of the house of the LORD, and for all that was laid out for the house to repair it.

13 Howbeit there were not made for the house of the LORD bowls of silver, snuffers, basons, trumpets, any vessels of gold, or vessels of silver, of the money that was brought into the house of the LORD:

14 But they gave that to the workmen, and repaired therewith the house of the LORD.

15 Moreover they reckoned not with the men, into whose hand they delivered the money to be bestowed on workmen: for they dealt faithfully.

16 The trespass money and sin money was not brought into the house of the LORD: it was the priests’.

17 Then Hazael king of Syria went up, and fought against Gath, and took it: and Hazael set his face to go up to Jerusalem.

18 And Jehoash king of Judah took all the hallowed things that Jehoshaphat, and Jehoram, and Ahaziah, his fathers, kings of Judah, had dedicated, and his own hallowed things, and all the gold that was found in the treasures of the house of the LORD, and in the king’s house, and sent it to Hazael king of Syria: and he went away from Jerusalem.

19 And the rest of the acts of Joash, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

20 And his servants arose, and made a conspiracy, and slew Joash in the house of Millo, which goeth down to Silla.

21 For Jozachar the son of Shimeath, and Jehozabad the son of Shomer, his servants, smote him, and he died; and they buried him with his fathers in the city of David: and Amaziah his son reigned in his stead.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Joash, guided by Jehoiada, reigns well and repairs the temple with the people's offerings, honestly administered; he buys off Hazael with temple treasure and is murdered by his own servants. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the seventh year of Jehu, Jehoash began to reign, reigning forty years in Jerusalem, his mother Zibiah of Beersheba. JFB's section "Jehoash reigns well while Jehoiada lived" opens with this reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Jehoash did right in the LORD's sight all his days wherein Jehoiada the priest instructed him. JFB qualifies this: his outward rectitude owed more to the salutary influence of his preserver and tutor Jehoiada than to his own mind's sincere dictates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. But the high places were not taken away, and the people still sacrificed and burnt incense there. JFB explains the popular fondness for private rites in groves and hills was so inveterate that even powerful monarchs could not suppress it, and the difficulty grew after Athaliah's maladministration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Jehoash told the priests all the money of dedicated things brought into the LORD's house should be used for repairs. JFB lists the three levies: the half-shekel of everyone passing the account, redemption prices of devoted persons or things, and freewill offerings, the first two paid annually. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The priests were to take the money, every man from his acquaintance, and repair the breaches of the house wherever found. JFB presents this as the young king's measure for repairing the temple by levying taxes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But in Jehoash's twenty-third year the priests had not repaired the breaches. JFB notes this mode of collection proved less productive than expected, chiefly through the priests' dilatoriness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Jehoash called Jehoiada and the priests and asked why they had not repaired the breaches. JFB explains a new arrangement was then proposed: a chest placed by the high priest at the temple entrance, into which door-keeping Levites put the people's repair money, separating building funds from priests' general money. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The priests consented to receive no more money from the people, nor to repair the breaches themselves. JFB notes the duty of attending to the work no longer devolved on the priests but was undertaken by the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Jehoiada took a chest, bored a hole in its lid, and set it beside the altar on the right as one entered the LORD's house, and the door-keeping priests put in all money brought to the house. JFB describes this chest as the means of separating repair money so people would give more liberally, knowing offerings were devoted to repairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. When they saw much money in the chest, the king's scribe and the high priest came, put it up in bags, and told it. JFB notes the king sent his secretary with the high priest's agent to count the chest money from time to time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They gave the counted money to those who did the work and had oversight of the LORD's house. JFB explains the overseers paid the workmen and purchased materials, and sums put in labelled, sealed bags were a common Eastern currency custom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The money went to masons, hewers of stone, and to buy timber and hewed stone to repair the breaches, and for all else needed to repair the house. JFB notes the overseers paid workmen and purchased all necessary materials. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. No silver bowls, snuffers, basons, trumpets, or vessels of gold or silver were made for the LORD's house from the money brought in. JFB explains that when repairs were completed, the surplus was appropriated to temple furniture, but repair money itself was not diverted to vessels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The money was given to the workmen, and they repaired the LORD's house with it. JFB notes the overseers' integrity was undoubted, so no account was exacted of how they applied the money. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. No reckoning was made with the men into whose hands the money was delivered to bestow on workmen, for they dealt faithfully. JFB stresses this undoubted integrity as the reason no account was exacted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The trespass money and sin money was not brought into the LORD's house; it was the priests'. JFB notes other moneys levied at the temple were left to the priests' disposal as the law directed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Hazael king of Syria went up, fought against Gath, took it, and set his face to go up to Jerusalem. JFB cross-refers this campaign to the fuller account in Chronicles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Jehoash king of Judah took all the hallowed things of Jehoshaphat, Jehoram, and Ahaziah, with his own hallowed things and all the gold in the treasuries, and sent them to Hazael, who went away from Jerusalem. JFB's section places this tribute after Hazael's advance on Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Joash's servants arose, conspired, and slew him in the house of Millo going down to Silla. JFB heads this section "He is slain" and cross-refers the conspiracy to Chronicles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Jozachar son of Shimeath and Jehozabad son of Shomer, his servants, smote him, and he died, and they buried him with his fathers in the city of David, and Amaziah his son reigned in his stead. JFB's closing section records the assassination and succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a question examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a parallel; it does not conclude one. The study notes for this chapter cross-refer two episodes to Chronicles: Hazael's campaign against Gath and Jerusalem (verse 17) and the conspiracy that killed Joash (verse 20).

Parallels. The notes cross-refer this chapter's Hazael campaign and Joash's murder by his servants to Chronicles, presenting Chronicles as the fuller account of the Hazael campaign (verse 17's note wording); the verse 20 note cross-refers the conspiracy without that claim.

Differences. The notes consulted for this page do not spell out where the two accounts differ in wording or detail, and no difference is asserted here on their basis.

Whether and how the Kings and Chronicles accounts of Joash relate beyond the cross-references above is not established from the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved No source consulted for this page settles the relationship beyond the cross-references
