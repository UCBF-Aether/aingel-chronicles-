The Text — Psalms 119 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Blessed are the undefiled in the way, who walk in the law of the LORD.

2 Blessed are they that keep his testimonies, and that seek him with the whole heart.

3 They also do no iniquity: they walk in his ways.

4 Thou hast commanded us to keep thy precepts diligently.

5 O that my ways were directed to keep thy statutes!

6 Then shall I not be ashamed, when I have respect unto all thy commandments.

7 I will praise thee with uprightness of heart, when I shall have learned thy righteous judgments.

8 I will keep thy statutes: O forsake me not utterly.

9 Wherewithal shall a young man cleanse his way? by taking heed thereto according to thy word.

10 With my whole heart have I sought thee: O let me not wander from thy commandments.

11 Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.

12 Blessed art thou, O LORD: teach me thy statutes.

13 With my lips have I declared all the judgments of thy mouth.

14 I have rejoiced in the way of thy testimonies, as much as in all riches.

15 I will meditate in thy precepts, and have respect unto thy ways.

16 I will delight myself in thy statutes: I will not forget thy word.

17 Deal bountifully with thy servant, that I may live, and keep thy word.

18 Open thou mine eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of thy law.

19 I am a stranger in the earth: hide not thy commandments from me.

20 My soul breaketh for the longing that it hath unto thy judgments at all times.

21 Thou hast rebuked the proud that are cursed, which do err from thy commandments.

22 Remove from me reproach and contempt; for I have kept thy testimonies.

23 Princes also did sit and speak against me: but thy servant did meditate in thy statutes.

24 Thy testimonies also are my delight and my counsellors.

25 My soul cleaveth unto the dust: quicken thou me according to thy word.

26 I have declared my ways, and thou heardest me: teach me thy statutes.

27 Make me to understand the way of thy precepts: so shall I talk of thy wondrous works.

28 My soul melteth for heaviness: strengthen thou me according unto thy word.

29 Remove from me the way of lying: and grant me thy law graciously.

30 I have chosen the way of truth: thy judgments have I laid before me.

31 I have stuck unto thy testimonies: O LORD, put me not to shame.

32 I will run the way of thy commandments, when thou shalt enlarge my heart.

33 Teach me, O LORD, the way of thy statutes; and I shall keep it unto the end.

34 Give me understanding, and I shall keep thy law; yea, I shall observe it with my whole heart.

35 Make me to go in the path of thy commandments; for therein do I delight.

36 Incline my heart unto thy testimonies, and not to covetousness.

37 Turn away mine eyes from beholding vanity; and quicken thou me in thy way.

38 Stablish thy word unto thy servant, who is devoted to thy fear.

39 Turn away my reproach which I fear: for thy judgments are good.

40 Behold, I have longed after thy precepts: quicken me in thy righteousness.

41 Let thy mercies come also unto me, O LORD, even thy salvation, according to thy word.

42 So shall I have wherewith to answer him that reproacheth me: for I trust in thy word.

43 And take not the word of truth utterly out of my mouth; for I have hoped in thy judgments.

44 So shall I keep thy law continually for ever and ever.

45 And I will walk at liberty: for I seek thy precepts.

46 I will speak of thy testimonies also before kings, and will not be ashamed.

47 And I will delight myself in thy commandments, which I have loved.

48 My hands also will I lift up unto thy commandments, which I have loved; and I will meditate in thy statutes.

49 Remember the word unto thy servant, upon which thou hast caused me to hope.

50 This is my comfort in my affliction: for thy word hath quickened me.

51 The proud have had me greatly in derision: yet have I not declined from thy law.

52 I remembered thy judgments of old, O LORD; and have comforted myself.

53 Horror hath taken hold upon me because of the wicked that forsake thy law.

54 Thy statutes have been my songs in the house of my pilgrimage.

55 I have remembered thy name, O LORD, in the night, and have kept thy law.

56 This I had, because I kept thy precepts.

57 Thou art my portion, O LORD: I have said that I would keep thy words.

58 I intreated thy favour with my whole heart: be merciful unto me according to thy word.

59 I thought on my ways, and turned my feet unto thy testimonies.

60 I made haste, and delayed not to keep thy commandments.

61 The bands of the wicked have robbed me: but I have not forgotten thy law.

62 At midnight I will rise to give thanks unto thee because of thy righteous judgments.

63 I am a companion of all them that fear thee, and of them that keep thy precepts.

64 The earth, O LORD, is full of thy mercy: teach me thy statutes.

65 Thou hast dealt well with thy servant, O LORD, according unto thy word.

66 Teach me good judgment and knowledge: for I have believed thy commandments.

67 Before I was afflicted I went astray: but now have I kept thy word.

68 Thou art good, and doest good; teach me thy statutes.

69 The proud have forged a lie against me: but I will keep thy precepts with my whole heart.

70 Their heart is as fat as grease; but I delight in thy law.

71 It is good for me that I have been afflicted; that I might learn thy statutes.

72 The law of thy mouth is better unto me than thousands of gold and silver.

73 Thy hands have made me and fashioned me: give me understanding, that I may learn thy commandments.

74 They that fear thee will be glad when they see me; because I have hoped in thy word.

75 I know, O LORD, that thy judgments are right, and that thou in faithfulness hast afflicted me.

76 Let, I pray thee, thy merciful kindness be for my comfort, according to thy word unto thy servant.

77 Let thy tender mercies come unto me, that I may live: for thy law is my delight.

78 Let the proud be ashamed; for they dealt perversely with me without a cause: but I will meditate in thy precepts.

79 Let those that fear thee turn unto me, and those that have known thy testimonies.

80 Let my heart be sound in thy statutes; that I be not ashamed.

81 My soul fainteth for thy salvation: but I hope in thy word.

82 Mine eyes fail for thy word, saying, When wilt thou comfort me?

83 For I am become like a bottle in the smoke; yet do I not forget thy statutes.

84 How many are the days of thy servant? when wilt thou execute judgment on them that persecute me?

85 The proud have digged pits for me, which are not after thy law.

86 All thy commandments are faithful: they persecute me wrongfully; help thou me.

87 They had almost consumed me upon earth; but I forsook not thy precepts.

88 Quicken me after thy lovingkindness; so shall I keep the testimony of thy mouth.

89 For ever, O LORD, thy word is settled in heaven.

90 Thy faithfulness is unto all generations: thou hast established the earth, and it abideth.

91 They continue this day according to thine ordinances: for all are thy servants.

92 Unless thy law had been my delights, I should then have perished in mine affliction.

93 I will never forget thy precepts: for with them thou hast quickened me.

94 I am thine, save me: for I have sought thy precepts.

95 The wicked have waited for me to destroy me: but I will consider thy testimonies.

96 I have seen an end of all perfection: but thy commandment is exceeding broad.

97 O how I love thy law! it is my meditation all the day.

98 Thou through thy commandments hast made me wiser than mine enemies: for they are ever with me.

99 I have more understanding than all my teachers: for thy testimonies are my meditation.

100 I understand more than the ancients, because I keep thy precepts.

101 I have refrained my feet from every evil way, that I might keep thy word.

102 I have not departed from thy judgments: for thou hast taught me.

103 How sweet are thy words unto my taste! yea, sweeter than honey to my mouth!

104 Through thy precepts I get understanding: therefore I hate every false way.

105 Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.

106 I have sworn, and I will perform it, that I will keep thy righteous judgments.

107 I am afflicted very much: quicken me, O LORD, according unto thy word.

108 Accept, I beseech thee, the freewill offerings of my mouth, O LORD, and teach me thy judgments.

109 My soul is continually in my hand: yet do I not forget thy law.

110 The wicked have laid a snare for me: yet I erred not from thy precepts.

111 Thy testimonies have I taken as an heritage for ever: for they are the rejoicing of my heart.

112 I have inclined mine heart to perform thy statutes alway, even unto the end.

113 I hate vain thoughts: but thy law do I love.

114 Thou art my hiding place and my shield: I hope in thy word.

115 Depart from me, ye evildoers: for I will keep the commandments of my God.

116 Uphold me according unto thy word, that I may live: and let me not be ashamed of my hope.

117 Hold thou me up, and I shall be safe: and I will have respect unto thy statutes continually.

118 Thou hast trodden down all them that err from thy statutes: for their deceit is falsehood.

119 Thou puttest away all the wicked of the earth like dross: therefore I love thy testimonies.

120 My flesh trembleth for fear of thee; and I am afraid of thy judgments.

121 I have done judgment and justice: leave me not to mine oppressors.

122 Be surety for thy servant for good: let not the proud oppress me.

123 Mine eyes fail for thy salvation, and for the word of thy righteousness.

124 Deal with thy servant according unto thy mercy, and teach me thy statutes.

125 I am thy servant; give me understanding, that I may know thy testimonies.

126 It is time for thee, LORD, to work: for they have made void thy law.

127 Therefore I love thy commandments above gold; yea, above fine gold.

128 Therefore I esteem all thy precepts concerning all things to be right; and I hate every false way.

129 Thy testimonies are wonderful: therefore doth my soul keep them.

130 The entrance of thy words giveth light; it giveth understanding unto the simple.

131 I opened my mouth, and panted: for I longed for thy commandments.

132 Look thou upon me, and be merciful unto me, as thou usest to do unto those that love thy name.

133 Order my steps in thy word: and let not any iniquity have dominion over me.

134 Deliver me from the oppression of man: so will I keep thy precepts.

135 Make thy face to shine upon thy servant; and teach me thy statutes.

136 Rivers of waters run down mine eyes, because they keep not thy law.

137 Righteous art thou, O LORD, and upright are thy judgments.

138 Thy testimonies that thou hast commanded are righteous and very faithful.

139 My zeal hath consumed me, because mine enemies have forgotten thy words.

140 Thy word is very pure: therefore thy servant loveth it.

141 I am small and despised: yet do not I forget thy precepts.

142 Thy righteousness is an everlasting righteousness, and thy law is the truth.

143 Trouble and anguish have taken hold on me: yet thy commandments are my delights.

144 The righteousness of thy testimonies is everlasting: give me understanding, and I shall live.

145 I cried with my whole heart; hear me, O LORD: I will keep thy statutes.

146 I cried unto thee; save me, and I shall keep thy testimonies.

147 I prevented the dawning of the morning, and cried: I hoped in thy word.

148 Mine eyes prevent the night watches, that I might meditate in thy word.

149 Hear my voice according unto thy lovingkindness: O LORD, quicken me according to thy judgment.

150 They draw nigh that follow after mischief: they are far from thy law.

151 Thou art near, O LORD; and all thy commandments are truth.

152 Concerning thy testimonies, I have known of old that thou hast founded them for ever.

153 Consider mine affliction, and deliver me: for I do not forget thy law.

154 Plead my cause, and deliver me: quicken me according to thy word.

155 Salvation is far from the wicked: for they seek not thy statutes.

156 Great are thy tender mercies, O LORD: quicken me according to thy judgments.

157 Many are my persecutors and mine enemies; yet do I not decline from thy testimonies.

158 I beheld the transgressors, and was grieved; because they kept not thy word.

159 Consider how I love thy precepts: quicken me, O LORD, according to thy lovingkindness.

160 Thy word is true from the beginning: and every one of thy righteous judgments endureth for ever.

161 Princes have persecuted me without a cause: but my heart standeth in awe of thy word.

162 I rejoice at thy word, as one that findeth great spoil.

163 I hate and abhor lying: but thy law do I love.

164 Seven times a day do I praise thee because of thy righteous judgments.

165 Great peace have they which love thy law: and nothing shall offend them.

166 LORD, I have hoped for thy salvation, and done thy commandments.

167 My soul hath kept thy testimonies; and I love them exceedingly.

168 I have kept thy precepts and thy testimonies: for all my ways are before thee.

169 Let my cry come near before thee, O LORD: give me understanding according to thy word.

170 Let my supplication come before thee: deliver me according to thy word.

171 My lips shall utter praise, when thou hast taught me thy statutes.

172 My tongue shall speak of thy word: for all thy commandments are righteousness.

173 Let thine hand help me; for I have chosen thy precepts.

174 I have longed for thy salvation, O LORD; and thy law is my delight.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The longest psalm in the book — 176 verses — and a single sustained meditation on God's law, named verse after verse as law, testimonies, statutes, precepts, commandments, judgments and word. It opens with blessing on the undefiled in the way who walk in the law of the LORD, keep his testimonies and seek him with the whole heart, and asks how a young man shall cleanse his way: by taking heed according to God's word, hid in the heart that he might not sin. The speaker prays repeatedly for opened eyes to behold wondrous things out of the law, for teaching and understanding, and for quickening according to the word when his soul cleaves to the dust. Affliction runs through the psalm — it is good for him that he has been afflicted, that he might learn God's statutes; his word is his comfort in affliction, a lamp to his feet and a light to his path, loved and meditated on all the day, settled for ever in heaven. Princes persecute him without a cause, yet his heart stands in awe of God's word; and the psalm closes as a lost sheep asking to be sought: "seek thy servant; for I do not forget thy commandments." What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry marks the happy as those who make God's will the rule of their whole conversation, walking within the law's hedges and pressing forward in its paths by rule, never at adventures, and undefiled — habitually sincere, really as good as they seem, rowing the way they look. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry says the blessed keep God's testimonies as a treasure and covenant deposit, as the apple of the eye, carrying their comfort to another world and leaving the knowledge to those after them — and seek him with a single eye and whole heart, for a divided heart is faulty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry distinguishes clogging sin from drawing-out sin: the happy do no iniquity as a practice or trade, not allowing themselves in any sin as servants of sin, though conscious of much iniquity that clogs them in God's ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry warns against the tempter's liberty lie, as with our first parents questioning Hath God said: we are bound to keep God's precepts diligently, making religion our rule and our business, carefully and constantly, at our peril. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry turns command into prayer: O that my ways were directed — not only events ordered as furtherance, but hearts guided by the Spirit so eyes behold statutes and hearts keep them; the good man's desire exactly agrees with God's will, even our sanctification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry makes respect to all commandments the good man's character: eyeing each command as copy, conforming to it, sorry where short, regarding prohibitions and precepts, inward and outward, pleasant and hazardous alike — and such shall not be ashamed, having confidence, credit and clearness of conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry pictures David still pressing forward as a scholar in Christ's school, aiming to be a head-scholar mighty in Scripture, coveting learning not for name or speculation but to give God glory — for praise with uprightness of heart, inward and aiming at his glory in conversation as in devotion, is what God accepts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry joins learning to keeping and to dependence: we cannot keep statutes unless we learn them, nor learn them in vain without keeping them, in full resolution by grace — and good men are undone if God forsakes them, for then the tempter is too hard; desertion must never be total and final, for that is hell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry asks for the next generation: a young man's way, polluted by original corruption and youthful lusts, is cleansed by taking heed according to God's word — making it his rule, examining his way by it as touchstone and chart, for ruin lies in living at large by no rule or by false rules. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry pleads sought grace against wandering: David sought God as oracle, happiness and God, with his whole heart, only and diligently, and God never says Seek in vain; the best are sensible of aptness to wander, and the more pleasure found in keeping commands, the more earnest the prayer for preventing grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry treasures the word in the heart, not merely house, hand or head where enemies or failing memory may take it: laid up ready for use and valued, it answers every temptation as our Master did, It is written, opposing precepts to sin's dominion, promises to its allurements, threatenings to its menaces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry intermixes praise with prayer: Blessed art thou, O Lord, infinitely happy in himself yet pleased to reckon himself honoured by our service — so teach me thy statutes, one drop from the fountain of blessedness, that I may know how to bless the blessed God and be blessed in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry looks back with comfort: David declared all God's judgments with his lips, not only as king and prophet but in common discourse, out of abundance of heart, hiding God's word for others and bringing forth good things — and pleads good use of knowledge for increase, for to him that has shall be given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry rejoices in the way of commandments as in all riches: not only speculations and histories but precepts and the path of serious godliness, a constant even course of obedience, where true religion holds the unsearchable riches of Christ and the soul may take its ease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry meditates and respects: not only discoursing to others to show knowledge, but communing with his own heart and digesting what he declared, keeping God's ways always in eye as a traveller his road, so that good thoughts produce good works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry resolves on perpetual delight and remembrance: not for a season but still and for ever delighting in statutes, more than court pleasures, camp honours, sword or harp — for when law is written in the heart duty becomes delight, and meditators who delight are in no great danger of forgetting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry owes life itself to bounty: God's bounty gave life and continues it with supports and comforts, for in strict justice we perish; life is a choice mercy as opportunity to obey God where few glorify him, and to transmit his word to those after. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry finds wondrous things in the law — strange, excellent, long hidden from the wise yet revealed to babes, and more in the gospel where Christ is all — but we are by nature blind till grace opens eyes, and even the enlightened must pray to see more clearly, as men first seen as trees walking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry confesses pilgrim status: heaven is home, earth but inn, and even David, established and served by strangers, calls himself a stranger — therefore needing commandments as guide, guard, companion and comforter, preparing him for removal hence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry pleads importunate, constant longing: the soul wholly employed in longing for God's judgments, broken if it wants the word's direction, converse and comfort — not now and then in a good humour, but as habitual hunger for necessary food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry traces wilful sin to pride: the proud magnify themselves above others and against God, setting their wills against his, erring in judgment into contrary principles and then in practice — and God resists them, cursing those who throw off the law, rebuking them here as earnest of worse, which makes saints cleave closer to the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry rolls reproach off by prayer: neither greatness nor goodness secured David from libel, and hard words grieve a tender spirit and may drive or discourage duty — so appeal to God who holds hearts and tongues, can silence lying lips, and will clear righteousness as light; and plead keeping testimonies despite jeers for well-doing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry answers princes' council-room slander by meditation: as a deaf man he heard not, going on in duty, for the word spoke for him and comforted him — and those with pleasure in communion with God may easily despise censures, even of princes, as David typed Christ vilified by princes of this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry makes testimonies both delight and counsellors: when affliction saddened, the word comforted above court, camp, city or country pleasures; when it perplexed, statutes counselled patient bearing and committing the cause to God — more safety than a multitude of counsellors, delight and counsel going together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry complains of a soul cleaving to dust — inclination to world and body and deadness to holy duties, like Paul's body of death, or affliction bringing him to death's dust — and begs quickening by providence into affairs and by grace into affections, for God's words are spirit and life and his word is guide and plea in every prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry prizes intimacy: David opened his very heart, declared his ways, wants, burdens and sins ingenuously, and God heard patiently and took cognizance — unspeakable comfort — and he asks not for visions or news of his lot, but teach me thy statutes, that acknowledging God in all ways he may have steps directed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry seeks methodical understanding to talk well of wonders: give a clear, distinct knowledge of the way of precepts, so he may speak with assurance and purpose of God's wondrous works of providence and especially redeeming love, walking intelligently in that way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry melts and asks strength: heaviness makes the soul melt like a wasting candle, penitent for sin or patient under affliction, and he begs strength in the soul according to the word, the bread of life, to do duties, resist temptations and bear burdens, as thy days so thy strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry dreads the way of lying under difficulty: conscious of cheating Ahimelech and Achish in straits, he prays God prevent pious fraud and necessary self-defence excuses settling into deceit — and begs the law graciously, not merely transcribed by hand as kings must, but written in the heart, a charter of privileges and unspeakable gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 30. Henry chooses deliberately: the way of serious godliness is the way of truth, founded on eternal truth and the only true way to happiness, chosen not from ignorance of another but because no better, safer way is known, though narrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 31. Henry lays judgments before him as copy and model: as a learner lays his copy to write by and a workman his platform to work exactly, so the word must be in the eye by actual regard on all occasions, as well as in the heart by habitual conformity, that walking be accurate and by rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 32. Henry runs when the heart is enlarged: the choosing Christian sticks to testimonies with unchanged affection through trials and may expect not to be put to shame — and when God by his Spirit gives wisdom, sheds abroad love and puts gladness in the heart, that joy becomes wheels to obedience, pressing forward and redeeming time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 33. Henry begs God himself as teacher despite prophets, priests and his own learning, for none teaches like him: teach not notions or language but the way of statutes applied to self, the word behind saying This is the way, walk in it — promising constancy to the end as surest proof of sincerity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 34. Henry asks understanding as God of grace, not merely nature's faculties: an enlightened, sanctified understanding to use powers about things belonging to peace, since revelation in the word needs wisdom in the heart — and then obedience will be cordial, with the whole heart, for that way the whole man goes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 35. Henry prays God to bow the will as well as enlighten the head, for God works both to will and to do: make me able, strengthening for every good work by his Spirit causing us to walk in statutes, and make me willing, subduing natural aversion — duty done with delight when the heart is inclined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 36. Henry prays against covetousness, the sin opposed to all testimonies, root of much sin: that love of God may be rooted in, love of the world must be rooted out, for world-friendship is enmity with God — and God deals not by compulsion but draws with cords of a man toward good and away from evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 37. Henry asks restraining and constraining grace together: turn eyes from beholding vanity — honours, pleasures, profits whose prospect infects the heart and alienates from God, covenanting with eyes as Job did — and quicken me in thy way to redeem time and press forward, for gazing travellers rid no ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 38. Henry defines the good man as God's servant devoted to his fear, subject to law, employed in work, governed in affections by fear of God — and such may boldly pray God establish his word, fulfilling promises in due time and assuring meanwhile; what God promised we must pray for, neither more nor less. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 39. Henry appeals from men's censures to God's good judgments: conscious of giving occasion for blasphemy, he prays God who holds hearts and tongues prevent reproach falling on religion through his side, for God's throne judgments are right and kind to the wronged, and it is small to be judged by man's judgment while the Lord judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 40. Henry professes longing that grows with tasting: not only loving and delighting in what is attained, but earnestly desiring to know more and do better, pressing toward perfection — and prays quickening in righteousness, for where God wrought desire he will satisfy it, and where he wrought to will he will work to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 41. Henry builds hope on two pillars: God's mercy alone, not merit, and God's promise — let salvation come, temporal hastened from distress and eternal hastened by qualifications and pledges, for eternal life is expected as the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 42. Henry expects an answer for reproachers: when God saves those who trusted him, he silences those who shamed the counsel of the poor as if trust would deceive, and at completed salvation reproaches will be for ever silenced, proving it was not in vain to trust God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 43. Henry begs the tongue of the learned never be taken utterly: wisdom and courage to use knowledge for others, bring forth things new and old, and profess faith when called — for sometimes the word is not ready, yet enough must remain for duty, lest support and defence depart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 44. Henry resolves continual, everlasting keeping: with word in heart and mouth he shall stand complete in God's whole will, served in constant course every day and all day, never weary of well-doing, and transmitted from generation to generation as promised, word not departing from seed's seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 45. Henry promises liberty from seeking precepts: desiring to know and do duty and consulting the word, he will walk free from evil's fetters and free to good willingly, not by constraint — for sin's service is perfect slavery and God's service perfect liberty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

Henry weeps rivers because others keep not law: great sorrow, gracious heart with weeping eye conformed to Christ, man of sorrows — pleading qualification for comfort as mourner in Zion who shall be comforted, grieving not chiefly his own wrongs but dishonour done to God by law-breaking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 137. Henry confesses threefold righteousness: God's nature infinite rectitude, nothing wanting or amiss, will eternal rule of equity; his government never wronging any creature, promises, threatenings and executions upright, every word pure and true to merits; and his commands exceedingly righteous and faithful, requiring us act like him, just to all and true to engagements. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 138. Henry repeats that testimonies commanded with sovereign authority are righteousness and faithfulness itself: what we practise is righteous, what we believe faithful — necessary conviction for faith and obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 139. Henry's zeal consumes him over forgotten words: enemies heard God's words yet willingly cast them behind backs, forgetting at bottom of all wickedness and enmity to God's people — and David, in league offensive and defensive with religion, felt indignation preying on spirits like Christ's zeal, constraining him to do more in religion himself: the worse others, the better we should be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 140. Henry loves word for its purity: every servant of God loves it as revealing Master's will and directing Master's work, warm attachment wherever grace is — and love is evidence of love to God when loved for purity, bearing holiness' image, commanding purity, refined from corrupt mixture, refining us from worldliness and fleshly-mindedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 141. Henry, small and despised yet not forgetting: man after God's heart, honoured by King of kings, yet low in own and others' account, for excellency often exposes to scorn — and poor yet pious, refusing to throw off religion though it brings contempt, for contempt tries constancy, and low condition most needs precepts' support. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 142. Henry binds reason and subject together: word is everlasting righteousness, rule of judgment consonant to eternal counsels, determining everlasting state, reverenced as standard — and law is truth, double obligation twisted as cords: truth ruling understanding, law binding will, God's truth a law and law truth, inseparable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 143. Henry raises constancy higher in trouble and anguish: trouble without, anguish within surprising, seizing, holding — yet commandments are delights, mouth not put out of taste, peace and pleasure calamities cannot deprive, sweetest enjoyment of word's variety often in trouble, as in 2 Corinthians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 144. Henry infers understanding and life from everlasting righteousness: testimonies unalterable, admitted in power an abiding well of living water — meditate on equity and eternity, pray not for further revelation but further understanding of what is revealed, and then live for ever, eternally happy and presently comforted in prospect, for life eternal is knowing God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 145. Henry prays inwardly and importunately: not boasting or trusting merit, but comforted to have taken appointed way — heart going with prayer, lip-labour alone lost, crying with whole heart, all soul's powers exerted, directed to Father, seeking comprehensive salvation, rescue and happiness, and looking after prayers to see what becomes of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 146. Henry binds purpose to petition: I will keep thy statutes by grace, for turning ear from law forfeits answer of peace — and pleads it humbly, save me from sins, corruptions, temptations and hindrances, not for ease but opportunity to serve God more cheerfully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 147. Henry prevents dawn by hope: crying before morning, encouraged to instant prayer though answer delayed, vision for appointed time speaking at end and not lying — hope in word that will not fail, beginning day with God while mind fresh, first thoughts keeping fear all day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 148. Henry redeems night-watches by meditation: awaking from first sleep, he would meditate and pray rather than turn again, esteeming God's words more than necessary repose as Job esteemed them more than food — for reading alone will not serve, meditation teaches God's own language and what to pray as we ought, and throne of grace is never unseasonable, for Israel's God never slumbers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 149. Henry begs quickening in little with much: Lord, hear my voice, shall I obtain gracious audience? — quicken me, stir habits of grace into act, vigorous, lively, cheerful — depending on lovingkindness, good God being good to hopers in mercy, and on judgment, wisdom knowing need and promise securing mercy by new covenant's tenor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 150. Henry contrasts nearness: mischief-followers industrious, letting slip no opportunity, impious and far from law, out of reach of its convictions, drawing nigh at his heels, a step from death — yet God brings his people into imminent peril, as Jacob, that like him they may wrestle for a blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 151. Henry assures nearness beats nearness: they draw nigh to destroy, but thou art near, O Lord, to save — mightier and nearer, ready to help, never far to seek, within our call — and all commandments are truth, inviolably true and infallibly performed, beyond enemies' power to defeat promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 152. Henry confirms founded-for-ever testimonies: covenant commanded to a thousand generations, firm as truth itself, framed for perpetuity, well ordered and sure, every iota standing when heaven and earth pass — known of old from youth by grace persuading his heart and providence fulfilling beyond expectation, owned faithful by all who ever dealt with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 153. Henry prays pity, power and advocacy together: consider affliction, take all circumstances into thought, not sitting unconcerned — God never unmindful, yet will have us put him in remembrance and spread the case, leaving it to compassionate wisdom in his time and way — and deliver, as promised, with submission and regard to glory that we may serve better. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 154. Henry pleads cause and quickening by word: plead my cause as patron and advocate against many mighty adversaries, clearing integrity and silencing false accusations, for if God does not plead his people's cause, who will? — and quicken weak, drooping spirit till deliverance wrought, according to promise, for closer cleaving to word as rule and stay gives more assurance of due deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 155. Henry dooms the unseeking: wicked do not merely disobey but do not so much as seek statutes, not acquainting themselves, desiring duty, or endeavouring — resolved to live at large in heart's way — so salvation is far, temporal deliverance groundless, eternal certainly far, thrust away by thrusting Saviour away, further the longer sin persists, while damnation slumbers not and Judge stands at door. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 156. Henry admires tender mercies great and many, a fountain never exhausted, rich to all who call: God good notwithstanding wicked misery, mercies sufficient to have saved them had they not despised riches — and begs reviving grace according to judgments, new covenant's tenor or God's manner with lovers of his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 157. Henry stands amid many persecutors: Saul's abused authority drawing multitudes, public person with many enemies yet many friends to set against them, type of Christ and church whose persecutors are very many — yet not declining, knowing while adhering God is for him, no matter who against, steady duty fearing none. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 158. Henry grieves at transgressors, not for vexation but provocation: looking abroad at open sinners numerous, daring, impudent, industriously snaring unstable souls, dishonouring God, serving Satan, debauching world, ruining souls — grief to all regarding God's glory and mankind's welfare, hating sin truly as transgression of God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 159. Henry appeals to love, not performance: consider how I love thy precepts, thou knowest all things — not consider how I fulfil them, for in many things he came short — but love as principle making obedience pleasing and pleasant, and begs quickening according to lovingkindness, not merit, for to that we owe lives, nay it is better than life itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 160. Henry comforts with word true from beginning and enduring for ever: always faithful hitherto, never failing any venturing, church built on this rock from its beginning, validity not gained by lapse like governments by prescription, but true at beginning — and faithful to end because righteous, every judgment unalterable, perpetual, adjusting everlasting doom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 161. Henry stands in awe of word above princes: persecuted without cause as traitor, bidden serve other gods, princes with sword and law's colour doing hurt with reputation — sad power from God used against him — yet heart stands in awe of God's word, authority of precepts and terror of threatenings, resolving to please God though all the world frowns, obeying God rather than men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 162. Henry rejoices as one finding great spoil: pleasure in reading, hearing, meditating, every meeting agreeable, rejoicing God discovered his mind and Israel blessed with light while nations sat in darkness — and more reverence brings more joy, spoil supposing victory through opposition, word making us more than conquerors, unspeakable gainers by plunder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 163. Henry fixes leading affections aright: rooted antipathy to lying taken for all sin — treachery with God, cheat upon ourselves, hypocrisy, false doctrine, breach of faith, commerce or conversation lies doubly hated, for a lying tongue and false witness are among seven things the Lord hates — and rooted affection to law, loved for its truth, beauty of truth revealing deformity of lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 164. Henry praises seven times a day without complaints jostling praises out of tune: very frequently, not weekly or twice daily, at every meal and occasion, in everything giving thanks, subject never exhausted and affections never tired — praising righteous judgments, precepts just and good, promises and threatenings performed in providence, even afflictions if grace gets good by them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 165. Henry gives lovers of law great peace and no offence: holy serenity, abundant satisfaction in duty and reflecting on it, work of righteousness as peace world can neither give nor take, troubles without yet peace within — and holy security, no event an invincible temptation or intolerable affliction, integrity held and tranquillity preserved, everything working for good and reconciled, without needless scruples or offence at brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 166. Henry joins end and rule, never to be put asunder: eye on God's favour as end, hoping temporal and eternal salvation as happiness of God's preparing, promising and being-with, raised above world and borne under burdens — and eye on word as rule, conscience conforming in everything; hope without commandment-keeping is groundless, sincere endeavour ought to keep hope, and livelier hope makes livelier obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 167. Henry witnesses soul-keeping and superlative love: loving testimonies exceedingly, better than wealth and pleasure, victorious love subduing lusts and extirpating carnal affections — and soul keeping them, heart-work not bodily exercise, soul sanctified, delivered into word's mould, employed glorifying God in spirit, keeping precepts by obedience and promises by reliance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 168. Henry lives under God's eye: therefore kept precepts because by faith he saw God's eye always upon him, every step known and strictly observed, seeing and accepting well-done, seeing and displeased with amiss — consideration that should make us very careful in everything, as walking before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 169. Henry cries and supplicates as beggar: prayer as cry denoting fervency, supplication denoting humility, concerned it come near before God, lifted by faith and fervency, no guilt interposing, graciously received — with deep unworthiness and fear of miscarrying, access only because Christ approached as advocate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 170. Henry asks wisdom and rescue according to word: understanding as wisdom of prudent to understand his way, know God, self and duty — and deliverance, with temptation a way to escape — directed and limited by promise, asking no other understanding or deliverance than promised, and expecting God to be as good as his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 171. Henry promises lips uttering praise when taught: teaching taken for granted after importunate, Jacob-like wrestling not letting go without spiritual blessing — taught of God have foundation of all spiritual blessings and earnest of eternal, know how to praise when understanding, heart and lips are opened, and pray for grace aiming at God's glory; learned nothing to purpose without learning praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 172. Henry speaks word because commandments are righteousness itself: knowing it with satisfaction as rule and standard, tongue shall not only utter praise to God's glory but discourse for others' instruction, full heart speaking, desiring others filled — more sight of righteousness makes more industry to bring others under rule, word governing discourse against sinful speaking and silence, and feeding many with grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 173. Henry pleads chosen precepts for helping hand: own hands insufficient, no creature helping to purpose, hand that made him must help — and choice as pledge, religion serious and deliberate, taken on trial as knowing no better, not by chance; choosers may depend on helping hand in services and sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 174. Henry longs for salvation while delighting in law: heart upon heaven despite throne's worldly courts saying it is good to be here, looking further to eternal salvation all saints long for — and law as greatest delight, cheerful obedience begging help to carry on, hopes increasing delight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 175. Henry asks life to praise and judgments to help praise: prolonged life not to serve country or provide for family chiefly, but to praise God in land of living, doing honour in conflict — soul living as sanctified and comforted, fitted by grace and peace — and ordinances and providences, both God's judgments, furthering glorifying, matter of praise and fitting for work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 176. Henry closes penitent, believing and obedient: gone astray wandering like lost sheep, as unconverted sinners and weak unsteady saints, apt to wander and unapt to find way, losing green pastures' comfort and exposing to mischiefs — yet seek thy servant as good shepherd seeks wandering sheep, David once tender shepherd himself, own me for thine with thy mark, send after me by word, conscience and providences, find me for God never seeks in vain — though strayed, not wickedly departed, not forgetting commandments, so wanderers mindful of duty may humbly commit to grace's care, living and dying repenting and praying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

Readings below are grouped by the commentator voice each study note summarizes. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations.

Matthew Henry (Complete)

  • Verse 1. Henry marks the happy as those who make God's will the rule of their whole conversation, walking within the law's hedges and pressing forward in its paths by rule, never at adventures, and undefiled — habitually sincere, really as good as they seem, rowing the way they look. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry says the blessed keep God's testimonies as a treasure and covenant deposit, as the apple of the eye, carrying their comfort to another world and leaving the knowledge to those after them — and seek him with a single eye and whole heart, for a divided heart is faulty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry distinguishes clogging sin from drawing-out sin: the happy do no iniquity as a practice or trade, not allowing themselves in any sin as servants of sin, though conscious of much iniquity that clogs them in God's ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry warns against the tempter's liberty lie, as with our first parents questioning Hath God said: we are bound to keep God's precepts diligently, making religion our rule and our business, carefully and constantly, at our peril. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry turns command into prayer: O that my ways were directed — not only events ordered as furtherance, but hearts guided by the Spirit so eyes behold statutes and hearts keep them; the good man's desire exactly agrees with God's will, even our sanctification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry makes respect to all commandments the good man's character: eyeing each command as copy, conforming to it, sorry where short, regarding prohibitions and precepts, inward and outward, pleasant and hazardous alike — and such shall not be ashamed, having confidence, credit and clearness of conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry pictures David still pressing forward as a scholar in Christ's school, aiming to be a head-scholar mighty in Scripture, coveting learning not for name or speculation but to give God glory — for praise with uprightness of heart, inward and aiming at his glory in conversation as in devotion, is what God accepts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry joins learning to keeping and to dependence: we cannot keep statutes unless we learn them, nor learn them in vain without keeping them, in full resolution by grace — and good men are undone if God forsakes them, for then the tempter is too hard; desertion must never be total and final, for that is hell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry asks for the next generation: a young man's way, polluted by original corruption and youthful lusts, is cleansed by taking heed according to God's word — making it his rule, examining his way by it as touchstone and chart, for ruin lies in living at large by no rule or by false rules. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry pleads sought grace against wandering: David sought God as oracle, happiness and God, with his whole heart, only and diligently, and God never says Seek in vain; the best are sensible of aptness to wander, and the more pleasure found in keeping commands, the more earnest the prayer for preventing grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry treasures the word in the heart, not merely house, hand or head where enemies or failing memory may take it: laid up ready for use and valued, it answers every temptation as our Master did, It is written, opposing precepts to sin's dominion, promises to its allurements, threatenings to its menaces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry intermixes praise with prayer: Blessed art thou, O Lord, infinitely happy in himself yet pleased to reckon himself honoured by our service — so teach me thy statutes, one drop from the fountain of blessedness, that I may know how to bless the blessed God and be blessed in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry looks back with comfort: David declared all God's judgments with his lips, not only as king and prophet but in common discourse, out of abundance of heart, hiding God's word for others and bringing forth good things — and pleads good use of knowledge for increase, for to him that has shall be given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry rejoices in the way of commandments as in all riches: not only speculations and histories but precepts and the path of serious godliness, a constant even course of obedience, where true religion holds the unsearchable riches of Christ and the soul may take its ease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry meditates and respects: not only discoursing to others to show knowledge, but communing with his own heart and digesting what he declared, keeping God's ways always in eye as a traveller his road, so that good thoughts produce good works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry resolves on perpetual delight and remembrance: not for a season but still and for ever delighting in statutes, more than court pleasures, camp honours, sword or harp — for when law is written in the heart duty becomes delight, and meditators who delight are in no great danger of forgetting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry owes life itself to bounty: God's bounty gave life and continues it with supports and comforts, for in strict justice we perish; life is a choice mercy as opportunity to obey God where few glorify him, and to transmit his word to those after. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry finds wondrous things in the law — strange, excellent, long hidden from the wise yet revealed to babes, and more in the gospel where Christ is all — but we are by nature blind till grace opens eyes, and even the enlightened must pray to see more clearly, as men first seen as trees walking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry confesses pilgrim status: heaven is home, earth but inn, and even David, established and served by strangers, calls himself a stranger — therefore needing commandments as guide, guard, companion and comforter, preparing him for removal hence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry pleads importunate, constant longing: the soul wholly employed in longing for God's judgments, broken if it wants the word's direction, converse and comfort — not now and then in a good humour, but as habitual hunger for necessary food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry traces wilful sin to pride: the proud magnify themselves above others and against God, setting their wills against his, erring in judgment into contrary principles and then in practice — and God resists them, cursing those who throw off the law, rebuking them here as earnest of worse, which makes saints cleave closer to the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry rolls reproach off by prayer: neither greatness nor goodness secured David from libel, and hard words grieve a tender spirit and may drive or discourage duty — so appeal to God who holds hearts and tongues, can silence lying lips, and will clear righteousness as light; and plead keeping testimonies despite jeers for well-doing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry answers princes' council-room slander by meditation: as a deaf man he heard not, going on in duty, for the word spoke for him and comforted him — and those with pleasure in communion with God may easily despise censures, even of princes, as David typed Christ vilified by princes of this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry makes testimonies both delight and counsellors: when affliction saddened, the word comforted above court, camp, city or country pleasures; when it perplexed, statutes counselled patient bearing and committing the cause to God — more safety than a multitude of counsellors, delight and counsel going together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry complains of a soul cleaving to dust — inclination to world and body and deadness to holy duties, like Paul's body of death, or affliction bringing him to death's dust — and begs quickening by providence into affairs and by grace into affections, for God's words are spirit and life and his word is guide and plea in every prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry prizes intimacy: David opened his very heart, declared his ways, wants, burdens and sins ingenuously, and God heard patiently and took cognizance — unspeakable comfort — and he asks not for visions or news of his lot, but teach me thy statutes, that acknowledging God in all ways he may have steps directed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry seeks methodical understanding to talk well of wonders: give a clear, distinct knowledge of the way of precepts, so he may speak with assurance and purpose of God's wondrous works of providence and especially redeeming love, walking intelligently in that way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry melts and asks strength: heaviness makes the soul melt like a wasting candle, penitent for sin or patient under affliction, and he begs strength in the soul according to the word, the bread of life, to do duties, resist temptations and bear burdens, as thy days so thy strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry dreads the way of lying under difficulty: conscious of cheating Ahimelech and Achish in straits, he prays God prevent pious fraud and necessary self-defence excuses settling into deceit — and begs the law graciously, not merely transcribed by hand as kings must, but written in the heart, a charter of privileges and unspeakable gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 30. Henry chooses deliberately: the way of serious godliness is the way of truth, founded on eternal truth and the only true way to happiness, chosen not from ignorance of another but because no better, safer way is known, though narrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 31. Henry lays judgments before him as copy and model: as a learner lays his copy to write by and a workman his platform to work exactly, so the word must be in the eye by actual regard on all occasions, as well as in the heart by habitual conformity, that walking be accurate and by rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 32. Henry runs when the heart is enlarged: the choosing Christian sticks to testimonies with unchanged affection through trials and may expect not to be put to shame — and when God by his Spirit gives wisdom, sheds abroad love and puts gladness in the heart, that joy becomes wheels to obedience, pressing forward and redeeming time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 33. Henry begs God himself as teacher despite prophets, priests and his own learning, for none teaches like him: teach not notions or language but the way of statutes applied to self, the word behind saying This is the way, walk in it — promising constancy to the end as surest proof of sincerity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 34. Henry asks understanding as God of grace, not merely nature's faculties: an enlightened, sanctified understanding to use powers about things belonging to peace, since revelation in the word needs wisdom in the heart — and then obedience will be cordial, with the whole heart, for that way the whole man goes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 35. Henry prays God to bow the will as well as enlighten the head, for God works both to will and to do: make me able, strengthening for every good work by his Spirit causing us to walk in statutes, and make me willing, subduing natural aversion — duty done with delight when the heart is inclined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 36. Henry prays against covetousness, the sin opposed to all testimonies, root of much sin: that love of God may be rooted in, love of the world must be rooted out, for world-friendship is enmity with God — and God deals not by compulsion but draws with cords of a man toward good and away from evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 37. Henry asks restraining and constraining grace together: turn eyes from beholding vanity — honours, pleasures, profits whose prospect infects the heart and alienates from God, covenanting with eyes as Job did — and quicken me in thy way to redeem time and press forward, for gazing travellers rid no ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 38. Henry defines the good man as God's servant devoted to his fear, subject to law, employed in work, governed in affections by fear of God — and such may boldly pray God establish his word, fulfilling promises in due time and assuring meanwhile; what God promised we must pray for, neither more nor less. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 39. Henry appeals from men's censures to God's good judgments: conscious of giving occasion for blasphemy, he prays God who holds hearts and tongues prevent reproach falling on religion through his side, for God's throne judgments are right and kind to the wronged, and it is small to be judged by man's judgment while the Lord judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 40. Henry professes longing that grows with tasting: not only loving and delighting in what is attained, but earnestly desiring to know more and do better, pressing toward perfection — and prays quickening in righteousness, for where God wrought desire he will satisfy it, and where he wrought to will he will work to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 132. Henry asks only a good look, neighbour's fare: look graciously, smiles and light of countenance, cognizance of affairs, sweetness and gifts of mercy — not operations of hand, only face — pleaded as one loving God's name, desiring no better than dealings reserved for lovers, eye-hath-not-seen mercies, truly and eternally happy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 133. Henry prays direction and deliverance equally earnest: order my steps in thy word, every step under grace's guidance, motions kept within bounds and carried in prescribed paths, not trifling — and let no iniquity gain consent or lead captive, dominion dreaded and deprecated, answered by promise, sin shall not have dominion over you. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 134. Henry seeks quiet life to serve better: deliver from oppression of man, whose power God controls and limits, that enemies' hands loose bonds for cheerfuller, enlarged keeping — not that he would not keep under oppression, but temporal blessings may be expected when desired with serving God better in eye. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 135. Henry, king glorying in servant title, is ambitious of Master's favour and solicitous for Master's work: asks not corn, wine, silver or gold, but make thy face shine, accepted and knowing it, comfort in cloudy days though world frowns — and teach me statutes, praying as earnestly for grace as comfort, for hidden face follows careless keeping, and wisdom qualifies for favour's return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 136. Henry weeps rivers because others keep not law: great sorrow, gracious heart with weeping eye conformed to Christ, man of sorrows — pleading qualification for comfort as mourner in Zion who shall be comforted, grieving not chiefly his own wrongs but dishonour done to God by law-breaking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 137. Henry confesses threefold righteousness: God's nature infinite rectitude, nothing wanting or amiss, will eternal rule of equity; his government never wronging any creature, promises, threatenings and executions upright, every word pure and true to merits; and his commands exceedingly righteous and faithful, requiring us act like him, just to all and true to engagements. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 138. Henry repeats that testimonies commanded with sovereign authority are righteousness and faithfulness itself: what we practise is righteous, what we believe faithful — necessary conviction for faith and obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 139. Henry's zeal consumes him over forgotten words: enemies heard God's words yet willingly cast them behind backs, forgetting at bottom of all wickedness and enmity to God's people — and David, in league offensive and defensive with religion, felt indignation preying on spirits like Christ's zeal, constraining him to do more in religion himself: the worse others, the better we should be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 140. Henry loves word for its purity: every servant of God loves it as revealing Master's will and directing Master's work, warm attachment wherever grace is — and love is evidence of love to God when loved for purity, bearing holiness' image, commanding purity, refined from corrupt mixture, refining us from worldliness and fleshly-mindedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 141. Henry, small and despised yet not forgetting: man after God's heart, honoured by King of kings, yet low in own and others' account, for excellency often exposes to scorn — and poor yet pious, refusing to throw off religion though it brings contempt, for contempt tries constancy, and low condition most needs precepts' support. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 142. Henry binds reason and subject together: word is everlasting righteousness, rule of judgment consonant to eternal counsels, determining everlasting state, reverenced as standard — and law is truth, double obligation twisted as cords: truth ruling understanding, law binding will, God's truth a law and law truth, inseparable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 143. Henry raises constancy higher in trouble and anguish: trouble without, anguish within surprising, seizing, holding — yet commandments are delights, mouth not put out of taste, peace and pleasure calamities cannot deprive, sweetest enjoyment of word's variety often in trouble, as in 2 Corinthians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 144. Henry infers understanding and life from everlasting righteousness: testimonies unalterable, admitted in power an abiding well of living water — meditate on equity and eternity, pray not for further revelation but further understanding of what is revealed, and then live for ever, eternally happy and presently comforted in prospect, for life eternal is knowing God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 145. Henry prays inwardly and importunately: not boasting or trusting merit, but comforted to have taken appointed way — heart going with prayer, lip-labour alone lost, crying with whole heart, all soul's powers exerted, directed to Father, seeking comprehensive salvation, rescue and happiness, and looking after prayers to see what becomes of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 146. Henry binds purpose to petition: I will keep thy statutes by grace, for turning ear from law forfeits answer of peace — and pleads it humbly, save me from sins, corruptions, temptations and hindrances, not for ease but opportunity to serve God more cheerfully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 147. Henry prevents dawn by hope: crying before morning, encouraged to instant prayer though answer delayed, vision for appointed time speaking at end and not lying — hope in word that will not fail, beginning day with God while mind fresh, first thoughts keeping fear all day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 148. Henry redeems night-watches by meditation: awaking from first sleep, he would meditate and pray rather than turn again, esteeming God's words more than necessary repose as Job esteemed them more than food — for reading alone will not serve, meditation teaches God's own language and what to pray as we ought, and throne of grace is never unseasonable, for Israel's God never slumbers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 149. Henry begs quickening in little with much: Lord, hear my voice, shall I obtain gracious audience? — quicken me, stir habits of grace into act, vigorous, lively, cheerful — depending on lovingkindness, good God being good to hopers in mercy, and on judgment, wisdom knowing need and promise securing mercy by new covenant's tenor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 150. Henry contrasts nearness: mischief-followers industrious, letting slip no opportunity, impious and far from law, out of reach of its convictions, drawing nigh at his heels, a step from death — yet God brings his people into imminent peril, as Jacob, that like him they may wrestle for a blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 151. Henry assures nearness beats nearness: they draw nigh to destroy, but thou art near, O Lord, to save — mightier and nearer, ready to help, never far to seek, within our call — and all commandments are truth, inviolably true and infallibly performed, beyond enemies' power to defeat promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 152. Henry confirms founded-for-ever testimonies: covenant commanded to a thousand generations, firm as truth itself, framed for perpetuity, well ordered and sure, every iota standing when heaven and earth pass — known of old from youth by grace persuading his heart and providence fulfilling beyond expectation, owned faithful by all who ever dealt with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 153. Henry prays pity, power and advocacy together: consider affliction, take all circumstances into thought, not sitting unconcerned — God never unmindful, yet will have us put him in remembrance and spread the case, leaving it to compassionate wisdom in his time and way — and deliver, as promised, with submission and regard to glory that we may serve better. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 154. Henry pleads cause and quickening by word: plead my cause as patron and advocate against many mighty adversaries, clearing integrity and silencing false accusations, for if God does not plead his people's cause, who will? — and quicken weak, drooping spirit till deliverance wrought, according to promise, for closer cleaving to word as rule and stay gives more assurance of due deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 155. Henry dooms the unseeking: wicked do not merely disobey but do not so much as seek statutes, not acquainting themselves, desiring duty, or endeavouring — resolved to live at large in heart's way — so salvation is far, temporal deliverance groundless, eternal certainly far, thrust away by thrusting Saviour away, further the longer sin persists, while damnation slumbers not and Judge stands at door. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 156. Henry admires tender mercies great and many, a fountain never exhausted, rich to all who call: God good notwithstanding wicked misery, mercies sufficient to have saved them had they not despised riches — and begs reviving grace according to judgments, new covenant's tenor or God's manner with lovers of his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 157. Henry stands amid many persecutors: Saul's abused authority drawing multitudes, public person with many enemies yet many friends to set against them, type of Christ and church whose persecutors are very many — yet not declining, knowing while adhering God is for him, no matter who against, steady duty fearing none. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 158. Henry grieves at transgressors, not for vexation but provocation: looking abroad at open sinners numerous, daring, impudent, industriously snaring unstable souls, dishonouring God, serving Satan, debauching world, ruining souls — grief to all regarding God's glory and mankind's welfare, hating sin truly as transgression of God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 159. Henry appeals to love, not performance: consider how I love thy precepts, thou knowest all things — not consider how I fulfil them, for in many things he came short — but love as principle making obedience pleasing and pleasant, and begs quickening according to lovingkindness, not merit, for to that we owe lives, nay it is better than life itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 160. Henry comforts with word true from beginning and enduring for ever: always faithful hitherto, never failing any venturing, church built on this rock from its beginning, validity not gained by lapse like governments by prescription, but true at beginning — and faithful to end because righteous, every judgment unalterable, perpetual, adjusting everlasting doom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 161. Henry stands in awe of word above princes: persecuted without cause as traitor, bidden serve other gods, princes with sword and law's colour doing hurt with reputation — sad power from God used against him — yet heart stands in awe of God's word, authority of precepts and terror of threatenings, resolving to please God though all the world frowns, obeying God rather than men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 162. Henry rejoices as one finding great spoil: pleasure in reading, hearing, meditating, every meeting agreeable, rejoicing God discovered his mind and Israel blessed with light while nations sat in darkness — and more reverence brings more joy, spoil supposing victory through opposition, word making us more than conquerors, unspeakable gainers by plunder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 163. Henry fixes leading affections aright: rooted antipathy to lying taken for all sin — treachery with God, cheat upon ourselves, hypocrisy, false doctrine, breach of faith, commerce or conversation lies doubly hated, for a lying tongue and false witness are among seven things the Lord hates — and rooted affection to law, loved for its truth, beauty of truth revealing deformity of lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 164. Henry praises seven times a day without complaints jostling praises out of tune: very frequently, not weekly or twice daily, at every meal and occasion, in everything giving thanks, subject never exhausted and affections never tired — praising righteous judgments, precepts just and good, promises and threatenings performed in providence, even afflictions if grace gets good by them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 165. Henry gives lovers of law great peace and no offence: holy serenity, abundant satisfaction in duty and reflecting on it, work of righteousness as peace world can neither give nor take, troubles without yet peace within — and holy security, no event an invincible temptation or intolerable affliction, integrity held and tranquillity preserved, everything working for good and reconciled, without needless scruples or offence at brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 166. Henry joins end and rule, never to be put asunder: eye on God's favour as end, hoping temporal and eternal salvation as happiness of God's preparing, promising and being-with, raised above world and borne under burdens — and eye on word as rule, conscience conforming in everything; hope without commandment-keeping is groundless, sincere endeavour ought to keep hope, and livelier hope makes livelier obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 167. Henry witnesses soul-keeping and superlative love: loving testimonies exceedingly, better than wealth and pleasure, victorious love subduing lusts and extirpating carnal affections — and soul keeping them, heart-work not bodily exercise, soul sanctified, delivered into word's mould, employed glorifying God in spirit, keeping precepts by obedience and promises by reliance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 168. Henry lives under God's eye: therefore kept precepts because by faith he saw God's eye always upon him, every step known and strictly observed, seeing and accepting well-done, seeing and displeased with amiss — consideration that should make us very careful in everything, as walking before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 169. Henry cries and supplicates as beggar: prayer as cry denoting fervency, supplication denoting humility, concerned it come near before God, lifted by faith and fervency, no guilt interposing, graciously received — with deep unworthiness and fear of miscarrying, access only because Christ approached as advocate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 170. Henry asks wisdom and rescue according to word: understanding as wisdom of prudent to understand his way, know God, self and duty — and deliverance, with temptation a way to escape — directed and limited by promise, asking no other understanding or deliverance than promised, and expecting God to be as good as his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 171. Henry promises lips uttering praise when taught: teaching taken for granted after importunate, Jacob-like wrestling not letting go without spiritual blessing — taught of God have foundation of all spiritual blessings and earnest of eternal, know how to praise when understanding, heart and lips are opened, and pray for grace aiming at God's glory; learned nothing to purpose without learning praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 172. Henry speaks word because commandments are righteousness itself: knowing it with satisfaction as rule and standard, tongue shall not only utter praise to God's glory but discourse for others' instruction, full heart speaking, desiring others filled — more sight of righteousness makes more industry to bring others under rule, word governing discourse against sinful speaking and silence, and feeding many with grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 173. Henry pleads chosen precepts for helping hand: own hands insufficient, no creature helping to purpose, hand that made him must help — and choice as pledge, religion serious and deliberate, taken on trial as knowing no better, not by chance; choosers may depend on helping hand in services and sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 174. Henry longs for salvation while delighting in law: heart upon heaven despite throne's worldly courts saying it is good to be here, looking further to eternal salvation all saints long for — and law as greatest delight, cheerful obedience begging help to carry on, hopes increasing delight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 175. Henry asks life to praise and judgments to help praise: prolonged life not to serve country or provide for family chiefly, but to praise God in land of living, doing honour in conflict — soul living as sanctified and comforted, fitted by grace and peace — and ordinances and providences, both God's judgments, furthering glorifying, matter of praise and fitting for work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 176. Henry closes penitent, believing and obedient: gone astray wandering like lost sheep, as unconverted sinners and weak unsteady saints, apt to wander and unapt to find way, losing green pastures' comfort and exposing to mischiefs — yet seek thy servant as good shepherd seeks wandering sheep, David once tender shepherd himself, own me for thine with thy mark, send after me by word, conscience and providences, find me for God never seeks in vain — though strayed, not wickedly departed, not forgetting commandments, so wanderers mindful of duty may humbly commit to grace's care, living and dying repenting and praying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
