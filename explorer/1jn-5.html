The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 John 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ is born of God: and every one that loveth him that begat loveth him also that is begotten of him.

2 By this we know that we love the children of God, when we love God, and keep his commandments.

3 For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.

4 For whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith.

5 Who is he that overcometh the world, but he that believeth that Jesus is the Son of God?

6 This is he that came by water and blood, even Jesus Christ; not by water only, but by water and blood. And it is the Spirit that beareth witness, because the Spirit is truth.

7 For there are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one.

8 And there are three that bear witness in earth, the Spirit, and the water, and the blood: and these three agree in one.

9 If we receive the witness of men, the witness of God is greater: for this is the witness of God which he hath testified of his Son.

10 He that believeth on the Son of God hath the witness in himself: he that believeth not God hath made him a liar; because he believeth not the record that God gave of his Son.

11 And this is the record, that God hath given to us eternal life, and this life is in his Son.

12 He that hath the Son hath life; and he that hath not the Son of God hath not life.

13 These things have I written unto you that believe on the name of the Son of God; that ye may know that ye have eternal life, and that ye may believe on the name of the Son of God.

14 And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us:

15 And if we know that he hear us, whatsoever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we desired of him.

16 If any man see his brother sin a sin which is not unto death, he shall ask, and he shall give him life for them that sin not unto death. There is a sin unto death: I do not say that he shall pray for it.

17 All unrighteousness is sin: and there is a sin not unto death.

18 We know that whosoever is born of God sinneth not; but he that is begotten of God keepeth himself, and that wicked one toucheth him not.

19 And we know that we are of God, and the whole world lieth in wickedness.

20 And we know that the Son of God is come, and hath given us an understanding, that we may know him that is true, and we are in him that is true, even in his Son Jesus Christ. This is the true God, and eternal life.

21 Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Everyone believing Jesus is the Christ is born of God, the writer says, and loving God and keeping his commandments, which are not burdensome, is how love for God's children is known, overcoming the world through faith. The witness is threefold, water, blood and Spirit, with three bearing witness in heaven and three on earth, and eternal life is in the Son: whoever has the Son has life. Confidence in prayer, the distinction between sin unto death and sin not unto death, and the closing guard, keep yourselves from idols, complete the letter. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Whoever believes Jesus is the Christ is born of God, and everyone who loves him who begot loves also the one begotten of him. True love for God's people is distinguished from natural kindness or party attachment by being united with love of God and obedience to his commands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. By this we know we love the children of God: when we love God and keep his commandments. The same Spirit that taught love will also have taught obedience, and a person cannot truly love God's children while habitually committing sin or neglecting known duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. This is the love of God, that we keep his commandments, and his commandments are not grievous. As holy, just, and good rules of liberty and happiness, they are not counted burdensome by those born of God, who lament only that they cannot serve him more perfectly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Whatever is born of God overcomes the world, and the victory that overcomes the world is our faith. Faith is the cause, means, instrument, and spiritual armour of victory, carrying believers above hindrances, though the conflict is often sharp and the regenerate may be cast down and rise again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Who overcomes the world but the one who believes Jesus is the Son of God? The real Christian, seeing in Christ's earthly life that this world is to be renounced and overcome, cannot be satisfied with it but presses toward heaven, for we must overcome the world or it will overcome us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. This is he who came by water and blood, Jesus Christ, not by water only but by water and blood, and the Spirit bears witness because the Spirit is truth. Henry explains the water as washing and purification for heaven, and the blood as justification and reconciliation, both coming from the side of the sacrificed Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. There are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost, and these three are one. Henry notes the Father's voice from heaven declaring Jesus his beloved Son, the Word declaring he and the Father are one, and the Holy Ghost descending on Christ at his baptism and testifying by the prophets and apostles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. There are three that bear witness on earth, the Spirit, the water, and the blood, and these three agree in one. The Spirit's regenerating work, the water's picture of purity and holiness, and the blood's ransom all testify to the same person and salvation, for one and the same purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. If we receive the witness of men, the witness of God is greater, for this is the witness God testified of his Son. Henry calls it absurd to doubt Christianity while proceeding on human testimony in common affairs, and deeming out of his senses anyone who declined to do so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Whoever believes on the Son of God has the witness in himself, having felt the power of Christ's word humbling, healing, quickening, and comforting the soul, while whoever does not believe God has made him a liar. Unbelief is awful precisely because it rejects the record God gave of his Son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. This is the record: God has given us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. The gift is not a thing apart from Christ but is located in him, so that possessing the Son and possessing life stand or fall together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Whoever has the Son has life, and whoever does not have the Son of God does not have life. No outward morality, learning, forms, or confidences can substitute for the Son, and one who refuses to trust his teaching, rely on his atonement, or obey him as King remains dead in sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. These things are written to those who believe on the name of the Son of God, that they may know they have eternal life and may go on believing. Believers already hold eternal life in the covenant of the gospel, and the letter's purpose is assured knowledge, not anxious guessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. This is the confidence we have in him: if we ask anything according to his will, he hears us. Prayer must always be offered in submission to God's will, and Christ invites believers to come in all circumstances, notwithstanding the sin that besets them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. If we know he hears us, whatever we ask, we know we have the petitions we desired of him. Some prayers are speedily answered and others granted in the best manner though not as requested, but a hearing God is the ground of certainty either way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. If a brother sins a sin not unto death, prayer should be made for him, and life will be given to such sinners, but for a sin unto death John does not say prayer for forgiveness should be made. Henry explains the limit: we cannot pray that impenitent, unbelieving sin be forgiven while the sinner wilfully continues in it, though we may pray for repentance and faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. All unrighteousness is sin, and there is a sin not unto death. Sins differ in their war against spiritual life, and the distinction matters for prayer: fallen believers are to be prayed for, recovered, and relieved, while no sin of which anyone truly repents is unto death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. We know that whoever is born of God does not go on sinning, but the one begotten of God keeps himself, and the wicked one does not touch him. This repeats the letter's central incompatibility between the new birth and the practice of sin, now as a ground of assurance rather than warning alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. We know that we are of God, and the whole world lies in wickedness. All mankind divide into two dominions, God's and the wicked one's, and this general declaration includes unbelievers of every profession, station, and name, who do the wicked one's works and support his cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. We know the Son of God has come and given us understanding, that we may know him who is true, and we are in him who is true, even in his Son Jesus Christ, who is the true God and eternal life. The Son leads believers to the Father, and they are in the love and favour of both by the indwelling Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Little children, keep yourselves from idols. The letter closes with a guard against every false god and false doctrine, and against the idolatrous love of worldly objects, so that believers are kept by God's power through faith unto eternal salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
