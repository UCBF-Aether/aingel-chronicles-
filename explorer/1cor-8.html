The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Corinthians 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now as touching things offered unto idols, we know that we all have knowledge. Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth.

2 And if any man think that he knoweth any thing, he knoweth nothing yet as he ought to know.

3 But if any man love God, the same is known of him.

4 As concerning therefore the eating of those things that are offered in sacrifice unto idols, we know that an idol is nothing in the world, and that there is none other God but one.

5 For though there be that are called gods, whether in heaven or in earth, (as there be gods many, and lords many,)

6 But to us there is but one God, the Father, of whom are all things, and we in him; and one Lord Jesus Christ, by whom are all things, and we by him.

7 Howbeit there is not in every man that knowledge: for some with conscience of the idol unto this hour eat it as a thing offered unto an idol; and their conscience being weak is defiled.

8 But meat commendeth us not to God: for neither, if we eat, are we the better; neither, if we eat not, are we the worse.

9 But take heed lest by any means this liberty of yours become a stumblingblock to them that are weak.

10 For if any man see thee which hast knowledge sit at meat in the idol’s temple, shall not the conscience of him which is weak be emboldened to eat those things which are offered to idols;

11 And through thy knowledge shall the weak brother perish, for whom Christ died?

12 But when ye sin so against the brethren, and wound their weak conscience, ye sin against Christ.

13 Wherefore, if meat make my brother to offend, I will eat no flesh while the world standeth, lest I make my brother to offend.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

About food offered to idols, Paul agrees that an idol is nothing and there is one God, but warns that knowledge puffs up while love builds up, and not everyone has this knowledge. A brother with a weak conscience may be emboldened to eat and so be destroyed, and therefore Paul says he would eat no flesh at all rather than make his brother stumble. Interpreted Overview of this chapter's text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. As for food offered to idols — portions of temple victims belonged to priests and offerers and were eaten at temple and private feasts or sold in markets, so Christians constantly faced the temptation. The Corinthians appealed to their knowledge that such meats are indifferent, and Paul agrees in theory, but adds the decisive qualification: knowledge puffs up when without love, while love edifies, building up the spiritual temple. Knowledge says all things are lawful; love adds that all things do not edify. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. If anyone thinks he knows something, he does not yet know as he ought to know. The first step to knowledge is knowing one's own ignorance, and without love there is only the appearance of knowledge — personal experimental acquaintance, not mere awareness of a fact. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. If anyone loves God — the source of love to neighbor — that man is known by God, known with the knowledge of approval and acknowledged as his. To love God is to know God, and he who thus knows God has first been known by God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. As for eating food offered to idols, an idol is nothing — it has no true being, since the god it supposedly represents is not a living reality. This does not contradict the truth that idolaters worship devils, for it is the supposed gods, not the deluding devils behind them, that are denied existence. There is no God but one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Even supposing there are so-called gods, whether in heaven like sun, moon, and stars, or on earth like deified kings and beasts — as indeed there are many gods and many lords in men's reckoning — angels and men in authority are called gods in Scripture only as exercising divinely delegated power under God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. To believers there is one God, the Father, from whom as Creator all things derive existence, and for whom and for whose glory believers live. And there is one Lord, Jesus Christ, contrasted with the many lords of heathendom, by whom are all things, and by whom believers are restored to the Father by the new creation. Father and Son are entirely one in this work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Yet this knowledge is not in everyone in the same degree. Some Gentile Christians, from old association of ideas or misdirected conscience, still ate such meats feeling as if the idol were something real that had made the meat holy or polluted. Their conscience was defiled by eating consciously as a thing offered to idols, sinning against conscience, since they lacked the knowledge that an idol is nothing and can neither pollute nor sanctify meat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Meat does not commend us to God: neither if we eat are we the better, nor if we do not eat are we the worse. Food in itself does not affect our standing before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. But take heed lest this liberty of yours — the watchword of lax Corinthians — become a stumbling-block to the weak. The very indifference of meats is the reason for care: liberty must not tempt weak brethren to act against conscience, which itself constitutes sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. If a weak brother sees you, a man with knowledge, reclining in an idol's temple, his weak conscience will be emboldened — literally built up — to eat what he believes wrong. You ought to have built him up in good, but your example builds him up to violate his conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And so the weak brother perishes through your knowledge — a single seemingly unimportant act may have everlasting consequences, for he loses his faith and, if he does not recover it, his salvation. This is the brother for whom Christ died, and for whose sake believers ought to be willing to die, rather than tempt him toward damnation while sacrificing nothing for his salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. To sin against the brethren and wound their weak conscience — literally to smite a conscience still in a weak state, as if striking an invalid — is to sin against Christ, because of the sympathy between Christ and his members. What is done to the weak member is done to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Therefore, if food makes my brother stumble, I will eat no flesh at all while the world stands, lest I make my brother stumble. To ensure avoiding idol-offered flesh, Paul would abstain from every kind of flesh rather than be a stumbling-block. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • things offered unto idols. Meat from temple sacrifices, portions of which were eaten in idol feasts or sold in the market. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • weak conscience. A conscience still associating idol food with idol worship, which knowledge alone has not yet freed. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
