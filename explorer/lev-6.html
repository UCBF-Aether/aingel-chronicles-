The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 If a soul sin, and commit a trespass against the LORD, and lie unto his neighbour in that which was delivered him to keep, or in fellowship, or in a thing taken away by violence, or hath deceived his neighbour;

3 Or have found that which was lost, and lieth concerning it, and sweareth falsely; in any of all these that a man doeth, sinning therein:

4 Then it shall be, because he hath sinned, and is guilty, that he shall restore that which he took violently away, or the thing which he hath deceitfully gotten, or that which was delivered him to keep, or the lost thing which he found,

5 Or all that about which he hath sworn falsely; he shall even restore it in the principal, and shall add the fifth part more thereto, and give it unto him to whom it appertaineth, in the day of his trespass offering.

6 And he shall bring his trespass offering unto the LORD, a ram without blemish out of the flock, with thy estimation, for a trespass offering, unto the priest:

7 And the priest shall make an atonement for him before the LORD: and it shall be forgiven him for any thing of all that he hath done in trespassing therein.

8 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

9 Command Aaron and his sons, saying, This is the law of the burnt offering: It is the burnt offering, because of the burning upon the altar all night unto the morning, and the fire of the altar shall be burning in it.

10 And the priest shall put on his linen garment, and his linen breeches shall he put upon his flesh, and take up the ashes which the fire hath consumed with the burnt offering on the altar, and he shall put them beside the altar.

11 And he shall put off his garments, and put on other garments, and carry forth the ashes without the camp unto a clean place.

12 And the fire upon the altar shall be burning in it; it shall not be put out: and the priest shall burn wood on it every morning, and lay the burnt offering in order upon it; and he shall burn thereon the fat of the peace offerings.

13 The fire shall ever be burning upon the altar; it shall never go out.

14 And this is the law of the meat offering: the sons of Aaron shall offer it before the LORD, before the altar.

15 And he shall take of it his handful, of the flour of the meat offering, and of the oil thereof, and all the frankincense which is upon the meat offering, and shall burn it upon the altar for a sweet savour, even the memorial of it, unto the LORD.

16 And the remainder thereof shall Aaron and his sons eat: with unleavened bread shall it be eaten in the holy place; in the court of the tabernacle of the congregation they shall eat it.

17 It shall not be baken with leaven. I have given it unto them for their portion of my offerings made by fire; it is most holy, as is the sin offering, and as the trespass offering.

18 All the males among the children of Aaron shall eat of it. It shall be a statute for ever in your generations concerning the offerings of the LORD made by fire: every one that toucheth them shall be holy.

19 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

20 This is the offering of Aaron and of his sons, which they shall offer unto the LORD in the day when he is anointed; the tenth part of an ephah of fine flour for a meat offering perpetual, half of it in the morning, and half thereof at night.

21 In a pan it shall be made with oil; and when it is baken, thou shalt bring it in: and the baken pieces of the meat offering shalt thou offer for a sweet savour unto the LORD.

22 And the priest of his sons that is anointed in his stead shall offer it: it is a statute for ever unto the LORD; it shall be wholly burnt.

23 For every meat offering for the priest shall be wholly burnt: it shall not be eaten.

24 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

25 Speak unto Aaron and to his sons, saying, This is the law of the sin offering: In the place where the burnt offering is killed shall the sin offering be killed before the LORD: it is most holy.

26 The priest that offereth it for sin shall eat it: in the holy place shall it be eaten, in the court of the tabernacle of the congregation.

27 Whatsoever shall touch the flesh thereof shall be holy: and when there is sprinkled of the blood thereof upon any garment, thou shalt wash that whereon it was sprinkled in the holy place.

28 But the earthen vessel wherein it is sodden shall be broken: and if it be sodden in a brasen pot, it shall be both scoured, and rinsed in water.

29 All the males among the priests shall eat thereof: it is most holy.

30 And no sin offering, whereof any of the blood is brought into the tabernacle of the congregation to reconcile withal in the holy place, shall be eaten: it shall be burnt in the fire.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The first half of this chapter finishes the trespass law: wrongs against a neighbour, such as lying over a deposit, fraud, or keeping lost property, require full restoration with a fifth added, and then a ram as a trespass offering. The second half turns to the priests' own duties: the altar fire must never go out, the meat offering's remainder feeds the priests, the high priest offers a daily meat offering wholly burnt, and the rules for eating the sin offering are set out, including that no sin offering whose blood enters the sanctuary may be eaten. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. This law belongs with the previous chapter and covers witting trespass against the LORD expressed against a neighbour, such as lying over a deposit or fellowship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Things stolen, fraudulently gotten, or wrongfully kept are all covered, and the offender must restore the article to its rightful owner together with a fifth part from his own possessions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Finding a lost thing and lying about it with a false oath is likewise a trespass, since the sin lies in the deceitful keeping of what belongs to another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Because he has sinned and is guilty, he must restore what was taken violently, gotten deceitfully, delivered to keep, or found and kept. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Whatever was sworn about falsely is restored in full principal with a fifth part more, given to its owner on the day of his trespass offering, so restitution precedes sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Repair to neighbour and society is not enough; he must also bring a ram without blemish at the priest's estimation as a trespass offering, as a token of sorrow for having hurt the cause of religion and of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The priest makes atonement before the LORD, and he is forgiven for anything he has done in trespassing, the penalty acting as a mitigated fine joined to a sacred duty that revives responsibility to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. A new section of priestly instruction begins, addressed to Aaron and his sons concerning their official duties in the daily service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The burnt offering, a sacrifice that goes up in smoke, consists of two daily lambs, morning and evening, consumed by a slow fire so placed that the pieces feed it all night, a daily expression of national repentance and faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The priest, in linen garment and linen breeches, takes up the ashes the fire has consumed and puts them beside the altar, tending the heaven-kindled fire so it is not extinguished or replaced with common fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Changing out of his holy garments into others, he carries the ashes without the camp to a clean place, keeping the approach to the fireplace itself in officially holy garments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Wood is burnt on the altar every morning and the burnt offering laid in order upon it, with the fat of the peace offerings, so the fire is continually fed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The fire shall ever be burning and never go out, preserving the fire kindled from heaven at the tabernacle's consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The law of the meat offering follows, though it is a provision for the priests and their families it is to be regarded as most holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The priest carries the meat offering to the altar, takes a handful of flour and oil with all the frankincense as an oblation, salts it, and burns it for a sweet savour as the memorial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The remainder is eaten by Aaron and his sons with unleavened bread in the holy place, in the court of the tabernacle, as the food of those who attend the service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. It shall not be baked with leaven, for it is their portion of the offerings made by fire and is most holy, as are the sin offering and the trespass offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. All the males among Aaron's children eat of it by a perpetual statute, and everyone who touches these offerings shall be holy, while no one under ceremonial defilement may partake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The high priest's own meat offering is now prescribed for the day of his anointing, a perpetual offering distinct from the ordinary priests' portions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. This daily meat offering of the high priest, a tenth of an ephah of fine flour, half in the morning and half at night, was probably meant for his successors in the high office, and was wholly consumed on the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Made in a pan with oil and brought in baked in pieces, it is offered for a sweet savour, designed to remind the priests that their own persons and services are accepted only through faith daily nourished from above. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The priest anointed in Aaron's stead offers this same offering by a perpetual statute, wholly burnt to the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Every meat offering for a priest shall be wholly burnt and not eaten, unlike the ordinary meat offerings whose remainder feeds the priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. A further priestly law is spoken to Aaron and his sons, now concerning the sin offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The sin offering is killed where the burnt offering is killed before the LORD and is most holy, its fat and inwards being washed, salted, and burnt on the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The officiating priest eats the sin offering in the holy place, in the court of the tabernacle, the rest of the carcass belonging to him and his family alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Whatever touches its flesh shall be holy, and blood sprinkled on a garment must be washed in a holy place, these minute ceremonies impressing the evil nature of sin and the care to prevent any taint clinging to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. An earthen vessel in which the flesh is boiled must be broken, being porous and likely to imbibe particles, while a bronze pot must be scoured and rinsed, not because the vessels are defiled but because they are now too sacred for ordinary use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. All the males among the priests may eat of it, since it is most holy, but only within the precincts of the tabernacle and only if free from ceremonial defilement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. No sin offering whose blood is brought into the tabernacle to reconcile in the holy place, as for the high priest or the whole congregation, shall be eaten; it shall be burnt in the fire outside the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
