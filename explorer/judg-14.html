The Text — Judges 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Samson went down to Timnath, and saw a woman in Timnath of the daughters of the Philistines.

2 And he came up, and told his father and his mother, and said, I have seen a woman in Timnath of the daughters of the Philistines: now therefore get her for me to wife.

3 Then his father and his mother said unto him, Is there never a woman among the daughters of thy brethren, or among all my people, that thou goest to take a wife of the uncircumcised Philistines? And Samson said unto his father, Get her for me; for she pleaseth me well.

4 But his father and his mother knew not that it was of the LORD, that he sought an occasion against the Philistines: for at that time the Philistines had dominion over Israel.

5 Then went Samson down, and his father and his mother, to Timnath, and came to the vineyards of Timnath: and, behold, a young lion roared against him.

6 And the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him, and he rent him as he would have rent a kid, and he had nothing in his hand: but he told not his father or his mother what he had done.

7 And he went down, and talked with the woman; and she pleased Samson well.

8 And after a time he returned to take her, and he turned aside to see the carcase of the lion: and, behold, there was a swarm of bees and honey in the carcase of the lion.

9 And he took thereof in his hands, and went on eating, and came to his father and mother, and he gave them, and they did eat: but he told not them that he had taken the honey out of the carcase of the lion.

10 So his father went down unto the woman: and Samson made there a feast; for so used the young men to do.

11 And it came to pass, when they saw him, that they brought thirty companions to be with him.

12 And Samson said unto them, I will now put forth a riddle unto you: if ye can certainly declare it me within the seven days of the feast, and find it out, then I will give you thirty sheets and thirty change of garments:

13 But if ye cannot declare it me, then shall ye give me thirty sheets and thirty change of garments. And they said unto him, Put forth thy riddle, that we may hear it.

14 And he said unto them, Out of the eater came forth meat, and out of the strong came forth sweetness. And they could not in three days expound the riddle.

15 And it came to pass on the seventh day, that they said unto Samson’s wife, Entice thy husband, that he may declare unto us the riddle, lest we burn thee and thy father’s house with fire: have ye called us to take that we have? is it not so?

16 And Samson’s wife wept before him, and said, Thou dost but hate me, and lovest me not: thou hast put forth a riddle unto the children of my people, and hast not told it me. And he said unto her, Behold, I have not told it my father nor my mother, and shall I tell it thee?

17 And she wept before him the seven days, while their feast lasted: and it came to pass on the seventh day, that he told her, because she lay sore upon him: and she told the riddle to the children of her people.

18 And the men of the city said unto him on the seventh day before the sun went down, What is sweeter than honey? And what is stronger than a lion? and he said unto them, If ye had not plowed with my heifer, ye had not found out my riddle.

19 And the Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he went down to Ashkelon, and slew thirty men of them, and took their spoil, and gave change of garments unto them which expounded the riddle. And his anger was kindled, and he went up to his father’s house.

20 But Samson’s wife was given to his companion, whom he had used as his friend.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Samson goes down to Timnath and insists on a Philistine wife, though his parents object; the chapter notes that the LORD was seeking an occasion against the Philistines, who then had dominion over Israel. On the way the Spirit comes on him and he tears a young lion apart with his bare hands; returning, he finds bees and honey in the carcass and makes his riddle of it at the wedding feast, out of the eater came meat, out of the strong came sweetness. His wife, pressed by her countrymen's threats, betrays the answer, and Samson pays the wager's thirty garments with spoil taken at Ashkelon, returning home in anger while his wife is given to his companion. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Samson went down to Timnath, now Tibna, about three miles from Zorah, and saw a Philistine woman, and JFB notes that in Philistine ascendency the two peoples often lived familiarly enough for matrimonial relations, and the Philistines were not among the seven devoted nations whose daughters the law forbade. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He told his father and mother and asked them to get her for him, since in the East parents negotiated their sons' marriages, and Samson followed that custom even in an unusual match. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. His parents asked whether no woman among his own tribe would do, rather than an uncircumcised Philistine, but Samson insisted, she pleaseth me well, and JFB explains she was right in his eyes, fit for his purpose, not merely beautiful, since the Lord sought an occasion against the Philistines through this private quarrel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. His father and mother knew not that it was of the Lord, that he sought an occasion against the Philistines, who then had dominion over Israel, and JFB notes the means God meant to use was not a numerous army but the miraculous prowess of a single-handed champion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. As Samson went down to Timnath with his parents, a young lion roared against him in the vineyards, and JFB notes the wild passes of Judah were lions' lairs, and the lion was in the pride of youthful prime. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The Spirit of the Lord came mightily upon him, and he rent the lion as he would a kid, with nothing in his hand, and told neither father nor mother, and JFB notes this superhuman strength, put forth in private incidents, trained him to trust it for his public work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He went down and talked with the woman, and she pleased Samson well, and JFB notes such social intercourse between young people in the East was rare and generally followed betrothal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. After a time he returned to take her, probably after the usual year between betrothal and marriage, and turning aside to see the lion's carcass, he found a swarm of bees and honey in it, which JFB explains by the climate's insects, birds, and sun reducing the carcass in a few months to a state inviting to bees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. He took the honey, went on eating, and gave some to his parents, who ate, but he told them not that he had taken it from the lion's carcass, keeping the wonder private as he had the killing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. His father went down to the woman, and Samson made a feast there, as young men used to do, and JFB notes the wedding festivity lasted a week, with the bride among her female relatives and Samson apart as a stranger, attended by paranymphs who were really spies on his proceedings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. When the Philistines saw him they brought thirty companions to be with him, the large number of bridegroom's friends furnished by the bride's family, ostensibly to honour the nuptials. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Samson put forth a riddle, offering thirty sheets and thirty changes of garments if they declared it within the seven feast days, and JFB notes riddles were a favourite Oriental amusement at such feasts, and Samson's riddle related to honey in the lion's carcass. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. If they could not declare it, they would give him thirty sheets and thirty garments, so that the wager bound both sides equally. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The riddle was, Out of the eater came forth meat, and out of the strong came forth sweetness, and for three days they could not declare it, as JFB notes, until the festive week drew to a close. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. On the seventh day they told Samson's wife to entice her husband and declare the riddle, lest they burn her and her father's house, and JFB notes they secretly enlisted the newly married wife, who got the secret and revealed it to her friends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Samson's wife wept before him, saying he hated her and did not love her, since he had put a riddle to her people and not told her, and her tears pressed the secret from him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. She wept the seven days of the feast, and on the seventh day he told her because she lay sore upon him, and she told the riddle to her people, completing the betrayal JFB traces to the companions' secret enlistment of the wife. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Before sunset on the seventh day the men of the city answered, What is sweeter than honey, and what is stronger than a lion, and Samson replied, If ye had not plowed with my heifer ye had not found out my riddle, and JFB explains the ploughing metaphor as resorting to his wife's aid, an unworthy expedient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The Spirit of the Lord came upon Samson, and he went to Ashkelon, slew thirty men, took their spoil, and gave the changes of garments to those who had expounded the riddle, and JFB notes Ashkelon, about twenty-four miles from Timnah, was chosen by the Spirit probably for its bitter hostility to Israel, though stripping a slain enemy was unknown in Hebrew warfare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Samson's anger was kindled, and he went up to his father's house, while his wife was given to his companion, whom he had used as his friend, and JFB calls this acceptance an act of base treachery, since the friend of the bridegroom had been the medium between husband and bride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
