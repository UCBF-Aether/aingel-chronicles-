The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 73 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Truly God is good to Israel, even to such as are of a clean heart.

2 But as for me, my feet were almost gone; my steps had well nigh slipped.

3 For I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked.

4 For there are no bands in their death: but their strength is firm.

5 They are not in trouble as other men; neither are they plagued like other men.

6 Therefore pride compasseth them about as a chain; violence covereth them as a garment.

7 Their eyes stand out with fatness: they have more than heart could wish.

8 They are corrupt, and speak wickedly concerning oppression: they speak loftily.

9 They set their mouth against the heavens, and their tongue walketh through the earth.

10 Therefore his people return hither: and waters of a full cup are wrung out to them.

11 And they say, How doth God know? and is there knowledge in the most High?

12 Behold, these are the ungodly, who prosper in the world; they increase in riches.

13 Verily I have cleansed my heart in vain, and washed my hands in innocency.

14 For all the day long have I been plagued, and chastened every morning.

15 If I say, I will speak thus; behold, I should offend against the generation of thy children.

16 When I thought to know this, it was too painful for me;

17 Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end.

18 Surely thou didst set them in slippery places: thou castedst them down into destruction.

19 How are they brought into desolation, as in a moment! they are utterly consumed with terrors.

20 As a dream when one awaketh; so, O Lord, when thou awakest, thou shalt despise their image.

21 Thus my heart was grieved, and I was pricked in my reins.

22 So foolish was I, and ignorant: I was as a beast before thee.

23 Nevertheless I am continually with thee: thou hast holden me by my right hand.

24 Thou shalt guide me with thy counsel, and afterward receive me to glory.

25 Whom have I in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee.

26 My flesh and my heart faileth: but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever.

27 For, lo, they that are far from thee shall perish: thou hast destroyed all them that go a whoring from thee.

28 But it is good for me to draw near to God: I have put my trust in the Lord GOD, that I may declare all thy works.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Envy cured in the sanctuary: thesis first, God is good to the clean in heart, then feet almost slipped envying the foolish, who die without pangs, wear pride as a chain and violence as a garment, mouth against heaven, tongue through earth, prospering and increasing in riches, while the speaker, plagued all day, thinks cleansing was in vain. Until he enters the sanctuary and sees their end, slippery places, desolation in a moment, a dream on waking. Foolish and beast-like, yet held by the right hand, guided by counsel, received to glory: whom have I in heaven but you? Flesh and heart failing, God is strength and portion for ever, and it is good to draw near. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Truly God is good to Israel, even to such as are of a clean heart: the thesis is stated first — God is good to Israel, to the clean in heart — before the doubt that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. But as for me, my feet were almost gone; my steps had well nigh slipped: almost gone feet, well-nigh slipped steps — the psalmist's faith nearly fell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked: envy at the foolish, at the wicked's prosperity, was the slipping cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For there are no bands in their death, but their strength is firm: no bands — no pangs — in their death, strength firm — the wicked die easy and strong, it seems. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. They are not in trouble as other men, neither are they plagued like other men: no common trouble, no common plague — exemption from ordinary suffering is their lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Therefore pride compasses them about as a chain; violence covers them as a garment: pride as a chain, violence as a garment — prosperity dresses them in pride and violence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Their eyes stand out with fatness; they have more than heart could wish: eyes standing out with fatness, more than heart could wish — excess beyond desire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They are corrupt, and speak wickedly concerning oppression; they speak loftily: corrupt, wicked speech about oppression, lofty speech — their talk matches their pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They set their mouth against the heavens, and their tongue walks through the earth: mouth against heaven, tongue walking through earth — blasphemy above, oppression below, in one speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Therefore his people return hither, and waters of a full cup are wrung out to them: God's people return hither — to this perplexity — and drink a full cup wrung out, of sorrow or of the wicked's teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And they say, How does God know? And is there knowledge in the Most High? 'How does God know?' and 'Is there knowledge in the Most High?' — the wicked's denial of divine knowledge is quoted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Behold, these are the ungodly, who prosper in the world; they increase in riches: behold the ungodly prospering in the world, increasing in riches — the fact that caused the envy, stated as conclusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Verily I have cleansed my heart in vain, and washed my hands in innocency: cleansing heart and washing hands in innocency seem vain — the psalmist's bitter inference. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. For all the day long have I been plagued, and chastened every morning: plagued all day, chastened every morning — the godly man's lot, against the wicked's ease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. If I say, I will speak thus, behold, I should offend against the generation of your children: speaking thus would offend God's children — loyalty to the generation restrains his doubt from becoming public unbelief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. When I thought to know this, it was too painful for me: thinking to know this was too painful — reason alone could not solve it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end: the sanctuary is the turning point — entering God's presence, he understood their end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Surely you did set them in slippery places; you cast them down into destruction: slippery places set, casting down into destruction — prosperity itself was the slippery ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. How are they brought into desolation, as in a moment! They are utterly consumed with terrors: desolation in a moment, utterly consumed with terrors — suddenness and completeness mark their fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. As a dream when one awakes, so, O Lord, when you awake, you shall despise their image: as a dream on waking, so God on awaking despises their image — their glory was a dream. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Thus my heart was grieved, and I was pricked in my reins: grieved heart, pricked reins — the envy had wounded him inwardly, and he now sees it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. So foolish was I, and ignorant; I was as a beast before you: foolish, ignorant, beast-like before God — the confession of his envy as brutishness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Nevertheless I am continually with you; you have held me by my right hand: nevertheless — despite beast-like folly — continually with God, held by the right hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. You shall guide me with your counsel, and afterward receive me to glory: guided by counsel now, received to glory afterward — guidance and glory are the two ends of God's holding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is none upon earth that I desire beside you: none in heaven but God, none on earth desired beside him — the psalm's climax of exclusive desire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. My flesh and my heart fail, but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever: failing flesh and heart, yet God as heart's strength and portion for ever — the answer to prosperity-envy is an everlasting portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. For, lo, they who are far from you shall perish; you have destroyed all them that go a whoring from you: far from God perishing, whoring from God destroyed — distance from God is itself ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. But it is good for me to draw near to God; I have put my trust in the Lord God, that I may declare all your works: good to draw near, trust in the Lord God, declaring all his works — nearness, trust, and testimony close the psalm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
