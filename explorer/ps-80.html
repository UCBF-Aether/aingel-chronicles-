The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 80 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Give ear, O Shepherd of Israel, thou that leadest Joseph like a flock; thou that dwellest between the cherubims, shine forth.

2 Before Ephraim and Benjamin and Manasseh stir up thy strength, and come and save us.

3 Turn us again, O God, and cause thy face to shine; and we shall be saved.

4 O LORD God of hosts, how long wilt thou be angry against the prayer of thy people?

5 Thou feedest them with the bread of tears; and givest them tears to drink in great measure.

6 Thou makest us a strife unto our neighbours: and our enemies laugh among themselves.

7 Turn us again, O God of hosts, and cause thy face to shine; and we shall be saved.

8 Thou hast brought a vine out of Egypt: thou hast cast out the heathen, and planted it.

9 Thou preparedst room before it, and didst cause it to take deep root, and it filled the land.

10 The hills were covered with the shadow of it, and the boughs thereof were like the goodly cedars.

11 She sent out her boughs unto the sea, and her branches unto the river.

12 Why hast thou then broken down her hedges, so that all they which pass by the way do pluck her?

13 The boar out of the wood doth waste it, and the wild beast of the field doth devour it.

14 Return, we beseech thee, O God of hosts: look down from heaven, and behold, and visit this vine;

15 And the vineyard which thy right hand hath planted, and the branch that thou madest strong for thyself.

16 It is burned with fire, it is cut down: they perish at the rebuke of thy countenance.

17 Let thy hand be upon the man of thy right hand, upon the son of man whom thou madest strong for thyself.

18 So will not we go back from thee: quicken us, and we will call upon thy name.

19 Turn us again, O LORD God of hosts, cause thy face to shine; and we shall be saved.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A repeated refrain — "Turn us again, O God, and cause thy face to shine; and we shall be saved" — frames a plea for a suffering Israel, pictured as a vine brought out of Egypt and planted until it filled the land, now with its hedge broken down and wasted by wild beasts. The psalm asks God to look down, visit the vine, and strengthen the man of his right hand, so that the people will not turn back from him. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Shepherd, flock-leader, and dweller between cherubim are titles for prayer: Henry reads the address as looking to a throne of grace, where petitions are received and directions given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Three tribes named are the squadron that marched next after the tabernacle: strength stirred up before them means deliverance shown in sight of all Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Turn us again, O God, and cause thy face to shine, and we shall be saved — the psalm's refrain of restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Anger against the prayer of his own people is the strangest grief in the psalm: Henry suggests either prayers asked amiss, with secret sin or doubting, or a seeming anger meant to try patience, as with the Canaanite woman. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Bread of tears eaten day by day and tears drunk in great measure make sorrow a diet, not an episode: Henry notes many spend time in sorrow who shall spend eternity in joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Made a strife to neighbours, with enemies laughing among themselves, turns divine frowning into public shame: a people become cheap prey, and strife is over who shall strip them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Again the refrain: turn us again, O God of hosts, cause thy face to shine, and we shall be saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Vine brought from Egypt, heathen cast out, planting in their place: Henry's church is weak and unsightly yet spreading and fruitful, its root Christ and its branches believers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Room swept before it and deep root taken describe establishment in church and state so firm that neighbours' attempts could not pluck it up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Hills covered with shadow and boughs like goodly cedars show a land fully peopled and strong men raised, yet Henry notes the praise is for shadow and branches, not fruit: Israel was an empty vine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Boughs to the sea and branches to the river map dominion west, south, north, and east: conquest extended, while fruit, the one thing a vine exists for, is missing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Hedges broken down raise Henry's question of desertion: a plant of God's own planting is exposed, yet former favours are urged as pleas that he will not wholly cast off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Boar and wild beast ravaging only after the hedge falls teach restraint: until God withdrew protection, enemies could not pluck a leaf, so defence departed is the real calamity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Return, look down from heaven, behold and visit: heaven is at once place of prospect, of power, and of judgment, and the prayer refers its own case to God's compassionate consideration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Vineyard planted by the right hand and branch made strong for God's own praise: Henry hears branch as son, the stock preserved until the Branch himself should come, the true vine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. It is burned with fire, it is cut down, they perish at the rebuke of thy countenance, is the deplorable state of Israel described: it is burnt with fire, it is cut down — the people are treated like thorns and briers, that are nigh unto cursing and whose end is to be burned, and no longer like vines that are protected and cherished. They perish not through the rage of the wild beast and the boar, but at the rebuke of thy countenance; that was it which they dreaded and to which they attributed all their calamities. It is well or ill with us according as we are under God's smiles or frowns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Hand upon the man of the right hand asks not only protection but ownership, strengthening, and success for the Davidic king as under-shepherd under the great Shepherd, an instrument in God's right hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Not going back, quickening, and calling on the name form the closing bargain of the section: a people prepared for mercy ask to be kept from apostasy, so that prayer itself becomes the evidence of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. The full refrain closes the psalm: turn us again, O Lord God of hosts, cause thy face to shine, and we shall be saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
