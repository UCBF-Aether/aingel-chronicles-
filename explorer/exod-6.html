The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then the LORD said unto Moses, Now shalt thou see what I will do to Pharaoh: for with a strong hand shall he let them go, and with a strong hand shall he drive them out of his land.

2 And God spake unto Moses, and said unto him, I am the LORD:

3 And I appeared unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, by the name of God Almighty, but by my name JEHOVAH was I not known to them.

4 And I have also established my covenant with them, to give them the land of Canaan, the land of their pilgrimage, wherein they were strangers.

5 And I have also heard the groaning of the children of Israel, whom the Egyptians keep in bondage; and I have remembered my covenant.

6 Wherefore say unto the children of Israel, I am the LORD, and I will bring you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians, and I will rid you out of their bondage, and I will redeem you with a stretched out arm, and with great judgments:

7 And I will take you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God: and ye shall know that I am the LORD your God, which bringeth you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians.

8 And I will bring you in unto the land, concerning the which I did swear to give it to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob; and I will give it you for an heritage: I am the LORD.

9 And Moses spake so unto the children of Israel: but they hearkened not unto Moses for anguish of spirit, and for cruel bondage.

10 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

11 Go in, speak unto Pharaoh king of Egypt, that he let the children of Israel go out of his land.

12 And Moses spake before the LORD, saying, Behold, the children of Israel have not hearkened unto me; how then shall Pharaoh hear me, who am of uncircumcised lips?

13 And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron, and gave them a charge unto the children of Israel, and unto Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt.

14 These be the heads of their fathers’ houses: The sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel; Hanoch, and Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi: these be the families of Reuben.

15 And the sons of Simeon; Jemuel, and Jamin, and Ohad, and Jachin, and Zohar, and Shaul the son of a Canaanitish woman: these are the families of Simeon.

16 And these are the names of the sons of Levi according to their generations; Gershon, and Kohath, and Merari: and the years of the life of Levi were an hundred thirty and seven years.

17 The sons of Gershon; Libni, and Shimi, according to their families.

18 And the sons of Kohath; Amram, and Izhar, and Hebron, and Uzziel: and the years of the life of Kohath were an hundred thirty and three years.

19 And the sons of Merari; Mahali and Mushi: these are the families of Levi according to their generations.

20 And Amram took him Jochebed his father’s sister to wife; and she bare him Aaron and Moses: and the years of the life of Amram were an hundred and thirty and seven years.

21 And the sons of Izhar; Korah, and Nepheg, and Zichri.

22 And the sons of Uzziel; Mishael, and Elzaphan, and Zithri.

23 And Aaron took him Elisheba, daughter of Amminadab, sister of Naashon, to wife; and she bare him Nadab, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.

24 And the sons of Korah; Assir, and Elkanah, and Abiasaph: these are the families of the Korhites.

25 And Eleazar Aaron’s son took him one of the daughters of Putiel to wife; and she bare him Phinehas: these are the heads of the fathers of the Levites according to their families.

26 These are that Aaron and Moses, to whom the LORD said, Bring out the children of Israel from the land of Egypt according to their armies.

27 These are they which spake to Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring out the children of Israel from Egypt: these are that Moses and Aaron.

28 And it came to pass on the day when the LORD spake unto Moses in the land of Egypt,

29 That the LORD spake unto Moses, saying, I am the LORD: speak thou unto Pharaoh king of Egypt all that I say unto thee.

30 And Moses said before the LORD, Behold, I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh hearken unto me?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God renews his promise to Moses: he is the LORD who made covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and he will bring Israel out of Egypt with a stretched-out arm. Moses speaks this to Israel, but they do not listen for anguish of spirit and cruel bondage. The chapter then lists the families of Reuben, Simeon, and Levi, and charges Moses and Aaron again to speak to Pharaoh. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD answered Moses's complaint: Now shalt thou see what I will do to Pharaoh, for with a strong hand he would both let Israel go and drive them out. Henry notes the answer to increased affliction was not explanation but a nearer promise of deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. God spake to Moses, saying, I am the LORD. Henry notes the name stands at the head of every promise in this chapter, grounding each one in God's unchanging being. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. God had appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as God Almighty, but by his name JEHOVAH he was not known to them. Henry explains the patriarchs knew God by promise, while Israel would know him by performance, in the great works of deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. God had established his covenant to give the patriarchs Canaan, the land of their pilgrimage. Henry notes the covenant was made long before and had waited through centuries, yet stood unbroken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. God had also heard Israel's groaning under Egyptian bondage and remembered his covenant. Henry observes the two motives joined: God's compassion for present misery and his faithfulness to ancient promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Wherefore Moses was to tell Israel: I am the LORD, I will bring you out from under Egypt's burdens, deliver you from bondage, and redeem you with a stretched-out arm and great judgments. Henry notes redemption here is by power and judgment together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. I will take you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God, and ye shall know I am the LORD who brought you out. Henry calls this the heart of the covenant: deliverance ends in belonging, and knowledge of God comes by experience of his acts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. God would bring Israel into the land he swore to give Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and give it them for a heritage, for he is the LORD. Henry notes the promise reaches past Egypt to Canaan: deliverance is not an end in itself but the road to inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Moses spake so to Israel, but they hearkened not for anguish of spirit and cruel bondage. Henry observes affliction had dulled their ears: they could not take comfort from a promise while the brick-kilns still smoked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The LORD spake again to Moses, renewing the command after the people's refusal. Henry notes God did not withdraw the commission because the first hearing failed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Moses was told to go in and speak to Pharaoh, that he let Israel go out of his land. Henry observes the command was repeated after disappointment, for God's purposes do not change with man's reception. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Moses answered that Israel had not hearkened to him, so how should Pharaoh hearken, seeing he was of uncircumcised lips? Henry notes Moses argued from the lesser to the greater, and made his own weakness the reason to shrink. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The LORD gave Moses and Aaron a charge unto Israel and unto Pharaoh, to bring Israel out of Egypt. Henry notes the charge was laid on both brothers together, so that neither could plead the other's duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Here begins the record of the heads of the fathers' houses, starting with Reuben, the firstborn of Israel. Henry notes the genealogy is set here to show the stock from which the deliverers came, beginning in birth order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. These are the names of Levi's sons according to their generations: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari, and Levi lived a hundred and thirty-seven years. Henry notes the long lives spanning the bondage, a quiet witness that God preserved the tribe through the oppression. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The sons of Kohath were Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel, and Kohath lived a hundred and thirty-three years. Henry notes the generations are counted to bring the line steadily down to Moses and Aaron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Amram took Jochebed his father's sister to wife, and she bare him Aaron and Moses, and Amram lived a hundred and thirty-seven years. Henry notes this is the hinge of the genealogy: from this marriage came the prophet and the priest of the Exodus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Aaron took Elisheba, daughter of Amminadab and sister of Naashon, to wife, and she bare him Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar. Henry notes the priestly house is thus recorded, the family from which Israel's priesthood would come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Eleazar, Aaron's son, took a daughter of Putiel to wife, and she bare him Phinehas. Henry notes the priestly line continues here, carrying the house of Aaron into the next generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. These are that Aaron and Moses to whom the LORD said, Bring out Israel from Egypt according to their armies. Henry notes the genealogy ends where it began, with the two men charged to lead a people ordered like an army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. These are they which spake to Pharaoh to bring out Israel from Egypt, even that Moses and Aaron. Henry observes the repetition seals their identity: the men of the pedigree are the men of the commission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. On the day the LORD spake to Moses in Egypt, the narrative resumes after the genealogy. Henry notes the break was purposeful: the reader now knows who speaks to Pharaoh and by what right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. The LORD spake to Moses, saying, I am the LORD: speak thou unto Pharaoh all that I say unto thee. Henry notes the command is total: Moses must deliver all, not a softened part, of God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Moses said, Behold, I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh hearken unto me? Henry notes the old excuse returned at the very threshold, and God's answer would again be his own presence, not Moses's eloquence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
