The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 44 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word that came to Jeremiah concerning all the Jews which dwell in the land of Egypt, which dwell at Migdol, and at Tahpanhes, and at Noph, and in the country of Pathros, saying,

2 Thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Ye have seen all the evil that I have brought upon Jerusalem, and upon all the cities of Judah; and, behold, this day they are a desolation, and no man dwelleth therein,

3 Because of their wickedness which they have committed to provoke me to anger, in that they went to burn incense, and to serve other gods, whom they knew not, neither they, ye, nor your fathers.

4 Howbeit I sent unto you all my servants the prophets, rising early and sending them, saying, Oh, do not this abominable thing that I hate.

5 But they hearkened not, nor inclined their ear to turn from their wickedness, to burn no incense unto other gods.

6 Wherefore my fury and mine anger was poured forth, and was kindled in the cities of Judah and in the streets of Jerusalem; and they are wasted and desolate, as at this day.

7 Therefore now thus saith the LORD, the God of hosts, the God of Israel; Wherefore commit ye this great evil against your souls, to cut off from you man and woman, child and suckling, out of Judah, to leave you none to remain;

8 In that ye provoke me unto wrath with the works of your hands, burning incense unto other gods in the land of Egypt, whither ye be gone to dwell, that ye might cut yourselves off, and that ye might be a curse and a reproach among all the nations of the earth?

9 Have ye forgotten the wickedness of your fathers, and the wickedness of the kings of Judah, and the wickedness of their wives, and your own wickedness, and the wickedness of your wives, which they have committed in the land of Judah, and in the streets of Jerusalem?

10 They are not humbled even unto this day, neither have they feared, nor walked in my law, nor in my statutes, that I set before you and before your fathers.

11 Therefore thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Behold, I will set my face against you for evil, and to cut off all Judah.

12 And I will take the remnant of Judah, that have set their faces to go into the land of Egypt to sojourn there, and they shall all be consumed, and fall in the land of Egypt; they shall even be consumed by the sword and by the famine: they shall die, from the least even unto the greatest, by the sword and by the famine: and they shall be an execration, and an astonishment, and a curse, and a reproach.

13 For I will punish them that dwell in the land of Egypt, as I have punished Jerusalem, by the sword, by the famine, and by the pestilence:

14 So that none of the remnant of Judah, which are gone into the land of Egypt to sojourn there, shall escape or remain, that they should return into the land of Judah, to the which they have a desire to return to dwell there: for none shall return but such as shall escape.

15 Then all the men which knew that their wives had burned incense unto other gods, and all the women that stood by, a great multitude, even all the people that dwelt in the land of Egypt, in Pathros, answered Jeremiah, saying,

16 As for the word that thou hast spoken unto us in the name of the LORD, we will not hearken unto thee.

17 But we will certainly do whatsoever thing goeth forth out of our own mouth, to burn incense unto the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto her, as we have done, we, and our fathers, our kings, and our princes, in the cities of Judah, and in the streets of Jerusalem: for then had we plenty of victuals, and were well, and saw no evil.

18 But since we left off to burn incense to the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto her, we have wanted all things, and have been consumed by the sword and by the famine.

19 And when we burned incense to the queen of heaven, and poured out drink offerings unto her, did we make her cakes to worship her, and pour out drink offerings unto her, without our men?

20 Then Jeremiah said unto all the people, to the men, and to the women, and to all the people which had given him that answer, saying,

21 The incense that ye burned in the cities of Judah, and in the streets of Jerusalem, ye, and your fathers, your kings, and your princes, and the people of the land, did not the LORD remember them, and came it not into his mind?

22 So that the LORD could no longer bear, because of the evil of your doings, and because of the abominations which ye have committed; therefore is your land a desolation, and an astonishment, and a curse, without an inhabitant, as at this day.

23 Because ye have burned incense, and because ye have sinned against the LORD, and have not obeyed the voice of the LORD, nor walked in his law, nor in his statutes, nor in his testimonies; therefore this evil is happened unto you, as at this day.

24 Moreover Jeremiah said unto all the people, and to all the women, Hear the word of the LORD, all Judah that are in the land of Egypt:

25 Thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, saying; Ye and your wives have both spoken with your mouths, and fulfilled with your hand, saying, We will surely perform our vows that we have vowed, to burn incense to the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto her: ye will surely accomplish your vows, and surely perform your vows.

26 Therefore hear ye the word of the LORD, all Judah that dwell in the land of Egypt; Behold, I have sworn by my great name, saith the LORD, that my name shall no more be named in the mouth of any man of Judah in all the land of Egypt, saying, The Lord GOD liveth.

27 Behold, I will watch over them for evil, and not for good: and all the men of Judah that are in the land of Egypt shall be consumed by the sword and by the famine, until there be an end of them.

28 Yet a small number that escape the sword shall return out of the land of Egypt into the land of Judah, and all the remnant of Judah, that are gone into the land of Egypt to sojourn there, shall know whose words shall stand, mine, or theirs.

29 And this shall be a sign unto you, saith the LORD, that I will punish you in this place, that ye may know that my words shall surely stand against you for evil:

30 Thus saith the LORD; Behold, I will give Pharaohhophra king of Egypt into the hand of his enemies, and into the hand of them that seek his life; as I gave Zedekiah king of Judah into the hand of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon, his enemy, and that sought his life.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

To all the Jews dwelling in Egypt — at Migdol, Tahpanhes, Noph and Pathros — Jeremiah declares that their idolatry, and in particular their worship of the queen of heaven, will bring sword and famine on the remnant. The people answer that they will keep their vows to the queen of heaven, and the chapter ends with the sign that Pharaohhophra will be given into the hand of his enemies. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry notes the Jews in Egypt were dispersed into Migdol, Noph, and other places, and Jeremiah was sent on an errand from God to them, delivering this message either when most of them were together in Pathros or by going about from place to place preaching to this purport. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry observes that the fugitives in Egypt seem to have forgotten the desolations of Judah and Jerusalem, though the captives by the rivers of Babylon were daily mindful of them, and so needed to be put in mind of what they had seen with their own eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry traces the desolation to idolatry in particular: the people gave to counterfeit deities, creatures of their own fancy and work of their own hands, the honour due to the true God only, forsaking a God known among them for gods they knew not, upstart deities whose original was obscure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry stresses the frequent and fair warnings given by the prophets, sent with great care saying, Oh, do not this abominable thing that I hate, and draws the lesson that sin should be spoken of with dread and detestation because it is what God hates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry notes the aggravation: they hearkened not nor inclined their ear, but still persisted in their idolatries, and the outcome showed what came of it, since God's anger was poured out upon them as at this day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry insists that whoever were the instruments of the destruction, they were but instruments: it was God's fury and anger that kindled the fire which made Jerusalem and the cities of Judah waste and desolate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry reads this as self-injury: in sinning they commit a great evil against their own souls, wronging and deceiving them with what is false, and cutting off from themselves man and woman, child and suckling, so that Judah would be left without heirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry explains that God forbade the going into Egypt because he knew it would be a snare, and contrasts the two exiles: those God sent to Chaldea were by grace weaned from idolatry, while those who went against God's mind into Egypt became more wedded than ever to their idolatries, since thrusting oneself without cause or call into temptation leaves one justly to oneself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry notes they filled up the measure of their fathers' iniquity, adding to it rather than being humbled by it, and were reminded of the sins and punishments of the kings of Judah, who great as they were did not escape judgment for idolatry, and of their wives who had seduced them to it, with Lightfoot's suggestion that Solomon's Egyptian wives are tacitly reflected on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry laments that they are not humbled even unto this day by all the humbling providences they have been under, and draws the rule that those who walk not in the law of God thereby show they are destitute of the fear of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry notes the reversal of resolve: they set their faces to go into Egypt against God, and now God is resolute against them, setting his face to cut off all Judah, since those who think not only to affront but to confront God Almighty will find themselves outfaced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry stresses the sentence admits no exception of rank or order: they shall all be consumed, from the least to the greatest, high and low, rich and poor, by the very judgments they thought to escape by flying into Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry notes they shall be consumed by the same judgments used for Jerusalem, sword, famine, and pestilence, not wasted by natural deaths as Israel in the wilderness, but by these sore judgments, which they thought Egypt would put out of their reach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry observes the irony of their desires: in Judah they desired Egypt, and in Egypt they desired to return to Judah, lifting up their soul to it, yet because they would not dwell there when God commanded it, they shall not dwell there when they desire it, and only a very few shall escape to return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry notes the defiance was general, not one man's obstinacy: the men who knew their wives had burned incense, and the women who stood by and had joined in the idolatrous usages, were touched in a sore place by reproof and kicked against the pricks, and consciousness of guilt made them impatient of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry calls this scarcely matched for downright daring contradiction to God himself: they own Jeremiah spoke in the name of the LORD, yet tell him flatly, we will not hearken unto thee, which Henry reads as the genuine language of the rebellious heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry identifies the queen of heaven with the moon, though some understand the sun, much worshipped in Egypt and at Jerusalem, or all the host of heaven, and notes the rebels plead antiquity, authority, unity, universality, visibility, the Jerusalem mother-church, and prosperity, yet the law of God, not the practice of men, must rule and judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry exposes their perverse reading of providence: they claimed judgments came because they had left off burning incense to the queen of heaven, whereas the true reason was that they still retained idols in their hearts, so afflictions meant to separate them from sin instead confirmed them in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry notes the women's plea that they did not act without their husbands, since the men kindled the fire while the women kneaded the dough for cakes and poured the drink offerings, but calls it frivolous: it is sad when nearest relations harden each other in sin instead of quickening each other to good, and husbands' approval avails nothing against God's mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry remarks that daring sinners may speak many bold words, but God will have the last word, and Jeremiah immediately replied to correct their wilful mistake about the cause of their calamities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry explains that the incense burned in Judah and Jerusalem went long unpunished because God was long-suffering, and during the day of his patience it was perhaps well with them, but at length their old guilt, unrepented, came into God's mind and was brought to account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry notes the Lord could no longer bear the evil of their doings and their abominations, so their land became a desolation, an astonishment, and a curse without inhabitant, not for any late reformation but for old transgressions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry draws the general lesson that whatever evil comes upon us, it is because we have sinned against the LORD, having not obeyed his voice nor walked in his law, statutes, and testimonies, and we should therefore stand in awe and sin not, since rightly understanding the cause of troubles should go far toward curing our sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry notes the privilege and duty in this address: Judah dwelling in Egypt still has God speaking to them even there, and they are to observe what he says, with Jeremiah speaking to the women particularly from the LORD of hosts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry warns that no vow can make lawful what God has made sin: the people claim they will surely perform their vows to the queen of heaven, as if swearing to do it justified doing it, and God answers by repeating their own words back to them before declaring his own vow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry explains the oath by God's great name: what little remained of religion among them should be lost, so that his name would no more be named by any man of Judah in Egypt in their solemn oath, the LORD liveth, and they would forget their religion and learn to swear by the life of Pharaoh, or else God would resent any mention of his name from them as an affront. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry reads the watching over them for evil as God letting slip no opportunity to bring judgment upon them until there be an end of them, and notes that to those God finds impenitent sinners he will be found an implacable Judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry frames the contest as whose word shall stand, God's or theirs: they said they would recover themselves by returning to the queen of heaven, God said they would ruin themselves, and the event would show which was right, while only a small number escaping the sword would return, next to none beside the great numbers returning from Chaldea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry notes the sign is given so they may know God's words shall surely stand against them for evil: the threatened consumption in Egypt will be accomplished in its season, and the sign itself will confirm the whole sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 30. Henry reports the two identifications of Pharaoh-hophra's enemies, his own rebellious subjects under Amasis according to Herodotus, or Nebuchadnezzar invading his kingdom according to Josephus, and notes the Jews depended on this potent and politic prince for protection, yet creature-comforts and confidences we promise ourselves most from may fail as soon as those we promise least from, and the sacred history's silence afterward implies these Jews in Egypt were lost there, for no word of God shall fall to the ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

Where the notes differ

On the identity of the queen of heaven (verse 17), Matthew Henry identifies her with the moon, while, as his own note records, some understand the sun — much worshipped in Egypt and at Jerusalem — or all the host of heaven. The note does not name those other readers, and this page does not adjudicate between the readings. Interpreted Study note for Jeremiah 44:17, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
