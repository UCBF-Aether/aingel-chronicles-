The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 There were present at that season some that told him of the Galilaeans, whose blood Pilate had mingled with their sacrifices.

2 And Jesus answering said unto them, Suppose ye that these Galilaeans were sinners above all the Galilaeans, because they suffered such things?

3 I tell you, Nay: but, except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish.

4 Or those eighteen, upon whom the tower in Siloam fell, and slew them, think ye that they were sinners above all men that dwelt in Jerusalem?

5 I tell you, Nay: but, except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish.

6 He spake also this parable; A certain man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard; and he came and sought fruit thereon, and found none.

7 Then said he unto the dresser of his vineyard, Behold, these three years I come seeking fruit on this fig tree, and find none: cut it down; why cumbereth it the ground?

8 And he answering said unto him, Lord, let it alone this year also, till I shall dig about it, and dung it:

9 And if it bear fruit, well: and if not, then after that thou shalt cut it down.

10 And he was teaching in one of the synagogues on the sabbath.

11 And, behold, there was a woman which had a spirit of infirmity eighteen years, and was bowed together, and could in no wise lift up herself.

12 And when Jesus saw her, he called her to him, and said unto her, Woman, thou art loosed from thine infirmity.

13 And he laid his hands on her: and immediately she was made straight, and glorified God.

14 And the ruler of the synagogue answered with indignation, because that Jesus had healed on the sabbath day, and said unto the people, There are six days in which men ought to work: in them therefore come and be healed, and not on the sabbath day.

15 The Lord then answered him, and said, Thou hypocrite, doth not each one of you on the sabbath loose his ox or his ass from the stall, and lead him away to watering?

16 And ought not this woman, being a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan hath bound, lo, these eighteen years, be loosed from this bond on the sabbath day?

17 And when he had said these things, all his adversaries were ashamed: and all the people rejoiced for all the glorious things that were done by him.

18 Then said he, Unto what is the kingdom of God like? and whereunto shall I resemble it?

19 It is like a grain of mustard seed, which a man took, and cast into his garden; and it grew, and waxed a great tree; and the fowls of the air lodged in the branches of it.

20 And again he said, Whereunto shall I liken the kingdom of God?

21 It is like leaven, which a woman took and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened.

22 And he went through the cities and villages, teaching, and journeying toward Jerusalem.

23 Then said one unto him, Lord, are there few that be saved? And he said unto them,

24 Strive to enter in at the strait gate: for many, I say unto you, will seek to enter in, and shall not be able.

25 When once the master of the house is risen up, and hath shut to the door, and ye begin to stand without, and to knock at the door, saying, Lord, Lord, open unto us; and he shall answer and say unto you, I know you not whence ye are:

26 Then shall ye begin to say, We have eaten and drunk in thy presence, and thou hast taught in our streets.

27 But he shall say, I tell you, I know you not whence ye are; depart from me, all ye workers of iniquity.

28 There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, when ye shall see Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and all the prophets, in the kingdom of God, and you yourselves thrust out.

29 And they shall come from the east, and from the west, and from the north, and from the south, and shall sit down in the kingdom of God.

30 And, behold, there are last which shall be first, and there are first which shall be last.

31 The same day there came certain of the Pharisees, saying unto him, Get thee out, and depart hence: for Herod will kill thee.

32 And he said unto them, Go ye, and tell that fox, Behold, I cast out devils, and I do cures to day and to morrow, and the third day I shall be perfected.

33 Nevertheless I must walk to day, and to morrow, and the day following: for it cannot be that a prophet perish out of Jerusalem.

34 O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, which killest the prophets, and stonest them that are sent unto thee; how often would I have gathered thy children together, as a hen doth gather her brood under her wings, and ye would not!

35 Behold, your house is left unto you desolate: and verily I say unto you, Ye shall not see me, until the time come when ye shall say, Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Pilate’s slaughter of Galileans and the tower of Siloam become warnings that all must repent, illustrated by a fig tree given one more year to bear fruit (vv. 1–9). On a Sabbath Jesus straightens a woman bent double for eighteen years, to the synagogue ruler’s indignation (vv. 10–17). The mustard seed and the leaven picture a kingdom growing from the smallest beginnings (vv. 18–21); the narrow door, the shut door, and the weeping outside follow, and the chapter closes with Herod called a fox and Jerusalem lamented as the city that kills the prophets (vv. 22–35). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 13

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Some told him of Galileans whose blood Pilate had mingled with their sacrifices. Henry notes this tragical story is briefly related here and met with in no historians, yet it becomes the text for repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Suppose ye that these Galileans were sinners above all Galileans, because they suffered such things? The question challenges the link between spectacular suffering and spectacular sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. I tell you, Nay: but except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish. Henry cautions not to blame great sufferers as therefore great sinners, and founds on sudden removals a call to repentance: the same Jesus who bids us repent because the kingdom is at hand bids us repent, or we shall perish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Or those eighteen, upon whom the tower in Siloam fell and slew them, think ye they were sinners above all men in Jerusalem? A second sudden death, by a tower built for safety, makes the same point. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. I tell you, Nay: but except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish. Henry notes no place or employment secures us from death's stroke, so others' sudden removals are warnings to ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. A man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard, came seeking fruit, and found none. Planting in a vineyard implies care and expectation, not wild growth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Behold, these three years I come seeking fruit on this fig tree and find none: cut it down; why cumbereth it the ground? Three years of seeking frame the sentence: the tree occupies ground without fruit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Lord, let it alone this year also, till I shall dig about it, and dung it. The dresser pleads for one more year of cultivation, not for acquittal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And if it bear fruit, well; and if not, then after that thou shalt cut it down. Henry notes the parable enforces the warning just given: the barren tree, except it bear fruit, will be cut down; it refers first to Israel, yet awakens all who enjoy means of grace, for God may bear a little longer, but not always. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. He was teaching in one of the synagogues on the sabbath. Henry notes the Lord attended public worship on sabbaths, and even bodily infirmities, unless very grievous, should not keep us from it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. There was a woman with a spirit of infirmity eighteen years, bowed together, unable to lift herself. Eighteen years bent is the measure of her bondage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. When Jesus saw her, he called her and said, Woman, thou art loosed from thine infirmity. She came to be taught and get good to her soul, Henry notes, and then he relieved her bodily infirmity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. He laid hands on her, and immediately she was made straight, and glorified God. Henry notes this cure represents grace upon the soul, and when crooked souls are made straight, they show it by glorifying God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The ruler answered with indignation, because Jesus healed on the sabbath, and said, there are six days to work; in them come and be healed, not on the sabbath day. Indignation cloaks enmity with sabbath zeal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Thou hypocrite, doth not each one of you on the sabbath loose his ox or ass from the stall and lead him away to watering? The ruler's own sabbath practice condemns his objection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And ought not this woman, a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan has bound eighteen years, be loosed from this bond on the sabbath day? Henry notes Christ knew the ruler's real enmity, cloaked with pretended sabbath zeal, for he would not have them healed any day; yet if Jesus speaks and puts forth healing power, sinners are set free, and such deliverance often wrought on the Lord's day agrees with that day's design. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. When he had said these things, all his adversaries were ashamed, and all the people rejoiced for all the glorious things done by him. Shame and rejoicing divide the audience by their response to mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Then said he, Unto what is the kingdom of God like, and whereunto shall I resemble it? The question introduces two parables of progress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. It is like a grain of mustard seed, which a man took and cast into his garden; it grew into a great tree, and fowls lodged in its branches. Henry notes the progress of the gospel foretold here: may grace grow in our hearts, faith and love exceedingly, giving undoubted evidence of reality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And again he said, Whereunto shall I liken the kingdom of God? The second likeness repeats the question for a different image. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. It is like leaven, which a woman took and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened. Henry prays the saints' example be blessed to neighbours, and grace flow heart to heart, until the little one becomes a thousand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. He went through cities and villages, teaching, and journeying toward Jerusalem. Teaching on the way to Jerusalem frames the strait-gate exhortation that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Then said one, Lord, are there few that be saved? And he said unto them. Henry notes the Saviour came to guide consciences, not gratify curiosity: ask not how many shall be saved, but shall I be one? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Strive to enter in at the strait gate, for many, I say, will seek to enter in, and shall not be able. Henry stresses the direction is to each of us, strive ye; all who will be saved must enter the strait gate and undergo a change of the whole man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. When once the master of the house is risen and shut the door, and ye stand without knocking, Lord, Lord, open unto us, he shall answer, I know you not whence ye are. A shut door answers late knocking with non-recognition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Then shall ye begin to say, We have eaten and drunk in thy presence, and thou hast taught in our streets. Proximity to his presence and streets is pleaded as acquaintance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. But he shall say, I tell you, I know you not whence ye are; depart from me, all ye workers of iniquity. Eating in his presence without doing his will is counted iniquity, not intimacy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, when ye shall see Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and all prophets in the kingdom, and you yourselves thrust out. Seeing the patriarchs inside while oneself is thrust out is the torment pictured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And they shall come from east, west, north, and south, and sit down in the kingdom of God. The kingdom's table gathers from every compass point. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Behold, there are last which shall be first, and first which shall be last. Henry warns none to despond for self or others, for if we reach heaven we shall meet many we little thought to meet, and miss many we expected to find. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. The same day certain Pharisees said, Get thee out and depart hence, for Herod will kill thee. The warning may be friendly or a trap; Jesus answers it as a message to Herod. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Go ye, and tell that fox, Behold, I cast out devils, and I do cures today and tomorrow, and the third day I shall be perfected. Henry notes Christ, calling Herod a fox, gave him his true character, though it is not an example for us; and I shall be perfected means, when I die, I shall have completed my undertaking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Nevertheless I must walk today, tomorrow, and the day following, for it cannot be that a prophet perish out of Jerusalem. His timetable is set, and Jerusalem is the appointed place of a prophet's death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, which killest the prophets and stonest them sent unto thee; how often would I have gathered thy children together, as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, and ye would not! Henry notes the wickedness of places professing religion especially grieves the Lord, and urges welcoming all who come in the Lord's name to call us to salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Behold, your house is left unto you desolate, and verily ye shall not see me, until ye shall say, Blessed is he that comes in the name of the Lord. Desolation follows refusal, until blessing replaces rejection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
