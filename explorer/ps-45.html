The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 45 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 My heart is inditing a good matter: I speak of the things which I have made touching the king: my tongue is the pen of a ready writer.

2 Thou art fairer than the children of men: grace is poured into thy lips: therefore God hath blessed thee for ever.

3 Gird thy sword upon thy thigh, O most mighty, with thy glory and thy majesty.

4 And in thy majesty ride prosperously because of truth and meekness and righteousness; and thy right hand shall teach thee terrible things.

5 Thine arrows are sharp in the heart of the king’s enemies; whereby the people fall under thee.

6 Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever: the sceptre of thy kingdom is a right sceptre.

7 Thou lovest righteousness, and hatest wickedness: therefore God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows.

8 All thy garments smell of myrrh, and aloes, and cassia, out of the ivory palaces, whereby they have made thee glad.

9 Kings’ daughters were among thy honourable women: upon thy right hand did stand the queen in gold of Ophir.

10 Hearken, O daughter, and consider, and incline thine ear; forget also thine own people, and thy father’s house;

11 So shall the king greatly desire thy beauty: for he is thy Lord; and worship thou him.

12 And the daughter of Tyre shall be there with a gift; even the rich among the people shall intreat thy favour.

13 The king’s daughter is all glorious within: her clothing is of wrought gold.

14 She shall be brought unto the king in raiment of needlework: the virgins her companions that follow her shall be brought unto thee.

15 With gladness and rejoicing shall they be brought: they shall enter into the king’s palace.

16 Instead of thy fathers shall be thy children, whom thou mayest make princes in all the earth.

17 I will make thy name to be remembered in all generations: therefore shall the people praise thee for ever and ever.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A royal wedding song: the heart overflows for the King, fairer than men, grace on his lips, sword girded, riding for truth, meekness, and righteousness, throne for ever, sceptre right, anointed with the oil of gladness. Then the bride, bidden to forget her people, is brought in needlework with gladness to the palace, children becoming princes, and his name remembered in all generations. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. My heart is inditing a good matter; I speak of things made touching the king; my tongue is the pen of a ready writer: the heart overflows with a good theme — the King — and the tongue writes swiftly, as a ready scribe's pen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. You are fairer than the children of men; grace is poured into your lips; therefore God has blessed you for ever: beauty and gracious speech mark the King, and God's everlasting blessing rests on him — the portrait Henry and JFB read as Christ, fairer than all men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Gird your sword upon your thigh, O most mighty, with your glory and majesty: the mighty King arms himself, and glory and majesty are his equipment, not mere ornament. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. And in your majesty ride prosperously because of truth and meekness and righteousness; and your right hand shall teach you terrible things: his prosperous ride is for truth, meekness, and righteousness, and his right hand will do terrible deeds against their enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Your arrows are sharp in the heart of the king's enemies, whereby the people fall under you: sharp arrows in enemies' hearts bring peoples into subjection — conviction and conquest together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Your throne, O God, is for ever and ever; the sceptre of your kingdom is a right sceptre: the King is addressed as God, his throne everlasting, his sceptre straight — Hebrews applies this to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. You love righteousness and hate wickedness; therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness above your fellows: love of righteousness and hatred of wickedness are the ground of an anointing with gladness above all fellows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. All your garments smell of myrrh, aloes, and cassia, out of the ivory palaces, whereby they have made you glad: perfumed garments from ivory palaces picture the King's gladness and royal fragrance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Kings' daughters were among your honourable women; upon your right hand stood the queen in gold of Ophir: honourable women and a queen in Ophir gold attend the royal wedding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Hearken, O daughter, and consider, and incline your ear; forget also your own people and your father's house: the bride is bidden to listen, consider, and leave her former people and house — a new allegiance for a new marriage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. So shall the king greatly desire your beauty, for he is your Lord; and worship him: the King's desire follows her forsaking, and her worship follows his lordship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And the daughter of Tyre shall be there with a gift; even the rich among the people shall entreat your favour: Tyre's daughter with gifts, and the rich entreating favour, show Gentiles drawn to the royal marriage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The king's daughter is all glorious within; her clothing is of wrought gold: inward glory and gold-wrought clothing together — the bride is glorious within before she is glorious without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. She shall be brought to the king in raiment of needlework; the virgins her companions that follow her shall be brought to you: needlework raiment and virgin companions in procession mark the bridal entry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. With gladness and rejoicing shall they be brought; they shall enter into the king's palace: gladness and rejoicing, not compulsion, bring them into the palace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Instead of your fathers shall be your children, whom you may make princes in all the earth: children replace fathers, and become princes in all the earth — the dynasty spreads worldwide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. I will make your name to be remembered in all generations; therefore the people shall praise you for ever and ever: the King's name remembered through generations draws praise for ever — the psalm ends in the eternity it began to describe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
