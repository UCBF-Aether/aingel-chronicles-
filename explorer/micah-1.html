The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Micah 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word of the LORD that came to Micah the Morasthite in the days of Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah, which he saw concerning Samaria and Jerusalem.

2 Hear, all ye people; hearken, O earth, and all that therein is: and let the Lord GOD be witness against you, the LORD from his holy temple.

3 For, behold, the LORD cometh forth out of his place, and will come down, and tread upon the high places of the earth.

4 And the mountains shall be molten under him, and the valleys shall be cleft, as wax before the fire, and as the waters that are poured down a steep place.

5 For the transgression of Jacob is all this, and for the sins of the house of Israel. What is the transgression of Jacob? is it not Samaria? and what are the high places of Judah? are they not Jerusalem?

6 Therefore I will make Samaria as an heap of the field, and as plantings of a vineyard: and I will pour down the stones thereof into the valley, and I will discover the foundations thereof.

7 And all the graven images thereof shall be beaten to pieces, and all the hires thereof shall be burned with the fire, and all the idols thereof will I lay desolate: for she gathered it of the hire of an harlot, and they shall return to the hire of an harlot.

8 Therefore I will wail and howl, I will go stripped and naked: I will make a wailing like the dragons, and mourning as the owls.

9 For her wound is incurable; for it is come unto Judah; he is come unto the gate of my people, even to Jerusalem.

10 Declare ye it not at Gath, weep ye not at all: in the house of Aphrah roll thyself in the dust.

11 Pass ye away, thou inhabitant of Saphir, having thy shame naked: the inhabitant of Zaanan came not forth in the mourning of Bethezel; he shall receive of you his standing.

12 For the inhabitant of Maroth waited carefully for good: but evil came down from the LORD unto the gate of Jerusalem.

13 O thou inhabitant of Lachish, bind the chariot to the swift beast: she is the beginning of the sin to the daughter of Zion: for the transgressions of Israel were found in thee.

14 Therefore shalt thou give presents to Moreshethgath: the houses of Achzib shall be a lie to the kings of Israel.

15 Yet will I bring an heir unto thee, O inhabitant of Mareshah: he shall come unto Adullam the glory of Israel.

16 Make thee bald, and poll thee for thy delicate children; enlarge thy baldness as the eagle; for they are gone into captivity from thee.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The word of the LORD comes to Micah the Morasthite concerning Samaria and Jerusalem, and all peoples are summoned to hear while the LORD comes forth from his holy temple as witness. Mountains melt like wax and valleys split as judgment falls for Jacob's transgression, Samaria is made a heap of the field with its foundations laid bare, and the prophet wails through a string of town names until the wound reaches the gate of Jerusalem itself. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing John Gill, Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Micah, from Moresheth in Judah, prophesied under Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, contemporary with Isaiah. Gill notes Samaria stands for the ten tribes and Jerusalem for Judah and Benjamin, and Samaria is named first because it was first in transgression and therefore first in punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. All the people, and the whole land with its inhabitants, are summoned to hear, as in a court. Gill reads the Lord from his holy temple as the all-knowing witness who will testify that this message is God's own word, not the prophet's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The Lord comes forth from heaven, not by changing place but by changing his dealing with his people, from mercy to judgment. Gill notes he treads down the high places: proud fortified cities and proud men, and the idol shrines that provoked his wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Mountains melting like wax and valleys splitting picture kingdoms and their great men losing heart and glory under God's judgment, while the lowly people of the valleys share the calamity. Neither high nor low escapes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. All this judgment comes for Jacob's transgression, above all idolatry. Gill traces it to Samaria, the calf-worship and the kings and nobles who encouraged it, and to Jerusalem, where even the temple was defiled under kings like Ahaz. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Samaria will be reduced to a heap of stones in a field, its walls poured into the valley and its foundations laid bare, so that no trace of the city remains. Gill notes the total desolation was fulfilled when Hyrcanus dug the city up until its site could hardly be known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Samaria's graven images will be smashed and her idol-hires burned, her idols left desolate. Henry's point is that what is given to idols never prospers: what one lust gains, another wastes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The prophet answers the sentence with wailing, going stripped and naked like a mourner, crying like desert creatures. Henry reads this as lament over a case grown desperate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Her wound is incurable, and the judgment has reached Judah itself, even to the gate of Jerusalem. The blow that fell on Samaria is now at the capital's door. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The sorrow is not to be declared at Gath or wept over before mockers who would rejoice at Israel's shame. Instead, in the house of Aphrah, a "house of dust," the people are to roll in the dust as mourners humble themselves under God's hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Saphir's inhabitant goes out in naked shame, Zaanan does not come forth, and Bethezel's mourning leaves its neighbours without help. Henry notes these place-names themselves point to the miseries coming on them, meant to awaken a holy fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Maroth waited anxiously for good, but evil came down from the Lord to the gate of Jerusalem. Hope of relief is answered by judgment arriving at the city's entrance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Lachish is told to bind the chariot to the swift beast, a vain preparation for flight. Henry's heading calls such precautions vain: Lachish was the beginning of the sin to Zion, for Israel's transgressions were found in her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Parting presents must be given to Moreshethgath, and the houses of Achzib will prove a lie to the kings of Israel. Henry's warning fits here: refuges other than Christ are refuges of lies to those who trust them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Yet an heir, a dispossessor, will be brought against Mareshah, and the glory of Israel will flee to Adullam. Henry notes other heirs succeed to every inheritance but that of heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The people are to shave themselves bald in mourning for their delicate children, enlarging their baldness like the eagle's, because those children have gone into captivity. All glory is turned to shame except the honour that comes from God alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

  • Samaria. the capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, standing in the prophets for the ten tribes as Jerusalem stands for Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing John Gill, Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Morasthite. a man of Moresheth in Judah, Micah's home town, named in the study note for Micah 1. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing John Gill, Matthew Henry (Concise)
