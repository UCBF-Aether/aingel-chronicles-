The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Josiah was eight years old when he began to reign, and he reigned thirty and one years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Jedidah, the daughter of Adaiah of Boscath.

2 And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, and walked in all the way of David his father, and turned not aside to the right hand or to the left.

3 And it came to pass in the eighteenth year of king Josiah, that the king sent Shaphan the son of Azaliah, the son of Meshullam, the scribe, to the house of the LORD, saying,

4 Go up to Hilkiah the high priest, that he may sum the silver which is brought into the house of the LORD, which the keepers of the door have gathered of the people:

5 And let them deliver it into the hand of the doers of the work, that have the oversight of the house of the LORD: and let them give it to the doers of the work which is in the house of the LORD, to repair the breaches of the house,

6 Unto carpenters, and builders, and masons, and to buy timber and hewn stone to repair the house.

7 Howbeit there was no reckoning made with them of the money that was delivered into their hand, because they dealt faithfully.

8 And Hilkiah the high priest said unto Shaphan the scribe, I have found the book of the law in the house of the LORD. And Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan, and he read it.

9 And Shaphan the scribe came to the king, and brought the king word again, and said, Thy servants have gathered the money that was found in the house, and have delivered it into the hand of them that do the work, that have the oversight of the house of the LORD.

10 And Shaphan the scribe shewed the king, saying, Hilkiah the priest hath delivered me a book. And Shaphan read it before the king.

11 And it came to pass, when the king had heard the words of the book of the law, that he rent his clothes.

12 And the king commanded Hilkiah the priest, and Ahikam the son of Shaphan, and Achbor the son of Michaiah, and Shaphan the scribe, and Asahiah a servant of the king’s, saying,

13 Go ye, enquire of the LORD for me, and for the people, and for all Judah, concerning the words of this book that is found: for great is the wrath of the LORD that is kindled against us, because our fathers have not hearkened unto the words of this book, to do according unto all that which is written concerning us.

14 So Hilkiah the priest, and Ahikam, and Achbor, and Shaphan, and Asahiah, went unto Huldah the prophetess, the wife of Shallum the son of Tikvah, the son of Harhas, keeper of the wardrobe; (now she dwelt in Jerusalem in the college;) and they communed with her.

15 And she said unto them, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Tell the man that sent you to me,

16 Thus saith the LORD, Behold, I will bring evil upon this place, and upon the inhabitants thereof, even all the words of the book which the king of Judah hath read:

17 Because they have forsaken me, and have burned incense unto other gods, that they might provoke me to anger with all the works of their hands; therefore my wrath shall be kindled against this place, and shall not be quenched.

18 But to the king of Judah which sent you to enquire of the LORD, thus shall ye say to him, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, As touching the words which thou hast heard;

19 Because thine heart was tender, and thou hast humbled thyself before the LORD, when thou heardest what I spake against this place, and against the inhabitants thereof, that they should become a desolation and a curse, and hast rent thy clothes, and wept before me; I also have heard thee, saith the LORD.

20 Behold therefore, I will gather thee unto thy fathers, and thou shalt be gathered into thy grave in peace; and thine eyes shall not see all the evil which I will bring upon this place. And they brought the king word again.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In Josiah's eighteenth year the temple repairs uncover the book of the law, and its reading terrifies the young king, who tears his clothes. Huldah the prophetess confirms the judgment on Jerusalem but promises Josiah peace in his own days because his heart was tender. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Josiah was eight when he began to reign and reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem, his mother Jedidah daughter of Adaiah of Boscath. JFB notes that, happier than his grandfather Manasseh, he fell in his minority under better guardians who trained him in piety, and his young affections were so enlisted for true religion that he adhered all his life with undeviating perseverance to God and righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Josiah did right in the LORD's sight, walking in all David his father's way, turning not aside to the right or left. JFB presents this lifelong perseverance as the fruit of his godly upbringing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. In Josiah's eighteenth year he sent Shaphan the scribe to the LORD's house. JFB notes that before this period he had already begun national reformation, with builders employed, money brought by the people and received by Levites at the door, and the whole land purified from idolatry's traces, and now he addressed the temple's repair. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Shaphan was to go to Hilkiah the high priest, that he might sum the silver brought into the LORD's house, which the door-keepers had gathered from the people. JFB explains the king directed Hilkiah to take a general survey to ascertain what repair was necessary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The silver was to be delivered to the doers of the work overseeing the LORD's house, to repair the breaches. JFB notes the preliminary steps were already taken, with builders employed and preparations made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The money went to carpenters, builders, and masons, and to buy timber and hewn stone to repair the house. JFB's account of the repair preparations covers these workmen and materials. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. No reckoning was made with the workmen for the money delivered into their hand, because they dealt faithfully. JFB's section on the temple repair presents this faithful administration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Hilkiah the high priest told Shaphan, "I have found the book of the law in the house of the LORD," and gave the book to Shaphan. JFB identifies the book as the law of Moses, the Pentateuch, the temple copy laid beside the ark in the most holy place, lost during Manasseh's and Amon's ungodly reigns, or under Ahaz when the temple was profaned, and now found during the repair. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Shaphan came to the king, reported his servants had gathered the money brought into the house and delivered it to the work's overseers. JFB notes the book was delivered by Hilkiah the discoverer to Shaphan the scribe, who then brought the king word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Shaphan showed the king that Hilkiah had delivered him a book, and read it before the king. JFB notes the book was shown and read to the king, and thinks the passage read was probably Deuteronomy 28-30, with its renewal of the national covenant and terrible curses against lawbreakers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. When the king heard the words of the book of the law, he rent his clothes. JFB explains the grief and terror this reading produced: though other copies circulated and Josiah must have known the contents in some degree, this reading found his heart as never before, with strong faith in God's word and painful consciousness that long apostasies had exposed the nation to the denounced judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The king commanded Hilkiah, Ahikam son of Shaphan, Achbor son of Michaiah, Shaphan, and Asahiah to go and inquire of the LORD. JFB notes the king's agitated feelings prompted immediate counsel on averting the curses under which his kingdom lay, sending a deputation of principal officers to one endowed with the prophetic spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They were to inquire of the LORD for the king, the people, and all Judah, concerning the book's words, for great was the LORD's wrath kindled against them because their fathers had not hearkened to the book's words. JFB notes Ahikam was a friend of Jeremiah, and the deputation sought counsel on the curses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Hilkiah, Ahikam, Achbor, Shaphan, and Asahiah went to Huldah the prophetess, wife of Shallum son of Tikvah, keeper of the wardrobe, who dwelt in Jerusalem in the college. JFB notes the occasion was urgent, so they were sent not to the young Zephaniah nor to Jeremiah, probably absent at Anathoth, but to Huldah at hand and known for prophetic gifts, dwelling in the Misnah, a suburb of Jerusalem, and held in such veneration that she and Jehoiada were the only non-Davidic persons buried in Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Huldah said, "Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Tell the man that sent you to me." JFB notes her oracular response blended judgment with mercy: it announced impending calamities for the city, yet consoled the king that punishment would not come in his lifetime, because of his faith, penitence, and pious zeal for God's glory and worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The LORD declared he would bring evil upon this place and its inhabitants, even all the words of the book Judah's king had read. JFB presents this as the announced impending calamity, at no distant period, for the city and its inhabitants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. This was because they had forsaken the LORD, burned incense to other gods, and provoked him with all the works of their hands, so his wrath would be kindled and not quenched. JFB's account of Huldah's response covers this ground of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. But to Judah's king who sent them to inquire, the LORD would speak a different word. JFB notes the response's mercy side: the king was consoled with assurance tied to his own faith and piety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Because Josiah's heart was tender and he had humbled himself before the LORD, hearing the words against this place, rending his clothes and weeping, the LORD had heard him. JFB attributes the mercy to Josiah's faith, penitence, and pious zeal displayed in his public capacity and royal influence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Therefore the LORD would gather Josiah to his fathers, and he would be gathered to his grave in peace, his eyes not seeing all the evil to be brought on this place, and they brought the king word again. JFB notes the season of punishment and sorrow would not come during Josiah's lifetime. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
