The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Peter 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 This second epistle, beloved, I now write unto you; in both which I stir up your pure minds by way of remembrance:

2 That ye may be mindful of the words which were spoken before by the holy prophets, and of the commandment of us the apostles of the Lord and Saviour:

3 Knowing this first, that there shall come in the last days scoffers, walking after their own lusts,

4 And saying, Where is the promise of his coming? for since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of the creation.

5 For this they willingly are ignorant of, that by the word of God the heavens were of old, and the earth standing out of the water and in the water:

6 Whereby the world that then was, being overflowed with water, perished:

7 But the heavens and the earth, which are now, by the same word are kept in store, reserved unto fire against the day of judgment and perdition of ungodly men.

8 But, beloved, be not ignorant of this one thing, that one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day.

9 The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.

10 But the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night; in the which the heavens shall pass away with a great noise, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat, the earth also and the works that are therein shall be burned up.

11 Seeing then that all these things shall be dissolved, what manner of persons ought ye to be in all holy conversation and godliness,

12 Looking for and hasting unto the coming of the day of God, wherein the heavens being on fire shall be dissolved, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat?

13 Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness.

14 Wherefore, beloved, seeing that ye look for such things, be diligent that ye may be found of him in peace, without spot, and blameless.

15 And account that the longsuffering of our Lord is salvation; even as our beloved brother Paul also according to the wisdom given unto him hath written unto you;

16 As also in all his epistles, speaking in them of these things; in which are some things hard to be understood, which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other scriptures, unto their own destruction.

17 Ye therefore, beloved, seeing ye know these things before, beware lest ye also, being led away with the error of the wicked, fall from your own stedfastness.

18 But grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. To him be glory both now and for ever. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This second letter is a reminder, Peter says, to recall the prophets' words and the apostles' commandment. Scoffers will ask where the promised coming is, arguing all continues as from creation, but they wilfully forget the world made by God's word and destroyed by water, and that the present heavens and earth are kept for fire. The Lord is not slow but patient, not wanting any to perish; his day will come like a thief, so readers should live holy, godly lives, looking for new heavens and a new earth, and grow in grace and knowledge. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. This second epistle, Peter writes to stir up his readers' pure minds by way of remembrance. Henry notes purified minds are to be stirred up so believers are active and lively in the work of holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Readers are to be mindful of the words spoken before by the holy prophets and of the commandment of the apostles of the Lord and Saviour. Henry's design for the chapter is to remind believers of Christ's final coming to judgment, grounded in that prophetic and apostolic word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. In the last days scoffers will come, walking after their own lusts. Henry describes them as men who make light of sin and mock at salvation by Jesus Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The scoffers ask, "Where is the promise of his coming?" arguing that since the fathers fell asleep all things continue as from the beginning of creation. Henry notes one principal article of faith rests only on a promise, and scoffers will attack it until the Lord comes; because they see no changes, they fear not God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The scoffers are willingly ignorant that by God's word the heavens were of old, and the earth stood out of the water and in the water. Henry answers that had they considered God's past vengeance, they would not scoff at his threatening an equally terrible judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. By that word the world that then was, overflowed with water, perished. Henry points to the flood as proof God once swept away a whole world of ungodly men at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The present heavens and earth are by the same word kept in store, reserved unto fire against the day of judgment and perdition of ungodly men. Henry declares this destruction by fire as sure as the truth and power of God can make it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. One day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day. Henry explains that in God's account there is no difference: all things past, present, and future are ever before him, and a delay of a thousand years is less to him than putting off a thing for a day is to us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance. Henry stresses what men count slackness is longsuffering, giving God's people more time to advance in knowledge, holiness, and good works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night, when the heavens pass away, the elements melt, and the earth and its works are burned up. Henry warns that palaces and all desirable things of worldly-minded men shall be burned, and asks what will become of us if we make this earth our portion, seeing all these things shall be dissolved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Seeing all these things shall be dissolved, what manner of persons ought we to be in holy conversation and godliness? Henry infers from the second coming a call to purity: very exact and universal holiness is enjoined, not resting in any low measure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Believers look for and hasten unto the coming of the day of God, when the heavens on fire shall be dissolved and the elements melt with fervent heat. Henry notes this is the effect of real knowledge, pressing believers toward godliness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. According to his promise we look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness. Henry says true Christians look for this new world freed from present vanity and pollution, and only those clothed with Christ's righteousness and sanctified by the Holy Ghost shall dwell there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Seeing we look for such things, we must be diligent to be found of him in peace, without spot, and blameless. Henry warns never to expect to be found in peace at that day if lazy and idle in this day, in which we must finish the work given us to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. We are to account the longsuffering of our Lord as salvation, as our beloved brother Paul also wrote according to the wisdom given him. Henry urges a right use of the Lord's patience, who as yet delays his coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. In all his epistles Paul speaks of these things, in which are some things hard to be understood, which the unlearned and unstable wrest to their own destruction. Henry notes proud, carnal men wrest some things into agreement with wicked doctrines, but this is no reason to lay aside Paul's epistles or any Scripture, since men left to themselves pervert every gift of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Seeing we know these things before, we must beware lest, being led away with the error of the wicked, we fall from our own stedfastness. Henry warns those led away by error fall from their stedfastness, and believers must disclaim and abhor opinions not warranted by God's law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Instead we are to grow in grace and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, to whom be glory now and for ever. Henry closes with the remedy against error: labour to know Christ more clearly and fully, so as to be more like him and love him better, giving him thanks and praise in full assurance of doing the same hereafter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
