The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he began to speak unto them by parables. A certain man planted a vineyard, and set an hedge about it, and digged a place for the winefat, and built a tower, and let it out to husbandmen, and went into a far country.

2 And at the season he sent to the husbandmen a servant, that he might receive from the husbandmen of the fruit of the vineyard.

3 And they caught him, and beat him, and sent him away empty.

4 And again he sent unto them another servant; and at him they cast stones, and wounded him in the head, and sent him away shamefully handled.

5 And again he sent another; and him they killed, and many others; beating some, and killing some.

6 Having yet therefore one son, his wellbeloved, he sent him also last unto them, saying, They will reverence my son.

7 But those husbandmen said among themselves, This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and the inheritance shall be ours.

8 And they took him, and killed him, and cast him out of the vineyard.

9 What shall therefore the lord of the vineyard do? he will come and destroy the husbandmen, and will give the vineyard unto others.

10 And have ye not read this scripture; The stone which the builders rejected is become the head of the corner:

11 This was the Lord’s doing, and it is marvellous in our eyes?

12 And they sought to lay hold on him, but feared the people: for they knew that he had spoken the parable against them: and they left him, and went their way.

13 And they send unto him certain of the Pharisees and of the Herodians, to catch him in his words.

14 And when they were come, they say unto him, Master, we know that thou art true, and carest for no man: for thou regardest not the person of men, but teachest the way of God in truth: Is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar, or not?

15 Shall we give, or shall we not give? But he, knowing their hypocrisy, said unto them, Why tempt ye me? bring me a penny, that I may see it.

16 And they brought it. And he saith unto them, Whose is this image and superscription? And they said unto him, Caesar’s.

17 And Jesus answering said unto them, Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s. And they marvelled at him.

18 Then come unto him the Sadducees, which say there is no resurrection; and they asked him, saying,

19 Master, Moses wrote unto us, If a man’s brother die, and leave his wife behind him, and leave no children, that his brother should take his wife, and raise up seed unto his brother.

20 Now there were seven brethren: and the first took a wife, and dying left no seed.

21 And the second took her, and died, neither left he any seed: and the third likewise.

22 And the seven had her, and left no seed: last of all the woman died also.

23 In the resurrection therefore, when they shall rise, whose wife shall she be of them? for the seven had her to wife.

24 And Jesus answering said unto them, Do ye not therefore err, because ye know not the scriptures, neither the power of God?

25 For when they shall rise from the dead, they neither marry, nor are given in marriage; but are as the angels which are in heaven.

26 And as touching the dead, that they rise: have ye not read in the book of Moses, how in the bush God spake unto him, saying, I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob?

27 He is not the God of the dead, but the God of the living: ye therefore do greatly err.

28 And one of the scribes came, and having heard them reasoning together, and perceiving that he had answered them well, asked him, Which is the first commandment of all?

29 And Jesus answered him, The first of all the commandments is, Hear, O Israel; The Lord our God is one Lord:

30 And thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind, and with all thy strength: this is the first commandment.

31 And the second is like, namely this, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. There is none other commandment greater than these.

32 And the scribe said unto him, Well, Master, thou hast said the truth: for there is one God; and there is none other but he:

33 And to love him with all the heart, and with all the understanding, and with all the soul, and with all the strength, and to love his neighbour as himself, is more than all whole burnt offerings and sacrifices.

34 And when Jesus saw that he answered discreetly, he said unto him, Thou art not far from the kingdom of God. And no man after that durst ask him any question.

35 And Jesus answered and said, while he taught in the temple, How say the scribes that Christ is the son of David?

36 For David himself said by the Holy Ghost, The LORD said to my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand, till I make thine enemies thy footstool.

37 David therefore himself calleth him Lord; and whence is he then his son? And the common people heard him gladly.

38 And he said unto them in his doctrine, Beware of the scribes, which love to go in long clothing, and love salutations in the marketplaces,

39 And the chief seats in the synagogues, and the uppermost rooms at feasts:

40 Which devour widows’ houses, and for a pretence make long prayers: these shall receive greater damnation.

41 And Jesus sat over against the treasury, and beheld how the people cast money into the treasury: and many that were rich cast in much.

42 And there came a certain poor widow, and she threw in two mites, which make a farthing.

43 And he called unto him his disciples, and saith unto them, Verily I say unto you, That this poor widow hath cast more in, than all they which have cast into the treasury:

44 For all they did cast in of their abundance; but she of her want did cast in all that she had, even all her living.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jesus tells the vineyard parable, in which tenants beat the owner’s servants and kill his beloved son — a story his hearers recognize as aimed at them (vv. 1–12). Three challenges follow: tribute to Caesar, answered “render to Caesar”; Sadducees on resurrection, answered from the God of the living; and a scribe’s question on the first commandment, answered with love of God and neighbour (vv. 13–34). He then asks how the Christ can be both David’s son and David’s Lord, denounces scribes who devour widows’ houses, and watches a poor widow give two mites — all she had (vv. 35–44). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 12

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He began to speak to them by parables: A certain man planted a vineyard, set a hedge about it, dug a place for the winefat, built a tower, let it out to husbandmen, and went into a far country. The vineyard is Israel, hedged, equipped, and entrusted to husbandmen, and the owner's departure tests their stewardship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. At the season he sent to the husbandmen a servant, that he might receive from the husbandmen of the fruit of the vineyard. The season's servant asks only the agreed fruit, for the vineyard's produce belongs to its owner, and prophets come to collect God's due. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They caught him, beat him, and sent him away empty. The first servant's beating and empty return is the pattern of Israel's treatment of prophets, whose message was answered with violence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Again he sent another servant, and at him they cast stones, wounded him in the head, and sent him away shamefully handled. A second servant met stoning and shame, for rejection grew bolder with repetition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Again he sent another, and him they killed, and many others, beating some and killing some. The catalogue rises from beating to killing, and many prophets share the same fate, until patience seems exhausted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Having yet therefore one son, his wellbeloved, he sent him also last to them, saying, They will reverence my son. The son, last and beloved, is sent in hope that reverence for the heir will succeed where servants failed, and the owner's love is the measure of the mission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But those husbandmen said among themselves, This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and the inheritance shall be ours. The heir's coming became the motive for murder, for the husbandmen reasoned that killing the son would make the vineyard theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They took him, killed him, and cast him out of the vineyard. Killing and casting out repeat the son's fate outside the vineyard, as Christ would be cast out of Jerusalem and killed beyond its walls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. What shall therefore the lord of the vineyard do? He will come and destroy the husbandmen, and will give the vineyard to others. The question answers itself: destruction for the murderers and transfer to others, for God's vineyard is not left to faithless keepers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Have you not read this scripture, The stone which the builders rejected is become the head of the corner? The rejected stone becomes the corner, for the builders' judgment is reversed by the Lord, and rejection is the prelude to exaltation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. This was the Lord's doing, and it is marvellous in our eyes? The exaltation is the Lord's work, not man's, and its marvel lies in reversal: the stone men cast aside holds the building together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They sought to lay hold on him, but feared the people, for they knew he had spoken the parable against them, and they left him and went their way. They knew the parable was against them, yet fear of the people, not repentance, stayed their hands, and they left the judge they could not answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They sent to him certain Pharisees and Herodians, to catch him in his words. Pharisees and Herodians, enemies in politics, united to trap him in speech, for a word against Caesar or against God would serve either party. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. When they came, they said, Master, we know you are true, and care for no man, for you regard not the person of men, but teach the way of God in truth: Is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar, or not? Flattery prefaced the trap, praising his truthfulness to bind him to an answer that must offend one side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Shall we give, or shall we not give? But he, knowing their hypocrisy, said, Why tempt you me? Bring me a penny, that I may see it. He names their hypocrisy and asks for a coin, turning the trap into a lesson the coin itself will teach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. They brought it, and he said, Whose is this image and superscription? And they said, Caesar's. The image and superscription are Caesar's, and their own coin convicts them of using Caesar's money while disputing Caesar's due. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Jesus answering said, Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's, and they marvelled at him. The answer divides the duties without confusing them: Caesar's coin to Caesar, and God's image, man himself, to God, and marvel silenced the trappers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Then came to him the Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, and they asked him, saying. Sadducees, denying resurrection, brought a case meant to make resurrection absurd, for their question assumed what it sought to prove. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Master, Moses wrote to us, If a man's brother dies and leaves his wife behind, and leaves no children, his brother should take his wife and raise up seed to his brother. The levirate law is cited correctly, for Moses did command a brother to raise seed, and the Sadducees build their case on it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Now there were seven brothers, and the first took a wife, and dying left no seed. The case is stacked: seven brothers in succession marry one woman, each dying childless, to force an impossible choice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The second took her and died, neither left seed, and the third likewise. Each brother in turn repeats the pattern, and the accumulation is meant to bury resurrection under absurdity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The seven had her and left no seed; last of all the woman died also. All seven and the woman die, and the Sadducees' question waits on the resurrection they deny. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. In the resurrection therefore, when they shall rise, whose wife shall she be of them? For the seven had her to wife. The question assumes resurrection life repeats earthly marriage, and on that false assumption it seems unanswerable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Jesus answering said, Do you not therefore err, because you know not the scriptures, neither the power of God? Their error has two roots: ignorance of Scripture and ignorance of God's power, and both together produce the false dilemma. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. For when they shall rise from the dead, they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels in heaven. Resurrection life is angelic, not earthly: without death there is no need for marriage's succession, and the Sadducees' case dissolves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. As touching the dead, that they rise: have you not read in the book of Moses, how in the bush God spoke to him, saying, I am the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob? Moses' own book, which Sadducees honoured, calls God the God of patriarchs long dead, and the present tense proves they live. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. He is not the God of the dead, but the God of the living: you therefore do greatly err. A God of the living cannot be God of the extinct, and the Sadducees' error is great because it denies both Scripture and the living God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. One of the scribes came, and having heard them reasoning together, and perceiving he had answered well, asked him, Which is the first commandment of all? A scribe, pleased with the answer, asks sincerely, seeking the chief commandment among many. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Jesus answered, The first of all the commandments is, Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God is one Lord. The Shema opens the answer: one Lord is the foundation, and all obedience rests on God's unity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, mind, and strength: this is the first commandment. Love with heart, soul, mind, and strength is total, leaving no faculty unclaimed by God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. The second is like, namely this, You shall love your neighbour as yourself: there is none other commandment greater than these. The second is like the first, for love to God shows itself in love to neighbours, and no commandment outranks these two. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The scribe said, Well, Master, you have said the truth, for there is one God, and there is none other but he. The scribe repeats the answer with approval, owning one God and none other, a confession that answers the Shema he quoted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. To love him with all the heart, understanding, soul, and strength, and to love his neighbour as himself, is more than all whole burnt offerings and sacrifices. The scribe adds the practical conclusion: love outweighs all sacrifice, for offerings without love are empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. When Jesus saw that he answered discreetly, he said, You are not far from the kingdom of God, and no man after that dared ask him any question. Discreet answering brought him near the kingdom, though not yet in it, and his question silenced all further traps. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Jesus answered and said, while he taught in the temple, How say the scribes that Christ is the son of David? Christ now questions the scribes, turning from defence to teaching, and David's sonship is the puzzle they cannot solve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. For David himself said by the Holy Ghost, The LORD said to my Lord, Sit on my right hand, till I make your enemies your footstool. David, speaking by the Spirit, calls the Messiah Lord, and seats him at God's right hand above his enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. David therefore himself calls him Lord, and from where is he then his son? And the common people heard him gladly. If David calls him Lord, he is more than David's son, and the common people, unlike the scribes, heard this gladly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. He said to them in his doctrine, Beware of the scribes, who love to go in long clothing, and love salutations in the marketplaces. Long clothing and market salutations are the scribes' love of display, and Christ warns his hearers against admiring what he condemns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. And the chief seats in the synagogues, and the uppermost rooms at feasts. Chief seats and upper rooms are the same ambition in worship and in society, seeking honour wherever men gather. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. Who devour widows' houses, and for a pretence make long prayers: these shall receive greater damnation. Devouring widows under cover of long prayers is hypocrisy at its worst, and greater pretence brings greater damnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Jesus sat over against the treasury, and beheld how the people cast money into the treasury, and many rich cast in much. Sitting over against the treasury, Christ watched giving itself, for the manner and measure of gifts are seen by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. There came a certain poor widow, and she threw in two mites, which make a farthing. Two mites, a farthing's worth, are the smallest gift in the treasury, and the widow's poverty makes it the greatest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. He called to him his disciples and said, Verily, this poor widow has cast more in than all they which have cast into the treasury. More is measured not by amount but by proportion, and the widow's farthing outweighs the rich men's much. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. For all they did cast in of their abundance, but she of her want did cast in all that she had, even all her living. Abundance gives without feeling it, but want giving all its living gives everything, and God counts the all, not the amount. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 28. One of the scribes came, and having heard them reasoning together, and perceiving he had answered well, asked him, Which is the first commandment of all? A scribe, pleased with the answer, asks sincerely, seeking the chief commandment among many. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Jesus answered, The first of all the commandments is, Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God is one Lord. The Shema opens the answer: one Lord is the foundation, and all obedience rests on God's unity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, mind, and strength: this is the first commandment. Love with heart, soul, mind, and strength is total, leaving no faculty unclaimed by God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. The second is like, namely this, You shall love your neighbour as yourself: there is none other commandment greater than these. The second is like the first, for love to God shows itself in love to neighbours, and no commandment outranks these two. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The scribe said, Well, Master, you have said the truth, for there is one God, and there is none other but he. The scribe repeats the answer with approval, owning one God and none other, a confession that answers the Shema he quoted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. To love him with all the heart, understanding, soul, and strength, and to love his neighbour as himself, is more than all whole burnt offerings and sacrifices. The scribe adds the practical conclusion: love outweighs all sacrifice, for offerings without love are empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. When Jesus saw that he answered discreetly, he said, You are not far from the kingdom of God, and no man after that dared ask him any question. Discreet answering brought him near the kingdom, though not yet in it, and his question silenced all further traps. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Jesus answered and said, while he taught in the temple, How say the scribes that Christ is the son of David? Christ now questions the scribes, turning from defence to teaching, and David's sonship is the puzzle they cannot solve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. For David himself said by the Holy Ghost, The LORD said to my Lord, Sit on my right hand, till I make your enemies your footstool. David, speaking by the Spirit, calls the Messiah Lord, and seats him at God's right hand above his enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. David therefore himself calls him Lord, and from where is he then his son? And the common people heard him gladly. If David calls him Lord, he is more than David's son, and the common people, unlike the scribes, heard this gladly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. He said to them in his doctrine, Beware of the scribes, who love to go in long clothing, and love salutations in the marketplaces. Long clothing and market salutations are the scribes' love of display, and Christ warns his hearers against admiring what he condemns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. And the chief seats in the synagogues, and the uppermost rooms at feasts. Chief seats and upper rooms are the same ambition in worship and in society, seeking honour wherever men gather. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. Who devour widows' houses, and for a pretence make long prayers: these shall receive greater damnation. Devouring widows under cover of long prayers is hypocrisy at its worst, and greater pretence brings greater damnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Jesus sat over against the treasury, and beheld how the people cast money into the treasury, and many rich cast in much. Sitting over against the treasury, Christ watched giving itself, for the manner and measure of gifts are seen by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. There came a certain poor widow, and she threw in two mites, which make a farthing. Two mites, a farthing's worth, are the smallest gift in the treasury, and the widow's poverty makes it the greatest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. He called to him his disciples and said, Verily, this poor widow has cast more in than all they which have cast into the treasury. More is measured not by amount but by proportion, and the widow's farthing outweighs the rich men's much. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. For all they did cast in of their abundance, but she of her want did cast in all that she had, even all her living. Abundance gives without feeling it, but want giving all its living gives everything, and God counts the all, not the amount. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
