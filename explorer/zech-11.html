The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Zechariah 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Open thy doors, O Lebanon, that the fire may devour thy cedars.

2 Howl, fir tree; for the cedar is fallen; because the mighty are spoiled: howl, O ye oaks of Bashan; for the forest of the vintage is come down.

3 There is a voice of the howling of the shepherds; for their glory is spoiled: a voice of the roaring of young lions; for the pride of Jordan is spoiled.

4 Thus saith the LORD my God; Feed the flock of the slaughter;

5 Whose possessors slay them, and hold themselves not guilty: and they that sell them say, Blessed be the LORD; for I am rich: and their own shepherds pity them not.

6 For I will no more pity the inhabitants of the land, saith the LORD: but, lo, I will deliver the men every one into his neighbour’s hand, and into the hand of his king: and they shall smite the land, and out of their hand I will not deliver them.

7 And I will feed the flock of slaughter, even you, O poor of the flock. And I took unto me two staves; the one I called Beauty, and the other I called Bands; and I fed the flock.

8 Three shepherds also I cut off in one month; and my soul lothed them, and their soul also abhorred me.

9 Then said I, I will not feed you: that that dieth, let it die; and that that is to be cut off, let it be cut off; and let the rest eat every one the flesh of another.

10 And I took my staff, even Beauty, and cut it asunder, that I might break my covenant which I had made with all the people.

11 And it was broken in that day: and so the poor of the flock that waited upon me knew that it was the word of the LORD.

12 And I said unto them, If ye think good, give me my price; and if not, forbear. So they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver.

13 And the LORD said unto me, Cast it unto the potter: a goodly price that I was prised at of them. And I took the thirty pieces of silver, and cast them to the potter in the house of the LORD.

14 Then I cut asunder mine other staff, even Bands, that I might break the brotherhood between Judah and Israel.

15 And the LORD said unto me, Take unto thee yet the instruments of a foolish shepherd.

16 For, lo, I will raise up a shepherd in the land, which shall not visit those that be cut off, neither shall seek the young one, nor heal that that is broken, nor feed that that standeth still: but he shall eat the flesh of the fat, and tear their claws in pieces.

17 Woe to the idol shepherd that leaveth the flock! the sword shall be upon his arm, and upon his right eye: his arm shall be clean dried up, and his right eye shall be utterly darkened.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Open your doors, Lebanon, that fire may devour your cedars, and fir, oak, shepherds, and lions howl together as their glory is spoiled. Then the prophet is told to feed the flock of slaughter, whose possessors slay them and hold themselves not guilty while their own shepherds pity them not, and he takes two staves, Beauty and Bands. Three shepherds are cut off in one month, the flock loathes its shepherd, Beauty is broken to break the covenant with all the people, the shepherd's price is weighed at thirty pieces of silver and cast to the potter in the house of the LORD, Bands is broken to break the brotherhood between Judah and Israel, and the chapter ends with a foolish shepherd who will not visit, seek, heal, or feed, and woe to the idol shepherd who leaves the flock. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Open thy doors, O Lebanon, that the fire may devour thy cedars, because Lebanon is told to open its doors, since fire will devour the cedars, the tall timber of the temple and the pride of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Howl, fir tree, for the cedar is fallen, because the mighty are spoiled, howl, O ye oaks of Bashan, for the forest of the vintage is come down, because the fir howls for the fallen cedar, and the oaks of Bashan howl, since the mighty are spoiled and the forest is come down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. There is a voice of the howling of the shepherds, for their glory is spoiled, a voice of the roaring of young lions, for the pride of Jordan is spoiled, because shepherds howl for their spoiled glory, and lions roar for the pride of Jordan spoiled, since pasture and forest are both laid waste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Thus says the Lord my God, feed the flock of the slaughter, because the prophet is commanded to feed the flock marked for slaughter, a charge that makes him the shepherd of a doomed people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Whose possessors slay them, and hold themselves not guilty, and they that sell them say, blessed be the Lord, for I am rich, and their own shepherds pity them not, because the possessors slay without guilt, the sellers bless God for their riches, and the shepherds themselves pity not the flock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For I will no more pity the inhabitants of the land, says the Lord, but, lo, I will deliver every man into his neighbour's hand, and into the hand of his king, and they shall smite the land, and out of their hand I will not deliver them, because pity is withdrawn, and every man is delivered to his neighbour and his king, who will smite the land without deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And I will feed the flock of slaughter, even you, O poor of the flock, and I took unto me two staves, the one I called Beauty, and the other I called Bands, and I fed the flock, because the prophet feeds the poor of the flock with two staves, Beauty for favour and Bands for unity, the two gifts of God's shepherding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Three shepherds also I cut off in one month, and my soul loathed them, and their soul also abhorred me, because three shepherds, the civil, priestly and prophetic rulers, are cut off in one month, and shepherd and flock loathe one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Then said I, I will not feed you, that that dies, let it die, and that that is to be cut off, let it be cut off, and let the rest eat every one the flesh of another, because the shepherd resigns the flock, leaving the dying to die and the rest to devour one another, which is the end of rejected shepherding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. And I took my staff, even Beauty, and cut it asunder, that I might break my covenant which I had made with all the people, because the staff Beauty is broken, breaking the covenant of favour made with all the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And it was broken in that day, and so the poor of the flock that waited upon me knew that it was the word of the Lord, because the breaking happened in that day, and the poor of the flock who waited on the prophet knew it was the Lord's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And I said unto them, if ye think good, give me my price, and if not, forbear, so they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver, because the shepherd asks his wages, and the flock values him at thirty pieces of silver, the price of a slave, which Henry reads as fulfilled in the price paid for Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. And the Lord said unto me, cast it unto the potter, a goodly price that I was prised at of them, and I took the thirty pieces of silver, and cast them to the potter in the house of the Lord, because God calls the thirty pieces a goodly price in irony, and commands them cast to the potter in the Lord's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Then I cut asunder mine other staff, even Bands, that I might break the brotherhood between Judah and Israel, because the second staff, Bands, is broken, breaking the brotherhood between Judah and Israel, so that unity is lost with favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And the Lord said unto me, take unto thee yet the instruments of a foolish shepherd, because after the true shepherd is rejected, the prophet must take the instruments of a foolish shepherd, to show what shepherding the people will get next. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. For, lo, I will raise up a shepherd in the land, which shall not visit those that be cut off, neither shall seek the young one, nor heal that that is broken, nor feed that that stands still, but he shall eat the flesh of the fat, and tear their claws in pieces, because the foolish shepherd does none of the shepherd's work, neither visiting, seeking, healing nor feeding, but eats the fat and tears the claws. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Woe to the idol shepherd that leaves the flock, the sword shall be upon his arm, and upon his right eye, his arm shall be clean dried up, and his right eye shall be utterly darkened, because woe is pronounced on the worthless shepherd who leaves the flock, and the sword falls on his arm and his right eye, drying the strength and darkening the sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
