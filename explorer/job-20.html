The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then answered Zophar the Naamathite, and said,

2 Therefore do my thoughts cause me to answer, and for this I make haste.

3 I have heard the check of my reproach, and the spirit of my understanding causeth me to answer.

4 Knowest thou not this of old, since man was placed upon earth,

5 That the triumphing of the wicked is short, and the joy of the hypocrite but for a moment?

6 Though his excellency mount up to the heavens, and his head reach unto the clouds;

7 Yet he shall perish for ever like his own dung: they which have seen him shall say, Where is he?

8 He shall fly away as a dream, and shall not be found: yea, he shall be chased away as a vision of the night.

9 The eye also which saw him shall see him no more; neither shall his place any more behold him.

10 His children shall seek to please the poor, and his hands shall restore their goods.

11 His bones are full of the sin of his youth, which shall lie down with him in the dust.

12 Though wickedness be sweet in his mouth, though he hide it under his tongue;

13 Though he spare it, and forsake it not; but keep it still within his mouth:

14 Yet his meat in his bowels is turned, it is the gall of asps within him.

15 He hath swallowed down riches, and he shall vomit them up again: God shall cast them out of his belly.

16 He shall suck the poison of asps: the viper’s tongue shall slay him.

17 He shall not see the rivers, the floods, the brooks of honey and butter.

18 That which he laboured for shall he restore, and shall not swallow it down: according to his substance shall the restitution be, and he shall not rejoice therein.

19 Because he hath oppressed and hath forsaken the poor; because he hath violently taken away an house which he builded not;

20 Surely he shall not feel quietness in his belly, he shall not save of that which he desired.

21 There shall none of his meat be left; therefore shall no man look for his goods.

22 In the fulness of his sufficiency he shall be in straits: every hand of the wicked shall come upon him.

23 When he is about to fill his belly, God shall cast the fury of his wrath upon him, and shall rain it upon him while he is eating.

24 He shall flee from the iron weapon, and the bow of steel shall strike him through.

25 It is drawn, and cometh out of the body; yea, the glittering sword cometh out of his gall: terrors are upon him.

26 All darkness shall be hid in his secret places: a fire not blown shall consume him; it shall go ill with him that is left in his tabernacle.

27 The heaven shall reveal his iniquity; and the earth shall rise up against him.

28 The increase of his house shall depart, and his goods shall flow away in the day of his wrath.

29 This is the portion of a wicked man from God, and the heritage appointed unto him by God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Zophar answers a second time, provoked yet claiming calm reason: the triumphing of the wicked is short, and the joy of the hypocrite but for a moment. The wicked man swallows riches and vomits them up again, his children seek to please the poor he oppressed, and his portion — the heritage appointed him by God — is fire, darkness, and a heaven that reveals his iniquity. It is Zophar’s fullest statement of the rule Job keeps disputing. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Zophar the Naamathite answers a second time, provoked by Job's reply yet claiming calm reason. The speech is a second catalogue of the wicked's brief triumph and ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Therefore Zophar's thoughts cause him to answer, and for this he makes haste. JFB explains that the more excited he feels, the more his calm thoughts furnish his answer, notwithstanding his haste within. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Zophar has heard the check of his reproach, and the spirit of his understanding causes him to answer. JFB explains the check as castigation intended as shame to him, and the spirit as his rational spirit answering in calm reason despite reproach urging hastiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Know you not this of old, since man was placed upon earth, that the triumphing of the wicked is short? The appeal is to ancient knowledge, not new theory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The triumphing of the wicked is short, and the joy of the hypocrite but for a moment. JFB explains hypocrite literally as the ungodly, whose joy lasts only a moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Though the wicked's excellency mounts up to the heavens, and his head reaches unto the clouds, yet he shall perish forever like his own dung. JFB cites the same towering pride as in Isaiah and Obadiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Yet he shall perish forever like his own dung, and they who have seen him shall say where is he? JFB notes dung expresses disgust and lowest degradation against the sinner's haughtiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He shall fly away as a dream and shall not be found, chased away as a vision of the night. The brief triumph vanishes like a dream on waking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The eye that saw him shall see him no more, neither shall his place behold him any more. JFB renders it as the eye following him but discerning him no more, a sharp looking that finds nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. His children shall seek to please the poor, and his hands shall restore their goods. JFB explains this as atoning to the poor by restoring robbed property, or the children reduced to seeking favour from the very poor their father oppressed, with their hands restoring the poor's goods in righteous retribution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. His bones are full of the sin of his youth, which shall lie down with him in the dust. JFB notes bones allude to Job's disease and his own words, and that sin so pervades his nature it accompanies him to the grave, never to be rid of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Though wickedness is sweet in his mouth, and he hides it under his tongue, sin's fascination is poison sweet to the taste but deadly to vital organs, prolonged by keeping the sweet morsel long in the mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Though he spares it and forsakes it not, but keeps it still within his mouth, the sweet morsel is kept only to turn bitter within. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Yet his meat in his bowels is turned, and becomes the gall of asps within him. JFB explains turned as a total change into a disagreeable contrary, and gall as where the asp's poison was thought to lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He has swallowed down riches, and shall vomit them up again; God shall cast them out of his belly. JFB notes he is forced to disgorge ill-gotten wealth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He shall suck the poison of asps, and the viper's tongue shall slay him. JFB notes it shall turn out he has sucked the poison all along. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. He shall not see the rivers, the floods, the brooks of honey and butter. JFB explains floods as plentiful streams, and honey and butter poured from jars as emblems of prosperity in the sultry East, now denied him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. That which he laboured for he shall restore, and shall not swallow it down, and shall not rejoice in his substance. JFB gives the image of food taken away before swallowing, and restitution ending all enjoyment of ill-gotten gains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Because he oppressed and forsaken the poor, and violently taken away a house he did not build. JFB notes he ought to have espoused the poor's cause, but left them helpless and without shelter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Surely he shall not feel quietness in his belly, and shall not save of that which he desired. JFB renders it as inward parts knowing no rest from desires, with no inward peace, and not escaping with what he desired, alluding to Job stripped of all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. There shall none of his meat be left, therefore no man shall look for his goods. JFB renders it as because his goods shall have no endurance, no one will look for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. In the fulness of his sufficiency he shall be in straits, and every hand of the wicked shall come upon him. JFB renders straitened as feeling straitened, with the whole hand of the miserable he oppressed coming upon him, the sense of his oppression now turning on him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. When he is about to fill his belly, God shall cast the fury of his wrath upon him, and rain it upon him while he is eating. JFB explains the fiery rain as lightning, alluding to Job's misfortune, the opposite of refreshing desert rain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. He shall flee from the iron weapon, and the bow of steel shall strike him through. JFB notes steel as brass, and that fleeing one danger he falls into a greater from the opposite quarter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. It is drawn and comes out of the body, the glittering sword out of his gall, and terrors are upon him. JFB explains God drawing the sword so it passes right through the body, glittering like lightning, with gall as his life, inflicting a deadly wound. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. All darkness shall be hid in his secret places, and a fire not blown shall consume him, leaving evil in his tabernacle. JFB explains darkness as every calamity stored in God's secret treasures, and fire not blown as kindled by God, not man's hands, obliterating every trace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The heaven shall reveal his iniquity, and the earth shall rise up against him. JFB notes all creation is at enmity with him, proclaiming the guilt he would conceal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. The increase of his house shall depart, and his goods shall flow away in the day of his wrath. JFB explains increase as prosperity, ill got and ill gone, flowing away like waters running dry in summer, using Job's own brook metaphor against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. This is the portion of a wicked man from God, and the heritage appointed unto him by God. JFB notes appointed is not chance but divine decree and settled principle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
