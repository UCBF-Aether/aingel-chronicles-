The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 34 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Furthermore Elihu answered and said,

2 Hear my words, O ye wise men; and give ear unto me, ye that have knowledge.

3 For the ear trieth words, as the mouth tasteth meat.

4 Let us choose to us judgment: let us know among ourselves what is good.

5 For Job hath said, I am righteous: and God hath taken away my judgment.

6 Should I lie against my right? my wound is incurable without transgression.

7 What man is like Job, who drinketh up scorning like water?

8 Which goeth in company with the workers of iniquity, and walketh with wicked men.

9 For he hath said, It profiteth a man nothing that he should delight himself with God.

10 Therefore hearken unto me ye men of understanding: far be it from God, that he should do wickedness; and from the Almighty, that he should commit iniquity.

11 For the work of a man shall he render unto him, and cause every man to find according to his ways.

12 Yea, surely God will not do wickedly, neither will the Almighty pervert judgment.

13 Who hath given him a charge over the earth? or who hath disposed the whole world?

14 If he set his heart upon man, if he gather unto himself his spirit and his breath;

15 All flesh shall perish together, and man shall turn again unto dust.

16 If now thou hast understanding, hear this: hearken to the voice of my words.

17 Shall even he that hateth right govern? and wilt thou condemn him that is most just?

18 Is it fit to say to a king, Thou art wicked? and to princes, Ye are ungodly?

19 How much less to him that accepteth not the persons of princes, nor regardeth the rich more than the poor? for they all are the work of his hands.

20 In a moment shall they die, and the people shall be troubled at midnight, and pass away: and the mighty shall be taken away without hand.

21 For his eyes are upon the ways of man, and he seeth all his goings.

22 There is no darkness, nor shadow of death, where the workers of iniquity may hide themselves.

23 For he will not lay upon man more than right; that he should enter into judgment with God.

24 He shall break in pieces mighty men without number, and set others in their stead.

25 Therefore he knoweth their works, and he overturneth them in the night, so that they are destroyed.

26 He striketh them as wicked men in the open sight of others;

27 Because they turned back from him, and would not consider any of his ways:

28 So that they cause the cry of the poor to come unto him, and he heareth the cry of the afflicted.

29 When he giveth quietness, who then can make trouble? and when he hideth his face, who then can behold him? whether it be done against a nation, or against a man only:

30 That the hypocrite reign not, lest the people be ensnared.

31 Surely it is meet to be said unto God, I have borne chastisement, I will not offend any more:

32 That which I see not teach thou me: if I have done iniquity, I will do no more.

33 Should it be according to thy mind? he will recompense it, whether thou refuse, or whether thou choose; and not I: therefore speak what thou knowest.

34 Let men of understanding tell me, and let a wise man hearken unto me.

35 Job hath spoken without knowledge, and his words were without wisdom.

36 My desire is that Job may be tried unto the end because of his answers for wicked men.

37 For he addeth rebellion unto his sin, he clappeth his hands among us, and multiplieth his words against God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Elihu now addresses the wise men and the friends as well as Job: God cannot do wickedness, nor can the Almighty pervert judgment, for he repays every man according to his work and sets every man’s way before him. He accepts not the persons of princes nor regards the rich more than the poor, for all are the work of his hands, and his eyes are on the ways of man. Affliction, Elihu argues, is God’s discipline and warning — he opens the ear to instruction, and if men obey they spend their days in prosperity. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Furthermore Elihu answered and said. JFB renders answered as proceeded, continuing his speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Hear Elihu's words, O wise men, and give ear unto him, you that have knowledge. JFB notes this chapter is addressed also to the friends, as the thirty-third chapter was to Job alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For the ear tries words, as the mouth tastes meat. JFB renders palate for the discerning sense by which words are tried. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Let us choose to us judgment, and know among ourselves what is good. JFB renders judgment as selecting among the conflicting sentiments advanced what will stand the test of examination. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For Job has said, I am righteous, and God has taken away my judgment. JFB renders judgment as my right, Job's own words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Should Job lie against his right? His wound is incurable without transgression. JFB explains that were Job to renounce his right, confessing himself guilty, he should die, and renders wound literally as mine arrow by which he is pierced, his sickness, without fault to deserve it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. What man is like Job, who drinks up scorning like water? JFB notes the image from the camel, with scorning against God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Job goes in company with the workers of iniquity and walks with wicked men. JFB explains Job virtually makes common cause with the wicked by taking up their sentiments, or at least by saying those who act on them are unpunished, denying God's righteous government because he does not see the reasons of his acts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. For Job has said, it profits a man nothing that he should delight himself with God. JFB renders with God as in intimacy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Therefore hearken unto Elihu, you men of understanding: far be it from God that he should do wickedness, and from the Almighty that he should commit iniquity. JFB calls this the true answer to Job, which God follows up: man is to believe God's ways are right because they are his, not because we fully see they are so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. For the work of a man shall God render unto him, and cause every man to find according to his ways. JFB notes this holds partly here and fully hereafter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Yea, surely God will not do wickedly, neither will the Almighty pervert judgment. JFB notes this stands in opposition to Job's charge: God will not, and cannot, pervert judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Who has given God a charge over the earth, or who has disposed the whole world? JFB argues that if the world were not God's property as made by him, but committed to his charge by a superior, he might act unjustly without injuring himself, but as it is, injustice would undermine the whole order of the world and injure God's own property, with disposed as founded the circle of the globe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. If God set his heart upon man, if he gather unto himself his spirit and his breath. JFB explains that if God had regard to himself only and gathered man's spirit to himself, all flesh must perish together, so God's loving preservation of his creatures proves he cannot be selfish and therefore cannot be unjust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. All flesh shall perish together, and man shall turn again unto dust. The withdrawal of God's breath ends all life at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. If now Job has understanding, let him hear this and hearken to the voice of Elihu's words. JFB notes Elihu now calls Job's special attention, having spoken to all in general. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Shall even he that hates right govern, and will Job condemn him that is most just? JFB renders govern literally as bind by authority, and most just as him who is at once mighty and just: the government of the world would be impossible if injustice were sanctioned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Is it fit to say to a king, you are wicked, and to princes, you are ungodly? JFB notes it would be a gross outrage to reproach thus an earthly monarch, much more the King of kings, and that God punishes impartially the great as the small. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. How much less to him that accepts not the persons of princes, nor regards the rich more than the poor? For they all are the work of his hands. JFB cross-refers to Job 31:15, the common origin of master and servant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. In a moment shall they die, and the people shall be troubled at midnight, and pass away, and the mighty shall be taken away without hand. JFB explains they as the rich and princes who offend God, at midnight as the image of a night attack on a camp, and without hand as without visible agency, by the mere word of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. For God's eyes are upon the ways of man, and he sees all his goings. JFB notes God's omniscience and omnipotence enable him to execute immediate justice, without needing to be long on the watch, as Job thought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. There is no darkness, nor shadow of death, where the workers of iniquity may hide themselves. JFB renders shadow of death as thick darkness, in which no sinner can hide from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. For God will not lay upon man more than right, that he should enter into judgment with God. JFB explains God needs not regard man long in order that he may be brought into judgment, without needing any searching out such as human judgments require. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. God shall break in pieces mighty men without number, and set others in their stead. No formal investigation is needed before he replaces the mighty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Therefore God knows their works, and he overturns them in the night, so that they are destroyed. JFB notes God knows their works without a formal investigation, and overturns them suddenly and unexpectedly in the night, fitly in the night, as it was in it that the godless hid themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. God strikes them as wicked men in the open sight of others. JFB explains striketh as chasteneth, and sight of others as public execution: sinners hid themselves in darkness, therefore they are punished before all, in open day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Because they turned back from God, and would not consider any of his ways. The grounds of their punishment are apostasy and disregard of God's ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. So that they cause the cry of the poor to come unto God, and he hears the cry of the afflicted. JFB explains the grounds further: by oppression they caused the cry of the poor to come before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. When God gives quietness, who then can make trouble? And when he hides his face, who then can behold him, whether against a nation or a man only? JFB renders make trouble as condemn, and notes that whether against a guilty nation or an individual, God acts so. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. That the hypocrite reign not, lest the people be ensnared. JFB renders ensnared as into sin, or enthralled by further oppression. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Surely it is meet to be said unto God, I have borne chastisement, I will not offend any more. JFB explains borne as the penalty of sin, and offend literally as dealing destructively or corruptly: it was to lead Job to this humble confession that Elihu was sent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. That which I see not, teach thou me: if I have done iniquity, I will do no more. The penitent asks God to teach what he cannot see, and pledges amendment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. Should it be according to Job's mind? God will recompense it, whether Job refuse or choose, and not Elihu. JFB renders it as should God recompense sinners according to thy mind, then it is for thee to reject and to choose, not me: it is Job's part, not Elihu's, to show a better way than God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Let men of understanding tell Elihu, and let a wise man hearken unto him. JFB renders it as men will say to me, and the wise man who hearkens to me will say what follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Job has spoken without knowledge, and his words were without wisdom. This is the verdict of the men of understanding upon Job's speeches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. Elihu's desire is that Job may be tried unto the end because of his answers for wicked men. JFB notes tried as by calamities, and that trials of the godly are not removed until they produce the effect designed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. For Job adds rebellion unto his sin, he claps his hands among us, and multiplies his words against God. JFB explains clappeth hands as in scorn, and that to his original sin to correct which trials were sent, he adds rebellion, words arraigning God's justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
