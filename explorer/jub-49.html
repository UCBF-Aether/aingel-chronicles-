The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 49 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 Remember the commandment which the Lord commanded thee concerning the passover, that thou shouldst celebrate it in its season on the fourteenth of the first month, that thou shouldst kill it before it is evening, and that they should eat it by night on the evening of the fifteenth from the time of the setting of the sun.

2 For on this night — the beginning of the festival and the beginning of the joy — ye were eating the passover in Egypt, when all the powers of Mastema had been let loose to slay all the first-born in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh to the first-born of the captive

3 maidservant in the mill, and to the cattle.

4 And this is the sign which the Lord gave them : Into every house on the lintels of which they saw the blood of a lamb of the first year, into (that) house they should not enter to slay, but should pass by (it), that all those should be saved that were in the house because the sign of the blood was on its lintels.

5 And the powers of the Lord did everything according as the Lord commanded them, and they passed by all the children of Israel, and the plague came not upon them to destroy from amongst them any soul either of cattle, or man, or dog.

6 And the plague was very grievous in Egypt, and there was no house in Egypt where there was not one dead, and weeping and lamentation.

7 And all Israel was eating the flesh of the paschal lamb, and drinking the wine and was lauding and blessing, and giving thanks to the Lord God of their fathers, and was ready to go forth from under the yoke of Egypt, and from the evil bondage.

8 And remember thou this day all the days of thy life, and observe it from year to year all the days of thy life, once a year, on its day, according to all the law thereof, and do not adjourn (it) from day to day, or from month to month.

9 For it is an eternal ordinance, and engraven on the heavenly tables regarding all the children of Israel that they should observe it every year on its day once a year, throughout all their generations ; and there is no limit of days, for this is ordained for ever.

10 And the man who is free from uncleanness, and doth not come to observe it on occasion of its day, so as to bring an acceptable offering before the Lord, and to eat and to drink before the Lord on the day of its festival, that man who is clean and close at hand will be cut off ; because he offered not the oblation of the Lord in its appointed season, he will

11 take the guilt upon himself.

12 Let the children of Israel come and observe the passover on the day of its fixed time, on the fourteenth day of the first month, between the evenings, from the third part of the day to the third part of the night, for two portions of the day are given to the light, and a third part to the evening.

13 That is that which the Lord commanded thee that thou shouldst observe it between the evenings.

14 And it is not permissible to slay it during any period of the light, but during the period bordering on the evening and let them eat it at the time of the evening until the third part of the night and whatever is left over of all its flesh from the third part of the night and onwards, let them burn it with fire.

15 And they shall not cook it with water, nor shall they eat it raw, but roast on the fire : they shall eat it with diligence, its head with the inwards thereof and its feet they shall roast with fire, and not break any bone thereof; for of the children of Israel no bone shall be crushed.

16 For this reason the Lord commanded the children of Israel to observe the passover on the day of its fixed time, and they shall not break a bone thereof; for it is a festival day, and a day commanded, and

17 there may be no passing over from day to day, and month to month, but on the day of its festival let it be observed.

18 And do thou command the children of Israel to observe the passover throughout their days, every year, once a year on the day of its fixed time, . and it will come for a memorial well pleasing before the Lord, and no plague will come upon them to' slay or to smite in that year in which they celebrate the passover in its season in every respect according to His command.

19 And they shall not eat it outside the sanctuary of the Lord, but before the sanctuary of the Lord, and all the people of the congregation of Israel shall celebrate it in its appointed season.

20 And every man who hath come upon its day shall eat it in the sanctuary of your God before the Lord from twenty years old and upward ; for thus is it written and ordained that they should eat it in the sanctuary of the Lord.

21 And when the children of Israel come into the land which they are to possess, into the land of Canaan, and set up the tabernacle of the Lord in the midst of the land in one of their tribes until the sanctuary of the Lord hath been built in the land, let them come and celebrate the passover in the midst of the tabernacle of the Lord, and let them slay it before the Lord from year to year.

22 And in the days when the house hath been built in the name of the Lord in the land of their inheritance, they shall go there and slay the passover in the evening, at sunset, at the third part of the day.

23 And they will offer its blood on the threshold of the altar, and shall place its fat on the fire which is upon the altar, and they shall eat its flesh roasted with fire in the court of the house which hath been sanctified in

24 the name of the Lord.

25 And they may not celebrate the passover in their cities nor in any place save before the tabernacle of the Lord, or before His house where His name hath dwelt ; and they will not go astray from the Lord.

26 And do thou, Moses, command the children of Israel to observe the ordinances of the passover, as it was commanded unto thee ; declare thou unto them every year and the day of its days, and the festival of unleavened bread, that they should eat unleavened bread seven days, (and) that they should observe its festival, and that they bring an oblation every day during those seven days of joy before the Lord on the altar of your God.

27 For ye celebrated this festival with haste when ye went forth from Egypt till ye entered into the wilderness of Shur ; for on the shore of the sea ye completed it.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: Remember the commandment concerning the passover: celebrate it in its season on the fourteenth of the first month, kill it before evening, and eat it by night on the evening of the fifteenth from sunset. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. Remember the commandment concerning the passover: celebrate it in its season on the fourteenth of the first month, kill it before evening, and eat it by night on the evening of the fifteenth from sunset. Henry notes that God appointed that on the night of going out of Egypt each family should kill a lamb, and that the blood was to be sprinkled on the door-posts to mark Israel's houses from Egypt's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. On this night, the beginning of the festival and the beginning of the joy, Israel was eating the passover in Egypt when all the powers of Mastema were let loose to slay all the first-born in Egypt, from Pharaoh's first-born to the first-born of the captive maidservant. Henry notes that the plague struck the first-born, the joy and hope of families, reaching from throne to dungeon, prince and peasant alike before God's judgments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The slaying extended to the first-born of the captive maidservant in the mill, and to the cattle. No rank and no kind was exempt: the judgment fell on every first-born in the land except where the blood marked the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The sign the Lord gave was this: into every house on whose lintels they saw the blood of a yearling lamb, they should not enter to slay, but pass by, so that all in the house were saved because the blood was on its lintels. Henry explains the name: the angel, destroying Egypt's first-born, would pass over the houses marked by the lamb's blood, hence the holy feast's name, and the blood was the believer's protection from wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The powers of the Lord did as commanded, passing by all Israel, and the plague came not upon them to destroy any soul, whether cattle, man, or dog. Henry notes that the destroying angel had nothing to do where the blood was, and that Israel's safety was not a reward of their righteousness but the gift of mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The plague was very grievous in Egypt, and there was no house without one dead, with weeping and lamentation. Henry bids the reader imagine the cry that rang through Egypt, the long loud shriek of agony from every dwelling, leaving not a house without one dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. All Israel was eating the paschal lamb's flesh, drinking wine, lauding, blessing, and thanking the Lord God of their fathers, ready to go forth from under Egypt's yoke and evil bondage. Henry notes the lamb was to be eaten standing, with staves in hand, as ready to depart, and that the feast was kept with the bitterness of bondage remembered and deliverance at hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Remember this day all the days of life, and observe it year to year, once a year, on its day, according to all its law, without adjourning it from day to day or month to month. Henry notes that the passover was to be kept every year as a remembrance of preservation and deliverance, and as a type of Christ, and that the keeping of the solemnity looked backward to old mercies and forward to the Lamb of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. It is an eternal ordinance, engraven on the heavenly tables for all Israel, to be observed every year on its day once a year throughout all generations, with no limit of days, ordained for ever. Henry agrees on the perpetuity: it was by an ordinance for ever, so long as we live we must continue feeding on Christ and rejoicing in what he has done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The man free from uncleanness who does not come to observe the passover on its day, to bring an acceptable offering and eat and drink before the Lord on its festival, that man, clean and close at hand, will be cut off, because he offered not the Lord's oblation in its appointed season. Henry notes that in times to come all the congregation must keep the passover, and that all who share God's mercies should join in thankful praise, none neglecting the ordinance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The negligent man will take the guilt upon himself. The guilt is the penalty's ground: the appointed season was known, the offering was due, and the failure was his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Let Israel come and observe the passover on its fixed time, the fourteenth day of the first month, between the evenings, from the third part of the day to the third part of the night, for two portions of the day are given to light and a third part to the evening. Henry notes the lamb was to be set apart and slain as directed, and Jubilees fixes the between-the-evenings window by its thirds of day and night. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. That is what the Lord commanded: observe it between the evenings. The window is the ordinance's centre, neither in the full light nor in the full night, but at the passage between them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. It is not permissible to slay the lamb during any period of light, but during the period bordering on evening, and they must eat it at evening until the third part of the night, burning with fire whatever flesh remains from the third part of the night onward. Henry notes the lamb was to be eaten at once, not put by till morning, and what remained was to be burned, for the feast allowed no delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. They shall not cook it with water nor eat it raw, but roast it on the fire, eating with diligence its head with its inwards and its feet roasted with fire, breaking no bone, for of Israel no bone shall be crushed. Henry notes the lamb was to be roasted with fire, denoting Christ's sufferings even unto death, and that not a bone of it must be broken, which was fulfilled in Christ, denoting his unbroken strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. For this reason the Lord commanded Israel to observe the passover on its fixed time, breaking no bone, for it is a festival day and a commanded day. The unbroken bone is both a rule of eating and a sign: the lamb whole, the people whole, the deliverance whole. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. There may be no passing over from day to day or month to month, but on the day of its festival let it be observed. The fixed day is repeated because the whole ordinance stands on it: adjourning the passover would unfix the memorial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Moses is commanded to have Israel observe the passover throughout their days, every year, once a year on its fixed time, and it will come as a memorial well pleasing before the Lord, with no plague coming to slay or smite in the year they celebrate it in its season according to his command. Henry notes that the great things God does for his people are to be remembered throughout all ages, and that the passover night was a night of the Lord, much to be observed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Moses is commanded to declare to Israel every year the ordinances of the passover, the day of its days, and the festival of unleavened bread, eating unleavened bread seven days, observing its festival, and bringing an oblation every day during those seven days of joy before the Lord on the altar. Henry notes that the feast of unleavened bread was typical of the Christian life, kept in charity without malice's leaven and in sincerity without hypocrisy's leaven, and that in after-times parents should carefully teach their children the service's meaning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Israel celebrated this festival with haste when they went forth from Egypt until they entered the wilderness of Shur, for on the shore of the sea they completed it. Henry notes the lamb was eaten standing, with staves in hand, as ready to depart, and that the haste of the first passover became the pattern of its yearly keeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
