The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 33 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 These are the journeys of the children of Israel, which went forth out of the land of Egypt with their armies under the hand of Moses and Aaron.

2 And Moses wrote their goings out according to their journeys by the commandment of the LORD: and these are their journeys according to their goings out.

3 And they departed from Rameses in the first month, on the fifteenth day of the first month; on the morrow after the passover the children of Israel went out with an high hand in the sight of all the Egyptians.

4 For the Egyptians buried all their firstborn, which the LORD had smitten among them: upon their gods also the LORD executed judgments.

5 And the children of Israel removed from Rameses, and pitched in Succoth.

6 And they departed from Succoth, and pitched in Etham, which is in the edge of the wilderness.

7 And they removed from Etham, and turned again unto Pihahiroth, which is before Baalzephon: and they pitched before Migdol.

8 And they departed from before Pihahiroth, and passed through the midst of the sea into the wilderness, and went three days’ journey in the wilderness of Etham, and pitched in Marah.

9 And they removed from Marah, and came unto Elim: and in Elim were twelve fountains of water, and threescore and ten palm trees; and they pitched there.

10 And they removed from Elim, and encamped by the Red sea.

11 And they removed from the Red sea, and encamped in the wilderness of Sin.

12 And they took their journey out of the wilderness of Sin, and encamped in Dophkah.

13 And they departed from Dophkah, and encamped in Alush.

14 And they removed from Alush, and encamped at Rephidim, where was no water for the people to drink.

15 And they departed from Rephidim, and pitched in the wilderness of Sinai.

16 And they removed from the desert of Sinai, and pitched at Kibrothhattaavah.

17 And they departed from Kibrothhattaavah, and encamped at Hazeroth.

18 And they departed from Hazeroth, and pitched in Rithmah.

19 And they departed from Rithmah, and pitched at Rimmonparez.

20 And they departed from Rimmonparez, and pitched in Libnah.

21 And they removed from Libnah, and pitched at Rissah.

22 And they journeyed from Rissah, and pitched in Kehelathah.

23 And they went from Kehelathah, and pitched in mount Shapher.

24 And they removed from mount Shapher, and encamped in Haradah.

25 And they removed from Haradah, and pitched in Makheloth.

26 And they removed from Makheloth, and encamped at Tahath.

27 And they departed from Tahath, and pitched at Tarah.

28 And they removed from Tarah, and pitched in Mithcah.

29 And they went from Mithcah, and pitched in Hashmonah.

30 And they departed from Hashmonah, and encamped at Moseroth.

31 And they departed from Moseroth, and pitched in Benejaakan.

32 And they removed from Benejaakan, and encamped at Horhagidgad.

33 And they went from Horhagidgad, and pitched in Jotbathah.

34 And they removed from Jotbathah, and encamped at Ebronah.

35 And they departed from Ebronah, and encamped at Eziongaber.

36 And they removed from Eziongaber, and pitched in the wilderness of Zin, which is Kadesh.

37 And they removed from Kadesh, and pitched in mount Hor, in the edge of the land of Edom.

38 And Aaron the priest went up into mount Hor at the commandment of the LORD, and died there, in the fortieth year after the children of Israel were come out of the land of Egypt, in the first day of the fifth month.

39 And Aaron was an hundred and twenty and three years old when he died in mount Hor.

40 And king Arad the Canaanite, which dwelt in the south in the land of Canaan, heard of the coming of the children of Israel.

41 And they departed from mount Hor, and pitched in Zalmonah.

42 And they departed from Zalmonah, and pitched in Punon.

43 And they departed from Punon, and pitched in Oboth.

44 And they departed from Oboth, and pitched in Ijeabarim, in the border of Moab.

45 And they departed from Iim, and pitched in Dibongad.

46 And they removed from Dibongad, and encamped in Almondiblathaim.

47 And they removed from Almondiblathaim, and pitched in the mountains of Abarim, before Nebo.

48 And they departed from the mountains of Abarim, and pitched in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho.

49 And they pitched by Jordan, from Bethjesimoth even unto Abelshittim in the plains of Moab.

50 And the LORD spake unto Moses in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho, saying,

51 Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When ye are passed over Jordan into the land of Canaan;

52 Then ye shall drive out all the inhabitants of the land from before you, and destroy all their pictures, and destroy all their molten images, and quite pluck down all their high places:

53 And ye shall dispossess the inhabitants of the land, and dwell therein: for I have given you the land to possess it.

54 And ye shall divide the land by lot for an inheritance among your families: and to the more ye shall give the more inheritance, and to the fewer ye shall give the less inheritance: every man’s inheritance shall be in the place where his lot falleth; according to the tribes of your fathers ye shall inherit.

55 But if ye will not drive out the inhabitants of the land from before you; then it shall come to pass, that those which ye let remain of them shall be pricks in your eyes, and thorns in your sides, and shall vex you in the land wherein ye dwell.

56 Moreover it shall come to pass, that I shall do unto you, as I thought to do unto them.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter lists every stage of Israel's journey, from Rameses in Egypt, by the hand of Moses and Aaron, through the Red Sea, the wilderness of Sin, Sinai, and the long wandering, to the plains of Moab by Jericho. It is both a record and a command: when they cross the Jordan they must drive out the inhabitants, destroy their pictures, molten images, and high places, and divide the land by lot among their families. If they do not drive them out, those left will be pricks in their eyes and thorns in their sides, and God will do to Israel what he thought to do to the inhabitants. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These are the journeys of Israel, which went forth out of the land of Egypt with their armies, under the hand of Moses and Aaron. JFB notes this chapter winds up the history of Israel's wilderness travels, and the itinerary probably lists only the most important stations, those where they formed prolonged encampments and dispersed flocks to pasture until the herbage was exhausted, extending from departure out of Egypt to arrival on the plains of Moab, while Henry adds they were continually on the remove, led to and fro yet all the while under the pillar of cloud and fire, God leading them about yet leading them the right way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Moses wrote their goings out according to their journeys by the commandment of the Lord: these are their journeys according to their goings out. JFB notes the wisdom of this divine order: partly to establish the truth of the history, partly to preserve a memorial of God's marvellous interpositions for Israel, and partly to confirm their faith for the difficult enterprise ahead, the invasion of Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They departed from Rameses in the first month, on the fifteenth day of the first month; on the morrow after the passover Israel went out with a high hand in the sight of all the Egyptians. JFB identifies Rameses, probably the capital of Goshen, as the place of general rendezvous previous to departure, and the departure on the morrow after passover, with a high hand before all Egypt, marked the exodus itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For the Egyptians buried all their firstborn, which the Lord had smitten among them: upon their gods also the Lord executed judgments. JFB notes upon their gods denotes either their rulers, the firstborn of king and princes, or the idolatrous objects of Egyptian worship, so that Egypt buried its dead while its gods were judged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Israel removed from Rameses, and pitched in Succoth. JFB notes Succoth means booths, a place of no note except as a temporary halting place, the first pitch after leaving Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They departed from Succoth, and pitched in Etham, which is in the edge of the wilderness. JFB notes Etham was the edge or border of that part of Arabia Petraea contiguous to Egypt, known by the general name of Shur, the last station before the turn back toward the sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They removed from Etham, and turned again to Pi-hahiroth, which is before Baal-zephon, and they pitched before Migdol. The turn back to Pi-hahiroth before Baal-zephon set the trap for Pharaoh, placing Israel between Migdol and the sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They departed from before Pi-hahiroth, and passed through the midst of the sea into the wilderness, and went three days' journey in the wilderness of Etham, and pitched in Marah. JFB identifies Marah, thought to be Ain Howarah, by its position and the three days it would take so great a host with children and flocks to march there from the waters of Ayun Musa, and the sea passage stands at the head of the itinerary's wonders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They removed from Marah, and came to Elim, and in Elim were twelve fountains of water, and threescore and ten palm trees, and they pitched there. JFB supposes Elim to be Wady Ghurundel, where twelve fountains and seventy palms answered Marah's bitterness with abundance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They removed from Elim, and encamped by the Red sea. JFB notes the road from Wady Ghurundel leads inland behind a high continuous ridge, then after about three days' march opens again on a plain along the Red Sea's margin, and the narrative's minute accuracy is shown in describing Israel as proceeding by the only practicable route, this plain being the Desert of Sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They removed from the Red sea, and encamped in the wilderness of Sin. The wilderness of Sin, where the manna would fall, was the next encampment after the Red Sea shore, the road leading Israel deeper into dependence on daily bread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They took their journey out of the wilderness of Sin, and encamped in Dophkah. JFB groups Dophkah with Alush and Rephidim as three stations in the great valleys of El Sheikh and Feiran, equivalent to four days' journey for so great a host. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They departed from Dophkah, and encamped in Alush. Alush was the middle station of the three in the valleys of El Sheikh and Feiran, on the road from the wilderness of Sin toward Rephidim and Sinai. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. They removed from Alush, and encamped at Rephidim, where was no water for the people to drink. JFB notes Rephidim was in Horeb, the burnt region, a generic name for a hot mountainous country, and the station is remembered for its lack of water, which the smitten rock supplied. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They departed from Rephidim, and pitched in the wilderness of Sinai. JFB identifies the wilderness of Sinai with Wady Er-Raheh, the plain before the mountain where the law would be given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. They removed from the desert of Sinai, and pitched at Kibroth-hattaavah. JFB notes Kibroth-hattaavah means the graves of lust, and the route on breaking up Sinai's encampment led down Wady Sheikh and across Jebel-et-Tih into Wady Zalaka, pitching at two brief though memorable stations before Hazeroth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. They departed from Kibroth-hattaavah, and encamped at Hazeroth. JFB notes Hazeroth means unwalled villages, supposed to be at Ain-Hadera, the station of Miriam's leprosy and the people's next halt after the graves of lust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They departed from Hazeroth, and pitched in Rithmah. JFB notes Rithmah means the place of the broom, a station possibly in some wady extending westward of the Ghor, the first of the eighteen stations between Hazeroth and Kadesh that belong to the long wandering interval. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. They removed from Ezion-geber, and pitched in the wilderness of Zin, which is Kadesh. JFB notes the arrival at Kadesh in this catalogue corresponds to the second sojourn there, in the first month, while between the two visits to Kadesh there intervened thirty-eight years of wandering hither and thither through El-Tih, often returning to the same spots as their flocks' pasture required, and the intermediate stations belong to that long interval. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. They removed from Kadesh, and pitched in mount Hor, in the edge of the land of Edom. From Kadesh the host moved to mount Hor on Edom's edge, where the second generation's march toward Canaan would turn northward around Edom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. Aaron the priest went up into mount Hor at the commandment of the Lord, and died there, in the fortieth year after Israel was come out of the land of Egypt, in the first day of the fifth month. Aaron's death on mount Hor, at the Lord's commandment in the fortieth year, closed the high priest's wilderness ministry within sight of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. Aaron was a hundred and twenty and three years old when he died in mount Hor. Aaron died at one hundred and twenty-three, three years older than the age at which Moses would die, having served as priest from the exodus to the fortieth year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. And king Arad the Canaanite, which dwelt in the south in the land of Canaan, heard of the coming of Israel. Arad, the southern Canaanite king, heard of Israel's coming from mount Hor, the first Canaanite to take note of the host now turning toward his land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. They removed from Almondiblathaim, and pitched in the mountains of Abarim, before Nebo. JFB notes Abarim was a ridge of rugged hills northwest of the Arnon, whose part called Nebo was one of its highest peaks, opposite Jericho, and from these mountains Moses would soon view the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. They departed from the mountains of Abarim, and pitched in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho. The descent from Abarim brought Israel to the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho, the final staging ground before the crossing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. They pitched by Jordan, from Beth-jesimoth even to Abel-shittim in the plains of Moab. The final camp stretched along Jordan from Beth-jesimoth to Abel-shittim, a broad encampment in the plains of Moab facing Jericho, where the book's remaining events would unfold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. Speak to Israel, and say to them, when you are passed over Jordan into the land of Canaan. The itinerary closed with a command for the crossing: what Israel was to do once passed over Jordan into Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Then you shall drive out all the inhabitants of the land from before you, and destroy all their pictures, and destroy all their molten images, and quite pluck down all their high places. JFB notes drive out here means not mere expulsion but extermination, and pictures means obelisks for idolatrous worship, while molten images and high places stand by metonymy for all groves, altars and materials of worship on hilltops. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. You shall dispossess the inhabitants of the land, and dwell therein: for I have given you the land to possess it. Dispossession and dwelling went together: the land was given to possess, not merely to conquer, and possession required inhabiting it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. You shall divide the land by lot for an inheritance among your families: and to the more you shall give the more inheritance, and to the fewer you shall give the fewer inheritance: every man's inheritance shall be in the place where his lot falls, according to the tribes of your fathers shall you inherit. JFB notes the particular locality of each tribe was determined by lot, while a line was used in measuring the proportion, and Henry's equity rule from chapter 26 applied again: many received more, fewer less. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. But if you will not drive out the inhabitants of the land from before you, then it shall come to pass, that those which you let remain of them shall be pricks in your eyes, and thorns in your sides, and shall vex you in the land wherein you dwell. JFB warns no associations were to be formed with the inhabitants, otherwise the remnant would prove troublesome and dangerous neighbours, enticing to idolatry and depriving Israel of divine favour, and Henry adds if we do not drive out sin, sin will drive us out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 56. Moreover it shall come to pass, that I shall do to you, as I thought to do to them. The final warning reversed the sentence: if Israel learned the Canaanites' wicked ways, Israel too would be put out of the land, as the Canaanites were to be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Connections to other passages

  • The study note for verse 54 connects this passage to Numbers 26: The rule applied here, that the more receive more inheritance and the fewer less, is the equity rule of Numbers 26. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. Then you shall drive out all the inhabitants of the land from before you, and destroy all their pictures, and destroy all their molten images, and quite pluck down all their high places. JFB notes drive out here means not mere expulsion but extermination, and pictures means obelisks for idolatrous worship, while molten images and high places stand by metonymy for all groves, altars and materials of worship on hilltops. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. You shall divide the land by lot for an inheritance among your families: and to the more you shall give the more inheritance, and to the fewer you shall give the fewer inheritance: every man's inheritance shall be in the place where his lot falls, according to the tribes of your fathers shall you inherit. JFB notes the particular locality of each tribe was determined by lot, while a line was used in measuring the proportion, and Henry's equity rule from chapter 26 applied again: many received more, fewer less. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Matthew Henry

  • Verse 51. Speak to Israel, and say to them, when you are passed over Jordan into the land of Canaan. The itinerary closed with a command for the crossing: what Israel was to do once passed over Jordan into Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. You shall dispossess the inhabitants of the land, and dwell therein: for I have given you the land to possess it. Dispossession and dwelling went together: the land was given to possess, not merely to conquer, and possession required inhabiting it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Moreover it shall come to pass, that I shall do to you, as I thought to do to them. The final warning reversed the sentence: if Israel learned the Canaanites' wicked ways, Israel too would be put out of the land, as the Canaanites were to be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
