The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 If thou wilt return, O Israel, saith the LORD, return unto me: and if thou wilt put away thine abominations out of my sight, then shalt thou not remove.

2 And thou shalt swear, The LORD liveth, in truth, in judgment, and in righteousness; and the nations shall bless themselves in him, and in him shall they glory.

3 For thus saith the LORD to the men of Judah and Jerusalem, Break up your fallow ground, and sow not among thorns.

4 Circumcise yourselves to the LORD, and take away the foreskins of your heart, ye men of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem: lest my fury come forth like fire, and burn that none can quench it, because of the evil of your doings.

5 Declare ye in Judah, and publish in Jerusalem; and say, Blow ye the trumpet in the land: cry, gather together, and say, Assemble yourselves, and let us go into the defenced cities.

6 Set up the standard toward Zion: retire, stay not: for I will bring evil from the north, and a great destruction.

7 The lion is come up from his thicket, and the destroyer of the Gentiles is on his way; he is gone forth from his place to make thy land desolate; and thy cities shall be laid waste, without an inhabitant.

8 For this gird you with sackcloth, lament and howl: for the fierce anger of the LORD is not turned back from us.

9 And it shall come to pass at that day, saith the LORD, that the heart of the king shall perish, and the heart of the princes; and the priests shall be astonished, and the prophets shall wonder.

10 Then said I, Ah, Lord GOD! surely thou hast greatly deceived this people and Jerusalem, saying, Ye shall have peace; whereas the sword reacheth unto the soul.

11 At that time shall it be said to this people and to Jerusalem, A dry wind of the high places in the wilderness toward the daughter of my people, not to fan, nor to cleanse,

12 Even a full wind from those places shall come unto me: now also will I give sentence against them.

13 Behold, he shall come up as clouds, and his chariots shall be as a whirlwind: his horses are swifter than eagles. Woe unto us! for we are spoiled.

14 O Jerusalem, wash thine heart from wickedness, that thou mayest be saved. How long shall thy vain thoughts lodge within thee?

15 For a voice declareth from Dan, and publisheth affliction from mount Ephraim.

16 Make ye mention to the nations; behold, publish against Jerusalem, that watchers come from a far country, and give out their voice against the cities of Judah.

17 As keepers of a field, are they against her round about; because she hath been rebellious against me, saith the LORD.

18 Thy way and thy doings have procured these things unto thee; this is thy wickedness, because it is bitter, because it reacheth unto thine heart.

19 My bowels, my bowels! I am pained at my very heart; my heart maketh a noise in me; I cannot hold my peace, because thou hast heard, O my soul, the sound of the trumpet, the alarm of war.

20 Destruction upon destruction is cried; for the whole land is spoiled: suddenly are my tents spoiled, and my curtains in a moment.

21 How long shall I see the standard, and hear the sound of the trumpet?

22 For my people is foolish, they have not known me; they are sottish children, and they have none understanding: they are wise to do evil, but to do good they have no knowledge.

23 I beheld the earth, and, lo, it was without form, and void; and the heavens, and they had no light.

24 I beheld the mountains, and, lo, they trembled, and all the hills moved lightly.

25 I beheld, and, lo, there was no man, and all the birds of the heavens were fled.

26 I beheld, and, lo, the fruitful place was a wilderness, and all the cities thereof were broken down at the presence of the LORD, and by his fierce anger.

27 For thus hath the LORD said, The whole land shall be desolate; yet will I not make a full end.

28 For this shall the earth mourn, and the heavens above be black; because I have spoken it, I have purposed it, and will not repent, neither will I turn back from it.

29 The whole city shall flee for the noise of the horsemen and bowmen; they shall go into thickets, and climb up upon the rocks: every city shall be forsaken, and not a man dwell therein.

30 And when thou art spoiled, what wilt thou do? Though thou clothest thyself with crimson, though thou deckest thee with ornaments of gold, though thou rentest thy face with painting, in vain shalt thou make thyself fair; thy lovers will despise thee, they will seek thy life.

31 For I have heard a voice as of a woman in travail, and the anguish as of her that bringeth forth her first child, the voice of the daughter of Zion, that bewaileth herself, that spreadeth her hands, saying, Woe is me now! for my soul is wearied because of murderers.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The chapter begins with the condition for return — put away abominations, break up fallow ground, and circumcise the heart — and then turns to warning: a trumpet, a standard toward Zion, and an invader like a lion coming from the north. Jeremiah himself cries out in anguish at the sound of war, and the chapter closes with a despoiled Jerusalem whose lovers will despise her and seek her life. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. If Israel will return, let her return to God, putting away her abominations out of his sight, and then she shall not remove. Henry notes these verses belong with the last chapter: sin must be put away out of the heart, else it is not put away out of God's sight, for the heart is open before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Israel shall swear, 'The Lord liveth,' in truth, in judgment, and in righteousness, and the nations shall bless themselves in him and glory in him. Henry's condition is inward truth: an oath in truth and righteousness follows a heart truly returned, not a mouth only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Break up your fallow ground, and sow not among thorns, says the Lord to Judah and Jerusalem. Henry compares the unhumbled heart to ground untilled: it may be improved, it is our ground let out to us, but it lies fallow, overgrown with thorns and weeds, the natural product of the corrupt heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Circumcise yourselves to the Lord and take away the foreskins of your heart, lest his fury come forth like unquenchable fire because of the evil of your doings. Henry urges the prayer behind the command: entreat the Lord to create a clean heart and renew a right spirit, for except a man be born again he cannot enter the kingdom of heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Declare in Judah and publish in Jerusalem: blow the trumpet, gather together, and assemble into the defenced cities. Henry notes the fierce conqueror of the neighbouring nations was to make Judah desolate, and the trumpet call is the approach of that enemy described. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Set up the standard toward Zion, retire and stay not, for evil comes from the north and great destruction. Henry's section warns that the northern conqueror's approach is now the sentence against a people lesser calamities did not rouse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The lion has come up from his thicket, the destroyer of the Gentiles is on his way to make the land desolate and the cities waste without inhabitant. Henry notes the prophet was afflicted to see the people lulled into security by false prophets while this destroyer marched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. For this, gird with sackcloth, lament and howl, for the fierce anger of the Lord is not turned back. Henry observes some attention was paid in Jerusalem to outward reformation, but sackcloth without washed hearts is not enough: hearts must be washed in true repentance and faith from the love and pollution of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. At that day the heart of the king shall perish, and the heart of the princes, the priests shall be astonished and the prophets wonder. Henry's point is the collapse of false security: when misery approaches, every order that trusted in outward profession fails together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Jeremiah cries, 'Ah, Lord God! Surely thou hast greatly deceived this people and Jerusalem, saying, Ye shall have peace, whereas the sword reaches unto the soul.' Henry explains the complaint as the prophet's affliction at false prophets lulling the people into security with promises of peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. At that time it shall be said to this people and Jerusalem that a dry wind of the high places blows toward the daughter of God's people, not to fan nor to cleanse. Henry notes the wind is judgment, not winnowing: it comes to destroy, not to purify. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Even a full wind from those places shall come, and God will now give sentence against them. Henry states the principle: when lesser calamities do not rouse sinners and reform nations, sentence will be given against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Behold, he shall come up as clouds, his chariots as a whirlwind, his horses swifter than eagles, and woe to us, for we are spoiled. Henry's section describes the enemy's approach in exactly these terms: swift, overwhelming, and irresistible once sentence is given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. O Jerusalem, wash thine heart from wickedness, that thou mayest be saved; how long shall vain thoughts lodge within thee? Henry returns to the chapter's demand: outward reformation is not enough; the heart itself must be washed in true repentance and faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. A voice declares from Dan and publishes affliction from Mount Ephraim. Henry notes the Lord's voice declares misery approaching from the north, first heard at the border and moving toward Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Make mention to the nations and publish against Jerusalem that watchers come from a far country and give out their voice against the cities of Judah. Henry's section has the nations themselves summoned as witnesses to Judah's coming desolation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. As keepers of a field, the watchers are against her round about, because she has been rebellious against God. Henry notes the siege encirclement matches the rebellion: the city that rebelled is watched on every side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Thy way and thy doings have procured these things unto thee; this is thy wickedness, because it is bitter, because it reaches unto thine heart. Henry closes the section plainly: when misery overtakes wicked professors, it will be seen that the fruit of wickedness is bitter and the end fatal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. My bowels, my bowels! I am pained at my very heart, my heart makes a noise in me, I cannot hold my peace, because my soul has heard the trumpet, the alarm of war. Henry notes the prophet had no pleasure in delivering messages of wrath: the alarm pains him even as he sounds it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Destruction upon destruction is cried, for the whole land is spoiled, suddenly tents spoiled and curtains in a moment. Henry sees in vision the whole land in confusion, everything out of order compared with what it was. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. How long shall I see the standard and hear the sound of the trumpet? Henry's vision is unrelenting: standard and trumpet recur because the land's confusion is total, not partial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. My people is foolish, they have not known me, sottish children without understanding, wise to do evil but with no knowledge to do good. Henry calls this wretched: to be like foolish children in the concerns of their souls, whatever else they may know. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. I beheld the earth, and lo, it was without form and void, and the heavens had no light. Henry reads the vision as creation undone: the land in confusion mirrors a world returned to formlessness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. I beheld the mountains, and lo, they trembled, and all the hills moved lightly. Henry notes everything is out of order: even the mountains, the land's fixed frame, shake at God's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. I beheld, and lo, there was no man, and all the birds of the heavens were fled. Henry's vision empties the land of its life: man gone, birds fled, the country unpeopled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. I beheld, and lo, the fruitful place was a wilderness, and all its cities were broken down at the presence of the Lord and by his fierce anger. Henry stresses the agent: it is God's presence and fierce anger that break the cities down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The whole land shall be desolate, yet God will not make a full end. Henry draws the limit that saves the vision from despair: the ruin of the Jewish nation would not be final, and every end of our comforts is not a full end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. For this the earth shall mourn and the heavens above be black, because God has spoken it, purposed it, and will not repent or turn back from it. Henry notes that though the Lord may correct his people very severely, yet he will not cast them off; the sentence stands, but so does the remnant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. The whole city shall flee for the noise of horsemen and bowmen, going into thickets and climbing upon rocks, every city forsaken with not a man dwelling therein. Henry warns that no outward privileges, profession, or contrivances will prevent destruction once it is purposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. When thou art spoiled, what wilt thou do? Though thou clothest thyself with crimson, deckest thyself with gold, and rentest thy face with painting, in vain shalt thou make thyself fair, for thy lovers will despise thee and seek thy life. Henry notes ornaments and false colouring are of no avail: the lovers Judah courted will turn executioners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. I have heard a voice as of a woman in travail, anguish as of her first child, the voice of the daughter of Zion bewailing herself and spreading her hands: 'Woe is me now, for my soul is wearied because of murderers.' Henry closes that as sin will find out the sinner, so sorrow will sooner or later find out the secure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
