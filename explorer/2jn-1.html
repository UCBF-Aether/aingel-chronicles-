The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 John 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The elder unto the elect lady and her children, whom I love in the truth; and not I only, but also all they that have known the truth;

2 For the truth’s sake, which dwelleth in us, and shall be with us for ever.

3 Grace be with you, mercy, and peace, from God the Father, and from the Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, in truth and love.

4 I rejoiced greatly that I found of thy children walking in truth, as we have received a commandment from the Father.

5 And now I beseech thee, lady, not as though I wrote a new commandment unto thee, but that which we had from the beginning, that we love one another.

6 And this is love, that we walk after his commandments. This is the commandment, That, as ye have heard from the beginning, ye should walk in it.

7 For many deceivers are entered into the world, who confess not that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh. This is a deceiver and an antichrist.

8 Look to yourselves, that we lose not those things which we have wrought, but that we receive a full reward.

9 Whosoever transgresseth, and abideth not in the doctrine of Christ, hath not God. He that abideth in the doctrine of Christ, he hath both the Father and the Son.

10 If there come any unto you, and bring not this doctrine, receive him not into your house, neither bid him God speed:

11 For he that biddeth him God speed is partaker of his evil deeds.

12 Having many things to write unto you, I would not write with paper and ink: but I trust to come unto you, and speak face to face, that our joy may be full.

13 The children of thy elect sister greet thee. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The elder writes to the elect lady and her children, whom he loves in truth, for the truth's sake which dwells in believers and will be with them forever, wishing grace, mercy and peace from the Father and the Son. He rejoiced to find some of her children walking in truth, and now urges the old commandment made new in practice: love one another by walking in God's commandments. Many deceivers deny Jesus Christ come in the flesh, and such a teacher must not be received into the house or wished God speed, lest the welcomer share in his evil deeds, so the elder hopes instead to speak face to face, that joy may be full. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John writes as the elder, a title of age and office rather than of name, to the elect lady and her children, whom he loves in the truth. Henry reads the lady as a noble Christian matron whose household is known for holiness, and JFB notes that Christian love here rests on shared truth, not mere affection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. That love is for the truth's sake, the truth which dwells in believers and shall be with them for ever. Henry stresses that true religion is not a passing mood but an abiding inward life, and JFB joins this verse to the love of verse 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Grace, mercy, and peace are wished from God the Father and from Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, in truth and love. JFB orders the three gifts: grace covers guilt, mercy relieves misery, and peace follows from both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. John rejoiced greatly to find some of her children walking in truth, as commanded by the Father. Henry sees early godly training bearing fruit, and JFB notes John probably met them on a tour of superintendence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. John beseeches rather than commands, asking mutual love under a commandment old from the beginning. Henry notes love will prevail where bare authority is slighted, and JFB adds that the command is old in its preaching yet new in its gospel ground, imitation of God who first loved us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Love is defined as walking after God's commandments, and the commandment itself is to walk in that love. Henry calls universal obedience the proof of sincere love, and JFB sums it up: love is the fulfilling of the law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Many deceivers have gone out who confess not that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh, and such a one is a deceiver and an antichrist. Henry warns that denying Christ's person undermines his glory and kingdom, and JFB notes the denial strikes at both the possibility and the actuality of the incarnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Believers must look to themselves, lest they lose what has been wrought and miss a full reward. Henry warns that fair attainments in Christ's school can be lost at last, and JFB explains the full reward as fully consummated glory, proportioned to faithfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Whoever runs ahead and abides not in the doctrine of Christ has not God, but whoever abides in it has both Father and Son. Henry reads departure from gospel doctrine as departure from God himself, since that doctrine is the appointed guide to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. A teacher who brings not this doctrine is not to be received into the house nor wished God speed. Henry allows relief of pressing need but not encouragement for ill service, and JFB stresses the greeting forbidden is the token of Christian brotherhood, not mere courtesy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. To bid such a teacher God speed is to partake in his evil deeds. Henry warns that favour, silence, contribution, or open defence can all share another's guilt, and JFB notes the Greek is having communion with his evil deeds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. John defers much to a face-to-face visit, hoping their joy may be full, rather than writing with paper and ink. Henry observes some things are better spoken than written, and JFB notes the heart pours itself out more freely in person than by letter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The children of the elect sister greet the lady, closing the letter with a family salutation. Henry sees grace abounding toward the whole family, with two elect sisters and their children, and JFB notes the non-mention of the lady herself here has been taken to favour reading the lady as a church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Where the commentators differ

On the identity of the elect lady, the study note records two readings, and this page does not adjudicate between them. Matthew Henry reads her as an individual Christian woman, a noble matron whose household is known for holiness, with the elect sister of verse 13 as a second woman of the same standing. Jamieson-Fausset-Brown notes that the non-mention of the lady herself in the closing greeting of verse 13 has been taken to favour reading the lady as a church. Both readings are presented as commentary views, not as settled fact. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, 2 John 1, 13
