The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The words of Jeremiah the son of Hilkiah, of the priests that were in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin:

2 To whom the word of the LORD came in the days of Josiah the son of Amon king of Judah, in the thirteenth year of his reign.

3 It came also in the days of Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah, unto the end of the eleventh year of Zedekiah the son of Josiah king of Judah, unto the carrying away of Jerusalem captive in the fifth month.

4 Then the word of the LORD came unto me, saying,

5 Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations.

6 Then said I, Ah, Lord GOD! behold, I cannot speak: for I am a child.

7 But the LORD said unto me, Say not, I am a child: for thou shalt go to all that I shall send thee, and whatsoever I command thee thou shalt speak.

8 Be not afraid of their faces: for I am with thee to deliver thee, saith the LORD.

9 Then the LORD put forth his hand, and touched my mouth. And the LORD said unto me, Behold, I have put my words in thy mouth.

10 See, I have this day set thee over the nations and over the kingdoms, to root out, and to pull down, and to destroy, and to throw down, to build, and to plant.

11 Moreover the word of the LORD came unto me, saying, Jeremiah, what seest thou? And I said, I see a rod of an almond tree.

12 Then said the LORD unto me, Thou hast well seen: for I will hasten my word to perform it.

13 And the word of the LORD came unto me the second time, saying, What seest thou? And I said, I see a seething pot; and the face thereof is toward the north.

14 Then the LORD said unto me, Out of the north an evil shall break forth upon all the inhabitants of the land.

15 For, lo, I will call all the families of the kingdoms of the north, saith the LORD; and they shall come, and they shall set every one his throne at the entering of the gates of Jerusalem, and against all the walls thereof round about, and against all the cities of Judah.

16 And I will utter my judgments against them touching all their wickedness, who have forsaken me, and have burned incense unto other gods, and worshipped the works of their own hands.

17 Thou therefore gird up thy loins, and arise, and speak unto them all that I command thee: be not dismayed at their faces, lest I confound thee before them.

18 For, behold, I have made thee this day a defenced city, and an iron pillar, and brasen walls against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, against the princes thereof, against the priests thereof, and against the people of the land.

19 And they shall fight against thee; but they shall not prevail against thee; for I am with thee, saith the LORD, to deliver thee.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This opening chapter introduces Jeremiah and his commission. God calls him before birth, answers his protest that he is only a child, and gives him two visions: a rod of an almond tree, promising that God's word will be carried out swiftly, and a seething pot facing north, warning that disaster will come on Judah from the north because the people have forsaken God for other gods. The chapter closes with the promise that, though kings, princes, priests and people will fight against him, they will not prevail, because God is with him to deliver him. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Jeremiah was a priest from Anathoth, a town in Benjamin a few miles north of Jerusalem allotted to the Kohathites. JFB notes the stigma on that priestly family after Abiathar, and that God chooses weak and despised things to confound the mighty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The word came to Jeremiah in the thirteenth year of Josiah. JFB explains that Jehoahaz and Jehoiachin are omitted from the title because each reigned only three months, and that the first and last kings of a prophet's ministry are the ones usually named. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The word came also in the days of Jehoiakim, unto the end of the eleventh year of Zedekiah and the carrying away of Jerusalem in the fifth month. JFB notes these opening verses were probably prefixed by Jeremiah when he collected his prophecies for his countrymen to take to Babylon, and identifies the fifth month with the month of Jerusalem's fall in 2 Kings 25:8. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The word of the LORD came to Jeremiah, marking the start of his prophetic office. JFB notes this inscription was doubtless made by Jeremiah himself when he collected his prophecies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Before Jeremiah was formed, God knew him, meaning he approved him as his chosen instrument, and sanctified him, meaning he separated him to the prophetic office. JFB stresses this is predestination in God's secret counsel, not a claim that Jeremiah was cleansed from original sin at that point, and that his commission reached the nations as well as Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Jeremiah pleaded that he could not speak, for he was a child. JFB takes him to have been under twenty-five, with child here meaning young man, and notes that such reluctance in true prophets shows they did not seize the office from self-deceiving fanaticism, as false prophets did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God refused the excuse of youth: Jeremiah must go to all to whom he is sent and speak whatever he is commanded. JFB reads the sending in a hostile sense, to all against whom he is sent, and notes that Jeremiah, naturally timid, was moulded by the Spirit to the courage the task required without losing his own character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Jeremiah must not fear their faces, for God is with him to deliver him. Henry draws the lesson that the fear of God is the best remedy against the fear of man, and that it is better to have all men as enemies than God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The LORD touched Jeremiah's mouth and put his words there, a symbolic act showing God would give him utterance despite his inability to speak. Gill compares it to the seraph touching Isaiah's lips, and notes the mouth was now sanctified to the LORD's service, so Jeremiah would speak God's words and not his own. Interpreted Study note as filed (sources list: Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; the note text itself names Gill — attribution not resolved in the source file)
  • Verse 10. Jeremiah was set over nations and kingdoms, not as a prince but as a prophet with oversight of them, to foretell their destruction or restoration. JFB explains that prophets are said to do what they foretell, since God's word is his instrument, and that building and planting, restoration on repentance, follow the words of uprooting because denunciation came first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Jeremiah saw a rod of an almond tree. JFB explains the almond is literally the wakeful tree, flowering earliest in the year, and so a symbol of God's early and speedy execution of his purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. God confirmed the vision: he will hasten his word to perform it. JFB renders it, I will be wakeful as to my word, playing on the almond's name, so the vision promises swift fulfilment, not delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. In a second vision Jeremiah saw a seething pot with its face toward the north. JFB explains the pot, kept boiling by a flame blown under it, as an eastern symbol of raging war, and the northern side as the quarter from which the invasion would pour south upon Judah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Out of the north an evil shall break forth upon all the inhabitants of the land. JFB renders break forth as disclose itself, and identifies the evil with the Chaldeans, who would come upon Judah by the northern route since they could not cross the desert. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. God will call all the families of the kingdoms of the north, and they will come and set their thrones at the gates of Jerusalem. JFB takes families as the tribes and clans making up Babylon's kingdoms, and the thrones at the gates as the conquerors setting up their tribunal where justice was administered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. God will utter his judgments against Judah for forsaking him, burning incense to other gods and worshipping the works of their own hands. JFB notes that utter means pronounce, and that the judicial sentences of the invaders would in effect be the judgments of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Jeremiah must gird up his loins, resolutely preparing for his task like a man tucking up flowing robes for active work, and must not be dismayed at their faces. JFB notes dismayed and confound are the same Hebrew word, to break: if he breaks before them, God will let him be broken before them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. God makes Jeremiah a defenced city, an iron pillar and brazen walls against kings, princes, priests and people. JFB explains the plural walls as abundant strength, and the people of the land as the general masses as distinct from rulers and priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. They will fight against Jeremiah but not prevail, for God is with him to deliver him. Henry closes the chapter on this promise of divine protection: the whole counsel of God must be declared, and those who are sure God is with them need not fear whoever stands against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
