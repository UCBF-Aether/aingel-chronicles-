The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD said unto Moses, Go in unto Pharaoh: for I have hardened his heart, and the heart of his servants, that I might shew these my signs before him:

2 And that thou mayest tell in the ears of thy son, and of thy son’s son, what things I have wrought in Egypt, and my signs which I have done among them; that ye may know how that I am the LORD.

3 And Moses and Aaron came in unto Pharaoh, and said unto him, Thus saith the LORD God of the Hebrews, How long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself before me? let my people go, that they may serve me.

4 Else, if thou refuse to let my people go, behold, to morrow will I bring the locusts into thy coast:

5 And they shall cover the face of the earth, that one cannot be able to see the earth: and they shall eat the residue of that which is escaped, which remaineth unto you from the hail, and shall eat every tree which groweth for you out of the field:

6 And they shall fill thy houses, and the houses of all thy servants, and the houses of all the Egyptians; which neither thy fathers, nor thy fathers’ fathers have seen, since the day that they were upon the earth unto this day. And he turned himself, and went out from Pharaoh.

7 And Pharaoh’s servants said unto him, How long shall this man be a snare unto us? let the men go, that they may serve the LORD their God: knowest thou not yet that Egypt is destroyed?

8 And Moses and Aaron were brought again unto Pharaoh: and he said unto them, Go, serve the LORD your God: but who are they that shall go?

9 And Moses said, We will go with our young and with our old, with our sons and with our daughters, with our flocks and with our herds will we go; for we must hold a feast unto the LORD.

10 And he said unto them, Let the LORD be so with you, as I will let you go, and your little ones: look to it; for evil is before you.

11 Not so: go now ye that are men, and serve the LORD; for that ye did desire. And they were driven out from Pharaoh’s presence.

12 And the LORD said unto Moses, Stretch out thine hand over the land of Egypt for the locusts, that they may come up upon the land of Egypt, and eat every herb of the land, even all that the hail hath left.

13 And Moses stretched forth his rod over the land of Egypt, and the LORD brought an east wind upon the land all that day, and all that night; and when it was morning, the east wind brought the locusts.

14 And the locust went up over all the land of Egypt, and rested in all the coasts of Egypt: very grievous were they; before them there were no such locusts as they, neither after them shall be such.

15 For they covered the face of the whole earth, so that the land was darkened; and they did eat every herb of the land, and all the fruit of the trees which the hail had left: and there remained not any green thing in the trees, or in the herbs of the field, through all the land of Egypt.

16 Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron in haste; and he said, I have sinned against the LORD your God, and against you.

17 Now therefore forgive, I pray thee, my sin only this once, and intreat the LORD your God, that he may take away from me this death only.

18 And he went out from Pharaoh, and intreated the LORD.

19 And the LORD turned a mighty strong west wind, which took away the locusts, and cast them into the Red sea; there remained not one locust in all the coasts of Egypt.

20 But the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart, so that he would not let the children of Israel go.

21 And the LORD said unto Moses, Stretch out thine hand toward heaven, that there may be darkness over the land of Egypt, even darkness which may be felt.

22 And Moses stretched forth his hand toward heaven; and there was a thick darkness in all the land of Egypt three days:

23 They saw not one another, neither rose any from his place for three days: but all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings.

24 And Pharaoh called unto Moses, and said, Go ye, serve the LORD; only let your flocks and your herds be stayed: let your little ones also go with you.

25 And Moses said, Thou must give us also sacrifices and burnt offerings, that we may sacrifice unto the LORD our God.

26 Our cattle also shall go with us; there shall not an hoof be left behind; for thereof must we take to serve the LORD our God; and we know not with what we must serve the LORD, until we come thither.

27 But the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart, and he would not let them go.

28 And Pharaoh said unto him, Get thee from me, take heed to thyself, see my face no more; for in that day thou seest my face thou shalt die.

29 And Moses said, Thou hast spoken well, I will see thy face again no more.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Locusts devour what the hail left, and then a darkness that can be felt covers Egypt for three days while Israel has light in their dwellings. Pharaoh offers compromise after compromise, that the men alone go, or that the flocks stay behind, and Moses refuses each one: Israel must go whole, with their cattle, to serve the LORD. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD told Moses to go in to Pharaoh, for he had hardened Pharaoh's heart and his servants' hearts, that he might show his signs before him. Henry observes the hardening is stated before the plague, lest any think the plague caused what the heart had already chosen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The plagues were given that Israel might tell their sons and grandsons what God had wrought in Egypt, and know that he is the LORD. Henry notes the judgments were also lessons for generations: deliverance must be told, not merely enjoyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Moses and Aaron asked Pharaoh, How long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself before the LORD God of the Hebrews? Let my people go, that they may serve me. Henry observes the question exposed the root: not ignorance but refusal to humble himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Else, if Pharaoh refused, tomorrow God would bring the locusts into his coast. Henry notes the eighth plague was aimed at what the hail had spared, finishing the ruin of Egypt's crops. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The locusts would cover the face of the earth so one could not see the earth, and eat the residue escaped from the hail and every tree growing. Henry observes the plague left nothing green: it consumed the remnant, not the abundance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The locusts would fill Pharaoh's houses, his servants' houses, and all Egyptians' houses, such as their fathers had never seen. Henry notes the invasion was domestic as well as agricultural: the houses that held Egypt's wealth would hold its plague. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Pharaoh's servants asked, How long shall this man be a snare unto us? Let the men go and serve the LORD; knowest thou not that Egypt is destroyed? Henry observes the court at last spoke against the king, for ruin had taught them what wonders could not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Moses and Aaron were brought again to Pharaoh, who said, Go, serve the LORD your God, but who are they that shall go? Henry notes the concession hid a trap: Pharaoh would grant worship while keeping hostages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Moses answered, We will go with our young and old, our sons and daughters, our flocks and herds, for we must hold a feast unto the LORD. Henry observes the demand was total: no part of Israel could be left behind, for the whole people were God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Pharaoh retorted, Let the LORD be so with you as I will let you go with your little ones; look to it, for evil is before you. Henry reads the sneer: Pharaoh mocked God's presence while threatening evil, as if he, not God, held their fate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Not so, said Pharaoh: go now ye that are men and serve the LORD, for that ye did desire, and they were driven out from his presence. Henry notes the compromise was refused and the messengers expelled, for a divided Israel was no Israel at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The LORD told Moses to stretch out his hand over Egypt for the locusts, that they might come up and eat every herb the hail had left. Henry observes the command followed the expulsion at once: rejected mercy gave way to judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Moses stretched his rod over Egypt, and the LORD brought an east wind all that day and night, and in the morning the east wind brought the locusts. Henry notes the natural means were God's own: wind from the desert carried the army of insects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The locusts went up over all Egypt and rested in all its coasts, very grievous, such as neither before nor after were like them. Henry observes the plague was unmatched in scale, as the hail had been unmatched in kind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. They covered the face of the whole earth, so that the land was darkened, and they ate every herb and all the fruit the hail had left, so that no green thing remained. Henry notes the darkness was twofold: the sky darkened by swarms, the land stripped bare beneath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron in haste, confessing, I have sinned against the LORD your God and against you. Henry observes the confession now named both God and his messengers, yet it was spoken in haste, under pressure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Forgive my sin only this once, said Pharaoh, and intreat the LORD your God, that he may take away from me this death only. Henry notes he called the locusts death, rightly, for they devoured the life of the land, yet he sought pardon only for this once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Moses went out from Pharaoh and intreated the LORD. Henry observes Moses again interceded for a king who had confessed and would soon deny, showing prayer is not conditioned on the sinner's sincerity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The LORD turned a mighty strong west wind, which took away the locusts and cast them into the Red Sea, so that not one remained in all Egypt's coasts. Henry notes the removal was as miraculous as the coming: a wind reversed, a sea swallowed an army. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. But the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, so that he would not let Israel go. Henry observes the removal of the plague removed the repentance with it, the now-familiar pattern of a heart hardened by mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The LORD told Moses to stretch his hand toward heaven, that there might be darkness over Egypt, even darkness which may be felt. Henry observes the ninth plague struck the sun itself, Egypt's chief god, with a darkness thick enough to touch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Moses stretched his hand toward heaven, and there was thick darkness in all the land of Egypt three days. Henry notes the duration: three days of night without dawn, a figure of the darkness of a soul forsaken by God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. They saw not one another, neither rose any from his place for three days, but all Israel had light in their dwellings. Henry observes the division was now absolute: Egypt sat in palpable night while Goshen shone, neighbours separated by light itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Pharaoh called Moses and said, Go ye, serve the LORD; only let your flocks and herds be stayed, but let your little ones go with you. Henry notes the new bargain: the people might go, but their wealth must stay, a chain of property instead of persons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Moses answered, Thou must give us also sacrifices and burnt offerings, that we may sacrifice unto the LORD our God. Henry observes the reply turned the bargain upside down: Pharaoh must supply, not withhold, the means of worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Our cattle also shall go with us; there shall not a hoof be left behind, for thereof must we take to serve the LORD, and we know not with what we shall serve him until we come thither. Henry notes the principle: worship requires its substance, and God's demands cannot be met with what is left behind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. But the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he would not let them go. Henry observes the ninth plague ended as the eighth had: light restored to Egypt, darkness restored to Pharaoh's heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Pharaoh said, Get thee from me, take heed to thyself, see my face no more; for in the day thou seest my face thou shalt die. Henry notes the threat was the last word of a defeated king: barred from the court, the messenger was threatened with death for returning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Moses answered, Thou hast spoken well, I will see thy face again no more. Henry observes Moses accepted the dismissal calmly, for his work before Pharaoh was nearly done, and the next meeting would be on God's terms, not the king's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
