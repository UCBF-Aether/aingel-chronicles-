The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — John 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.

2 Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away: and every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit.

3 Now ye are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you.

4 Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me.

5 I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.

6 If a man abide not in me, he is cast forth as a branch, and is withered; and men gather them, and cast them into the fire, and they are burned.

7 If ye abide in me, and my words abide in you, ye shall ask what ye will, and it shall be done unto you.

8 Herein is my Father glorified, that ye bear much fruit; so shall ye be my disciples.

9 As the Father hath loved me, so have I loved you: continue ye in my love.

10 If ye keep my commandments, ye shall abide in my love; even as I have kept my Father’s commandments, and abide in his love.

11 These things have I spoken unto you, that my joy might remain in you, and that your joy might be full.

12 This is my commandment, That ye love one another, as I have loved you.

13 Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.

14 Ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you.

15 Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you.

16 Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain: that whatsoever ye shall ask of the Father in my name, he may give it you.

17 These things I command you, that ye love one another.

18 If the world hate you, ye know that it hated me before it hated you.

19 If ye were of the world, the world would love his own: but because ye are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you.

20 Remember the word that I said unto you, The servant is not greater than his lord. If they have persecuted me, they will also persecute you; if they have kept my saying, they will keep yours also.

21 But all these things will they do unto you for my name’s sake, because they know not him that sent me.

22 If I had not come and spoken unto them, they had not had sin: but now they have no cloak for their sin.

23 He that hateth me hateth my Father also.

24 If I had not done among them the works which none other man did, they had not had sin: but now have they both seen and hated both me and my Father.

25 But this cometh to pass, that the word might be fulfilled that is written in their law, They hated me without a cause.

26 But when the Comforter is come, whom I will send unto you from the Father, even the Spirit of truth, which proceedeth from the Father, he shall testify of me:

27 And ye also shall bear witness, because ye have been with me from the beginning.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jesus calls himself the true vine and his Father the husbandman: branches that bear no fruit are taken away, branches that abide are purged to bear more, and apart from him disciples can do nothing (vv. 1–11). The commandment follows — love one another as he has loved them, laying down his life for his friends — and he calls them friends, not servants, because he has made known all he heard from the Father (vv. 12–17). The chapter ends with the world’s hatred foretold: if it hated him, it will hate them, and the Comforter will testify of him as they too bear witness (vv. 18–27). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, John 15

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. I am the true vine, he said, and my Father is the husbandman, for the union of human and divine natures and the fullness of the Spirit in him resemble the root of a vine made fruitful by rich soil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Every branch in me that bears not fruit he takes away, and every branch that bears fruit, he purges it, that it may bring forth more fruit, for the unfruitful are taken away, and even fruitful branches need pruning of notions, passions, and humours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Now you are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you, for there is a cleansing virtue in Christ's word, as it works grace and works out corruption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Abide in me, and I in you, he said; as the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine, no more can you, except you abide in me, for fruitfulness requires constant dependence and communion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. I am the vine, you are the branches, he said; he who abides in me, and I in him, the same brings forth much fruit, for without me you can do nothing, for believers are weak branches borne up only by the root, and all supports and supplies are in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. If a man abides not in me, he is cast forth as a branch, and is withered, and men gather them, and cast them into the fire, and they are burned, for those who abide not, though they flourish awhile in outward profession, come to nothing, and the fire is the fittest place for withered branches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. If you abide in me, and my words abide in you, you shall ask what you will, and it shall be done unto you, for prayer rooted in his word is answered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Herein is my Father glorified, that you bear much fruit, so shall you be my disciples, for the more fruit we bring forth, the more our Lord is glorified. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. As the Father has loved me, so have I loved you, he said; continue you in my love, for those whom God loves as a Father may despise the hatred of all the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. If you keep my commandments, you shall abide in my love, even as I have kept my Father's commandments, and abide in his love, for they are to show love by keeping his commandments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. These things have I spoken unto you, that my joy might remain in you, and that your joy might be full, for the joy of those who abide in Christ's love is a continual feast, not the momentary joy of the hypocrite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. This is my commandment, that you love one another, as I have loved you, for Christ's love to us should direct us to love each other, and this one command includes many duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Greater love has no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends, the measure of Christ's own love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. You are my friends, if you do whatsoever I command you, for friendship with Christ is shown by obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Henceforth I call you not servants, he said, for the servant knows not what his lord does, but I have called you friends, for all things I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. You have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that you should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain, that whatsoever you shall ask of the Father in my name, he may give it you, for election precedes our choosing, and fruitfulness is the purpose of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. These things I command you, that you love one another, he repeated, for love is the sum of his charge to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. If the world hate you, you know that it hated me before it hated you, so hatred for Christ's sake should not surprise his disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. If you were of the world, the world would love his own, he said, but because you are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hates you. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Remember the word I said unto you, The servant is not greater than his lord, he said; if they have persecuted me, they will also persecute you, and if they have kept my saying, they will keep yours also. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. But all these things will they do unto you for my name's sake, because they know not him who sent me, for the world's ignorance of the Father is the true cause of its hatred to Christ's disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. If I had not come and spoken unto them, they had not had sin, he said, but now they have no cloak for their sin, for the clearer the discoveries of grace and truth, the greater the sin of refusing them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. He who hates me hates my Father also, for to oppose Christ as Prophet, Priest, and King is to prove ignorance of the one living and true God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. If I had not done among them the works which none other man did, they had not had sin, he said, but now they have both seen and hated both me and my Father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. But this comes to pass, that the word might be fulfilled that is written in their law, They hated me without a cause, for his rejection was foretold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. But when the Comforter is come, whom I will send unto you from the Father, even the Spirit of truth, which proceeds from the Father, he shall testify of me, for the blessed Spirit will maintain Christ's cause in the world despite opposition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And you also shall bear witness, because you have been with me from the beginning, for believers taught and encouraged by the Spirit bear testimony to Christ and his salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
