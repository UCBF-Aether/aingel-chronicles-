The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The burden of Moab. Because in the night Ar of Moab is laid waste, and brought to silence; because in the night Kir of Moab is laid waste, and brought to silence;

2 He is gone up to Bajith, and to Dibon, the high places, to weep: Moab shall howl over Nebo, and over Medeba: on all their heads shall be baldness, and every beard cut off.

3 In their streets they shall gird themselves with sackcloth: on the tops of their houses, and in their streets, every one shall howl, weeping abundantly.

4 And Heshbon shall cry, and Elealeh: their voice shall be heard even unto Jahaz: therefore the armed soldiers of Moab shall cry out; his life shall be grievous unto him.

5 My heart shall cry out for Moab; his fugitives shall flee unto Zoar, an heifer of three years old: for by the mounting up of Luhith with weeping shall they go it up; for in the way of Horonaim they shall raise up a cry of destruction.

6 For the waters of Nimrim shall be desolate: for the hay is withered away, the grass faileth, there is no green thing.

7 Therefore the abundance they have gotten, and that which they have laid up, shall they carry away to the brook of the willows.

8 For the cry is gone round about the borders of Moab; the howling thereof unto Eglaim, and the howling thereof unto Beerelim.

9 For the waters of Dimon shall be full of blood: for I will bring more upon Dimon, lions upon him that escapeth of Moab, and upon the remnant of the land.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A lament over Moab, laid waste in a single night: its cities Ar and Kir fall silent, its people flee weeping to the heights, and the waters of Dimon run full of blood. Chapters 15 and 16 form one prophecy on Moab's sudden ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Because in the night Ar of Moab is laid waste and brought to silence, and because in the night Kir of Moab is laid waste and brought to silence. Chapters 15–16 form one prophecy on Moab, delivered in Hezekiah's early years and fulfilled when Shalmaneser, on his way to invade Israel, seized Moab's strongholds, since Moab had made common cause with Israel and Syria against Assyria; the night is the time best suited for a hostile incursion, Ar is the metropolis of Moab south of the Arnon, and Kir a citadel not far from it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He is gone up to Bajith and to Dibon, the high places, to weep; Moab will howl over Nebo and over Medeba, and on all their heads will be baldness and every beard cut off. Bajith is rather the temple, answering to the sanctuary of chapter 16, and Dibon in a plain north of the Arnon has gone up to the high places, the usual places of sacrifice; Nebo is the town where Chemosh, Moab's idol, was worshipped, Medeba lies south of Heshbon, and cutting the beard, which Orientals venerated peculiarly, is the greatest mark of sorrow and mortification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. In their streets they will gird themselves with sackcloth, and on the tops of their houses and in their streets everyone will howl, weeping abundantly. The flat housetops were places of resort for prayer in the East, and the verse is built by chiasmus: howling belongs to the housetops, and descending to weep belongs to the streets and squares whither they descend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Heshbon will cry, and Elealeh; their voice will be heard even unto Jahaz; therefore the armed soldiers of Moab will cry out, and his life will be grievous unto him. Heshbon, an Amorite city twenty miles east of Jordan later taken by Moab, Elealeh near it in Reuben, and Jahaz east of Jordan where Moses defeated Sihon, all ring with the cry, and even armed men, instead of fighting for their land, join the general cry because of the sudden overthrow of their cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. My heart will cry out for Moab; his fugitives will flee unto Zoar, a heifer of three years old; for by the mounting up of Luhith with weeping will they go, and in the way of Horonaim they will raise up a cry of destruction. The prophet himself is moved with pity for Moab — ministers denouncing God's wrath should do so with tender sorrow, not exultation — and the fugitives wander as far as Zoar on the extreme southern boundary of the Dead Sea, crying like a heifer of three years old, at full vigour yet unsubdued and now about to be broken, up the ascent of Luhith toward Horonaim near Zoar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For the waters of Nimrim will be desolate, for the hay is withered away, the grass fails, and there is no green thing. This is the cause of their flight southwards: the northern regions and even Nimrim, whose very name means limpid waters, are without water or herbage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Therefore the abundance they have gotten and that which they have laid up will they carry away to the brook of the willows. Because of the devastation, what is over and above the necessaries of life will be carried by the fugitives from failed Nimrim to better-watered places, the valley on the boundary with Arabia-Petraea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For the cry is gone round about the borders of Moab; its howling unto Eglaim, and its howling unto Beer-elim. The cry circles the whole border, from Eglaim on the very borders of Moab or beyond them to Beer-elim, literally the well of the Princes, beyond Moab's east borders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. For the waters of Dimon will be full of blood, for God will bring more upon Dimon, lions upon him that escapes of Moab, and upon the remnant of the land. Dimon is the same as Dibon, and its waters are the Arnon, full of blood from the slain, while fresh calamities follow in the form of lions upon those who escape, with more blood added to Dimon's waters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
