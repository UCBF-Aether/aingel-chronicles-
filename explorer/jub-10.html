Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 10 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the third week of this jubilee the unclean demons began to lead astray the children of the sons of Noah, and to make to err and destroy them.

2 And the sons of Noah came to Noah their father, and they told him concerning the demons which were leading astray and blinding and slaying his sons' sons.

3 And he prayed before the Lord his God, and said :

4 " God of the spirits of all flesh who hast shown mercy unto me, And hast saved me and my sons from the waters

5 of the flood, And hast not caused me to perish as Thou didst the sons of perdition ;

6 For Thy grace hath been great towards me, And great hath been Thy mercy to my soul ;

7 Let Thy grace be lift up upon my sons, And let not wicked spirits rule over them Lest they should destroy them from the earth.

8 But do Thou bless me and my sons, that we may increase and multiply and replenish the earth.

9 And Thou knowest how Thy Watchers, the fathers of these spirits, acted in my day : and as for these spirits which are living, imprison them and hold them fast in the place of condemnation, and let them not bring destruction on the sons of thy servant, my God ; for these are malignant, and created in

10 order to destroy.

11 And let them not rule over the spirits of the living ; for Thou alone canst exercise dominion over them.

12 And let them not have power over the sons of the righteous from henceforth and for evermore." 7.

13 And the Lord our God bade us to bind all.

14 And the chief of the spirits, Mastema came and said : " Lord, Creator, let some of them remain before me, and let them hearken to my voice, and do all that I shall say unto them ; for if some of them are not left to me, I shall not be able to execute the power of my will on the sons of men ; for these are for corruption and leading astray before my judg-

15 ment, for great is the wickedness of the sons of men." 9.

16 And He said : " Let the tenth part of them remain before him, and let nine parts descend into the place of condemnation." 10.

17 And one of us He commanded that we should teach Noah all their medicines ; for He knew that they would not walk in uprightness, nor strive in righteousness.

18 And we did according to all His words : all the malignant evil ones we bound in the place of condemnation, and a tenth part of them we left that they might be subject before Satan on the earth.

19 And we explained to Noah all the medicines of their diseases, together with their seductions, how he might heal them with herbs of the earth.

20 And Noah wrote down all things in a book as we instructed him concerning every kind of medicine.

21 Thus the evil

22 spirits were precluded from (hurting) the sons of Noah.

23 And he gave all that he had written to Shem, his eldest son; for he loved him exceedingly above all his sons.

24 And Noah slept with his fathers, and was buried on Mount Lubar in the land of Ararat.

25 Nine hundred and fifty years he completed in his life, nineteen jubilees and two weeks and five years.

26 And in his life on earth he excelled the children of men save Enoch because of the righteousness, wherein he was perfect.

27 For Enoch's office was ordained for a testimony to the generations of the world so that he should recount all the deeds of generation unto generation, till the day of judgment.

28 And in the three and thirtieth jubilee, in the first year in the second week, Peleg took to himself a wife, whose name was Lomna the daughter of Sina'ar, and she bare him a son in the fourth year of this week, and he called his name Reu ; for he said :

29 'Behold the children of men have become evil through the wicked purpose of building for themselves a city and a tower in the land of Shinar.' For they departed from the land of Ararat eastward to Shinar; for in his days they built the city and the tower, saying, 'Go to, let us ascend thereby into heaven.' And they began to build, and in the fourth week they made brick with fire, and the bricks served them for stone, and the clay with which they cemented them together was asphalt which comes out of the sea, and out of the fountains of water in the land of Shinar. And they built it: forty and three years were they building it; its

30 breadth was 203 bricks, and the height (of a brick) was the third of one; its height amounted to 5433 cubits and 2 palms, and (the extent of one wall was) thirteen stades (and of the other thirty stades).

31 And the Lord our God said unto us : " Behold, they are one people, and (this) they begin to do, and now nothing will be withholden from them.

32 Go to, let us go down and confound their language, that they may not understand one another's speech, and they may be dispersed into cities and nations, and one purpose will no longer abide with them till the day of judgment." 23.

33 And the Lord descended, and we descended with Him to see the city and the tower which the children of men had built.

34 And He confounded their language, and they no longer understood one another's speech, and they ceased then to build the city and the tower.

35 For this reason the whole land of Shinar is called Babel, because the Lord did there confound all the language of the children of men, and from thence they were dispersed into their cities, each according to his language and his nation.

36 And the Lord sent a mighty wind against the tower and overthrew it upon the earth, and behold it was between Asshur and Babylon

37 in the land of Shinar, and they called its name " Overthrow." 27.

38 In the fourth week in the first year in the beginning thereof in the four and thirtieth jubilee, were they dispersed from the land of Shinar,:

39 The Children of Noah enter their Districts ; Canaan seizes Palestine wrongfully ; Madai receives Media.

40 And Ham and his sons went into the land which he was to occupy, which he acquired as his portion in the land of the south.

41 And Canaan saw the land of Lebanon to the river of Egypt that it was very good, and he went not into the land of his inheritance to the west (that is to) the sea and he dwelt in the land of Lebanon, eastward and westward from the border of Jordan and from the border of the sea. 30.

42 And Ham, his father, and Cush and Mizraim, his brothers, said unto him : " Thou hast settled in a land which is not thine, and which did not fall to us by lot : do not do so ; for if thou dost do so, thou and thy sons will fall in the land and (be) accursed through sedition ; for by sedition ye have settled, and by sedition will thy children fall, and thou shalt be rooted out for ever.

43 Dwell not in the dwelling of Shem ; for to Shem and to his sons did it come by their lot.

44 Cursed art thou, and cursed shalt thou be beyond all the sons of Noah, by the curse by which we bound ourselves by an oath in the presence of the holy judge and in the presence of Noah our father."

45 But he did not hearken unto them, and dwelt in the land of Lebanon from Hamath to the entering of Egypt he and his sons until this day.

46 And for this reason that land is named Canaan.

47 And Japheth and his sons went towards the sea and dwelt in the land of their portion, and Madai saw the land of the sea and it did not please him, and he begged a (portion) from Elam and Asshur and Arpachshad,

48 ' Canaan wrongfully seized Palestine, which belonged by right to Arpachshad.

49 his wife's brother, and he dwelt in the land of Media, near to his wife's brother until this day.

50 And he called his dwelling-place, and the dwelling-place of his sons, Media, after the name of their father Madai.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: In the third week of this jubilee the unclean demons began to lead astray the children of the sons of Noah, and to make them err and destroy them. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the third week of this jubilee the unclean demons began to lead astray the children of the sons of Noah, and to make them err and destroy them. After the division of the earth, seduction begins: the demons, spirits whose fathers are the Watchers, lead Noah's grandsons astray. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 2. The sons of Noah came to Noah their father and told him concerning the demons which were leading astray, blinding and slaying his sons' sons. The appeal goes to the patriarch: demons blind and slay, not merely tempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 3. He prayed before the Lord his God. The father's answer to the report is prayer, introducing the long petition that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 4. 'God of the spirits of all flesh, who hast shown mercy unto me, and hast saved me and my sons from the waters of the flood, and hast not caused me to perish as thou didst the sons of perdition.' The prayer opens by addressing God as God of the spirits of all flesh, fitting a petition about spirits, and recalls the flood mercy that saved Noah while the sons of perdition perished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 5. This verse completes the prayer's opening: God did not cause Noah to perish as he did the sons of perdition. Deliverance from the flood is the ground for asking deliverance from the spirits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 6. 'For thy grace hath been great towards me, and great hath been thy mercy to my soul.' Grace and mercy already shown are pleaded as the pattern for what follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. 'Let thy grace be lift up upon my sons, and let not wicked spirits rule over them lest they should destroy them from the earth.' The plea extends Noah's grace to his sons, asking that wicked spirits not rule them to destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. 'But do thou bless me and my sons, that we may increase and multiply and replenish the earth.' Blessing and fruitfulness are asked as the guard of the generations, echoing the covenant blessing of chapter 6. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. 'And thou knowest how thy Watchers, the fathers of these spirits, acted in my day: and as for these spirits which are living, imprison them and hold them fast in the place of condemnation, and let them not bring destruction on the sons of thy servant, my God; for these are malignant, and created in order to destroy.' The Watchers are named as fathers of these spirits, and the plea is imprisonment in the place of condemnation for spirits made to destroy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. This verse completes the plea's reason: the spirits are malignant and created in order to destroy. Their nature, not only their acts, justifies imprisonment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. 'And let them not rule over the spirits of the living; for thou alone canst exercise dominion over them.' Dominion over spirits belongs to God alone; no spirit may rule the living. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. 'And let them not have power over the sons of the righteous from henceforth and forevermore.' The prayer closes by asking perpetual exemption for the righteous' sons from the spirits' power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. The Lord our God bade the angels to bind all. The answer to prayer is a binding command: all the malignant spirits are to be bound. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. The chief of the spirits, Mastema, came and said: 'Lord, Creator, let some of them remain before me, and let them hearken to my voice, and do all that I shall say unto them; for if some of them are not left to me, I shall not be able to execute the power of my will on the sons of men; for these are for corruption and leading astray before my judgment, for great is the wickedness of the sons of men.' Mastema, chief of the evil spirits, pleads that without instruments he cannot execute his will of corruption and leading astray, and appeals to men's great wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. This verse completes Mastema's plea: the remaining spirits are for corruption and leading astray before his judgment, because the wickedness of the sons of men is great. Human wickedness itself is cited as the reason some spirits must remain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. God said: 'Let the tenth part of them remain before him, and let nine parts descend into the place of condemnation.' The decree divides the spirits: a tenth remains under Mastema, nine parts are condemned, fixing the book's demonology in a single proportion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. One of the angels was commanded to teach Noah all their medicines, for God knew that men would not walk in uprightness nor strive in righteousness. Medicines are taught precisely because uprightness will fail: healing is provided alongside the remaining seduction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. The angels did according to all his words: all the malignant evil ones were bound in the place of condemnation, and a tenth part of them was left that they might be subject before Satan on the earth. The binding is executed and the tenth is left subject to Satan, Mastema, on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. The angels explained to Noah all the medicines of their diseases, together with their seductions, how he might heal them with herbs of the earth. Noah learns both the diseases and the seductions, and healing comes through the earth's herbs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. Noah wrote down all things in a book as the angels instructed him concerning every kind of medicine. Charles's introduction notes the book incorporates sections of a Book of Noah, and this verse is its origin: Noah's written book of medicines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. This verse opens the result: thus the evil spirits were precluded from hurting the sons of Noah. The sentence is completed by the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. The evil spirits were precluded from hurting the sons of Noah. Binding, the remaining tenth's subjection, and the book of medicines together shut the spirits out from harming Noah's sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. Noah gave all that he had written to Shem, his eldest son, for he loved him exceedingly above all his sons. The book of medicines passes to Shem, the beloved eldest, as the ark's lore passes to the chosen line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. Noah slept with his fathers and was buried on Mount Lubar in the land of Ararat. The patriarch is buried on the ark's mountain, where his vineyard and his sons' cities stood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. Nine hundred and fifty years he completed in his life, nineteen jubilees and two weeks and five years. The lifespan is reckoned in the book's own units: nineteen jubilees, two weeks and five years make 950 years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. In his life on earth he excelled the children of men save Enoch, because of the righteousness wherein he was perfect. Noah stands second only to Enoch in righteousness among men on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. For Enoch's office was ordained for a testimony to the generations of the world, so that he should recount all the deeds of generation unto generation, till the day of judgment. Enoch's ordained office, recounting deeds from generation to generation until judgment, is restated at Noah's death, as in chapter 4. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. In the thirty-third jubilee, in the first year in the second week, Peleg took Lomna, daughter of Sina'ar, to wife, and she bare him a son in the fourth year of this week, and he called his name Reu, for he said: 'Behold the children of men have become evil through the wicked purpose of building for themselves a city and a tower in the land of Shinar.' Reu's name speech dates the tower generation: evil shown in the purpose of building city and tower in Shinar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. For they departed from the land of Ararat eastward to Shinar, for in his days they built the city and the tower, saying, 'Go to, let us ascend thereby into heaven.' They began to build, and in the fourth week they made brick with fire, and the bricks served them for stone, and the clay cementing them was asphalt from the sea and from the fountains of water in Shinar. They built it: forty and three years were they building it. JFB on Genesis 11:1-4 notes the builders, united by one language, were unwilling to separate despite the command to replenish the earth, chose Shinar's fertile Euphrates-Tigris valley, used brick as the only material where there was no stone, and bitumen as strong cement, and built to defeat heaven's counsels by preventing emigration, which was foolish and wicked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Its breadth was 203 bricks, and the height of a brick was the third of one; its height amounted to 5433 cubits and 2 palms, and the extent of one wall was thirteen stades and of the other thirty stades. The tower's measurements are recorded with the book's usual precision: 43 years building, 203 bricks broad, 5433 cubits and 2 palms high, walls of thirteen and thirty stades. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. The Lord our God said to the angels: 'Behold, they are one people, and this they begin to do, and now nothing will be withholden from them.' JFB on Genesis 11:6 reads this as an apparent admission that the design was practicable and would have been executed but for divine interposition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. 'Go to, let us go down and confound their language, that they may not understand one another's speech, and they may be dispersed into cities and nations, and one purpose will no longer abide with them till the day of judgment.' JFB on Genesis 11:7 explains 'confound' as a failure in utterance producing dialects intelligible only within each tribe, so their purpose was defeated and they were compelled to the dispersion they had combined to prevent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. The Lord descended, and the angels descended with him to see the city and the tower which the children of men had built. Divine inspection with the angels precedes the judgment on the tower. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. He confounded their language, and they no longer understood one another's speech, and they ceased then to build the city and the tower. Confusion stops the building: without common speech the common purpose collapses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. For this reason the whole land of Shinar is called Babel, because the Lord did there confound all the language of the children of men, and from thence they were dispersed into their cities, each according to his language and his nation. JFB notes it is only from Scripture we learn the true origin of the different nations and languages: by one miracle of tongues men were dispersed, and by another the barriers would be broken down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. This verse opens the tower's overthrow: the Lord sent a mighty wind against the tower and overthrew it upon the earth. The sentence is completed by the site and name in the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. Behold it was between Asshur and Babylon in the land of Shinar, and they called its name 'Overthrow.' The fallen tower's site between Asshur and Babylon takes its name from the event: Overthrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. In the fourth week, in the first year in the beginning thereof, in the thirty-fourth jubilee, they were dispersed from the land of Shinar. The dispersion is dated to week, year and jubilee, closing the tower account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the children of Noah enter their districts, Canaan seizes Palestine wrongfully, and Madai receives Media. It divides the tower story from the settlement violations that follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. Ham and his sons went into the land which he was to occupy, which he acquired as his portion in the land of the south. Ham enters his southern portion as divided in chapters 8 and 9. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. Canaan saw the land of Lebanon to the river of Egypt, that it was very good, and he went not into the land of his inheritance to the west, that is to the sea, and he dwelt in the land of Lebanon, eastward and westward from the border of Jordan and from the border of the sea. Charles's note states the wrong plainly: Canaan wrongfully seized Palestine, which belonged by right to Arpachshad, leaving his own western sea portion for Lebanon's goodness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. Ham his father, and Cush and Mizraim his brothers, said to him: 'Thou hast settled in a land which is not thine, and which did not fall to us by lot: do not do so; for if thou dost do so, thou and thy sons will fall in the land and be accursed through sedition; for by sedition ye have settled, and by sedition will thy children fall, and thou shalt be rooted out forever.' The family warns that sedition-settlement ends in sedition-fall and rooting out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. 'Dwell not in the dwelling of Shem; for to Shem and to his sons did it come by their lot.' Lebanon is Shem's dwelling by lot, and Canaan is told not to dwell in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. 'Cursed art thou, and cursed shalt thou be beyond all the sons of Noah, by the curse by which we bound ourselves by an oath in the presence of the holy judge and in the presence of Noah our father.' The curse is invoked under chapter 9's oath, sworn before the holy judge and Noah, and Canaan is cursed beyond all Noah's sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. But he did not hearken to them, and dwelt in the land of Lebanon from Hamath to the entering of Egypt, he and his sons until this day. Disobedience persists to the author's day, from Hamath to Egypt's border. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. For this reason that land is named Canaan. The seized land takes the seizer's name, as the first city took Enoch's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. Japheth and his sons went towards the sea and dwelt in the land of their portion, and Madai saw the land of the sea and it did not please him, and he begged a portion from Elam and Asshur and Arpachshad, his wife's brother. Madai, dissatisfied with his sea land, begs a portion from his wife's brother's line, the sentence completing in verse 49. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 49. His wife's brother, and he dwelt in the land of Media, near to his wife's brother until this day. Madai dwells in Media near his wife's brother to the author's day, a granted portion rather than a seized one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 50. He called his dwelling-place, and the dwelling-place of his sons, Media, after the name of their father Madai. Media is named for Madai, as Canaan's seized land was named for Canaan, but by grant rather than sedition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 16. God said: 'Let the tenth part of them remain before him, and let nine parts descend into the place of condemnation.' The decree divides the spirits: a tenth remains under Mastema, nine parts are condemned, fixing the book's demonology in a single proportion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. One of the angels was commanded to teach Noah all their medicines, for God knew that men would not walk in uprightness nor strive in righteousness. Medicines are taught precisely because uprightness will fail: healing is provided alongside the remaining seduction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. The angels did according to all his words: all the malignant evil ones were bound in the place of condemnation, and a tenth part of them was left that they might be subject before Satan on the earth. The binding is executed and the tenth is left subject to Satan, Mastema, on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. The angels explained to Noah all the medicines of their diseases, together with their seductions, how he might heal them with herbs of the earth. Noah learns both the diseases and the seductions, and healing comes through the earth's herbs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. Noah wrote down all things in a book as the angels instructed him concerning every kind of medicine. Charles's introduction notes the book incorporates sections of a Book of Noah, and this verse is its origin: Noah's written book of medicines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. This verse opens the result: thus the evil spirits were precluded from hurting the sons of Noah. The sentence is completed by the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. The evil spirits were precluded from hurting the sons of Noah. Binding, the remaining tenth's subjection, and the book of medicines together shut the spirits out from harming Noah's sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. Noah gave all that he had written to Shem, his eldest son, for he loved him exceedingly above all his sons. The book of medicines passes to Shem, the beloved eldest, as the ark's lore passes to the chosen line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. Noah slept with his fathers and was buried on Mount Lubar in the land of Ararat. The patriarch is buried on the ark's mountain, where his vineyard and his sons' cities stood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 25. Nine hundred and fifty years he completed in his life, nineteen jubilees and two weeks and five years. The lifespan is reckoned in the book's own units: nineteen jubilees, two weeks and five years make 950 years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. In his life on earth he excelled the children of men save Enoch, because of the righteousness wherein he was perfect. Noah stands second only to Enoch in righteousness among men on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. For Enoch's office was ordained for a testimony to the generations of the world, so that he should recount all the deeds of generation unto generation, till the day of judgment. Enoch's ordained office, recounting deeds from generation to generation until judgment, is restated at Noah's death, as in chapter 4. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. In the thirty-third jubilee, in the first year in the second week, Peleg took Lomna, daughter of Sina'ar, to wife, and she bare him a son in the fourth year of this week, and he called his name Reu, for he said: 'Behold the children of men have become evil through the wicked purpose of building for themselves a city and a tower in the land of Shinar.' Reu's name speech dates the tower generation: evil shown in the purpose of building city and tower in Shinar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. Its breadth was 203 bricks, and the height of a brick was the third of one; its height amounted to 5433 cubits and 2 palms, and the extent of one wall was thirteen stades and of the other thirty stades. The tower's measurements are recorded with the book's usual precision: 43 years building, 203 bricks broad, 5433 cubits and 2 palms high, walls of thirteen and thirty stades. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. The Lord descended, and the angels descended with him to see the city and the tower which the children of men had built. Divine inspection with the angels precedes the judgment on the tower. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. This verse opens the tower's overthrow: the Lord sent a mighty wind against the tower and overthrew it upon the earth. The sentence is completed by the site and name in the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. Behold it was between Asshur and Babylon in the land of Shinar, and they called its name 'Overthrow.' The fallen tower's site between Asshur and Babylon takes its name from the event: Overthrow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. In the fourth week, in the first year in the beginning thereof, in the thirty-fourth jubilee, they were dispersed from the land of Shinar. The dispersion is dated to week, year and jubilee, closing the tower account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the children of Noah enter their districts, Canaan seizes Palestine wrongfully, and Madai receives Media. It divides the tower story from the settlement violations that follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. Ham and his sons went into the land which he was to occupy, which he acquired as his portion in the land of the south. Ham enters his southern portion as divided in chapters 8 and 9. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. Canaan saw the land of Lebanon to the river of Egypt, that it was very good, and he went not into the land of his inheritance to the west, that is to the sea, and he dwelt in the land of Lebanon, eastward and westward from the border of Jordan and from the border of the sea. Charles's note states the wrong plainly: Canaan wrongfully seized Palestine, which belonged by right to Arpachshad, leaving his own western sea portion for Lebanon's goodness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. Ham his father, and Cush and Mizraim his brothers, said to him: 'Thou hast settled in a land which is not thine, and which did not fall to us by lot: do not do so; for if thou dost do so, thou and thy sons will fall in the land and be accursed through sedition; for by sedition ye have settled, and by sedition will thy children fall, and thou shalt be rooted out forever.' The family warns that sedition-settlement ends in sedition-fall and rooting out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. 'Dwell not in the dwelling of Shem; for to Shem and to his sons did it come by their lot.' Lebanon is Shem's dwelling by lot, and Canaan is told not to dwell in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. 'Cursed art thou, and cursed shalt thou be beyond all the sons of Noah, by the curse by which we bound ourselves by an oath in the presence of the holy judge and in the presence of Noah our father.' The curse is invoked under chapter 9's oath, sworn before the holy judge and Noah, and Canaan is cursed beyond all Noah's sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. But he did not hearken to them, and dwelt in the land of Lebanon from Hamath to the entering of Egypt, he and his sons until this day. Disobedience persists to the author's day, from Hamath to Egypt's border. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. For this reason that land is named Canaan. The seized land takes the seizer's name, as the first city took Enoch's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. Japheth and his sons went towards the sea and dwelt in the land of their portion, and Madai saw the land of the sea and it did not please him, and he begged a portion from Elam and Asshur and Arpachshad, his wife's brother. Madai, dissatisfied with his sea land, begs a portion from his wife's brother's line, the sentence completing in verse 49. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 49. His wife's brother, and he dwelt in the land of Media, near to his wife's brother until this day. Madai dwells in Media near his wife's brother to the author's day, a granted portion rather than a seized one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 50. He called his dwelling-place, and the dwelling-place of his sons, Media, after the name of their father Madai. Media is named for Madai, as Canaan's seized land was named for Canaan, but by grant rather than sedition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 29. For they departed from the land of Ararat eastward to Shinar, for in his days they built the city and the tower, saying, 'Go to, let us ascend thereby into heaven.' They began to build, and in the fourth week they made brick with fire, and the bricks served them for stone, and the clay cementing them was asphalt from the sea and from the fountains of water in Shinar. They built it: forty and three years were they building it. JFB on Genesis 11:1-4 notes the builders, united by one language, were unwilling to separate despite the command to replenish the earth, chose Shinar's fertile Euphrates-Tigris valley, used brick as the only material where there was no stone, and bitumen as strong cement, and built to defeat heaven's counsels by preventing emigration, which was foolish and wicked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 31. The Lord our God said to the angels: 'Behold, they are one people, and this they begin to do, and now nothing will be withholden from them.' JFB on Genesis 11:6 reads this as an apparent admission that the design was practicable and would have been executed but for divine interposition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. 'Go to, let us go down and confound their language, that they may not understand one another's speech, and they may be dispersed into cities and nations, and one purpose will no longer abide with them till the day of judgment.' JFB on Genesis 11:7 explains 'confound' as a failure in utterance producing dialects intelligible only within each tribe, so their purpose was defeated and they were compelled to the dispersion they had combined to prevent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. He confounded their language, and they no longer understood one another's speech, and they ceased then to build the city and the tower. Confusion stops the building: without common speech the common purpose collapses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. For this reason the whole land of Shinar is called Babel, because the Lord did there confound all the language of the children of men, and from thence they were dispersed into their cities, each according to his language and his nation. JFB notes it is only from Scripture we learn the true origin of the different nations and languages: by one miracle of tongues men were dispersed, and by another the barriers would be broken down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
