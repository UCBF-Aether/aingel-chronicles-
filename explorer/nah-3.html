The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Nahum 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Woe to the bloody city! it is all full of lies and robbery; the prey departeth not;

2 The noise of a whip, and the noise of the rattling of the wheels, and of the pransing horses, and of the jumping chariots.

3 The horseman lifteth up both the bright sword and the glittering spear: and there is a multitude of slain, and a great number of carcases; and there is none end of their corpses; they stumble upon their corpses:

4 Because of the multitude of the whoredoms of the wellfavoured harlot, the mistress of witchcrafts, that selleth nations through her whoredoms, and families through her witchcrafts.

5 Behold, I am against thee, saith the LORD of hosts; and I will discover thy skirts upon thy face, and I will shew the nations thy nakedness, and the kingdoms thy shame.

6 And I will cast abominable filth upon thee, and make thee vile, and will set thee as a gazingstock.

7 And it shall come to pass, that all they that look upon thee shall flee from thee, and say, Nineveh is laid waste: who will bemoan her? whence shall I seek comforters for thee?

8 Art thou better than populous No, that was situate among the rivers, that had the waters round about it, whose rampart was the sea, and her wall was from the sea?

9 Ethiopia and Egypt were her strength, and it was infinite; Put and Lubim were thy helpers.

10 Yet was she carried away, she went into captivity: her young children also were dashed in pieces at the top of all the streets: and they cast lots for her honourable men, and all her great men were bound in chains.

11 Thou also shalt be drunken: thou shalt be hid, thou also shalt seek strength because of the enemy.

12 All thy strong holds shall be like fig trees with the firstripe figs: if they be shaken, they shall even fall into the mouth of the eater.

13 Behold, thy people in the midst of thee are women: the gates of thy land shall be set wide open unto thine enemies: the fire shall devour thy bars.

14 Draw thee waters for the siege, fortify thy strong holds: go into clay, and tread the morter, make strong the brickkiln.

15 There shall the fire devour thee; the sword shall cut thee off, it shall eat thee up like the cankerworm: make thyself many as the cankerworm, make thyself many as the locusts.

16 Thou hast multiplied thy merchants above the stars of heaven: the cankerworm spoileth, and fleeth away.

17 Thy crowned are as the locusts, and thy captains as the great grasshoppers, which camp in the hedges in the cold day, but when the sun ariseth they flee away, and their place is not known where they are.

18 Thy shepherds slumber, O king of Assyria: thy nobles shall dwell in the dust: thy people is scattered upon the mountains, and no man gathereth them.

19 There is no healing of thy bruise; thy wound is grievous: all that hear the bruit of thee shall clap the hands over thee: for upon whom hath not thy wickedness passed continually?

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Woe to the bloody city, full of lies and robbery, where the prey never departs, whips crack, wheels rattle, swords flash, and corpses cannot be counted. Nineveh is named a well-favoured harlot and mistress of witchcrafts who sold nations, and her skirts will be uncovered before the nations. She is asked whether she is better than populous No, Thebes on the Nile, which fell despite its waters and allies, and the chapter closes with merchants and captains fleeing like locusts, shepherds slumbering, the people scattered, and a wound with no healing, while all who hear of her fall clap their hands. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Woe to the bloody city, all full of lies and robbery, the prey departing not, the city of blood, shed by Nineveh, now to have its own blood shed, never ceasing to live by rapine and extortion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The noise of a whip, and the noise of rattling wheels, and of prancing horses, and of jumping chariots, the reader being transported into the midst of the fight, whips urging chariot horses, wheels rattling, horses prancing, chariots jumping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The horseman lifts up both the bright sword and the glittering spear, and there is a multitude of slain, and a great number of carcases, and none end of corpses, they stumble upon their corpses, the horseman advancing to assault, glitter of sword and flash of spear presenting the battle graphically, the Medo-Babylonian enemy stumbling upon Assyrian corpses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Because of the multitude of the whoredoms of the wellfavoured harlot, mistress of witchcrafts, that sells nations through her whoredoms and families through her witchcrafts, this assigning the reason for Nineveh's destruction, her harlot-like artifices alluring neighbouring states, as Ahaz was made tributary by Tiglath-pileser, and her witchcrafts being subtle machinations and love incantations ensnaring nations, selling them into loss of liberty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Behold, I am against thee, says the LORD of hosts, and I will discover thy skirts upon thy face, and show nations thy nakedness, and kingdoms thy shame, Nineveh being treated not as a matron but as a harlot whose shame is exposed, stripped of glory and defences on which she prides herself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. I will cast abominable filth upon thee, and make thee vile, and set thee as a gazingstock, as infamous harlots used to be treated, exposed to public ignominy as a warning to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. It shall come to pass that all they that look upon thee shall flee from thee, and say, Nineveh is laid waste, who will bemoan her, whence shall I seek comforters for thee, onlookers fleeing her as horrible to look upon, with no comforters to be found. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Art thou better than populous No, that was situate among the rivers, that had waters round about it, whose rampart was the sea, and her wall was from the sea, No-ammon being Thebes in Upper Egypt, possession of Ammon, famed for a hundred gates and a circumference of twenty-seven miles, with the Nile as sea-like rampart, yet overcome by Assyria, so Nineveh in turn shall be overcome by Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Ethiopia and Egypt were her strength, and it was infinite, Put and Lubim were thy helpers, Cush mistress of Upper Egypt, Lower Egypt, Phut famed as archers west of Lower Egypt, and Lubim the Libyans, all endless resources and auxiliaries for No, yet insufficient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Yet was she carried away, she went into captivity, her young children also were dashed in pieces at the top of all streets, and they cast lots for her honourable men, and all her great men were bound in chains, notwithstanding all her might, her honourable men divided by lot as slaves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Thou also shalt be drunken, thou shalt be hid, thou also shalt seek strength because of the enemy, made to drink the cup of Jehovah's wrath, compelled to seek a hiding-place from the foe, and, like Thebes, to have recourse to other nations for help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. All thy strong holds shall be like fig trees with the firstripe figs, if they be shaken, they shall even fall into the mouth of the eater, border strongholds protecting approaches to Nineveh falling with the rapidity and ease of first ripe figs shaken into the eater's mouth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Behold, thy people in the midst of thee are women, the gates of thy land shall be set wide open unto thine enemies, the fire shall devour thy bars, thy soldiers unable to fight, fortified passes through hills northeast of Nineveh thrown open, and bars of fortresses at the passes devoured by fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Draw thee waters for the siege, fortify thy strong holds, go into clay, and tread the morter, make strong the brickkiln, an ironical exhortation to defend herself, drawing water lest cut off from fountains, and repairing the brick-kiln to supply kiln-burnt bricks for breaches or inner fortifications. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. There shall the fire devour thee, the sword shall cut thee off, it shall eat thee up like the cankerworm, make thyself many as the cankerworm, make thyself many as the locusts, in the very scene of her great preparations and security, fire as at Sardanapalus' palace conflagration, and however many her forces like swarming or licking locusts, the foe consuming her as the licking locust licks up all before it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Thou hast multiplied thy merchants above the stars of heaven, the cankerworm spoils, and flies away, Nineveh by canals having easy access to Babylon and the sea, Phoenicians carrying its wares everywhere, yet the licking locust, the Medo-Babylonian invaders, spoiling its merchants and flying away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Thy crowned are as the locusts, and thy captains as the great grasshoppers, which camp in the hedges in the cold day, but when the sun arises they flee away, and their place is not known where they are, princes wearing the tiara and captains, satraps or military leaders, as many as swarming locusts, cold depriving locusts of flight till the sun scatters them traceless, so Assyrian multitudes suddenly disappear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Thy shepherds slumber, O king of Assyria, thy nobles shall dwell in the dust, thy people is scattered upon the mountains, and no man gathers them, leaders lying in death's sleep, nobles in the dust, and people scattered as a necessary consequence of leaders laid low, with none to gather them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. There is no healing of thy bruise, thy wound is grievous, all that hear the bruit of thee shall clap hands over thee, for upon whom has not thy wickedness passed continually, the report of her fall bringing joyful hand-clapping, her long-forborne and continual wickedness rendering her case one admitting no hope of restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Nineveh. the great Assyrian city, three days' journey across, to which Jonah is sent and whose repentance spares it in Jonah 3, and whose fall Nahum announces. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Populous No. No-ammon, Thebes in Upper Egypt, famed for a hundred gates with the Nile as its sea-like rampart, yet carried into captivity; Nahum asks whether Nineveh is better than it. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
