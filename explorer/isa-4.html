The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And in that day seven women shall take hold of one man, saying, We will eat our own bread, and wear our own apparel: only let us be called by thy name, to take away our reproach.

2 In that day shall the branch of the LORD be beautiful and glorious, and the fruit of the earth shall be excellent and comely for them that are escaped of Israel.

3 And it shall come to pass, that he that is left in Zion, and he that remaineth in Jerusalem, shall be called holy, even every one that is written among the living in Jerusalem:

4 When the Lord shall have washed away the filth of the daughters of Zion, and shall have purged the blood of Jerusalem from the midst thereof by the spirit of judgment, and by the spirit of burning.

5 And the LORD will create upon every dwelling place of mount Zion, and upon her assemblies, a cloud and smoke by day, and the shining of a flaming fire by night: for upon all the glory shall be a defence.

6 And there shall be a tabernacle for a shadow in the day time from the heat, and for a place of refuge, and for a covert from storm and from rain.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After the slaughter of war, so few men remain that seven women cling to one man simply to be called by his name. Out of that ruin comes the promise of a cleansed remnant in Zion, washed and sheltered under God's own covering — a tabernacle by day against the heat and a refuge from storm and rain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In that day seven women will take hold of one man, asking only to be called by his name to take away their reproach, while eating their own bread and wearing their own apparel. So many men will have been slain that the women, contrary to their natural bashfulness, will forego the legal privileges of wives to escape the reproach of being unwedded and childless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. In contrast to those on whom vengeance falls, the Branch of the Lord will be beautiful and glorious to the escaped of Israel. The Branch is Messiah, a fruit-bearing sprout from David's family when it was low and reduced, springing as from a decayed tree, and of the earth in his birth and death while also of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He who is left in Zion and remains in Jerusalem will be called holy, being written among the living in the book of life. Primarily this is the register of Israel's families; antitypically it is the book of life, and the reference is specially to the elect of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. When the Lord has washed away the moral filth of the daughters of Zion and purged the blood of Jerusalem, it will be by the spirit of judgment and of burning. Judgments purify by destroying the ungodly and refining the godly, and the same Spirit who sanctifies believers by affliction's fire dooms unbelievers to perdition's fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Lord will create upon every dwelling place of Mount Zion and upon her assemblies a cloud and smoke by day and the shining of a flaming fire by night, and upon all the glory a defence. As the pillar of cloud stood over the tabernacle as a symbol of God's favour, so his presence will ensure safety, as it did against the Egyptians at the Red Sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. There will be a tabernacle for a shadow from the heat by day and a place of refuge and covert from storm and rain. Christ's body is the tabernacle in which the Word dwelt, a shadow from the heat and refuge from the storm of divine wrath, and the true mercy seat beneath whom man is covered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
