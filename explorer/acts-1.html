The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The former treatise have I made, O Theophilus, of all that Jesus began both to do and teach,

2 Until the day in which he was taken up, after that he through the Holy Ghost had given commandments unto the apostles whom he had chosen:

3 To whom also he shewed himself alive after his passion by many infallible proofs, being seen of them forty days, and speaking of the things pertaining to the kingdom of God:

4 And, being assembled together with them, commanded them that they should not depart from Jerusalem, but wait for the promise of the Father, which, saith he, ye have heard of me.

5 For John truly baptized with water; but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost not many days hence.

6 When they therefore were come together, they asked of him, saying, Lord, wilt thou at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel?

7 And he said unto them, It is not for you to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power.

8 But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.

9 And when he had spoken these things, while they beheld, he was taken up; and a cloud received him out of their sight.

10 And while they looked stedfastly toward heaven as he went up, behold, two men stood by them in white apparel;

11 Which also said, Ye men of Galilee, why stand ye gazing up into heaven? this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven.

12 Then returned they unto Jerusalem from the mount called Olivet, which is from Jerusalem a sabbath day’s journey.

13 And when they were come in, they went up into an upper room, where abode both Peter, and James, and John, and Andrew, Philip, and Thomas, Bartholomew, and Matthew, James the son of Alphaeus, and Simon Zelotes, and Judas the brother of James.

14 These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren.

15 And in those days Peter stood up in the midst of the disciples, and said, (the number of names together were about an hundred and twenty,)

16 Men and brethren, this scripture must needs have been fulfilled, which the Holy Ghost by the mouth of David spake before concerning Judas, which was guide to them that took Jesus.

17 For he was numbered with us, and had obtained part of this ministry.

18 Now this man purchased a field with the reward of iniquity; and falling headlong, he burst asunder in the midst, and all his bowels gushed out.

19 And it was known unto all the dwellers at Jerusalem; insomuch as that field is called in their proper tongue, Aceldama, that is to say, The field of blood.

20 For it is written in the book of Psalms, Let his habitation be desolate, and let no man dwell therein: and his bishoprick let another take.

21 Wherefore of these men which have companied with us all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us,

22 Beginning from the baptism of John, unto that same day that he was taken up from us, must one be ordained to be a witness with us of his resurrection.

23 And they appointed two, Joseph called Barsabas, who was surnamed Justus, and Matthias.

24 And they prayed, and said, Thou, Lord, which knowest the hearts of all men, shew whether of these two thou hast chosen,

25 That he may take part of this ministry and apostleship, from which Judas by transgression fell, that he might go to his own place.

26 And they gave forth their lots; and the lot fell upon Matthias; and he was numbered with the eleven apostles.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After his resurrection, Jesus appears to the apostles over forty days, tells them to wait in Jerusalem for the promised Holy Spirit, and ascends into heaven with the promise that he will return in the same way. The disciples pray together in Jerusalem, Peter explains Judas's fall from the Scriptures, and Matthias is chosen by lot to take his place among the twelve. Interpreted Overview of this chapter's text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Luke's former treatise is his Gospel, and Acts continues the same work of Jesus. JFB divides Christ's work into two branches: what he did on earth in his own person, and what he continues from heaven by his Spirit through the apostles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Before he was taken up, the risen Lord gave commandments to the apostles he had chosen, and JFB notes these parting charges are said to be given through the Holy Ghost. This marks his new relation to the church after the resurrection, as the Spirit now overflows from him to his disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Jesus showed himself alive after his suffering by many infallible proofs over forty days, speaking of the kingdom of God. JFB stresses that the disciples were not predisposed to believe, but were overpowered by the evidence of their own senses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The disciples were ordered not to depart from Jerusalem but to wait for the promised Spirit. JFB explains that the Spirit was to glorify the existing order by descending at its metropolitan seat, so that the word of the Lord might go forth from Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. John baptized with water, but the disciples would soon be baptized with the Holy Ghost. JFB counts this as ten days hence, to Pentecost, expressed indefinitely in order to exercise their faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The disciples still asked whether the kingdom would now be restored to Israel. Henry warns against a desire for forbidden knowledge, while JFB notes their carnal views had been modified and they are neither rebuked nor contradicted on this point. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. It is not for the disciples to know the times or seasons the Father has kept in his own power. JFB reads this as implying not only that this was not the time, but that the question was irrelevant to their present business and future work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They would receive power when the Holy Ghost came upon them and be witnesses in Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria, and to the uttermost part of the earth. JFB calls this order the key to the plan of Acts: Jerusalem through chapter 9, then the wider world to chapter 28. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. While the disciples watched, Jesus was taken up, and a cloud received him out of their sight. JFB stresses the point of their watching: he did not merely disappear unnoticed, but was seen to ascend. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. As they looked steadfastly toward heaven, two men in white apparel stood by them. JFB identifies these as angels in human form, and reads their eager gazing as part of the sense-evidence on which their later testimony would rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The angels asked why the men of Galilee stood gazing up into heaven, and promised that this same Jesus would come again in like manner. Henry adds that thoughts of his second coming should quicken the trifling and comfort the trembling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The disciples returned to Jerusalem from the Mount of Olives, a sabbath day's journey away. JFB reckons that distance at about two thousand cubits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They went up into an upper room, which JFB takes as their place of rendezvous, perhaps the same large upper room of the last Passover and first Supper. There the eleven apostles are named together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. They continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, Mary the mother of Jesus, and his brethren. Henry notes that those in the best frame to receive spiritual blessings are in a praying frame, and JFB adds that this is the last mention of Mary in the New Testament, found here with the rest in prayer, not above them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. In those days of expectant prayer Peter stood up among about a hundred and twenty disciples. JFB notes Peter as the leading spirit of the infant community, and observes that many of the five hundred brethren who saw the risen Lord must have remained in Galilee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Judas fell headlong and burst asunder, an account JFB says supplements but by no means contradicts what Matthew records of his hanging. The two accounts together fill out the manner of the traitor's death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Peter cites the Psalms for letting another take Judas's charge, or bishopric. JFB sees a combination of Psalm 69:25 and Psalm 109:8, in which the apostle discerns a greater than David and a worse than Ahithophel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The replacement must be a man who companied with the apostles all the time the Lord Jesus went in and out among them. JFB glosses this as the close intimacies of a three years' public life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The witness must span from the baptism of John to the day Jesus was taken up, so that he can be ordained a witness of the resurrection. JFB states the apostles' primary office plainly: to testify from personal observation to the resurrection, and to show how it glorified Christ's whole previous life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Two men were put forward in nomination, Joseph called Barsabas and Matthias. JFB notes the nomination came from the whole company, of whom Peter was the spokesman, not from the eleven alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. They prayed to the Lord who knows the hearts of all men to show which of the two he had chosen. JFB observes that the word Lord used absolutely in the New Testament almost universally denotes the Son, making this the first example of prayer offered to the exalted Redeemer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The chosen man was to take part in the ministry and apostleship from which Judas fell, that he might go to his own place. JFB reads that last phrase as a softened expression of the traitor's awful future, implying both a destined habitation and a congenial element. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. They gave forth their lots, the lot fell upon Matthias, and he was numbered with the eleven apostles. JFB glosses numbered as voted in by general suffrage, completing the broken twelve, and Henry adds that the great thing the apostles were to attest was Christ's resurrection, the foundation of hope in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • Theophilus. The person Luke addresses at the opening of the book; the chapter gives no further identification of him. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • bishoprick. An old word for an office or charge of oversight; here, the apostleship Judas left vacant. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • sabbath day's journey. A short distance, the distance permitted for travel on the Sabbath; the note reckons it at about two thousand cubits. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Investigation (editorial — investigates, does not conclude)

This section investigates a parallel question; it does not settle it. The two accounts it compares are presented side by side, with their differences stated plainly, and the section ends without a conclusion.

The death of Judas: Acts 1 and Matthew's account

Acts 1:18 says Judas, having purchased a field with the reward of iniquity, fell headlong, burst asunder, and all his bowels gushed out. Jamieson-Fausset-Brown's study note for this verse says this account supplements but by no means contradicts what Matthew records of Judas's hanging, and that the two accounts together fill out the manner of the traitor's death. Interpreted Study note for verse 18, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Parallels: both accounts concern the same man, Judas, after his betrayal of Jesus, and both are connected in Acts with the field called Aceldama, the field of blood. Differences: Acts describes a headlong fall and bursting asunder, while Matthew's account, as the study note refers to it, records hanging; Acts names the field as purchased with the reward of iniquity. This page does not harmonize the details beyond what the study note itself claims.

Closing: whether and how the two accounts fit together beyond Jamieson-Fausset-Brown's reading is not established by the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved No source consulted for this page settles the harmonization beyond the study note
