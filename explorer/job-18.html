The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then answered Bildad the Shuhite, and said,

2 How long will it be ere ye make an end of words? mark, and afterwards we will speak.

3 Wherefore are we counted as beasts, and reputed vile in your sight?

4 He teareth himself in his anger: shall the earth be forsaken for thee? and shall the rock be removed out of his place?

5 Yea, the light of the wicked shall be put out, and the spark of his fire shall not shine.

6 The light shall be dark in his tabernacle, and his candle shall be put out with him.

7 The steps of his strength shall be straitened, and his own counsel shall cast him down.

8 For he is cast into a net by his own feet, and he walketh upon a snare.

9 The gin shall take him by the heel, and the robber shall prevail against him.

10 The snare is laid for him in the ground, and a trap for him in the way.

11 Terrors shall make him afraid on every side, and shall drive him to his feet.

12 His strength shall be hungerbitten, and destruction shall be ready at his side.

13 It shall devour the strength of his skin: even the firstborn of death shall devour his strength.

14 His confidence shall be rooted out of his tabernacle, and it shall bring him to the king of terrors.

15 It shall dwell in his tabernacle, because it is none of his: brimstone shall be scattered upon his habitation.

16 His roots shall be dried up beneath, and above shall his branch be cut off.

17 His remembrance shall perish from the earth, and he shall have no name in the street.

18 He shall be driven from light into darkness, and chased out of the world.

19 He shall neither have son nor nephew among his people, nor any remaining in his dwellings.

20 They that come after him shall be astonied at his day, as they that went before were affrighted.

21 Surely such are the dwellings of the wicked, and this is the place of him that knoweth not God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Bildad answers a second time with a catalogue of the wicked man’s doom: his light goes out in his tabernacle, snares and traps take him by the heel, terror chases him, and his memory perishes from the earth, leaving neither son nor nephew among his people. The speech is aimed at Job by implication — this, Bildad says, is the place of him who knows not God. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Bildad the Shuhite answers a second time, replying to Job's continued defence. The speech turns from doctrine to a catalogue of the wicked's doom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. How long will it be ere you make an end of words? Mark, and afterwards we will speak. JFB explains Bildad charges the other friends with empty speeches, opposed to mark, coming to reason and considering intelligently. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Why are we counted as beasts, and reputed vile in your sight? JFB explains beasts as alluding to Job's appeal to beasts in chapter 12, and vile as stubborn, answering the stupidity implied in the first clause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He tears himself in his anger: shall the earth be forsaken for him, and the rock removed out of its place? JFB notes Bildad turns to Job the self-tearer, arguing bitterly that unless Job is punished, the eternal order would be disturbed and the earth desolate through unavenged wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Yea, the light of the wicked shall be put out, and the spark of his fire shall not shine. JFB notes God's decree is unalterable, and the fire alludes to Arabian hospitality, welcoming strangers to the tent fire: the ungodly shall be deprived even of that, his dwelling dark and desolate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The light shall be dark in his tabernacle, and his candle shall be put out with him. JFB explains the candle as the Eastern ceiling lamp kept burning all night, whose extinction images utter desolation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The steps of his strength shall be straitened, and his own counsel shall cast him down. JFB explains strong steps as firm health, straitened steps as inability to move at will, and his counsel as plans becoming the means of his fall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For he is cast into a net by his own feet, and he walks upon a snare. JFB renders it as letting himself go into the net, or walking on a covered pitfall that gives way beneath him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The gin shall take him by the heel, and the robber shall prevail against him. JFB explains the robber as the noose holding him fast, answering to the gin in the parallel clause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The snare is laid for him in the ground, and a trap for him in the way. The net, gin, snare, and trap multiply one image: the wicked's own path is mined against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Terrors shall make him afraid on every side, and shall drive him to his feet. JFB notes the terrors of an evil conscience personified, pursuing him close at his heels like a conqueror scattering the enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. His strength shall be hunger-bitten, and destruction shall be ready at his side. JFB gives the brief, bold Hebrew: his strength is hungry, with destruction as a great calamity close at hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. It shall devour the strength of his skin, and the firstborn of death shall devour his strength. JFB explains strength as members or branches, and the firstborn of death as the chiefest disease death ever engendered, a personification full of poetical horror. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. His confidence shall be rooted out of his tabernacle, and it shall bring him to the king of terrors. JFB explains confidence as all a father trusted for domestic happiness, rooted out suddenly, and brought slowly and solemnly to Death personified, the king of terrors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. It shall dwell in his tabernacle because it is none of his, and brimstone shall be scattered upon his habitation. JFB explains terror shall haunt his house, now his no longer, with brimstone comparing Job's fire of God to Sodom's destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. His roots shall be dried up beneath, and above shall his branch be cut off. JFB explains roots as himself, and branch as his children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. His remembrance shall perish from the earth, and he shall have no name in the street. JFB renders street as field or meadow, where shepherds shall no more mention his name, a picture from nomadic life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He shall be driven from light into darkness, and chased out of the world. JFB explains light and darkness as existence and nonexistence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. He shall neither have son nor nephew among his people, nor any remaining in his dwellings. JFB notes nephew is translated grandson elsewhere, but here means kinsman: no kin remains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. They that come after him shall be astonished at his day, as they that went before were affrighted. JFB explains after and before as West and East, all peoples, seized with terror at his day of ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Surely such are the dwellings of the wicked, and this is the place of him that knows not God. The catalogue closes with its application: this is the end of the godless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
