The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 49 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Concerning the Ammonites, thus saith the LORD; Hath Israel no sons? hath he no heir? why then doth their king inherit Gad, and his people dwell in his cities?

2 Therefore, behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will cause an alarm of war to be heard in Rabbah of the Ammonites; and it shall be a desolate heap, and her daughters shall be burned with fire: then shall Israel be heir unto them that were his heirs, saith the LORD.

3 Howl, O Heshbon, for Ai is spoiled: cry, ye daughters of Rabbah, gird you with sackcloth; lament, and run to and fro by the hedges; for their king shall go into captivity, and his priests and his princes together.

4 Wherefore gloriest thou in the valleys, thy flowing valley, O backsliding daughter? that trusted in her treasures, saying, Who shall come unto me?

5 Behold, I will bring a fear upon thee, saith the Lord GOD of hosts, from all those that be about thee; and ye shall be driven out every man right forth; and none shall gather up him that wandereth.

6 And afterward I will bring again the captivity of the children of Ammon, saith the LORD.

7 Concerning Edom, thus saith the LORD of hosts; Is wisdom no more in Teman? is counsel perished from the prudent? is their wisdom vanished?

8 Flee ye, turn back, dwell deep, O inhabitants of Dedan; for I will bring the calamity of Esau upon him, the time that I will visit him.

9 If grapegatherers come to thee, would they not leave some gleaning grapes? if thieves by night, they will destroy till they have enough.

10 But I have made Esau bare, I have uncovered his secret places, and he shall not be able to hide himself: his seed is spoiled, and his brethren, and his neighbours, and he is not.

11 Leave thy fatherless children, I will preserve them alive; and let thy widows trust in me.

12 For thus saith the LORD; Behold, they whose judgment was not to drink of the cup have assuredly drunken; and art thou he that shall altogether go unpunished? thou shalt not go unpunished, but thou shalt surely drink of it.

13 For I have sworn by myself, saith the LORD, that Bozrah shall become a desolation, a reproach, a waste, and a curse; and all the cities thereof shall be perpetual wastes.

14 I have heard a rumour from the LORD, and an ambassador is sent unto the heathen, saying, Gather ye together, and come against her, and rise up to the battle.

15 For, lo, I will make thee small among the heathen, and despised among men.

16 Thy terribleness hath deceived thee, and the pride of thine heart, O thou that dwellest in the clefts of the rock, that holdest the height of the hill: though thou shouldest make thy nest as high as the eagle, I will bring thee down from thence, saith the LORD.

17 Also Edom shall be a desolation: every one that goeth by it shall be astonished, and shall hiss at all the plagues thereof.

18 As in the overthrow of Sodom and Gomorrah and the neighbour cities thereof, saith the LORD, no man shall abide there, neither shall a son of man dwell in it.

19 Behold, he shall come up like a lion from the swelling of Jordan against the habitation of the strong: but I will suddenly make him run away from her: and who is a chosen man, that I may appoint over her? for who is like me? and who will appoint me the time? and who is that shepherd that will stand before me?

20 Therefore hear the counsel of the LORD, that he hath taken against Edom; and his purposes, that he hath purposed against the inhabitants of Teman: Surely the least of the flock shall draw them out: surely he shall make their habitations desolate with them.

21 The earth is moved at the noise of their fall, at the cry the noise thereof was heard in the Red sea.

22 Behold, he shall come up and fly as the eagle, and spread his wings over Bozrah: and at that day shall the heart of the mighty men of Edom be as the heart of a woman in her pangs.

23 Concerning Damascus. Hamath is confounded, and Arpad: for they have heard evil tidings: they are fainthearted; there is sorrow on the sea; it cannot be quiet.

24 Damascus is waxed feeble, and turneth herself to flee, and fear hath seized on her: anguish and sorrows have taken her, as a woman in travail.

25 How is the city of praise not left, the city of my joy!

26 Therefore her young men shall fall in her streets, and all the men of war shall be cut off in that day, saith the LORD of hosts.

27 And I will kindle a fire in the wall of Damascus, and it shall consume the palaces of Benhadad.

28 Concerning Kedar, and concerning the kingdoms of Hazor, which Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon shall smite, thus saith the LORD; Arise ye, go up to Kedar, and spoil the men of the east.

29 Their tents and their flocks shall they take away: they shall take to themselves their curtains, and all their vessels, and their camels; and they shall cry unto them, Fear is on every side.

30 Flee, get you far off, dwell deep, O ye inhabitants of Hazor, saith the LORD; for Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon hath taken counsel against you, and hath conceived a purpose against you.

31 Arise, get you up unto the wealthy nation, that dwelleth without care, saith the LORD, which have neither gates nor bars, which dwell alone.

32 And their camels shall be a booty, and the multitude of their cattle a spoil: and I will scatter into all winds them that are in the utmost corners; and I will bring their calamity from all sides thereof, saith the LORD.

33 And Hazor shall be a dwelling for dragons, and a desolation for ever: there shall no man abide there, nor any son of man dwell in it.

34 The word of the LORD that came to Jeremiah the prophet against Elam in the beginning of the reign of Zedekiah king of Judah, saying,

35 Thus saith the LORD of hosts; Behold, I will break the bow of Elam, the chief of their might.

36 And upon Elam will I bring the four winds from the four quarters of heaven, and will scatter them toward all those winds; and there shall be no nation whither the outcasts of Elam shall not come.

37 For I will cause Elam to be dismayed before their enemies, and before them that seek their life: and I will bring evil upon them, even my fierce anger, saith the LORD; and I will send the sword after them, till I have consumed them:

38 And I will set my throne in Elam, and will destroy from thence the king and the princes, saith the LORD.

39 But it shall come to pass in the latter days, that I will bring again the captivity of Elam, saith the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gathers oracles against Ammon, Edom, Damascus, the Arab tribes of Kedar and Hazor, and finally Elam, each charged with seizing what was not theirs, trusting in treasures and valleys, or breaking the bow that was their strength. Ammon and Elam both close with a latter-days return, while Damascus and Edom are left in desolation and terror. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry brings God's action against Ammon for illegally encroaching on Gad's territory after the Assyrian deportation left it almost unguarded. He notes the Ammonites even murdered unborn heirs to bar future claims (Amos 1:13), yet might among men, however often it prevails against right, is controlled by the Almighty who judges aright. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry reads the alarm of war in Rabbah, the strong capital, and the burning of her daughter cities as the judgment for that violence. He adds the reversal at the verse's end, that Israel shall be heir to those who were his heirs, as the equity of divine providence recompensing the injured out of the unjust gains of the injurious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry pictures the flight and sackcloth lament as the people lose all they had and know not where to betake themselves. The king, his priests and his princes going into captivity completes the evacuation of the land, from the least to the greatest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry calls Ammon a backsliding daughter, since all idolaters turn away from the worship of the true God that came first, and charges her with glorying in her flowing valley and treasures, saying, Who shall come unto me? Those who turn from God, he notes, have little reason to take complacency or confidence in any worldly enjoyment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry notes that God, who has all armies at his command, brings fear upon Ammon from all around, so that each man is driven straight out and none gathers up the wanderer. God has many ways, he observes, to terrify those who have been a terror to his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry closes the Ammonite oracle with a prospect of mercy hereafter, as before to Moab: the captivity of Ammon shall be brought again. In human affairs, he remarks, the wheel goes round. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry observes that Edom, once famous for wisdom with statesmen thought to excel in politics, shall take such wrong measures that onlookers ask in wonder whether wisdom is gone from Teman. When God designs a people's ruin, he notes, whom he will destroy he infatuates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry reads the call to flee, turn back and dwell deep as the onset of the calamity of Esau, long deserved for old sins and now at hand as the time of God's visitation. The Edomites must hide in close caverns from the sword that is coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry contrasts ordinary spoilers with Edom's destroyers: grape-gatherers leave some gleanings, and even thieves know when they have enough and destroy no further. Those who destroy Edom, he says, shall never be satiated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry says Esau shall be made quite bare, his most hidden treasures discovered and rifled, so that he cannot hide himself and his seed is spoiled. His brethren the Moabites and his neighbours the Philistines are spoiled as well, and so disabled from giving him succour or shelter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry notes the desolation leaves no relation, friend, or parish officer to care for the widows and fatherless left behind. He also records the Chaldee reading, which takes these words as God's promise to his own people, distinguishing Israel from Edom: whatever becomes of Edom's orphans, God will preserve Israel's and let her widows rest on his word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry argues from the lesser to the greater: even those whose doom was not to drink of the cup, including Israel itself, have been made to drink the bitter cup, so Edom shall surely drink it and not go unpunished. When judgment begins at God's house, he notes, it will reach the strangers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry stresses that God has sworn by himself that Bozrah shall become a desolation, a reproach, a waste and a curse, with all its cities perpetual wastes. The decree has gone forth and cannot be resisted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry reads the rumour from the LORD and the ambassador sent to the nations as God's own summons into this service: the nations are mustered and told to gather and come against Edom. The matter is settled, for this is the counsel God has taken against her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry notes the reversal of Edom's standing: a people that made a mighty figure shall be made small among the heathen and despised among men. Those who despised God's people, he observes, shall themselves be despised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry says Edom's terribleness deceived her: feared by all her neighbours and secure in mountain passes she thought unassailable, she grew proud and conceited of her strength. High as she builds her nest, God will bring her down, for there is no wisdom and no might against the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry says Edom shall be made such a desolation that everyone going by shall be astonished, and even made a prodigy and a terror. Her fall becomes a spectacle to the nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry compares Edom's ruin to the overthrow of Sodom and Gomorrah, so frightful a place that no man shall abide there or dwell in it. The land is left uninhabitable, not merely conquered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 19. Henry identifies the lion coming up from the swelling of Jordan as Nebuchadnezzar, roaring to devour all in his way and coming suddenly upon the strong habitation. God's questions, who is like me and who can stand before me, show that when God has work to do he finds the fittest instruments, and none can engage against it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 20. Henry treats this verse as the settled counsel of the LORD against Edom and Teman: the decree has gone forth and there is no resisting it. Even the least of the flock, the meanest servant in Nebuchadnezzar's retinue, shall draw them out for slaughter, for God can bring about the greatest works by instruments least likely. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 21. Henry says the noise of Edom's fall shall move the earth and put all nations into concern, with the outcry heard as far as the Red Sea, where ships take in lading and carry the news to the remotest shore. The fall of those who affected to make a noise with pomp and power makes so much the greater noise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 22. Henry pictures Nebuchadnezzar coming not only like a lion but like an eagle, the king of birds, flying swiftly and strongly to clap his wings upon Bozrah. At once the hearts of Edom's mighty men fail them, like a woman in her pangs, seeing an enemy it is vain to struggle with. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 23. Henry says Syria's judgment begins with terrible fright: Hamath and Arpad hear evil tidings of Babylon's approach, are confounded, and lie like the troubled sea that cannot be quiet. See how easily God can dispirit nations most celebrated for valour, he remarks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 24. Henry says Damascus, a city that thought she could face the most formidable enemy, now waxes feeble, turns to flee, and owns it is as vain to contend with her fate as for a woman in labour to contend with her pains. Fear seizes her outright, and anguish takes her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 25. Henry explains the city of praise as a city commended by strangers, and the city of joy as one where there was an affluence of all the delights of the sons of men. Those who place their happiness in carnal joys deceive themselves, he notes, for God can soon make a city of praise a reproach and a city of joy a terror to itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 26. Henry says Damascus's fall ends in slaughter: the young men who should defend the city shall fall by the sword in her streets, and all the men of war, expert and engaged in their country's service, shall be cut off in that day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 27. Henry says the fire kindled in the wall of Damascus by the besiegers shall devour all before it, the palaces of Ben-hadad particularly. Those palaces are marked because so much mischief was formerly hatched there against God's Israel, for which it is now thus visited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 28. Henry says this oracle foretells the desolation Nebuchadnezzar shall make among Kedar, descended from Ishmael's son, and the petty kingdoms of Hazor joined to them. Babylon acts to gratify its own covetousness and ambition, he notes, but God orders it for correcting an unthankful people and warning a careless world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 29. Henry observes that the Chaldeans shall take the Kedarenes' tents, flocks, curtains, vessels and camels, crying, Fear is on every side. The enemy need not strike a stroke, he says: terror alone shall shout them out of their tents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 30. Henry remarks it is strange that so great a prince should stoop to such small game, yet all is fish that comes to the ambitious covetous man's net. The command to flee far off and dwell deep answers Nebuchadnezzar's counsel and purpose, and shows that not having given offence is no defence against such a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 31. Henry describes Kedar's people as shepherds dwelling in tents with neither gates nor bars, no fortified cities, no soldiers and no merchants, living within themselves. They are called a wealthy nation, he notes, because they had sufficiency for life's occasions and were content with it, and those who live innocently may live securely though they have neither gates nor bars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 32. Henry says the Kedarenes' camels shall be booty and their cattle a spoil, and they themselves scattered into all winds from the utmost corners, with calamity brought from all sides. No marvel there are fears on every side, he remarks, when there are foes on every side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 33. Henry says Hazor shall lie uninhabited forever, a dwelling for dragons, because it lies remote and out of all high roads, with neither cities nor lands inviting to strangers. Busy men's places are striven for when they fall, he notes, but no man cared to abide where these easy, quiet men had lived meanly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 34. Henry dates this prophecy to the beginning of Zedekiah's reign and thinks the other prophecies against the Gentiles going before were probably at the same time. The Elamites, he says, were the Persians, descended from Elam the son of Shem, or at least that part of Persia nearest the Jews called Elymais. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 35. Henry says the Elamites were famous archers, and breaking the bow of Elam ruins their artillery, so that the chief of their might is gone. God often orders it, he notes, so that what we most trust to first fails us, and what was the chief of our might proves the least of our help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 36. Henry pictures the four winds bringing enemies from all parts of the world, carrying some Elamites captive and driving others to flee in every direction, so that no nation lacks Elam's outcasts. We know not from what point the wind of trouble may blow, he remarks, but if God encompasses us with his favour we are safe whichever way the storm comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 37. Henry says Elam shall be dismayed before her enemies, and the sword shall be sent after her till she is consumed, for God's fierce anger has all evil in it. God can make his judgments follow those who think by flight to escape them, he notes, for evil pursues sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 38. Henry reads setting God's throne in Elam as the throne of Nebuchadnezzar or of Cyrus, who began his conquests with Elymais, or else the throne on which God sits for judgment. Either way, the king and princes of Elam are destroyed, for kings are accountable to God, who is above the highest of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 39. Henry closes with the limit on Elam's destruction: it shall not be perpetual, for in the latter days God will bring again the captivity of Elam. As with Ammon and Moab, judgment gives way at last to a promised return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 31. Henry describes Kedar's people as shepherds dwelling in tents with neither gates nor bars, no fortified cities, no soldiers and no merchants, living within themselves. They are called a wealthy nation, he notes, because they had sufficiency for life's occasions and were content with it, and those who live innocently may live securely though they have neither gates nor bars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 32. Henry says the Kedarenes' camels shall be booty and their cattle a spoil, and they themselves scattered into all winds from the utmost corners, with calamity brought from all sides. No marvel there are fears on every side, he remarks, when there are foes on every side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 33. Henry says Hazor shall lie uninhabited forever, a dwelling for dragons, because it lies remote and out of all high roads, with neither cities nor lands inviting to strangers. Busy men's places are striven for when they fall, he notes, but no man cared to abide where these easy, quiet men had lived meanly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 34. Henry dates this prophecy to the beginning of Zedekiah's reign and thinks the other prophecies against the Gentiles going before were probably at the same time. The Elamites, he says, were the Persians, descended from Elam the son of Shem, or at least that part of Persia nearest the Jews called Elymais. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 35. Henry says the Elamites were famous archers, and breaking the bow of Elam ruins their artillery, so that the chief of their might is gone. God often orders it, he notes, so that what we most trust to first fails us, and what was the chief of our might proves the least of our help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 36. Henry pictures the four winds bringing enemies from all parts of the world, carrying some Elamites captive and driving others to flee in every direction, so that no nation lacks Elam's outcasts. We know not from what point the wind of trouble may blow, he remarks, but if God encompasses us with his favour we are safe whichever way the storm comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 37. Henry says Elam shall be dismayed before her enemies, and the sword shall be sent after her till she is consumed, for God's fierce anger has all evil in it. God can make his judgments follow those who think by flight to escape them, he notes, for evil pursues sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 38. Henry reads setting God's throne in Elam as the throne of Nebuchadnezzar or of Cyrus, who began his conquests with Elymais, or else the throne on which God sits for judgment. Either way, the king and princes of Elam are destroyed, for kings are accountable to God, who is above the highest of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 39. Henry closes with the limit on Elam's destruction: it shall not be perpetual, for in the latter days God will bring again the captivity of Elam. As with Ammon and Moab, judgment gives way at last to a promised return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
