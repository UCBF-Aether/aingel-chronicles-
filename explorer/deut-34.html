The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Deuteronomy 34 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Moses went up from the plains of Moab unto the mountain of Nebo, to the top of Pisgah, that is over against Jericho. And the LORD shewed him all the land of Gilead, unto Dan,

2 And all Naphtali, and the land of Ephraim, and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah, unto the utmost sea,

3 And the south, and the plain of the valley of Jericho, the city of palm trees, unto Zoar.

4 And the LORD said unto him, This is the land which I sware unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, saying, I will give it unto thy seed: I have caused thee to see it with thine eyes, but thou shalt not go over thither.

5 So Moses the servant of the LORD died there in the land of Moab, according to the word of the LORD.

6 And he buried him in a valley in the land of Moab, over against Bethpeor: but no man knoweth of his sepulchre unto this day.

7 And Moses was an hundred and twenty years old when he died: his eye was not dim, nor his natural force abated.

8 And the children of Israel wept for Moses in the plains of Moab thirty days: so the days of weeping and mourning for Moses were ended.

9 And Joshua the son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom; for Moses had laid his hands upon him: and the children of Israel hearkened unto him, and did as the LORD commanded Moses.

10 And there arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face,

11 In all the signs and the wonders, which the LORD sent him to do in the land of Egypt to Pharaoh, and to all his servants, and to all his land,

12 And in all that mighty hand, and in all the great terror which Moses shewed in the sight of all Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Moses climbs Nebo to Pisgah and is shown the whole land, from Gilead to Dan, from Judah to the western sea — the land promised to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob — and there he dies, in Moab, at a hundred and twenty, his eye undimmed. No one knows his burial place. Joshua, filled with the spirit of wisdom, takes up the leadership, and the book closes by saying no prophet has since arisen like Moses, whom the Lord knew face to face. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And Moses went up from the plains of Moab to the mountain of Nebo, to the top of Pisgah, over against Jericho, and the Lord showed him all the land of Gilead, to Dan; this chapter appears from internal evidence to have been written after Moses' death, and probably once formed an introduction to Joshua, the Pisgah summit being the head of the Abarim range east of Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And all Naphtali, and the land of Ephraim, and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah, to the utmost sea; westward lay Naphtali's distant hills, nearer Ephraim and Manasseh, opposite all Judah, and beyond it the Mediterranean, the four great marks of the future inheritance, seen in that subtle, vapour-free air without any miraculous power of vision being imparted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And the south, and the plain of the valley of Jericho, the city of palm trees, to Zoar; immediately below him lay the circle of Jericho's oasis, and far to his left Zoar, the last inhabited spot before the great desert, while the deep valley of Jordan intervened between him and the good land he was not to enter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. And the Lord said to him, This is the land which I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, saying, I will give it to your seed; I have caused you to see it with your eyes, but you shall not go over there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. So Moses the servant of the Lord died there in the land of Moab, according to the word of the Lord, after having governed the Israelites forty years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And he buried him in a valley in the land of Moab, over against Beth-peor, but no man knows of his sepulchre to this day; this concealment seems a special and wise arrangement of Providence, to prevent the grave being ranked among holy places and made a resort of superstitious pilgrims or idolatrous veneration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And Moses was a hundred and twenty years old when he died; his eye was not dim, nor his natural force abated, his powers having suffered no decay even at the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. And the children of Israel wept for Moses in the plains of Moab thirty days; so the days of weeping and mourning for Moses were ended; seven days was the usual mourning period, but for persons of high rank it was extended to thirty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And Joshua son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom, for Moses had laid his hands upon him, and the children of Israel hearkened to him, and did as the Lord commanded Moses; Joshua was appointed to a peculiar office, not as prophet or civil ruler in Moses' place, but as general to head the invasion and the allocation of the tribes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. And there arose not a prophet since in Israel like to Moses, whom the Lord knew face to face; in whatever light we view this extraordinary man, the eulogy is just, for no Hebrew prophet or ruler equalled him in character, official dignity, or knowledge of God's will and opportunities of announcing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. In all the signs and wonders, which the Lord sent him to do in the land of Egypt to Pharaoh, and to all his servants, and to all his land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And in all that mighty hand, and in all the great terror which Moses showed in the sight of all Israel, closing both the book and the life with the signs, wonders, and mighty acts that marked him out above all other prophets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
