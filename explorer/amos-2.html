The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Amos 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Thus saith the LORD; For three transgressions of Moab, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof; because he burned the bones of the king of Edom into lime:

2 But I will send a fire upon Moab, and it shall devour the palaces of Kirioth: and Moab shall die with tumult, with shouting, and with the sound of the trumpet:

3 And I will cut off the judge from the midst thereof, and will slay all the princes thereof with him, saith the LORD.

4 Thus saith the LORD; For three transgressions of Judah, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof; because they have despised the law of the LORD, and have not kept his commandments, and their lies caused them to err, after the which their fathers have walked:

5 But I will send a fire upon Judah, and it shall devour the palaces of Jerusalem.

6 Thus saith the LORD; For three transgressions of Israel, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof; because they sold the righteous for silver, and the poor for a pair of shoes;

7 That pant after the dust of the earth on the head of the poor, and turn aside the way of the meek: and a man and his father will go in unto the same maid, to profane my holy name:

8 And they lay themselves down upon clothes laid to pledge by every altar, and they drink the wine of the condemned in the house of their god.

9 Yet destroyed I the Amorite before them, whose height was like the height of the cedars, and he was strong as the oaks; yet I destroyed his fruit from above, and his roots from beneath.

10 Also I brought you up from the land of Egypt, and led you forty years through the wilderness, to possess the land of the Amorite.

11 And I raised up of your sons for prophets, and of your young men for Nazarites. Is it not even thus, O ye children of Israel? saith the LORD.

12 But ye gave the Nazarites wine to drink; and commanded the prophets, saying, Prophesy not.

13 Behold, I am pressed under you, as a cart is pressed that is full of sheaves.

14 Therefore the flight shall perish from the swift, and the strong shall not strengthen his force, neither shall the mighty deliver himself:

15 Neither shall he stand that handleth the bow; and he that is swift of foot shall not deliver himself: neither shall he that rideth the horse deliver himself.

16 And he that is courageous among the mighty shall flee away naked in that day, saith the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The same formula falls on Moab for burning the king of Edom's bones into lime, then on Judah for despising the law of the LORD and following lies, and then at length on Israel itself, for selling the righteous for silver and the poor for a pair of shoes, and for turning aside the way of the meek. The chapter that began with foreign nations ends at home. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Moab, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because he burned Edom's king's bones into lime, cruelty extending even to the dead in hatred and contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. God will send fire upon Moab, devouring Kerioth's palaces, and Moab shall die with tumult, shouting, and trumpet sound, cruelly dealt with because it dealt cruelly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He will cut off the judge from Moab's midst and slay all its princes with him, says the Lord, government itself removed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Judah, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because they despised the Lord's law, kept not his commandments, and their lies, their idols, caused them to err after their fathers' way, other nations being reckoned with for injuries to men, Judah for dishonor done to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. God will send fire upon Judah, devouring Jerusalem's palaces, the holy city not spared for its privileges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Israel, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because they sold the righteous for silver and the poor for a pair of shoes, justice sold for the smallest bribe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They pant after dust on the poor's head and turn aside the way of the meek, while father and son go in to the same maid to profane God's holy name, oppression joined to shameless lust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. They lie down on clothes taken in pledge beside every altar and drink the wine of the condemned in their god's house, worship itself turned into a cover for extortion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Yet God destroyed the Amorite before them, tall as cedars and strong as oaks, destroying fruit above and roots beneath, the gift of the land showing God's power on their behalf. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Also God brought them up from Egypt and led them forty years through the wilderness to possess the Amorite's land, mercies received adding much to the evil of sins committed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. God raised up their sons for prophets and young men for Nazarites, is it not so, O children of Israel, faithful ministers being great blessings and God himself raising them up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. But they gave Nazarites wine to drink and commanded prophets, saying, prophesy not, doing what they could to lead believers aside and silence God's messengers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Behold, God is pressed under them as a cart pressed full of sheaves, the Lord complaining of his professing people's sins as a burden to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Therefore flight shall perish from the swift, the strong shall not strengthen his force, and the mighty shall not deliver himself, boasted power and resources failing when a people rejects God's word with obstinacy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Neither shall the bowman stand, nor the swift of foot deliver himself, nor the horseman deliver himself, every military advantage proving useless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And the courageous among the mighty shall flee away naked in that day, says the Lord, so that sinners should humble themselves for ingratitude and unfaithfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Three transgressions, and for four. not a literal count: the study note for Amos 1 explains the number as meaning many transgressions, the measure of sins filled and ripe for vengeance. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
