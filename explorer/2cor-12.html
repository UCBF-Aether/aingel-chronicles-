The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Corinthians 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 It is not expedient for me doubtless to glory. I will come to visions and revelations of the Lord.

2 I knew a man in Christ above fourteen years ago, (whether in the body, I cannot tell; or whether out of the body, I cannot tell: God knoweth;) such an one caught up to the third heaven.

3 And I knew such a man, (whether in the body, or out of the body, I cannot tell: God knoweth;)

4 How that he was caught up into paradise, and heard unspeakable words, which it is not lawful for a man to utter.

5 Of such an one will I glory: yet of myself I will not glory, but in mine infirmities.

6 For though I would desire to glory, I shall not be a fool; for I will say the truth: but now I forbear, lest any man should think of me above that which he seeth me to be, or that he heareth of me.

7 And lest I should be exalted above measure through the abundance of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in the flesh, the messenger of Satan to buffet me, lest I should be exalted above measure.

8 For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me.

9 And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.

10 Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ’s sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong.

11 I am become a fool in glorying; ye have compelled me: for I ought to have been commended of you: for in nothing am I behind the very chiefest apostles, though I be nothing.

12 Truly the signs of an apostle were wrought among you in all patience, in signs, and wonders, and mighty deeds.

13 For what is it wherein ye were inferior to other churches, except it be that I myself was not burdensome to you? forgive me this wrong.

14 Behold, the third time I am ready to come to you; and I will not be burdensome to you: for I seek not yours but you: for the children ought not to lay up for the parents, but the parents for the children.

15 And I will very gladly spend and be spent for you; though the more abundantly I love you, the less I be loved.

16 But be it so, I did not burden you: nevertheless, being crafty, I caught you with guile.

17 Did I make a gain of you by any of them whom I sent unto you?

18 I desired Titus, and with him I sent a brother. Did Titus make a gain of you? walked we not in the same spirit? walked we not in the same steps?

19 Again, think ye that we excuse ourselves unto you? we speak before God in Christ: but we do all things, dearly beloved, for your edifying.

20 For I fear, lest, when I come, I shall not find you such as I would, and that I shall be found unto you such as ye would not: lest there be debates, envyings, wraths, strifes, backbitings, whisperings, swellings, tumults:

21 And lest, when I come again, my God will humble me among you, and that I shall bewail many which have sinned already, and have not repented of the uncleanness and fornication and lasciviousness which they have committed.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul reluctantly turns to visions and revelations, telling of a man caught up to the third heaven fourteen years before, then of his thorn in the flesh, a messenger of Satan to buffet him, lest he be exalted above measure. The Lord's answer, 'My grace is sufficient for thee,' teaches Paul to glory in infirmities, and he closes fearing that on his third visit he will find strife, envy, and impurity among the Corinthians and be humbled among them. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. It is not expedient for me doubtless to glory. I will come to visions and revelations of the Lord. JFB notes glorying is not expedient, yet Paul will come to visions and revelations, because the rivals force him to speak of what he would rather hide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. I knew a man in Christ above fourteen years ago, (whether in the body, I cannot tell; or whether out of the body, I cannot tell: God knows;) such an one caught up to the third heaven. Henry notes the man is Paul himself, spoken of in the third person, caught up fourteen years before to the third heaven, and even he cannot tell whether in body or out of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And I knew such a man, (whether in the body, or out of the body, I cannot tell: God knows;). JFB notes the repetition stresses the ignorance: twice Paul confesses he does not know the manner, and leaves it to God who knows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. How that he was caught up into paradise, and heard unspeakable words, which it is not lawful for a man to utter. Henry notes paradise is named alongside the third heaven, and the words heard there are unspeakable and unlawful to utter, so the vision gives Paul nothing to boast with in detail. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Of such an one will I glory: yet of myself I will not glory, but in mine infirmities. JFB notes the distinction: Paul will glory of the man caught up, but of himself, his present self, he will glory only in infirmities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For though I would desire to glory, I shall not be a fool; for I will say the truth: but now I forbear, lest any man should think of me above that which he sees me to be, or that he hears of me. Henry notes Paul forbears for the hearers' sake: he will not let men think him above what they see and hear of him, though the truth would allow more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And lest I should be exalted above measure through the abundance of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in the flesh, the messenger of Satan to buffet me, lest I should be exalted above measure. JFB notes the thorn is given against exaltation: a messenger of Satan to buffet, permitted by God, to keep the man of revelations low. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me. Henry notes the prayer is thrice repeated, like the Lord's own prayer in Gethsemane, and its answer is not removal but sufficiency. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me. JFB notes the answer turns weakness into the place of strength: grace sufficient, strength perfected in weakness, so Paul glories gladly in infirmities that Christ's power may rest on him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong. Henry notes pleasure in such a list is the paradox proved: when weak, then strong, because Christ's strength fills the weakness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. I am become a fool in glorying; ye have compelled me: for I ought to have been commended of you: for in nothing am I behind the very chiefest apostles, though I be nothing. JFB notes the compulsion is Corinth's: they ought to have commended Paul, and their silence compelled his foolish glorying, in which he is nothing, yet behind no apostle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Truly the signs of an apostle were wrought among you in all patience, in signs, and wonders, and mighty deeds. Henry notes the signs are the apostle's true credentials, wrought among Corinth itself in patience, signs, wonders and mighty deeds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. For what is it wherein ye were inferior to other churches, except it be that I myself was not burdensome to you? forgive me this wrong. JFB notes the irony returns: Corinth's only inferiority is that Paul was not burdensome, and he asks forgiveness for that 'wrong.' Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Behold, the third time I am ready to come to you; and I will not be burdensome to you: for I seek not yours but you: for the children ought not to lay up for the parents, but the parents for the children. Henry notes the third coming is announced with the same policy: not burdensome, seeking not theirs but them, as parents lay up for children, not children for parents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And I will very gladly spend and be spent for you; though the more abundantly I love you, the less I be loved. JFB notes the spending is total: spend and be spent, gladly, even though greater love is met with less love in return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. But be it so, I did not burden you: nevertheless, being crafty, I caught you with guile. Henry notes Paul quotes the slander: granted he did not burden, yet, say the critics, being crafty he caught them with guile, through Titus and the collection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Did I make a gain of you by any of them whom I sent unto you? JFB notes the question invites audit: name any emissary, and ask whether Paul gained by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Henry notes Titus is the test case: if he made no gain and walked in the same spirit and steps as Paul, then the guile charge against Paul falls with it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Again, think ye that we excuse ourselves unto you? we speak before God in Christ: but we do all things, dearly beloved, for your edifying. JFB notes the defence is not an excuse: spoken before God in Christ, and done for Corinth's edifying, not for Paul's reputation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. For I fear, lest, when I come, I shall not find you such as I would, and that I shall be found unto you such as ye would not: lest there be debates, envyings, wraths, strifes, backbitings, whisperings, swellings, tumults. Henry notes the fear is mutual disappointment: Paul may find Corinth unlike his hope, and Corinth may find Paul unlike its wish, with eight sins of discord named as the danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. And lest, when I come again, my God will humble me among you, and that I shall bewail many which have sinned already, and have not repented of the uncleanness and fornication and lasciviousness which they have committed. JFB notes the humbling is pastoral: Paul would be humbled to bewail unrepented sinners, whose uncleanness, fornication and lasciviousness remain unrepented. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.
