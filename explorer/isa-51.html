The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 51 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Hearken to me, ye that follow after righteousness, ye that seek the LORD: look unto the rock whence ye are hewn, and to the hole of the pit whence ye are digged.

2 Look unto Abraham your father, and unto Sarah that bare you: for I called him alone, and blessed him, and increased him.

3 For the LORD shall comfort Zion: he will comfort all her waste places; and he will make her wilderness like Eden, and her desert like the garden of the LORD; joy and gladness shall be found therein, thanksgiving, and the voice of melody.

4 Hearken unto me, my people; and give ear unto me, O my nation: for a law shall proceed from me, and I will make my judgment to rest for a light of the people.

5 My righteousness is near; my salvation is gone forth, and mine arms shall judge the people; the isles shall wait upon me, and on mine arm shall they trust.

6 Lift up your eyes to the heavens, and look upon the earth beneath: for the heavens shall vanish away like smoke, and the earth shall wax old like a garment, and they that dwell therein shall die in like manner: but my salvation shall be for ever, and my righteousness shall not be abolished.

7 Hearken unto me, ye that know righteousness, the people in whose heart is my law; fear ye not the reproach of men, neither be ye afraid of their revilings.

8 For the moth shall eat them up like a garment, and the worm shall eat them like wool: but my righteousness shall be for ever, and my salvation from generation to generation.

9 Awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of the LORD; awake, as in the ancient days, in the generations of old. Art thou not it that hath cut Rahab, and wounded the dragon?

10 Art thou not it which hath dried the sea, the waters of the great deep; that hath made the depths of the sea a way for the ransomed to pass over?

11 Therefore the redeemed of the LORD shall return, and come with singing unto Zion; and everlasting joy shall be upon their head: they shall obtain gladness and joy; and sorrow and mourning shall flee away.

12 I, even I, am he that comforteth you: who art thou, that thou shouldest be afraid of a man that shall die, and of the son of man which shall be made as grass;

13 And forgettest the LORD thy maker, that hath stretched forth the heavens, and laid the foundations of the earth; and hast feared continually every day because of the fury of the oppressor, as if he were ready to destroy? and where is the fury of the oppressor?

14 The captive exile hasteneth that he may be loosed, and that he should not die in the pit, nor that his bread should fail.

15 But I am the LORD thy God, that divided the sea, whose waves roared: The LORD of hosts is his name.

16 And I have put my words in thy mouth, and I have covered thee in the shadow of mine hand, that I may plant the heavens, and lay the foundations of the earth, and say unto Zion, Thou art my people.

17 Awake, awake, stand up, O Jerusalem, which hast drunk at the hand of the LORD the cup of his fury; thou hast drunken the dregs of the cup of trembling, and wrung them out.

18 There is none to guide her among all the sons whom she hath brought forth; neither is there any that taketh her by the hand of all the sons that she hath brought up.

19 These two things are come unto thee; who shall be sorry for thee? desolation, and destruction, and the famine, and the sword: by whom shall I comfort thee?

20 Thy sons have fainted, they lie at the head of all the streets, as a wild bull in a net: they are full of the fury of the LORD, the rebuke of thy God.

21 Therefore hear now this, thou afflicted, and drunken, but not with wine:

22 Thus saith thy Lord the LORD, and thy God that pleadeth the cause of his people, Behold, I have taken out of thine hand the cup of trembling, even the dregs of the cup of my fury; thou shalt no more drink it again:

23 But I will put it into the hand of them that afflict thee; which have said to thy soul, Bow down, that we may go over: and thou hast laid thy body as the ground, and as the street, to them that went over.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Look to the rock from which you were hewn — to Abraham and Sarah, one called alone and blessed. The faithful remnant is encouraged for deliverance from exile and dispersion, the heavens and earth will pass away but God's salvation endures, and the cup of trembling is taken from Jerusalem's hand and put into the hand of her afflicters. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hearken to me, you that follow after righteousness, you that seek the Lord; look unto the rock whence you are hewn, and to the hole of the pit whence you are digged. This chapter is encouragement to the faithful remnant of Israel to trust in God for deliverance, both from their long Babylonian exile, and from their present dispersion. Me means the God of your fathers. Ye follow after righteousness means the godly portion of the nation, as in verse 7; ye follow righteousness, seek it therefore from Me, who bring it near, and that a righteousness not about to be abolished, as in verses 6 and 7; look to Abraham, your father in verse 2, as a sample of how righteousness before Me is to be obtained; I, the same God who blessed him, will bless you at last in verse 3; therefore trust in Me, and fear not man's opposition in verses 7, 8, 12, and 13. The mistake of the Jews, heretofore, has been, not in that they followed after righteousness, but in that they followed it by the works of the law, instead of by faith, as Abraham did. Hole of the pit means the idea is not, as it is often quoted, the inculcation of humility, by reminding men of the fallen state from which they have been taken, but that as Abraham, the quarry, as it were, as in chapter 48 verse 1, whence their nation was hewn, had been called out of a strange land to the inheritance of Canaan, and blessed by God, the same God is able to deliver and restore them also, as in Matthew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Look unto Abraham your father, and unto Sarah that bare you, for I called him alone, and blessed him, and increased him. Alone means translate, I called him when he was but one, as in Ezekiel. The argument is: the same God who had so blessed one individual, as to become a mighty nation, as in Genesis, can also increase and bless the small remnant of Israel, both that left in the Babylonish captivity, and that left in the present and latter days, as in Zechariah; the residue, as in chapter 13. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For the Lord will comfort Zion; he will comfort all her waste places, and he will make her wilderness like Eden, and her desert like the garden of the Lord; joy and gladness will be found therein, thanksgiving, and the voice of melody. For means see for the argument, verse 2. The garden of the Lord means restoration of the primeval paradise, as in Genesis, Ezekiel, and Revelation. Melody is Hebrew, psalm; God's praises shall again be heard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Hearken unto me, my people, and give ear unto me, O my nation, for a law will proceed from me, and I will make my judgment to rest for a light of the people. Nations means namely, the Gentiles. The Jews are called on to hear and rejoice in the extension of the true religion to the nations; for, at the first preaching of the Gospel, as in the final age to come, it was from Jerusalem that the gospel law was, and is, to go forth, as in chapter 2. Law and judgment mean the gospel dispensation and institutions, as in chapter 42, judgment. Make to rest means establish firmly, found. Light, etc., recalls chapter 42 verse 6. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. My righteousness is near; my salvation is gone forth, and my arms will judge the people; the isles will wait upon me, and on my arm will they trust. Near means that is, faithful fulfilment of the promised deliverance, answering to salvation in the parallel clause, as in chapters 46 and 56 and Romans. Ye follow after righteousness; seek it therefore, from Me, and you will not have far to go for it, as in verse 1. Arms means put for Himself; I by My might. Judge recalls chapters 2 and Psalm 98. Isles, etc., recalls chapter 60. Arm recalls Romans, the power of God unto, the Gentiles as well as the Jews, salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Lift up your eyes to the heavens, and look upon the earth beneath, for the heavens will vanish away like smoke, and the earth will wax old like a garment, and they that dwell therein will die in like manner, but my salvation will be for ever, and my righteousness will not be abolished. This recalls chapters 40 and Psalm 102 and Hebrews. Vanish away is literally, shall be torn asunder, as a garment, which accords with the context. In like manner means but some, Like a gnat, like the smallest and vilest insect. Jerome translates, as in the English version, and infers that in like manner as man, the heavens, that is, the sky, and earth are not to be annihilated, but changed for the better, as in chapter 65. Righteousness means My faithfully fulfilled promise, as in verse 5. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Hearken unto me, you that know righteousness, the people in whose heart is my law; fear not the reproach of men, neither be afraid of their revilings. Know righteousness recalls verse 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For the moth will eat them up like a garment, and the worm will eat them like wool, but my righteousness will be for ever, and my salvation from generation to generation. This recalls chapter 50 verse 9 and Job: not that the moth eats men up, but they shall be destroyed by as insignificant instrumentality as the moth that eats a garment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of the Lord; awake, as in the ancient days, in the generations of old. Are you not it that has cut Rahab, and wounded the dragon? This is the impassioned prayer of the exiled Jews. Ancient days recalls Psalm 44. Rahab is a poetical name for Egypt, as in chapter 30. Dragon is Hebrew, tannin: the crocodile, an emblem of Egypt, as represented on coins struck after the conquest of Egypt by Augustus; or rather here, its king, Pharaoh, as in chapters 27 and Psalm 74 and Ezekiel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Are you not it which has dried the sea, the waters of the great deep, that has made the depths of the sea a way for the ransomed to pass over? It means the arm. Art not Thou the same Almighty power that dried the sea, the Red Sea, as in chapter 43 and Exodus? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Therefore the redeemed of the Lord will return, and come with singing unto Zion, and everlasting joy will be upon their head; they will obtain gladness and joy, and sorrow and mourning will flee away. This recalls chapter 35. Therefore means assurance of faith, or else the answer of Jehovah corresponding to their prayer. As surely as God redeemed Israel out of Egypt, He shall redeem them from Babylon, both the literal in the age following, and mystical in the last ages, as in Revelation. There shall be a second exodus, as in chapters 11 and 27. Singing is an image from the custom of singing on a journey when a caravan is passing along the extended plains in the East. Everlasting joy recalls Judges. Sorrow flee away recalls Revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. I, even I, am he that comforts you; who are you, that you should be afraid of a man that will die, and of the son of man which will be made as grass? Comforteth recalls chapters 51 verse 3 and 40 verse 1. Thou means Zion. Son of man means frail and dying as his parent Adam. Be made as grass means wither as grass, as in chapter 40. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. And forget the Lord your maker, that has stretched forth the heavens, and laid the foundations of the earth, and have feared continually every day because of the fury of the oppressor, as if he were ready to destroy? And where is the fury of the oppressor? This recalls chapters 40, the same argument of comfort drawn from the omnipotence of the Creator. As if ready, etc., is literally, when he directs, namely, his arrow, to destroy, as in Psalms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The captive exile hastens that he may be loosed, and that he should not die in the pit, nor that his bread should fail. Captive exile is literally, one bowed down as a captive, as in chapter 10. The scene is primarily Babylon, and the time near the close of the captivity. Secondarily, and antitypically, the mystical Babylon, the last enemy of Israel and the church, in which they have long suffered, but from which they are to be gloriously delivered. Pit means such as were many of the ancient dungeons, as in Jeremiah and Genesis. Nor bread fail recalls chapters 33 and Jeremiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. But I am the Lord your God, that divided the sea, whose waves roared; The Lord of hosts is his name. Sea means the Red Sea. The same Hebrew word as make to rest in verse 4. Rather, that terrify the sea, that is, restrain it by My rebuke, when its waves roar. The Hebrew favours some, that terrify the sea so that the waves roar. The sense favours others, as in Jeremiah, or the English version, as in verses 9 and 10, which favours the special reference to the exodus from Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. I have put my words in your mouth, and I have covered you in the shadow of my hand, that I may plant the heavens, and lay the foundations of the earth, and say unto Zion: You are my people. This is addressed to Israel, embodied in the servant of Jehovah in chapter 42, Messiah, its ideal and representative Head, through whom the elect remnant is to be restored. Put my words in thy mouth is true of Israel, the depository of true religion, but fully realised only in Israel's Head and antitype, Messiah, as in chapters 49, 50, and 59, Deuteronomy, and John. Covered in the shadow of hand means protected thee, as in chapter 49 verse 2. Plant is rather, fix as a tabernacle, so it ought to be rendered, as in Daniel. The new creation, now going on in the spiritual world by the Gospel, as in Ephesians, and hereafter to be extended to the visible world, is meant, as in chapters 65 and 66, chapter 13, and 2 Peter. Zion means its restoration is a leading part in the new creation to come, as in chapter 65. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Awake, awake, stand up, O Jerusalem, which have drunk at the hand of the Lord the cup of his fury; you have drunken the dregs of the cup of trembling, and wrung them out. Awake, awake, stand up, O Jerusalem, etc., recalls chapter 52 verse 1. Drunk means Jehovah's wrath is compared to an intoxicating draught because it confounds the sufferer under it, and makes him fall, as in Job, Psalms, Jeremiah, Zechariah, and Revelation; poured out without mixture, rather, the pure wine juice mixed with intoxicating drugs. Of trembling means which produced trembling or intoxication. Wrung out means drained the last drop out; the dregs were the sediments from various substances, as honey, dates, and drugs, put into the wine to increase the strength and sweetness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. There is none to guide her among all the sons whom she has brought forth, neither is there any that takes her by the hand of all the sons that she has brought up. Following up the image in verse 17, intoxicated and confused by the cup of God's anger, she has none to guide her in her helpless state; she has not yet awakened out of the sleep caused by that draught. This cannot apply to the Babylonish captivity, for in it they had Ezekiel and Daniel, Ezra and Nehemiah, as guides, and soon awoke out of that sleep; but it applies to the Jews now, and will be still more applicable in their coming oppression by Antichrist. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. These two things are come unto you; who will be sorry for you? Desolation, and destruction, and the famine, and the sword; by whom will I comfort you? Two means classes of evils, for he enumerates four, namely, desolation and destruction to the land and state, famine and the sword to the people. Who shall be sorry for thee means so as to give thee effectual relief, as the parallel clause, By whom shall I comfort thee? shows, as in Lamentations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Your sons have fainted, they lie at the head of all the streets, as a wild bull in a net; they are full of the fury of the Lord, the rebuke of your God. Streets recalls Lamentations. Wild bull is rather, oryx, or gazelle, or wild goat; commonly in the East taken in a net, of a wide sweep, into which the beasts were hunted together. The streets of cities in the East often have gates, which are closed at night; a person wishing to escape would be stopped by them and caught, as a wild animal in a net. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Therefore hear now this, you afflicted, and drunken, but not with wine. Not with wine recalls chapters 29 and 51 verses 17 and 20 here and Lamentations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Thus says your Lord the Lord, and your God that pleads the cause of his people: Behold, I have taken out of your hand the cup of trembling, even the dregs of the cup of my fury; you will no more drink it again. Cause recalls Psalm 35, Jeremiah, and Micah. No more drink it recalls chapters 54: this cannot apply to Israel after the return from Babylon, but only to them after their final restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. But I will put it into the hand of them that afflict you, which have said to your soul: Bow down, that we may go over, and you have laid your body as the ground, and as the street, to them that went over. This recalls chapters 49 and Jeremiah and Zechariah. Bow down that go over means conquerors often literally trod on the necks of conquered kings, as Sapor of Persia did to the Roman emperor Valerian, as in Joshua and Psalms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Wrung out means drained the last drop out; the dregs were the sediments from various substances, as honey, dates, and drugs, put into the wine to increase the strength and sweetness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. There is none to guide her among all the sons whom she has brought forth, neither is there any that takes her by the hand of all the sons that she has brought up. Following up the image in verse 17, intoxicated and confused by the cup of God's anger, she has none to guide her in her helpless state; she has not yet awakened out of the sleep caused by that draught. This cannot apply to the Babylonish captivity, for in it they had Ezekiel and Daniel, Ezra and Nehemiah, as guides, and soon awoke out of that sleep; but it applies to the Jews now, and will be still more applicable in their coming oppression by Antichrist. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. These two things are come unto you; who will be sorry for you? Desolation, and destruction, and the famine, and the sword; by whom will I comfort you? Two means classes of evils, for he enumerates four, namely, desolation and destruction to the land and state, famine and the sword to the people. Who shall be sorry for thee means so as to give thee effectual relief, as the parallel clause, By whom shall I comfort thee? shows, as in Lamentations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Your sons have fainted, they lie at the head of all the streets, as a wild bull in a net; they are full of the fury of the Lord, the rebuke of your God. Streets recalls Lamentations. Wild bull is rather, oryx, or gazelle, or wild goat; commonly in the East taken in a net, of a wide sweep, into which the beasts were hunted together. The streets of cities in the East often have gates, which are closed at night; a person wishing to escape would be stopped by them and caught, as a wild animal in a net. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Therefore hear now this, you afflicted, and drunken, but not with wine. Not with wine recalls chapters 29 and 51 verses 17 and 20 here and Lamentations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Thus says your Lord the Lord, and your God that pleads the cause of his people: Behold, I have taken out of your hand the cup of trembling, even the dregs of the cup of my fury; you will no more drink it again. Cause recalls Psalm 35, Jeremiah, and Micah. No more drink it recalls chapters 54: this cannot apply to Israel after the return from Babylon, but only to them after their final restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. But I will put it into the hand of them that afflict you, which have said to your soul: Bow down, that we may go over, and you have laid your body as the ground, and as the street, to them that went over. This recalls chapters 49 and Jeremiah and Zechariah. Bow down that go over means conquerors often literally trod on the necks of conquered kings, as Sapor of Persia did to the Roman emperor Valerian, as in Joshua and Psalms.
