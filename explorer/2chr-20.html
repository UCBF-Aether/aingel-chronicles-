The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 It came to pass after this also, that the children of Moab, and the children of Ammon, and with them other beside the Ammonites, came against Jehoshaphat to battle.

2 Then there came some that told Jehoshaphat, saying, There cometh a great multitude against thee from beyond the sea on this side Syria; and, behold, they be in Hazazontamar, which is Engedi.

3 And Jehoshaphat feared, and set himself to seek the LORD, and proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah.

4 And Judah gathered themselves together, to ask help of the LORD: even out of all the cities of Judah they came to seek the LORD.

5 And Jehoshaphat stood in the congregation of Judah and Jerusalem, in the house of the LORD, before the new court,

6 And said, O LORD God of our fathers, art not thou God in heaven? and rulest not thou over all the kingdoms of the heathen? and in thine hand is there not power and might, so that none is able to withstand thee?

7 Art not thou our God, who didst drive out the inhabitants of this land before thy people Israel, and gavest it to the seed of Abraham thy friend for ever?

8 And they dwelt therein, and have built thee a sanctuary therein for thy name, saying,

9 If, when evil cometh upon us, as the sword, judgment, or pestilence, or famine, we stand before this house, and in thy presence, (for thy name is in this house,) and cry unto thee in our affliction, then thou wilt hear and help.

10 And now, behold, the children of Ammon and Moab and mount Seir, whom thou wouldest not let Israel invade, when they came out of the land of Egypt, but they turned from them, and destroyed them not;

11 Behold, I say, how they reward us, to come to cast us out of thy possession, which thou hast given us to inherit.

12 O our God, wilt thou not judge them? for we have no might against this great company that cometh against us; neither know we what to do: but our eyes are upon thee.

13 And all Judah stood before the LORD, with their little ones, their wives, and their children.

14 Then upon Jahaziel the son of Zechariah, the son of Benaiah, the son of Jeiel, the son of Mattaniah, a Levite of the sons of Asaph, came the Spirit of the LORD in the midst of the congregation;

15 And he said, Hearken ye, all Judah, and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem, and thou king Jehoshaphat, Thus saith the LORD unto you, Be not afraid nor dismayed by reason of this great multitude; for the battle is not yours, but God’s.

16 To morrow go ye down against them: behold, they come up by the cliff of Ziz; and ye shall find them at the end of the brook, before the wilderness of Jeruel.

17 Ye shall not need to fight in this battle: set yourselves, stand ye still, and see the salvation of the LORD with you, O Judah and Jerusalem: fear not, nor be dismayed; to morrow go out against them: for the LORD will be with you.

18 And Jehoshaphat bowed his head with his face to the ground: and all Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem fell before the LORD, worshipping the LORD.

19 And the Levites, of the children of the Kohathites, and of the children of the Korhites, stood up to praise the LORD God of Israel with a loud voice on high.

20 And they rose early in the morning, and went forth into the wilderness of Tekoa: and as they went forth, Jehoshaphat stood and said, Hear me, O Judah, and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem; Believe in the LORD your God, so shall ye be established; believe his prophets, so shall ye prosper.

21 And when he had consulted with the people, he appointed singers unto the LORD, and that should praise the beauty of holiness, as they went out before the army, and to say, Praise the LORD; for his mercy endureth for ever.

22 And when they began to sing and to praise, the LORD set ambushments against the children of Ammon, Moab, and mount Seir, which were come against Judah; and they were smitten.

23 For the children of Ammon and Moab stood up against the inhabitants of mount Seir, utterly to slay and destroy them: and when they had made an end of the inhabitants of Seir, every one helped to destroy another.

24 And when Judah came toward the watch tower in the wilderness, they looked unto the multitude, and, behold, they were dead bodies fallen to the earth, and none escaped.

25 And when Jehoshaphat and his people came to take away the spoil of them, they found among them in abundance both riches with the dead bodies, and precious jewels, which they stripped off for themselves, more than they could carry away: and they were three days in gathering of the spoil, it was so much.

26 And on the fourth day they assembled themselves in the valley of Berachah; for there they blessed the LORD: therefore the name of the same place was called, The valley of Berachah, unto this day.

27 Then they returned, every man of Judah and Jerusalem, and Jehoshaphat in the forefront of them, to go again to Jerusalem with joy; for the LORD had made them to rejoice over their enemies.

28 And they came to Jerusalem with psalteries and harps and trumpets unto the house of the LORD.

29 And the fear of God was on all the kingdoms of those countries, when they had heard that the LORD fought against the enemies of Israel.

30 So the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet: for his God gave him rest round about.

31 And Jehoshaphat reigned over Judah: he was thirty and five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned twenty and five years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Azubah the daughter of Shilhi.

32 And he walked in the way of Asa his father, and departed not from it, doing that which was right in the sight of the LORD.

33 Howbeit the high places were not taken away: for as yet the people had not prepared their hearts unto the God of their fathers.

34 Now the rest of the acts of Jehoshaphat, first and last, behold, they are written in the book of Jehu the son of Hanani, who is mentioned in the book of the kings of Israel.

35 And after this did Jehoshaphat king of Judah join himself with Ahaziah king of Israel, who did very wickedly:

36 And he joined himself with him to make ships to go to Tarshish: and they made the ships in Eziongaber.

37 Then Eliezer the son of Dodavah of Mareshah prophesied against Jehoshaphat, saying, Because thou hast joined thyself with Ahaziah, the LORD hath broken thy works. And the ships were broken, that they were not able to go to Tarshish.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the invasion by Moab and Ammon, Jehoshaphat's prayer before all Judah, the victory won as the singers went before the army, and the later broken alliance with Ahaziah over ships. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. It came to pass after this also, that Moab, Ammon, and others beside the Ammonites, came against Jehoshaphat to battle. Henry notes Jehoshaphat, well employed in reforming his kingdom, was surprised by a formidable invasion, with several nations joined, especially Lot's children, and the ten tribes beyond Jordan giving them passage, ungrateful after Jehoshaphat had helped them at Ramoth-Gilead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Then there came some that told Jehoshaphat, saying, There cometh a great multitude against thee from beyond the sea on this side Syria, and behold, they be in Hazazontamar, which is Engedi. Henry notes Jehoshaphat was surprised with intelligence when the enemy had already entered his country, coming from beyond the Dead Sea where Sodom had stood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And Jehoshaphat feared, and set himself to seek the Lord, and proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah. Henry notes holy fear spurred prayer: consciousness of guilt made him fear, yet he set himself to seek the Lord with fixed thought, sincerity and utmost resolution, and proclaimed a fast as a national act of humiliation, self-judging for sin and self-denial for the future. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And Judah gathered themselves together, to ask help of the Lord, even out of all the cities of Judah they came to seek the Lord. Henry notes the people readily assembled from all Judah's cities in the temple court, standing before the Lord as beggars at his door, with wives and children, since families were in danger together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And Jehoshaphat stood in the congregation of Judah and Jerusalem, in the house of the Lord, before the new court. Henry notes Jehoshaphat himself was the mouth of the congregation, not devolving prayer on chaplains, standing in the house of the Lord before the new court, within reach of God's promise to attend prayer made in that place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

And said, O Lord God of our fathers, art not thou God in heaven? and rulest not thou over all the kingdoms of the heathen? and in thine hand is there not power and might, so that none is able to withstand thee? Henry notes Jehoshaphat lays hold on covenant relation: God of their fathers and their God, and on title to the land, given to Abraham God's friend, whose seed they were, hoping to be beloved for the father's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry notes he mentions the sanctuary built for God's name, not as meriting anything, for of God's own they gave him, but as a token of God's favourable presence, with promise that he would hear and help when they cried before that house. Henry notes the temple was built for encouragement of faith at such a time: here thy name is, here we are, Lord help us for thy name's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And now, behold, Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir, whom thou wouldest not let Israel invade when they came out of Egypt, but they turned from them and destroyed them not. Henry notes he pleads the enemies' ingratitude: Israel was forbidden to meddle with Edom, Moab or Ammon, yet now they invade, rendering evil for good. Henry notes the prayer professes entire dependence: though a great army was on foot, they had no might without God, knew not what to do, but their eyes were upon him, an eye of faith, desire, hope and patient expectation. Henry notes God's answer was speedy, while he was yet speaking: the Spirit came upon a Levite in the midst of the congregation, not in eminency, for the Spirit blows where he lists, honouring a singer's office. And he said, Hearken ye, all Judah, and inhabitants of Jerusalem, and thou King Jehoshaphat, Thus saith the Lord unto you, Be not afraid nor dismayed by reason of this great multitude, for the battle is not yours, but God's. Henry notes Jahaziel encouraged trust: fear enough to bring them to God should not now drive them from him, for the battle is not theirs in their own strength or cause, but God's, who interests himself in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. To morrow go ye down against them: behold, they come up by the cliff of Ziz, and ye shall find them at the end of the brook, before the wilderness of Jeruel. Henry notes the prophet gave intelligence of the enemy's motions and orders to march, with time and place, for he who commands deliverance commands those for whom it is wrought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Ye shall not need to fight in this battle: set yourselves, stand ye still, and see the salvation of the Lord with you, O Judah and Jerusalem: fear not, nor be dismayed, to morrow go out against them, for the Lord will be with you. Henry notes they should be not glorious instruments but joyful spectators: they need not strike a stroke, only stand still and see, as Moses said at the Red Sea, for if the battle be God's, the victory shall be his too. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And Jehoshaphat bowed his head with his face to the ground, and all Judah and Jerusalem's inhabitants fell before the Lord, worshipping the Lord. Henry notes they received assurances with faith, reverence and thankfulness, bowing first Jehoshaphat then all the people, saying with faith, Be it unto us according to thy word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And the Levites, of the Kohathites and Korhites, stood up to praise the Lord God of Israel with a loud voice on high. Henry notes they lifted voices in praise, for active faith can give thanks for a promise not yet performed, knowing God's bonds are as good as ready money. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And they rose early and went forth into the wilderness of Tekoa, and as they went forth, Jehoshaphat stood and said, Hear me, O Judah, and inhabitants of Jerusalem: Believe in the Lord your God, so shall ye be established; believe his prophets, so shall ye prosper. Henry notes faith was their armour: instead of calling them to handle arms and keep ranks, Jehoshaphat bade them believe God and his prophets, for true courage is what faith inspires, and a heart fixed in trust is kept in perfect peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And when he had consulted with the people, he appointed singers unto the Lord, that should praise the beauty of holiness, as they went out before the army, and to say, Praise the Lord, for his mercy endureth for ever. Henry notes praise was their vanguard: singers went before the army with nothing but to praise God's holiness, expressing reliance on God's word, animating soldiers, confounding the enemy, and engaging God, for praise pleases God better than burnt offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And when they began to sing and to praise, the Lord set ambushments against Ammon, Moab and Mount Seir, which were come against Judah, and they were smitten. Henry notes the enemy was destroyed unaccountably, not by thunder or angel's sword, but by the Lord setting ambushments, either angels or their own ambushments struck with confusion, and this God did when his people began to sing and praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. For Ammon and Moab stood up against Seir's inhabitants, utterly to slay and destroy them, and when they had made an end of Seir, every one helped to destroy another. Henry notes God often makes wicked people instruments of destruction to one another: Ammon and Moab fell on Edom, then fell out with one another, for what alliances can hold together those God designs to dash in pieces? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And when Judah came toward the watch tower in the wilderness, they looked unto the multitude, and behold, they were dead bodies fallen to the earth, and none escaped. Henry notes Judah's army, expecting living men to fight, found only dead bodies spread as dung, and God not only delivered them from being spoiled but enriched them with the enemy's spoil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And when Jehoshaphat and his people came to take the spoil, they found among them in abundance both riches with the dead bodies, and precious jewels, which they stripped off, more than they could carry away, and they were three days gathering the spoil, it was so much. Henry notes the plunder was very great and rich, precious jewels with bodies that could not save their owners, and the spoil took three days to gather, showing God's end in bringing the army was to humble and prove Judah, that he might do them good in their latter end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And on the fourth day they assembled in the valley of Berachah, for there they blessed the Lord, therefore the place was called the valley of Berachah unto this day. Henry notes they kept a day of praise in the camp before leaving the field, blessing God with such zeal that the day's work named the place Berachah, blessing, perpetuating the wonder for succeeding generations to trust in God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Then they returned, every man of Judah and Jerusalem, and Jehoshaphat in the forefront of them, to go again to Jerusalem with joy, for the Lord had made them to rejoice over their enemies. Henry notes they came in solemn procession, Jehoshaphat at their head, that the country might join their praises and thanks be given where prayer had obtained mercy, for praising God must not be a day's work only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And they came to Jerusalem with psalteries and harps and trumpets unto the house of the Lord. Henry notes public mercies call for public acknowledgments in the courts of the Lord's house, and the procession came with music to the temple where prayer had been made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And the fear of God was on all the kingdoms of those countries, when they had heard that the Lord fought against the enemies of Israel. Henry notes the victory made Judah look great abroad: neighbours, hearing God fought for Israel, reverenced God and feared injuring his people, for it is dangerous fighting those who have God with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. So the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet, for his God gave him rest round about. Henry notes they were quiet among themselves, objectors to image-destroying now satisfied that Israel's God alone delivers, and quiet from neighbours' insults, God having given rest round about, and if he give rest, who can give disturbance? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And Jehoshaphat reigned over Judah: he was thirty-five when he began to reign, and reigned twenty-five years in Jerusalem, and his mother's name was Azubah daughter of Shilhi. Henry notes the close of Jehoshaphat's reign is drawn toward, with his general character that he did right in the Lord's sight, keeping close to God's worship and keeping his people close to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And he walked in the way of Asa his father, and departed not from it, doing that which was right in the sight of the Lord. Henry notes Jehoshaphat kept close to the worship of God himself and did what he could to keep his people close to it, departing not from Asa's good way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Howbeit the high places were not taken away, for as yet the people had not prepared their hearts unto the God of their fathers. Henry notes two things are lamented: the people retained partiality for high places, those for the true God being thought allowable, and Jehoshaphat was loth to disoblige them, for they had not prepared their hearts, complying for shame but not heartily, and the best magistrates cannot reform a cool people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Now the rest of Jehoshaphat's acts, first and last, behold, they are written in the book of Jehu son of Hanani, who is mentioned in the book of the kings of Israel. Henry notes readers were referred to Jehu the prophet's authentic history, then extant, for a further account of Jehoshaphat's reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And after this did Jehoshaphat king of Judah join himself with Ahaziah king of Israel, who did very wickedly. Henry notes Jehoshaphat still retained partiality for Ahab's house, having married his son to its daughter, and after God had done such great things for him without scandalous confederacies, he joined himself with Ahaziah, who did very wickedly, an ungrateful return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And he joined himself with him to make ships to go to Tarshish, and they made the ships in Eziongaber. Henry notes he joined Ahaziah not in war as with his father, but in trade, becoming partner in a fleet bound for Ophir, built at Eziongaber. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Then Eliezer son of Dodavah of Mareshah prophesied against Jehoshaphat, saying, Because thou hast joined thyself with Ahaziah, the Lord hath broken thy works. And the ships were broken, that they were not able to go to Tarshish. Henry notes God sent a prophet foretelling the blasting of the project, and a storm broke the ships in port before they sailed, warning him to break off the alliance, which he took, refusing Ahaziah's later pressing, for joining friendship with evil-doers is a pernicious snare hard to escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And they rose early and went forth into the wilderness of Tekoa, and as they went forth, Jehoshaphat stood and said, Hear me, O Judah, and inhabitants of Jerusalem: Believe in the Lord your God, so shall ye be established; believe his prophets, so shall ye prosper. Henry notes faith was their armour: instead of calling them to handle arms and keep ranks, Jehoshaphat bade them believe God and his prophets, for true courage is what faith inspires, and a heart fixed in trust is kept in perfect peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And when he had consulted with the people, he appointed singers unto the Lord, that should praise the beauty of holiness, as they went out before the army, and to say, Praise the Lord, for his mercy endureth for ever. Henry notes praise was their vanguard: singers went before the army with nothing but to praise God's holiness, expressing reliance on God's word, animating soldiers, confounding the enemy, and engaging God, for praise pleases God better than burnt offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And when they began to sing and to praise, the Lord set ambushments against Ammon, Moab and Mount Seir, which were come against Judah, and they were smitten. Henry notes the enemy was destroyed unaccountably, not by thunder or angel's sword, but by the Lord setting ambushments, either angels or their own ambushments struck with confusion, and this God did when his people began to sing and praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. For Ammon and Moab stood up against Seir's inhabitants, utterly to slay and destroy them, and when they had made an end of Seir, every one helped to destroy another. Henry notes God often makes wicked people instruments of destruction to one another: Ammon and Moab fell on Edom, then fell out with one another, for what alliances can hold together those God designs to dash in pieces? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And when Judah came toward the watch tower in the wilderness, they looked unto the multitude, and behold, they were dead bodies fallen to the earth, and none escaped. Henry notes Judah's army, expecting living men to fight, found only dead bodies spread as dung, and God not only delivered them from being spoiled but enriched them with the enemy's spoil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And when Jehoshaphat and his people came to take the spoil, they found among them in abundance both riches with the dead bodies, and precious jewels, which they stripped off, more than they could carry away, and they were three days gathering the spoil, it was so much. Henry notes the plunder was very great and rich, precious jewels with bodies that could not save their owners, and the spoil took three days to gather, showing God's end in bringing the army was to humble and prove Judah, that he might do them good in their latter end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And on the fourth day they assembled in the valley of Berachah, for there they blessed the Lord, therefore the place was called the valley of Berachah unto this day. Henry notes they kept a day of praise in the camp before leaving the field, blessing God with such zeal that the day's work named the place Berachah, blessing, perpetuating the wonder for succeeding generations to trust in God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Then they returned, every man of Judah and Jerusalem, and Jehoshaphat in the forefront of them, to go again to Jerusalem with joy, for the Lord had made them to rejoice over their enemies. Henry notes they came in solemn procession, Jehoshaphat at their head, that the country might join their praises and thanks be given where prayer had obtained mercy, for praising God must not be a day's work only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And they came to Jerusalem with psalteries and harps and trumpets unto the house of the Lord. Henry notes public mercies call for public acknowledgments in the courts of the Lord's house, and the procession came with music to the temple where prayer had been made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And the fear of God was on all the kingdoms of those countries, when they had heard that the Lord fought against the enemies of Israel. Henry notes the victory made Judah look great abroad: neighbours, hearing God fought for Israel, reverenced God and feared injuring his people, for it is dangerous fighting those who have God with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. So the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet, for his God gave him rest round about. Henry notes they were quiet among themselves, objectors to image-destroying now satisfied that Israel's God alone delivers, and quiet from neighbours' insults, God having given rest round about, and if he give rest, who can give disturbance? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And Jehoshaphat reigned over Judah: he was thirty-five when he began to reign, and reigned twenty-five years in Jerusalem, and his mother's name was Azubah daughter of Shilhi. Henry notes the close of Jehoshaphat's reign is drawn toward, with his general character that he did right in the Lord's sight, keeping close to God's worship and keeping his people close to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And he walked in the way of Asa his father, and departed not from it, doing that which was right in the sight of the Lord. Henry notes Jehoshaphat kept close to the worship of God himself and did what he could to keep his people close to it, departing not from Asa's good way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Howbeit the high places were not taken away, for as yet the people had not prepared their hearts unto the God of their fathers. Henry notes two things are lamented: the people retained partiality for high places, those for the true God being thought allowable, and Jehoshaphat was loth to disoblige them, for they had not prepared their hearts, complying for shame but not heartily, and the best magistrates cannot reform a cool people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Now the rest of Jehoshaphat's acts, first and last, behold, they are written in the book of Jehu son of Hanani, who is mentioned in the book of the kings of Israel. Henry notes readers were referred to Jehu the prophet's authentic history, then extant, for a further account of Jehoshaphat's reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And after this did Jehoshaphat king of Judah join himself with Ahaziah king of Israel, who did very wickedly. Henry notes Jehoshaphat still retained partiality for Ahab's house, having married his son to its daughter, and after God had done such great things for him without scandalous confederacies, he joined himself with Ahaziah, who did very wickedly, an ungrateful return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And he joined himself with him to make ships to go to Tarshish, and they made the ships in Eziongaber. Henry notes he joined Ahaziah not in war as with his father, but in trade, becoming partner in a fleet bound for Ophir, built at Eziongaber. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Then Eliezer son of Dodavah of Mareshah prophesied against Jehoshaphat, saying, Because thou hast joined thyself with Ahaziah, the Lord hath broken thy works. And the ships were broken, that they were not able to go to Tarshish. Henry notes God sent a prophet foretelling the blasting of the project, and a storm broke the ships in port before they sailed, warning him to break off the alliance, which he took, refusing Ahaziah's later pressing, for joining friendship with evil-doers is a pernicious snare hard to escape. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
