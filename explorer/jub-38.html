The Text — Jubilees 38 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And after that Judah spake to Jacob, his father, and said unto him : " Bend thy bow, father, and send forth thy arrows and cast down the adversary and slay the enemy ; and mayest thou have the power, for we shall not slay thy brother, for he is such as thou, and he is like thee : let us give him (this) honour." 2.

2 Then Jacob bent his bow and sent forth the arrow and struck Esau, his brother, (on his right breast) and slew him.

3 And again he sent forth an arrow and struck 'Adoran the Aramaean on the left breast, and drove him backward and slew him.

4 And then went forth the sons of Jacob, they and their servants, dividing themselves into companies on the four sides of the tower.

5 And

6 Judah went forth in front, and Naphtali and Gad" with him and fifty servants with him on the south side of the tower, and they slew all they found before them, and not one individual of them escaped.

7 And Levi and Dan and Asher went forth on the east side of the tower, and fifty (men) with them, and they slew the fighting men of Moab and Ammon.

8 And Reuben and Issachar and Zebulon went forth on the north side of the tower, and fifty men with them, and they slew the fighting men of the Philistines.

9 And Simeon and Benjamin and Enoch, Reuben's son, went forth on the west side of the tower, and fifty (men) with them, and they slew of Edom and of the Horites four hundred men, stout warriors; and six hundred fled, and four of the sons of Esau fled with them, and left their father lying slain, as he had fallen on the hill which is in 'Aduram.

10 And the sons of Jacob pursued after them to the mountains of Seir.

11 And Jacob buried his brother on the hill which is in 'Aduram, and he returned to his house.

12 And the sons of Jacob pressed hard upon the sons of Esau in the mountains of Seir, and bowed their necks so that they became servants of the sons of Jacob.

13 And they sent to their father (to inquire) whether they should make peace with them or slay them.

14 And Jacob sent word to his sons that they should make peace, and they made peace with them, and placed the yoke of servitude upon them, so that they paid tribute to Jacob and to his sons always.

15 And they continued to pay tribute to Jacob until the day that he went down into Egypt.

16 And the sons of Edom have not got quit of the yoke of servitude which the twelve sons of Jacob had imposed on them until this day.

17 And these are the kings that reigned in Edom before there reigned any king over the children of Israel [until this day] in the land of Edom.

18 And Balaq the son of Beor, reigned in Edom, and the name of his city was Danaba.

19 And Balaq died, and Jobab, the son of Zara of Boser reigned in his stead.

20 And Jobab died, and 'Asam of the land of Teman, reigned in his stead.

21 And 'Asam died, and 'Adath the son of Barad who slew Midian in the field of Moab, reigned in his stead, and the name of his city was Avith.

22 And 'Adath died, and Salman from 'Amaseqa reigned in his stead.

23 And Salman died, and Saul of Ra'aboth (by the) river, reigned in his stead.

24 And Saul died, and Ba'elunan, the son of Achbor, reigned in his stead.

25 And Ba'elunan, the son of Achbor, died, and 'Adath reigned in his stead, and the name of his wife was Maitabith, the daughter of Matarat, the daughter of Metabedza'ab.

26 These are the kings who reigned in the land of Edom.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 17. These are the kings who reigned in Edom before any king reigned over the children of Israel in the land of Edom. Henry notes that the registers of Edom show God's faithfulness to his promise to Abraham, and that the pomp and power of Edom's kings tried the faith of God's Israel while they were bond-slaves in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Balaq the son of Beor reigned in Edom, and his city was Danaba. The list of Edomite kings, each with his city, records a settled kingdom with capitals and successions, while Jacob's seed still held only a promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Balaq died, and Jobab the son of Zara of Boser reigned in his stead. The successions run steadily through the list, reign following reign, the ordinary course of a kingdom established in Seir. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Jobab died, and Asam of the land of Teman reigned in his stead. Teman, the southern district of Edom, gives this king his origin, as each king's place marks the spread of Edomite settlement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Asam died, and Adath the son of Barad, who slew Midian in the field of Moab, reigned in his stead, and his city was Avith. The note of Adath's victory over Midian shows Edom's kings were warriors as well as rulers, winning their standing by the sword. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Adath died, and Salman from Amaseqa reigned in his stead. The list continues without comment, reign after reign, the record of a kingdom that had its all in hand while Israel waited in hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Salman died, and Saul of Ra'aboth by the river reigned in his stead. Henry observes that it may be supposed a trial to Israel's faith to hear of Edom's kings while they themselves were slaves, but that those who look for great things from God must be content to wait, for God's time is the best time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Saul died, and Ba'elunan the son of Achbor reigned in his stead. The succession from father to son and from city to city gives Edom a continuous history, the foil to Israel's sojourn and slavery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Ba'elunan the son of Achbor died, and Adath reigned in his stead, and his wife was Maitabith, daughter of Matarat, daughter of Metabedza'ab. The naming of the queen by her lineage closes the royal record with the same genealogical care as the kings themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. These are the kings who reigned in the land of Edom. The closing line seals the register: eight kings before Israel had a king, a complete Edomite history set against Israel's waiting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
