The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ezra 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Darius the king made a decree, and search was made in the house of the rolls, where the treasures were laid up in Babylon.

2 And there was found at Achmetha, in the palace that is in the province of the Medes, a roll, and therein was a record thus written:

3 In the first year of Cyrus the king the same Cyrus the king made a decree concerning the house of God at Jerusalem, Let the house be builded, the place where they offered sacrifices, and let the foundations thereof be strongly laid; the height thereof threescore cubits, and the breadth thereof threescore cubits;

4 With three rows of great stones, and a row of new timber: and let the expenses be given out of the king’s house:

5 And also let the golden and silver vessels of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar took forth out of the temple which is at Jerusalem, and brought unto Babylon, be restored, and brought again unto the temple which is at Jerusalem, every one to his place, and place them in the house of God.

6 Now therefore, Tatnai, governor beyond the river, Shetharboznai, and your companions the Apharsachites, which are beyond the river, be ye far from thence:

7 Let the work of this house of God alone; let the governor of the Jews and the elders of the Jews build this house of God in his place.

8 Moreover I make a decree what ye shall do to the elders of these Jews for the building of this house of God: that of the king’s goods, even of the tribute beyond the river, forthwith expenses be given unto these men, that they be not hindered.

9 And that which they have need of, both young bullocks, and rams, and lambs, for the burnt offerings of the God of heaven, wheat, salt, wine, and oil, according to the appointment of the priests which are at Jerusalem, let it be given them day by day without fail:

10 That they may offer sacrifices of sweet savours unto the God of heaven, and pray for the life of the king, and of his sons.

11 Also I have made a decree, that whosoever shall alter this word, let timber be pulled down from his house, and being set up, let him be hanged thereon; and let his house be made a dunghill for this.

12 And the God that hath caused his name to dwell there destroy all kings and people, that shall put to their hand to alter and to destroy this house of God which is at Jerusalem. I Darius have made a decree; let it be done with speed.

13 Then Tatnai, governor on this side the river, Shetharboznai, and their companions, according to that which Darius the king had sent, so they did speedily.

14 And the elders of the Jews builded, and they prospered through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah the son of Iddo. And they builded, and finished it, according to the commandment of the God of Israel, and according to the commandment of Cyrus, and Darius, and Artaxerxes king of Persia.

15 And this house was finished on the third day of the month Adar, which was in the sixth year of the reign of Darius the king.

16 And the children of Israel, the priests, and the Levites, and the rest of the children of the captivity, kept the dedication of this house of God with joy.

17 And offered at the dedication of this house of God an hundred bullocks, two hundred rams, four hundred lambs; and for a sin offering for all Israel, twelve he goats, according to the number of the tribes of Israel.

18 And they set the priests in their divisions, and the Levites in their courses, for the service of God, which is at Jerusalem; as it is written in the book of Moses.

19 And the children of the captivity kept the passover upon the fourteenth day of the first month.

20 For the priests and the Levites were purified together, all of them were pure, and killed the passover for all the children of the captivity, and for their brethren the priests, and for themselves.

21 And the children of Israel, which were come again out of captivity, and all such as had separated themselves unto them from the filthiness of the heathen of the land, to seek the LORD God of Israel, did eat,

22 And kept the feast of unleavened bread seven days with joy: for the LORD had made them joyful, and turned the heart of the king of Assyria unto them, to strengthen their hands in the work of the house of God, the God of Israel.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Darius orders the search, and at Achmetha in Media a roll is found: Cyrus in his first year decreed the house at Jerusalem be built, its foundations strong, its cost from the king’s house. Darius confirms the decree, orders Tatnai and his companions to stay far from the work, to supply its costs speedily and without fail, and warns that anyone altering the decree shall be hanged on his own house’s timber. The temple is finished, dedicated with joy, the priests and Levites set in their divisions, and the Passover kept. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text in the Text panel above

Unfamiliar terms

  • The house of the rolls. Interpreted The archive where treasures and records were laid up, searched at Darius’s command (verse 1). Ezra 6:1, Text panel

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then Darius made a decree, and search was made in the house of the rolls where treasures were laid up in Babylon. Henry notes the very inquiry meant to stop the work becomes the means of finding its warrant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. There was found at Achmetha, in the palace in Media, a roll with a record written. JFB notes the decree was not in Babylon's archive but in the Median summer palace, showing God's providence guiding the search to the right place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. In Cyrus's first year, Cyrus decreed concerning God's house at Jerusalem: let the house be built where sacrifices were offered, its foundations strongly laid, sixty cubits high and sixty broad. The dimensions recall a substantial temple, not a mere shrine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. With three rows of great stones and a row of new timber, and expenses given out of the king's house. JFB notes the mixed stone and timber construction follows Solomon's pattern, and the king himself pays. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The gold and silver vessels Nebuchadnezzar took from Jerusalem's temple to Babylon must be restored to the temple at Jerusalem, each to its place. Henry notes restoration is complete only when vessels and house are together again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Now therefore Tatnai, governor beyond the river, Shethar-boznai and the Apharsachites beyond the river, be ye far from thence. The accusers are told to keep their distance, a reversal of their attempt to stand over the work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Let the work of this house of God alone; let the governor of the Jews and the elders build this house of God in its place. JFB notes Darius does not merely permit the building, he forbids interference with it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Darius further decrees what shall be done for the elders: from the king's goods, even the tribute beyond the river, expenses are to be given forthwith, that they be not hindered. Henry notes the adversaries' own tribute now funds the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Whatever they need, young bullocks, rams, lambs for burnt offerings, wheat, salt, wine and oil, according to the priests' appointment at Jerusalem, is to be given day by day without fail. The daily supply matches the daily sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. That they may offer sacrifices of sweet savours to the God of heaven, and pray for the life of the king and of his sons. JFB notes the heathen king asks the prayers of Israel's priests, and the temple's worship is to bless even its patron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Whoever alters this word shall have timber pulled from his house, and being set up, be hanged thereon, and his house made a dunghill. Henry notes the grim penalty guards the decree: the king's own house becomes the warning against tampering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And the God who caused his name to dwell there destroy all kings and people who put their hand to alter and destroy this house at Jerusalem. Darius invokes Israel's God against future destroyers, and adds, 'let it be done with speed.' Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Then Tatnai, Shethar-boznai and their companions did speedily according to what Darius had sent. JFB notes the same officials who questioned the work now carry out its funding with the same diligence they once used to stop it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The elders built and prospered through the prophesying of Haggai and Zechariah, and finished according to the commandment of the God of Israel and of Cyrus, Darius and Artaxerxes. Henry notes three authorities converge: God's command, the prophets' word, and three kings' decrees. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. This house was finished on the third day of Adar, in the sixth year of Darius. JFB notes the date closes a work begun under Cyrus, halted for years, and finished by prophecy and providence together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The children of Israel, priests, Levites and the rest of the returned exiles kept the dedication of this house of God with joy. Henry notes joy is the proper dedication mood: the house is received as a gift, not merely completed as a task. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. At the dedication they offered a hundred bullocks, two hundred rams, four hundred lambs, and for a sin offering for all Israel twelve he-goats, according to the tribes. JFB notes the twelve goats confess the whole nation's sin, though only a remnant returned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They set the priests in their divisions and Levites in their courses for God's service at Jerusalem, as written in the book of Moses. The temple is not only built but staffed, in Moses' order, so worship can continue after the feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The returned exiles kept the passover on the fourteenth day of the first month. Henry notes the first passover in the new temple rehearses the Exodus: a delivered people eat the feast of deliverance in their own house again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Priests and Levites were purified together, all pure, and killed the passover for all the exiles, for their brethren the priests, and for themselves. JFB notes the Levites serve the whole congregation, including priests, in one common purification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The Israelites who had come again out of captivity ate, and all such as had separated themselves from the filthiness of the heathen of the land to seek the LORD God of Israel. Henry notes the feast is open to converts who separate from idolatry, not only to blood descendants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. They kept unleavened bread seven days with joy, for the LORD had made them joyful and turned the heart of the king of Assyria to them, to strengthen their hands. JFB notes 'king of Assyria' is Darius as lord of the old Assyrian lands, whose changed heart is itself God's work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
