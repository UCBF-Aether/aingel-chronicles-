Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 22 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? why art thou so far from helping me, and from the words of my roaring?

2 O my God, I cry in the day time, but thou hearest not; and in the night season, and am not silent.

3 But thou art holy, O thou that inhabitest the praises of Israel.

4 Our fathers trusted in thee: they trusted, and thou didst deliver them.

5 They cried unto thee, and were delivered: they trusted in thee, and were not confounded.

6 But I am a worm, and no man; a reproach of men, and despised of the people.

7 All they that see me laugh me to scorn: they shoot out the lip, they shake the head, saying,

8 He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him, seeing he delighted in him.

9 But thou art he that took me out of the womb: thou didst make me hope when I was upon my mother’s breasts.

10 I was cast upon thee from the womb: thou art my God from my mother’s belly.

11 Be not far from me; for trouble is near; for there is none to help.

12 Many bulls have compassed me: strong bulls of Bashan have beset me round.

13 They gaped upon me with their mouths, as a ravening and a roaring lion.

14 I am poured out like water, and all my bones are out of joint: my heart is like wax; it is melted in the midst of my bowels.

15 My strength is dried up like a potsherd; and my tongue cleaveth to my jaws; and thou hast brought me into the dust of death.

16 For dogs have compassed me: the assembly of the wicked have inclosed me: they pierced my hands and my feet.

17 I may tell all my bones: they look and stare upon me.

18 They part my garments among them, and cast lots upon my vesture.

19 But be not thou far from me, O LORD: O my strength, haste thee to help me.

20 Deliver my soul from the sword; my darling from the power of the dog.

21 Save me from the lion’s mouth: for thou hast heard me from the horns of the unicorns.

22 I will declare thy name unto my brethren: in the midst of the congregation will I praise thee.

23 Ye that fear the LORD, praise him; all ye the seed of Jacob, glorify him; and fear him, all ye the seed of Israel.

24 For he hath not despised nor abhorred the affliction of the afflicted; neither hath he hid his face from him; but when he cried unto him, he heard.

25 My praise shall be of thee in the great congregation: I will pay my vows before them that fear him.

26 The meek shall eat and be satisfied: they shall praise the LORD that seek him: your heart shall live for ever.

27 All the ends of the world shall remember and turn unto the LORD: and all the kindreds of the nations shall worship before thee.

28 For the kingdom is the LORD’s: and he is the governor among the nations.

29 All they that be fat upon earth shall eat and worship: all they that go down to the dust shall bow before him: and none can keep alive his own soul.

30 A seed shall serve him; it shall be accounted to the Lord for a generation.

31 They shall come, and shall declare his righteousness unto a people that shall be born, that he hath done this.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Forsakenness turning to worldwide praise: the sufferer cries unheard day and night, mocked, pierced, his garments divided by lot, then deliverance already heard turns to praise in the congregation, widening until all nations, and generations yet unborn, hear what God has done. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Heading note: the text panel prints the verses only, without a heading. The reference to David's sorrows in the verse notes (verse 1) comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? Why so far from helping and from the words of my roaring? The cry of dereliction, uttered by Christ on the cross, opens the great psalm of his sufferings; Henry and JFB read David's sorrows here as the shadow of Messiah's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. He cries by day and is not heard, and by night and is not silent: prayer continues without answer, which is the sharpest part of the trial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. But God is holy, inhabiting the praises of Israel: even in forsakenness, God's holiness and his dwelling among his people's praise are confessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The fathers trusted and were delivered: past generations' deliverances are recalled as a pattern the sufferer longs to share. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. They cried and were delivered, trusted and were not confounded: the pattern is repeated to sharpen the contrast with his own case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But I am a worm and no man, a reproach of men, despised of the people: the sufferer is debased below humanity in men's eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. All who see him laugh him to scorn, shooting out the lip and shaking the head: mockery takes the exact gestures the Gospels record at the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He trusted the Lord to deliver him; let him deliver him, seeing he delighted in him: enemies turn his very trust into a taunt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. But God took him from the womb and made him hope upon his mother's breasts: God's care from birth is the oldest ground of trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. He was cast upon God from the womb; God is his God from his mother's belly: a lifelong relation is pleaded against a present sense of abandonment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Be not far from me, for trouble is near and there is none to help: nearness of trouble and absence of helpers make God's nearness the only plea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Many bulls compass him, strong bulls of Bashan beset him round: powerful, brutal enemies surround him like well-fed, fierce cattle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They gape on him with their mouths, as a ravening, roaring lion: the enemy is both gaping maw and roaring predator. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He is poured out like water, his bones out of joint, his heart like wax melted within: the body dissolves under suffering, and the heart fails. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. His strength is dried like a potsherd, his tongue cleaves to his jaws, and he is brought into the dust of death: thirst and exhaustion mark the approach of death itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Dogs compass him, the assembly of the wicked encloses him, and they pierce his hands and feet: the piercing, centuries before crucifixion, is the psalm's most literal prophecy of the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. He can tell all his bones, while they look and stare on him: emaciation and the gaze of onlookers complete the public shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They part his garments among them and cast lots on his vesture: the division of clothing, fulfilled at the cross, is named as the enemies' last indignity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. But be not far from me, O Lord, my strength; haste to help me: the prayer returns, now addressed to God as strength itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Deliver my soul from the sword, my darling — his precious life — from the power of the dog: the sword and the dog name violent and contemptuous death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Save me from the lion's mouth, for you have heard me from the horns of the unicorns: deliverance is already heard, and the psalm turns toward praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. I will declare your name to my brethren, and praise you in the midst of the congregation: the delivered sufferer becomes the preacher of God's name to his brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. You who fear the Lord, praise him; all the seed of Jacob, glorify him; all the seed of Israel, fear him: the call to praise widens to the whole people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. For he has not despised nor abhorred the affliction of the afflicted, nor hidden his face, but heard when he cried: the forsakenness of verse 1 is answered — God did hear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. My praise shall be of you in the great congregation; I will pay my vows before those who fear him: public praise and kept vows follow deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The meek shall eat and be satisfied, and praise the Lord who seek him; your heart shall live for ever: the thank-offering becomes a feast for the humble, with life for ever as its promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. All the ends of the world shall remember and turn to the Lord, and all kindreds of nations worship before him: the sufferer's deliverance draws the nations to repentance and worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. For the kingdom is the Lord's, and he governs among the nations: universal worship rests on universal kingship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. All the fat of the earth shall eat and worship, and all going down to dust shall bow before him, and none can keep alive his own soul: rich and dying alike must bow, for life is not in human keeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. A seed shall serve him, accounted to the Lord for a generation: the kingdom continues in a posterity reckoned as God's own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. They shall come and declare his righteousness to a people yet to be born, that he has done this: the gospel of what God has done is preached to the unborn — the psalm ends in mission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
