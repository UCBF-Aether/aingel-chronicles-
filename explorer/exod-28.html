The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 28 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And take thou unto thee Aaron thy brother, and his sons with him, from among the children of Israel, that he may minister unto me in the priest’s office, even Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar, Aaron’s sons.

2 And thou shalt make holy garments for Aaron thy brother for glory and for beauty.

3 And thou shalt speak unto all that are wise hearted, whom I have filled with the spirit of wisdom, that they may make Aaron’s garments to consecrate him, that he may minister unto me in the priest’s office.

4 And these are the garments which they shall make; a breastplate, and an ephod, and a robe, and a broidered coat, a mitre, and a girdle: and they shall make holy garments for Aaron thy brother, and his sons, that he may minister unto me in the priest’s office.

5 And they shall take gold, and blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linen.

6 And they shall make the ephod of gold, of blue, and of purple, of scarlet, and fine twined linen, with cunning work.

7 It shall have the two shoulderpieces thereof joined at the two edges thereof; and so it shall be joined together.

8 And the curious girdle of the ephod, which is upon it, shall be of the same, according to the work thereof; even of gold, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen.

9 And thou shalt take two onyx stones, and grave on them the names of the children of Israel:

10 Six of their names on one stone, and the other six names of the rest on the other stone, according to their birth.

11 With the work of an engraver in stone, like the engravings of a signet, shalt thou engrave the two stones with the names of the children of Israel: thou shalt make them to be set in ouches of gold.

12 And thou shalt put the two stones upon the shoulders of the ephod for stones of memorial unto the children of Israel: and Aaron shall bear their names before the LORD upon his two shoulders for a memorial.

13 And thou shalt make ouches of gold;

14 And two chains of pure gold at the ends; of wreathen work shalt thou make them, and fasten the wreathen chains to the ouches.

15 And thou shalt make the breastplate of judgment with cunning work; after the work of the ephod thou shalt make it; of gold, of blue, and of purple, and of scarlet, and of fine twined linen, shalt thou make it.

16 Foursquare it shall be being doubled; a span shall be the length thereof, and a span shall be the breadth thereof.

17 And thou shalt set in it settings of stones, even four rows of stones: the first row shall be a sardius, a topaz, and a carbuncle: this shall be the first row.

18 And the second row shall be an emerald, a sapphire, and a diamond.

19 And the third row a ligure, an agate, and an amethyst.

20 And the fourth row a beryl, and an onyx, and a jasper: they shall be set in gold in their inclosings.

21 And the stones shall be with the names of the children of Israel, twelve, according to their names, like the engravings of a signet; every one with his name shall they be according to the twelve tribes.

22 And thou shalt make upon the breastplate chains at the ends of wreathen work of pure gold.

23 And thou shalt make upon the breastplate two rings of gold, and shalt put the two rings on the two ends of the breastplate.

24 And thou shalt put the two wreathen chains of gold in the two rings which are on the ends of the breastplate.

25 And the other two ends of the two wreathen chains thou shalt fasten in the two ouches, and put them on the shoulderpieces of the ephod before it.

26 And thou shalt make two rings of gold, and thou shalt put them upon the two ends of the breastplate in the border thereof, which is in the side of the ephod inward.

27 And two other rings of gold thou shalt make, and shalt put them on the two sides of the ephod underneath, toward the forepart thereof, over against the other coupling thereof, above the curious girdle of the ephod.

28 And they shall bind the breastplate by the rings thereof unto the rings of the ephod with a lace of blue, that it may be above the curious girdle of the ephod, and that the breastplate be not loosed from the ephod.

29 And Aaron shall bear the names of the children of Israel in the breastplate of judgment upon his heart, when he goeth in unto the holy place, for a memorial before the LORD continually.

30 And thou shalt put in the breastplate of judgment the Urim and the Thummim; and they shall be upon Aaron’s heart, when he goeth in before the LORD: and Aaron shall bear the judgment of the children of Israel upon his heart before the LORD continually.

31 And thou shalt make the robe of the ephod all of blue.

32 And there shall be an hole in the top of it, in the midst thereof: it shall have a binding of woven work round about the hole of it, as it were the hole of an habergeon, that it be not rent.

33 And beneath upon the hem of it thou shalt make pomegranates of blue, and of purple, and of scarlet, round about the hem thereof; and bells of gold between them round about:

34 A golden bell and a pomegranate, a golden bell and a pomegranate, upon the hem of the robe round about.

35 And it shall be upon Aaron to minister: and his sound shall be heard when he goeth in unto the holy place before the LORD, and when he cometh out, that he die not.

36 And thou shalt make a plate of pure gold, and grave upon it, like the engravings of a signet, HOLINESS TO THE LORD.

37 And thou shalt put it on a blue lace, that it may be upon the mitre; upon the forefront of the mitre it shall be.

38 And it shall be upon Aaron’s forehead, that Aaron may bear the iniquity of the holy things, which the children of Israel shall hallow in all their holy gifts; and it shall be always upon his forehead, that they may be accepted before the LORD.

39 And thou shalt embroider the coat of fine linen, and thou shalt make the mitre of fine linen, and thou shalt make the girdle of needlework.

40 And for Aaron’s sons thou shalt make coats, and thou shalt make for them girdles, and bonnets shalt thou make for them, for glory and for beauty.

41 And thou shalt put them upon Aaron thy brother, and his sons with him; and shalt anoint them, and consecrate them, and sanctify them, that they may minister unto me in the priest’s office.

42 And thou shalt make them linen breeches to cover their nakedness; from the loins even unto the thighs they shall reach:

43 And they shall be upon Aaron, and upon his sons, when they come in unto the tabernacle of the congregation, or when they come near unto the altar to minister in the holy place; that they bear not iniquity, and die: it shall be a statute for ever unto him and his seed after him.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Aaron and his sons are set apart for the priest's office, and their garments are described in detail: the ephod of gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen, the breastplate of judgment bearing the twelve tribes' names, the blue robe with bells and pomegranates, the mitre with its gold plate engraved Holiness to the LORD, and the coats, girdles, and linen breeches. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Take thou unto thee Aaron thy brother, and his sons with him, from among Israel, that he may minister unto me in the priest's office, even Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar. Henry notes hitherto the heads of families were priests, but now the office was confined to Aaron's family, and so continued till the gospel dispensation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Thou shalt make holy garments for Aaron thy brother for glory and for beauty. Henry observes the holy garments not only distinguished the priests from the people, but were emblems of that holy conduct which should ever be the glory and beauty of ministers of religion, and typified the glory of the Divine majesty and the beauty of complete holiness in Christ the great High Priest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Thou shalt speak unto all that are wise hearted, whom I have filled with the spirit of wisdom, that they may make Aaron's garments to consecrate him, that he may minister unto me in the priest's office. Henry notes the workmen were Spirit-filled: wisdom for holy garments was itself a gift of God, not mere craft. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. These are the garments which they shall make: a breastplate, and an ephod, and a robe, and a broidered coat, a mitre, and a girdle, and they shall make holy garments for Aaron and his sons, that he may minister unto me in the priest's office. Henry observes the full set marked the priest from head to foot, for the whole man was consecrated to service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And they shall take gold, and blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linen. Henry notes the materials matched the tabernacle itself: gold and royal colours and fine linen, for the priest's garments shared the house's glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And they shall make the ephod of gold, of blue, and of purple, of scarlet, and fine twined linen, with cunning work. Henry notes this richly-wrought ephod was the outmost garment of the high priest, a short coat without sleeves, fastened close to the body with a girdle, and as Christ's coat had no seam but was woven from the top throughout, so it was with the ephod. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. It shall have the two shoulderpieces thereof joined at the two edges thereof, and so it shall be joined together. Henry observes the shoulderpieces were buttoned together, joining the ephod front and back into one garment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And the curious girdle of the ephod, which is upon it, shall be of the same, according to the work thereof, even of gold, of blue, and purple, of scarlet, and fine twined linen. Henry notes the girdle was of the same work as the ephod, binding it close, for the priest's service required a girded, ready frame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Thou shalt take two onyx stones, and grave on them the names of the children of Israel. Henry observes the shoulder stones bore the tribes' names engraven, that Christ our High Priest might present his people before the Lord for a memorial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Six of their names on one stone, and the other six names of the rest on the other stone, according to their birth. Henry notes the division by birth order: six and six, the whole house of Israel divided evenly upon the priest's shoulders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. With the work of an engraver in stone, like the engravings of a signet, shalt thou engrave the two stones with the names of Israel, and thou shalt make them to be set in ouches of gold. Henry observes the engraving was like a signet, deep and durable, for the memorial was not to be washed off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Thou shalt put the two stones upon the shoulders of the ephod for stones of memorial unto the children of Israel, and Aaron shall bear their names before the LORD upon his two shoulders for a memorial. Henry notes the high priest bore the names on his shoulders, which reminds us of the power with which our Lord Jesus pleads for those that are his, bearing them up in his arms with almighty strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Thou shalt make ouches of gold. Henry observes the gold settings held the memorial stones, for the people's names were set in gold before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And two chains of pure gold at the ends, of wreathen work shalt thou make them, and fasten the wreathen chains to the ouches. Henry notes the wreathen chains linked the shoulder stones to the breastplate, joining strength and affection in one ornament. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Thou shalt make the breastplate of judgment with cunning work, after the work of the ephod thou shalt make it, of gold, of blue, and purple, of scarlet, and fine twined linen. Henry notes the breastplate was the chief ornament of the high priest, a rich piece of cloth curiously worked, called the breastplate of judgment because it bore the Urim and Thummim by which God's will was made known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Foursquare it shall be being doubled, a span shall be the length thereof, and a span shall be the breadth thereof. Henry observes the breastplate was doubled and foursquare, a span each way, a square of cloth folded to hold the stones and the oracle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Thou shalt set in it settings of stones, even four rows of stones: the first row shall be a sardius, a topaz, and a carbuncle. Henry notes each tribe's name was graven in a precious stone, to signify how precious in God's sight believers are, and how honourable, however small and poor the tribe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And the second row shall be an emerald, a sapphire, and a diamond. Henry observes the second row continued the pattern: every tribe, great or small, stood as a precious stone in the breastplate, and all the saints are dear to Christ, however men esteem them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And the third row a ligure, an agate, and an amethyst. Henry notes the third row added three more stones, that the twelve might be complete, no tribe missing from the priest's breast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And the fourth row a beryl, and an onyx, and a jasper, and they shall be set in gold in their inclosings. Henry observes the fourth row completed the four rows of three, each stone set in gold, for the tribes were held in gold before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And the stones shall be with the names of the children of Israel, twelve, according to their names, like the engravings of a signet, every one with his name shall they be according to the twelve tribes. Henry notes the signet engraving made each stone personal: every tribe by name, graven deep, that the priest might bear them individually. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Thou shalt make upon the breastplate chains at the ends of wreathen work of pure gold. Henry observes the wreathen chains of pure gold linked the breastplate to the ephod, that judgment and memorial might hang together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Thou shalt make upon the breastplate two rings of gold, and shalt put the two rings on the two ends of the breastplate. Henry notes the rings at the ends received the chains, small fittings for a great purpose: holding the oracle close to the priest's heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Thou shalt put the two wreathen chains of gold in the two rings which are on the ends of the breastplate. Henry observes the chains were put through the rings, fastening the breastplate to the shoulders, that it might not slip in ministry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And the other two ends of the two wreathen chains thou shalt fasten in the two ouches, and put them on the shoulderpieces of the ephod before it. Henry notes the chains ran from breastplate to shoulder ouches, joining the heart and the shoulders: love and power together in the priest's bearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Thou shalt make two rings of gold, and thou shalt put them upon the two ends of the breastplate, upon the border thereof, which is in the side of the ephod inward. Henry observes the lower rings, on the inward border, received the lace that bound the breastplate below. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And two other rings of gold thou shalt make, and shalt put them on the two sides of the ephod underneath, toward the forepart thereof, over against the other coupling thereof, above the curious girdle of the ephod. Henry notes the matching rings on the ephod, above the girdle, answered the breastplate's rings, that the two might be laced together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And they shall bind the breastplate by the rings thereof unto the rings of the ephod with a lace of blue, that it may be above the curious girdle of the ephod, and that the breastplate be not loosed from the ephod. Henry observes the blue lace bound breastplate to ephod above the girdle, that the breastplate might not be loosed: the priest's judgment and memorial were inseparable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And Aaron shall bear the names of the children of Israel in the breastplate of judgment upon his heart, when he goeth in before the LORD, for a memorial before the LORD continually. Henry observes the high priest had the names both on his shoulders and on his breast, which reminds us of the power and the love with which Christ pleads for his own: he bears them up with almighty strength and carries them in his bosom with tender affection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And thou shalt put in the breastplate of judgment the Urim and the Thummim, and they shall be upon Aaron's heart, when he goeth in before the LORD, and Aaron shall bear the judgment of the children of Israel upon his heart before the LORD continually. Henry notes the Urim and Thummim, signifying light and integrity, were put in the breastplate, by which God's will was made known in doubtful cases, and now Christ is our Oracle, the true Light and faithful Witness, from whom we receive the Spirit of Truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Thou shalt make the robe of the ephod all of blue. Henry notes the robe of the ephod was under the ephod and reached down to the knees, without sleeves, all of blue, the colour of heaven, for the priest who ministered below the veil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And there shall be a hole in the top of it, in the midst thereof, and it shall have a binding of woven work round about the hole of it, as it were the hole of an habergeon, that it be not rent. Henry observes the reinforced hole kept the robe from tearing, for the priest's garment must not be rent in ministry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And beneath upon the hem of it thou shalt make pomegranates of blue, and of purple, and of scarlet, round about the hem thereof, and bells of gold between them round about. Henry notes the golden bells, by their preciousness and pleasant sound, well represent the good profession saints make, and the pomegranates the fruit they bring forth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. A golden bell and a pomegranate, a golden bell and a pomegranate, upon the hem of the robe round about. Henry observes the alternation was exact: bell and fruit, bell and fruit, sound and fruit together, for profession without fruit is empty, and fruit without profession is silent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And it shall be upon Aaron to minister, and his sound shall be heard when he goeth in unto the holy place before the LORD, and when he cometh out, that he die not. Henry notes Aaron must minister in the garments appointed, serving the Lord with holy fear, as those who know they deserve to die, and the sound of the bells marked his going in and coming out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Thou shalt make a plate of pure gold, and grave upon it, like the engravings of a signet, Holiness to the LORD. Henry notes a golden plate was fixed on Aaron's forehead, engraven with Holiness to the Lord, reminding him that God is holy and his priests must be holy, devoted to the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Thou shalt put it on a blue lace, that it may be upon the mitre, upon the forefront of the mitre it shall be. Henry observes the plate stood on the forefront of the mitre, in open profession of relation to God, engraven like a signet, deep and durable, not painted to be washed off, and such must our holiness to the Lord be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. And it shall be upon Aaron's forehead, that Aaron may bear the iniquity of the holy things, which the children of Israel shall hallow in all their holy gifts, and it shall be always upon his forehead, that they may be accepted before the LORD. Henry notes Christ is our High Priest, and through him sins are forgiven and not laid to our charge, and our persons and doings are pleasing to God upon the account of Christ, and not otherwise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Thou shalt embroider the coat of fine linen, and thou shalt make the mitre of fine linen, and thou shalt make the girdle of needlework. Henry observes the linen coat, mitre, and girdle were the undergarments of white, the priest's purity beneath all the colour and gold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. And for Aaron's sons thou shalt make coats, and thou shalt make for them girdles, and bonnets for glory and for beauty. Henry notes the sons' garments were simpler, coats, girdles, and bonnets, yet still for glory and for beauty, for every priest shared the consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. And thou shalt put them upon Aaron thy brother, and his sons with him, and shalt anoint them, and consecrate them, and sanctify them, that they may minister unto me in the priest's office. Henry observes the putting on, anointing, consecrating, and sanctifying were all required: the garments alone did not make the priest, but God's setting apart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. And thou shalt make them linen breeches to cover their nakedness, from the loins even unto the thighs they shall reach. Henry notes the linen breeches guarded modesty in ministry, for the priest who approached the altar must be covered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. And they shall be upon Aaron, and upon his sons, when they come in unto the tabernacle of the congregation, or when they come near unto the altar to minister in the holy place, that they bear not iniquity, and die, and it shall be a statute forever unto him and his seed after him. Henry closes on the warning: the priest's garments typify the righteousness of Christ, and if we appear not before God in that, we shall bear our iniquity and die. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • ephod. The high priest's outer garment of gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen, bearing the tribes' names on its shoulder stones. Interpreted Study notes on verses 6, 9, and 12, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Urim and Thummim. Put in the breastplate of judgment; Henry notes they signify light and integrity, and by them God's will was made known. Interpreted Study note on verse 30, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry observes the fourth row completed the four rows of three, each stone set in gold, for the tribes were held in gold before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And the stones shall be with the names of the children of Israel, twelve, according to their names, like the engravings of a signet, every one with his name shall they be according to the twelve tribes. Henry notes the signet engraving made each stone personal: every tribe by name, graven deep, that the priest might bear them individually. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Thou shalt make upon the breastplate chains at the ends of wreathen work of pure gold. Henry observes the wreathen chains of pure gold linked the breastplate to the ephod, that judgment and memorial might hang together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Thou shalt make upon the breastplate two rings of gold, and shalt put the two rings on the two ends of the breastplate. Henry notes the rings at the ends received the chains, small fittings for a great purpose: holding the oracle close to the priest's heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Thou shalt put the two wreathen chains of gold in the two rings which are on the ends of the breastplate. Henry observes the chains were put through the rings, fastening the breastplate to the shoulders, that it might not slip in ministry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And the other two ends of the two wreathen chains thou shalt fasten in the two ouches, and put them on the shoulderpieces of the ephod before it. Henry notes the chains ran from breastplate to shoulder ouches, joining the heart and the shoulders: love and power together in the priest's bearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Thou shalt make two rings of gold, and thou shalt put them upon the two ends of the breastplate, upon the border thereof, which is in the side of the ephod inward. Henry observes the lower rings, on the inward border, received the lace that bound the breastplate below. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And two other rings of gold thou shalt make, and shalt put them on the two sides of the ephod underneath, toward the forepart thereof, over against the other coupling thereof, above the curious girdle of the ephod. Henry notes the matching rings on the ephod, above the girdle, answered the breastplate's rings, that the two might be laced together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And they shall bind the breastplate by the rings thereof unto the rings of the ephod with a lace of blue, that it may be above the curious girdle of the ephod, and that the breastplate be not loosed from the ephod. Henry observes the blue lace bound breastplate to ephod above the girdle, that the breastplate might not be loosed: the priest's judgment and memorial were inseparable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And Aaron shall bear the names of the children of Israel in the breastplate of judgment upon his heart, when he goeth in before the LORD, for a memorial before the LORD continually. Henry observes the high priest had the names both on his shoulders and on his breast, which reminds us of the power and the love with which Christ pleads for his own: he bears them up with almighty strength and carries them in his bosom with tender affection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And thou shalt put in the breastplate of judgment the Urim and the Thummim, and they shall be upon Aaron's heart, when he goeth in before the LORD, and Aaron shall bear the judgment of the children of Israel upon his heart before the LORD continually. Henry notes the Urim and Thummim, signifying light and integrity, were put in the breastplate, by which God's will was made known in doubtful cases, and now Christ is our Oracle, the true Light and faithful Witness, from whom we receive the Spirit of Truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Thou shalt make the robe of the ephod all of blue. Henry notes the robe of the ephod was under the ephod and reached down to the knees, without sleeves, all of blue, the colour of heaven, for the priest who ministered below the veil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And there shall be a hole in the top of it, in the midst thereof, and it shall have a binding of woven work round about the hole of it, as it were the hole of an habergeon, that it be not rent. Henry observes the reinforced hole kept the robe from tearing, for the priest's garment must not be rent in ministry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And beneath upon the hem of it thou shalt make pomegranates of blue, and of purple, and of scarlet, round about the hem thereof, and bells of gold between them round about. Henry notes the golden bells, by their preciousness and pleasant sound, well represent the good profession saints make, and the pomegranates the fruit they bring forth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. A golden bell and a pomegranate, a golden bell and a pomegranate, upon the hem of the robe round about. Henry observes the alternation was exact: bell and fruit, bell and fruit, sound and fruit together, for profession without fruit is empty, and fruit without profession is silent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And it shall be upon Aaron to minister, and his sound shall be heard when he goeth in unto the holy place before the LORD, and when he cometh out, that he die not. Henry notes Aaron must minister in the garments appointed, serving the Lord with holy fear, as those who know they deserve to die, and the sound of the bells marked his going in and coming out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Thou shalt make a plate of pure gold, and grave upon it, like the engravings of a signet, Holiness to the LORD. Henry notes a golden plate was fixed on Aaron's forehead, engraven with Holiness to the Lord, reminding him that God is holy and his priests must be holy, devoted to the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Thou shalt put it on a blue lace, that it may be upon the mitre, upon the forefront of the mitre it shall be. Henry observes the plate stood on the forefront of the mitre, in open profession of relation to God, engraven like a signet, deep and durable, not painted to be washed off, and such must our holiness to the Lord be. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. And it shall be upon Aaron's forehead, that Aaron may bear the iniquity of the holy things, which the children of Israel shall hallow in all their holy gifts, and it shall be always upon his forehead, that they may be accepted before the LORD. Henry notes Christ is our High Priest, and through him sins are forgiven and not laid to our charge, and our persons and doings are pleasing to God upon the account of Christ, and not otherwise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Thou shalt embroider the coat of fine linen, and thou shalt make the mitre of fine linen, and thou shalt make the girdle of needlework. Henry observes the linen coat, mitre, and girdle were the undergarments of white, the priest's purity beneath all the colour and gold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. And for Aaron's sons thou shalt make coats, and thou shalt make for them girdles, and bonnets for glory and for beauty. Henry notes the sons' garments were simpler, coats, girdles, and bonnets, yet still for glory and for beauty, for every priest shared the consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. And thou shalt put them upon Aaron thy brother, and his sons with him, and shalt anoint them, and consecrate them, and sanctify them, that they may minister unto me in the priest's office. Henry observes the putting on, anointing, consecrating, and sanctifying were all required: the garments alone did not make the priest, but God's setting apart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. And thou shalt make them linen breeches to cover their nakedness, from the loins even unto the thighs they shall reach. Henry notes the linen breeches guarded modesty in ministry, for the priest who approached the altar must be covered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. And they shall be upon Aaron, and upon his sons, when they come in unto the tabernacle of the congregation, or when they come near unto the altar to minister in the holy place, that they bear not iniquity, and die, and it shall be a statute forever unto him and his seed after him. Henry closes on the warning: the priest's garments typify the righteousness of Christ, and if we appear not before God in that, we shall bear our iniquity and die. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
