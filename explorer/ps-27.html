The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 27 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? the LORD is the strength of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?

2 When the wicked, even mine enemies and my foes, came upon me to eat up my flesh, they stumbled and fell.

3 Though an host should encamp against me, my heart shall not fear: though war should rise against me, in this will I be confident.

4 One thing have I desired of the LORD, that will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD, and to enquire in his temple.

5 For in the time of trouble he shall hide me in his pavilion: in the secret of his tabernacle shall he hide me; he shall set me up upon a rock.

6 And now shall mine head be lifted up above mine enemies round about me: therefore will I offer in his tabernacle sacrifices of joy; I will sing, yea, I will sing praises unto the LORD.

7 Hear, O LORD, when I cry with my voice: have mercy also upon me, and answer me.

8 When thou saidst, Seek ye my face; my heart said unto thee, Thy face, LORD, will I seek.

9 Hide not thy face far from me; put not thy servant away in anger: thou hast been my help; leave me not, neither forsake me, O God of my salvation.

10 When my father and my mother forsake me, then the LORD will take me up.

11 Teach me thy way, O LORD, and lead me in a plain path, because of mine enemies.

12 Deliver me not over unto the will of mine enemies: for false witnesses are risen up against me, and such as breathe out cruelty.

13 I had fainted, unless I had believed to see the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living.

14 Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Confidence, then supplication: the Lord is light, salvation, and strength, so hosts and war do not frighten. One desire, to dwell in God's house beholding his beauty, is joined to a cry for mercy when father and mother might forsake, false witnesses rise, and the psalm preaches to itself: wait on the Lord, be of good courage. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Lord is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? The Lord is the strength of my life; of whom shall I be afraid? Light, salvation, and strength in God answer every fear by name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When the wicked, my enemies and foes, came to eat up my flesh, they stumbled and fell: the devourers themselves fall, and their fall is the proof of God's defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Though a host encamp against me, my heart shall not fear; though war rise against me, in this I will be confident: numbers and war cannot shake confidence rooted in verse 1. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. One thing I have desired and will seek: to dwell in the house of the Lord all my days, to behold the beauty of the Lord and enquire in his temple: a single desire — God's presence, beauty, and counsel — outranks deliverance itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For in trouble he shall hide me in his pavilion, in the secret of his tabernacle hide me, and set me up on a rock: God's house is also God's hiding-place and high ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Now my head shall be lifted above my enemies round about; therefore I will offer sacrifices of joy in his tabernacle and sing praises: lifted head leads to joyful sacrifice and song. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Hear, Lord, when I cry with my voice; have mercy on me and answer me: the tone shifts from confidence to supplication, as it must in real prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. When you said, Seek my face, my heart said, Your face, Lord, will I seek: God's command and the heart's answer meet — seeking is obedience, not bargaining. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Hide not your face from me; do not put your servant away in anger; you have been my help; leave me not, nor forsake me, O God of my salvation: past help is pleaded against the fear of present abandonment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. When my father and mother forsake me, then the Lord will take me up: even the surest human bonds may fail, and God's taking-up remains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Teach me your way, Lord, and lead me in a plain path, because of my enemies: a level, plain path is prayed for precisely because enemies watch for a stumble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Deliver me not over to the will of my enemies, for false witnesses rise against me, breathing out cruelty: false witness and cruelty are the enemies' weapons, and God's withholding is the only defence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. I had fainted unless I had believed to see the goodness of the Lord in the land of the living: faith in seeing God's goodness in this life, not only the next, kept him from fainting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Wait on the Lord; be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart; wait, I say, on the Lord: the psalm ends by preaching to itself — waiting, courage, and a strengthened heart, with waiting repeated for emphasis. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
