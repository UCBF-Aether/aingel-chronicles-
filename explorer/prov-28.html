The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 28 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The wicked flee when no man pursueth: but the righteous are bold as a lion.

2 For the transgression of a land many are the princes thereof: but by a man of understanding and knowledge the state thereof shall be prolonged.

3 A poor man that oppresseth the poor is like a sweeping rain which leaveth no food.

4 They that forsake the law praise the wicked: but such as keep the law contend with them.

5 Evil men understand not judgment: but they that seek the LORD understand all things.

6 Better is the poor that walketh in his uprightness, than he that is perverse in his ways, though he be rich.

7 Whoso keepeth the law is a wise son: but he that is a companion of riotous men shameth his father.

8 He that by usury and unjust gain increaseth his substance, he shall gather it for him that will pity the poor.

9 He that turneth away his ear from hearing the law, even his prayer shall be abomination.

10 Whoso causeth the righteous to go astray in an evil way, he shall fall himself into his own pit: but the upright shall have good things in possession.

11 The rich man is wise in his own conceit; but the poor that hath understanding searcheth him out.

12 When righteous men do rejoice, there is great glory: but when the wicked rise, a man is hidden.

13 He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.

14 Happy is the man that feareth alway: but he that hardeneth his heart shall fall into mischief.

15 As a roaring lion, and a ranging bear; so is a wicked ruler over the poor people.

16 The prince that wanteth understanding is also a great oppressor: but he that hateth covetousness shall prolong his days.

17 A man that doeth violence to the blood of any person shall flee to the pit; let no man stay him.

18 Whoso walketh uprightly shall be saved: but he that is perverse in his ways shall fall at once.

19 He that tilleth his land shall have plenty of bread: but he that followeth after vain persons shall have poverty enough.

20 A faithful man shall abound with blessings: but he that maketh haste to be rich shall not be innocent.

21 To have respect of persons is not good: for for a piece of bread that man will transgress.

22 He that hasteth to be rich hath an evil eye, and considereth not that poverty shall come upon him.

23 He that rebuketh a man afterwards shall find more favour than he that flattereth with the tongue.

24 Whoso robbeth his father or his mother, and saith, It is no transgression; the same is the companion of a destroyer.

25 He that is of a proud heart stirreth up strife: but he that putteth his trust in the LORD shall be made fat.

26 He that trusteth in his own heart is a fool: but whoso walketh wisely, he shall be delivered.

27 He that giveth unto the poor shall not lack: but he that hideth his eyes shall have many a curse.

28 When the wicked rise, men hide themselves: but when they perish, the righteous increase.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The wicked flee when no one pursues, but the righteous are bold as a lion, and the chapter tests rulers, law and wealth together: many princes follow a land's transgression, the king who judges the poor faithfully has a throne established for ever, and haste to be rich brings poverty. Covering sins prospers nothing, confessing and forsaking them finds mercy, and giving to the poor means never lacking. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 17. A man who does violence to the blood of any person shall flee to the pit, let no man stay him, for the murderer shall be haunted with terrors, and none shall desire to save him from deserved punishment, nor pity him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 19. He who tills his land shall have plenty of bread, but he who follows after vain persons shall have poverty enough, for those who are diligent take the way to live comfortably. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. A faithful man shall abound with blessings, but he who makes haste to be rich shall not be innocent, for the true way to be happy is to be holy and honest, not to raise an estate suddenly without regard to right or wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. To have respect of persons is not good, for for a piece of bread that man will transgress, for judgment is perverted when anything but pure right is considered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. He who hastens to be rich has an evil eye, and considers not that poverty shall come upon him, for he who hastens to be rich never seriously thinks how quickly God may take his wealth and leave him in poverty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. He who rebukes a man afterwards shall find more favour than he who flatters with the tongue, for upon reflection, most will have a better opinion of a faithful reprover than of a soothing flatterer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Whoso robs his father or mother, and says, It is no transgression, is the companion of a destroyer, for here is the wickedness of those who think it no sin to rob their parents, by wheedling or threatening them, or by wasting what they have and running into debt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. He who is of a proud heart stirs up strife, but he who puts his trust in the LORD shall be made fat, for those who make themselves always easy live in continual dependence upon God and his grace, and live by faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. He who trusts in his own heart is a fool, but whoso walks wisely shall be delivered, for a fool trusts to his own strength, merit, and righteousness, and to his own heart, which is deceitful above all things and has often deceived him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. He who gives unto the poor shall not lack, but he who hides his eyes shall have many a curse, for a selfish man not only will not look out for objects of compassion, but will look off from those calling for his attention. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. When the wicked rise, men hide themselves, but when they perish, the righteous increase, for when power is put into the wicked's hands, wise men decline public business, and yet where at first there seems least of Christ, diligent reading will still find what leads to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
