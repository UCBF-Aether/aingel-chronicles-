The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 25 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Israel abode in Shittim, and the people began to commit whoredom with the daughters of Moab.

2 And they called the people unto the sacrifices of their gods: and the people did eat, and bowed down to their gods.

3 And Israel joined himself unto Baalpeor: and the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel.

4 And the LORD said unto Moses, Take all the heads of the people, and hang them up before the LORD against the sun, that the fierce anger of the LORD may be turned away from Israel.

5 And Moses said unto the judges of Israel, Slay ye every one his men that were joined unto Baalpeor.

6 And, behold, one of the children of Israel came and brought unto his brethren a Midianitish woman in the sight of Moses, and in the sight of all the congregation of the children of Israel, who were weeping before the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

7 And when Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest, saw it, he rose up from among the congregation, and took a javelin in his hand;

8 And he went after the man of Israel into the tent, and thrust both of them through, the man of Israel, and the woman through her belly. So the plague was stayed from the children of Israel.

9 And those that died in the plague were twenty and four thousand.

10 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

11 Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest, hath turned my wrath away from the children of Israel, while he was zealous for my sake among them, that I consumed not the children of Israel in my jealousy.

12 Wherefore say, Behold, I give unto him my covenant of peace:

13 And he shall have it, and his seed after him, even the covenant of an everlasting priesthood; because he was zealous for his God, and made an atonement for the children of Israel.

14 Now the name of the Israelite that was slain, even that was slain with the Midianitish woman, was Zimri, the son of Salu, a prince of a chief house among the Simeonites.

15 And the name of the Midianitish woman that was slain was Cozbi, the daughter of Zur; he was head over a people, and of a chief house in Midian.

16 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

17 Vex the Midianites, and smite them:

18 For they vex you with their wiles, wherewith they have beguiled you in the matter of Peor, and in the matter of Cozbi, the daughter of a prince of Midian, their sister, which was slain in the day of the plague for Peor’s sake.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the sin at Shittim and its stopping. While Israel abides in Shittim, the people commit whoredom with the daughters of Moab, join themselves to Baal-peor, and eat the sacrifices of their gods, and a plague breaks out in which twenty-four thousand die. When an Israelite brings a Midianitish woman into the camp before Moses and the weeping congregation, Phinehas son of Eleazar follows them into the tent and thrusts them both through, and the plague is stayed. For this zeal Phinehas receives the covenant of an everlasting priesthood, and Israel is commanded to vex the Midianites who beguiled them. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Israel abode in Shittim, and the people began to commit whoredom with the daughters of Moab. JFB describes Shittim as a verdant meadow named for a grove of acacia trees lining the Jordan's eastern side, and Henry warns the friendship of the wicked is more dangerous than their enmity, since none can prevail against God's people unless they are first overcome by their inbred lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. They called the people to the sacrifices of their gods, and the people did eat, and bowed down to their gods. The descent ran in steps: invitation, feasting, then worship, as the daughters' call led Israel from table fellowship to bowing before foreign gods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Israel joined himself to Baal-peor, and the anger of the Lord was kindled against Israel. JFB explains Baal means lord and Peor a mount in Moab, the idol being in reality Chemosh, whose rites were celebrated with the grossest obscenity, so Israel committed the double offence of idolatry and licentiousness, and Henry notes this sin did what all Balaam's enchantments could not: it set God against them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The Lord said to Moses, take all the heads of the people, and hang them up before the Lord against the sun, that the fierce anger of the Lord may be turned away from Israel. JFB notes Israelite criminals were first stoned or slain and then gibbeted, the persons here being the principal delinquents, subordinate officers and rulers of tens or hundreds, hung in public ignominy before the Lord to vindicate his honour, and removed toward sunset, while Henry adds ringleaders in sin ought to be made examples of justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Moses said to the judges of Israel, slay every one his men that were joined to Baal-peor. JFB identifies the judges as the seventy elders, commanded not only to superintend execution in their jurisdictions but to inflict the punishment with their own hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Behold, one of the children of Israel came and brought to his brethren a Midianitish woman in the sight of Moses, and in the sight of all the congregation, who were weeping before the door of the tabernacle. JFB notes the flagitious act most probably occurred about the time of the order and before its execution, while rulers and well-disposed persons wept at the tabernacle door, deploring the wickedness and supplicating mercy to avert impending judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. When Phinehas, son of Eleazar, son of Aaron the priest, saw it, he rose up from among the congregation and took a javelin in his hand. Henry notes Phinehas acted in the courage of zeal and faith, and warns this act can never be an example for private revenge, religious persecution, or irregular public vengeance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He went after the man of Israel into the tent, and thrust both of them through, the man of Israel and the woman through her belly, so the plague was stayed from the children of Israel. The thrust stopped the plague at its public display: open defiance in the sight of the weeping congregation was answered in the same sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Those that died in the plague were twenty and four thousand. JFB reconciles the number: only twenty-three thousand perished from pestilence itself, while Moses' twenty-four thousand includes those who died by the judges' executions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Phinehas, son of Eleazar, son of Aaron the priest, has turned my wrath away from the children of Israel, while he was zealous for my sake among them, that I consumed not the children of Israel in my jealousy. JFB calls this a signal mark of honour: the stain of blood, instead of defiling, confirmed him in office, and his posterity should continue as long as Israel's national existence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Wherefore say, behold, I give to him my covenant of peace. The covenant of peace answered the plague with peace: the zeal that stopped wrath was rewarded with a covenant whose very name is peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. And he shall have it, and his seed after him, even the covenant of an everlasting priesthood, because he was zealous for his God, and made an atonement for the children of Israel. The everlasting priesthood was tied to zeal and atonement together: Phinehas' act both avenged God and atoned for Israel, and his seed inherited the office on that ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Now the name of the Israelite that was slain, even that was slain with the Midianitish woman, was Zimri, son of Salu, a prince of a chief house among the Simeonites. JFB notes the slaughter of so high-ranking a man proves Phinehas' undaunted zeal, since there might have been numerous avengers of his blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And the name of the Midianitish woman that was slain was Cozbi, daughter of Zur; he was head over a people, and of a chief house in Midian. Both names are recorded: a Simeonite prince and a Midianite chief's daughter, high rank on both sides of the defiance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Vex the Midianites, and smite them. JFB notes the Midianites seem to have been the most guilty parties, and Henry adds God punished them with the sword of an enemy, not with the rod of a father, since we read of no Midianites dying of the plague. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. For they vex you with their wiles, wherewith they have beguiled you in the matter of Peor, and in the matter of Cozbi, daughter of a prince of Midian, their sister, which was slain in the day of the plague for Peor's sake. JFB notes that instead of open war, they plotted insidious ways to ruin Israel by idolatry and corruption, and sister here means countrywoman, while Henry warns whatever draws us to sin should be a vexation to us as a thorn in the flesh, and none will be more surely punished than those who, after Satan's example, tempt others to sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
