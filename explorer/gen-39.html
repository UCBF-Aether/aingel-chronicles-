The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 39 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Joseph was brought down to Egypt; and Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh, captain of the guard, an Egyptian, bought him of the hands of the Ishmeelites, which had brought him down thither.

2 And the LORD was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man; and he was in the house of his master the Egyptian.

3 And his master saw that the LORD was with him, and that the LORD made all that he did to prosper in his hand.

4 And Joseph found grace in his sight, and he served him: and he made him overseer over his house, and all that he had he put into his hand.

5 And it came to pass from the time that he had made him overseer in his house, and over all that he had, that the LORD blessed the Egyptian’s house for Joseph’s sake; and the blessing of the LORD was upon all that he had in the house, and in the field.

6 And he left all that he had in Joseph’s hand; and he knew not ought he had, save the bread which he did eat. And Joseph was a goodly person, and well favoured.

7 And it came to pass after these things, that his master’s wife cast her eyes upon Joseph; and she said, Lie with me.

8 But he refused, and said unto his master’s wife, Behold, my master wotteth not what is with me in the house, and he hath committed all that he hath to my hand;

9 There is none greater in this house than I; neither hath he kept back any thing from me but thee, because thou art his wife: how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?

10 And it came to pass, as she spake to Joseph day by day, that he hearkened not unto her, to lie by her, or to be with her.

11 And it came to pass about this time, that Joseph went into the house to do his business; and there was none of the men of the house there within.

12 And she caught him by his garment, saying, Lie with me: and he left his garment in her hand, and fled, and got him out.

13 And it came to pass, when she saw that he had left his garment in her hand, and was fled forth,

14 That she called unto the men of her house, and spake unto them, saying, See, he hath brought in an Hebrew unto us to mock us; he came in unto me to lie with me, and I cried with a loud voice:

15 And it came to pass, when he heard that I lifted up my voice and cried, that he left his garment with me, and fled, and got him out.

16 And she laid up his garment by her, until his lord came home.

17 And she spake unto him according to these words, saying, The Hebrew servant, which thou hast brought unto us, came in unto me to mock me:

18 And it came to pass, as I lifted up my voice and cried, that he left his garment with me, and fled out.

19 And it came to pass, when his master heard the words of his wife, which she spake unto him, saying, After this manner did thy servant to me; that his wrath was kindled.

20 And Joseph’s master took him, and put him into the prison, a place where the king’s prisoners were bound: and he was there in the prison.

21 But the LORD was with Joseph, and shewed him mercy, and gave him favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison.

22 And the keeper of the prison committed to Joseph’s hand all the prisoners that were in the prison; and whatsoever they did there, he was the doer of it.

23 The keeper of the prison looked not to any thing that was under his hand; because the LORD was with him, and that which he did, the LORD made it to prosper.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Joseph, bought by Potiphar in Egypt, prospers because the LORD is with him and is put over his master's house. He refuses his master's wife, is falsely accused by her garment, and is thrown into prison, where again the LORD is with him and the keeper puts the prisoners under his hand. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Joseph was brought down to Egypt, and Potiphar, Pharaoh's officer and captain of the guard, bought him from the Ishmaelites. JFB notes Potiphar's name means devoted to the sun, fixing his home in the Delta, and that providence drew him to Joseph so Joseph could be trained in a court-connected house for higher service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The Lord was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man in his Egyptian master's house. JFB notes Joseph, though changed in condition, was not changed in spirit: stripped of his coat, he kept his character, and in an idolater's house he still worshipped the true God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. His master saw that the Lord was with Joseph and made all he did prosper. Henry's point is that God's presence makes all we do prosperous, and a good servant can be a blessing to the place where he lives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Joseph found grace in Potiphar's sight, served him, and was made overseer of his house, with all Potiphar had put into his hand. JFB notes the observant master soon saw Joseph's superior qualities and made him his confidential servant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. From the time Joseph was made overseer, the Lord blessed the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake, in house and field alike. JFB adds that such blessing usually follows faithful service in the ordinary course of things, and even worldly masters admire religion supported by a consistent life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Potiphar left all he had in Joseph's hand and knew nothing of it except the bread he ate, and Joseph was a goodly person and well favoured. The trust was total, and the closing note about Joseph's appearance prepares the temptation that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. After these things his master's wife cast her eyes on Joseph and said, lie with me. JFB notes Egyptian women enjoyed more freedom than women in most Eastern countries then, giving her constant opportunity to meet Joseph. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Joseph refused, telling his master's wife that his master knew nothing of what was in the house with him and had committed all to his hand. The refusal appeals first to trust betrayed, before it appeals to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. None was greater in the house than Joseph, and nothing was kept from him except Potiphar's wife, so he asked how he could do this great wickedness and sin against God. JFB calls this the true principle of moral purity: sin is against God, and that argument alone is sufficient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. As she spoke to Joseph day by day, he did not listen to her, neither to lie with her nor to be with her. Henry stresses the constancy of the urging, and Joseph's refusal even to keep company that would feed the temptation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. About this time Joseph went into the house to do his business, and none of the men of the house were within. The empty house set the scene for the crisis: opportunity, privacy, and a tempter in authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. She caught him by his garment, saying, lie with me, and he left his garment in her hand, fled, and got out. Henry notes Joseph would not stay to parley with temptation but fled from it, as escaping for his life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. When she saw he had left his garment in her hand and fled forth, her disappointment turned to revenge. JFB notes she vowed revenge and accused Joseph first to the household servants, then to her lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. She called the men of her house and said Potiphar had brought in a Hebrew to mock them, claiming Joseph came to lie with her and she cried aloud. JFB calls this an affected aspersion on her husband for keeping a Hebrew, the very abomination of Egyptians, in the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. She added that when Joseph heard her lift her voice and cry, he left his garment with her and fled out. The true fact — the abandoned garment — was now retold as proof of guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. She laid up his garment by her until his lord came home. Keeping the garment preserved the false evidence for Potiphar's return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. She spoke to her lord in the same words, saying the Hebrew servant Potiphar had brought came to mock her. Henry notes it is no new thing for the best of men to be falsely accused of the worst crimes by those who are themselves the worst of criminals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. She repeated that as she lifted her voice and cried, Joseph left his garment with her and fled. The story told to the servants was now told, unchanged, to the master. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. When Potiphar heard his wife's words, his wrath was kindled. The verse records anger, not inquiry, and Henry notes Potiphar believed the accusation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Joseph's master took him and put him in the prison where the king's prisoners were bound, and he was there in the prison. JFB describes the roundhouse attached to such an officer's dwelling, partly a dungeon, where state prisoners were kept under strict watch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. But the Lord was with Joseph, showed him mercy, and gave him favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison. Henry notes that those with a good conscience in prison have a good God there, and JFB suggests the keeper may already have known Joseph's innocence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The keeper committed all the prisoners to Joseph's hand, and whatever they did there, Joseph was the doer of it. Trust returned to Joseph even in prison, as it had in Potiphar's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The keeper looked not to anything under Joseph's hand, because the Lord was with him and made what he did prosper. Henry closes the chapter with the lesson that a good man will do good wherever he is, and will be a blessing even in bonds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
