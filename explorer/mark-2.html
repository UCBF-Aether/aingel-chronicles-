The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And again he entered into Capernaum after some days; and it was noised that he was in the house.

2 And straightway many were gathered together, insomuch that there was no room to receive them, no, not so much as about the door: and he preached the word unto them.

3 And they come unto him, bringing one sick of the palsy, which was borne of four.

4 And when they could not come nigh unto him for the press, they uncovered the roof where he was: and when they had broken it up, they let down the bed wherein the sick of the palsy lay.

5 When Jesus saw their faith, he said unto the sick of the palsy, Son, thy sins be forgiven thee.

6 But there was certain of the scribes sitting there, and reasoning in their hearts,

7 Why doth this man thus speak blasphemies? who can forgive sins but God only?

8 And immediately when Jesus perceived in his spirit that they so reasoned within themselves, he said unto them, Why reason ye these things in your hearts?

9 Whether is it easier to say to the sick of the palsy, Thy sins be forgiven thee; or to say, Arise, and take up thy bed, and walk?

10 But that ye may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins, (he saith to the sick of the palsy,)

11 I say unto thee, Arise, and take up thy bed, and go thy way into thine house.

12 And immediately he arose, took up the bed, and went forth before them all; insomuch that they were all amazed, and glorified God, saying, We never saw it on this fashion.

13 And he went forth again by the sea side; and all the multitude resorted unto him, and he taught them.

14 And as he passed by, he saw Levi the son of Alphaeus sitting at the receipt of custom, and said unto him, Follow me. And he arose and followed him.

15 And it came to pass, that, as Jesus sat at meat in his house, many publicans and sinners sat also together with Jesus and his disciples: for there were many, and they followed him.

16 And when the scribes and Pharisees saw him eat with publicans and sinners, they said unto his disciples, How is it that he eateth and drinketh with publicans and sinners?

17 When Jesus heard it, he saith unto them, They that are whole have no need of the physician, but they that are sick: I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.

18 And the disciples of John and of the Pharisees used to fast: and they come and say unto him, Why do the disciples of John and of the Pharisees fast, but thy disciples fast not?

19 And Jesus said unto them, Can the children of the bridechamber fast, while the bridegroom is with them? as long as they have the bridegroom with them, they cannot fast.

20 But the days will come, when the bridegroom shall be taken away from them, and then shall they fast in those days.

21 No man also seweth a piece of new cloth on an old garment: else the new piece that filled it up taketh away from the old, and the rent is made worse.

22 And no man putteth new wine into old bottles: else the new wine doth burst the bottles, and the wine is spilled, and the bottles will be marred: but new wine must be put into new bottles.

23 And it came to pass, that he went through the corn fields on the sabbath day; and his disciples began, as they went, to pluck the ears of corn.

24 And the Pharisees said unto him, Behold, why do they on the sabbath day that which is not lawful?

25 And he said unto them, Have ye never read what David did, when he had need, and was an hungred, he, and they that were with him?

26 How he went into the house of God in the days of Abiathar the high priest, and did eat the shewbread, which is not lawful to eat but for the priests, and gave also to them which were with him?

27 And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:

28 Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Back in Capernaum, Jesus forgives and then heals a paralyzed man let down through the roof, a claim of authority that angers the scribes (vv. 1–12). He calls Levi the tax collector and eats with publicans and sinners, defending mercy for the sick rather than the well (vv. 13–17). Questions about fasting lead to the bridegroom, the new cloth, and the new wine (vv. 18–22), and two Sabbath disputes — disciples plucking grain, answered from David, and the declaration that the Sabbath was made for man — close the chapter (vv. 23–28). What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Again Jesus entered Capernaum, and it was noised that he was in the house. His return to a known house drew crowds as surely as his miracles did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Straightway many gathered, so there was no room, not even about the door, and he preached the word to them. Christ used the crowd's attention for teaching, not only for healing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. They brought to him one sick of the palsy, borne of four. Four bearers sharing one burden show how faith works by the help of friends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Unable to come near for the press, they uncovered the roof and let down the bed where the palsied man lay. Faith finds a way through obstacles and will break through roofs rather than miss Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the sick man, Son, thy sins be forgiven thee. He first heals the soul's disease, which is the root of misery, before healing the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Certain scribes sat there reasoning in their hearts against him. Silent inward reasoning does not escape Christ, and unbelief often hides behind inward objection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They reasoned, why does this man speak blasphemies, for who can forgive sins but God only? Their premise was true, but they refused the conclusion it pointed to: that this man was God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Jesus immediately perceived in his spirit what they reasoned within themselves and asked why they reasoned so in their hearts. He proves his divine knowledge by reading thoughts, and answers objections before they are spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. He asked whether it is easier to say, thy sins be forgiven, or, arise, take up thy bed, and walk. Forgiveness is unseen, so he will prove it by a visible work that only God can do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. That they might know the Son of man has power on earth to forgive sins, he turned to the palsied man. The miracle is given as evidence, not as a display: it seals the pardon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. He said, arise, take up thy bed, and go into thine house. Strength returns with the command, and the bed that carried him becomes the proof he carries home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Immediately he arose, took up his bed, and went forth before them all, so that all were amazed and glorified God, saying, we never saw it on this fashion. A cure so public and complete left even onlookers glorifying God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He went forth again by the sea side, and all the multitude resorted to him, and he taught them. Christ sought the open places where crowds could hear, and teaching remained his work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Passing by, he saw Levi son of Alphaeus sitting at the receipt of custom, and said, Follow me, and he arose and followed him. Grace calls men in the very place of their worldly business, and Levi's rise was as ready as the fishermen's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. As Jesus sat at meat in his house, many publicans and sinners sat with him and his disciples, for there were many who followed him. Christ's table fellowship with outcasts was evangelism, not approval of their sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The scribes and Pharisees asked his disciples how he could eat and drink with publicans and sinners. Their complaint was aimed at the disciples because they dared not yet attack Christ directly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Jesus answered, the whole have no need of the physician, but the sick: I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance. He came as a physician to the diseased, and self-righteous men who feel no sickness refuse the cure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The disciples of John and of the Pharisees fasted, and people asked why Jesus' disciples did not fast. The question confuses the discipline of one season with the duty of another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Jesus answered, can the children of the bridechamber fast while the bridegroom is with them? Fasting suits mourning, not a wedding; Christ's presence was a feast to his disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. But days will come when the bridegroom is taken away, and then they shall fast in those days. Mourning will have its season after his departure, and fasting then will be fitting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. No man sews new cloth on an old garment, else the new piece takes away from the old and the rent is made worse. New gospel duties cannot be patched onto old ceremonial frames without tearing both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. No man puts new wine into old bottles, else the wine bursts the bottles and is spilled; new wine must go into new bottles. The gospel's living power needs vessels fitted to it, not rigid old forms that cannot hold it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. He went through the corn fields on the sabbath, and his disciples began to pluck ears of corn as they went. Hunger on a journey, not harvest work, explains their act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The Pharisees said, behold, why do they on the sabbath what is not lawful? They turned a work of necessity into a charge of sabbath-breaking by their traditions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Jesus asked, have you never read what David did when he had need and was hungry, he and those with him? Scripture itself supplies the precedent that necessity overrides ceremony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. David went into the house of God in the days of Abiathar the high priest, ate the shewbread, lawful only for priests, and gave it to those with him. God himself accepted David's necessity, and the Pharisees' rule condemned what God had allowed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. He said, the sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath. The sabbath is a gift for man's rest and worship, not a yoke to starve him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath. As Lord of the institution, Christ interprets its true use and frees it from the elders' additions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
