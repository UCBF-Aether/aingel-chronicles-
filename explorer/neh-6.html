The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Nehemiah 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now it came to pass when Sanballat, and Tobiah, and Geshem the Arabian, and the rest of our enemies, heard that I had builded the wall, and that there was no breach left therein; (though at that time I had not set up the doors upon the gates;)

2 That Sanballat and Geshem sent unto me, saying, Come, let us meet together in some one of the villages in the plain of Ono. But they thought to do me mischief.

3 And I sent messengers unto them, saying, I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down: why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?

4 Yet they sent unto me four times after this sort; and I answered them after the same manner.

5 Then sent Sanballat his servant unto me in like manner the fifth time with an open letter in his hand;

6 Wherein was written, It is reported among the heathen, and Gashmu saith it, that thou and the Jews think to rebel: for which cause thou buildest the wall, that thou mayest be their king, according to these words.

7 And thou hast also appointed prophets to preach of thee at Jerusalem, saying, There is a king in Judah: and now shall it be reported to the king according to these words. Come now therefore, and let us take counsel together.

8 Then I sent unto him, saying, There are no such things done as thou sayest, but thou feignest them out of thine own heart.

9 For they all made us afraid, saying, Their hands shall be weakened from the work, that it be not done. Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands.

10 Afterward I came unto the house of Shemaiah the son of Delaiah the son of Mehetabeel, who was shut up; and he said, Let us meet together in the house of God, within the temple, and let us shut the doors of the temple: for they will come to slay thee; yea, in the night will they come to slay thee.

11 And I said, Should such a man as I flee? and who is there, that, being as I am, would go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in.

12 And, lo, I perceived that God had not sent him; but that he pronounced this prophecy against me: for Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him.

13 Therefore was he hired, that I should be afraid, and do so, and sin, and that they might have matter for an evil report, that they might reproach me.

14 My God, think thou upon Tobiah and Sanballat according to these their works, and on the prophetess Noadiah, and the rest of the prophets, that would have put me in fear.

15 So the wall was finished in the twenty and fifth day of the month Elul, in fifty and two days.

16 And it came to pass, that when all our enemies heard thereof, and all the heathen that were about us saw these things, they were much cast down in their own eyes: for they perceived that this work was wrought of our God.

17 Moreover in those days the nobles of Judah sent many letters unto Tobiah, and the letters of Tobiah came unto them.

18 For there were many in Judah sworn unto him, because he was the son in law of Shechaniah the son of Arah; and his son Johanan had taken the daughter of Meshullam the son of Berechiah.

19 Also they reported his good deeds before me, and uttered my words to him. And Tobiah sent letters to put me in fear.

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

With the wall built and no breach left, though the doors are not yet set up, the enemies change tactics: repeated invitations to meet in the plain of Ono, an open letter accusing Nehemiah of plotting kingship, a hired prophet urging him to hide in the temple, and letters from Tobiah to put him in fear. Nehemiah refuses each trap, answers that he will not flee into the temple to save his life, and the wall is finished in fifty-two days. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Plain of Ono. the place of the enemies' repeated invitations, a meeting Nehemiah refuses as a trap to stop the work (verse 2). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Shemaiah. the shut-up prophet hired to frighten Nehemiah into the temple, which would have given his enemies matter for an evil report (verses 10-13). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem, and the other enemies heard the wall was built with no breach left, though the doors were not yet hung on the gates, they knew they must act now or never. Henry notes they could not attack the well-guarded Nehemiah directly, so they turned to wheedling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. They sent word to meet together in one of the villages in the plain of Ono, pretending to consult about common interests, but intending mischief. Henry observes the subtlety: a village, not a city, lest suspicion arise that they meant to seize him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Nehemiah answered by messengers: "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down; why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?" Henry's rule: compliment must always give way to business, and idle meetings must not pull us from work we must not neglect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They sent the same solicitation four times, and he answered after the same manner each time. Henry notes we must never be overcome by importunity to do anything sinful or imprudent, but resist the same temptation with the same reason and resolution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Then Sanballat sent his servant a fifth time with an open letter in his hand. Henry notes the open letter was meant to suggest the charge was generally known and talked of, not a private whisper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The letter claimed it was reported among the nations, and Gashmu would aver it, that Nehemiah and the Jews meant to rebel, that he built the wall to be their king, and had appointed prophets to preach a king in Judah. Henry calls this the old trick: representing the malice of a few as the sense of the many. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The letter closed by urging Nehemiah to come and take counsel together to quell the report. Henry exposes the lure: words softer than oil with war in the heart, hoping either to draw him off his work or to frighten him into staying his proceedings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Nehemiah sent back that no such things were done as claimed; Sanballat feigned them out of his own heart. Henry notes he denied not only that the charges were true but that they were even reported, and trusted God with his good name rather than omitting known duty for fear of misconstruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. He saw their aim: to make them afraid and weaken their hands so the work would not be done. His answer was prayer: "Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands." Henry calls this the great relief of good people in every strait: to fetch grace by faith and prayer when enemies fill them with fears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. He then visited Shemaiah son of Delaiah, who was shut up in his house and proposed they meet in the temple, shut its doors, for enemies would come by night to slay Nehemiah. Henry notes the plausible pretence: a strong, sacred refuge, with concern for his safety as its cover. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Nehemiah refused: "Should such a man as I flee? and who is there, that, being as I am, would go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in." Henry's lesson: when tempted, remember who and what you are, and let holy courage refuse both to decline good work and to do a bad thing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. He perceived God had not sent Shemaiah, who pronounced this prophecy against him because Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him. Henry notes the wickedness of mercenary prophets who betray God's cause under a pretence of communion with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The hireling's purpose was that Nehemiah should be afraid, do so, and sin, giving his enemies matter for an evil report to reproach him. Henry observes the devil's own pattern: tempt men to sin so as to accuse them and glory in their shame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Nehemiah committed the cause to God: think upon Tobiah and Sanballat according to their works, and upon the prophetess Noadiah and the other prophets who would put him in fear. Henry notes he prescribed no revenge, but referred the matter to the Judge who knows hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The wall was finished on the twenty-fifth day of Elul, in fifty-two days. Henry marvels at how much work earnest, cheerful hands can do in a little time, noting the threats meant to weaken them only quickened them to finish before the enemy came. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. When the enemies and surrounding heathen heard, they were much cast down in their own eyes, for they perceived the work was wrought of God. Henry notes even heathens could see a special providence in the church's prosperity, and conclude that what is of God will prevail. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Even then, nobles of Judah kept up a treacherous correspondence, sending many letters to Tobiah and receiving his. Henry laments that great men are not always wise or honest, and that nobles of Judah could do so mean a thing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Many in Judah were sworn to Tobiah because he was son-in-law to Shechaniah son of Arah, and his son Jehohanan had married the daughter of Meshullam son of Berechiah. Henry warns of the mischief of marrying strangers: a sinful love leads to a sinful league. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. They reported Tobiah's good deeds before Nehemiah and carried Nehemiah's words to him, while Tobiah sent letters to put Nehemiah in fear. Henry notes the double treachery: praising the wicked to his face and betraying his counsels behind his back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
