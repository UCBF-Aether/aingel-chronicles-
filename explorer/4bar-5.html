The Text — 4 Baruch 5 (Ge'ez text (project Ge'ez corpus))

1 And Abimelech took the figs at the time of midday, as Jeremiah had sent him [?]; and he found a shady tree [?], and abode and rested, to take a little rest; and he leaned his head upon the basket of figs, and slept sixty and six years, and awoke not from his sleep.

2 And after a time he arose and awoke from his sleep, and said: I have slept sweetly [?] a little while; for my head is heavy, and I have not had my fill of sleep.

3 And he uncovered that basket of figs, and found the figs fresh, and their milk dripping.

4 And he desired to sleep again, for his head was heavy, and he was not sated with slumber; and he said: I fear lest I sleep and be late, lest my father Jeremiah rebuke me; for he sent me early in the morning. And now I will arise and go; for is there not heat, is there not toil, every day? [?]

5 And he arose, and took up the basket of his figs, and entered into the city Jerusalem; and he knew not the city, nor his house. And he said: Blessed art thou, O Lord; for a great trance is fallen upon me.

6 And he said: Is this indeed the city Jerusalem? I have surely gone astray [?], for I came by the mountain way; and if my head be heavy and I have not had my fill of sleep, my heart is perplexed within me [?]; and how shall I declare this thing unto Jeremiah, that the city is hidden from me?

7 And he sought out all the landmarks that were in the city, to know whether this were Jerusalem. And he went again into the city, and sought if he knew any, and found none. And he said: Blessed art thou, O Lord; for a great trance is fallen upon me. And he went again out of the city afar off, and abode mourning; and whither he would go, he knew not.

8 And he set down that basket of figs, and said: I will abide here, until the Lord take this trance from me. And after he had sat, he saw an old man coming from the field. And Abimelech said unto him: Unto thee I speak, thou old man: which is this city? And the old man said unto him: This is Jerusalem.

9 And Abimelech said unto him: Where is Jeremiah the priest, and Baruch the Levite [?], and all the people of this city? for I have found none. And the old man said unto him: Art thou not of this city? and dost thou remember Jeremiah even now, that thou askest concerning him after all this time?

10 But Jeremiah is in Babylon with the people; for they were taken captive, and delivered into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar the king of Persia [?]; and he went thither to prophesy unto them.

11 And when Abimelech heard this from the old man, Abimelech said unto him: If thou wert not an old man, I would laugh thee to scorn [?]; but it is not meet that men should mock an old man; and were it not so, [I would shew thee] [?].

12 But as for that thou sayest, that the people were taken captive into Babylon — though the floods of heaven [?] had descended upon them, there hath not been time for them to go into Babylon; and thou sayest the people were taken captive into Babylon!

13 And I, as my father Jeremiah sent me, went into the vineyard of Agrippa for a few figs, to give unto the sick that are among the people. I went and came thither, and took that which he commanded me, and returned; and as I went I found a tree, and abode under it to rest, for it was midday; and there I leaned upon the basket of figs, and slept; and I awoke, thinking I had been late, and uncovered this basket of figs, and found them dripping with milk, as when I gathered them, my chosen ones [?]. And thou sayest the people were taken captive into Babylon!

14 But behold, see that my figs are not withered. And he uncovered for him the basket of figs, and shewed him; and the old man looked, and lo, the figs were fresh, and their milk dripping.

15 Then that old man marvelled, and said unto Abimelech: Thou art righteous, my son; for God would not that thou shouldest see the destruction of the city, and God brought this trance upon thee, and kept thee alive. Behold, it is sixty and six years this day since the people were taken captive into Babylon.

16 And if thou wilt know and be sure, my son, look and see in thy field that the crops have not put forth their increase [?], and that it is not the season for my figs; and know that the time is not the time for all this.

17 Then Abimelech cried with a loud voice, saying: I bless thee, O Lord my God; the God of heaven and earth; the rest of the souls of the righteous in all the world [?].

18 And he said unto the old man: What month is this? And he said unto him: The tenth and second [?] of the month Nisan, which is Miyazya. And after this Abimelech gave unto that old man of these figs, and said unto him: God lighten thy way unto the city above, Jerusalem.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Abimelech, sent for figs at midday, finds a shady tree, leans his head on his basket, and sleeps — for sixty-six years. Waking, he thinks he has slept only a little while, his head still heavy, and uncovers the basket to find the figs fresh. The chapter is the book's great sign that time itself has been suspended for the sleeper while Jerusalem's exile ran its course. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
