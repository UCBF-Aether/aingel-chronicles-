The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 27 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And David said in his heart, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul: there is nothing better for me than that I should speedily escape into the land of the Philistines; and Saul shall despair of me, to seek me any more in any coast of Israel: so shall I escape out of his hand.

2 And David arose, and he passed over with the six hundred men that were with him unto Achish, the son of Maoch, king of Gath.

3 And David dwelt with Achish at Gath, he and his men, every man with his household, even David with his two wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess, and Abigail the Carmelitess, Nabal’s wife.

4 And it was told Saul that David was fled to Gath: and he sought no more again for him.

5 And David said unto Achish, If I have now found grace in thine eyes, let them give me a place in some town in the country, that I may dwell there: for why should thy servant dwell in the royal city with thee?

6 Then Achish gave him Ziklag that day: wherefore Ziklag pertaineth unto the kings of Judah unto this day.

7 And the time that David dwelt in the country of the Philistines was a full year and four months.

8 And David and his men went up, and invaded the Geshurites, and the Gezrites, and the Amalekites: for those nations were of old the inhabitants of the land, as thou goest to Shur, even unto the land of Egypt.

9 And David smote the land, and left neither man nor woman alive, and took away the sheep, and the oxen, and the asses, and the camels, and the apparel, and returned, and came to Achish.

10 And Achish said, Whither have ye made a road to day? And David said, Against the south of Judah, and against the south of the Jerahmeelites, and against the south of the Kenites.

11 And David saved neither man nor woman alive, to bring tidings to Gath, saying, Lest they should tell on us, saying, So did David, and so will be his manner all the while he dwelleth in the country of the Philistines.

12 And Achish believed David, saying, He hath made his people Israel utterly to abhor him; therefore he shall be my servant for ever.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

David says in his heart, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul, and there is nothing better for me than to escape speedily into the land of the Philistines, so that Saul shall despair of seeking me any more in Israel. He passes over with his six hundred men and their households to Achish son of Maoch, king of Gath; Saul hears David is fled to Gath and seeks no more again for him. David asks for a town in the country rather than dwelling in the royal city, and Achish gives him Ziklag, which pertains to the kings of Judah to this day, where he dwells a full year and four months. From Ziklag he raids the Geshurites, the Gezrites and the Amalekites, old inhabitants of the land toward Shur and Egypt, leaving neither man nor woman alive to bring tidings to Gath, and tells Achish his road was against the south of Judah, the Jerahmeelites and the Kenites, so that Achish believes him, saying, he has made his people Israel utterly to abhor him, therefore he shall be my servant for ever, an equivocation which, the notes warn, serves the purpose of a lie. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David said in his heart, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul; there is nothing better for me than that I should speedily escape into the land of the Philistines, and Saul shall despair of seeking me any more in Israel, so shall I escape out of his hand. Unbelief is a sin that easily besets even good men, Henry observes, when without are fightings and within are fears; Lord, increase our faith! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. David arose and passed over with the six hundred men that were with him unto Achish son of Maoch, king of Gath. The fugitive crossed the border with his whole band. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. David dwelt with Achish at Gath, he and his men, every man with his household, even David with his two wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess and Abigail the Carmelitess, Nabal's wife. We may blush to think, Henry says, that the word of a Philistine should go further than the word of an Israelite, and that Gath should be a refuge for a good man when Israel's cities refused him safe abode. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. It was told Saul that David was fled to Gath, and he sought no more again for him. The flight across the border ended the hunt at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. David said to Achish, If I have now found grace in thine eyes, let them give me a place in some town in the country, that I may dwell there; for why should thy servant dwell in the royal city with thee? A separate town would free both guest and host from the court's watchfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Then Achish gave him Ziklag that day; wherefore Ziklag pertaineth unto the kings of Judah unto this day. David gained a comfortable settlement, Henry notes, not only at a distance from Gath but bordering upon Israel, where he might keep up correspondence with his own countrymen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The time that David dwelt in the country of the Philistines was a full year and four months. Sixteen months of exile in the enemy's land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. David and his men went up and invaded the Geshurites, the Gezrites, and the Amalekites, for those nations were of old the inhabitants of the land, as thou goest to Shur, even unto Egypt. While David was in Philistine land, Henry notes, he attacked some remains of the devoted nations; the people he cut off were long before doomed to destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. David smote the land and left neither man nor woman alive, and took away sheep, oxen, asses, camels, and apparel, and returned and came to Achish. It is often wisdom to shun public notice, Henry observes, but we must in no situation be idle; we must always try to do somewhat in God's cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Achish said, Whither have ye made a road today? And David said, Against the south of Judah, and against the south of the Jerahmeelites, and against the south of the Kenites. David's answer pointed his raids at Judah's own neighbours, concealing their true targets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. David saved neither man nor woman alive, to bring tidings to Gath, saying, Lest they should tell on us, saying, So did David, and so will be his manner all the while he dwelleth in the country of the Philistines. No witness survived to correct the story Achish was told. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Achish believed David, saying, He hath made his people Israel utterly to abhor him; therefore he shall be my servant for ever. But an equivocation which serves the purpose of a lie, Henry warns, is as like to it as a hypocrite is to a profane person — only better in appearance, therefore more dangerous. Yet what a train of evils follows from unbelief, he adds: when we forget the Lord's past mercies and gracious assurances, we are overwhelmed with desponding fears and led to dishonourable methods; nothing so effectually establishes us as firm, unshaken dependence upon God's promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
