The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The proverbs of Solomon. A wise son maketh a glad father: but a foolish son is the heaviness of his mother.

2 Treasures of wickedness profit nothing: but righteousness delivereth from death.

3 The LORD will not suffer the soul of the righteous to famish: but he casteth away the substance of the wicked.

4 He becometh poor that dealeth with a slack hand: but the hand of the diligent maketh rich.

5 He that gathereth in summer is a wise son: but he that sleepeth in harvest is a son that causeth shame.

6 Blessings are upon the head of the just: but violence covereth the mouth of the wicked.

7 The memory of the just is blessed: but the name of the wicked shall rot.

8 The wise in heart will receive commandments: but a prating fool shall fall.

9 He that walketh uprightly walketh surely: but he that perverteth his ways shall be known.

10 He that winketh with the eye causeth sorrow: but a prating fool shall fall.

11 The mouth of a righteous man is a well of life: but violence covereth the mouth of the wicked.

12 Hatred stirreth up strifes: but love covereth all sins.

13 In the lips of him that hath understanding wisdom is found: but a rod is for the back of him that is void of understanding.

14 Wise men lay up knowledge: but the mouth of the foolish is near destruction.

15 The rich man’s wealth is his strong city: the destruction of the poor is their poverty.

16 The labour of the righteous tendeth to life: the fruit of the wicked to sin.

17 He is in the way of life that keepeth instruction: but he that refuseth reproof erreth.

18 He that hideth hatred with lying lips, and he that uttereth a slander, is a fool.

19 In the multitude of words there wanteth not sin: but he that refraineth his lips is wise.

20 The tongue of the just is as choice silver: the heart of the wicked is little worth.

21 The lips of the righteous feed many: but fools die for want of wisdom.

22 The blessing of the LORD, it maketh rich, and he addeth no sorrow with it.

23 It is as sport to a fool to do mischief: but a man of understanding hath wisdom.

24 The fear of the wicked, it shall come upon him: but the desire of the righteous shall be granted.

25 As the whirlwind passeth, so is the wicked no more: but the righteous is an everlasting foundation.

26 As vinegar to the teeth, and as smoke to the eyes, so is the sluggard to them that send him.

27 The fear of the LORD prolongeth days: but the years of the wicked shall be shortened.

28 The hope of the righteous shall be gladness: but the expectation of the wicked shall perish.

29 The way of the LORD is strength to the upright: but destruction shall be to the workers of iniquity.

30 The righteous shall never be removed: but the wicked shall not inhabit the earth.

31 The mouth of the just bringeth forth wisdom: but the froward tongue shall be cut out.

32 The lips of the righteous know what is acceptable: but the mouth of the wicked speaketh frowardness.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The first collection of short contrasting proverbs begins: a wise son gladdens his father, a foolish son grieves his mother, and saying after saying turns on speech, diligence, wealth and integrity. Treasures of wickedness profit nothing, a slack hand makes poor, the mouth of the righteous is a well of life, love covers sins, and the righteous, like an everlasting foundation, stands when the whirlwind passes. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. A wise son makes a glad father while a foolish son is his mother's heaviness, for the comfort of parents much depends on their children, suggesting motives to duty for both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Treasures of wickedness profit nothing, but righteousness delivers from death, for though the righteous may be poor, the Lord will not suffer him to want what is needful for spiritual life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The LORD will not suffer the righteous soul to famish, but casts away the substance of the wicked, for the poor righteous man is supplied with what spiritual life needs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. A slack hand makes poor, but the diligent hand makes rich, for those fervent in spirit, serving the Lord, are likely to be rich in faith and in good works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Gathering in summer marks the wise son, while sleeping in harvest shames, for here is just blame of those who trifle away opportunities both for here and hereafter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Blessings are upon the head of the just, while violence covers the wicked mouth, for abundance of real blessings shall abide on good men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The memory of the just is blessed, but the name of the wicked shall rot, for both just and wicked must die, yet between their souls there is a vast difference. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The wise in heart receives commandments, while a prating fool shall fall, for the wise in heart puts his knowledge into practice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. He who walks uprightly walks surely, but the perverter of his ways shall be known, for dissemblers, after all their shuffling, will be exposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Winking with the eye causes sorrow, while a prating fool shall fall, for trick and artifice will be no excuse for iniquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The righteous mouth is a well of life, while violence covers the wicked mouth, for the good man's mouth is always open to teach, comfort, and correct others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Hatred stirs up strifes, but love covers all sins, for where hatred reigns everything stirs strife, while bearing with each other preserves peace and harmony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Wisdom is found in the lips of the understanding, but a rod is for the back of him void of understanding, for those who foolishly go on in wicked ways prepare rods for themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Wise men lay up knowledge, but the foolish mouth is near destruction, for useful knowledge must be laid up so it is not to seek when wanted, gained by reading, hearing, meditation, prayer, and faith in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The rich man's wealth is his strong city, while the poor's destruction is their poverty, reflecting common mistakes about outward condition: wealth exposes to dangers, while a content poor man with a good conscience lives comfortably by faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The labour of the righteous tends to life, while the fruit of the wicked is sin, for even a righteous man with no more than hard work earns finds that labour tending to life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. He who keeps instruction is in the way of life, but he who refuses reproof errs, like a traveller who has missed his way and cannot bear to be told and shown the right way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Hiding hatred with lying lips and uttering slander mark a fool, for he is especially a fool who thinks to hide anything from God, and malice is no better. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. In the multitude of words sin is not lacking, but he who refrains his lips is wise, for those who speak much speak much amiss, and he who checks himself consults his own peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. The tongue of the just is choice silver, while the wicked heart is little worth, for the just tongue is sincere, freed from guile and evil design. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. The lips of the righteous feed many, but fools die for want of wisdom, for pious discourse is spiritual food to the needy, and fools die for want of a heart, that is, for want of thought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. The blessing of the LORD makes rich and adds no sorrow, for truly desirable wealth has no vexation in enjoyment, no grief in loss, no guilt in abuse, since what comes from God's love has God's grace as companion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. It is sport to a fool to do mischief, but a man of understanding has wisdom, for only foolish and wicked men divert themselves with harming others or tempting to sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. The fear of the wicked shall come upon him, but the desire of the righteous shall be granted, for the largest desire of eternal blessings the righteous can form will be granted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. As the whirlwind passes, so the wicked is no more, but the righteous is an everlasting foundation, for the course of prosperous sinners soon spends itself and is gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. As vinegar to teeth and smoke to eyes, so is the sluggard to those who send him, vexing his employer as vinegar sets teeth on edge and smoke makes eyes smart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. The fear of the LORD prolongs days, but the years of the wicked shall be shortened, for he who loves life should fear God, securing life enough here and eternal life hereafter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. The hope of the righteous shall be gladness, but the expectation of the wicked shall perish, for fearing God secures life in this world and eternal life in the other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. The way of the LORD is strength to the upright, but destruction to workers of iniquity, for the believer grows stronger in faith and obeys with increased delight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. The righteous shall never be removed, but the wicked shall not inhabit the earth, for the wicked would gladly make earth their home for ever, but they must die and leave their idols behind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. The mouth of the just brings forth wisdom, but the froward tongue shall be cut out, for a good man discourses wisely for others' benefit, while the wicked speaks what displeases God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. The lips of the righteous know what is acceptable, but the wicked mouth speaks frowardness, and the righteous is kept by God's power, inseparable from his love in Christ Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
