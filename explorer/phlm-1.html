The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Philemon 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Paul, a prisoner of Jesus Christ, and Timothy our brother, unto Philemon our dearly beloved, and fellowlabourer,

2 And to our beloved Apphia, and Archippus our fellowsoldier, and to the church in thy house:

3 Grace to you, and peace, from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

4 I thank my God, making mention of thee always in my prayers,

5 Hearing of thy love and faith, which thou hast toward the Lord Jesus, and toward all saints;

6 That the communication of thy faith may become effectual by the acknowledging of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus.

7 For we have great joy and consolation in thy love, because the bowels of the saints are refreshed by thee, brother.

8 Wherefore, though I might be much bold in Christ to enjoin thee that which is convenient,

9 Yet for love’s sake I rather beseech thee, being such an one as Paul the aged, and now also a prisoner of Jesus Christ.

10 I beseech thee for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds:

11 Which in time past was to thee unprofitable, but now profitable to thee and to me:

12 Whom I have sent again: thou therefore receive him, that is, mine own bowels:

13 Whom I would have retained with me, that in thy stead he might have ministered unto me in the bonds of the gospel:

14 But without thy mind would I do nothing; that thy benefit should not be as it were of necessity, but willingly.

15 For perhaps he therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever;

16 Not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved, specially to me, but how much more unto thee, both in the flesh, and in the Lord?

17 If thou count me therefore a partner, receive him as myself.

18 If he hath wronged thee, or oweth thee ought, put that on mine account;

19 I Paul have written it with mine own hand, I will repay it: albeit I do not say to thee how thou owest unto me even thine own self besides.

20 Yea, brother, let me have joy of thee in the Lord: refresh my bowels in the Lord.

21 Having confidence in thy obedience I wrote unto thee, knowing that thou wilt also do more than I say.

22 But withal prepare me also a lodging: for I trust that through your prayers I shall be given unto you.

23 There salute thee Epaphras, my fellowprisoner in Christ Jesus;

24 Marcus, Aristarchus, Demas, Lucas, my fellowlabourers.

25 The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul, a prisoner of Jesus Christ, writes with Timothy to Philemon, Apphia, Archippus and the church in Philemon's house, thanking God for Philemon's love and faith, which refresh the saints. Though he could command, Paul instead appeals in love for Onesimus, his child begotten in bonds, once unprofitable to Philemon but now profitable to both, sending him back no longer merely as a servant but as a beloved brother, and offering to repay anything Onesimus owes. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul, a prisoner of Jesus Christ, and Timothy our brother, unto Philemon our dearly beloved, and fellowlabourer, for faith in Christ and love to him should unite saints more closely than any outward relation can unite the people of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. And to our beloved Apphia, and Archippus our fellowsoldier, and to the church in thy house, for faith in Christ and love to him should unite saints more closely than any outward relation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Grace to you, and peace, from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, for grace is the best wish for ourselves and others, with which the apostle begins and ends, and all grace is from Christ, who purchased and bestows it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. I thank my God, making mention of thee always in my prayers, for Paul in his private prayers was particular in remembering his friends, and we must remember Christian friends much and often, bearing them in our thoughts and upon our hearts before our God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Hearing of thy love and faith, which thou hast toward the Lord Jesus, and toward all saints, for Paul inquired concerning his friends as to the truth, growth, and fruitfulness of their graces, their faith in Christ and love to him and to all the saints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. That the communication of thy faith may become effectual by the acknowledging of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus, for different sentiments and ways in what is not essential must not make difference of affection as to the truth, and the good Philemon did was matter of joy and comfort to him and others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. For we have great joy and consolation in thy love, because the bowels of the saints are refreshed by thee, brother, for the good which Philemon did was matter of joy and comfort to him and others, who therefore desired that he would continue and abound in good fruits, more and more, to God's honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Wherefore, though I might be much bold in Christ to enjoin thee that which is convenient, for it does not lower any one to condescend, and sometimes even to beseech, where in strictness of right we might command, and the apostle argues from love rather than authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Yet for love's sake I rather beseech thee, being such an one as Paul the aged, and now also a prisoner of Jesus Christ, for the apostle argues from love rather than authority in behalf of one converted through his means, and this was Onesimus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. I beseech thee for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds, for the apostle argues from love rather than authority in behalf of one converted through his means, and what happy changes conversion makes, of evil, good, and of unprofitable, useful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Which in time past was to thee unprofitable, but now profitable to thee and to me, for in allusion to that name, which signifies profitable, the apostle allows that in time past he had been unprofitable, but hastens to mention the change by which he had become profitable, and unholy persons are unprofitable, answering not the great end of their being. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Whom I have sent again, thou therefore receive him, that is, mine own bowels, for religious servants are treasures in a family, making conscience of their time and trusts, and one great evidence of true repentance consists in returning to practise the duties which have been neglected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Whom I would have retained with me, that in thy stead he might have ministered unto me in the bonds of the gospel, for no prospect of usefulness should lead any to neglect their obligations, or to fail in obedience to superiors, and in his unconverted state Onesimus had withdrawn to his master's injury, but now, having seen his sin and repented, he was willing and desirous to return to his duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. But without thy mind would I do nothing, that thy benefit should not be as it were of necessity, but willingly, for no prospect of usefulness should lead any to neglect obligations, and little do men know for what purposes the Lord leaves some to change their situations, or engage in undertakings, perhaps from evil motives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. For perhaps he therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever, for when we speak of the nature of any sin or offence against God, its evil is not to be lessened, but in a penitent sinner, as God covers it, so must we, and such changed characters often become a blessing to all among whom they reside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved, specially to me, but how much more unto thee, both in the flesh, and in the Lord, for Christianity does not do away our duties to others, but directs to the right doing of them, and the communion of saints does not destroy distinction of property. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. If thou count me therefore a partner, receive him as myself, for Philemon was Paul's son in the faith, yet he entreated him as a brother, and Onesimus was a poor slave, yet Paul besought for him as if seeking some great thing for himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. If he hath wronged thee, or oweth thee ought, put that on mine account, for true penitents will be open in owning their faults, especially in cases of injury done to others, and this passage is an instance of that being imputed to one, which is contracted by another, and of one becoming answerable for another by voluntary engagement, that he might be freed from punishment due to his crimes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. I Paul have written it with mine own hand, I will repay it, albeit I do not say to thee how thou owest unto me even thine own self besides, for this is an instance of one becoming answerable for another by voluntary engagement, according to the doctrine that Christ of his own will bore the punishment of our sins, that we might receive the reward of his righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Yea, brother, let me have joy of thee in the Lord, refresh my bowels in the Lord, for Christians should do what may give joy to the hearts of one another, and from the world they expect trouble, so they should find comfort and joy in one another. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Having confidence in thy obedience I wrote unto thee, knowing that thou wilt also do more than I say, for Christians should do what may give joy to the hearts of one another, and the example of Onesimus may encourage the vilest sinners to return to God, but it is shamefully perverted if any are made bold thereby to persist in evil courses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. But withal prepare me also a lodging, for I trust that through your prayers I shall be given unto you, for when any mercies are taken away, our trust and hope must be in God, we must diligently use the means, and if no other should be at hand, abound in prayer, yet though prayer prevails, it does not merit the things obtained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. There salute thee Epaphras, my fellowprisoner in Christ Jesus, for never have believers found more enjoyment of God than when suffering together for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Marcus, Aristarchus, Demas, Lucas, my fellowlabourers, for never have believers found more enjoyment of God than when suffering together for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit, Amen, for grace is the best wish for ourselves and others, with this the apostle begins and ends, and all grace is from Christ, who purchased and bestows it, and what need we more to make us happy than to have the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ with our spirit, and if Christians do not meet on earth, still the grace of the Lord Jesus will be with their spirits, and they will soon meet before the throne to join for ever in admiring the riches of redeeming love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

  • bowels. in this older wording, the heart or seat of deep affection, here refreshed by Philemon and tenderly invoked for Onesimus (verses 7, 12, 20) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
  • Onesimus. a name signifying profitable: once unprofitable to Philemon, now profitable to him and to Paul (verse 11) Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
