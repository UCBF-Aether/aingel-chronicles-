The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 21 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD said unto Moses, Speak unto the priests the sons of Aaron, and say unto them, There shall none be defiled for the dead among his people:

2 But for his kin, that is near unto him, that is, for his mother, and for his father, and for his son, and for his daughter, and for his brother.

3 And for his sister a virgin, that is nigh unto him, which hath had no husband; for her may he be defiled.

4 But he shall not defile himself, being a chief man among his people, to profane himself.

5 They shall not make baldness upon their head, neither shall they shave off the corner of their beard, nor make any cuttings in their flesh.

6 They shall be holy unto their God, and not profane the name of their God: for the offerings of the LORD made by fire, and the bread of their God, they do offer: therefore they shall be holy.

7 They shall not take a wife that is a whore, or profane; neither shall they take a woman put away from her husband: for he is holy unto his God.

8 Thou shalt sanctify him therefore; for he offereth the bread of thy God: he shall be holy unto thee: for I the LORD, which sanctify you, am holy.

9 And the daughter of any priest, if she profane herself by playing the whore, she profaneth her father: she shall be burnt with fire.

10 And he that is the high priest among his brethren, upon whose head the anointing oil was poured, and that is consecrated to put on the garments, shall not uncover his head, nor rend his clothes;

11 Neither shall he go in to any dead body, nor defile himself for his father, or for his mother;

12 Neither shall he go out of the sanctuary, nor profane the sanctuary of his God; for the crown of the anointing oil of his God is upon him: I am the LORD.

13 And he shall take a wife in her virginity.

14 A widow, or a divorced woman, or profane, or an harlot, these shall he not take: but he shall take a virgin of his own people to wife.

15 Neither shall he profane his seed among his people: for I the LORD do sanctify him.

16 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

17 Speak unto Aaron, saying, Whosoever he be of thy seed in their generations that hath any blemish, let him not approach to offer the bread of his God.

18 For whatsoever man he be that hath a blemish, he shall not approach: a blind man, or a lame, or he that hath a flat nose, or any thing superfluous,

19 Or a man that is brokenfooted, or brokenhanded,

20 Or crookbackt, or a dwarf, or that hath a blemish in his eye, or be scurvy, or scabbed, or hath his stones broken;

21 No man that hath a blemish of the seed of Aaron the priest shall come nigh to offer the offerings of the LORD made by fire: he hath a blemish; he shall not come nigh to offer the bread of his God.

22 He shall eat the bread of his God, both of the most holy, and of the holy.

23 Only he shall not go in unto the vail, nor come nigh unto the altar, because he hath a blemish; that he profane not my sanctuaries: for I the LORD do sanctify them.

24 And Moses told it unto Aaron, and to his sons, and unto all the children of Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the holiness rules for the priests themselves. An ordinary priest may be defiled for the dead only for his nearest kin, and may not make baldness, shave his beard's corners, or cut his flesh in mourning; he may not marry a whore, a profane woman, or a divorced woman. The high priest is stricter still: he may not uncover his head or rend his clothes even for father or mother, may not leave the sanctuary, and must marry a virgin of his own people. A descendant of Aaron with a blemish may eat the bread of God but may not offer it or approach the veil or the altar. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the priests' mourning law, whose design is to keep inviolate the purity and dignity of the sacred office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. A priest shall not be defiled for the dead among his people, except for his kin nearest to him, his mother, father, son, daughter, and brother, since contact with a corpse debars a mourner from the tabernacle for a week, an inconvenience the whole order must avoid except for family affection or necessity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For his sister, a virgin near to him who has had no husband, he may be defiled, the exception being strictly confined to nearest kindred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. But he shall not defile himself, being a chief man among his people, to profane himself, or, as others read it, being a husband, he shall not defile himself by a wife's obsequies beyond the kin exceptions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Priests shall not make baldness on their heads, shave the corners of their beards, or make cuttings in their flesh, a special injunction beyond the general law, that they may show moderation of grief, restrain passion to console others, and sorrow not as those without hope of resurrection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They shall be holy unto their God and not profane His name, for they offer the LORD's offerings made by fire, the bread of their God, therefore they shall be holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They shall not take a wife who is a whore or profane, nor one put away from her husband, for he is holy unto his God, private individuals being allowed connections forbidden to priests, whose office requires unblemished sanctity in family as well as self. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Therefore he shall sanctify the priest, for he offers the bread of God, and he shall be holy unto Israel, for the LORD who sanctifies them is holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. If a priest's daughter profanes herself by playing the whore, she profanes her father, and she shall be burnt with fire, departures in priests' families being visited with severer punishment than in others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. He who is high priest among his brethren, on whose head anointing oil is poured and who is consecrated to put on garments, shall not uncover his head nor rend his clothes, the family-bereavement indulgence granted to common priests being denied to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Neither shall he go in to any dead body, nor defile himself for his father or his mother, since his absence from the sanctuary for defilement removal could not be dispensed with, nor could he intercede unless ceremonially clean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Neither shall he go out of the sanctuary nor profane it, for the crown of his God's anointing oil is upon him, his high dignity demanding corresponding superiority in personal holiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He shall take a wife in her virginity, a stringent rule upholding the dignity of his station and family, as later extended to Christian ministers' families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. A widow, divorced woman, profane woman, or harlot he shall not take, but a virgin of his own people shall he take to wife, the high priest's marriage being the most restricted of all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Neither shall he profane his seed among his people, disgracing his house by a low marriage, for the LORD sanctifies him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The LORD speaks to Moses again, now concerning blemished priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. No man of Aaron's seed in generations who has any blemish shall approach to offer the bread of his God, the blemish rule governing all succeeding generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. A blind or lame man, or one with a flat nose or anything superfluous, shall not approach, any physical infirmity disturbing associations or exciting ridicule and detracting from the office's weight and authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. A man with a broken foot or broken hand shall not approach, under the same blemish rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. A crookbacked man, dwarf, one with a blemish in his eye, scurvy, scabbed, or broken stones, shall not approach, the list covering visible malformations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. No descendant of Aaron with a blemish shall come near to offer the LORD's offerings made by fire, for he has a blemish and shall not offer the bread of his God, though he may be employed in inferior sanctuary duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. He may eat the bread of his God, both most holy and holy, the blemish barring office, not maintenance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Only he shall not go in unto the veil nor come near the altar, because he has a blemish, lest he profane the sanctuaries, for the LORD sanctifies them, the holiness required being external and relative, with typical reference to Christ's priesthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Moses speaks this to Aaron, his sons, and all Israel, so the blemish and mourning laws are published to the whole congregation, not priests alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
