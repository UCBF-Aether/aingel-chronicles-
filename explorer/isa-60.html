The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 60 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Arise, shine; for thy light is come, and the glory of the LORD is risen upon thee.

2 For, behold, the darkness shall cover the earth, and gross darkness the people: but the LORD shall arise upon thee, and his glory shall be seen upon thee.

3 And the Gentiles shall come to thy light, and kings to the brightness of thy rising.

4 Lift up thine eyes round about, and see: all they gather themselves together, they come to thee: thy sons shall come from far, and thy daughters shall be nursed at thy side.

5 Then thou shalt see, and flow together, and thine heart shall fear, and be enlarged; because the abundance of the sea shall be converted unto thee, the forces of the Gentiles shall come unto thee.

6 The multitude of camels shall cover thee, the dromedaries of Midian and Ephah; all they from Sheba shall come: they shall bring gold and incense; and they shall shew forth the praises of the LORD.

7 All the flocks of Kedar shall be gathered together unto thee, the rams of Nebaioth shall minister unto thee: they shall come up with acceptance on mine altar, and I will glorify the house of my glory.

8 Who are these that fly as a cloud, and as the doves to their windows?

9 Surely the isles shall wait for me, and the ships of Tarshish first, to bring thy sons from far, their silver and their gold with them, unto the name of the LORD thy God, and to the Holy One of Israel, because he hath glorified thee.

10 And the sons of strangers shall build up thy walls, and their kings shall minister unto thee: for in my wrath I smote thee, but in my favour have I had mercy on thee.

11 Therefore thy gates shall be open continually; they shall not be shut day nor night; that men may bring unto thee the forces of the Gentiles, and that their kings may be brought.

12 For the nation and kingdom that will not serve thee shall perish; yea, those nations shall be utterly wasted.

13 The glory of Lebanon shall come unto thee, the fir tree, the pine tree, and the box together, to beautify the place of my sanctuary; and I will make the place of my feet glorious.

14 The sons also of them that afflicted thee shall come bending unto thee; and all they that despised thee shall bow themselves down at the soles of thy feet; and they shall call thee; The city of the LORD, The Zion of the Holy One of Israel.

15 Whereas thou hast been forsaken and hated, so that no man went through thee, I will make thee an eternal excellency, a joy of many generations.

16 Thou shalt also suck the milk of the Gentiles, and shalt suck the breast of kings: and thou shalt know that I the LORD am thy Saviour and thy Redeemer, the mighty One of Jacob.

17 For brass I will bring gold, and for iron I will bring silver, and for wood brass, and for stones iron: I will also make thy officers peace, and thine exactors righteousness.

18 Violence shall no more be heard in thy land, wasting nor destruction within thy borders; but thou shalt call thy walls Salvation, and thy gates Praise.

19 The sun shall be no more thy light by day; neither for brightness shall the moon give light unto thee: but the LORD shall be unto thee an everlasting light, and thy God thy glory.

20 Thy sun shall no more go down; neither shall thy moon withdraw itself: for the LORD shall be thine everlasting light, and the days of thy mourning shall be ended.

21 Thy people also shall be all righteous: they shall inherit the land for ever, the branch of my planting, the work of my hands, that I may be glorified.

22 A little one shall become a thousand, and a small one a strong nation: I the LORD will hasten it in his time.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Arise, shine, for your light has come and the glory of the Lord has risen upon you, while darkness still covers the earth. Nations and kings walk toward Zion's light, bringing gold, frankincense, and flocks; the gates stand open continually, the sun is replaced by the Lord as an everlasting light, and a little one becomes a thousand in God's own time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Arise, shine, for your light is come, and the glory of the Lord is risen upon you. This chapter treats Israel's glory after her affliction. It is an ode of congratulation to Zion on her restoration at the Lord's second advent to her true position as the mother church from which the Gospel is to be diffused to the whole Gentile world; the first promulgation of the Gospel among the Gentiles, beginning at Jerusalem, as in Luke, is an earnest of this. The language is too glorious to apply to anything that as yet has happened. Arise means from the dust in which thou hast been sitting as a mourning female captive, as in chapters 3 and 52. Shine means or, be enlightened; for thy light cometh; impart to others the spiritual light now given thee, as in verse 3. The margin and Gesenius translate, Be enlightened; be resplendent with posterity; imperative for the future indicative, Thou shalt be enlightened, as in chapters 58 and Ephesians. Glory of the Lord means not merely the Shekinah, or cloud of glory, such as rested above the ark in the old dispensation, but the glory of the Lord in person, as in Jeremiah. Is risen means as the sun, as in Malachi and Luke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. For, behold, the darkness will cover the earth, and gross darkness the people, but the Lord will arise upon you, and his glory will be seen upon you. Earth means the rest of the earth: in contrast with light upon thee in verse 1. The earth will be afterwards enlightened through Israel, as in chapter 9. Be seen means conspicuously, so the Hebrew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The Gentiles will come to your light, and kings to the brightness of your rising. This recalls chapters 2, 11, 43, 49, and 66. Kings recalls chapters 49 and 52. Thy rising is rather, thy sun-rising, that is, to the brightness that riseth upon thee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Lift up your eyes round about, and see; all they gather themselves together, they come to you; your sons will come from far, and your daughters will be nursed at your side. Eyes means Jerusalem is addressed as a female with eyes cast down from grief. All they, they means the Gentile peoples come together to bring back the dispersed Hebrews, restore their city, and worship Jehovah with offerings. Nursed at thy side is rather carried at thy side. It is the custom in the East to carry the children astride on the hip, with the arms around the body, as in chapter 66. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Then you will see, and flow together, and your heart will fear, and be enlarged, because the abundance of the sea will be converted unto you, and the forces of the Gentiles will come unto you. See means, as in verse 4, namely, the bringing back of thy sons. Flow together is rather, overflow with joy, or, from a different Hebrew root, be bright with joy, as in Job. Fear is rather, beat with the agitation of solemn joy at the marvellous sight, as in Jeremiah. Be enlarged means swell with delight. Grief, on the contrary, contracts the heart. Abundance of sea means the wealth of the lands beyond the sea, as in Solomon's time, the type of the coming reign of the Prince of peace. Converted is rather, be turned, instead of being turned to purposes of sin and idolatry. Forces is rather, riches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The multitude of camels will cover you, the dromedaries of Midian and Ephah; all they from Sheba will come; they will bring gold and incense, and they will show forth the praises of the Lord. Camels means laden with merchandise; the camel is the ship of the desert, as in chapter 30. Cover thee means so many of them shall there be. Dromedaries means they have one hunch on the back, whereas the camel has two: distinguished for swiftness, as in Jeremiah. Midian means east of the Elanitic branch of the Red Sea, and stretching northward along Mount Seir. Associated with the Ishmaelites in traffic, as in Genesis. Ephah means part of Midian, east of the Dead Sea. It abounded in camels, as in Judges. Sheba means in Arabia-Felix, famed for frankincense and gold, as in Psalm 72 and Jeremiah, which they traded in, as in chapters 45 and Job and Ezekiel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. All the flocks of Kedar will be gathered together unto you, the rams of Nebaioth will minister unto you; they will come up with acceptance on my altar, and I will glorify the house of my glory. Kedar recalls chapters 21 and Song of Solomon, in the south of Arabia-Deserta, or north of Arabia-Petraea; they traded in flocks, as in Ezekiel. Nebaioth means son of Ishmael, as was Kedar. Father of the Nabatheans in Arabia-Petraea. Minister means by coming up as an acceptable sacrifice. Come up with acceptance means that is, acceptably. The rams offering themselves voluntarily, as in Psalm 68, Romans, and 1 Peter, without waiting for any other priest, answer to believers strong in faith and lamb-like meekness; and in the white fleece-like robe of sanctity, as Vitringa. House of my glory means the temple, as in Ezekiel, Haggai, and Malachi. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Who are these that fly as a cloud, and as the doves to their windows? The prophet, seeing in vision new hosts approaching quickly like a cloud of doves, asks who they are. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Surely the isles will wait for me, and the ships of Tarshish first, to bring your sons from far, their silver and their gold with them, unto the name of the Lord your God, and to the Holy One of Israel, because he has glorified you. This recalls chapter 42. Tarshish first means the ships of Tartessus, as in chapters 2 and 23, that is, vessels that trade to the most distant regions, will be among the foremost to bring back the scattered Israelites, as in chapter 66. Their silver means the nations among whom the Jews have been scattered shall help them with their money in returning, as in verses 5 to 7 and 11 and 16, as was the case at the return from Babylon, as in Ezra, compare Psalm 68. Unto the name, to the Holy One is rather, because of the name, because of the Holy One, as in chapter 55. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The sons of strangers will build up your walls, and their kings will minister unto you, for in my wrath I smote you, but in my favour have I had mercy on you. Minister unto thee recalls verses 7 and chapter 49. In my wrath I smote thee recalls chapters 54 and 57. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Therefore your gates will be open continually; they will not be shut day nor night, that men may bring unto you the forces of the Gentiles, and that their kings may be brought. This recalls Revelation: the gates are ever open to receive new offerings and converts, as in chapters 26 and Acts and Revelation. In time of peace the gates of a city are open: so, under the Prince of peace, there shall be no need of barring gates against invaders. Forces means riches. Be brought means as willing captives to the truth; or, if not willingly, be bought by judgments to submit to Israel, as in verses 12 and 14. Some explain it, may come escorted by a retinue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. For the nation and kingdom that will not serve you will perish, yes, those nations will be utterly wasted. For means the reason which will lead Gentile kings and people to submit themselves; fear of the God in Israel, as in Zechariah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The glory of Lebanon will come unto you, the fir tree, the pine tree, and the box together, to beautify the place of my sanctuary, and I will make the place of my feet glorious. Glory means that is, the trees which adorned Lebanon; emblem of men eminent in natural gifts, devoting all that is in them to the God of Israel, as in Hosea. Fir, pine, box is rather, the cypress, ilex, cedar. Place of my sanctuary means Jerusalem, as in Jeremiah. Place of my feet means no longer the ark, as in Jeremiah, the footstool of Jehovah, as in Psalms and 1 Chronicles; but the place of His throne, the place of the soles of His feet, where He will dwell in the midst of the children of Israel for ever, in the new temple, as in Ezekiel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The sons also of them that afflicted you will come bending unto you, and all they that despised you will bow themselves down at the soles of your feet, and they will call you: The city of the Lord, The Zion of the Holy One of Israel. The sons means their fathers who afflicted Israel having been cut off by divine judgments, as in chapters 14 and 49. The Zion of the Holy One means the royal court of the Holy One. Some translate, Zion, the sanctuary, holy place, of Israel, as in chapters 57 and Psalm 46. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Whereas you have been forsaken and hated, so that no man went through you, I will make you an eternal excellency, a joy of many generations. Forsaken recalls Psalm 78. No man went through thee means thy land was so desolate that no traveller, or caravan, passed through thee; true only of Israel, not true of the church, as in Lamentations. Excellency means glory, that is, for ever honoured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. You will also suck the milk of the Gentiles, and will suck the breast of kings, and you will know that I the Lord am your Saviour and your Redeemer, the mighty One of Jacob. Suck means thou shalt draw to thyself and enjoy all that is valuable of the possessions of the Gentiles, etc., as in chapters 49, 61, and 66. Know means by the favours bestowed on thee, and through thee on the Gentiles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For brass I will bring gold, and for iron I will bring silver, and for wood brass, and for stones iron; I will also make your officers peace, and your exactors righteousness. Poetically, with figurative allusion to the furniture of the temple; all things in that happy age to come shall be changed for the better. Exactors means namely, of tribute. Righteousness means all rulers in restored Jerusalem shall not only be peaceable and righteous, but shall be, as it were, peace and righteousness itself in their administration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Violence will no more be heard in your land, wasting nor destruction within your borders, but you will call your walls Salvation, and your gates Praise. This recalls chapter 2: not only shall thy walls keep thee safe from foes, but Salvation shall serve as thy walls, converting thy foes into friends, and so ensuring thee perfect safety, as in chapter 26. Gates means once the scene of destruction when victorious foes burst through them, as in Nehemiah; henceforth to be not only the scene of praises, but Praise itself; the gates, as the place of public concourse, were the scene of thanksgivings, as in 2 Chronicles and Psalms. Judah, the favoured tribe, means praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The sun will be no more your light by day, neither for brightness will the moon give light unto you, but the Lord will be unto you an everlasting light, and your God your glory. The sun and moon, the brightest objects by day and night, shall be eclipsed by the surpassing glory of God manifesting Himself to thee, as in chapters 30 and Zechariah and Revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Your sun will no more go down, neither will your moon withdraw itself, for the Lord will be your everlasting light, and the days of your mourning will be ended. There shall be no national and spiritual obscuration again as formerly, as in Joel and Amos. Mourning ended recalls chapters 25 and Revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Your people also will be all righteous; they will inherit the land for ever, the branch of my planting, the work of my hands, that I may be glorified. All righteous recalls chapters 4 and 52 and Revelation. Inherit land recalls chapters 49, 54, and 65, Psalm 37, and Matthew. Branch of my planting recalls chapters 61 and Psalm 92 and Matthew. Work of my hands means the converted Israelites, as in chapters 29 and 45. That I may be glorified means the final end of all God's gracious dealings, as in chapters 49 and 61. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. A little one will become a thousand, and a small one a strong nation; I the Lord will hasten it in his time. Little one means even one, and that the smallest in number and rank, shall be multiplied a thousandfold in both respects, as in Micah and Matthew. His time means not our time; we might wish to hasten it, but it will come in due time, as in the case of Jesus' first coming, as in Galatians; so in that of the restoration of Israel and the conversion of the world, as in chapters 66 and Habakkuk and Acts and Hebrews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Your people also will be all righteous; they will inherit the land for ever, the branch of my planting, the work of my hands, that I may be glorified. All righteous recalls chapters 4 and 52 and Revelation. Inherit land recalls chapters 49, 54, and 65, Psalm 37, and Matthew. Branch of my planting recalls chapters 61 and Psalm 92 and Matthew. Work of my hands means the converted Israelites, as in chapters 29 and 45. That I may be glorified means the final end of all God's gracious dealings, as in chapters 49 and 61. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. A little one will become a thousand, and a small one a strong nation; I the Lord will hasten it in his time. Little one means even one, and that the smallest in number and rank, shall be multiplied a thousandfold in both respects, as in Micah and Matthew. His time means not our time; we might wish to hasten it, but it will come in due time, as in the case of Jesus' first coming, as in Galatians; so in that of the restoration of Israel and the conversion of the world, as in chapters 66 and Habakkuk and Acts and Hebrews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
