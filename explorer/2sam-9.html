The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And David said, Is there yet any that is left of the house of Saul, that I may shew him kindness for Jonathan’s sake?

2 And there was of the house of Saul a servant whose name was Ziba. And when they had called him unto David, the king said unto him, Art thou Ziba? And he said, Thy servant is he.

3 And the king said, Is there not yet any of the house of Saul, that I may shew the kindness of God unto him? And Ziba said unto the king, Jonathan hath yet a son, which is lame on his feet.

4 And the king said unto him, Where is he? And Ziba said unto the king, Behold, he is in the house of Machir, the son of Ammiel, in Lodebar.

5 Then king David sent, and fetched him out of the house of Machir, the son of Ammiel, from Lodebar.

6 Now when Mephibosheth, the son of Jonathan, the son of Saul, was come unto David, he fell on his face, and did reverence. And David said, Mephibosheth. And he answered, Behold thy servant!

7 And David said unto him, Fear not: for I will surely shew thee kindness for Jonathan thy father’s sake, and will restore thee all the land of Saul thy father; and thou shalt eat bread at my table continually.

8 And he bowed himself, and said, What is thy servant, that thou shouldest look upon such a dead dog as I am?

9 Then the king called to Ziba, Saul’s servant, and said unto him, I have given unto thy master’s son all that pertained to Saul and to all his house.

10 Thou therefore, and thy sons, and thy servants, shall till the land for him, and thou shalt bring in the fruits, that thy master’s son may have food to eat: but Mephibosheth thy master’s son shall eat bread alway at my table. Now Ziba had fifteen sons and twenty servants.

11 Then said Ziba unto the king, According to all that my lord the king hath commanded his servant, so shall thy servant do. As for Mephibosheth, said the king, he shall eat at my table, as one of the king’s sons.

12 And Mephibosheth had a young son, whose name was Micha. And all that dwelt in the house of Ziba were servants unto Mephibosheth.

13 So Mephibosheth dwelt in Jerusalem: for he did eat continually at the king’s table; and was lame on both his feet.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Remembering his covenant with Jonathan, David seeks out any survivor of Saul's house to show kindness to, and finds Mephibosheth, Jonathan's lame son, living at Lo-debar. David restores Saul's lands to him and seats him at the king's table for life. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David asks whether any is left of Saul's house, that he may show him kindness for Jonathan's sake. This comes a great while after his accession, Henry notes, and it is good to bethink ourselves whether any promises we have neglected should be made good, better late than never. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. A servant of Saul's house named Ziba is called, and David asks if he is Ziba; he answers, Thy servant is he. Ziba, an old retainer, knows the state of Saul's family and can inform the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. David asks whether there is any left of Saul's house to whom he may show the kindness of God. Henry explains this kindness as covenant kindness after God's example, greater than one can ordinarily expect from men, done with an eye to God's honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Ziba answers that Jonathan has yet a son lame on his feet, living in Machir's house in Lodebar. The son lives in obscurity, forgotten as a dead man out of mind, bearing it the more easily because he remembers little of the honour he fell from. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. David sends and fetches him out of Machir's house in Lodebar. In sending for him David eases Machir's trouble, and Machir, a generous man who sheltered the reduced son of a prince, will later furnish David himself at Mahanaim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Mephibosheth, son of Jonathan son of Saul, comes to David, falls on his face, and does reverence, answering, Behold thy servant. Though lame, he presents himself with all respect due to the king's character, and those who show respect in inferior relations shall have respect shown to them when advanced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. David says, Fear not, for he will surely show kindness for Jonathan's sake, restore all Saul's land, and Mephibosheth shall eat bread at the king's table continually. Great men, Henry notes, should not take pleasure in the timorous approaches of inferiors, but encourage them; true friendship will be generous, giving the estate and a constant place at table. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Mephibosheth bows and asks what his servant is, that David should look upon such a dead dog as he is. He accepts kindness with great humility, magnifying David's favour and vilifying himself, and Henry notes it is good to have the heart humble under humbling providences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. David calls Ziba and gives him all that pertained to Saul and to all his house, for his master's son. The grant of the father's estate is confirmed, with Ziba called as witness to it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Ziba, his sons, and his servants shall till the land and bring in the fruits, that Mephibosheth may have food, while Mephibosheth eats at the king's table. Ziba, who knows the estate and has hands enough for it, manages it, though Henry warns that all who dwelt in Ziba's house lived upon Mephibosheth's estate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Ziba answers that as the king commanded, so his servant will do, and the king adds that Mephibosheth shall eat at his table as one of the king's sons. David will have him at his own table, not merely at Ziba's, and Mephibosheth is as pleased with his post as Ziba with his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Mephibosheth has a young son named Micha, and all who dwell in Ziba's house are servants to Mephibosheth. The household that serves the estate also lives upon it, a reminder that as goods increase, so do those who eat them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. So Mephibosheth dwells in Jerusalem, eating continually at the king's table, lame on both his feet. Because David was a type of Christ, Henry says, his kindness to Mephibosheth illustrates the Saviour's kindness to fallen man: the Son of God seeks a race that sought not him, restores the forfeited inheritance, and sets them with his children at his table. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
