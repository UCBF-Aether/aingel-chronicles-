The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 John 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life;

2 (For the life was manifested, and we have seen it, and bear witness, and shew unto you that eternal life, which was with the Father, and was manifested unto us;)

3 That which we have seen and heard declare we unto you, that ye also may have fellowship with us: and truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ.

4 And these things write we unto you, that your joy may be full.

5 This then is the message which we have heard of him, and declare unto you, that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all.

6 If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness, we lie, and do not the truth:

7 But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.

8 If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.

9 If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

10 If we say that we have not sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The writer proclaims what was from the beginning, heard, seen and touched, the word of life, so readers may have fellowship with the Father and the Son and joy made full. The message is that God is light and in him is no darkness: claiming fellowship while walking in darkness is false, but walking in the light brings fellowship and cleansing by Jesus' blood, and confessing sins meets a faithful and just God who forgives and cleanses, while claiming sinlessness makes God a liar. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John writes as an eyewitness, not a theorist: the Word of life was heard, seen, looked upon, and handled. JFB notes the rising force of those verbs, from hearing to seeing to touching, as proof that Christ came in a real body and was known by the apostles over years of company with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. This verse breaks in as a parenthesis to explain the first: the life itself was manifested, having first been with the Father. The apostles saw that eternal life and now bear witness to it, so the epistle opens and closes on the same gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The apostles declare what they saw and heard so that readers may share their fellowship, and that fellowship is with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ. Henry stresses that this communion is open to any number, since its joy, unlike worldly goods, is not lessened by sharing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. John writes these things so that joy may be full, the joy that belongs to fellowship with God. Henry treats this communion as all-sufficient happiness, and JFB notes that Christ himself is the source, object, and centre of that joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The message handed on from Christ is that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all. As light is the source of all natural life and growth, so God is the fountain of wisdom, purity, and joy, with no speck of ignorance, error, or sin in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. To claim fellowship with God while walking in darkness is to lie and to fail the truth in practice, whatever is said. Henry's point is blunt: God holds no heavenly fellowship with unholy souls, and a profession contradicted by conduct shows its own falsehood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Walking in the light, as God himself is in the light, brings fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus Christ cleanses from all sin. JFB explains the cleansing as present and ongoing, the daily sanctification of believers already justified, rather than a single past act alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Saying we have no sin deceives ourselves, not God, and shows the truth is not in us. JFB distinguishes this present sin, the corruption of the old nature still adhering to believers, from the actual sins confessed in the next verse, so that even holy walkers must own their need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Confession made with a contrite heart meets a God who is faithful to his promises and just in keeping them, so that he forgives sins and cleanses from all unrighteousness. Henry urges believers to plead guilty honestly and rely wholly on mercy through the righteousness of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Claiming we have not sinned goes further than the self-deception of verse 8 and makes God a liar, driving his word from our hearts. Since the whole law and gospel rest on the fact that all have sinned, denying it rejects his word as a whole, not merely on one point. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
