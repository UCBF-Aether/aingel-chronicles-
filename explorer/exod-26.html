The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 26 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover thou shalt make the tabernacle with ten curtains of fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet: with cherubims of cunning work shalt thou make them.

2 The length of one curtain shall be eight and twenty cubits, and the breadth of one curtain four cubits: and every one of the curtains shall have one measure.

3 The five curtains shall be coupled together one to another; and other five curtains shall be coupled one to another.

4 And thou shalt make loops of blue upon the edge of the one curtain from the selvedge in the coupling; and likewise shalt thou make in the uttermost edge of another curtain, in the coupling of the second.

5 Fifty loops shalt thou make in the one curtain, and fifty loops shalt thou make in the edge of the curtain that is in the coupling of the second; that the loops may take hold one of another.

6 And thou shalt make fifty taches of gold, and couple the curtains together with the taches: and it shall be one tabernacle.

7 And thou shalt make curtains of goats’ hair to be a covering upon the tabernacle: eleven curtains shalt thou make.

8 The length of one curtain shall be thirty cubits, and the breadth of one curtain four cubits: and the eleven curtains shall be all of one measure.

9 And thou shalt couple five curtains by themselves, and six curtains by themselves, and shalt double the sixth curtain in the forefront of the tabernacle.

10 And thou shalt make fifty loops on the edge of the one curtain that is outmost in the coupling, and fifty loops in the edge of the curtain which coupleth the second.

11 And thou shalt make fifty taches of brass, and put the taches into the loops, and couple the tent together, that it may be one.

12 And the remnant that remaineth of the curtains of the tent, the half curtain that remaineth, shall hang over the backside of the tabernacle.

13 And a cubit on the one side, and a cubit on the other side of that which remaineth in the length of the curtains of the tent, it shall hang over the sides of the tabernacle on this side and on that side, to cover it.

14 And thou shalt make a covering for the tent of rams’ skins dyed red, and a covering above of badgers’ skins.

15 And thou shalt make boards for the tabernacle of shittim wood standing up.

16 Ten cubits shall be the length of a board, and a cubit and a half shall be the breadth of one board.

17 Two tenons shall there be in one board, set in order one against another: thus shalt thou make for all the boards of the tabernacle.

18 And thou shalt make the boards for the tabernacle, twenty boards on the south side southward.

19 And thou shalt make forty sockets of silver under the twenty boards; two sockets under one board for his two tenons, and two sockets under another board for his two tenons.

20 And for the second side of the tabernacle on the north side there shall be twenty boards:

21 And their forty sockets of silver; two sockets under one board, and two sockets under another board.

22 And for the sides of the tabernacle westward thou shalt make six boards.

23 And two boards shalt thou make for the corners of the tabernacle in the two sides.

24 And they shall be coupled together beneath, and they shall be coupled together above the head of it unto one ring: thus shall it be for them both; they shall be for the two corners.

25 And they shall be eight boards, and their sockets of silver, sixteen sockets; two sockets under one board, and two sockets under another board.

26 And thou shalt make bars of shittim wood; five for the boards of the one side of the tabernacle,

27 And five bars for the boards of the other side of the tabernacle, and five bars for the boards of the side of the tabernacle, for the two sides westward.

28 And the middle bar in the midst of the boards shall reach from end to end.

29 And thou shalt overlay the boards with gold, and make their rings of gold for places for the bars: and thou shalt overlay the bars with gold.

30 And thou shalt rear up the tabernacle according to the fashion thereof which was shewed thee in the mount.

31 And thou shalt make a vail of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen of cunning work: with cherubims shall it be made:

32 And thou shalt hang it upon four pillars of shittim wood overlaid with gold: their hooks shall be of gold, upon the four sockets of silver.

33 And thou shalt hang up the vail under the taches, that thou mayest bring in thither within the vail the ark of the testimony: and the vail shall divide unto you between the holy place and the most holy.

34 And thou shalt put the mercy seat upon the ark of the testimony in the most holy place.

35 And thou shalt set the table without the vail, and the candlestick over against the table on the side of the tabernacle toward the south: and thou shalt put the table on the north side.

36 And thou shalt make an hanging for the door of the tent, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, wrought with needlework.

37 And thou shalt make for the hanging five pillars of shittim wood, and overlay them with gold, and their hooks shall be of gold: and thou shalt cast five sockets of brass for them.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The tabernacle itself is described: curtains of fine twined linen with cherubim, a second covering of goats' hair, coverings of rams' skins and badgers' skins, boards of shittim wood standing upright with silver sockets, and the veil that divides the holy place from the most holy, where the ark of the testimony stands. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Moreover thou shalt make the tabernacle with ten curtains of fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet, with cherubims of cunning work shalt thou make them. Henry notes God manifested his presence in a tent because of Israel's wilderness condition, suiting the tokens of his favour to his people's state, and the curtains were very rich, embroidered with cherubim, signifying that the angels of God pitch their tents round about the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The length of one curtain shall be eight and twenty cubits, and the breadth of one curtain four cubits, and every one of the curtains shall have one measure. Henry observes the single measure for all ten curtains: the house of God was uniform, no curtain larger or smaller than its fellow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The five curtains shall be coupled together one to another, and other five curtains shall be coupled together one to another. Henry notes the two sets of five made one tabernacle: the church is joined together, curtain to curtain, into a single dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Thou shalt make loops of blue upon the edge of the one curtain from the selvedge in the coupling, and likewise shalt thou make in the uttermost edge of another curtain in the coupling of the second. Henry observes the blue loops were the joining points, coloured like the curtains themselves, that the coupling might be both strong and seemly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Fifty loops shalt thou make in the one curtain, and fifty loops shalt thou make in the edge of the curtain that is in the coupling of the second, that the loops may take hold one of another. Henry notes the fifty matching loops: each loop found its fellow, that the two sets might hold as one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Thou shalt make fifty taches of gold, and couple the curtains together with the taches, and it shall be one tabernacle. Henry observes the gold clasps completed the union: with loops and taches, the ten curtains became one tabernacle, not ten tents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Thou shalt make curtains of goats' hair to be a covering upon the tabernacle, eleven curtains shalt thou make. Henry notes the curtains of meaner materials, made longer and broader, covered the rich ones, and the whole represents Christ and the church, outwardly mean but inwardly, and in the sight of God, glorious and precious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The length of one curtain shall be thirty cubits, and the breadth of one curtain four cubits, and the eleven curtains shall be all of one measure. Henry observes the goats' hair curtains were longer than the inner ones, that they might cover them fully and hang over the sides. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Thou shalt couple five curtains by themselves, and six curtains by themselves, and shalt double the sixth curtain in the forefront of the tabernacle. Henry notes the doubling of the sixth curtain at the front strengthened the entrance, where weather and wear struck hardest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Thou shalt make fifty loops on the edge of the one curtain that is outmost in the coupling, and fifty loops in the edge of the curtain which coupleth the second. Henry observes the same fifty loops joined the outer covering as joined the inner, for both coverings were coupled alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Thou shalt make fifty taches of brass, and put the taches into the loops, and couple the tent together, that it may be one. Henry notes the brass clasps for the outer tent, where gold was used within: the outer covering was joined with a meaner metal, fitting its meaner material. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And the remnant that remaineth of the curtains of the tent, the half curtain that remaineth, shall hang over the backside of the tabernacle. Henry observes the leftover half curtain was not cut off but hung over the back, that no part of the covering be wasted and the rear be protected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And a cubit on the one side, and a cubit on the other side of that which remaineth in the length of the curtains of the tent, shall hang over the sides of the tabernacle on this side and on that side, to cover it. Henry notes the overhang of a cubit on each side: the covering was made to overlap the walls, that rain might run clear of the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Thou shalt make a covering for the tent of rams' skins dyed red, and a covering above of badgers' skins. Henry observes the skin coverings defended the whole: rams' skins dyed red beneath, and badgers' skins above, weatherproof layers over the meaner and the richer curtains alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Thou shalt make boards for the tabernacle of shittim wood standing up. Henry notes the boards were the walls: strong shittim wood standing upright, the frame upon which all the curtains would rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Ten cubits shall be the length of a board, and a cubit and a half shall be the breadth of one board. Henry observes the boards were tall and uniform, ten cubits high, that the house might stand to its full height in the wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Two tenons shall there be in one board, set in order one against another, and thus shalt thou make for all the boards of the tabernacle. Henry notes the two tenons per board, fitted by mortises into the sockets, made the walls firm though movable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Thou shalt make the boards for the tabernacle, twenty boards on the south side southward. Henry observes the south wall was twenty boards, a full side of the house, set toward the sun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Thou shalt make forty sockets of silver under the twenty boards, two sockets under one board for his two tenons. Henry notes the sockets of silver each weighed about 115 pounds, placed in rows on the ground, and in every pair a board was fitted, so the walls stood on silver foundations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And for the second side of the tabernacle on the north side there shall be twenty boards. Henry observes the north side matched the south, twenty boards again, that the house might be symmetrical. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And their forty sockets of silver, two sockets under one board, and two sockets under another board. Henry notes the north sockets matched the south: forty more of silver, for both sides stood on the same costly foundation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And for the sides of the tabernacle westward thou shalt make six boards. Henry observes the west end, the rear of the house, was six boards, closing the tabernacle behind the most holy place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And two boards shalt thou make for the corners of the tabernacle in the two sides. Henry notes the corner boards bound the sides to the rear, that the walls might hold together at the angles where strain falls hardest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And they shall be coupled together beneath, and they shall be coupled together above the head of it unto one ring, thus shall it be for them both, they shall be for the two corners. Henry observes the corner boards were coupled both below and above, double-joined, that the corners might be the strongest part of the frame. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And they shall be eight boards, and their sockets of silver, sixteen sockets, two sockets under every board. Henry notes the west end counted eight boards in all, six plus two corners, on sixteen sockets of silver. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And thou shalt make bars of shittim wood, five for the boards of the one side of the tabernacle. Henry notes the bars passed through rings of gold and held the walls together, five bars for one side, that the boards might stand as one wall. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And five bars for the boards of the other side of the tabernacle, and five bars for the boards of the tabernacle for the sides westward. Henry observes the bars ran on every side, south, north, and west, binding the whole frame into one house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And the middle bar in the midst of the boards shall reach from end to end. Henry notes the middle bar ran the whole length, a single spine through the wall, holding all the boards in line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. And thou shalt overlay the boards with gold, and make their rings of gold for places for the bars, and overlay the bars with gold. Henry observes the boards were covered with plates of gold, and the rings and bars also gold, for in all this the tabernacle was a type of the church of God, built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief Corner-stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And thou shalt rear up the tabernacle according to the fashion thereof which was showed thee in the mount. Henry notes the rearing itself was by pattern: the house was raised as it was shown, not as it seemed best to the builders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And thou shalt make a vail of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen of cunning work, with cherubims shall it be made. Henry notes the vail, or curtain, separated the holy place from the most holy place, woven richly like the inner curtains, with cherubim guarding the way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. And thou shalt hang it upon four pillars of shittim wood overlaid with gold, their hooks shall be of gold, upon the four sockets of silver. Henry observes the vail hung on four gold-covered pillars on silver sockets, a rich partition for a holy separation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And thou shalt hang up the vail under the taches, that thou mayest bring in thither within the vail the ark of the testimony, and the vail shall divide unto you between the holy place and the most holy. Henry notes the vail forbade any to look into the holiest of all, signifying that the way into the holiest was not made manifest while the first tabernacle stood, for life and immortality lay hidden till brought to light by the gospel, as signified by the rending of this vail at Christ's death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. And thou shalt put the mercy seat upon the ark of the testimony in the most holy place. Henry observes the mercy seat stood in the most holy place itself, the throne within the vail, where God dwelt between the cherubim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And thou shalt set the table without the vail, and the candlestick over against the table on the side of the tabernacle toward the south, and thou shalt put the table on the north side. Henry notes the furniture of the holy place stood outside the vail: the table on the north and the candlestick on the south, facing each other across the holy place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And thou shalt make an hanging for the door of the tent, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, wrought with needlework. Henry notes another vail was for the outer door of the tabernacle, and this vail was all the defence the tabernacle had, yet God takes care of his church on earth, and a curtain shall be, if God please to make it so, as strong a defence as gates of brass and bars of iron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. And thou shalt make for the hanging five pillars of shittim wood, and overlay them with gold, and their hooks shall be of gold, and thou shalt cast five sockets of brass for them. Henry observes the door hung on five pillars with brass sockets, brass below and gold above, for the entrance stood between the outer court and the holy place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
