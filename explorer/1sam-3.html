The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the child Samuel ministered unto the LORD before Eli. And the word of the LORD was precious in those days; there was no open vision.

2 And it came to pass at that time, when Eli was laid down in his place, and his eyes began to wax dim, that he could not see;

3 And ere the lamp of God went out in the temple of the LORD, where the ark of God was, and Samuel was laid down to sleep;

4 That the LORD called Samuel: and he answered, Here am I.

5 And he ran unto Eli, and said, Here am I; for thou calledst me. And he said, I called not; lie down again. And he went and lay down.

6 And the LORD called yet again, Samuel. And Samuel arose and went to Eli, and said, Here am I; for thou didst call me. And he answered, I called not, my son; lie down again.

7 Now Samuel did not yet know the LORD, neither was the word of the LORD yet revealed unto him.

8 And the LORD called Samuel again the third time. And he arose and went to Eli, and said, Here am I; for thou didst call me. And Eli perceived that the LORD had called the child.

9 Therefore Eli said unto Samuel, Go, lie down: and it shall be, if he call thee, that thou shalt say, Speak, LORD; for thy servant heareth. So Samuel went and lay down in his place.

10 And the LORD came, and stood, and called as at other times, Samuel, Samuel. Then Samuel answered, Speak; for thy servant heareth.

11 And the LORD said to Samuel, Behold, I will do a thing in Israel, at which both the ears of every one that heareth it shall tingle.

12 In that day I will perform against Eli all things which I have spoken concerning his house: when I begin, I will also make an end.

13 For I have told him that I will judge his house for ever for the iniquity which he knoweth; because his sons made themselves vile, and he restrained them not.

14 And therefore I have sworn unto the house of Eli, that the iniquity of Eli’s house shall not be purged with sacrifice nor offering for ever.

15 And Samuel lay until the morning, and opened the doors of the house of the LORD. And Samuel feared to shew Eli the vision.

16 Then Eli called Samuel, and said, Samuel, my son. And he answered, Here am I.

17 And he said, What is the thing that the LORD hath said unto thee? I pray thee hide it not from me: God do so to thee, and more also, if thou hide any thing from me of all the things that he said unto thee.

18 And Samuel told him every whit, and hid nothing from him. And he said, It is the LORD: let him do what seemeth him good.

19 And Samuel grew, and the LORD was with him, and did let none of his words fall to the ground.

20 And all Israel from Dan even to Beersheba knew that Samuel was established to be a prophet of the LORD.

21 And the LORD appeared again in Shiloh: for the LORD revealed himself to Samuel in Shiloh by the word of the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The child Samuel ministers before Eli in days when the word of the LORD is precious, rare, with no open vision. Three times the LORD calls Samuel in the night and three times he runs to Eli, until Eli perceives that the LORD has called the child and teaches him to answer, Speak, LORD, for thy servant heareth. The LORD comes and stands and calls as before, and tells Samuel the judgment on Eli's house, which Eli's sons made vile and he restrained not, a judgment not to be purged with sacrifice or offering for ever. Samuel fears to show Eli the vision, is pressed to hide nothing, tells him every whit, and Eli answers, It is the LORD, let him do what seemeth him good. Samuel grows, none of his words fall to the ground, and all Israel from Dan to Beersheba knows he is established a prophet. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The child Samuel ministered before Eli, and the word of the LORD was precious in those days, with no open vision. God's word was rare, which makes the call that follows all the more significant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Eli was laid down in his place, his eyes grown dim so he could not see — the old priest in decline, while the young servant slept near the ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Before the lamp of God went out in the temple where the ark was, Samuel lay down to sleep. The night setting, with the lamp still burning, marks the hour of God's visit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The LORD called Samuel, and he answered, Here am I. The call Divine grace designs to make effectual, Henry notes, will be repeated until it takes effect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Samuel ran to Eli, thinking Eli had called him, and Eli said, I called not; lie down again. Samuel's ready obedience mistook the voice, but it showed a servant's ear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The LORD called yet again, and Samuel again went to Eli, who again said he had not called. The repeated call patiently drew Samuel toward recognising God's voice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Samuel did not yet know the LORD, neither was the word of the LORD yet revealed to him. His ignorance was not unbelief but inexperience — he had not yet learned to distinguish God's voice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. At the third call Eli perceived that the LORD had called the child. Though it was a disgrace to Eli that God's call passed him by to Samuel, he still told the boy how to meet it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Eli instructed Samuel to lie down and answer, Speak, LORD, for thy servant heareth. Good words should be put into children's mouths betimes, Henry observes, so they may be prepared to learn Divine things; and elders should assist the younger rising up, even those soon to be preferred before them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The LORD came and stood and called as before, Samuel, Samuel, and Samuel answered, Speak, for thy servant heareth. The call made effectual at last found a listening servant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. God told Samuel he would do a thing in Israel at which both ears of everyone who heard it would tingle. The coming judgment on Eli's house would shock the whole nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. In that day God would perform against Eli all he had spoken concerning his house: when he began, he would also make an end. The sentence already pronounced would now be fully executed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. God would judge Eli's house for ever for the iniquity he knew, because his sons made themselves vile and he restrained them not. Those who do not restrain the sins of others when it is in their power, Henry warns, make themselves partakers of the guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Therefore God swore the iniquity of Eli's house would not be purged with sacrifice nor offering for ever. The very sacrifices his sons had profaned could not atone for this settled, unrestrained wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Samuel lay until morning and opened the doors of the house of the LORD, but feared to show Eli the vision. His first prophetic burden was a hard word for the man who had taught him to listen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Eli called Samuel, my son, and Samuel answered, Here am I — the same ready answer he had given all night, now to a summons he dreaded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Eli pressed him to hide nothing of what the LORD had said, under a solemn curse if he withheld any of it. Eli would hear the whole truth, however bitter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Samuel told him every whit and hid nothing, and Eli answered, It is the LORD: let him do what seemeth him good. Eli acknowledged the Lord's right to do as he saw good, assured he would do nothing wrong; Henry reads his meekness and patience as true repentance, accepting the punishment of his sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Samuel grew, and the LORD was with him, and let none of his words fall to the ground. All increase in wisdom and grace, Henry notes, is owing to the presence of God with us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. All Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew Samuel was established to be a prophet of the LORD. His unfailing word proved his calling to the whole land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. The LORD appeared again in Shiloh, revealing himself to Samuel by the word of the LORD. God graciously repeats his visits to those who receive them aright, and early piety became Samuel's greatest honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
