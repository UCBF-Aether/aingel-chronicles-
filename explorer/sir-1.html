The Text — Sirach 1 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 All wisdom cometh from the Lord, and is with him for ever.

2 Who can number the sand of the sea, and the drops of rain, and the days of eternity?

3 Who can find out the height of heaven, and the breadth of the earth, and the deep, and wisdom?

4 Wisdom hath been created before all things, and the understanding of prudence from everlasting.

5 The word of God most high is the fountain of wisdom; and her ways are everlasting commandments.

6 To whom hath the root of wisdom been revealed? or who hath known her wise counsels?

7 [Unto whom hath the knowledge of wisdom been made manifest? and who hath understood her great experience?]

8 There is one wise and greatly to be feared, the Lord sitting upon his throne.

9 He created her, and saw her, and numbered her, and poured her out upon all his works.

10 She is with all flesh according to his gift, and he hath given her to them that love him.

11 The fear of the Lord is honour, and glory, and gladness, and a crown of rejoicing.

12 The fear of the Lord maketh a merry heart, and giveth joy, and gladness, and a long life.

13 Whoso feareth the Lord, it shall go well with him at the last, and he shall find favour in the day of his death.

14 To fear the Lord is the beginning of wisdom: and it was created with the faithful in the womb.

15 She hath built an everlasting foundation with men, and she shall continue with their seed.

16 To fear the Lord is fulness of wisdom, and filleth men with her fruits.

17 She filleth all their house with things desirable, and the garners with her increase.

18 The fear of the Lord is a crown of wisdom, making peace and perfect health to flourish; both which are the gifts of God: and it enlargeth their rejoicing that love him.

19 Wisdom raineth down skill and knowledge of understanding standing, and exalteth them to honour that hold her fast.

20 The root of wisdom is to fear the Lord, and the branches thereof are long life.

21 The fear of the Lord driveth away sins: and where it is present, it turneth away wrath.

22 A furious man cannot be justified; for the sway of his fury shall be his destruction.

23 A patient man will tear for a time, and afterward joy shall spring up unto him.

24 He will hide his words for a time, and the lips of many shall declare his wisdom.

25 The parables of knowledge are in the treasures of wisdom: but godliness is an abomination to a sinner.

26 If thou desire wisdom, keep the commandments, and the Lord shall give her unto thee.

27 For the fear of the Lord is wisdom and instruction: and faith and meekness are his delight.

28 Distrust not the fear of the Lord when thou art poor: and come not unto him with a double heart.

29 Be not an hypocrite in the sight of men, and take good heed what thou speakest.

30 Exalt not thyself, lest thou fall, and bring dishonour upon thy soul, and so God discover thy secrets, and cast thee down in the midst of the congregation, because thou camest not in truth to the fear of the Lord, but thy heart is full of deceit.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter sets the book's foundation: all wisdom belongs to the Lord alone and has been with him for ever, and the fear of the Lord is both its beginning and its fullness. From that root grow patience under provocation, restraint in speech, obedience to the commandments, and humility instead of hypocrisy or self-exaltation. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. All wisdom belongs to the Lord alone and has always been with him. Oesterley stresses this opening as the text for all that follows: wisdom is God's peculiar possession, dispensed as he wills. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. No one can count the sand, the raindrops, or the days of eternity, and the same limit bounds human knowledge. The verse piles up uncountables to show that divine wisdom cannot be measured by man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. The height of heaven, the breadth of earth, and the deep are all beyond man's measuring, and wisdom is more unsearchable still. Even the created world defeats our instruments, so its Maker's wisdom must lie further beyond us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. Wisdom existed before anything was made, and prudent understanding is from everlasting. Wisdom is not a human discovery but a reality older than creation itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. The word of God most high is the fountain from which wisdom flows, and her ways are his everlasting commandments. Wisdom is therefore found not apart from God's word but in obeying it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. The root of wisdom has been revealed to no one by his own power, and her wise counsels are known to no unaided mind. Oesterley notes that root here means the essence of wisdom, not merely its origin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. The knowledge of wisdom and her great experience have not been laid open to man as a possession. The bracketed verse repeats the question of verse 6: wisdom is received, not seized. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. There is only one who is wise and greatly to be feared: the Lord seated on his throne. All claims to wisdom are judged against this one sovereign wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. God himself created wisdom, saw her, numbered her, and poured her out upon all his works. Wisdom is woven into creation by God's own ordering, portioned out to every creature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Wisdom is present with all flesh in measured gift, but God gives her freely to those who love him. Oesterley explains the contrast: all receive something, while those who love God receive without restriction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

  • Verse 21. The fear of the Lord drives away sins, and where it is present it turns away wrath. Reverence works as a guard, checking both wrongdoing and the anger that follows it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

  • Verse 26. If you desire wisdom, keep the commandments, and the Lord will give her to you. The way to wisdom is practical obedience, and the gift follows the keeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 27. The fear of the Lord is wisdom and instruction, and faith and meekness are his delight. God delights not in showy religion but in trusting, humble obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 28. Do not distrust the fear of the Lord when you are poor, and do not come to him with a double heart. Poverty is no reason to abandon reverence, and divided loyalty cancels the approach to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 29. Do not be a hypocrite before men, and take good heed what you speak. Outward pretence and careless speech both betray a heart that is not single toward God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 30. Do not exalt yourself, lest you fall and bring dishonour on your soul, and God expose your secrets and cast you down in the congregation. Pride that comes to God without truth ends in public disgrace, because the heart was full of deceit rather than fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
