The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Kings 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants unto Solomon; for he had heard that they had anointed him king in the room of his father: for Hiram was ever a lover of David.

2 And Solomon sent to Hiram, saying,

3 Thou knowest how that David my father could not build an house unto the name of the LORD his God for the wars which were about him on every side, until the LORD put them under the soles of his feet.

4 But now the LORD my God hath given me rest on every side, so that there is neither adversary nor evil occurrent.

5 And, behold, I purpose to build an house unto the name of the LORD my God, as the LORD spake unto David my father, saying, Thy son, whom I will set upon thy throne in thy room, he shall build an house unto my name.

6 Now therefore command thou that they hew me cedar trees out of Lebanon; and my servants shall be with thy servants: and unto thee will I give hire for thy servants according to all that thou shalt appoint: for thou knowest that there is not among us any that can skill to hew timber like unto the Sidonians.

7 And it came to pass, when Hiram heard the words of Solomon, that he rejoiced greatly, and said, Blessed be the LORD this day, which hath given unto David a wise son over this great people.

8 And Hiram sent to Solomon, saying, I have considered the things which thou sentest to me for: and I will do all thy desire concerning timber of cedar, and concerning timber of fir.

9 My servants shall bring them down from Lebanon unto the sea: and I will convey them by sea in floats unto the place that thou shalt appoint me, and will cause them to be discharged there, and thou shalt receive them: and thou shalt accomplish my desire, in giving food for my household.

10 So Hiram gave Solomon cedar trees and fir trees according to all his desire.

11 And Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand measures of wheat for food to his household, and twenty measures of pure oil: thus gave Solomon to Hiram year by year.

12 And the LORD gave Solomon wisdom, as he promised him: and there was peace between Hiram and Solomon; and they two made a league together.

13 And king Solomon raised a levy out of all Israel; and the levy was thirty thousand men.

14 And he sent them to Lebanon, ten thousand a month by courses: a month they were in Lebanon, and two months at home: and Adoniram was over the levy.

15 And Solomon had threescore and ten thousand that bare burdens, and fourscore thousand hewers in the mountains;

16 Beside the chief of Solomon’s officers which were over the work, three thousand and three hundred, which ruled over the people that wrought in the work.

17 And the king commanded, and they brought great stones, costly stones, and hewed stones, to lay the foundation of the house.

18 And Solomon’s builders and Hiram’s builders did hew them, and the stonesquarers: so they prepared timber and stones to build the house.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Hiram of Tyre, David's old friend, sends to Solomon, and the two kings agree: cedar and fir from Lebanon, cut by Sidonians, floated down by sea, in exchange for wheat and oil year by year. Solomon levies a workforce of thirty thousand to the mountain, and the two kingdoms are at peace. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hiram of Tyre sent servants to Solomon on hearing of his anointing, for he had ever loved David, and friendship passed to the son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Solomon sent to Hiram, opening the temple business by message rather than by force, as befits a work of peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Solomon explained that David could not build the house because wars were round about him until God put his enemies under his feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Now God had given Solomon rest on every side, with neither adversary nor evil occurrence, the very season for building God's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Solomon purposed to build a house for the Lord's name, as God had spoken to David, that his son would build it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He asked Hiram to command cedars hewn in Lebanon, with his servants beside Hiram's, and wages as Hiram should appoint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Hiram rejoiced greatly and blessed the Lord for giving David a wise son over a great people, a Gentile king honouring Israel's God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Hiram agreed to furnish cedar and fir according to Solomon's desire, joining Tyre's skill to Israel's work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. His servants would bring timber down to the sea and float it to the place Solomon appointed, then Solomon's men would take it up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Hiram gave cedar and fir according to all Solomon's desire, a full supply for a house meant to be costly and lasting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Each side made good its engagement, corn for timber, justice kept on both sides of the bargain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Solomon's wisdom ripened mutual love into a lasting league, for wisdom teaches us to strengthen friendship with those proved honest and fair. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Solomon raised a levy of thirty thousand men out of all Israel, a service that was honourable rotation, not slavery of Israelites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Ten thousand a month went to Lebanon in courses, a month there and two months at home, so the burden was shared and bearable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Seventy thousand bore burdens and eighty thousand hewed in the mountains, the great labour force behind the temple's stone and timber. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Besides these, three thousand three hundred chief officers ruled over the work, a hierarchy that kept so vast a project in order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The king commanded great, costly, hewed stones for the foundation, since the house of God was to rest on stone fit for its dignity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Many hands and many eyes went to the preparation, for the undertaking was vast, and the temple was only the chief of Solomon's buildings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
