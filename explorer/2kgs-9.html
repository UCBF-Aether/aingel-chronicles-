The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Elisha the prophet called one of the children of the prophets, and said unto him, Gird up thy loins, and take this box of oil in thine hand, and go to Ramothgilead:

2 And when thou comest thither, look out there Jehu the son of Jehoshaphat the son of Nimshi, and go in, and make him arise up from among his brethren, and carry him to an inner chamber;

3 Then take the box of oil, and pour it on his head, and say, Thus saith the LORD, I have anointed thee king over Israel. Then open the door, and flee, and tarry not.

4 So the young man, even the young man the prophet, went to Ramothgilead.

5 And when he came, behold, the captains of the host were sitting; and he said, I have an errand to thee, O captain. And Jehu said, Unto which of all us? And he said, To thee, O captain.

6 And he arose, and went into the house; and he poured the oil on his head, and said unto him, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, I have anointed thee king over the people of the LORD, even over Israel.

7 And thou shalt smite the house of Ahab thy master, that I may avenge the blood of my servants the prophets, and the blood of all the servants of the LORD, at the hand of Jezebel.

8 For the whole house of Ahab shall perish: and I will cut off from Ahab him that pisseth against the wall, and him that is shut up and left in Israel:

9 And I will make the house of Ahab like the house of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, and like the house of Baasha the son of Ahijah:

10 And the dogs shall eat Jezebel in the portion of Jezreel, and there shall be none to bury her. And he opened the door, and fled.

11 Then Jehu came forth to the servants of his lord: and one said unto him, Is all well? wherefore came this mad fellow to thee? And he said unto them, Ye know the man, and his communication.

12 And they said, It is false; tell us now. And he said, Thus and thus spake he to me, saying, Thus saith the LORD, I have anointed thee king over Israel.

13 Then they hasted, and took every man his garment, and put it under him on the top of the stairs, and blew with trumpets, saying, Jehu is king.

14 So Jehu the son of Jehoshaphat the son of Nimshi conspired against Joram. (Now Joram had kept Ramothgilead, he and all Israel, because of Hazael king of Syria.

15 But king Joram was returned to be healed in Jezreel of the wounds which the Syrians had given him, when he fought with Hazael king of Syria.) And Jehu said, If it be your minds, then let none go forth nor escape out of the city to go to tell it in Jezreel.

16 So Jehu rode in a chariot, and went to Jezreel; for Joram lay there. And Ahaziah king of Judah was come down to see Joram.

17 And there stood a watchman on the tower in Jezreel, and he spied the company of Jehu as he came, and said, I see a company. And Joram said, Take an horseman, and send to meet them, and let him say, Is it peace?

18 So there went one on horseback to meet him, and said, Thus saith the king, Is it peace? And Jehu said, What hast thou to do with peace? turn thee behind me. And the watchman told, saying, The messenger came to them, but he cometh not again.

19 Then he sent out a second on horseback, which came to them, and said, Thus saith the king, Is it peace? And Jehu answered, What hast thou to do with peace? turn thee behind me.

20 And the watchman told, saying, He came even unto them, and cometh not again: and the driving is like the driving of Jehu the son of Nimshi; for he driveth furiously.

21 And Joram said, Make ready. And his chariot was made ready. And Joram king of Israel and Ahaziah king of Judah went out, each in his chariot, and they went out against Jehu, and met him in the portion of Naboth the Jezreelite.

22 And it came to pass, when Joram saw Jehu, that he said, Is it peace, Jehu? And he answered, What peace, so long as the whoredoms of thy mother Jezebel and her witchcrafts are so many?

23 And Joram turned his hands, and fled, and said to Ahaziah, There is treachery, O Ahaziah.

24 And Jehu drew a bow with his full strength, and smote Jehoram between his arms, and the arrow went out at his heart, and he sunk down in his chariot.

25 Then said Jehu to Bidkar his captain, Take up, and cast him in the portion of the field of Naboth the Jezreelite: for remember how that, when I and thou rode together after Ahab his father, the LORD laid this burden upon him;

26 Surely I have seen yesterday the blood of Naboth, and the blood of his sons, saith the LORD; and I will requite thee in this plat, saith the LORD. Now therefore take and cast him into the plat of ground, according to the word of the LORD.

27 But when Ahaziah the king of Judah saw this, he fled by the way of the garden house. And Jehu followed after him, and said, Smite him also in the chariot. And they did so at the going up to Gur, which is by Ibleam. And he fled to Megiddo, and died there.

28 And his servants carried him in a chariot to Jerusalem, and buried him in his sepulchre with his fathers in the city of David.

29 And in the eleventh year of Joram the son of Ahab began Ahaziah to reign over Judah.

30 And when Jehu was come to Jezreel, Jezebel heard of it; and she painted her face, and tired her head, and looked out at a window.

31 And as Jehu entered in at the gate, she said, Had Zimri peace, who slew his master?

32 And he lifted up his face to the window, and said, Who is on my side? who? And there looked out to him two or three eunuchs.

33 And he said, Throw her down. So they threw her down: and some of her blood was sprinkled on the wall, and on the horses: and he trode her under foot.

34 And when he was come in, he did eat and drink, and said, Go, see now this cursed woman, and bury her: for she is a king’s daughter.

35 And they went to bury her: but they found no more of her than the skull, and the feet, and the palms of her hands.

36 Wherefore they came again, and told him. And he said, This is the word of the LORD, which he spake by his servant Elijah the Tishbite, saying, In the portion of Jezreel shall dogs eat the flesh of Jezebel:

37 And the carcase of Jezebel shall be as dung upon the face of the field in the portion of Jezreel; so that they shall not say, This is Jezebel.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A young prophet anoints Jehu king in secret and flees, and Jehu drives furiously to Jezreel, where Joram and Ahaziah are killed and Jezebel is thrown from her window and eaten by dogs, as Elijah had said. Jehu's revolution has begun in blood. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Elisha called one of the children of the prophets, telling him to gird up his loins, take a box of oil, and go to Ramothgilead. JFB notes this errand fulfilled the last commission given to Elijah at Horeb, and Ramothgilead was a fortress of defence against the Syrians east of Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The messenger was to look out Jehu son of Jehoshaphat son of Nimshi, take him from among his brethren into an inner chamber. JFB explains the inner chamber ensured the messenger's safety and prevented obstruction of the business. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He was to pour the oil on Jehu's head and say the LORD had anointed him king over Israel, then open the door and flee without tarrying. JFB notes this was only part of the message, the full announcement following in verses 7-10, and the flight was for fear of being surprised by court spies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The young prophet went to Ramothgilead. JFB calls his ready undertaking of this delicate and hazardous mission an eminent proof of piety and obedience, and the anointing through a commissioned prophet a divine intimation of investiture with sovereign power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. When he came, the captains of the host were sitting, and he said he had an errand to Jehu. JFB's section "Jehu is anointed" covers this approach to the army's commanders at Ramothgilead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Jehu arose and went into the house, where the prophet poured oil on his head and declared the LORD had anointed him king over Israel. JFB notes anointing was sometimes done long before actual possession of the throne, and Elijah had left this commission in Elisha's charge until God's time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Jehu was to smite the house of Ahab his master, that God might avenge the blood of his servants the prophets and all the LORD's servants at Jezebel's hand. JFB notes the full announcement of the message is given here in verses 7-10. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The whole house of Ahab was to perish, cut off to the last male. JFB's exposition of the anointing commission presents this as the divine sentence on Ahab's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The house of Ahab would be made like the house of Jeroboam son of Nebat and the house of Baasha son of Ahijah. JFB treats this as the pattern of dynastic judgment already seen in Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Dogs would eat Jezebel in the portion of Jezreel, with none to bury her, and then the prophet opened the door and fled. JFB notes the portion of Jezreel was what had formerly been Naboth's vineyard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. When Jehu came out to his lord's servants, one asked why this mad fellow had come to him. JFB explains the attendants knew the stranger was a prophet by his garb, gestures, and address, and soldiers readily concluded such persons crackbrained from their contempt of the world and grotesque actions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. They hasted, took every man his garment, put it under Jehu on the top of the stairs, and blew trumpets saying, "Jehu is king." JFB explains the upper cloaks spread on the ground were a token of homage, and the top of the stairs, the most conspicuous place of an Oriental structure, served as a substitute throne, while Jehu's popularity with the army favoured Providence's design. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Jehu son of Jehoshaphat son of Nimshi conspired against Joram, who had kept Ramothgilead with the army. JFB notes Joram was guarding or besieging Ramothgilead with most of Israel's force, but his wounds had compelled his retirement, leaving the troops under Jehu. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. King Joram had returned to Jezreel to be healed of the wounds the Syrians gave him at Ramah, and Jehu said none should escape the city to tell it in Jezreel. JFB notes the king's wounds had compelled his retirement from the scene of action. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Jehu rode in a chariot to Jezreel, where Joram lay, and Ahaziah king of Judah had come down to see him. JFB describes Jehu, full of ambitious designs, crossing the Jordan to execute his commission on the house of Ahab. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. A watchman on Jezreel's tower spied Jehu's company coming and told Joram. JFB describes the tower commanding the whole region eastward toward the Jordan, so that Jehu's retinue could be descried from Gilboa and Beth-shan, and a horseman was dispatched to question their approach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. A horseman went to meet Jehu asking, "Is it peace?" and Jehu answered, "What hast thou to do with peace? turn thee behind me," and the watchman reported the messenger had been detained. JFB's account follows this first messenger meeting Jehu miles out on the plain and being turned into the rear of the advancing troops. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. A second horseman was sent with the same question, received the same answer, and likewise came not back. JFB notes the second messenger met Jehu closer to the city and was likewise turned into the rear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The watchman reported that the messenger had come to them but returned not, and the driving was like Jehu son of Nimshi's, for he drove furiously. JFB quotes this very observation as the moment the alarmed monarch sensed his impending danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Joram ordered his chariot made ready, and he and Ahaziah king of Judah went out against Jehu, meeting him in the portion of Naboth the Jezreelite. JFB describes the two sovereigns ascending their chariots for a feeble resistance, meeting Jehu where the portion of Naboth lay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Jehu drew a bow with his full strength and smote Jehoram between his arms, and the arrow went out at his heart, so he sank down in his chariot. JFB notes Joram was quickly dispatched by an arrow from Jehu's strong arm in the portion of Naboth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Jehu told Bidkar his captain to take up Jehoram and cast him in the portion of Naboth's field, recalling the LORD's word. JFB explains this fulfilled the doom pronounced on Ahab, deferred on his repentance to be executed on his son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Jehu recalled that the LORD had said he had seen the blood of Naboth and his sons and would requite him in this plat. JFB notes that although the sons' deaths are not expressly mentioned, they are plainly implied in the confiscation of Naboth's property. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. When Ahaziah king of Judah saw this, he fled by the way of the garden house, and Jehu followed after him. JFB heads this section "Ahaziah is slain," noting Ahaziah was grandnephew to Joram and great-grandson to Ahab, and locating Ibleam near Megiddo and Gur as an adjoining hill. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. When Jehu came to Jezreel, Jezebel heard of it, painted her face, tired her head, and looked out at a window. JFB explains she painted her eyes after the universal Eastern custom of staining eyelids with black powder, aiming by royal attire not to captivate but to overawe Jehu. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. They went to bury Jezebel but found no more of her than the skull, the feet, and the palms of her hands. JFB notes the dog has a rooted aversion to prey on human hands and feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. They reported back to Jehu, and he said this was the LORD's word spoken by Elijah the Tishbite, that dogs would eat Jezebel's flesh in Jezreel's portion. JFB notes Jehu's statement was not a literal but a paraphrased quotation of Elijah's prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Jezebel's carcase would be as dung upon the field in Jezreel's portion, so that none could say, "This is Jezebel." JFB heads this closing section "Jezebel eaten by dogs," fulfilling Elijah's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
