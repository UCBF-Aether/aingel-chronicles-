The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ruth 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then went Boaz up to the gate, and sat him down there: and, behold, the kinsman of whom Boaz spake came by; unto whom he said, Ho, such a one! turn aside, sit down here. And he turned aside, and sat down.

2 And he took ten men of the elders of the city, and said, Sit ye down here. And they sat down.

3 And he said unto the kinsman, Naomi, that is come again out of the country of Moab, selleth a parcel of land, which was our brother Elimelech’s:

4 And I thought to advertise thee, saying, Buy it before the inhabitants, and before the elders of my people. If thou wilt redeem it, redeem it: but if thou wilt not redeem it, then tell me, that I may know: for there is none to redeem it beside thee; and I am after thee. And he said, I will redeem it.

5 Then said Boaz, What day thou buyest the field of the hand of Naomi, thou must buy it also of Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance.

6 And the kinsman said, I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I mar mine own inheritance: redeem thou my right to thyself; for I cannot redeem it.

7 Now this was the manner in former time in Israel concerning redeeming and concerning changing, for to confirm all things; a man plucked off his shoe, and gave it to his neighbour: and this was a testimony in Israel.

8 Therefore the kinsman said unto Boaz, Buy it for thee. So he drew off his shoe.

9 And Boaz said unto the elders, and unto all the people, Ye are witnesses this day, that I have bought all that was Elimelech’s, and all that was Chilion’s and Mahlon’s, of the hand of Naomi.

10 Moreover Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon, have I purchased to be my wife, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance, that the name of the dead be not cut off from among his brethren, and from the gate of his place: ye are witnesses this day.

11 And all the people that were in the gate, and the elders, said, We are witnesses. The LORD make the woman that is come into thine house like Rachel and like Leah, which two did build the house of Israel: and do thou worthily in Ephratah, and be famous in Bethlehem:

12 And let thy house be like the house of Pharez, whom Tamar bare unto Judah, of the seed which the LORD shall give thee of this young woman.

13 So Boaz took Ruth, and she was his wife: and when he went in unto her, the LORD gave her conception, and she bare a son.

14 And the women said unto Naomi, Blessed be the LORD, which hath not left thee this day without a kinsman, that his name may be famous in Israel.

15 And he shall be unto thee a restorer of thy life, and a nourisher of thine old age: for thy daughter in law, which loveth thee, which is better to thee than seven sons, hath born him.

16 And Naomi took the child, and laid it in her bosom, and became nurse unto it.

17 And the women her neighbours gave it a name, saying, There is a son born to Naomi; and they called his name Obed: he is the father of Jesse, the father of David.

18 Now these are the generations of Pharez: Pharez begat Hezron,

19 And Hezron begat Ram, and Ram begat Amminadab,

20 And Amminadab begat Nahshon, and Nahshon begat Salmon,

21 And Salmon begat Boaz, and Boaz begat Obed,

22 And Obed begat Jesse, and Jesse begat David.

How to read the labels

  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Boaz goes up to the gate, the place of legal business, takes ten elders as witnesses, and lays the bargain before the nearer kinsman: Naomi sells Elimelech's parcel, and buying it means also buying Ruth the Moabitess, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance. The kinsman, fearing to mar his own inheritance, resigns by the old custom of plucking off his shoe, and Boaz declares before all the people that he has bought all Elimelech's, Chilion's and Mahlon's, and Ruth to be his wife. The witnesses pray the LORD will make Ruth like Rachel and Leah, who built the house of Israel, and Boaz's house like the house of Pharez, whom Tamar bare to Judah; a son is born, named Obed, father of Jesse, father of David, and the book closes with the genealogy from Pharez to David. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Boaz went up to the gate and sat down, and called the kinsman of whom he had spoken to turn aside and sit down. Henry notes the whole matter was settled in the regular, legal manner under Moses' laws about inheritances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Boaz took ten men of the elders of the city and had them sit down as witnesses. Henry's emphasis is fair and open dealing in all matters of contract and trade, which all who would approve themselves true Israelites without guile must make conscience of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Boaz told the kinsman that Naomi, returned from Moab, was selling a parcel of land that had been their brother Elimelech's. Henry treats this as the opening of the legal bargain over the inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Boaz invited the kinsman to redeem the land before the inhabitants and elders, or declare if he would not, since Boaz was next after him. Henry notes the settlement proceeded openly, in the regular and legal manner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The kinsman first said he would redeem it, but when Boaz added that buying the field from Naomi meant also buying it of Ruth the Moabitess, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance, the bargain's true conditions emerged. Henry observes that many are likewise shy of the great redemption when they count its cost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The kinsman then refused, saying he could not redeem it lest he mar his own inheritance, and told Boaz to redeem his right to himself. Henry compares him to those who have nothing to say against religion but will part with it for fear of marring their worldly inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. In former time in Israel, a man plucked off his shoe and gave it to his neighbour to confirm a redemption or exchange, and this was a testimony in Israel. Henry cites this as the legal custom by which the right was resigned to Boaz. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. So the kinsman drew off his shoe and said to Boaz, "Buy it for thee." Henry notes the right was thus resigned, and adds that honesty will be found the best policy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Boaz then declared before the elders and all the people that they were witnesses he had bought all that was Elimelech's, and Chilion's and Mahlon's, from Naomi's hand. Henry observes that men seize opportunities for increasing their estates, but few know the value of godliness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Boaz further declared he had purchased Ruth the Moabitess, Mahlon's widow, to be his wife, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance. Henry contrasts Boaz with the kinsman: God honoured Boaz by bringing him into the line of the Messiah, while the fearful kinsman's name and family were forgotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. All the people at the gate and the elders said they were witnesses, and prayed the LORD would make Ruth like Rachel and Leah, who built the house of Israel, and that Boaz would do worthily in Ephratah and be famous in Bethlehem. Henry notes prayer to God attended the marriage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. They prayed Boaz's house would be like the house of Pharez, whom Tamar bare to Judah, of the seed the LORD would give him of Ruth. Henry's theme is that prayer attended the marriage and praise would attend the birth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Boaz took Ruth, she became his wife, and the LORD gave her conception, and she bare a son. Henry notes that through this son thousands and myriads were born to God, and Ruth, a Gentile witness, became a lineal ancestor of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The women said to Naomi, "Blessed be the LORD, which hath not left thee this day without a kinsman, that his name may be famous in Israel." Henry observes praise to God attended the birth of the child, as prayer had attended the marriage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. They said the child would be a restorer of Naomi's life and a nourisher of her old age, since her daughter-in-law, who loved her and was better to her than seven sons, had born him. Henry presents Ruth as a witness to the Gentile world that God had not utterly forsaken them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Naomi took the child, laid it in her bosom, and became nurse to it. Henry's passage frames this as the happy close of Naomi's bitterness: the empty returner now nurses her redeemer-grandson. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The women her neighbours gave the child a name, saying a son was born to Naomi, and they called his name Obed, the father of Jesse, the father of David. Henry notes here is the descent of David from Ruth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. These are the generations of Pharez: Pharez begat Hezron. Henry closes the book by pointing from this genealogy to Bethlehem-Judah's greater wonder yet to come, when another child of the same race would draw princes and wise men with treasures to his feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Hezron begat Ram, and Ram begat Amminadab. Henry traces this line forward as the descent of David from Ruth, leading toward the Messiah in whom all nations shall be blessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Amminadab begat Nahshon, and Nahshon begat Salmon. Henry's closing note keeps the genealogy's purpose in view: it is the line through which Ruth became instrumental in the happiness of all who shall be saved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Salmon begat Boaz, and Boaz begat Obed. Henry notes Boaz's honour: God brought him into the line of the Messiah, unlike the kinsman who feared lessening himself and was forgotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Obed begat Jesse, and Jesse begat David. Henry ends with the book's great horizon: in that Seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed, and his name shall endure for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
