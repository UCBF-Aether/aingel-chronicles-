The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Romans 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.

2 And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.

3 For I say, through the grace given unto me, to every man that is among you, not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think; but to think soberly, according as God hath dealt to every man the measure of faith.

4 For as we have many members in one body, and all members have not the same office:

5 So we, being many, are one body in Christ, and every one members one of another.

6 Having then gifts differing according to the grace that is given to us, whether prophecy, let us prophesy according to the proportion of faith;

7 Or ministry, let us wait on our ministering: or he that teacheth, on teaching;

8 Or he that exhorteth, on exhortation: he that giveth, let him do it with simplicity; he that ruleth, with diligence; he that sheweth mercy, with cheerfulness.

9 Let love be without dissimulation. Abhor that which is evil; cleave to that which is good.

10 Be kindly affectioned one to another with brotherly love; in honour preferring one another;

11 Not slothful in business; fervent in spirit; serving the Lord;

12 Rejoicing in hope; patient in tribulation; continuing instant in prayer;

13 Distributing to the necessity of saints; given to hospitality.

14 Bless them which persecute you: bless, and curse not.

15 Rejoice with them that do rejoice, and weep with them that weep.

16 Be of the same mind one toward another. Mind not high things, but condescend to men of low estate. Be not wise in your own conceits.

17 Recompense to no man evil for evil. Provide things honest in the sight of all men.

18 If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.

19 Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.

20 Therefore if thine enemy hunger, feed him; if he thirst, give him drink: for in so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head.

21 Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In view of God's mercies, Paul urges believers to present their bodies as a living sacrifice and be transformed by the renewing of their minds, then describes the church as one body with many gifts differing according to grace. The chapter turns practical: love without dissimulation, honouring one another, blessing persecutors, weeping and rejoicing with others, and overcoming evil with good. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. By God's mercies, Paul beseeches the brethren to present their bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, their reasonable service. Henry notes that the body itself, not only the soul, is offered in gospel worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Be not conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of the mind, that you may prove God's good, acceptable, and perfect will. JFB stresses that transformation begins in the mind, not in outward imitation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Through the grace given to Paul, he tells every man not to think of himself more highly than he ought, but soberly, as God has dealt to each the measure of faith. Henry observes that humility is the first fruit of renewed thinking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. As we have many members in one body, and all members do not have the same office, so we, being many, are one body in Christ. JFB notes that diversity of function does not break unity of body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. We are every one members one of another. Henry stresses that mutual membership makes mutual service a duty, not an option. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Having gifts differing according to the grace given us, whether prophecy, let us prophesy according to the proportion of faith. JFB notes that gifts are measured by grace, and must be used within that measure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Or ministry, let us wait on our ministering; or he that teaches, on teaching. Henry observes that faithfulness in one's own calling matters more than reaching for another's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He that exhorts, on exhortation; he that gives, with simplicity; he that rules, with diligence; he that shows mercy, with cheerfulness. JFB notes that each gift has its proper manner: giving without ostentation, mercy without gloom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Let love be without dissimulation; abhor what is evil; cleave to what is good. Henry stresses that unfeigned love hates evil as truly as it loves good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Be kindly affectioned to one another with brotherly love, in honour preferring one another. JFB notes that honour is to be given, not grasped. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Not slothful in business, fervent in spirit, serving the Lord. Henry observes that diligence in ordinary work and fervour in spirit belong together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Rejoicing in hope, patient in tribulation, continuing instant in prayer. JFB notes the threefold posture: joy for the future, patience in the present, prayer always. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Distributing to the necessity of saints, given to hospitality. Henry stresses that generosity to fellow believers and welcome to strangers are both marks of love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Bless those who persecute you; bless, and curse not. JFB notes that blessing persecutors is the direct opposite of the natural heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Rejoice with those who rejoice, and weep with those who weep. Henry observes that sympathy in both joy and sorrow is love's truest test. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Be of the same mind one toward another; mind not high things, but condescend to men of low estate; be not wise in your own conceits. JFB notes that lowliness, not ambition, preserves unity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Recompense no man evil for evil; provide things honest in the sight of all men. Henry stresses that Christian conduct must be honest even before unbelievers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. If it is possible, as much as lies in you, live peaceably with all men. JFB notes the double qualification: peace is pursued as far as possible and as far as it depends on us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Do not avenge yourselves, but give place to wrath, for it is written, vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord. Henry observes that leaving vengeance to God is faith, not weakness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. If your enemy hungers, feed him; if he thirsts, give him drink; in so doing you shall heap coals of fire on his head. JFB notes that kindness is the fire that melts an enemy, not that tortures him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good. Henry stresses the final rule: evil is conquered only by good, never by evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • living sacrifice. Paul's image for the believer's whole bodily life offered to God, holy and acceptable, as reasonable service. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • dissimulation. Pretence or hypocrisy; Paul says love must be without it. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • heap coals of fire. Paul's proverb for kindness to an enemy, feeding him if hungry and giving him drink if thirsty. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
