The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 34 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Command the children of Israel, and say unto them, When ye come into the land of Canaan; (this is the land that shall fall unto you for an inheritance, even the land of Canaan with the coasts thereof:)

3 Then your south quarter shall be from the wilderness of Zin along by the coast of Edom, and your south border shall be the outmost coast of the salt sea eastward:

4 And your border shall turn from the south to the ascent of Akrabbim, and pass on to Zin: and the going forth thereof shall be from the south to Kadeshbarnea, and shall go on to Hazaraddar, and pass on to Azmon:

5 And the border shall fetch a compass from Azmon unto the river of Egypt, and the goings out of it shall be at the sea.

6 And as for the western border, ye shall even have the great sea for a border: this shall be your west border.

7 And this shall be your north border: from the great sea ye shall point out for you mount Hor:

8 From mount Hor ye shall point out your border unto the entrance of Hamath; and the goings forth of the border shall be to Zedad:

9 And the border shall go on to Ziphron, and the goings out of it shall be at Hazarenan: this shall be your north border.

10 And ye shall point out your east border from Hazarenan to Shepham:

11 And the coast shall go down from Shepham to Riblah, on the east side of Ain; and the border shall descend, and shall reach unto the side of the sea of Chinnereth eastward:

12 And the border shall go down to Jordan, and the goings out of it shall be at the salt sea: this shall be your land with the coasts thereof round about.

13 And Moses commanded the children of Israel, saying, This is the land which ye shall inherit by lot, which the LORD commanded to give unto the nine tribes, and to the half tribe:

14 For the tribe of the children of Reuben according to the house of their fathers, and the tribe of the children of Gad according to the house of their fathers, have received their inheritance; and half the tribe of Manasseh have received their inheritance:

15 The two tribes and the half tribe have received their inheritance on this side Jordan near Jericho eastward, toward the sunrising.

16 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

17 These are the names of the men which shall divide the land unto you: Eleazar the priest, and Joshua the son of Nun.

18 And ye shall take one prince of every tribe, to divide the land by inheritance.

19 And the names of the men are these: Of the tribe of Judah, Caleb the son of Jephunneh.

20 And of the tribe of the children of Simeon, Shemuel the son of Ammihud.

21 Of the tribe of Benjamin, Elidad the son of Chislon.

22 And the prince of the tribe of the children of Dan, Bukki the son of Jogli.

23 The prince of the children of Joseph, for the tribe of the children of Manasseh, Hanniel the son of Ephod.

24 And the prince of the tribe of the children of Ephraim, Kemuel the son of Shiphtan.

25 And the prince of the tribe of the children of Zebulun, Elizaphan the son of Parnach.

26 And the prince of the tribe of the children of Issachar, Paltiel the son of Azzan.

27 And the prince of the tribe of the children of Asher, Ahihud the son of Shelomi.

28 And the prince of the tribe of the children of Naphtali, Pedahel the son of Ammihud.

29 These are they whom the LORD commanded to divide the inheritance unto the children of Israel in the land of Canaan.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter fixes the borders of the land Israel is to inherit in Canaan: from the wilderness of Zin and the Salt Sea on the south, around to the Great Sea on the west, up to Mount Hor and Hamath on the north, and down the east side by the Sea of Chinnereth to the Salt Sea. It then names the men who will divide the inheritance: Eleazar the priest and Joshua the son of Nun, with one prince from each of the ten tribes that have not yet received land east of the Jordan, Reuben, Gad, and half Manasseh having already received theirs. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. Command Israel, and say to them, when you come into the land of Canaan, this is the land that shall fall to you for an inheritance, even the land of Canaan with the coasts thereof. JFB notes these details mark the general boundary of Israel's inheritance west of Jordan, which Israel never actually possessed in full, even when most extended under David and Solomon, while Henry notes Canaan as bounded here was but about one hundred and sixty miles long and fifty broad, yet this was the country promised to the father of the faithful, the vineyard of the Lord, the garden enclosed, whose narrowness was made up by the fruitfulness of its soil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Then your south quarter shall be from the wilderness of Zin along by the coast of Edom, and your south border shall be the outmost coast of the salt sea eastward. JFB notes the southern line is the most difficult to trace, beginning where the wilderness of Zin touches Edom's border, so that the boundary extends eastward from the Dead Sea's extremity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. And your border shall turn from the south to the ascent of Akrabbim, and pass on to Zin, and the going forth thereof shall be from the south to Kadesh-barnea, and shall go on to Hazar-addar, and pass on to Azmon. JFB notes the line winds around the precipitous ridge of Akrabbim, meaning scorpions, thought to be the high and difficult Pass of Safeh, crossing the great valley of the Arabah that reaches from the Dead Sea to the Red Sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. And the border shall fetch a compass from Azmon to the river of Egypt, and the goings out of it shall be at the sea. JFB identifies the river of Egypt as the ancient brook Sihor, the Rhinocolura of the Greeks, a little south of El-Arish, where the wady gently descends toward the Mediterranean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And as for the western border, you shall even have the great sea for a border: this shall be your west border. JFB notes there is no uncertainty about this boundary, universally allowed to be the Mediterranean, called the great sea in comparison with the small inland seas or lakes known to the Hebrews. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And this shall be your north border: from the great sea you shall point out for you mount Hor. JFB notes the difficulty arises from what translators called mount Hor, since the Hebrew Hor-ha-Hor properly signifies the mountain of the mountain, or the high double mountain, which from its situation can mean nothing else than mount Amana, a member of the great Lebanon range. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. From mount Hor you shall point out your border to the entrance of Hamath, and the goings forth of the border shall be to Zedad. JFB notes the entrance of Hamath is the northern plain between the mountain ranges, now the valley of Balbeck, and Zedad is identified as the present Sudud. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And the border shall go on to Ziphron, and the goings out of it shall be at Hazar-enan: this shall be your north border. JFB notes Ziphron means sweet odour and Hazar-enan village of fountains, though the places are unknown, and the frontier meant is an imaginary line from mount Cassius on the coast along Lebanon's northern base to the entering of the Bekaa valley at Kamosa Hermel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. And you shall point out your east border from Hazar-enan to Shepham. The eastern border began at Hazar-enan and ran to Shepham, the first leg of a clearly defined eastern line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And the coast shall go down from Shepham to Riblah, on the east side of Ain, and the border shall descend, and shall reach to the side of the sea of Chinnereth eastward. JFB notes Shepham and Riblah lay in the valley of Lebanon, the boundary commencing a little higher than the Jordan's sources, Ain being supposed the source of that river, whence the eastern boundary extended along the Jordan and the sea of Chinnereth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And the border shall go down to Jordan, and the goings out of it shall be at the salt sea: this shall be your land with the coasts thereof round about. JFB notes the line, drawn on the east of the river and the seas, included those waters within the western tribes' territory, and the border ended at the salt sea, closing the circuit of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Moses commanded Israel, saying, this is the land which you shall inherit by lot, which the Lord commanded to give to the nine tribes, and to the half tribe. The land was to be inherited by lot, as the Lord commanded, for the nine tribes and the half tribe west of Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. For the tribe of Reuben according to the house of their fathers, and the tribe of Gad according to the house of their fathers, have received their inheritance, and half the tribe of Manasseh have received their inheritance. JFB notes the conquered territories of Sihon and Og between Arnon and Hermon were allotted to them, Reuben in the most southerly part, Gad north of it, and half Manasseh in the northernmost portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The two tribes and the half tribe have received their inheritance on this side Jordan near Jericho eastward, toward the sunrising. The two tribes and the half tribe already held their inheritance east of Jordan near Jericho, toward the sunrise, and were therefore excluded from the western lot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. These are the names of the men which shall divide the land to you: Eleazar the priest, and Joshua son of Nun. JFB notes this appointment before the Jordan tended both to animate Israel's faith in the certainty of the conquest and to prevent subsequent dispute and discontent, dangerous in the natives' presence, and the dividers stood subordinate to the great military and ecclesiastical chiefs, Joshua and Eleazar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. And you shall take one prince of every tribe, to divide the land by inheritance. One prince from every western tribe would divide the land, so that each tribe's inheritance was assigned by its own prince under Joshua and Eleazar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. And the names of the men are these: of the tribe of Judah, Caleb son of Jephunneh. JFB notes the princes' names are mentioned in the exact order in which the tribes obtained possession of the land, and according to brotherly connection, beginning with Judah's Caleb, the faithful spy who had wholly followed the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. And of the tribe of Simeon, Shemuel son of Ammihud. Simeon's prince was Shemuel son of Ammihud, second in the order of division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Of the tribe of Benjamin, Elidad son of Chislon. Benjamin's prince was Elidad son of Chislon, dividing for the tribe that would hold Jerusalem's hills. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. And the prince of the tribe of Dan, Bukki son of Jogli. Dan's prince was Bukki son of Jogli, for the northern and southern Danite portions alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The prince of Joseph, for the tribe of Manasseh, Hanniel son of Ephod. Joseph's western prince for Manasseh was Hanniel son of Ephod, representing the half tribe that crossed Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. And the prince of the tribe of Ephraim, Kemuel son of Shiphtan. Ephraim's prince was Kemuel son of Shiphtan, for the tribe of Joshua himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. And the prince of the tribe of Zebulun, Elizaphan son of Parnach. Zebulun's prince was Elizaphan son of Parnach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. And the prince of the tribe of Issachar, Paltiel son of Azzan. Issachar's prince was Paltiel son of Azzan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. And the prince of the tribe of Asher, Ahihud son of Shelomi. Asher's prince was Ahihud son of Shelomi, for the northern coastal tribe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. And the prince of the tribe of Naphtali, Pedahel son of Ammihud. Naphtali's prince was Pedahel son of Ammihud, closing the ten princes for the nine and a half western tribes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. These are they whom the Lord commanded to divide the inheritance to Israel in the land of Canaan. The ten princes, with Eleazar and Joshua, were those the Lord commanded to divide the inheritance in Canaan, so that the division was settled by divine appointment before a single lot was cast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
