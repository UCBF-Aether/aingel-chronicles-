The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the men of Ephraim said unto him, Why hast thou served us thus, that thou calledst us not, when thou wentest to fight with the Midianites? And they did chide with him sharply.

2 And he said unto them, What have I done now in comparison of you? Is not the gleaning of the grapes of Ephraim better than the vintage of Abiezer?

3 God hath delivered into your hands the princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb: and what was I able to do in comparison of you? Then their anger was abated toward him, when he had said that.

4 And Gideon came to Jordan, and passed over, he, and the three hundred men that were with him, faint, yet pursuing them.

5 And he said unto the men of Succoth, Give, I pray you, loaves of bread unto the people that follow me; for they be faint, and I am pursuing after Zebah and Zalmunna, kings of Midian.

6 And the princes of Succoth said, Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in thine hand, that we should give bread unto thine army?

7 And Gideon said, Therefore when the LORD hath delivered Zebah and Zalmunna into mine hand, then I will tear your flesh with the thorns of the wilderness and with briers.

8 And he went up thence to Penuel, and spake unto them likewise: and the men of Penuel answered him as the men of Succoth had answered him.

9 And he spake also unto the men of Penuel, saying, When I come again in peace, I will break down this tower.

10 Now Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor, and their hosts with them, about fifteen thousand men, all that were left of all the hosts of the children of the east: for there fell an hundred and twenty thousand men that drew sword.

11 And Gideon went up by the way of them that dwelt in tents on the east of Nobah and Jogbehah, and smote the host; for the host was secure.

12 And when Zebah and Zalmunna fled, he pursued after them, and took the two kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna, and discomfited all the host.

13 And Gideon the son of Joash returned from battle before the sun was up,

14 And caught a young man of the men of Succoth, and enquired of him: and he described unto him the princes of Succoth, and the elders thereof, even threescore and seventeen men.

15 And he came unto the men of Succoth, and said, Behold Zebah and Zalmunna, with whom ye did upbraid me, saying, Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in thine hand, that we should give bread unto thy men that are weary?

16 And he took the elders of the city, and thorns of the wilderness and briers, and with them he taught the men of Succoth.

17 And he beat down the tower of Penuel, and slew the men of the city.

18 Then said he unto Zebah and Zalmunna, What manner of men were they whom ye slew at Tabor? And they answered, As thou art, so were they; each one resembled the children of a king.

19 And he said, They were my brethren, even the sons of my mother: as the LORD liveth, if ye had saved them alive, I would not slay you.

20 And he said unto Jether his firstborn, Up, and slay them. But the youth drew not his sword: for he feared, because he was yet a youth.

21 Then Zebah and Zalmunna said, Rise thou, and fall upon us: for as the man is, so is his strength. And Gideon arose, and slew Zebah and Zalmunna, and took away the ornaments that were on their camels’ necks.

22 Then the men of Israel said unto Gideon, Rule thou over us, both thou, and thy son, and thy son’s son also: for thou hast delivered us from the hand of Midian.

23 And Gideon said unto them, I will not rule over you, neither shall my son rule over you: the LORD shall rule over you.

24 And Gideon said unto them, I would desire a request of you, that ye would give me every man the earrings of his prey. (For they had golden earrings, because they were Ishmaelites.)

25 And they answered, We will willingly give them. And they spread a garment, and did cast therein every man the earrings of his prey.

26 And the weight of the golden earrings that he requested was a thousand and seven hundred shekels of gold; beside ornaments, and collars, and purple raiment that was on the kings of Midian, and beside the chains that were about their camels’ necks.

27 And Gideon made an ephod thereof, and put it in his city, even in Ophrah: and all Israel went thither a whoring after it: which thing became a snare unto Gideon, and to his house.

28 Thus was Midian subdued before the children of Israel, so that they lifted up their heads no more. And the country was in quietness forty years in the days of Gideon.

29 And Jerubbaal the son of Joash went and dwelt in his own house.

30 And Gideon had threescore and ten sons of his body begotten: for he had many wives.

31 And his concubine that was in Shechem, she also bare him a son, whose name he called Abimelech.

32 And Gideon the son of Joash died in a good old age, and was buried in the sepulchre of Joash his father, in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

33 And it came to pass, as soon as Gideon was dead, that the children of Israel turned again, and went a whoring after Baalim, and made Baalberith their god.

34 And the children of Israel remembered not the LORD their God, who had delivered them out of the hands of all their enemies on every side:

35 Neither shewed they kindness to the house of Jerubbaal, namely, Gideon, according to all the goodness which he had shewed unto Israel.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ephraim complains at being uncalled, and Gideon answers mildly, crediting Ephraim with Oreb and Zeeb, so that their anger abates. Succoth and Penuel refuse bread to his faint pursuing men and are punished on his return; Zebah and Zalmunna are taken at Karkor. Offered kingship for himself and his house, Gideon refuses, saying the LORD shall rule over Israel, yet the golden ephod he makes at Ophrah becomes a snare. The chapter closes in the text itself with Gideon's death in old age, Israel turning again to Baalim and Baal-berith, and showing no kindness to his house. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The men of Ephraim complained that Gideon had not called them to fight Midian, and JFB notes their wounded pride and old jealous rivalry with Gideon's tribe, though the complaint was groundless since Gideon had acted by divine direction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Gideon answered mildly, asking what he had done in comparison with Ephraim, and JFB calls this a truly modest answer breathing the spirit of a great and good man, which threw oil on troubled waters by ascribing to his brethren more merit than belonged to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He reminded them that God had delivered Oreb and Zeeb into their hands, and then their anger abated, so that the pacification JFB describes in the preceding verse was completed by crediting Ephraim with the princes' capture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Gideon came to Jordan and passed over, faint yet pursuing, and JFB stresses he was much exhausted but eager to continue the pursuit until the victory was consummated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He asked Succoth, a place of tents in Gad's territory, for loaves for his faint pursuing people, and JFB notes that, being engaged in Israel's common cause, he had a right to expect support from his countrymen everywhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The princes of Succoth refused, asking whether Zebah's and Zalmunna's hands were already in his, and JFB calls this insolent and time-serving, since they dreaded the vengeance of the neighbouring Midianite chiefs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Gideon threatened to tear their flesh with thorns of the wilderness and briers, a cruel torture JFB describes as thorns pressed on naked bodies by sledges or heavy implements dragged over them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He went up to Penuel, a neighbouring city in Gad near the Jabbok named by Jacob, and spoke to them likewise, receiving the same refusal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Gideon promised that when he returned in peace he would break down Penuel's tower, postponing merited vengeance until his return, and JFB notes his confident anticipation of triumph and the proud boast the threat probably answered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor with about fifteen thousand men, all that remained of the eastern host, and JFB notes the wreck of the Midianite army had halted there on Gad's eastern confines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Gideon went up by the way of tent-dwellers on the east and came upon them unexpectedly while they rested secure among their nomadic tribes, tracking them across Gilead to the northeast of the Jabbok. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. When the two kings fled, Gideon pursued and took them, and JFB calls this a third conflict in which the conquest of the Midianite horde was completed by surprise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Gideon returned from the battle by the heights of Heres, the sun-hills, which the translation renders as returning before the sun was up, taking a nearer route back toward Succoth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He caught a young man of Succoth who wrote down the names of the city's seventy-seven princes and elders, the very men from whom he had received such inhospitable treatment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Gideon showed the men of Succoth their captured kings and taunted them with the upbraiding they had given him, introducing the punishment JFB describes in the following verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He took the elders of the city and with thorns and briers taught the men of Succoth, and JFB notes that refusing refreshment to soldiers was a public crime as well as inhumanity, punished horribly with the region's great thorn bushes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Gideon beat down the tower of Penuel and slew the men of the city, executing the threat he had made on his way out and completing the vengeance postponed in verse 9. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He asked Zebah and Zalmunna what manner of men they had slain at Tabor, and JFB notes this was one of countless atrocities of their seven lawless years, raised only now when their fate was to be determined, while their victims resembled children of a king in beauty and majesty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Gideon answered that they were his brethren, the sons of his mother, and JFB explains that son of my mother implies a closeness and warmth never awakened by the looser term brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He told Jether his firstborn to rise and slay them, but the youth feared because he was still young, and JFB notes Gideon intended to honour his son by the task, since the executioner was chosen of rank proportioned to the condemned, and Gideon then did the deed himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The kings themselves told Gideon to rise and fall upon them, for as the man is so is his strength, and Gideon arose, slew them, and took the ornaments from their camels' necks, finishing what his son's fear had left to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The men of Israel asked Gideon and his house to rule over them because he had delivered them, and Gideon refused, saying the Lord shall rule over you, and JFB praises his refusal to sacrifice the theocracy to personal and family ambition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Gideon repeated that neither he nor his son would rule, but the Lord shall rule over them, and JFB notes he would willingly act as judge, but the Lord alone was King of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Gideon asked only for the earrings of the prey, and JFB notes the contribution of a single earring each from soldiers laden with Arabian gold and pearl, estimated at a great quantity of gold, given liberally. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The men answered willingly, spread a garment, and cast in every man the earrings of his prey, so that the request was met with the same liberality JFB notes in the offering itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The gold earrings weighed seventeen hundred shekels, besides the crescent ornaments, collars, and purple raiment of the Midianite kings, and JFB explains the ornaments as gold plates on camels' necks and royal purple housings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Gideon made an ephod of the gold and set it up in Ophrah, and Israel went whoring after it, and JFB argues Gideon intended it only as a civil magistrate's robe, but it became by perversion a snare to him and his house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Thus Midian was subdued before Israel and the country was quiet forty years in Gideon's days, and JFB calls this invasion as alarming as the Huns' irruption into Europe, a deliverance remembered for centuries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words in this chapter that a modern reader may stumble on. They explain the chapter text; they are not Scripture.

  • ephod. here a garment or object made of the captured gold, intended, the notes argue, as a magistrate's robe, but perverted into an idolatrous snare Interpreted Editorial gloss, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes
  • faint yet pursuing. exhausted but still pressing the pursuit, Gideon's condition at Jordan Interpreted Editorial gloss, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes
