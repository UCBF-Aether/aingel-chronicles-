The Text — Sirach 16 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Desire not a multitude of unprofitable children, neither delight in ungodly sons.

2 Though they multiply, rejoice not in them, except the fear of the Lord be with them.

3 Trust not thou in their life, neither respect their multitude: for one that is just is better than a thousand; and better it is to die without children, than to have them that are ungodly.

4 For by one that hath understanding shall the city be replenished: but the kindred of the wicked shall speedily become desolate.

5 Many such things have I seen with mine eyes, and mine ear hath heard greater things than these.

6 In the congregation of the ungodly shall a fire be kindled; and in a rebellious nation wrath is set on fire.

7 He was not pacified toward the old giants, who fell away in the strength of their foolishness.

8 Neither spared he the place where Lot sojourned, but abhorred them for their pride.

9 He pitied not the people of perdition, who were taken away in their sins:

10 Nor the six hundred thousand footmen, who were gathered together in the hardness of their hearts.

11 And if there be one stiffnecked among the people, it is marvel if he escape unpunished: for mercy and wrath are with him; he is mighty to forgive, and to pour out displeasure.

12 As his mercy is great, so is his correction also: he judgeth a man according to his works

13 The sinner shall not escape with his spoils: and the patience of the godly shall not be frustrate.

14 Make way for every work of mercy: for every man shall find according to his works.

15 The Lord hardened Pharaoh, that he should not know him, that his powerful works might be known to the world.

16 His mercy is manifest to every creature; and he hath separated his light from the darkness with an adamant.

17 Say not thou, I will hide myself from the Lord: shall any remember me from above? I shall not be remembered among so many people: for what is my soul among such an infinite number of creatures?

18 Behold, the heaven, and the heaven of heavens, the deep, and the earth, and all that therein is, shall be moved when he shall visit.

19 The mountains also and foundations of the earth be shaken with trembling, when the Lord looketh upon them.

20 No heart can think upon these things worthily: and who is able to conceive his ways?

21 It is a tempest which no man can see: for the most part of his works are hid.

22 Who can declare the works of his justice? or who can endure them? for his covenant is afar off, and the trial of all things is in the end.

23 He that wanteth understanding will think upon vain things: and a foolish man erring imagineth follies.

24 by son, hearken unto me, and learn knowledge, and mark my words with thy heart.

25 I will shew forth doctrine in weight, and declare his knowledge exactly.

26 The works of the Lord are done in judgment from the beginning: and from the time he made them he disposed the parts thereof.

27 He garnished his works for ever, and in his hand are the chief of them unto all generations: they neither labour, nor are weary, nor cease from their works.

28 None of them hindereth another, and they shall never disobey his word.

29 After this the Lord looked upon the earth, and filled it with his blessings.

30 With all manner of living things hath he covered the face thereof; and they shall return into it again.

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A multitude of unprofitable children is no blessing, and delight in ungodly sons is warned against. God's mercy is shown to every creature, his light separated from darkness, and the earth covered with living things that return into it again. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Do not desire a multitude of unprofitable children, nor delight in ungodly sons. Many children are no blessing if they are unprofitable, and ungodly sons are no delight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Though they multiply, do not rejoice in them, except the fear of the Lord be with them. Numbers alone are no cause for rejoicing; only the fear of the Lord in them makes multiplication good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. Do not trust in their life, nor respect their multitude, for one just man is better than a thousand, and it is better to die without children than to have ungodly ones. One just person outweighs a thousand, and childlessness is better than ungodly children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. By one man of understanding a city will be replenished, but the kindred of the wicked will speedily become desolate. A single understanding man can fill a city, while a wicked kindred quickly empties into desolation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Many such things have I seen with my eyes, and my ear has heard greater things than these. The writer appeals to seen and heard experience, greater even than the examples given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. In the congregation of the ungodly a fire will be kindled, and in a rebellious nation wrath is set on fire. Ungodly assemblies kindle fire, and rebellious nations set wrath ablaze. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. He was not pacified toward the old giants, who fell away in the strength of their foolishness. God was not appeased toward the ancient giants who fell in the strength of their folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Neither did he spare the place where Lot sojourned, but abhorred them for their pride. Sodom, where Lot lived, was not spared, because God abhorred its pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. He pitied not the people of perdition, who were taken away in their sins. The people of perdition, carried off in their sins, received no pity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Nor the six hundred thousand footmen, gathered together in the hardness of their hearts. Even the six hundred thousand of the wilderness generation, hardened in heart, were not spared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. If there is one stiffnecked among the people, it is a marvel if he escapes unpunished, for mercy and wrath are with him; he is mighty to forgive and to pour out displeasure. One stiffnecked person can hardly escape, because God holds both mercy and wrath and is mighty in each. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. As his mercy is great, so is his correction also; he judges a man according to his works. God's correction matches his mercy in greatness, and judgment follows works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. The sinner will not escape with his spoils, and the patience of the godly will not be frustrated. Sinners do not get away with their plunder, and godly patience is not disappointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. Make way for every work of mercy, for every man will find according to his works. Mercy should be given room in every work, since each man finds according to his own works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. The Lord hardened Pharaoh, that he should not know him, so that his powerful works might be known to the world. Pharaoh's hardening served to make God's powerful works known to the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. His mercy is manifest to every creature, and he has separated his light from the darkness with an adamant. God's mercy shows to every creature, and his light is divided from darkness as firmly as adamant divides. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. Do not say, I will hide myself from the Lord; shall any remember me from above? I shall not be remembered among so many people, for what is my soul among such an infinite number of creatures? Hiding from God is impossible, and smallness among countless creatures is no excuse for thinking oneself unremembered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. Behold, the heaven and the heaven of heavens, the deep, the earth, and all therein will be moved when he visits. At God's visitation heaven, deep, earth, and all within them are shaken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. The mountains also and the foundations of the earth are shaken with trembling when the Lord looks upon them. Even mountains and earth's foundations tremble under God's look. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 20. No heart can think upon these things worthily, and who is able to conceive his ways? The heart cannot worthily conceive these things, and no one can fully grasp God's ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 21. It is a tempest which no man can see, for the most part of his works are hid. God's working is like an unseen tempest, since most of his works are hidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 22. Who can declare the works of his justice, or who can endure them? For his covenant is afar off, and the trial of all things is in the end. God's just works cannot be fully declared or endured, because his covenant stretches far and the trial of all things comes at the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 23. He who wants understanding will think upon vain things, and a foolish man erring imagines follies. Lack of understanding turns thought to vanity, and a fool's errors breed imagined follies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 24. My son, hearken to me, learn knowledge, and mark my words with your heart. The call is to listen, learn knowledge, and mark the words in the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 25. I will show forth doctrine in weight, and declare his knowledge exactly. Doctrine will be set out with weight and exactness, not lightly or loosely. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 26. The works of the Lord are done in judgment from the beginning, and from the time he made them he disposed the parts thereof. God's works have been ordered in judgment from the beginning, each part disposed by him from creation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 27. He garnished his works for ever, and in his hand are the chief of them unto all generations; they neither labour, nor are weary, nor cease from their works. God's adorned works endure for ever in his hand across generations, never labouring, wearying, or ceasing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 28. None of them hinders another, and they will never disobey his word. Created works do not hinder each other and never disobey God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 29. After this the Lord looked upon the earth and filled it with his blessings. God's look upon the earth filled it with his blessings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 30. With all manner of living things he has covered the face thereof, and they will return into it again. The earth is covered with living things, and they return into it again, dust to dust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
