The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Corinthians 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 We then, as workers together with him, beseech you also that ye receive not the grace of God in vain.

2 (For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)

3 Giving no offence in any thing, that the ministry be not blamed:

4 But in all things approving ourselves as the ministers of God, in much patience, in afflictions, in necessities, in distresses,

5 In stripes, in imprisonments, in tumults, in labours, in watchings, in fastings;

6 By pureness, by knowledge, by longsuffering, by kindness, by the Holy Ghost, by love unfeigned,

7 By the word of truth, by the power of God, by the armour of righteousness on the right hand and on the left,

8 By honour and dishonour, by evil report and good report: as deceivers, and yet true;

9 As unknown, and yet well known; as dying, and, behold, we live; as chastened, and not killed;

10 As sorrowful, yet alway rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing all things.

11 O ye Corinthians, our mouth is open unto you, our heart is enlarged.

12 Ye are not straitened in us, but ye are straitened in your own bowels.

13 Now for a recompence in the same, (I speak as unto my children,) be ye also enlarged.

14 Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness?

15 And what concord hath Christ with Belial? or what part hath he that believeth with an infidel?

16 And what agreement hath the temple of God with idols? for ye are the temple of the living God; as God hath said, I will dwell in them, and walk in them; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.

17 Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you.

18 And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters, saith the Lord Almighty.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

As workers together with God, Paul begs the Corinthians not to receive his grace in vain, for now is the accepted time and now the day of salvation, and he commends his ministry through afflictions, paradoxes, and the open heart of love. He then urges separation from unequal yoking with unbelievers and from idols, resting on God's promise to dwell among his people and be their Father, with them as his sons and daughters. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. We then, as workers together with him, beseech you also that ye receive not the grace of God in vain. JFB notes ministers are fellow-workers with God, and the beseeching is that grace received be not received in vain, without fruit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. (For he says, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.) Henry notes the quotation from Isaiah is applied to the present: the accepted time is not future, it is now, and the day of salvation is today. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Giving no offence in any thing, that the ministry be not blamed. JFB notes the minister's care extends to offence: nothing in his conduct must give the ministry a bad name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. But in all things approving ourselves as the ministers of God, in much patience, in afflictions, in necessities, in distresses. Henry notes the approval is proved in endurance: patience under affliction, necessity and distress is the minister's credential. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. In stripes, in imprisonments, in tumults, in labours, in watchings, in fastings. JFB notes the list moves from sufferings inflicted by others to disciplines imposed by himself: stripes and prisons, tumults, labours, sleepless nights and fasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. By pureness, by knowledge, by longsuffering, by kindness, by the Holy Ghost, by love unfeigned. Henry notes the inner furniture matches the outer endurance: purity, knowledge, patience, kindness, the Spirit's power and unfeigned love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. By the word of truth, by the power of God, by the armour of righteousness on the right hand and on the left. JFB notes the weapons are truth and God's power, and the armour of righteousness covers both hands, for offence and defence alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. By honour and dishonour, by evil report and good report: as deceivers, and yet true. Henry notes the minister passes through both honours and dishonours, and is called a deceiver while being true, the world's misreading of a faithful life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. As unknown, and yet well known; as dying, and, behold, we live; as chastened, and not killed. JFB notes the paradoxes continue: unknown to the world yet known to God, dying yet living, chastened yet not killed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. As sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing all things. Henry notes the final paradoxes are the ministry's summary: sorrowful yet rejoicing, poor yet enriching many, having nothing yet possessing all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. O ye Corinthians, our mouth is open unto you, our heart is enlarged. JFB notes the open mouth and enlarged heart are the fruit of the preceding catalogue: Paul speaks freely because his heart is wide toward Corinth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Ye are not straitened in us, but ye are straitened in your own bowels. Henry notes the narrowness is not Paul's: his heart is enlarged, and if Corinth feels cramped, the cramping is in its own affections. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Now for a recompense in the same, (I speak as unto my children,) be ye also enlarged. JFB notes Paul asks a recompense in kind: as a father to children, he asks for enlarged hearts to match his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship has righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion has light with darkness? Henry notes the yoke image forbids partnerships that pull in opposite directions: righteousness and unrighteousness cannot share a yoke, nor light with darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And what concord has Christ with Belial? or what part has he that believes with an infidel? JFB notes the pairs sharpen: Christ against Belial, believer against unbeliever, with no part in common. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. And what agreement has the temple of God with idols? for ye are the temple of the living God; as God has said, I will dwell in them, and walk in them; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people. Henry notes the temple argument is the ground: believers are God's dwelling, so agreement with idols is unthinkable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, says the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you. JFB notes separation is commanded with a promise: come out, touch nothing unclean, and the Lord himself will receive you. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters, says the Lord Almighty. Henry notes the promise crowns the command: separation from unbelievers ends in adoption, sons and daughters of the Almighty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • unequally yoked. Paul's image, from ploughing with mismatched animals, for binding believers together with unbelievers. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • Belial. A name Paul uses for the worthless or lawless one, contrasted with Christ, in his call to separation. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • accepted time. The present season of salvation, quoted from Isaiah, which Paul says is now. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
