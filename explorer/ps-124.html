The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 124 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 If it had not been the LORD who was on our side, now may Israel say;

2 If it had not been the LORD who was on our side, when men rose up against us:

3 Then they had swallowed us up quick, when their wrath was kindled against us:

4 Then the waters had overwhelmed us, the stream had gone over our soul:

5 Then the proud waters had gone over our soul.

6 Blessed be the LORD, who hath not given us as a prey to their teeth.

7 Our soul is escaped as a bird out of the snare of the fowlers: the snare is broken, and we are escaped.

8 Our help is in the name of the LORD, who made heaven and earth.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A communal "if it had not been" psalm: if the LORD had not been on Israel's side when men rose up against them, they would have been swallowed up quick, waters would have overwhelmed their soul, the proud waters gone over them. Instead, blessed be the LORD, who has not given them as prey to their enemies' teeth; their soul has escaped as a bird out of the fowler's snare, the snare broken, and they are escaped. The close is the ground of the whole rescue: our help is in the name of the LORD, who made heaven and earth. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry bids Israel say it: if the Lord had not been on our side — giving all glory to God, none to conquerors as usual — for the more desperate the disease, the more the Physician's skill appears in the cure, and happy the people whose God is Jehovah all-sufficient. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry repeats for confirmation and attention: again and again the Lord appeared on their side when men rose up — God taking part, espousing cause, very present help nigh at hand, with us and among us as commander-in-chief, and if Jehovah himself had not undertaken, enemies would have overpowered us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry pictures wrath kindled as fire ready to consume, proud persecutors daring to swallow alive: men, creatures of our kind from whom humanity is expected, yet thirsting for blood — Absalom for his father's, a woman drunk with saints' blood — wrath cruel, anger outrageous, no less than destruction serving displeasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry dwells on deluge similitude with ascents: waters overwhelming, stream going over souls — lives, comforts, all dear — as low grounds by land-flood or sands by spring-tide, God sometimes suffering enemies to prevail very far that his power appear more illustrious in deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry names them proud waters: the same overwhelming stream repeated in ascent, proud enemies in pride persecuting the poor, whose prevailing is permitted only to magnify the rescue that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry blesses the Lord for narrow escape like lamb from jaws: God author of all deliverances must have glory, robbed if thanks are not returned — and enemies had no power but what was given from above, could not be prey unless God gave up, therefore rescued because he would not suffer ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry escapes like a sparrow from fowlers' snare: subtle, spiteful enemies laying snares to bring into sin and trouble, sometimes seemingly prevailing, God's people as unable as weak birds — then God's time to appear when all friends fail, breaking snare and turning counsel to foolishness, as Isaac saved ready for sacrifice, Jehovah-jireh in the mount seen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry trusts public help as personal help was trusted: our help is in the name of the Lord, as my help was in Psalm 121 — comfort that Israel's God made the world, therefore will have a church in the world and secure it in greatest danger, so church's friends putting confidence in him shall not be put to confusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
