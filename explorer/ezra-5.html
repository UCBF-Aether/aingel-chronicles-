The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ezra 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then the prophets, Haggai the prophet, and Zechariah the son of Iddo, prophesied unto the Jews that were in Judah and Jerusalem in the name of the God of Israel, even unto them.

2 Then rose up Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and Jeshua the son of Jozadak, and began to build the house of God which is at Jerusalem: and with them were the prophets of God helping them.

3 At the same time came to them Tatnai, governor on this side the river, and Shetharboznai and their companions, and said thus unto them, Who hath commanded you to build this house, and to make up this wall?

4 Then said we unto them after this manner, What are the names of the men that make this building?

5 But the eye of their God was upon the elders of the Jews, that they could not cause them to cease, till the matter came to Darius: and then they returned answer by letter concerning this matter.

6 The copy of the letter that Tatnai, governor on this side the river, and Shetharboznai and his companions the Apharsachites, which were on this side the river, sent unto Darius the king:

7 They sent a letter unto him, wherein was written thus; Unto Darius the king, all peace.

8 Be it known unto the king, that we went into the province of Judea, to the house of the great God, which is builded with great stones, and timber is laid in the walls, and this work goeth fast on, and prospereth in their hands.

9 Then asked we those elders, and said unto them thus, Who commanded you to build this house, and to make up these walls?

10 We asked their names also, to certify thee, that we might write the names of the men that were the chief of them.

11 And thus they returned us answer, saying, We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth, and build the house that was builded these many years ago, which a great king of Israel builded and set up.

12 But after that our fathers had provoked the God of heaven unto wrath, he gave them into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, the Chaldean, who destroyed this house, and carried the people away into Babylon.

13 But in the first year of Cyrus the king of Babylon the same king Cyrus made a decree to build this house of God.

14 And the vessels also of gold and silver of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar took out of the temple that was in Jerusalem, and brought them into the temple of Babylon, those did Cyrus the king take out of the temple of Babylon, and they were delivered unto one, whose name was Sheshbazzar, whom he had made governor;

15 And said unto him, Take these vessels, go, carry them into the temple that is in Jerusalem, and let the house of God be builded in his place.

16 Then came the same Sheshbazzar, and laid the foundation of the house of God which is in Jerusalem: and since that time even until now hath it been in building, and yet it is not finished.

17 Now therefore, if it seem good to the king, let there be search made in the king’s treasure house, which is there at Babylon, whether it be so, that a decree was made of Cyrus the king to build this house of God at Jerusalem, and let the king send his pleasure to us concerning this matter.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Haggai and Zechariah prophesy to the Jews in Judah and Jerusalem, and Zerubbabel and Jeshua rise and begin again to build the house of God, with the prophets helping them. Tatnai, governor beyond the river, with Shethar-boznai and their companions, asks by whose command they build, takes their names, and writes to Darius asking that search be made in the king’s treasure house for Cyrus’s decree. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then Haggai and Zechariah son of Iddo prophesied to the Jews in Judah and Jerusalem in the name of the God of Israel. Henry notes the work restarted not by a new royal decree but by God's word through his prophets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Then Zerubbabel and Jeshua rose up and began to build the house of God, and the prophets of God were helping them. JFB notes the prophets helped not only by preaching but by presence, sharing the risk of an unauthorised resumption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Tatnai, governor beyond the river, with Shethar-boznai and their companions, asked who had commanded them to build this house and make up this wall. Henry notes the question is fair on its face, and the builders answer it without defiance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Then said we unto them after this manner, What are the names of the men that make this building? The question demands the builders' names, so that responsibility can be fixed if the king disallows the work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. But the eye of their God was upon the elders of the Jews, so that they could not cause them to cease until the matter came to Darius. Henry's key note: God's watchful eye, not the builders' arguments, kept the work going through the inquiry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. This is the copy of the letter Tatnai, Shethar-boznai and the Apharsachites beyond the river sent to Darius. Unlike the adversaries' letter, this report is preserved as a fair, factual account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They sent a letter written thus: 'Unto Darius the king, all peace.' The courteous opening matches the measured tone of the whole report, which states facts before asking for a ruling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They report they went into Judea to the house of the great God, built with great stones, timber laid in the walls, the work going fast on and prospering. JFB notes even opponents testify the building is solid and the work vigorous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They asked the elders who commanded them to build this house and make up these walls. The question is repeated in the report exactly as asked, showing the inquiry was orderly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They asked their names also, to certify the king and write the names of the chief men. Henry notes the builders were not anonymous: they stood to their work openly, ready to answer for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The elders answered: 'We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth, and build the house that was built many years ago, which a great king of Israel built and set up.' JFB notes the answer claims a title older than any Persian decree: they rebuild what Solomon built for the God of heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. But after their fathers provoked the God of heaven to wrath, he gave them into Nebuchadnezzar's hand, who destroyed this house and carried the people to Babylon. Henry notes the frank confession: the builders do not hide that exile was judgment for sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But in the first year of Cyrus king of Babylon, Cyrus made a decree to build this house of God. The elders rest their case on a royal decree, which any king's archive can verify. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The gold and silver vessels Nebuchadnezzar took from Jerusalem's temple to Babylon, Cyrus took out and delivered to Sheshbazzar, whom he had made governor. JFB notes the vessels' return is evidence: the decree was acted on, not merely spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Cyrus said to him: 'Take these vessels, go, carry them into the temple at Jerusalem, and let the house of God be built in his place.' The charge ties vessels and building together: the house must be rebuilt because its vessels have a home to return to. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Then Sheshbazzar came and laid the foundation of the house of God at Jerusalem, and since that time even until now it has been in building, and yet is not finished. Henry notes the long, interrupted history is stated plainly, without blaming any king for the delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Now therefore, if it seem good to the king, let search be made in the king's treasure house at Babylon, whether Cyrus made such a decree, and let the king send his pleasure concerning this matter. JFB notes the fair proposal: the builders invite the very search that will vindicate them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
