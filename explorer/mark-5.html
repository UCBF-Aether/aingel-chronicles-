The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And they came over unto the other side of the sea, into the country of the Gadarenes.

2 And when he was come out of the ship, immediately there met him out of the tombs a man with an unclean spirit,

3 Who had his dwelling among the tombs; and no man could bind him, no, not with chains:

4 Because that he had been often bound with fetters and chains, and the chains had been plucked asunder by him, and the fetters broken in pieces: neither could any man tame him.

5 And always, night and day, he was in the mountains, and in the tombs, crying, and cutting himself with stones.

6 But when he saw Jesus afar off, he ran and worshipped him,

7 And cried with a loud voice, and said, What have I to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of the most high God? I adjure thee by God, that thou torment me not.

8 For he said unto him, Come out of the man, thou unclean spirit.

9 And he asked him, What is thy name? And he answered, saying, My name is Legion: for we are many.

10 And he besought him much that he would not send them away out of the country.

11 Now there was there nigh unto the mountains a great herd of swine feeding.

12 And all the devils besought him, saying, Send us into the swine, that we may enter into them.

13 And forthwith Jesus gave them leave. And the unclean spirits went out, and entered into the swine: and the herd ran violently down a steep place into the sea, (they were about two thousand;) and were choked in the sea.

14 And they that fed the swine fled, and told it in the city, and in the country. And they went out to see what it was that was done.

15 And they come to Jesus, and see him that was possessed with the devil, and had the legion, sitting, and clothed, and in his right mind: and they were afraid.

16 And they that saw it told them how it befell to him that was possessed with the devil, and also concerning the swine.

17 And they began to pray him to depart out of their coasts.

18 And when he was come into the ship, he that had been possessed with the devil prayed him that he might be with him.

19 Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee.

20 And he departed, and began to publish in Decapolis how great things Jesus had done for him: and all men did marvel.

21 And when Jesus was passed over again by ship unto the other side, much people gathered unto him: and he was nigh unto the sea.

22 And, behold, there cometh one of the rulers of the synagogue, Jairus by name; and when he saw him, he fell at his feet,

23 And besought him greatly, saying, My little daughter lieth at the point of death: I pray thee, come and lay thy hands on her, that she may be healed; and she shall live.

24 And Jesus went with him; and much people followed him, and thronged him.

25 And a certain woman, which had an issue of blood twelve years,

26 And had suffered many things of many physicians, and had spent all that she had, and was nothing bettered, but rather grew worse,

27 When she had heard of Jesus, came in the press behind, and touched his garment.

28 For she said, If I may touch but his clothes, I shall be whole.

29 And straightway the fountain of her blood was dried up; and she felt in her body that she was healed of that plague.

30 And Jesus, immediately knowing in himself that virtue had gone out of him, turned him about in the press, and said, Who touched my clothes?

31 And his disciples said unto him, Thou seest the multitude thronging thee, and sayest thou, Who touched me?

32 And he looked round about to see her that had done this thing.

33 But the woman fearing and trembling, knowing what was done in her, came and fell down before him, and told him all the truth.

34 And he said unto her, Daughter, thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace, and be whole of thy plague.

35 While he yet spake, there came from the ruler of the synagogue’s house certain which said, Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?

36 As soon as Jesus heard the word that was spoken, he saith unto the ruler of the synagogue, Be not afraid, only believe.

37 And he suffered no man to follow him, save Peter, and James, and John the brother of James.

38 And he cometh to the house of the ruler of the synagogue, and seeth the tumult, and them that wept and wailed greatly.

39 And when he was come in, he saith unto them, Why make ye this ado, and weep? the damsel is not dead, but sleepeth.

40 And they laughed him to scorn. But when he had put them all out, he taketh the father and the mother of the damsel, and them that were with him, and entereth in where the damsel was lying.

41 And he took the damsel by the hand, and said unto her, Talitha cumi; which is, being interpreted, Damsel, I say unto thee, arise.

42 And straightway the damsel arose, and walked; for she was of the age of twelve years. And they were astonished with a great astonishment.

43 And he charged them straitly that no man should know it; and commanded that something should be given her to eat.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Across the sea, Jesus meets a demon-possessed man living among the tombs; the unclean spirits, calling themselves Legion, enter a herd of swine that rushes into the sea, and the healed man is sent home to tell what the Lord has done (vv. 1–20). Back across the water, two healings are woven together: a woman with twelve years of bleeding touches his garment and is made whole, and Jairus’s twelve-year-old daughter, reported dead on the way, is raised with the words “Talitha cumi” (vv. 21–43). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 5

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. They came over to the other side of the sea, into the country of the Gadarenes. Christ crossed a storm to reach one miserable man on a Gentile shore, showing his errand was not bounded by Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When he came out of the ship, immediately there met him out of the tombs a man with an unclean spirit. The possessed man met Christ at once, as if his misery sought its only physician. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. He had his dwelling among the tombs, and no man could bind him, not even with chains. Tombs were his home because death and uncleanness suited his tormentor, and human bonds were useless against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He had often been bound with fetters and chains, and the chains had been plucked asunder and the fetters broken in pieces, and no man could tame him. Repeated restraint only proved his strength, and man's taming power had wholly failed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Always, night and day, he was in the mountains and in the tombs, crying and cutting himself with stones. His misery never rested, and the devil that possessed him made him his own tormentor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. But when he saw Jesus afar off, he ran and worshipped him. Even at a distance the unclean spirit knew its master, and ran to him it could not flee from. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He cried with a loud voice, What have I to do with you, Jesus, Son of the most high God? I adjure you by God, that you torment me not. The devil confesses Christ's sonship yet dreads his judgment, desiring only to be left in his rebellion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For Jesus had said to him, Come out of the man, you unclean spirit. The command came before the cry, and the devil's bargaining was already answered by authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Jesus asked him, What is your name? He answered, My name is Legion, for we are many. The question exposes the multitude of the enemy, that the greatness of the deliverance may be known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. He besought him much that he would not send them away out of the country. Devils cling to their haunts and beg for any leave rather than face their doom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Now there was there near the mountains a great herd of swine feeding. Swine, unclean by law, were fit company for unclean spirits, and their presence sets up the next act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. All the devils besought him, saying, Send us into the swine, that we may enter into them. They preferred any body to none, and their request shows they cannot move without Christ's leave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Forthwith Jesus gave them leave, and the unclean spirits went out and entered the swine, and the herd ran violently down a steep place into the sea, about two thousand, and were choked. Christ permitted the loss to prove the reality of the possession and the devils' destructiveness, which would have destroyed the man as they destroyed the swine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The swineherds fled and told it in the city and in the country, and they went out to see what was done. Bad news travels fast, and the whole country came to judge a miracle by its cost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They came to Jesus and saw him that was possessed and had the legion, sitting, clothed, and in his right mind, and they were afraid. Sitting, clothed, and sane are the three marks of deliverance, yet the sight bred fear rather than faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Those who saw it told them how it befell the possessed man, and also concerning the swine. The witnesses reported both the mercy and the loss, and the hearers weighed them together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. They began to pray him to depart out of their coasts. Men who prized their swine above a soul asked Christ to leave, choosing their goods over their Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. When he came into the ship, he that had been possessed prayed him that he might be with him. Gratitude desired constant company with Christ, the natural wish of a delivered soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Jesus did not suffer him, but said, Go home to your friends, and tell them how great things the Lord has done for you, and has had compassion on you. Home is the first pulpit of a convert, and his own story, told to those who knew his misery, is the fittest testimony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He departed and began to publish in Decapolis how great things Jesus had done for him, and all men marvelled. Obedience turned one man's deliverance into a ten-city proclamation, and wonder prepared the ground for later preaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. When Jesus passed over again by ship to the other side, much people gathered to him, and he was near the sea. His return found a waiting crowd, for his fame had gone before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Behold, there came one of the rulers of the synagogue, Jairus by name, and when he saw him, he fell at his feet. A ruler's public falling at Christ's feet shows affliction breaking through rank and pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. He besought him greatly, saying, My little daughter lies at the point of death: come and lay your hands on her, that she may be healed, and she shall live. The father's prayer joins urgency with faith, asking Christ's presence and touch for a dying child. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Jesus went with him, and much people followed him and thronged him. Christ went at once to the house of grief, and the crowd thronged a journey meant for one family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. A certain woman, who had an issue of blood twelve years, pressed into that throng. Twelve years of uncleanness and isolation frame a suffering that human help had only lengthened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. She had suffered many things of many physicians, spent all she had, and was nothing bettered, but rather grew worse. Earthly physicians had drained her purse and left her disease, a picture of all cures that fail before Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. When she heard of Jesus, she came in the press behind and touched his garment. Hearing bred faith, and faith, though timid, pressed through a crowd to reach him from behind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. For she said, If I may touch but his clothes, I shall be whole. Her faith fixed on even the hem of his garment, trusting that virtue went with Christ beyond his hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Straightway the fountain of her blood was dried up, and she felt in her body that she was healed of that plague. The cure was immediate and sensible: she felt what faith had received. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Jesus, immediately knowing in himself that virtue had gone out of him, turned about in the press and said, Who touched my clothes? He knew a healing touch from a thronging touch, and would not let a secret cure stay secret. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. His disciples said, You see the multitude thronging you, and say, Who touched me? They mistook the crowd's pressure for the woman's touch, and could not distinguish curiosity from faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 33. The woman fearing and trembling, knowing what was done in her, came and fell down before him and told him all the truth. Fear and trembling gave way to full confession, and Christ drew out the whole story that her faith might be owned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 39. When he came in, he said, Why make this ado and weep? The damsel is not dead, but sleeps. Death in Christ's presence is but sleep, for he who will wake her speaks of her as sleeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. They laughed him to scorn, but when he had put them all out, he took the father and mother of the damsel and those with him, and entered where the damsel lay. Scorners were shut out, and only faith's witnesses were admitted to see the work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. He took the damsel by the hand and said, Talitha cumi, which is, Damsel, I say to you, arise. His word in her own tongue, joined to his hand, commanded life back into death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. Straightway the damsel arose and walked, for she was twelve years old, and they were astonished with a great astonishment. Rising and walking proved a complete return to life, not a mere sign, and astonishment was the fitting response. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. He charged them straitly that no man should know it, and commanded that something should be given her to eat. Secrecy guarded against a stir at the wrong time, and food proved she lived indeed and needed ordinary care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 41. He took the damsel by the hand and said, Talitha cumi, which is, Damsel, I say to you, arise. His word in her own tongue, joined to his hand, commanded life back into death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. Straightway the damsel arose and walked, for she was twelve years old, and they were astonished with a great astonishment. Rising and walking proved a complete return to life, not a mere sign, and astonishment was the fitting response. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. He charged them straitly that no man should know it, and commanded that something should be given her to eat. Secrecy guarded against a stir at the wrong time, and food proved she lived indeed and needed ordinary care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
