The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 25 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses in mount Sinai, saying,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When ye come into the land which I give you, then shall the land keep a sabbath unto the LORD.

3 Six years thou shalt sow thy field, and six years thou shalt prune thy vineyard, and gather in the fruit thereof;

4 But in the seventh year shall be a sabbath of rest unto the land, a sabbath for the LORD: thou shalt neither sow thy field, nor prune thy vineyard.

5 That which groweth of its own accord of thy harvest thou shalt not reap, neither gather the grapes of thy vine undressed: for it is a year of rest unto the land.

6 And the sabbath of the land shall be meat for you; for thee, and for thy servant, and for thy maid, and for thy hired servant, and for thy stranger that sojourneth with thee.

7 And for thy cattle, and for the beast that are in thy land, shall all the increase thereof be meat.

8 And thou shalt number seven sabbaths of years unto thee, seven times seven years; and the space of the seven sabbaths of years shall be unto thee forty and nine years.

9 Then shalt thou cause the trumpet of the jubile to sound on the tenth day of the seventh month, in the day of atonement shall ye make the trumpet sound throughout all your land.

10 And ye shall hallow the fiftieth year, and proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof: it shall be a jubile unto you; and ye shall return every man unto his possession, and ye shall return every man unto his family.

11 A jubile shall that fiftieth year be unto you: ye shall not sow, neither reap that which groweth of itself in it, nor gather the grapes in it of thy vine undressed.

12 For it is the jubile; it shall be holy unto you: ye shall eat the increase thereof out of the field.

13 In the year of this jubile ye shall return every man unto his possession.

14 And if thou sell ought unto thy neighbour, or buyest ought of thy neighbour’s hand, ye shall not oppress one another:

15 According to the number of years after the jubile thou shalt buy of thy neighbour, and according unto the number of years of the fruits he shall sell unto thee:

16 According to the multitude of years thou shalt increase the price thereof, and according to the fewness of years thou shalt diminish the price of it: for according to the number of the years of the fruits doth he sell unto thee.

17 Ye shall not therefore oppress one another; but thou shalt fear thy God: for I am the LORD your God.

18 Wherefore ye shall do my statutes, and keep my judgments, and do them; and ye shall dwell in the land in safety.

19 And the land shall yield her fruit, and ye shall eat your fill, and dwell therein in safety.

20 And if ye shall say, What shall we eat the seventh year? behold, we shall not sow, nor gather in our increase:

21 Then I will command my blessing upon you in the sixth year, and it shall bring forth fruit for three years.

22 And ye shall sow the eighth year, and eat yet of old fruit until the ninth year; until her fruits come in ye shall eat of the old store.

23 The land shall not be sold for ever: for the land is mine, for ye are strangers and sojourners with me.

24 And in all the land of your possession ye shall grant a redemption for the land.

25 If thy brother be waxen poor, and hath sold away some of his possession, and if any of his kin come to redeem it, then shall he redeem that which his brother sold.

26 And if the man have none to redeem it, and himself be able to redeem it;

27 Then let him count the years of the sale thereof, and restore the overplus unto the man to whom he sold it; that he may return unto his possession.

28 But if he be not able to restore it to him, then that which is sold shall remain in the hand of him that hath bought it until the year of jubile: and in the jubile it shall go out, and he shall return unto his possession.

29 And if a man sell a dwelling house in a walled city, then he may redeem it within a whole year after it is sold; within a full year may he redeem it.

30 And if it be not redeemed within the space of a full year, then the house that is in the walled city shall be established for ever to him that bought it throughout his generations: it shall not go out in the jubile.

31 But the houses of the villages which have no wall round about them shall be counted as the fields of the country: they may be redeemed, and they shall go out in the jubile.

32 Notwithstanding the cities of the Levites, and the houses of the cities of their possession, may the Levites redeem at any time.

33 And if a man purchase of the Levites, then the house that was sold, and the city of his possession, shall go out in the year of jubile: for the houses of the cities of the Levites are their possession among the children of Israel.

34 But the field of the suburbs of their cities may not be sold; for it is their perpetual possession.

35 And if thy brother be waxen poor, and fallen in decay with thee; then thou shalt relieve him: yea, though he be a stranger, or a sojourner; that he may live with thee.

36 Take thou no usury of him, or increase: but fear thy God; that thy brother may live with thee.

37 Thou shalt not give him thy money upon usury, nor lend him thy victuals for increase.

38 I am the LORD your God, which brought you forth out of the land of Egypt, to give you the land of Canaan, and to be your God.

39 And if thy brother that dwelleth by thee be waxen poor, and be sold unto thee; thou shalt not compel him to serve as a bondservant:

40 But as an hired servant, and as a sojourner, he shall be with thee, and shall serve thee unto the year of jubile.

41 And then shall he depart from thee, both he and his children with him, and shall return unto his own family, and unto the possession of his fathers shall he return.

42 For they are my servants, which I brought forth out of the land of Egypt: they shall not be sold as bondmen.

43 Thou shalt not rule over him with rigour; but shalt fear thy God.

44 Both thy bondmen, and thy bondmaids, which thou shalt have, shall be of the heathen that are round about you; of them shall ye buy bondmen and bondmaids.

45 Moreover of the children of the strangers that do sojourn among you, of them shall ye buy, and of their families that are with you, which they begat in your land: and they shall be your possession.

46 And ye shall take them as an inheritance for your children after you, to inherit them for a possession; they shall be your bondmen for ever: but over your brethren the children of Israel, ye shall not rule one over another with rigour.

47 And if a sojourner or stranger wax rich by thee, and thy brother that dwelleth by him wax poor, and sell himself unto the stranger or sojourner by thee, or to the stock of the stranger’s family:

48 After that he is sold he may be redeemed again; one of his brethren may redeem him:

49 Either his uncle, or his uncle’s son, may redeem him, or any that is nigh of kin unto him of his family may redeem him; or if he be able, he may redeem himself.

50 And he shall reckon with him that bought him from the year that he was sold to him unto the year of jubile: and the price of his sale shall be according unto the number of years, according to the time of an hired servant shall it be with him.

51 If there be yet many years behind, according unto them he shall give again the price of his redemption out of the money that he was bought for.

52 And if there remain but few years unto the year of jubile, then he shall count with him, and according unto his years shall he give him again the price of his redemption.

53 And as a yearly hired servant shall he be with him: and the other shall not rule with rigour over him in thy sight.

54 And if he be not redeemed in these years, then he shall go out in the year of jubile, both he, and his children with him.

55 For unto me the children of Israel are servants; they are my servants whom I brought forth out of the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the sabbath year and the jubilee, as laws spoken on Mount Sinai. Every seventh year the land itself rests: there is no sowing or reaping, and what grows of itself is left for the poor, the servant, the stranger, and the beasts. In the fiftieth year, the jubilee, liberty is proclaimed: sold land returns to its family, houses and persons are redeemed on stated terms, the poor kinsman must be helped without interest or harsh service, and no one may oppress his neighbour, because the land is the LORD's and Israel are his servants whom he brought out of Egypt. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Sabbath year. Every seventh year, in which the land rests from sowing and reaping and its spontaneous growth is left for the poor, servants, strangers, and beasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Jubilee. The fiftieth year, when liberty is proclaimed, sold land returns to its family, and every man returns to his possession and to his family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

  • Verse 1. The LORD speaks to Moses in Mount Sinai, giving the sabbath-year and jubilee laws as Sinai legislation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

When they come into the land God gives them, the land shall keep a sabbath unto the LORD, the observance beginning, some think, in the seventh year after entrance, others in the fourteenth after six years of agriculture following conquest and division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Six years they shall sow the field and prune the vineyard and gather its fruit, the six working years preceding the sabbath year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 5. That which grows of its own accord of the harvest shall not be reaped, nor grapes of the undressed vine gathered, for it is a year of rest unto the land, its spontaneous produce being common property. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The sabbath of the land shall be meat for them, for servant, maid, hired servant, and stranger sojourning with them, all sharing the spontaneous produce. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For cattle and for beasts in the land shall all its increase be meat, the sabbath rest invigorating the land as the weekly Sabbath refreshes men and cattle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They shall number seven sabbaths of years, seven times seven years, forty-nine years, the space of seven sabbaths, the most extraordinary civil institution, named Jubilee from a horn or trumpet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Then shall the trumpet of jubilee sound on the tenth day of the seventh month, the day of atonement, throughout all the land, by public authority proclaiming universal redemption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They shall hallow the fiftieth year and proclaim liberty throughout the land to all inhabitants, it shall be a jubilee, every man returning to his possession and to his family, the prevailing opinion favouring the fiftieth year over the forty-ninth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. A jubilee shall that fiftieth year be, neither sowing nor reaping what grows of itself nor gathering undressed vines, the land enjoying a sabbath of repose as in the sabbatic year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. For it is the jubilee, holy unto them, and they shall eat the increase out of the field, spontaneous produce being eaten for subsistence but not hoarded as private stock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. In the year of jubilee every man shall return unto his possession, inheritances however often alienated coming back to original proprietors, preserving families and tribes distinct and genealogies recorded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. If they sell to a neighbour or buy from a neighbour's hand, they shall not oppress one another, the sale being limited by the jubilee period and requiring honest regard on both sides. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. According to the number of years after the jubilee shall he buy, and according to years of fruits shall he sell, the price following the remaining harvests until redemption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. According to the multitude of years the price shall be increased, and according to fewness decreased, for according to the number of fruits he sells to his neighbour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. They shall not oppress one another, but fear God, for He is the LORD their God, the same rule as verse 14, aimed at maintaining the original order of families and equality of condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Therefore they shall do His statutes and keep His judgments and do them, and dwell in the land in safety, obedience securing safe dwelling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The land shall yield her fruit, and they shall eat their fill and dwell safely therein, the sabbath and jubilee obedience being rewarded with plenty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. If they say, What shall we eat in the seventh year, behold, we shall not sow nor gather increase, the question anticipating the food deficiency from suspended labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. God will command His blessing in the sixth year, and it shall bring forth fruit for three years, a miraculous supply for the sabbatic year and for jubilee alike, staked by no legislator but one conscious of divine authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. They shall sow in the eighth year and eat yet of old fruit until the ninth year, until her fruits come in, they shall eat of the old store, the three-year supply covering sabbath, jubilee, and sowing years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The land shall not be sold for ever, for the land is God's, and they are strangers and sojourners with Him, holding it merely as tenants-at-will, with no power to dispose of it to strangers, though temporary sale with redemption at any time is allowed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. In all the land of their possession they shall grant redemption of the land, the inalienable land being redeemable by design. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. If a brother becomes poor and sells some possession, and his redeemer, his near kinsman, comes to redeem it, then he may redeem what his brother sold, the kinsman-redeemer restoring the family inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. If the man has no redeemer but himself becomes able to redeem it, he may redeem it himself, self-redemption being equally allowed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Then let him count the years of the sale and restore the overplus to the man to whom he sold it, that he may return to his possession, the price being reckoned by remaining years to jubilee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. But if he is not able to restore it, what was sold shall remain in the buyer's hand until the jubilee, and in the jubilee it shall go out and he shall return to his possession, recovering it free. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. If a man sells a dwelling house in a walled city, he may redeem it within a whole year after it is sold, within a full year he may redeem it, all house sales being subject to this condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. If it is not redeemed within a full year, the walled-city house shall be established forever to the buyer throughout generations and shall not go out in the jubilee, trading and foreign houses in walled towns differing from village houses connected with agriculture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. But houses of villages without walls round about shall be counted as the fields of the country, they may be redeemed, and they shall go out in the jubilee, village houses being treated as parts of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Notwithstanding, the cities of the Levites and houses of their cities may they redeem at any time, Levites having no possessions but towns and houses, with the same privileges as other Israelites' lands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. If a man purchases of the Levites, the house sold and the city of his possession shall go out in the jubilee, for Levite city houses are their possession among Israel, a permanent endowment for ministry support that cannot be alienated, though Levites may exchange among themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. But the field of the suburbs of their cities may not be sold, for it is their perpetual possession, pasture land for cattle and flocks appropriated to Levitical cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. If a brother becomes poor and falls in decay, then they shall relieve him, whether stranger or sojourner, that he may live with them, a most benevolent provision for poor and unfortunate, requiring food, lodging, and money without usury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. They shall take no usury nor increase from him, but fear God, that their brother may live with them, usury being severely condemned, though the prohibition is not applicable to modern business lending at legal rates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. They shall not give him money upon usury, nor lend him victuals for increase, the same no-usury rule applying to food as to money. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. God is the LORD their God, who brought them out of Egypt to give them Canaan and to be their God, the exodus grounding the poor-relief law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. If a brother becomes poor and is sold to his neighbour, he shall not be compelled to serve as a bondservant, an Israelite in misfortune being treated not as a slave but as a hired servant with temporary engagement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. But as a hired servant and as a sojourner shall he be with his master, and serve until the year of jubilee, the engagement ending at jubilee even without redemption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Then shall he depart from his master, he and his children with him, and return to his own family and to his fathers' possession, regaining liberty and inheritance together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. For they are God's servants, whom He brought out of Egypt, they shall not be sold as bondmen, Israel's servitude to God precluding perpetual servitude to man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. His master shall not rule over him with rigour, but fear God, the hired-servant treatment forbidding harsh rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. Bondmen and bondmaids may be had of the heathen round about, of them shall they buy bondmen and bondmaids, perpetual servitude being confined to foreigners, unlike Israelite hired service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. Moreover of strangers sojourning among them, and of their families born in the land, may they buy, and they shall be their possession, sojourner families being purchasable as perpetual servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. They may take them as inheritance for children after them, to inherit as possession, serving forever, but over Israelite brethren they shall not rule with rigour, the distinction between foreign bondage and brotherly hired service being absolute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. If a sojourner or stranger waxes rich, and a brother beside him waxes poor and sells himself to the stranger or to his stock, the poor Israelite may be sold even to a foreigner, and the jubilee redemption still applies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. After he is sold, he may be redeemed again, one of his brethren may redeem him, a kinsman redeeming him at any time before jubilee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. Either his uncle, his uncle's son, or any near kin of his family may redeem him, or, if he is able, he may redeem himself, the redemption circle extending through the family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. He shall reckon with him who bought him from the year he was sold until the jubilee, and the price of his sale shall be according to the number of years, as the wages of a hired servant, the ransom being years remaining multiplied by current wages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. If there are yet many years, according to them he shall give again the price of his redemption out of the money he was bought for, the equitable principle governing a long remaining term. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. If there remain but few years unto the jubilee, then he shall count with him, and according to his years shall he give again the price of his redemption, a short term costing proportionally less. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. And as a yearly hired servant shall he be with him, and the other shall not rule with rigour over him in their sight, even a foreign master being restrained from harsh rule over an Israelite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. If he is not redeemed in these years, then he shall go out in the year of jubilee, he and his children with him, liberty coming by jubilee as a matter of course. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. For unto God the children of Israel are servants, they are His servants whom He brought out of Egypt, for He is the LORD their God, the jubilee's liberty resting on Israel's prior servitude to God alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 26. If the man has no redeemer but himself becomes able to redeem it, he may redeem it himself, self-redemption being equally allowed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Then let him count the years of the sale and restore the overplus to the man to whom he sold it, that he may return to his possession, the price being reckoned by remaining years to jubilee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. But if he is not able to restore it, what was sold shall remain in the buyer's hand until the jubilee, and in the jubilee it shall go out and he shall return to his possession, recovering it free. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. If a man sells a dwelling house in a walled city, he may redeem it within a whole year after it is sold, within a full year he may redeem it, all house sales being subject to this condition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. If it is not redeemed within a full year, the walled-city house shall be established forever to the buyer throughout generations and shall not go out in the jubilee, trading and foreign houses in walled towns differing from village houses connected with agriculture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. But houses of villages without walls round about shall be counted as the fields of the country, they may be redeemed, and they shall go out in the jubilee, village houses being treated as parts of the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Notwithstanding, the cities of the Levites and houses of their cities may they redeem at any time, Levites having no possessions but towns and houses, with the same privileges as other Israelites' lands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. If a man purchases of the Levites, the house sold and the city of his possession shall go out in the jubilee, for Levite city houses are their possession among Israel, a permanent endowment for ministry support that cannot be alienated, though Levites may exchange among themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. But the field of the suburbs of their cities may not be sold, for it is their perpetual possession, pasture land for cattle and flocks appropriated to Levitical cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. If a brother becomes poor and falls in decay, then they shall relieve him, whether stranger or sojourner, that he may live with them, a most benevolent provision for poor and unfortunate, requiring food, lodging, and money without usury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. They shall take no usury nor increase from him, but fear God, that their brother may live with them, usury being severely condemned, though the prohibition is not applicable to modern business lending at legal rates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. They shall not give him money upon usury, nor lend him victuals for increase, the same no-usury rule applying to food as to money. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. God is the LORD their God, who brought them out of Egypt to give them Canaan and to be their God, the exodus grounding the poor-relief law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. If a brother becomes poor and is sold to his neighbour, he shall not be compelled to serve as a bondservant, an Israelite in misfortune being treated not as a slave but as a hired servant with temporary engagement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. But as a hired servant and as a sojourner shall he be with his master, and serve until the year of jubilee, the engagement ending at jubilee even without redemption. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Then shall he depart from his master, he and his children with him, and return to his own family and to his fathers' possession, regaining liberty and inheritance together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. For they are God's servants, whom He brought out of Egypt, they shall not be sold as bondmen, Israel's servitude to God precluding perpetual servitude to man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. His master shall not rule over him with rigour, but fear God, the hired-servant treatment forbidding harsh rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. Bondmen and bondmaids may be had of the heathen round about, of them shall they buy bondmen and bondmaids, perpetual servitude being confined to foreigners, unlike Israelite hired service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. Moreover of strangers sojourning among them, and of their families born in the land, may they buy, and they shall be their possession, sojourner families being purchasable as perpetual servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. They may take them as inheritance for children after them, to inherit as possession, serving forever, but over Israelite brethren they shall not rule with rigour, the distinction between foreign bondage and brotherly hired service being absolute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. If a sojourner or stranger waxes rich, and a brother beside him waxes poor and sells himself to the stranger or to his stock, the poor Israelite may be sold even to a foreigner, and the jubilee redemption still applies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. After he is sold, he may be redeemed again, one of his brethren may redeem him, a kinsman redeeming him at any time before jubilee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. Either his uncle, his uncle's son, or any near kin of his family may redeem him, or, if he is able, he may redeem himself, the redemption circle extending through the family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. He shall reckon with him who bought him from the year he was sold until the jubilee, and the price of his sale shall be according to the number of years, as the wages of a hired servant, the ransom being years remaining multiplied by current wages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 51. If there are yet many years, according to them he shall give again the price of his redemption out of the money he was bought for, the equitable principle governing a long remaining term. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 52. If there remain but few years unto the jubilee, then he shall count with him, and according to his years shall he give again the price of his redemption, a short term costing proportionally less. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 53. And as a yearly hired servant shall he be with him, and the other shall not rule with rigour over him in their sight, even a foreign master being restrained from harsh rule over an Israelite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 54. If he is not redeemed in these years, then he shall go out in the year of jubilee, he and his children with him, liberty coming by jubilee as a matter of course. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 55. For unto God the children of Israel are servants, they are His servants whom He brought out of Egypt, for He is the LORD their God, the jubilee's liberty resting on Israel's prior servitude to God alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
