The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 A wise son heareth his father’s instruction: but a scorner heareth not rebuke.

2 A man shall eat good by the fruit of his mouth: but the soul of the transgressors shall eat violence.

3 He that keepeth his mouth keepeth his life: but he that openeth wide his lips shall have destruction.

4 The soul of the sluggard desireth, and hath nothing: but the soul of the diligent shall be made fat.

5 A righteous man hateth lying: but a wicked man is loathsome, and cometh to shame.

6 Righteousness keepeth him that is upright in the way: but wickedness overthroweth the sinner.

7 There is that maketh himself rich, yet hath nothing: there is that maketh himself poor, yet hath great riches.

8 The ransom of a man’s life are his riches: but the poor heareth not rebuke.

9 The light of the righteous rejoiceth: but the lamp of the wicked shall be put out.

10 Only by pride cometh contention: but with the well advised is wisdom.

11 Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished: but he that gathereth by labour shall increase.

12 Hope deferred maketh the heart sick: but when the desire cometh, it is a tree of life.

13 Whoso despiseth the word shall be destroyed: but he that feareth the commandment shall be rewarded.

14 The law of the wise is a fountain of life, to depart from the snares of death.

15 Good understanding giveth favour: but the way of transgressors is hard.

16 Every prudent man dealeth with knowledge: but a fool layeth open his folly.

17 A wicked messenger falleth into mischief: but a faithful ambassador is health.

18 Poverty and shame shall be to him that refuseth instruction: but he that regardeth reproof shall be honoured.

19 The desire accomplished is sweet to the soul: but it is abomination to fools to depart from evil.

20 He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed.

21 Evil pursueth sinners: but to the righteous good shall be repayed.

22 A good man leaveth an inheritance to his children’s children: and the wealth of the sinner is laid up for the just.

23 Much food is in the tillage of the poor: but there is that is destroyed for want of judgment.

24 He that spareth his rod hateth his son: but he that loveth him chasteneth him betimes.

25 The righteous eateth to the satisfying of his soul: but the belly of the wicked shall want.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Speech, appetite and company dominate: keep your mouth and keep your life, the sluggard desires and has nothing, wealth gotten by vanity diminishes while labour gathers. Hope deferred sickens the heart, a companion of fools is destroyed while walking with the wise makes wise, and the chapter's discipline is plain: spare the rod and hate the son, chasten betimes and love him. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. A wise son hears his father's instruction, but a scorner hears not rebuke, for there is great hope of those who reverence their parents, and little hope of any who will not hear faithful dealers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. A man shall eat good by the fruit of his mouth, but the soul of transgressors shall eat violence, for by our words we must be justified or condemned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. He who keeps his mouth keeps his life, but he who opens wide his lips shall have destruction, for thinking before speaking and suppressing evil thought keeps the soul from much guilt and grief, while many are ruined by an ungoverned tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The soul of the sluggard desires and has nothing, but the soul of the diligent shall be made fat, for the slothful desire the diligent's gains but hate the diligent's pains, and this is especially true of the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. A righteous man hates lying, but a wicked man is loathsome and comes to shame, for where sin reigns the man is loathsome, and an awake conscience would abhor itself and repent in dust and ashes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Righteousness keeps him upright in the way, but wickedness overthrows the sinner, for an honest desire to do right preserves from fatal mistakes better than a thousand fine-drawn distinctions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. There is that makes himself rich yet has nothing, and that makes himself poor yet has great riches, for some really poor trade as if rich, which is sin and shame, while some really rich would be thought poor, and fearing Christians spiritually rich yet thinking themselves poor make themselves poor by doubts and griefs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The ransom of a man's life are his riches, but the poor hears not rebuke, for great riches often tempt to violence against their possessors, while the poor are free from such perils. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The light of the righteous rejoices, but the lamp of the wicked shall be put out, for the righteous light is as the sun, eclipsed and clouded yet continuing, while the wicked light is a lamp of their own kindling, easily put out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Only by pride comes contention, but with the well advised is wisdom, for all contentions, between persons, families, churches, or nations, are begun and carried forward by pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished, but he who gathers by labour shall increase, for wealth gotten by dishonesty or vice has a secret curse that speedily wastes it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Hope deferred makes the heart sick, but when desire comes it is a tree of life, for delay of anxiously hoped things is painful, obtaining them pleasant, and spiritual blessings are chiefly intended. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Whoso despises the word shall be destroyed, but he who fears the commandment shall be rewarded, for he who stands in awe of God and reverences his word escapes destruction and is rewarded for godly fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The law of the wise is a fountain of life, to depart from the snares of death, for the rule by which the wise regulate conduct is a fountain yielding life and happiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Good understanding gives favour, but the way of transgressors is hard, for the way of sinners is hard upon others and hard to the sinner himself, the service of sin being slavery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Every prudent man deals with knowledge, but a fool lays open his folly, for it is folly to talk of things of which we know nothing and to undertake what we are no way fit for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. A wicked messenger falls into mischief, but a faithful ambassador is health, for those wicked and false to Christ and to souls do mischief and fall into it, while the faithful find sound words healing to others and themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Poverty and shame shall be to him who refuses instruction, but he who regards reproof shall be honoured, for he who scorns to be taught will certainly be brought down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. The desire accomplished is sweet to the soul, but it is abomination to fools to depart from evil, for men have strong desires after happiness, yet none who will not leave their sins can expect anything truly sweet to their souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. He who walks with wise men shall be wise, but a companion of fools shall be destroyed, for multitudes are brought to ruin by bad company, and all who make themselves wicked will be destroyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Evil pursues sinners, but to the righteous good shall be repaid, for when God pursues sinners he is sure to overtake them, and he will reward the righteous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children, and the wealth of the sinner is laid up for the just, for the servant of God not anxious about riches takes the best method of providing for his children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Much food is in the tillage of the poor, but there is that is destroyed for want of judgment, for the poor yet industrious thrive though homely, while the greatly rich are often brought to poverty for want of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. He who spares his rod hates his son, but he who loves him chastens him betimes, for false indulgence permitting sinful habits to gather strength acts as if hating the child, bringing sorrow here and misery hereafter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. The righteous eats to the satisfying of his soul, but the belly of the wicked shall want, for the wicked's sensual appetites always crave, while the righteous feeds on the word and ordinances, satisfied with gospel promises and Christ, the Bread of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
