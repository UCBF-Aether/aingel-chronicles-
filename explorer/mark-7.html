The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then came together unto him the Pharisees, and certain of the scribes, which came from Jerusalem.

2 And when they saw some of his disciples eat bread with defiled, that is to say, with unwashen, hands, they found fault.

3 For the Pharisees, and all the Jews, except they wash their hands oft, eat not, holding the tradition of the elders.

4 And when they come from the market, except they wash, they eat not. And many other things there be, which they have received to hold, as the washing of cups, and pots, brasen vessels, and of tables.

5 Then the Pharisees and scribes asked him, Why walk not thy disciples according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with unwashen hands?

6 He answered and said unto them, Well hath Esaias prophesied of you hypocrites, as it is written, This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me.

7 Howbeit in vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.

8 For laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men, as the washing of pots and cups: and many other such like things ye do.

9 And he said unto them, Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition.

10 For Moses said, Honour thy father and thy mother; and, Whoso curseth father or mother, let him die the death:

11 But ye say, If a man shall say to his father or mother, It is Corban, that is to say, a gift, by whatsoever thou mightest be profited by me; he shall be free.

12 And ye suffer him no more to do ought for his father or his mother;

13 Making the word of God of none effect through your tradition, which ye have delivered: and many such like things do ye.

14 And when he had called all the people unto him, he said unto them, Hearken unto me every one of you, and understand:

15 There is nothing from without a man, that entering into him can defile him: but the things which come out of him, those are they that defile the man.

16 If any man have ears to hear, let him hear.

17 And when he was entered into the house from the people, his disciples asked him concerning the parable.

18 And he saith unto them, Are ye so without understanding also? Do ye not perceive, that whatsoever thing from without entereth into the man, it cannot defile him;

19 Because it entereth not into his heart, but into the belly, and goeth out into the draught, purging all meats?

20 And he said, That which cometh out of the man, that defileth the man.

21 For from within, out of the heart of men, proceed evil thoughts, adulteries, fornications, murders,

22 Thefts, covetousness, wickedness, deceit, lasciviousness, an evil eye, blasphemy, pride, foolishness:

23 All these evil things come from within, and defile the man.

24 And from thence he arose, and went into the borders of Tyre and Sidon, and entered into an house, and would have no man know it: but he could not be hid.

25 For a certain woman, whose young daughter had an unclean spirit, heard of him, and came and fell at his feet:

26 The woman was a Greek, a Syrophenician by nation; and she besought him that he would cast forth the devil out of her daughter.

27 But Jesus said unto her, Let the children first be filled: for it is not meet to take the children’s bread, and to cast it unto the dogs.

28 And she answered and said unto him, Yes, Lord: yet the dogs under the table eat of the children’s crumbs.

29 And he said unto her, For this saying go thy way; the devil is gone out of thy daughter.

30 And when she was come to her house, she found the devil gone out, and her daughter laid upon the bed.

31 And again, departing from the coasts of Tyre and Sidon, he came unto the sea of Galilee, through the midst of the coasts of Decapolis.

32 And they bring unto him one that was deaf, and had an impediment in his speech; and they beseech him to put his hand upon him.

33 And he took him aside from the multitude, and put his fingers into his ears, and he spit, and touched his tongue;

34 And looking up to heaven, he sighed, and saith unto him, Ephphatha, that is, Be opened.

35 And straightway his ears were opened, and the string of his tongue was loosed, and he spake plain.

36 And he charged them that they should tell no man: but the more he charged them, so much the more a great deal they published it;

37 And were beyond measure astonished, saying, He hath done all things well: he maketh both the deaf to hear, and the dumb to speak.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Pharisees and scribes challenge Jesus because his disciples eat with unwashen hands; he answers that their traditions, illustrated by Corban, set aside God’s commandment, and teaches that defilement comes from within the heart, not from food entering a man (vv. 1–23). He then travels to Tyre and Sidon, where a Syrophoenician woman’s persistent answer wins healing for her daughter (vv. 24–30), and back by the Sea of Galilee he opens a deaf man’s ears and loosens his tongue with the word “Ephphatha” (vv. 31–37). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 7

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then came together to him the Pharisees and certain scribes from Jerusalem. Jerusalem's learned men travelled to judge a Galilean teacher, and their coming shows the opposition was now organised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When they saw some of his disciples eat bread with defiled, that is, unwashen, hands, they found fault. Their fault was not with dirt but with ceremony: hands unwashed by ritual were called defiled, though they were clean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For the Pharisees and all the Jews, except they wash their hands oft, eat not, holding the tradition of the elders. Hand-washing before meals was an elders' tradition, not Moses' law, yet it bound conscience as if it were God's command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. When they come from the market, except they wash, they eat not, and many other things they hold, as washing cups, pots, brazen vessels, and tables. Tradition multiplied washings until vessels and furniture bore the same yoke as hands, and religion became a matter of pots. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Then the Pharisees and scribes asked him, Why do your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat with unwashen hands? They charged the disciples, aiming through them at Christ, and made custom the test of godliness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He answered, Well has Isaiah prophesied of you hypocrites, as it is written, This people honours me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. Isaiah's charge fit them exactly: lip-service with a distant heart is the definition of their religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. However, in vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. Worship built on men's commands is vain, however earnest, because God will not accept his own worship on man's terms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For laying aside the commandment of God, you hold the tradition of men, as washing pots and cups, and many such like things you do. Tradition did not merely add to God's law; it displaced it, and pots were honoured while precepts were set aside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. He said, Full well you reject the commandment of God, that you may keep your own tradition. Their rejection was deliberate and clever: tradition was kept precisely by breaking the commandment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For Moses said, Honour your father and mother, and, Whoever curses father or mother, let him die the death. The fifth commandment demands both honour in deed and reverence in speech, with a severe sanction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. But you say, If a man says to his father or mother, It is Corban, a gift, by whatever you might be profited by me, he shall be free. By vowing his goods to the temple as Corban, a son could claim release from supporting his parents, and the scribes allowed it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And you suffer him no more to do anything for his father or mother. The tradition did not merely excuse neglect; it forbade the very help the commandment required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Making the word of God of no effect through your tradition, which you have delivered, and many such like things you do. One nullified commandment proves the system: tradition that voids Scripture is itself condemned, however many cases follow it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. When he had called all the people to him, he said, Hearken to me, every one of you, and understand. Christ turns from the scribes to the people, for the truth about defilement concerns everyone, and all must understand it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. There is nothing from without a man, that entering into him can defile him, but the things which come out of him, those defile the man. Food and touch cannot stain the soul; defilement is moral, not ceremonial, and comes from within. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. If any man have ears to hear, let him hear. The saying is a call to attention, for a truth that overturns tradition must be weighed, not merely heard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. When he entered the house from the people, his disciples asked him concerning the parable. Private questions again follow public teaching, and disciples are not blamed for asking what the crowd could not follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He said, Are you so without understanding also? Do you not perceive that whatever from without enters the man cannot defile him? Their slowness grieved him, for the principle was plain: what is eaten does not reach the conscience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Because it enters not into his heart, but into the belly, and goes out into the draught, purging all meats. Food passes through the body without touching the heart, and in saying so Christ declared all meats clean in principle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He said, That which comes out of the man, that defiles the man. The mouth and the life reveal the heart, and it is the heart's outflow that stains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. For from within, out of the heart of men, proceed evil thoughts, adulteries, fornications, murders. The catalogue begins with thoughts, for sin is conceived within before it is committed without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Thefts, covetousness, wickedness, deceit, lasciviousness, an evil eye, blasphemy, pride, foolishness. The list moves from deeds to desires to dispositions, showing the heart's full corruption in every direction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. All these evil things come from within, and defile the man. Every one of them is born inside, and together they prove that washing hands can never wash a heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. From there he arose and went into the borders of Tyre and Sidon, and entered a house, and would have no man know it, but he could not be hid. Even in Gentile borders, seeking privacy, his fame found him, for a light cannot be hidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. For a certain woman, whose young daughter had an unclean spirit, heard of him, and came and fell at his feet. A mother's hearing brought her to his feet, and her daughter's demon became her own petition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The woman was a Greek, a Syrophenician by nation, and she besought him to cast forth the devil out of her daughter. A Gentile outsider sought a Jewish Messiah, and her earnest prayer crossed every boundary of nation and custom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. But Jesus said to her, Let the children first be filled, for it is not meet to take the children's bread and cast it to the dogs. The children are Israel, first served by the covenant, and the dogs are Gentiles; the saying tests whether she will accept the order of grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. She answered, Yes, Lord: yet the dogs under the table eat of the children's crumbs. She accepts the name of dog and turns it into a plea: even crumbs from the children's table are enough for her need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. He said to her, For this saying go your way; the devil is gone out of your daughter. Her wise and humble answer won the cure, and faith expressed in a single sentence prevailed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. When she came to her house, she found the devil gone out, and her daughter laid upon the bed. The cure was wrought at a distance by his word, and the quiet daughter on the bed proved the demon's departure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Again, departing from the coasts of Tyre and Sidon, he came to the Sea of Galilee, through the midst of the coasts of Decapolis. His route through Gentile Decapolis foreshadowed a gospel that would travel those roads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. They brought to him one that was deaf and had an impediment in his speech, and they besought him to put his hand upon him.   • Verse 33. He took him aside from the multitude, and put his fingers into his ears, and he spit, and touched his tongue. Private, tangible signs suited a man who could not hear explanation, and each sense was touched in turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Looking up to heaven, he sighed, and said to him, Ephphatha, that is, Be opened. The upward look owned heaven as the source, the sigh showed compassion for human misery, and the word commanded what nature could not do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Straightway his ears were opened, and the string of his tongue was loosed, and he spoke plain. Hearing and speech were healed together, for the same power opens both ear and tongue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. He charged them that they should tell no man, but the more he charged them, the more a great deal they published it. Disobedience to his charge, though from wonder, still hindered the quiet he sought, and their publishing could not be contained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Their verdict is the gospel's summary: all Christ does is well done, restoring creation's broken senses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Corban (v. 11): A gift devoted to God: the chapter text itself glosses it as “that is to say, a gift” — the excuse by which, Jesus says, men set aside caring for father or mother. Documented Standard corpus, Mark 7:11

Connections to other passages

  • Isaiah’s prophecy against hypocrites (Mark 7:6). The study note on verse 6 identifies the quoted prophecy — “This people honours me with their lips, but their heart is far from me” — as Isaiah’s, and says it fit the Pharisees exactly. Interpreted Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, study note on Mark 7:6
