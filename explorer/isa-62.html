The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 62 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 For Zion’s sake will I not hold my peace, and for Jerusalem’s sake I will not rest, until the righteousness thereof go forth as brightness, and the salvation thereof as a lamp that burneth.

2 And the Gentiles shall see thy righteousness, and all kings thy glory: and thou shalt be called by a new name, which the mouth of the LORD shall name.

3 Thou shalt also be a crown of glory in the hand of the LORD, and a royal diadem in the hand of thy God.

4 Thou shalt no more be termed Forsaken; neither shall thy land any more be termed Desolate: but thou shalt be called Hephzibah, and thy land Beulah: for the LORD delighteth in thee, and thy land shall be married.

5 For as a young man marrieth a virgin, so shall thy sons marry thee: and as the bridegroom rejoiceth over the bride, so shall thy God rejoice over thee.

6 I have set watchmen upon thy walls, O Jerusalem, which shall never hold their peace day nor night: ye that make mention of the LORD, keep not silence,

7 And give him no rest, till he establish, and till he make Jerusalem a praise in the earth.

8 The LORD hath sworn by his right hand, and by the arm of his strength, Surely I will no more give thy corn to be meat for thine enemies; and the sons of the stranger shall not drink thy wine, for the which thou hast laboured:

9 But they that have gathered it shall eat it, and praise the LORD; and they that have brought it together shall drink it in the courts of my holiness.

10 Go through, go through the gates; prepare ye the way of the people; cast up, cast up the highway; gather out the stones; lift up a standard for the people.

11 Behold, the LORD hath proclaimed unto the end of the world, Say ye to the daughter of Zion, Behold, thy salvation cometh; behold, his reward is with him, and his work before him.

12 And they shall call them, The holy people, The redeemed of the LORD: and thou shalt be called, Sought out, A city not forsaken.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

For Zion's sake the prophet will not hold his peace until her righteousness goes forth as brightness and her salvation as a burning lamp. Watchmen are set on the walls who must give God no rest until Jerusalem is made a praise in the earth, and the chapter ends with new names: the holy people, the redeemed of the Lord, a city sought out and not forsaken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. For Zion's sake I will not hold my peace, and for Jerusalem's sake I will not rest, until the righteousness thereof goes forth as brightness, and the salvation thereof as a lamp that burns. This chapter treats intercessory prayers for Zion's restoration, accompanying God's promises of it, as the appointed means of accomplishing it. I means the prophet, as representative of all the praying people of God who love and intercede for Zion, as in verses 6 and 7 and Psalm 102, or else Messiah, as in verse 6. So Messiah is represented as unfainting in His efforts for His people, as in chapters 42 and 50. Righteousness thereof means not its own inherently, but imputed to it, for its restoration to God's favour: hence salvation answers to it in the parallelism. Judah is to be saved through the Lord our, Judah's and the church's, righteousness, as in Jeremiah. As brightness means properly the bright shining of the rising sun, as in chapters 60 and 4, 2 Samuel, and Proverbs. Lamp means blazing torch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The Gentiles will see your righteousness, and all kings your glory, and you will be called by a new name, which the mouth of the Lord will name. This recalls chapters 11, 42, 49, and 60. New name means expression of thy new and improved condition, as in verse 4, the more valuable and lasting as being conferred by Jehovah Himself, as in verses 12 and chapter 65 and Revelation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. You will also be a crown of glory in the hand of the Lord, and a royal diadem in the hand of your God. This recalls Zechariah. In hand of Lord means as a crown is worn on the head, not in the hand, hand must here be figurative for under the Lord's protection, as in Deuteronomy. All His saints are in thy hand. His people are in His hand at the same time that they are a crown of glory to Him, as in Revelation; reciprocally, He is a crown of glory and a diadem of beauty to them, as in chapter 28, compare Malachi. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. You will no more be termed Forsaken, neither will your land any more be termed Desolate, but you will be called Hephzibah, and your land Beulah, for the Lord delights in you, and your land will be married. Be termed means be forsaken, so as that that term could be applicable to thee. Hephzibah recalls 2 Kings, the name of Hezekiah's wife, a type of Jerusalem, as Hezekiah was of Messiah, as in chapter 32: my delight is in her. Beulah means Thou art married. See the same contrast of Zion's past and future state under the same figure, as in chapters 54 and Revelation. Land married means to Jehovah as its Lord and Husband: implying not only ownership, but protection on the part of the Owner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For as a young man marries a virgin, so will your sons marry you, and as the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so will your God rejoice over you. Thy sons is rather, changing the points, which are of no authority in Hebrew, thy builder or restorer, that is, God; for in the parallel clause, and in verse 4, God is implied as being married to her; whereas her sons could hardly be said to marry their mother; and in chapter 49 verse 18, they are said to be her bridal ornaments, not her husband. The plural form, builders, is used of God in reverence as husbands, as in chapter 54. Over the bride means in the possession of the bride, as in chapters 65 and Jeremiah and Zephaniah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. I have set watchmen upon your walls, O Jerusalem, which will never hold their peace day nor night; you that make mention of the Lord, keep not silence. I means Isaiah speaking in the person of the Messiah. Watchmen upon walls means image from the watches set upon a city's wall to look out for the approach of a messenger with good tidings, as in chapter 52; the good tidings of the return of the Jewish exiles from Babylon, prefiguring the return from the present dispersion, as in chapters 21 and 56 and Ezekiel. The watches in the East are announced by a loud cry to mark the vigilance of the watchmen. Ye that mention Lord means Hebrew, ye that are the Lord's remembrancers; God's servants who by their prayers put God in remembrance of His promises, as in chapter 43; we are required to remind God, as if God could, which He cannot, forget His promises, as in Psalms and Jeremiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And give him no rest, till he establish, and till he make Jerusalem a praise in the earth. Peace, not rest recalls verse 1; Messiah's watchmen in verses 6 and 7 imitate Him in verse 1 in intercessory prayer without ceasing for Jerusalem, as in Psalms; also for the spiritual Jerusalem, the church, as in Luke and Romans. A praise recalls chapters 61 and Zephaniah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The Lord has sworn by his right hand, and by the arm of his strength, Surely I will no more give your corn to be meat for your enemies, and the sons of the stranger will not drink your wine, for which you have laboured. Right hand means His mighty instrument of accomplishing His will, as in chapters 45 and Hebrews. Sons of stranger means foreigners shall no more rob thee of the fruit of thy labours, as in chapters 65. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. But they that have gathered it will eat it, and praise the Lord, and they that have brought it together will drink it in the courts of my holiness. And praise means not consume it on their own lusts, and without thanksgiving. Drink it in courts means they who have gathered the vintage shall drink it at the feasts held in the courts surrounding the temple, as in Deuteronomy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Go through, go through the gates; prepare the way of the people; cast up, cast up the highway; gather out the stones; lift up a standard for the people. What Isaiah in the person of Messiah had engaged in, verse 1, unrestingly to seek, and what the watchmen were unrestingly to pray for, verse 7, and what Jehovah solemnly promised, verses 8 and 9, is now to be fulfilled; the Gentile nations are commanded to go through the gates, either of their own cities, or of Jerusalem, in order to remove all obstacles out of the way of the people, Israel, as in chapters 7, 40, and 52. Standard means for the dispersed Jews to rally round, with a view to their return, as in chapters 49 and 11. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Behold, the Lord has proclaimed unto the end of the world, Say to the daughter of Zion, Behold, your salvation comes; behold, his reward is with him, and his work before him. Salvation means embodied in the Saviour, as in Zechariah. His work is rather, recompense, as in chapter 40. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. They will call them, The holy people, The redeemed of the Lord, and you will be called, Sought out, A city not forsaken. Sought out means sought after and highly prized by Jehovah; answering to not forsaken in the parallel clause; no longer abandoned, but loved; image from a wife, as in verse 4 and Jeremiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
