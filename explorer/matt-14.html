The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Matthew 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 At that time Herod the tetrarch heard of the fame of Jesus,

2 And said unto his servants, This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead; and therefore mighty works do shew forth themselves in him.

3 For Herod had laid hold on John, and bound him, and put him in prison for Herodias’ sake, his brother Philip’s wife.

4 For John said unto him, It is not lawful for thee to have her.

5 And when he would have put him to death, he feared the multitude, because they counted him as a prophet.

6 But when Herod’s birthday was kept, the daughter of Herodias danced before them, and pleased Herod.

7 Whereupon he promised with an oath to give her whatsoever she would ask.

8 And she, being before instructed of her mother, said, Give me here John Baptist’s head in a charger.

9 And the king was sorry: nevertheless for the oath’s sake, and them which sat with him at meat, he commanded it to be given her.

10 And he sent, and beheaded John in the prison.

11 And his head was brought in a charger, and given to the damsel: and she brought it to her mother.

12 And his disciples came, and took up the body, and buried it, and went and told Jesus.

13 When Jesus heard of it, he departed thence by ship into a desert place apart: and when the people had heard thereof, they followed him on foot out of the cities.

14 And Jesus went forth, and saw a great multitude, and was moved with compassion toward them, and he healed their sick.

15 And when it was evening, his disciples came to him, saying, This is a desert place, and the time is now past; send the multitude away, that they may go into the villages, and buy themselves victuals.

16 But Jesus said unto them, They need not depart; give ye them to eat.

17 And they say unto him, We have here but five loaves, and two fishes.

18 He said, Bring them hither to me.

19 And he commanded the multitude to sit down on the grass, and took the five loaves, and the two fishes, and looking up to heaven, he blessed, and brake, and gave the loaves to his disciples, and the disciples to the multitude.

20 And they did all eat, and were filled: and they took up of the fragments that remained twelve baskets full.

21 And they that had eaten were about five thousand men, beside women and children.

22 And straightway Jesus constrained his disciples to get into a ship, and to go before him unto the other side, while he sent the multitudes away.

23 And when he had sent the multitudes away, he went up into a mountain apart to pray: and when the evening was come, he was there alone.

24 But the ship was now in the midst of the sea, tossed with waves: for the wind was contrary.

25 And in the fourth watch of the night Jesus went unto them, walking on the sea.

26 And when the disciples saw him walking on the sea, they were troubled, saying, It is a spirit; and they cried out for fear.

27 But straightway Jesus spake unto them, saying, Be of good cheer; it is I; be not afraid.

28 And Peter answered him and said, Lord, if it be thou, bid me come unto thee on the water.

29 And he said, Come. And when Peter was come down out of the ship, he walked on the water, to go to Jesus.

30 But when he saw the wind boisterous, he was afraid; and beginning to sink, he cried, saying, Lord, save me.

31 And immediately Jesus stretched forth his hand, and caught him, and said unto him, O thou of little faith, wherefore didst thou doubt?

32 And when they were come into the ship, the wind ceased.

33 Then they that were in the ship came and worshipped him, saying, Of a truth thou art the Son of God.

34 And when they were gone over, they came into the land of Gennesaret.

35 And when the men of that place had knowledge of him, they sent out into all that country round about, and brought unto him all that were diseased;

36 And besought him that they might only touch the hem of his garment: and as many as touched were made perfectly whole.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Herod Antipas, hearing of Jesus, thinks John the Baptist is risen, and the chapter recounts John's imprisonment for Herodias' sake and his beheading at Herodias' daughter's request. Jesus withdraws, yet feeds five thousand men besides women and children with five loaves and two fishes, walks on the sea in the night, bids Peter come to him on the water, and, landing in Gennesaret, heals all who touch the hem of his garment. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. At that time Herod the tetrarch heard of the fame of Jesus. Herod Antipas, one of the three sons of Herod the Great and own brother of Archelaus, ruled as tetrarch over Galilee and Perea; his name was spread abroad, and the fame of Jesus reached the court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And said unto his servants, This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead; and therefore mighty works do shew forth themselves in him. The murdered prophet haunted Herod's guilty breast like a spectre, seeming alive again and clothed with unearthly powers in the person of Jesus. The terror and reproach of conscience, which Herod could not shake off, are proofs and warnings of a future judgment, but there may be the terror of convictions where there is not the truth of conversion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For Herod had laid hold on John, and bound him, and put him in prison for Herodias' sake, his brother Philip's wife. John delivered his conscience faithfully against Herod's unlawful marriage; the world may call this rudeness and blind zeal, but the most powerful enemies can go no further than the Lord sees good to permit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. For John said unto him, It is not lawful for thee to have her. When men pretend to favour the gospel yet live in evil, we must not favour their self-delusion, but must deliver our consciences as John did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And when he would have put him to death, he feared the multitude, because they counted him as a prophet. Herod feared that putting John to death might raise a rebellion among the people, which it did not; but he never feared it might stir up his own conscience against him, which it did. Men fear being hanged for what they do not fear being damned for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. But when Herod's birthday was kept, the daughter of Herodias danced before them, and pleased Herod. Times of carnal mirth and jollity are convenient times for carrying on bad designs against God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Whereupon he promised with an oath to give her whatsoever she would ask. Herod would profusely reward a worthless dance, while imprisonment and death were the recompence of the man of God who sought the salvation of his soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And she, being before instructed of her mother, said, Give me here John Baptist's head in a charger. The daughter's request was her mother's malice speaking through her; there was real malice to John beneath Herod's consent, or else he would have found ways to get clear of his promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And the king was sorry: nevertheless for the oath's sake, and them which sat with him at meat, he commanded it to be given her. Herod's sorrow was the sorrow of a conscience-stricken man who yet feared the table more than God; an unlawful oath is better broken than kept to murder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And he sent, and beheaded John in the prison. The great forerunner dies by a courtier's cruelty at a birthday feast — a reminder that the world's honours are bought at terrible prices, and that faithfulness may cost the head yet lose nothing of the crown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And his head was brought in a charger, and given to the damsel: and she brought it to her mother. The ghastly trophy is paraded before the revellers, that malice might feast its eyes on the silenced prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And his disciples came, and took up the body, and buried it, and went and told Jesus. When the under shepherds are smitten, the sheep need not be scattered while they have the Great Shepherd to go to; it is better to be drawn to Christ by want and loss than not to come to him at all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. When Jesus heard of it, he departed thence by ship into a desert place apart: and when the people had heard thereof, they followed him on foot out of the cities. When Christ and his word withdraw, it is best for us to follow, seeking the means of grace for our souls before any worldly advantages; the presence of Christ and his gospel makes a desert not only tolerable but desirable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And Jesus went forth, and saw a great multitude, and was moved with compassion toward them, and he healed their sick. In seeking the welfare of men's souls, we should have compassion on their bodies likewise; his compassion did not weary at the crowd's intrusion on his retirement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And when it was evening, his disciples came to him, saying, This is a desert place, and the time is now past; send the multitude away, that they may go into the villages, and buy themselves victuals. The disciples' counsel was prudent by the world's measure, but it left the multitude unsupplied; Christ's answer would show that his house keeps open table even in a desert. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. But Jesus said unto them, They need not depart; give ye them to eat. He puts the work into his disciples' hands, that their faith and obedience might be exercised, though the provision was far beyond their own store. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And they say unto him, We have here but five loaves, and two fishes. The provisions of Christ's gospel appear mean and scanty to the world, yet they satisfy all that feed on him in their hearts by faith with thanksgiving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. He said, Bring them hither to me. Little is much when Christ takes it into his hands; what we have, however small, is to be brought to him rather than withheld for its smallness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And he commanded the multitude to sit down on the grass, and took the five loaves, and the two fishes, and looking up to heaven, he blessed, and brake, and gave the loaves to his disciples, and the disciples to the multitude. Let us always crave a blessing on our meals; this little supply was increased by Christ's creating power till the whole multitude were satisfied, an emblem of the Bread of life which came down from heaven to sustain our perishing souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And they did all eat, and were filled: and they took up of the fragments that remain twelve baskets full. None but the world's Creator could multiply the loaves; the abundance left over teaches us to avoid all waste, as frugality is the proper source of liberality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And they that had eaten were about five thousand men, beside women and children. The number is given by the men alone, so the multitude fed was greater still — a vast company satisfied from five loaves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And straightway Jesus constrained his disciples to get into a ship, and to go before him unto the other side, while he sent the multitudes away. He dismisses the crowd himself and sends the disciples on ahead, that he might be alone with God; those are not Christ's followers who cannot enjoy being alone with God and their own hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And when he had sent the multitudes away, he went up into a mountain apart to pray: and when the evening was come, he was there alone. It is good, upon special occasions and when we find our hearts enlarged, to continue long in secret prayer, pouring out our hearts before the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. But the ship was now in the midst of the sea, tossed with waves: for the wind was contrary. It is no new thing for Christ's disciples to meet with storms in the way of duty, but he thereby shows himself with the more grace to them and for them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And in the fourth watch of the night Jesus went unto them, walking on the sea. He can take what way he pleases to save his people; none but the world's Governor could tread upon the waters of the sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And when the disciples saw him walking on the sea, they were troubled, saying, It is a spirit; and they cried out for fear. Even appearances of deliverance sometimes occasion trouble and perplexity to God's people, from mistakes about Christ; nothing ought to affright those that have Christ near them and know he is theirs, not death itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. But straightway Jesus spake unto them, saying, Be of good cheer; it is I; be not afraid. His voice stills fear before his hand stills the sea; the presence of Christ is itself the comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And Peter answered him and said, Lord, if it be thou, bid me come unto thee on the water. Peter's request was not for diversion or to boast, but to go to Jesus; and in that he was thus wonderfully borne up. Special supports are promised and are to be expected, but only in spiritual pursuits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. And he said, Come. And when Peter was come down out of the ship, he walked on the water, to go to Jesus. Christ bade Peter come, not only that he might walk upon the water and so know his Lord's power, but that he might know his own weakness; nor can we ever come to Jesus unless upheld by his power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. But when he saw the wind boisterous, he was afraid; and beginning to sink, he cried, saying, Lord, save me. When we look off from Christ and look at the greatness of opposing difficulties, we shall begin to fall; but when we call to him, he will stretch out his arm and save us. We are never brought to cry thus till we find ourselves sinking: the sense of need drives us to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. And immediately Jesus stretched forth his hand, and caught him, and said unto him, O thou of little faith, wherefore didst thou doubt? He rebuked Peter. Could we but believe more, we should suffer less. The weakness of faith and the prevailing of doubts displease our Lord Jesus, for there is no good reason why Christ's disciples should be of a doubtful mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. And when they were come into the ship, the wind ceased. Even in a stormy day he is to them a very present help; his entrance into the ship ends the storm that his absence permitted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. Then they that were in the ship came and worshipped him, saying, Of a truth thou art the Son of God. The disciples yield to the evidence and confess their faith; they were suitably affected and worshipped Christ. He that comes to God must believe, and he that believes in God will come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. And when they were gone over, they came into the land of Gennesaret. Whithersoever Christ went, he was doing good; the landing brings a new harvest of healing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And when the men of that place had knowledge of him, they sent out into all that country round about, and brought unto him all that were diseased. The experiences of others may direct and encourage us in seeking for Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And besought him that they might only touch the hem of his garment: and as many as touched were made perfectly whole. They came humbly beseeching him to help them; those whom Christ heals, he heals perfectly. The healing virtue was not in the finger but in their faith, or rather in Christ whom their faith took hold upon. Were men more acquainted with Christ and with the diseased state of their souls, they would flock to receive his healing influences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
