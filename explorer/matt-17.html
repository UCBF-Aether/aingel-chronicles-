The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Matthew 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And after six days Jesus taketh Peter, James, and John his brother, and bringeth them up into an high mountain apart,

2 And was transfigured before them: and his face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light.

3 And, behold, there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him.

4 Then answered Peter, and said unto Jesus, Lord, it is good for us to be here: if thou wilt, let us make here three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elias.

5 While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him.

6 And when the disciples heard it, they fell on their face, and were sore afraid.

7 And Jesus came and touched them, and said, Arise, and be not afraid.

8 And when they had lifted up their eyes, they saw no man, save Jesus only.

9 And as they came down from the mountain, Jesus charged them, saying, Tell the vision to no man, until the Son of man be risen again from the dead.

10 And his disciples asked him, saying, Why then say the scribes that Elias must first come?

11 And Jesus answered and said unto them, Elias truly shall first come, and restore all things.

12 But I say unto you, That Elias is come already, and they knew him not, but have done unto him whatsoever they listed. Likewise shall also the Son of man suffer of them.

13 Then the disciples understood that he spake unto them of John the Baptist.

14 And when they were come to the multitude, there came to him a certain man, kneeling down to him, and saying,

15 Lord, have mercy on my son: for he is lunatick, and sore vexed: for ofttimes he falleth into the fire, and oft into the water.

16 And I brought him to thy disciples, and they could not cure him.

17 Then Jesus answered and said, O faithless and perverse generation, how long shall I be with you? how long shall I suffer you? bring him hither to me.

18 And Jesus rebuked the devil; and he departed out of him: and the child was cured from that very hour.

19 Then came the disciples to Jesus apart, and said, Why could not we cast him out?

20 And Jesus said unto them, Because of your unbelief: for verily I say unto you, If ye have faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye shall say unto this mountain, Remove hence to yonder place; and it shall remove; and nothing shall be impossible unto you.

21 Howbeit this kind goeth not out but by prayer and fasting.

22 And while they abode in Galilee, Jesus said unto them, The Son of man shall be betrayed into the hands of men:

23 And they shall kill him, and the third day he shall be raised again. And they were exceeding sorry.

24 And when they were come to Capernaum, they that received tribute money came to Peter, and said, Doth not your master pay tribute?

25 He saith, Yes. And when he was come into the house, Jesus prevented him, saying, What thinkest thou, Simon? of whom do the kings of the earth take custom or tribute? of their own children, or of strangers?

26 Peter saith unto him, Of strangers. Jesus saith unto him, Then are the children free.

27 Notwithstanding, lest we should offend them, go thou to the sea, and cast an hook, and take up the fish that first cometh up; and when thou hast opened his mouth, thou shalt find a piece of money: that take, and give unto them for me and thee.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Six days later Jesus takes Peter, James and John up a high mountain, is transfigured before them with Moses and Elias talking with him, and a voice from the cloud says, “This is my beloved Son, hear ye him.” Coming down, he heals a lunatick boy his disciples could not heal, foretells his betrayal, death and rising on the third day, and, at Capernaum, pays the temple tribute with a piece of money found in a fish's mouth, lest he offend. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And after six days Jesus taketh Peter, James, and John his brother, and bringeth them up into an high mountain apart. Now the disciples beheld somewhat of Christ's glory, as of the only begotten of the Father. It was intended to support their faith when they would have to witness his crucifixion, and to give them an idea of the glory prepared for them when changed by his power and made like him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And was transfigured before them: and his face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light. The glory was his own, breaking through the veil of his humiliation for a moment, that faith might hold fast what sight would soon lose at the cross. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And, behold, there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him. The Law and the Prophets bear witness to him in person, conversing with him of the decease he should accomplish at Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Then answered Peter, and said unto Jesus, Lord, it is good for us to be here: if thou wilt, let us make here three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elias. The apostles were overcome by the glorious sight. Peter thought it most desirable to continue there, and to go no more down to meet the sufferings of which he was so unwilling to hear. In this he knew not what he said. We are wrong if we look for a heaven here upon earth. Whatever tabernacles we propose to make for ourselves in this world, we must always remember to ask Christ's leave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him. That sacrifice was not yet offered, without which the souls of sinful men could not have been saved; and important services were to be done by Peter and his brethren. The bright cloud is an emblem of the Divine presence and glory, and the voice settles all rival claims: hear him, above Moses and Elias. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And when the disciples heard it, they fell on their face, and were sore afraid. Ever since man sinned, and heard God's voice in the garden, unusual appearances of God have been terrible to man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And Jesus came and touched them, and said, Arise, and be not afraid. They fell prostrate to the earth, till Jesus encouraged them; his touch and word lift fear into courage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And when they had lifted up their eyes, they saw no man, save Jesus only. When looking round, they beheld only their Lord as they commonly saw him. We must pass through varied experiences in our way to glory; and when we return to the world after an ordinance, it must be our care to take Christ with us, and then it may be our comfort that he is with us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And as they came down from the mountain, Jesus charged them, saying, Tell the vision to no man, until the Son of man be risen again from the dead. The vision is sealed until the resurrection gives it its key; premature telling would only feed misunderstanding of a suffering Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And his disciples asked him, saying, Why then say the scribes that Elias must first come? If Elias must precede the Messiah, and Elias has but now appeared, the disciples ask how the scribes' expectation stands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And Jesus answered and said unto them, Elias truly shall first come, and restore all things. The promise stands: the forerunner's work of restoration precedes the kingdom's full revealing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. But I say unto you, That Elias is come already, and they knew him not, but have done unto him whatsoever they listed. Likewise shall also the Son of man suffer of them. John the Baptist came in the spirit and power of Elias, and was rejected and slain; the same hands that refused the forerunner will refuse the Lord himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Then the disciples understood that he spake unto them of John the Baptist. The identification dawns on them: the Elias who was to come had come, unrecognized, in John. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And when they were come to the multitude, there came to him a certain man, kneeling down to him, and saying. From the mountain's glory they descend to a father's grief at the foot of the hill — the pattern of ministry, glory above and need below. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Lord, have mercy on my son: for he is lunatick, and sore vexed: for ofttimes he falleth into the fire, and oft into the water. The case of afflicted children should be presented to God by faithful and fervent prayer; the boy's sufferings, falling into fire and water, show a malice that would destroy him if it could. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And I brought him to thy disciples, and they could not cure him. When all other helps and succours fail, we are welcome to Christ, and may trust in him and in his power and goodness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Then Jesus answered and said, O faithless and perverse generation, how long shall I be with you? how long shall I suffer you? bring him hither to me. Though the people were perverse, and Christ was provoked, yet care was taken of the child. See here an emblem of Christ's undertaking as our Redeemer. It encourages parents to bring children to Christ, whose souls are under Satan's power; he is able to heal them, and as willing as he is able. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And Jesus rebuked the devil; and he departed out of him: and the child was cured from that very hour. Christ cured the child. Not only bring children to Christ by prayer, but bring them to the word of Christ, to means by which Satan's strong-holds in the soul are beaten down. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Then came the disciples to Jesus apart, and said, Why could not we cast him out? The disciples' private question is honest: they had cast out devils before, and want to know why this one resisted them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And Jesus said unto them, Because of your unbelief: for verily I say unto you, If ye have faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye shall say unto this mountain, Remove hence to yonder place; and it shall remove; and nothing shall be impossible unto you. It is good for us to distrust ourselves and our own strength; but it is displeasing to Christ when we distrust any power derived from him or granted by him. There was also something in the malady which rendered the cure difficult. The extraordinary power of Satan must not discourage our faith, but quicken us to more earnestness in praying to God for the increase of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Howbeit this kind goeth not out but by prayer and fasting. Do we wonder to see Satan's bodily possession of this young man from a child, when we see his spiritual possession of every son of Adam from the fall! The stubborn kind yields only to disciplined dependence on God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And while they abode in Galilee, Jesus said unto them, The Son of man shall be betrayed into the hands of men. Mark is very precise here: they departed thence and passed through Galilee, and he would not that any man should know it. So this was not a preaching but a private journey through Galilee; indeed, his public ministry in Galilee was now all but concluded. Till the hour of his final farewell arrived, he was chiefly occupied with the Twelve, preparing them for the coming events. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. And they shall kill him, and the third day he shall be raised again. And they were exceeding sorry. Though the shock would not be so great as at the first announcement, their sorrow would not be the less, but probably the greater, the deeper the intelligence went down into their hearts, and a new wave dashing upon them by this repetition of the heavy tidings. Luke adds that they understood not this saying, and it was hid from them, and they were afraid to ask him — for the plainest statements, when they encounter long-continued and obstinate prejudices, are seen through a distorting and dulling medium. Christ perfectly knew all things that should befall him, yet undertook the work of our redemption, which strongly shows his love. What outward debasement and Divine glory was the life of the Redeemer! And all his humiliation ended in his exaltation. Let us learn to endure the cross, to despise riches and worldly honours, and to be content with his will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. And when they were come to Capernaum, they that received tribute money came to Peter, and said, Doth not your master pay tribute? The tribute money was the double drachma, equal to two Attic drachmas and corresponding to the Jewish half-shekel, payable towards the maintenance of the temple and its services by every male Jew of twenty years old and upward. Thus it was not a civil but an ecclesiastical tax; the tax mentioned in the next verse was a civil one, and the whole teaching of this remarkable scene depends upon this distinction. They came to Peter, at whose house Jesus probably resided while at Capernaum. The question seems to imply the payment was voluntary but expected — a voluntary assessment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. He saith, Yes. And when he was come into the house, Jesus prevented him, saying, What thinkest thou, Simon? of whom do the kings of the earth take custom or tribute? of their own children, or of strangers? Peter felt sure his Master was ready to do what was right. Christ spoke first to give him proof that no thought can be withholden from him. “Prevented” means anticipated, in the old sense of the word. “Custom” means custom on goods exported or imported; “tribute,” the poll-tax payable to the Romans by everyone whose name was in the census, strictly a civil tax. “Strangers” cannot mean foreigners, from whom sovereigns certainly do not raise taxes, but those not of their own family, that is, their subjects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Peter saith unto him, Of strangers. Jesus saith unto him, Then are the children free. By “the children” our Lord cannot mean himself and the Twelve together in some loose sense of their near relationship to God as their common Father, for he never once mixes himself up with his disciples in speaking of their relation to God, but ever studiously keeps his relation and theirs apart. He can refer here, then, only to himself: if the sovereign's own family be exempt, you know the inference in my case — this is a tax for upholding my Father's House; as his Son, that tax is not due by me. I AM FREE. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Notwithstanding, lest we should offend them, go thou to the sea, and cast an hook, and take up the fish that first cometh up; and when thou hast opened his mouth, thou shalt find a piece of money: that take, and give unto them for me and thee. We must never decline our duty for fear of giving offence; but we must sometimes deny ourselves in our worldly interests rather than give offence, lest they, ignorant of his relation to the Lord of the Temple, misconstrue a claim to exemption into indifference to his honour who dwells in it. However the money was lodged in the fish, He who knows all things alone could know it, and only almighty power could bring it to Peter's hook. The power and the poverty of Christ should be mentioned together. The coin was a stater, an Attic silver coin equal to two didrachms of half a shekel's value, and so the exact sum required for both. “For me and thee” — literally “instead of me and thee” — perhaps because the payment was a redemption of the person paid for; observe, our Lord does not say “for us,” but “for me and thee,” thus distinguishing the Exempted One and his non-exempted disciple. If called by providence to be poor, like our Lord, let us trust in his power, and our God shall supply all our need. In the way of obedience, in the course perhaps of our usual calling, as he helped Peter, so he will help us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a parallel question, not a conclusion)

Parallel. The second passion prediction, given while Jesus abode in Galilee, is told in Matthew and, in the note's terms, in Mark as well. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Difference. Mark is very precise: they departed thence and passed through Galilee, and Jesus would not that any man should know it, so this was a private journey, not a preaching tour. Matthew's “while they abode in Galilee” does not carry that detail. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
