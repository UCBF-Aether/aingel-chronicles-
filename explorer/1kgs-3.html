The Text — 1 Kings 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Solomon made affinity with Pharaoh king of Egypt, and took Pharaoh’s daughter, and brought her into the city of David, until he had made an end of building his own house, and the house of the LORD, and the wall of Jerusalem round about.

2 Only the people sacrificed in high places, because there was no house built unto the name of the LORD, until those days.

3 And Solomon loved the LORD, walking in the statutes of David his father: only he sacrificed and burnt incense in high places.

4 And the king went to Gibeon to sacrifice there; for that was the great high place: a thousand burnt offerings did Solomon offer upon that altar.

5 In Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night: and God said, Ask what I shall give thee.

6 And Solomon said, Thou hast shewed unto thy servant David my father great mercy, according as he walked before thee in truth, and in righteousness, and in uprightness of heart with thee; and thou hast kept for him this great kindness, that thou hast given him a son to sit on his throne, as it is this day.

7 And now, O LORD my God, thou hast made thy servant king instead of David my father: and I am but a little child: I know not how to go out or come in.

8 And thy servant is in the midst of thy people which thou hast chosen, a great people, that cannot be numbered nor counted for multitude.

9 Give therefore thy servant an understanding heart to judge thy people, that I may discern between good and bad: for who is able to judge this thy so great a people?

10 And the speech pleased the LORD, that Solomon had asked this thing.

11 And God said unto him, Because thou hast asked this thing, and hast not asked for thyself long life; neither hast asked riches for thyself, nor hast asked the life of thine enemies; but hast asked for thyself understanding to discern judgment;

12 Behold, I have done according to thy words: lo, I have given thee a wise and an understanding heart; so that there was none like thee before thee, neither after thee shall any arise like unto thee.

13 And I have also given thee that which thou hast not asked, both riches, and honour: so that there shall not be any among the kings like unto thee all thy days.

14 And if thou wilt walk in my ways, to keep my statutes and my commandments, as thy father David did walk, then I will lengthen thy days.

15 And Solomon awoke; and, behold, it was a dream. And he came to Jerusalem, and stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD, and offered up burnt offerings, and offered peace offerings, and made a feast to all his servants.

16 Then came there two women, that were harlots, unto the king, and stood before him.

17 And the one woman said, O my lord, I and this woman dwell in one house; and I was delivered of a child with her in the house.

18 And it came to pass the third day after that I was delivered, that this woman was delivered also: and we were together; there was no stranger with us in the house, save we two in the house.

19 And this woman’s child died in the night; because she overlaid it.

20 And she arose at midnight, and took my son from beside me, while thine handmaid slept, and laid it in her bosom, and laid her dead child in my bosom.

21 And when I rose in the morning to give my child suck, behold, it was dead: but when I had considered it in the morning, behold, it was not my son, which I did bear.

22 And the other woman said, Nay; but the living is my son, and the dead is thy son. And this said, No; but the dead is thy son, and the living is my son. Thus they spake before the king.

23 Then said the king, The one saith, This is my son that liveth, and thy son is the dead: and the other saith, Nay; but thy son is the dead, and my son is the living.

24 And the king said, Bring me a sword. And they brought a sword before the king.

25 And the king said, Divide the living child in two, and give half to the one, and half to the other.

26 Then spake the woman whose the living child was unto the king, for her bowels yearned upon her son, and she said, O my lord, give her the living child, and in no wise slay it. But the other said, Let it be neither mine nor thine, but divide it.

27 Then the king answered and said, Give her the living child, and in no wise slay it: she is the mother thereof.

28 And all Israel heard of the judgment which the king had judged; and they feared the king: for they saw that the wisdom of God was in him, to do judgment.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Solomon marries Pharaoh's daughter and worships at Gibeon, where God offers him anything and he asks for a wise and understanding heart to judge his people. God gives wisdom, riches and honour besides, and Solomon shows it at once in the judgment between the two mothers claiming one living child. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Solomon made affinity with Pharaoh and took his daughter, a political marriage Henry censures as a first step toward forbidden alliances, though the kingdom was at peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The people still sacrificed in high places because no house had yet been built for the Lord's name, a fault of the times that Solomon's temple would remove. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Solomon loved the Lord and walked in David's statutes, only sacrificing in high places, so his heart was right even where the worship was still irregular. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. He went to Gibeon, the great high place where the tabernacle stood, and offered a thousand burnt offerings, a princely sacrifice at the start of his reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. At Gibeon the Lord appeared to Solomon in a dream by night and said, Ask what I shall give thee, an offer that tests what a king truly wants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Solomon praised God's great mercy to David, who walked before him in truth, righteousness, and uprightness of heart, and to whom a son on the throne was given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He confessed himself but a little child, not knowing how to go out or come in, though king over a great people, a humility fitting a young ruler. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. His people were great and uncountable, chosen by God, so the burden of judging them rightly was greater than any one man could bear alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. He asked for an understanding heart to judge the people and discern good and bad, since without wisdom a king cannot govern God's people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The speech pleased the Lord, because Solomon asked for what serves God and his people rather than for himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. God answered that because he had not asked for long life, riches, or his enemies' lives, but for understanding to discern judgment, his prayer would be granted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. God gave him a wise and understanding heart, such as none before or after him had, a wisdom that was God's gift, not Solomon's achievement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. God also gave what he had not asked, riches and honour, so that no king in his days would be like him, showing that seeking God's kingdom first adds the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Long life was promised on condition that he walk in God's ways and keep his statutes as David did, so even a gift of grace carried a condition of obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Solomon awoke, and it was a dream, yet he acted on it at once, coming to Jerusalem and standing before the ark and offering peace offerings and a feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Two harlots then came to the king and stood before him, bringing a dispute no witness could settle, a fit first case for his new wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The first woman told how she and the other woman lived in one house and she bore a child, with no stranger present to witness what followed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Three days later the other woman also bore a child, and they were alone together in the house, so only the two mothers knew the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The other woman's child died in the night because she overlaid it, the sad accident at the root of the whole dispute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. One mother, having overlaid her own child, put the cheat upon the other by a night exchange, and the wronged mother appealed to public justice to be righted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. In the morning the first mother found the child dead, but considering it closely saw it was not the son she had borne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The other woman denied it, claiming the living child as hers and the dead as her rival's, so the case stood as oath against oath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The king restated both claims plainly, showing he had heard each side and would judge by something deeper than their words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. He commanded a sword to be brought, an order that looked like cruelty but was designed to draw out the truth from the mothers' hearts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. He ordered the living child divided in two, half to each woman, a sentence no true mother could accept and no false mother would fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The true mother's bowels yearned for her son, and she begged the king to give the child alive to the other woman rather than slay it, love surrendering its right to save the child's life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The king gave the living child to the woman who would rather lose him than see him killed, for she was the mother, as her compassion proved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The judgment made Solomon both feared and loved, for his people knew no unjust thing could pass undiscovered before him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
