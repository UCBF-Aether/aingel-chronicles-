Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 117 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 O praise the LORD, all ye nations: praise him, all ye people.

2 For his merciful kindness is great toward us: and the truth of the LORD endureth for ever. Praise ye the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The shortest psalm in the book, two verses, and universal in scope: all nations and all peoples are called to praise the LORD, because his merciful kindness is great toward us and the truth of the LORD endures for ever. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry calls this short psalm sweet, and full of gospel: Paul quotes it as proof the gospel would be preached to and entertained by Gentiles, for in Judah only was God known for ages while nations praised wood and stone — but Christ takes down the partition-wall, the afar off are made nigh, and even former enemies become willing subjects, called by gospel tidings, ordinances and grace to praise him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry gives praise its matter: mercy and truth shine brightest in the gospel — mercy strong and mighty for pardon of mighty sins and a mighty salvation, prevailing above Gentile deserts, and truth enduring for ever as the promise to the fathers takes effect in believing Gentiles, the spiritual seed of Abraham; mercy is the fountain of comforts, truth the foundation of hopes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
