The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 50 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 The mighty God, even the LORD, hath spoken, and called the earth from the rising of the sun unto the going down thereof.

2 Out of Zion, the perfection of beauty, God hath shined.

3 Our God shall come, and shall not keep silence: a fire shall devour before him, and it shall be very tempestuous round about him.

4 He shall call to the heavens from above, and to the earth, that he may judge his people.

5 Gather my saints together unto me; those that have made a covenant with me by sacrifice.

6 And the heavens shall declare his righteousness: for God is judge himself. Selah.

7 Hear, O my people, and I will speak; O Israel, and I will testify against thee: I am God, even thy God.

8 I will not reprove thee for thy sacrifices or thy burnt offerings, to have been continually before me.

9 I will take no bullock out of thy house, nor he goats out of thy folds.

10 For every beast of the forest is mine, and the cattle upon a thousand hills.

11 I know all the fowls of the mountains: and the wild beasts of the field are mine.

12 If I were hungry, I would not tell thee: for the world is mine, and the fulness thereof.

13 Will I eat the flesh of bulls, or drink the blood of goats?

14 Offer unto God thanksgiving; and pay thy vows unto the most High:

15 And call upon me in the day of trouble: I will deliver thee, and thou shalt glorify me.

16 But unto the wicked God saith, What hast thou to do to declare my statutes, or that thou shouldest take my covenant in thy mouth?

17 Seeing thou hatest instruction, and casteth my words behind thee.

18 When thou sawest a thief, then thou consentedst with him, and hast been partaker with adulterers.

19 Thou givest thy mouth to evil, and thy tongue frameth deceit.

20 Thou sittest and speakest against thy brother; thou slanderest thine own mother’s son.

21 These things hast thou done, and I kept silence; thou thoughtest that I was altogether such an one as thyself: but I will reprove thee, and set them in order before thine eyes.

22 Now consider this, ye that forget God, lest I tear you in pieces, and there be none to deliver.

23 Whoso offereth praise glorifieth me: and to him that ordereth his conversation aright will I shew the salvation of God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God the Judge summons heaven and earth: out of Zion, perfection of beauty, he shines forth in fire and tempest, not to reprove sacrifices, for bullocks and goats are already his on a thousand hills, but to ask for thanksgiving, paid vows, and calling in trouble. The wicked who hate instruction yet quote his covenant are reproved, and the psalm ends: whoever offers praise glorifies God. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The mighty God, even the Lord, has spoken, and called the earth from the rising of the sun to the going down thereof: the Mighty God summons the whole earth, east to west, as witnesses to his judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Out of Zion, the perfection of beauty, God has shined: from Zion, beauty's perfection, God shines forth — judgment comes as light. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Our God shall come, and shall not keep silence; a fire shall devour before him, and it shall be very tempestuous round about him: coming, not silence, with devouring fire and tempest — the Judge arrives in majesty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He shall call to the heavens from above, and to the earth, that he may judge his people: heaven and earth are called as witnesses for the judgment of his own people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Gather my saints together unto me, those that have made a covenant with me by sacrifice: saints are gathered as covenant-makers by sacrifice — judgment begins at God's own covenant people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And the heavens shall declare his righteousness, for God is judge himself: heaven declares the Judge's righteousness, for God himself is judge — no deputy, no appeal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Hear, O my people, and I will speak; O Israel, and I will testify against you; I am God, even your God: the testimony is against Israel itself, from God who is their God — relation makes the charge heavier. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. I will not reprove you for your sacrifices or burnt offerings, to have been continually before me: sacrifices are not the complaint — they were continually offered, and that is not the reproof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. I will take no bullock out of your house, nor he-goats out of your folds: God needs no bullock or goat from them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For every beast of the forest is mine, and the cattle upon a thousand hills: forest beasts and cattle on a thousand hills are already his — sacrifice adds nothing to his wealth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. I know all the fowls of the mountains, and the wild beasts of the field are mine: known fowls and owned wild beasts — God's knowledge and ownership cover all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. If I were hungry, I would not tell you, for the world is mine, and the fullness thereof: even hunger — which God cannot know — would not make him dependent, for world and fullness are his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Will I eat the flesh of bulls, or drink the blood of goats? The question exposes the absurdity of imagining God fed by sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Offer unto God thanksgiving, and pay your vows unto the Most High: thanksgiving and paid vows are the sacrifices God does desire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And call upon me in the day of trouble; I will deliver you, and you shall glorify me: calling in trouble, deliverance, and glorifying God form the true sacrifice's sequence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. But unto the wicked God says, What have you to do to declare my statutes, or that you should take my covenant in your mouth? The wicked's mouthing of statutes and covenant is challenged — profession without practice has no right to God's words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Seeing you hate instruction, and cast my words behind you: hatred of instruction and words cast behind the back explain the challenge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. When you saw a thief, then you consented with him, and have been partaker with adulterers: consent with thieves and partnership with adulterers are the wicked's company. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. You give your mouth to evil, and your tongue frames deceit: mouth given to evil, tongue framing deceit — speech is the workshop of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. You sit and speak against your brother; you slander your own mother's son: sitting slander against a brother, even a mother's son, is the wicked's household sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. These things you have done, and I kept silence; you thought that I was altogether such a one as yourself; but I will reprove you, and set them in order before your eyes: God's silence was misread as likeness to the sinner, but reproof will set sins in order before his eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Now consider this, you that forget God, lest I tear you in pieces, and there be none to deliver: forgetters of God are warned to consider, lest tearing come with no deliverer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Whoso offers praise glorifies me, and to him that orders his conversation aright will I show the salvation of God: praise glorifies God, and an ordered life is shown salvation — the psalm ends with the twofold true worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
