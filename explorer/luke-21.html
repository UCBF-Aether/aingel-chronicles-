The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 21 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he looked up, and saw the rich men casting their gifts into the treasury.

2 And he saw also a certain poor widow casting in thither two mites.

3 And he said, Of a truth I say unto you, that this poor widow hath cast in more than they all:

4 For all these have of their abundance cast in unto the offerings of God: but she of her penury hath cast in all the living that she had.

5 And as some spake of the temple, how it was adorned with goodly stones and gifts, he said,

6 As for these things which ye behold, the days will come, in the which there shall not be left one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down.

7 And they asked him, saying, Master, but when shall these things be? and what sign will there be when these things shall come to pass?

8 And he said, Take heed that ye be not deceived: for many shall come in my name, saying, I am Christ; and the time draweth near: go ye not therefore after them.

9 But when ye shall hear of wars and commotions, be not terrified: for these things must first come to pass; but the end is not by and by.

10 Then said he unto them, Nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom:

11 And great earthquakes shall be in divers places, and famines, and pestilences; and fearful sights and great signs shall there be from heaven.

12 But before all these, they shall lay their hands on you, and persecute you, delivering you up to the synagogues, and into prisons, being brought before kings and rulers for my name’s sake.

13 And it shall turn to you for a testimony.

14 Settle it therefore in your hearts, not to meditate before what ye shall answer:

15 For I will give you a mouth and wisdom, which all your adversaries shall not be able to gainsay nor resist.

16 And ye shall be betrayed both by parents, and brethren, and kinsfolks, and friends; and some of you shall they cause to be put to death.

17 And ye shall be hated of all men for my name’s sake.

18 But there shall not an hair of your head perish.

19 In your patience possess ye your souls.

20 And when ye shall see Jerusalem compassed with armies, then know that the desolation thereof is nigh.

21 Then let them which are in Judaea flee to the mountains; and let them which are in the midst of it depart out; and let not them that are in the countries enter thereinto.

22 For these be the days of vengeance, that all things which are written may be fulfilled.

23 But woe unto them that are with child, and to them that give suck, in those days! for there shall be great distress in the land, and wrath upon this people.

24 And they shall fall by the edge of the sword, and shall be led away captive into all nations: and Jerusalem shall be trodden down of the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled.

25 And there shall be signs in the sun, and in the moon, and in the stars; and upon the earth distress of nations, with perplexity; the sea and the waves roaring;

26 Men’s hearts failing them for fear, and for looking after those things which are coming on the earth: for the powers of heaven shall be shaken.

27 And then shall they see the Son of man coming in a cloud with power and great glory.

28 And when these things begin to come to pass, then look up, and lift up your heads; for your redemption draweth nigh.

29 And he spake to them a parable; Behold the fig tree, and all the trees;

30 When they now shoot forth, ye see and know of your own selves that summer is now nigh at hand.

31 So likewise ye, when ye see these things come to pass, know ye that the kingdom of God is nigh at hand.

32 Verily I say unto you, This generation shall not pass away, till all be fulfilled.

33 Heaven and earth shall pass away: but my words shall not pass away.

34 And take heed to yourselves, lest at any time your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting, and drunkenness, and cares of this life, and so that day come upon you unawares.

35 For as a snare shall it come on all them that dwell on the face of the whole earth.

36 Watch ye therefore, and pray always, that ye may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of man.

37 And in the day time he was teaching in the temple; and at night he went out, and abode in the mount that is called the mount of Olives.

38 And all the people came early in the morning to him in the temple, for to hear him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Watching rich men cast gifts into the treasury, Jesus praises a poor widow who casts in two mites, all her living (vv. 1–4). Asked about the temple’s stones, he foretells its destruction, false Christs, wars, persecution, Jerusalem compassed with armies, and the coming of the Son of man in a cloud with power and great glory (vv. 5–28). The fig tree’s budding teaches that the kingdom is near, and the chapter ends with the charge to watch and pray always, lest hearts be overcharged with surfeiting and the cares of this life (vv. 29–38). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 21

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He looked up, and saw the rich men casting their gifts into the treasury. The treasury scene is watched, not just passed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He saw also a certain poor widow casting in thither two mites. Two mites, the smallest gift, are seen alongside rich gifts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Of a truth, this poor widow has cast in more than they all. Henry notes what we rightly give for the poor's relief and God's worship is given unto God, and the Saviour sees with pleasure whatever we have in our hearts to give. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. For all these have of their abundance cast in unto God's offerings, but she of her penury has cast in all the living that she had. Henry prays the poorest servants, who have two mites, a soul and a body, may offer both unto him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. As some spoke of the temple, how it was adorned with goodly stones and gifts, he said. Admiration of the temple's adornment prompts its doom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. As for these things which ye behold, the days will come in which there shall not be left one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down. Goodly stones will be thrown down stone by stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They asked, Master, but when shall these things be? And what sign will there be when these things shall come to pass? Henry notes curiosity about timing, and Christ answers with clearness as far as necessary to teach duty, for all knowledge is desirable as far as it is in order to practice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Take heed that ye be not deceived, for many shall come in my name, saying, I am Christ, and the time draws near: go ye not therefore after them. Deception, not dating, is the first danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. When ye shall hear of wars and commotions, be not terrified, for these things must first come to pass, but the end is not by and by. Wars are necessary first things, not the end itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom. The prophecy widens from Jerusalem to nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Great earthquakes shall be in divers places, and famines, and pestilences, and fearful sights and great signs shall there be from heaven. Earth, hunger, disease, and heaven all give signs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. But before all these, they shall lay hands on you, and persecute you, delivering you to synagogues and prisons, being brought before kings and rulers for my name's sake. Henry notes Christ tells what hard things they should suffer for his name, encouraging them to bear up and go on in their work despite opposition. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And it shall turn to you for a testimony. Persecution becomes testimony, not merely suffering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Settle it therefore in your hearts, not to meditate before what ye shall answer. Defence is settled beforehand by not meditating beforehand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. For I will give you a mouth and wisdom, which all your adversaries shall not be able to gainsay nor resist. Henry notes God will stand by, own, and assist them, remarkably fulfilled after the Spirit's pouring out, giving wisdom and utterance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Ye shall be betrayed both by parents, brethren, kinsfolks, and friends, and some of you shall they cause to be put to death. Betrayal comes from the nearest bonds, and some betrayals end in death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Ye shall be hated of all men for my name's sake. Hatred is universalised, and its cause is his name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. But there shall not a hair of your head perish. Henry notes though we may be losers for Christ, we shall not, we cannot be losers by him in the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. In your patience possess ye your souls. Henry notes it is by Christian patience we keep possession of our souls, keeping out impressions that would put us out of temper, and securing our souls' safety in perilous times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. When ye shall see Jerusalem compassed with armies, then know that the desolation thereof is nigh. Armies around Jerusalem are the sign of desolation near, not far. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Then let them in Judaea flee to the mountains, and let them in the midst depart out, and let not them in the countries enter thereinto. Flight, departure, and non-entry are the three commands for the siege. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. For these be the days of vengeance, that all things written may be fulfilled. Vengeance days fulfil what was written. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. But woe unto them with child, and to them that give suck, in those days! For there shall be great distress in the land, and wrath upon this people. Mothers and infants bear the siege's woe most sharply. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. They shall fall by the edge of the sword, and be led captive into all nations, and Jerusalem shall be trodden down of the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled. Henry notes the destruction of Jerusalem and dispersion of the Jews would be a type of Christ's second coming, and the city is set as an example that sins will not pass unpunished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. There shall be signs in sun, moon, and stars, and upon earth distress of nations, with perplexity, the sea and waves roaring. Cosmic signs join earthly distress and perplexity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Men's hearts failing them for fear, and for looking after those things coming on the earth, for the powers of heaven shall be shaken. Fear fails hearts before the events themselves arrive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And then shall they see the Son of man coming in a cloud with power and great glory. The shaken powers give way to the Son of man coming in cloud, power, and glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. When these things begin to come to pass, then look up, and lift up your heads, for your redemption draws nigh. Henry notes when Christ came to destroy the Jews, he came to redeem persecuted Christians, and when he comes to judge, he will redeem all his from troubles; so beginning terrors are a signal to lift heads, not bow them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. He spoke a parable: Behold the fig tree, and all the trees. The fig tree is joined by all trees as the sign's picture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. When they now shoot forth, ye see and know of yourselves that summer is now nigh at hand. Shooting forth is self-evident summer, needing no interpreter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. So likewise ye, when ye see these things come to pass, know ye that the kingdom of God is nigh at hand. Signs seen are kingdom nearness known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Verily, this generation shall not pass away, till all be fulfilled. The generation hearing will live to see fulfilment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away. Henry notes though heaven and earth pass, Jesus' words shall not pass away, and scattered Jews preach Christianity's truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Take heed to yourselves, lest at any time your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting, drunkenness, and cares of this life, and so that day come upon you unawares. Henry cautions against being secure and sensual, overpowered by temptations or betrayed by corruptions, lest death and judgment come unprepared. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. For as a snare shall it come on all them that dwell on the face of the whole earth. The day comes as a snare on all earth-dwellers, not on a few. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Watch ye therefore, and pray always, that ye may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of man. Henry urges watching against sin, in every duty, making most of every opportunity to do good, and praying always, for those who live a life of prayer here shall be accounted worthy to live a life of praise hereafter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. In the daytime he was teaching in the temple, and at night he went out, and abode in the mount called Olivet. Days in the temple, nights on Olivet: the rhythm before the passion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. All the people came early in the morning to him in the temple, for to hear him. Early crowds close the chapter, listening before the arrest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
