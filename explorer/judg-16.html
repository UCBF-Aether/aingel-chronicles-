The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then went Samson to Gaza, and saw there an harlot, and went in unto her.

2 And it was told the Gazites, saying, Samson is come hither. And they compassed him in, and laid wait for him all night in the gate of the city, and were quiet all the night, saying, In the morning, when it is day, we shall kill him.

3 And Samson lay till midnight, and arose at midnight, and took the doors of the gate of the city, and the two posts, and went away with them, bar and all, and put them upon his shoulders, and carried them up to the top of an hill that is before Hebron.

4 And it came to pass afterward, that he loved a woman in the valley of Sorek, whose name was Delilah.

5 And the lords of the Philistines came up unto her, and said unto her, Entice him, and see wherein his great strength lieth, and by what means we may prevail against him, that we may bind him to afflict him; and we will give thee every one of us eleven hundred pieces of silver.

6 And Delilah said to Samson, Tell me, I pray thee, wherein thy great strength lieth, and wherewith thou mightest be bound to afflict thee.

7 And Samson said unto her, If they bind me with seven green withs that were never dried, then shall I be weak, and be as another man.

8 Then the lords of the Philistines brought up to her seven green withs which had not been dried, and she bound him with them.

9 Now there were men lying in wait, abiding with her in the chamber. And she said unto him, The Philistines be upon thee, Samson. And he brake the withs, as a thread of tow is broken when it toucheth the fire. So his strength was not known.

10 And Delilah said unto Samson, Behold, thou hast mocked me, and told me lies: now tell me, I pray thee, wherewith thou mightest be bound.

11 And he said unto her, If they bind me fast with new ropes that never were occupied, then shall I be weak, and be as another man.

12 Delilah therefore took new ropes, and bound him therewith, and said unto him, The Philistines be upon thee, Samson. And there were liers in wait abiding in the chamber. And he brake them from off his arms like a thread.

13 And Delilah said unto Samson, Hitherto thou hast mocked me, and told me lies: tell me wherewith thou mightest be bound. And he said unto her, If thou weavest the seven locks of my head with the web.

14 And she fastened it with the pin, and said unto him, The Philistines be upon thee, Samson. And he awaked out of his sleep, and went away with the pin of the beam, and with the web.

15 And she said unto him, How canst thou say, I love thee, when thine heart is not with me? thou hast mocked me these three times, and hast not told me wherein thy great strength lieth.

16 And it came to pass, when she pressed him daily with her words, and urged him, so that his soul was vexed unto death;

17 That he told her all his heart, and said unto her, There hath not come a razor upon mine head; for I have been a Nazarite unto God from my mother’s womb: if I be shaven, then my strength will go from me, and I shall become weak, and be like any other man.

18 And when Delilah saw that he had told her all his heart, she sent and called for the lords of the Philistines, saying, Come up this once, for he hath shewed me all his heart. Then the lords of the Philistines came up unto her, and brought money in their hand.

19 And she made him sleep upon her knees; and she called for a man, and she caused him to shave off the seven locks of his head; and she began to afflict him, and his strength went from him.

20 And she said, The Philistines be upon thee, Samson. And he awoke out of his sleep, and said, I will go out as at other times before, and shake myself. And he wist not that the LORD was departed from him.

21 But the Philistines took him, and put out his eyes, and brought him down to Gaza, and bound him with fetters of brass; and he did grind in the prison house.

22 Howbeit the hair of his head began to grow again after he was shaven.

23 Then the lords of the Philistines gathered them together for to offer a great sacrifice unto Dagon their god, and to rejoice: for they said, Our god hath delivered Samson our enemy into our hand.

24 And when the people saw him, they praised their god: for they said, Our god hath delivered into our hands our enemy, and the destroyer of our country, which slew many of us.

25 And it came to pass, when their hearts were merry, that they said, Call for Samson, that he may make us sport. And they called for Samson out of the prison house; and he made them sport: and they set him between the pillars.

26 And Samson said unto the lad that held him by the hand, Suffer me that I may feel the pillars whereupon the house standeth, that I may lean upon them.

27 Now the house was full of men and women; and all the lords of the Philistines were there; and there were upon the roof about three thousand men and women, that beheld while Samson made sport.

28 And Samson called unto the LORD, and said, O Lord God, remember me, I pray thee, and strengthen me, I pray thee, only this once, O God, that I may be at once avenged of the Philistines for my two eyes.

29 And Samson took hold of the two middle pillars upon which the house stood, and on which it was borne up, of the one with his right hand, and of the other with his left.

30 And Samson said, Let me die with the Philistines. And he bowed himself with all his might; and the house fell upon the lords, and upon all the people that were therein. So the dead which he slew at his death were more than they which he slew in his life.

31 Then his brethren and all the house of his father came down, and took him, and brought him up, and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the buryingplace of Manoah his father. And he judged Israel twenty years.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Samson goes to Gaza, is shut in at night, and at midnight carries the city gates, posts and bar to a hill before Hebron. In the valley of Sorek he loves Delilah, who, for eleven hundred pieces of silver from each Philistine lord, presses him daily until, vexed to death, he tells her his Nazarite secret: no razor has come on his head, and if shaven his strength will go. Shaven asleep on her knees, his eyes put out, he grinds in Gaza's prison until his hair grows again; called to make sport at Dagon's feast, he pulls down the two middle pillars, and the dead he slays at his death are more than those he slew in his life. He is buried between Zorah and Eshtaol, having judged Israel twenty years. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Samson went to Gaza, saw a harlot, and went in unto her, and Henry calls this a profanation of his honour as Israelite and Nazarite, noting his sin began in his eye, with which he should have made a covenant, and lust conceived brought forth sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The Gazites were told Samson had come, compassed him in, and laid wait all night in the gate, saying they would kill him in the morning, and Henry notes the gates were shut and guards set in quiet, so that Samson might suspect no danger, as sensual sleepers are surrounded by spiritual enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Samson lay till midnight, arose, and took the city gate's doors, posts, and bar upon his shoulders to a hill before Hebron, and Henry reads this as disdain for their attempt to secure him, a proof of God-given strength, and a type of Christ carrying away the gates of the grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Afterwards he loved a woman in the valley of Sorek named Delilah, and Henry notes that though more than once brought into mischief by love of women, he would not take warning, but was again taken in the same snare, and this third time paid for all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The Philistine lords offered Delilah eleven hundred pieces of silver each if she would entice Samson and learn where his strength lay and how he might be bound to be afflicted, and Henry notes they bid high, over a thousand pounds sterling in all, and that the love of money is the root of this horrid wickedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Delilah asked Samson to tell her where his great strength lay and wherewith he might be bound to afflict him, pretending, Henry notes, only to satisfy curiosity, as though it were impossible he should be bound except by her charms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Samson answered that if bound with seven green withs never dried he would be weak as another man, the first of his put-offs to keep his counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The lords brought seven green withs, and Delilah bound him with them, trying the first false secret in earnest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. With men lying in wait in the chamber, Delilah cried, The Philistines be upon thee, Samson, and he broke the withs as a thread of tow broken when it touches fire, so his strength was not known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Delilah complained he had mocked her and told lies, and pressed him again to tell wherewith he might be bound, continuing the importunity Henry calls restless and unreasonable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Samson answered that if bound fast with new ropes never occupied he would be weak as another man, his second evasion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Delilah took new ropes and bound him, again crying that the Philistines were upon him, with liers in wait in the chamber, and he broke them from his arms like a thread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Delilah again said he had mocked her and told lies, and demanded wherewith he might be bound, and Samson answered that if she wove the seven locks of his head with the web he would be weakened, an answer Henry notes came nearer the matter than any before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. She fastened his locks with the pin, cried that the Philistines were upon him, and he awoke and went away with the pin of the beam and the web, showing by the very strength of his hair that the secret lay there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Delilah then teased him, asking how he could say he loved her when his heart was not with her, since he had mocked her three times and not told his strength, and Henry notes the expostulation rests on a true principle, that those have our love who have our hearts, while her use of it was falsehood and flattery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. She pressed him daily with her words and urged him until his soul was vexed unto death, and Henry asks why he did not leave her, answering that he was captivated and intoxicated by lust falsely called love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. He told her all his heart: no razor had come upon his head, for he was a Nazarite from his mother's womb, and if shaven his strength would go and he would be like any other man, and Henry explains the hair was the badge of consecration and pledge of strength, since his strength was by promise, not by nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. When Delilah saw he had told her all his heart, she sent for the Philistine lords, who came up with the money in their hands, and Henry notes her care to secure the wages of unrighteousness before delivering him, as one of the bravest men alive was sold like a sheep for slaughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. She made him sleep upon her knees and had a man shave off the seven locks of his head, and she began to afflict him, and his strength went from him, and Henry notes Satan ruins men by rocking them asleep and flattering them into safety, then robbing them of strength and honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. She cried, The Philistines be upon thee, Samson, and he awoke saying he would go out as at other times and shake himself, but he wist not that the Lord was departed from him, and Henry warns that many have lost God's favourable presence and are not aware of it, imputing their languishing to anything but the right cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The Philistines took him, put out his eyes, brought him down to Gaza, bound him with brass fetters, and made him grind in the prison, and Henry notes his eyes were the inlets of his sin, and now his punishment began there, while Gaza, where he had shown strength, now saw him in weakness and jest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. However, the hair of his head began to grow again after he was shaven, and Henry reads this as the return of the sign of his Nazariteship and a token that God accepted him again as a Nazarite upon his repentance, without the restoration ceremonies he had no opportunity to perform. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The Philistine lords gathered to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon and rejoice, saying their god had delivered Samson into their hand, and Henry calls Dagon a dunghill-deity fit only to patronise the villainy by which Samson had actually been taken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. When the people saw Samson they praised their god, saying he had delivered their enemy, the destroyer of their country who slew many of them, and Henry notes they ascribed to Dagon what Delilah's paid treachery had done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. When their hearts were merry they called for Samson to make them sport, and they set him between the pillars, and Henry notes that having sacrificed, eaten, and drunk, they rose up to play, and Samson must be the fool in the play, laughed at as, being blind, he stumbled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Samson asked the lad who held his hand to let him feel the pillars on which the house stood, that he might lean upon them, feeling for the supports of the theatre in which he was exhibited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. The house was full of men and women, all the Philistine lords were there, and about three thousand on the roof beheld while Samson made sport, so that the whole Philistine leadership was gathered in one place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Samson called on the Lord to remember him and strengthen him only this once, that he might be avenged of the Philistines for his two eyes, a prayer Henry sets within his repentance, since God was reconciled to him as the growing hair had already shown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. He took hold of the two middle pillars on which the house stood, one with his right hand and the other with his left, the blind champion laying hold of the building's strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Samson said, Let me die with the Philistines, and bowed himself with all his might, and the house fell upon the lords and all the people in it, so that the dead he slew at his death were more than those he slew in his life, and Henry calls this his setting beam, a type of Christ conquering by death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. His brethren and all his father's house came down, took him, brought him up, and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in Manoah's buryingplace, and he had judged Israel twenty years, his evening, Henry notes, not commending his day, yet his death lessening, though not rolling away, its reproach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
