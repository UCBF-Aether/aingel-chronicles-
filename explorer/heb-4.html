The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hebrews 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Let us therefore fear, lest, a promise being left us of entering into his rest, any of you should seem to come short of it.

2 For unto us was the gospel preached, as well as unto them: but the word preached did not profit them, not being mixed with faith in them that heard it.

3 For we which have believed do enter into rest, as he said, As I have sworn in my wrath, if they shall enter into my rest: although the works were finished from the foundation of the world.

4 For he spake in a certain place of the seventh day on this wise, And God did rest the seventh day from all his works.

5 And in this place again, If they shall enter into my rest.

6 Seeing therefore it remaineth that some must enter therein, and they to whom it was first preached entered not in because of unbelief:

7 Again, he limiteth a certain day, saying in David, To day, after so long a time; as it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts.

8 For if Jesus had given them rest, then would he not afterward have spoken of another day.

9 There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God.

10 For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his.

11 Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest, lest any man fall after the same example of unbelief.

12 For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart.

13 Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do.

14 Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession.

15 For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.

16 Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The promise of rest still stands, the writer argues, since neither the wilderness generation nor Joshua's entry into Canaan exhausted it, and David still speaks of a today. God's word is living and sharp, laying every creature bare, and believers have a great high priest, tempted in every way yet without sin, so they may come boldly to the throne of grace for mercy and help. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Let us therefore fear, lest, a promise being left us of entering into his rest, any of you should seem to come short of it — partial neglect and a loose, wavering profession often cause men to seem to come short, so we must give diligence for a clear entrance into God's kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. For unto us was the gospel preached, as well as unto them: but the word preached did not profit them, not being mixed with faith in them that heard it — unbelief is at the root of all unfruitfulness under the word, for faith in the hearer is the life of the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. For we which have believed do enter into rest, as he said, As I have sworn in my wrath, if they shall enter into my rest: although the works were finished from the foundation of the world — believers enter the rest God prepared from creation, while unbelievers are shut out by his oath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. For he spake in a certain place of the seventh day on this wise, And God did rest the seventh day from all his works — as God finished his work and then rested, so he will cause those who believe to finish their work and then enjoy their rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. And in this place again, If they shall enter into my rest — the warning is repeated, because the rest still stands open and still may be forfeited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Seeing therefore it remaineth that some must enter therein, and they to whom it was first preached entered not in because of unbelief — the first hearers failed through unbelief, but the promise remains for others who will believe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Again, he limiteth a certain day, saying in David, To day, after so long a time; as it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts — long after the wilderness generation, God still says "To day," so the offer of rest is present, not past. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. For if Jesus [Joshua] had given them rest, then would he not afterward have spoken of another day — the rest Joshua led Israel into was not the final rest, or David would not still speak of another day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God — a more spiritual and excellent sabbath than the seventh day or Joshua's Canaan: a rest of grace, comfort, and holiness now, and a rest in glory hereafter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his — entering God's rest means ceasing from one's own works, as God ceased from his at creation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest, lest any man fall after the same example of unbelief — the end proposed is rest spiritual and eternal, and after due and diligent labour, sweet and satisfying rest shall follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart — when God sets his word home by his Spirit, it convinces, converts, and comforts powerfully, discovering men's thoughts, purposes, and sinful ends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do — the word shows the sinner all that is in his heart, and nothing is hidden from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession — Christ executed one part of his priesthood on earth in dying for us, and the other in heaven, pleading our cause; so hold fast the doctrines of faith in head, heart, lips, and life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin — it was needful that the Saviour have the fellow-feeling only a fellow-creature could have, with actual experience of sin's effects apart from its guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need — we come not dragged to a seat of justice but kindly invited to the mercy-seat, where grace reigns, to obtain mercy to pardon our sins and grace to purify our souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
