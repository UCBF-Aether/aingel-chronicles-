The Text — Didascalia 40 (J. M. Harden, The Ethiopic Didascalia (London: SPCK, 1920), public domain (Cornell University Library scan, archive.org cu31924096083336))

*CONCERNING THE PRAYER WHICH IS SAID OVER THE WATER*

1 WHEN the priest standeth over the water, let him pray thus, saying, O Lord our God, the

2 Almighty, Father of Thine only-begotten Son our Lord Jesus Christ, I give Thee thanks for that thou didst send Thine only-begotten Son, and He hath saved the

3 unto Him, and proclaimeth concerning the Kingdom of heaven, and remission of sins, and the resurrection of the dead. I glorify Thee, and give Thee thanks, and praise Thee that Thou didst patiently endure the sufferings of death on the cross for all; Who hast given us baptism, which is the new birth.

4 I glorify Thee, O God the Father, God alone, in the name of Jesus Christ, and the Holy Spirit that Thou the Doer of all wonders hast not rejected mankind; Who didst create Adam and set him in the garden, and when he transgressed Thy commandment, Thou didst put him forth from the garden by Thy righteous judgment, but in Thy manifold mercy Thou didst not cast him off, but didst send Thy Son in the latter days to become man, and to endure suffering without sin.

5 I pray to Thee and entreat Thee; look down from heaven upon this water, and sanctify it by Thy power, that all they who are baptized thereby according to Christ’s command may be together with Him partakers in His crucifixion, and His death, and His resurrection, and His ascension into heaven, that Thou mayest save them from the death of sin, and that they may live in righteousness. (vii., .] And after this let him baptize into the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, and anoint with the oil of balsam, and say :

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In plain language: this chapter gives the prayer said over the water at baptism. Standing over the water, the priest thanks God for sending His only-begotten Son to save mankind, recalls Adam created and set in the garden, put forth for transgression yet not rejected in God's manifold mercy, and then prays God to look down from heaven and sanctify the water, so that all baptized in it according to Christ's command may share in His crucifixion, death, resurrection and ascension, and be saved from the death of sin.
