The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 64 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Hear my voice, O God, in my prayer: preserve my life from fear of the enemy.

2 Hide me from the secret counsel of the wicked; from the insurrection of the workers of iniquity:

3 Who whet their tongue like a sword, and bend their bows to shoot their arrows, even bitter words:

4 That they may shoot in secret at the perfect: suddenly do they shoot at him, and fear not.

5 They encourage themselves in an evil matter: they commune of laying snares privily; they say, Who shall see them?

6 They search out iniquities; they accomplish a diligent search: both the inward thought of every one of them, and the heart, is deep.

7 But God shall shoot at them with an arrow; suddenly shall they be wounded.

8 So they shall make their own tongue to fall upon themselves: all that see them shall flee away.

9 And all men shall fear, and shall declare the work of God; for they shall wisely consider of his doing.

10 The righteous shall be glad in the LORD, and shall trust in him; and all the upright in heart shall glory.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Secret counsel answered by God's arrow: tongues whetted like swords shoot bitter words suddenly at the perfect in secret, snares laid with 'Who shall see them?', iniquities diligently searched out. But God shoots suddenly, their own tongue falls on themselves, all men fear and declare God's work, and the righteous, glad and trusting, shall glory. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hear my voice, O God, in my prayer; preserve my life from fear of the enemy: hearing is asked, and preservation from fear — not only from the enemy, but from fear of him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Hide me from the secret counsel of the wicked, from the insurrection of workers of iniquity: secret counsel and insurrection — hidden plotting and open rising — both need hiding from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Who whet their tongue like a sword, and bend their bows to shoot their arrows, even bitter words: tongue whetted as sword, bows bent for bitter words — speech is the weapon, bitter words the arrows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. That they may shoot in secret at the perfect; suddenly do they shoot at him, and fear not: secret shooting at the perfect, suddenly, without fear — the upright is ambushed, not confronted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. They encourage themselves in an evil matter; they commune of laying snares privily; they say, Who shall see them? Self-encouragement in evil, communing over secret snares, and 'Who shall see them?' — conspiracy comforts itself with invisibility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They search out iniquities; they accomplish a diligent search; both the inward thought of every one of them, and the heart, is deep: diligent searching out of iniquities, deep inward thought and heart — the plot is deep because the heart is deep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. But God shall shoot at them with an arrow; suddenly shall they be wounded: God's arrow answers theirs — suddenly wounded, as suddenly they shot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. So they shall make their own tongue to fall upon themselves; all who see them shall flee away: their own tongue falls on themselves, and onlookers flee — the weapon recoils, and the sight appals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And all men shall fear, and shall declare the work of God, for they shall wisely consider of his doing: universal fear, declaration of God's work, wise consideration of his doing — judgment teaches all men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The righteous shall be glad in the Lord, and shall trust in him, and all the upright in heart shall glory: gladness, trust, and glory for the righteous and upright in heart — the psalm ends in the joy of those the plotters shot at. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
