The Text — Psalms 1 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Blessed is the man that walketh not in the counsel of the ungodly, nor standeth in the way of sinners, nor sitteth in the seat of the scornful.

2 But his delight is in the law of the LORD; and in his law doth he meditate day and night.

3 And he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season; his leaf also shall not wither; and whatsoever he doeth shall prosper.

4 The ungodly are not so: but are like the chaff which the wind driveth away.

5 Therefore the ungodly shall not stand in the judgment, nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous.

6 For the LORD knoweth the way of the righteous: but the way of the ungodly shall perish.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Two ways of life are set side by side: the person who delights in God's law and meditates on it is like a well-watered tree that bears fruit in season, while the ungodly are like chaff blown away by the wind. The psalm ends with the two opposite ends: the Lord knows the way of the righteous, and the way of the ungodly perishes. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Blessedness begins negatively: the godly man refuses the counsel, the path, and finally the settled company of the ungodly, for sin deepens by degrees from listening to sitting down with scorners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Positively, his delight is in God's law, and he meditates on it constantly; Henry stresses that meditation is close, applied thinking, not a passing glance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Such a man is like a tree planted by water: rooted where grace supplies him, he bears fruit in season, his profession does not wither, and his lawful work prospers in soul-prosperity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The ungodly are the reverse: light, worthless chaff with no substance, easily driven by every wind of temptation and finally blown away by God's wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Therefore they cannot stand in judgment nor keep their place among the righteous; their exclusion is the consequence of the character described in verse 4. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The Lord knows — approves and watches over — the way of the righteous, while the way of the ungodly ends in ruin; the two ways, and their two ends, are the whole psalm in miniature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Chaff. The light, worthless husk with no substance, easily driven by wind, as the note on verse 4 pictures the ungodly. Interpreted Study note on verse 4
