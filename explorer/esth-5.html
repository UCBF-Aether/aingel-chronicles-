The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Esther 5 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–10; King James Version, Apocrypha (the Rest of Esther) for the Greek additions)

1 Now it came to pass on the third day, that Esther put on her royal apparel, and stood in the inner court of the king’s house, over against the king’s house: and the king sat upon his royal throne in the royal house, over against the gate of the house.

2 And it was so, when the king saw Esther the queen standing in the court, that she obtained favour in his sight: and the king held out to Esther the golden sceptre that was in his hand. So Esther drew near, and touched the top of the sceptre.

3 Then said the king unto her, What wilt thou, queen Esther? and what is thy request? it shall be even given thee to the half of the kingdom.

4 And Esther answered, If it seem good unto the king, let the king and Haman come this day unto the banquet that I have prepared for him.

5 Then the king said, Cause Haman to make haste, that he may do as Esther hath said. So the king and Haman came to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

6 And the king said unto Esther at the banquet of wine, What is thy petition? and it shall be granted thee: and what is thy request? even to the half of the kingdom it shall be performed.

7 Then answered Esther, and said, My petition and my request is;

8 If I have found favour in the sight of the king, and if it please the king to grant my petition, and to perform my request, let the king and Haman come to the banquet that I shall prepare for them, and I will do to morrow as the king hath said.

9 Then went Haman forth that day joyful and with a glad heart: but when Haman saw Mordecai in the king’s gate, that he stood not up, nor moved for him, he was full of indignation against Mordecai.

10 Nevertheless Haman refrained himself: and when he came home, he sent and called for his friends, and Zeresh his wife.

11 And Haman told them of the glory of his riches, and the multitude of his children, and all the things wherein the king had promoted him, and how he had advanced him above the princes and servants of the king.

12 Haman said moreover, Yea, Esther the queen did let no man come in with the king unto the banquet that she had prepared but myself; and to morrow am I invited unto her also with the king.

13 Yet all this availeth me nothing, so long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king’s gate.

14 Then said Zeresh his wife and all his friends unto him, Let a gallows be made of fifty cubits high, and to morrow speak thou unto the king that Mordecai may be hanged thereon: then go thou in merrily with the king unto the banquet. And the thing pleased Haman; and he caused the gallows to be made.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

On the third day of the fast Esther enters the inner court in royal apparel, the king holds out the golden sceptre, and she invites him and Haman to a banquet that day, then to a second banquet the next day, without yet naming her request. Haman leaves joyful but, stung by Mordecai's refusal to stand up or move for him at the king's gate, has a gallows fifty cubits high built for Mordecai on the advice of his wife Zeresh and his friends. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Now it came to pass on the third day, that Esther put on her royal apparel, and stood in the inner court of the king's house, over against the king's house: and the king sat upon his royal throne in the royal house, over against the gate of the house. Henry notes the third day ends the fast, and Esther comes in royal dress, not in sackcloth, to a king who must be won, not confronted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And when the king saw Esther standing in the court, she obtained favour in his sight: and the king held out to Esther the golden sceptre in his hand. So Esther drew near, and touched the top of the sceptre. JFB notes the sceptre decides the case: life, not death, and Esther's touch of it is submission to the king's grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Henry notes the king's offer is royal generosity, and also the measure of Esther's favour: half a kingdom is laid open before she asks anything, which makes her restraint in asking only for a banquet all the more deliberate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. And Esther answered, If it seem good unto the king, let the king and Haman come this day unto the banquet that I have prepared for him. JFB notes Esther does not ask at once: a banquet invitation buys time, and brings Haman face to face with the king under her roof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Then the king said, Cause Haman to make haste, that he may do as Esther has said. So the king and Haman came to the banquet that Esther had prepared. Henry notes Haman is hastened to his own undoing, summoned eagerly to a feast that will expose him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The offer is repeated over wine at the first banquet, pressing Esther to speak, yet she still defers her petition to a second banquet rather than asking at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Then answered Esther, and said, My petition and my request is. Esther pauses on the threshold of her petition, holding the king's attention for a second banquet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. If I have found favour in the sight of the king, and if it please the king to grant my petition, and to perform my request, let the king and Haman come to the banquet that I shall prepare for them, and I will do to morrow as the king has said. Henry notes the delay is wisdom, not fear: one more day lets Haman's pride ripen and the night's providence work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Then went Haman forth that day joyful and with a glad heart: but when Haman saw Mordecai in the king's gate, that he stood not up, nor moved for him, he was full of indignation against Mordecai. JFB notes the same gate that saw Mordecai's loyalty now sees his refusal, and Haman's feast-day joy curdles at one standing man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Nevertheless Haman refrained himself: and when he came home, he sent and called for his friends, and Zeresh his wife. Henry notes Haman restrains himself in public and unburdens himself at home, where flattery will feed what restraint starved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And Haman told them of the glory of his riches, and the multitude of his children, and all the things wherein the king had promoted him, and how he had advanced him above the princes and servants of the king. JFB notes the catalogue of blessings is recited to an audience that already knows it, because pride loves to hear itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Haman said moreover, Yea, Esther the queen did let no man come in with the king unto the banquet that she had prepared but myself; and to morrow am I invited unto her also with the king. Henry notes Haman counts the queen's banquet as his highest honour, not knowing it is his summons to judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Yet all this availeth me nothing, so long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king's gate. JFB notes the sentence exposes pride's arithmetic: an empire's honours weigh nothing against one man's refusal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Then said Zeresh his wife and all his friends unto him, Let a gallows be made of fifty cubits high, and to morrow speak thou unto the king that Mordecai may be hanged thereon: then go thou in merrily with the king unto the banquet. And the thing pleased Haman; and he caused the gallows to be made. Henry notes the gallows is built fifty cubits high to make the hanging public, and Haman goes to bed pleased with a plan that will hang himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Golden sceptre. As in chapter 4, the king's outstretched sceptre means life and favour; Esther draws near and touches the top of it. Documented Chapter text, verse 2
  • To the half of the kingdom. The king's offer to Esther, repeated at the banquet: whatever she asks, even to half the kingdom, shall be granted, which makes her restraint in asking only for a banquet deliberate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Gallows fifty cubits high. The structure Haman has built for Mordecai on his wife's advice, to which he means to speak to the king on the morrow. Documented Chapter text, verse 14
