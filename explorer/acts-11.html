The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the apostles and brethren that were in Judaea heard that the Gentiles had also received the word of God.

2 And when Peter was come up to Jerusalem, they that were of the circumcision contended with him,

3 Saying, Thou wentest in to men uncircumcised, and didst eat with them.

4 But Peter rehearsed the matter from the beginning, and expounded it by order unto them, saying,

5 I was in the city of Joppa praying: and in a trance I saw a vision, A certain vessel descend, as it had been a great sheet, let down from heaven by four corners; and it came even to me:

6 Upon the which when I had fastened mine eyes, I considered, and saw fourfooted beasts of the earth, and wild beasts, and creeping things, and fowls of the air.

7 And I heard a voice saying unto me, Arise, Peter; slay and eat.

8 But I said, Not so, Lord: for nothing common or unclean hath at any time entered into my mouth.

9 But the voice answered me again from heaven, What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common.

10 And this was done three times: and all were drawn up again into heaven.

11 And, behold, immediately there were three men already come unto the house where I was, sent from Caesarea unto me.

12 And the Spirit bade me go with them, nothing doubting. Moreover these six brethren accompanied me, and we entered into the man’s house:

13 And he shewed us how he had seen an angel in his house, which stood and said unto him, Send men to Joppa, and call for Simon, whose surname is Peter;

14 Who shall tell thee words, whereby thou and all thy house shall be saved.

15 And as I began to speak, the Holy Ghost fell on them, as on us at the beginning.

16 Then remembered I the word of the Lord, how that he said, John indeed baptized with water; but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost.

17 Forasmuch then as God gave them the like gift as he did unto us, who believed on the Lord Jesus Christ; what was I, that I could withstand God?

18 When they heard these things, they held their peace, and glorified God, saying, Then hath God also to the Gentiles granted repentance unto life.

19 Now they which were scattered abroad upon the persecution that arose about Stephen travelled as far as Phenice, and Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to none but unto the Jews only.

20 And some of them were men of Cyprus and Cyrene, which, when they were come to Antioch, spake unto the Grecians, preaching the LORD Jesus.

21 And the hand of the Lord was with them: and a great number believed, and turned unto the Lord.

22 Then tidings of these things came unto the ears of the church which was in Jerusalem: and they sent forth Barnabas, that he should go as far as Antioch.

23 Who, when he came, and had seen the grace of God, was glad, and exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord.

24 For he was a good man, and full of the Holy Ghost and of faith: and much people was added unto the Lord.

25 Then departed Barnabas to Tarsus, for to seek Saul:

26 And when he had found him, he brought him unto Antioch. And it came to pass, that a whole year they assembled themselves with the church, and taught much people. And the disciples were called Christians first in Antioch.

27 And in these days came prophets from Jerusalem unto Antioch.

28 And there stood up one of them named Agabus, and signified by the Spirit that there should be great dearth throughout all the world: which came to pass in the days of Claudius Caesar.

29 Then the disciples, every man according to his ability, determined to send relief unto the brethren which dwelt in Judaea:

30 Which also they did, and sent it to the elders by the hands of Barnabas and Saul.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Back in Jerusalem, Peter explains his visit to Cornelius step by step, and the objectors fall silent, glorifying God that repentance unto life has been granted to the Gentiles too. The scattered believers preach at Antioch, Barnabas and Saul build the church there, where disciples are first called Christians, and the Antioch church sends famine relief to Judea by their hands. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The apostles and brethren in Judaea heard that the Gentiles had also received the word of God. Henry sees the imperfect state of human nature in what follows: godly persons are displeased even to hear that the word of God has been received, because their own system has not been attended to. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. When Peter was come up to Jerusalem, they that were of the circumcision contended with him. Henry calls this shutting out the bane and damage of the church: to shut out those from it, and from the benefit of the means of grace, who are not in every thing as we are. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Saying, Thou wentest in to men uncircumcised, and didst eat with them. Henry's contention point is the same exclusion: the circumcision party contended because Peter ate with uncircumcised men, judging fellowship by their own system rather than by God's reception. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. But Peter rehearsed the matter from the beginning, and expounded it by order unto them. Henry commends the method: Peter stated the whole affair, and we should at all times bear with the infirmities of our brethren, and instead of taking offence or answering with warmth, explain our motives and show the nature of our proceedings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The Spirit bade me go with them, nothing doubting. Moreover these six brethren accompanied me, and we entered into the man's house. Henry's defence principle is that Peter explained his motives and showed the nature of his proceedings: he went because the Spirit bade him, nothing doubting, with six brethren as witnesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. As I began to speak, the Holy Ghost fell on them, as on us at the beginning. Henry's test for the preaching is this fall: that preaching is certainly right, with which the Holy Ghost is given, and the Gentiles' reception matches the beginning at Pentecost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Then remembered I the word of the Lord, how that he said, John indeed baptized with water, but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost. Henry's frame for the gift is remembrance of the Lord's word: the same baptism promised at the beginning has now been given to the Gentiles, and Peter recognizes the pattern. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Forasmuch then as God gave them the like gift as he did unto us, who believed on the Lord Jesus Christ; what was I, that I could withstand God? Henry warns the zealous directly: while men are very zealous for their own regulations, they should take care that they do not withstand God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. When they heard these things, they held their peace, and glorified God, saying, Then hath God also to the Gentiles granted repentance unto life. Henry says those who love the Lord will glorify him when made sure that he has given repentance to life to any fellow-sinners, for repentance is God's gift: not only his free grace accepts it, but his mighty grace works it in us, taking away the heart of stone and giving a heart of flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Now they which were scattered abroad upon the persecution that arose about Stephen travelled as far as Phenice, and Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to none but unto the Jews only. Henry's providence point opens the Antioch story: the first preachers at Antioch were dispersed from Jerusalem by persecution, and thus what was meant to hurt the church was made to work for its good, for the wrath of man is made to praise God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Some of them were men of Cyprus and Cyrene, which, when they were come to Antioch, spake unto the Grecians, preaching the Lord Jesus. Henry asks what ministers of Christ should preach but Christ, and him crucified, Christ, and him glorified, and notes their preaching was accompanied with divine power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The hand of the Lord was with them, and a great number believed, and turned unto the Lord. Henry describes the conversion: the hand of the Lord brought home to hearts and consciences what the preachers could but speak to the outward ear, and they believed, convinced of the truth of the gospel, turning from a careless, carnal way of living to a holy, heavenly, spiritual life, and from worship in show and ceremony to worship in the Spirit and in truth, until the Lord Jesus became all in all with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Who, when he came, and had seen the grace of God, was glad, and exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord. Henry says when sinners are thus brought to the Lord, really good men, who are full of faith and of the Holy Ghost, will admire and rejoice in the grace of God bestowed on them, and Barnabas's gladness and exhortation are that rejoicing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. For he was a good man, and full of the Holy Ghost and of faith: and much people was added unto the Lord. Henry describes Barnabas himself: he was full of faith, full of the grace of faith, and full of the fruits of the faith that works by love, and under such a man much people was added unto the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. When he had found him, he brought him unto Antioch. And it came to pass, that a whole year they assembled themselves with the church, and taught much people. And the disciples were called Christians first in Antioch. Henry explains the new name: hitherto the followers of Christ were called disciples, learners, scholars, but from that time they were called Christians, whose proper meaning is a follower of Christ, one who from serious thought embraces the religion of Christ, believes his promises, and makes it his chief care to shape his life by Christ's precepts and example, while multitudes take the name to whom it does not rightly belong, and the name without the reality will only add to guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Then the disciples, every man according to his ability, determined to send relief unto the brethren which dwelt in Judaea. Henry's fruit of true Christianity is this relief: true Christians will feel for their brethren under afflictions, and thus fruit is brought forth to the praise and glory of God, for if all mankind were true Christians, how cheerfully would they help one another, the whole earth like one large family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Which also they did, and sent it to the elders by the hands of Barnabas and Saul. Henry's family picture is fulfilled in the sending: they did what they determined, and the relief went by the hands of Barnabas and Saul, cheerful help from one part of the family of Christians to another under affliction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
