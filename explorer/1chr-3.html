The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now these were the sons of David, which were born unto him in Hebron; the firstborn Amnon, of Ahinoam the Jezreelitess; the second Daniel, of Abigail the Carmelitess:

2 The third, Absalom the son of Maachah the daughter of Talmai king of Geshur: the fourth, Adonijah the son of Haggith:

3 The fifth, Shephatiah of Abital: the sixth, Ithream by Eglah his wife.

4 These six were born unto him in Hebron; and there he reigned seven years and six months: and in Jerusalem he reigned thirty and three years.

5 And these were born unto him in Jerusalem; Shimea, and Shobab, and Nathan, and Solomon, four, of Bathshua the daughter of Ammiel:

6 Ibhar also, and Elishama, and Eliphelet,

7 And Nogah, and Nepheg, and Japhia,

8 And Elishama, and Eliada, and Eliphelet, nine.

9 These were all the sons of David, beside the sons of the concubines, and Tamar their sister.

10 And Solomon’s son was Rehoboam, Abia his son, Asa his son, Jehoshaphat his son,

11 Joram his son, Ahaziah his son, Joash his son,

12 Amaziah his son, Azariah his son, Jotham his son,

13 Ahaz his son, Hezekiah his son, Manasseh his son,

14 Amon his son, Josiah his son.

15 And the sons of Josiah were, the firstborn Johanan, the second Jehoiakim, the third Zedekiah, the fourth Shallum.

16 And the sons of Jehoiakim: Jeconiah his son, Zedekiah his son.

17 And the sons of Jeconiah; Assir, Salathiel his son,

18 Malchiram also, and Pedaiah, and Shenazar, Jecamiah, Hoshama, and Nedabiah.

19 And the sons of Pedaiah were, Zerubbabel, and Shimei: and the sons of Zerubbabel; Meshullam, and Hananiah, and Shelomith their sister:

20 And Hashubah, and Ohel, and Berechiah, and Hasadiah, Jushabhesed, five.

21 And the sons of Hananiah; Pelatiah, and Jesaiah: the sons of Rephaiah, the sons of Arnan, the sons of Obadiah, the sons of Shechaniah.

22 And the sons of Shechaniah; Shemaiah: and the sons of Shemaiah; Hattush, and Igeal, and Bariah, and Neariah, and Shaphat, six.

23 And the sons of Neariah; Elioenai, and Hezekiah, and Azrikam, three.

24 And the sons of Elioenai were, Hodaiah, and Eliashib, and Pelaiah, and Akkub, and Johanan, and Dalaiah, and Anani, seven.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter lists David's sons, those born at Hebron and those born at Jerusalem, then follows Solomon's line through the kings of Judah down to the captivity and on to Zerubbabel's descendants. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These were David's sons born in Hebron, and JFB stresses place and time of birth matter: the oldest son born after his father's accession was, by Eastern notions, the proper heir, which explains Amnon's ambition when a younger brother was placed above him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The sixth son in Hebron was Ithream by Eglah his wife. JFB supposes Eglah another name of Michal, who may have had a son before her mockery of David, and notes she is called wife because she was his proper wife, restored after being taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. These six were born in Hebron, where David reigned seven years and six months, and in Jerusalem thirty-three years. JFB's section on David's sons frames the Hebron list against the Jerusalem list that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Four sons were born in Jerusalem of Bath-shua, daughter of Ammiel, that is Bath-sheba, whose father is called Eliam elsewhere. JFB notes Solomon, though named last, was not her only son but is called so from the distinguished affection of which he was the object. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Elishama and Eliphelet appear twice among David's Jerusalem sons. JFB explains the sons of the same name were children of different mothers, distinguished by some title, or the first two died and their names were given to later sons to preserve their memories. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Elishama, Eliada, and Eliphelet make nine Jerusalem sons in this list, though Samuel counts eleven. JFB explains two are omitted, either for early deaths or because they left no issue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Solomon's line is drawn down to the captivity through a succession of good and bad but influential monarchs. JFB marvels that a crown passed father to son in lineal descent for seventeen reigns, the promised reward of David's piety, with vacillation only near the close. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Josiah's sons were Johanan, Jehoiakim, Zedekiah, and Shallum. JFB notes Zedekiah is called Josiah's son but is elsewhere his brother's son in effect, since Hebrew words of relationship are used with great latitude, and Shallum is generally supposed to be Jehoahaz. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The sons of Jeconiah begin with Assir, rather Jeconiah the prisoner or captive. JFB explains the record of his condition shows Salathiel was born during the Babylonian captivity, and that written childless means no son succeeding him on the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Malchiram, Pedaiah, Shenazar, and the rest follow in a text JFB suspects is dislocated after Jeconiah. He gives Davidson's rearrangement tracing the royal stem through Salathiel to Zerubbabel, the historian's object being to trace the line through Zerubbabel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The sons of Pedaiah were Zerubbabel and Shimei, and Zerubbabel's sons Meshullam, Hananiah, and their sister Shelomith. JFB's rearrangement places Zerubbabel as son of Salathiel, making him the link by which the royal line continues after the captivity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages

  • The study note for verse 8 connects this passage to Samuel's count of eleven sons of David born at Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a question, not a conclusion)

This section investigates a question; it does not conclude it. The study note for verse 8 observes that this list counts nine sons of David born at Jerusalem, while Samuel counts eleven.

Parallels. Both lists name David's Jerusalem sons. Differences. Two names present in Samuel's count are absent here; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown explains the omission by early deaths or by sons who left no issue.

Whether that explanation is the correct one is not established by the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
