The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 24 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And it came to pass after the death of Abraham, that the Lord blessed Isaac his son, and he arose from Hebron and went and dwelt at the Well of the Vision 1 in the first year of the third week of this jubilee, seven years.

2 And in the first year of the fourth week a famine began in the land, besides the first famine, which had been in the days of Abraham.

3 And Jacob sod lentil pottage, and Esau came from the field hungry.

4 And he said to Jacob his brother: "Give me of this red pottage." And Jacob said to him : " Sell to me thy [primogeniture, this] birthright and I will give thee bread, and also some of this lentil pottage." 4.

5 And Esau said in his heart : " I shall die ; of what profit to me is this birthright ?

6 " And he said to Jacob : " I give it to thee." 5.

7 And Jacob said : " Swear to me, this day," and he sware unto him.

8 And Jacob gave his brother Esau bread and pottage, and he ate till he was satisfied, and Esau despised his birthright ; for this reason was Esau's name called Edom, on account of the red pottage which Jacob gave him for his birthright.

9 And Jacob became the elder, and Esau was brought down from his dignity.

10 And the famine was over the land, and Isaac departed to go down into Egypt in the second year of this week, and went to the king of the Philistines to Gerar, unto Abimelech. And the Lord appeared unto him and said unto him : " Go not down into Egypt ; dwell in the land that I shall tell thee of, and sojourn in this land, and I shall be

11 with thee and bless thee.

12 For to thee and to thy seed shall I give all this land, and I shall establish My oath which I sware unto Abraham thy father, and I shall multiply thy seed as the stars of heaven, and shall give unto thy seed all this land.

13 And in thy seed will all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thy father obeyed My voice, and kept My charge and My commandments, and' My laws, and My ordinances, and My covenant ; and now obey My voice and dwell in this land."

14 And he dwelt in Gerar three weeks of years. 13.

15 And Abimelech charged concerning him, and concerning all that was his, saying : " Any man that shall touch him or aught that is his shall surely die." 14.

16 And Isaac waxed strong among the Philistines, and he got many possessions, oxen and sheep and camels and asses and a great household.

17 And he sowed in the land of the Philistines and brought in a hundred-fold, and Isaac became exceedingly great, and the Philistines envied him.

18 Now all the wells which the servants of Abraham had dug during the life of Abraham, the Philistines had stopped them after the death of Abraham, and filled them with earth.' 17.

19 And Abimelech said unto Isaac : " Go from us, for thou art much mightier than we " ; and Isaac departed thence in the first year of the seventh week, and sojourned in the valleys of Gerar.

20 And they digged again the wells of water which the servants of Abraham, his father, had digged, and which the Philistines had closed after the death of Abraham his father, and he called their names as Abraham his father had named them.

21 And the servants of Isaac dug a well in the valley, and found living water, and the shepherds of Gerar strove with the shepherds of Isaac, saying : " The water is ours " ; and Isaac called the name of the well " Perversity," because

22 they had been perverse with us.

23 And they dug a second well, and they strove for that also, and he called its name " Enmity."

24 And he arose from thence and they digged another well, and for that they strove not, and he called the name of it " Room," and Isaac said : " Now the Lord hath made room for us, and we have increased in the land." 21.

25 And he went up from thence to the Well of the Oath in the first year of the first week in the forty-fourth jubilee.

26 And the Lord appeared to him that night, on the new moon of the first month, and said unto him : "I am the God of Abraham thy father; fear not, for I am with thee, and shall bless thee and shall surely multiply thy seed as the sand of the earth, for the sake of Abraham my servant." 23.

27 And he built an altar there, which Abraham his father had first built, and he called upon the name of the Lord, and he offered sacrifice to the God of Abraham his father.

28 And they digged a well and they found living water.

29 And the servants of Isaac digged another well and did not find water, and they went and told Isaac that they had not found water, and Isaac said : " I have sworn this day to the Philistines and this thing hath been announced to us." 26.

30 And he called the name of that place the " Well of the Oath " ; for there he had sworn to Abimelech and Ahuzzath his friend and Phicol the prefect of his host.

31 And Isaac knew that day that under constraint he had sworn to them to make peace with them.

32 Isaac curses the Philistines.

33 And Isaac on that day cursed the Philistines and said : " Cursed be the Philistines unto the day of wrath and indignation from the midst of all nations ; may God make them a derision and a curse and an object of wrath and indignation in the hands of the sinners the Gentiles and in the hands of the Kittim.

34 And whoever escapeth the sword of the enemy and the Kittim, may the righteous nation root out in judgment from under heaven ; for they will be the enemies and foes of my children throughout their generations upon the earth.

35 And no remnant will be left to them,

36 Nor one that will be saved on the day of the

37 wrath of judgment ; For destruction and rooting out and expulsion from the earth is the whole seed of the Philistines (reserved), And there will no longer be left for these Caphtorim a name or a seed on the earth. For though he ascend unto heaven, Thence shall he be brought down, And though he make himself strong on earth, Thence shall he be dragged forth, And though he hide himself amongst the nations, Even from thence shall he be rooted out; And though he descend into Sheol, There also shall his condemnation be great, And there also he shall have no peace. And if he go into captivity,

38 By the hands of those that seek his life will they slay him on the way,

39 And neither name nor seed will be left to him on all the earth ;

40 For into eternal malediction will he depart." And thus is it written and engraved concerning him on the heavenly tables, to do unto him on the day of judgment, so that he may be rooted out of the earth.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: After Abraham's death Isaac is marked as heir, a second famine distinct from the famine in Abraham's days drives him from Canaan, and the chapter moves through Jacob's lentil-pottage bargain with the hungry Esau toward Rebekah's blessing. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. The blessing after Abraham's death marks Isaac as heir: JFB's reading of the Genesis parallel is a general statement of the prosperity attending the father's heir, and the move to the Well of the Vision opens that heirship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. In the first year of the fourth week a famine began in the land, besides the first famine, which had been in the days of Abraham. A second famine begins, distinct from the first famine in Abraham's days. JFB on Genesis 26:1 notes the pressure of famine in Canaan forced Isaac with family and flocks to migrate into the land of the Philistines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Jacob sod lentil pottage, and Esau came from the field hungry. JFB on Genesis 25:29 notes the pottage was made of lentils or small beans, common in Egypt and Syria, probably Egyptian beans procured as a dainty, very palatable, and to the weary hunter, faint with hunger, its odor irresistibly tempting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He said to Jacob his brother: 'Give me of this red pottage.' And Jacob said to him: 'Sell to me thy primogeniture, this birthright, and I will give thee bread, and also some of this lentil pottage.' JFB on Genesis 25:31 notes the birthright is the rights and privileges of the first-born, very important, the chief being that they were the family priests and had a double portion of the inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Esau said in his heart: 'I shall die; of what profit to me is this birthright?' JFB on Genesis 25:32 notes 'I am at the point to die' means running daily risk of life, and asks of what use the birthright will be to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Esau's spoken gift is only the first step: it still needs Jacob's oath in the next verse to seal the transfer before any bread changes hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. Jacob insists on a sworn oath the same day, turning a hungry bargain into a binding transfer before the pottage is even given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. Jacob gave his brother Esau bread and pottage, and he ate till he was satisfied, and Esau despised his birthright; for this reason was Esau's name called Edom, on account of the red pottage which Jacob gave him for his birthright. JFB on Genesis 25:32 notes he despised or cared little about it, in comparison with gratifying his appetite, throwing away his religious privileges for a trifle, and thence styled 'a profane person'; here the red pottage gives Esau the name Edom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The birth order itself is overturned here: Jacob stands as elder in right, Esau is lowered from his dignity, the chapter's reversal in one line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. The famine was over the land, and Isaac departed to go down into Egypt in the second year of this week, and went to the king of the Philistines to Gerar, unto Abimelech. And the Lord appeared unto him and said unto him: 'Go not down into Egypt; dwell in the land that I shall tell thee of, and sojourn in this land, and I shall be.' JFB on Genesis 26:1-2 notes Gerar lay on the great central road from Canaan into Egypt and was the usual stage for travelling caravans, so Isaac, arriving as a great emir with large flocks, had to seek the pastoral king's permission or a lease to pasture nearby; and this appearance was the first divine communication made to Isaac personally, after he had succeeded to his father's inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. 'With thee and bless thee.' The Lord will be with Isaac and bless him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. 'For to thee and to thy seed shall I give all this land, and I shall establish my oath which I sware unto Abraham thy father, and I shall multiply thy seed as the stars of heaven, and shall give unto thy seed all this land.' JFB on Genesis 26:2-5 notes this is a renewal, not a first grant: the covenant originated entirely in divine grace, was suspended on Abraham walking before God and being perfect, and Abraham having fully met that condition, Isaac is now assured it will progressively take effect, the oath sworn to Abraham making the assurance doubly sure; the first instalment is possession of Canaan, 'all these countries' from its many petty tribal subdivisions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. 'And in thy seed will all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thy father obeyed my voice, and kept my charge and my commandments, and my laws, and my ordinances, and my covenant; and now obey my voice and dwell in this land.' JFB on Genesis 26:2-5: the blessing to the nations reaches Isaac as an inherited assurance, not a reward for his own obedience, because Abraham had already met the covenant's condition through grace-enabled faith honoured with the strongest expression of divine approval; Isaac's own charge is simply to obey and dwell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The stay in Gerar lasts three weeks of years, twenty-one years of dwelling among the Philistines before the expulsion that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. Abimelech charged concerning him, and concerning all that was his, saying: 'Any man that shall touch him or aught that is his shall surely die.' Abimelech's charge protects Isaac and all that is his under pain of death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. Prosperity itself becomes the provocation: Isaac's wealth among the Philistines sets up Abimelech's expulsion order in the verses that follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. He sowed in the land of the Philistines and brought in a hundred-fold, and Isaac became exceedingly great, and the Philistines envied him. JFB on Genesis 26:12 notes during his sojourn he farmed a piece of land, which by God's blessing on his skill and industry was very productive, and by his plentiful returns he increased so rapidly in wealth and influence that the Philistines, afraid or envious of his prosperity, obliged him to leave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Now all the wells which the servants of Abraham had dug during the life of Abraham, the Philistines had stopped them after the death of Abraham, and filled them with earth. JFB on Genesis 26:15 notes the same base stratagem for annoying those against whom they have taken an umbrage is practiced still by choking the wells with sand or stones, or defiling them with putrid carcases; here the Philistines stop and earth-fill Abraham's wells after his death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Abimelech said unto Isaac: 'Go from us, for thou art much mightier than we'; and Isaac departed thence in the first year of the seventh week, and sojourned in the valleys of Gerar. Expelled for mightiness, Isaac departs in the first year of the seventh week and sojourns in the valleys of Gerar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. They digged again the wells of water which the servants of Abraham his father had digged, and which the Philistines had closed after the death of Abraham his father, and he called their names as Abraham his father had named them. JFB on Genesis 26:18-22 notes the naming of wells by Abraham, the hereditary right of his family, the change of names by the Philistines to obliterate traces of origin, the restoration of names by Isaac, and the contests between shepherds; here Isaac redigs Abraham's closed wells and restores Abraham's names. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The servants of Isaac dug a well in the valley, and found living water, and the shepherds of Gerar strove with the shepherds of Isaac, saying: 'The water is ours'; and Isaac called the name of the well 'Perversity,' because. A valley well yields living water, Gerar's shepherds claim 'The water is ours,' and Isaac names the well Perversity, because they had been perverse, completed by the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. 'They had been perverse with us.' The well is named Perversity because they had been perverse with Isaac. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. They dug a second well, and they strove for that also, and he called its name 'Enmity.' A second well brings strife again, and is named Enmity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. He arose from thence and they digged another well, and for that they strove not, and he called the name of it 'Room,' and Isaac said: 'Now the Lord hath made room for us, and we have increased in the land.' Matthew Henry on Genesis 26:22 notes Isaac cleaved to his peaceable principle, rather to fly than fight, preferring quietness to victory: those that follow peace, sooner or later, shall find peace; the two former wells show what the earth is, straitness and strife, while this well shows what heaven is, enlargement and peace, room enough there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Isaac returns to the Well of the Oath, the place of oath-taking, at the opening of the forty-fourth jubilee, closing the Gerar wanderings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. The Lord appeared to him that night, on the new moon of the first month, and said unto him: 'I am the God of Abraham thy father; fear not, for I am with thee, and shall bless thee and shall surely multiply thy seed as the sand of the earth, for the sake of Abraham my servant.' JFB on Genesis 26:24-25: 'Man's extremity is God's opportunity' - Isaac arrived at Beersheba disappointed, wearied and troubled by unceasing molestation, and that same night God delighted his drooping spirit with the comforts of grace, raising him above the fear of man; Jewish writers remark the altar he then builds is the only one he is recorded to have raised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. He built an altar there, which Abraham his father had first built, and he called upon the name of the Lord, and he offered sacrifice to the God of Abraham his father. Isaac rebuilds the altar Abraham first built there, calls on the Lord's name, and offers sacrifice to the God of Abraham his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. At the Well of the Oath the digging succeeds at once: living water where the Gerar wells brought strife, and where the next well finds none. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. The servants of Isaac digged another well and did not find water, and they went and told Isaac that they had not found water, and Isaac said: 'I have sworn this day to the Philistines and this thing hath been announced to us.' A second well finds no water, and Isaac links the report to the day's oath to the Philistines, announced to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. He called the name of that place the 'Well of the Oath'; for there he had sworn to Abimelech and Ahuzzath his friend and Phicol the prefect of his host. JFB on Genesis 26:26-33 notes the king and two of his courtiers paid a visit to Isaac's tent, an overture of peace fulfilling the promise, and Isaac, of pacific disposition, forgave, accepted their proposals, and treated them to the banquet by which a covenant's ratification was usually crowned; here the place is named Well of the Oath for the oath to Abimelech, Ahuzzath and Phicol. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Isaac himself names the pressure: the peace oath was sworn under constraint, not freely, and the chapter is careful to record that it was forced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. This unit is the heading for the curse that follows: Isaac curses the Philistines. It divides the oath at the Well of the Oath from the curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. The curse is dated, not vague: it runs unto the day of wrath, so the Philistines' judgment is reserved for a set day rather than spent at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. No remnant is left: the total verdict whose scope the following verses unfold, from heaven to Sheol to captivity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. 'Nor one that will be saved on the day of the.' Nor one saved on the day of the wrath of judgment, completed by the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. Even flight fails in the curse: captives are cut down on the road itself by those hunting their lives, no escape by captivity either. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. The sentence is engraved, not merely spoken: written on the heavenly tables for the day of judgment, so the rooting-out is decreed in advance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse . This unit is the heading for the admonition that follows: Rebekah admonishes Jacob not to marry a Canaanitish woman. It divides the curse of the Philistines from Rebekah's admonition in chapter 25. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse . This unit is the heading for the blessing that follows: Rebekah's blessing. It marks the blessing embedded in chapter 25. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

JFB on Genesis 26:18-22 notes the naming of wells by Abraham, the hereditary right of his family, the change of names by the Philistines to obliterate traces of origin, the restoration of names by Isaac, and the contests between shepherds; here Isaac redigs Abraham's closed wells and restores Abraham's names.   • Verse 6. Esau's spoken gift is only the first step: it still needs Jacob's oath in the next verse to seal the transfer before any bread changes hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. Jacob insists on a sworn oath the same day, turning a hungry bargain into a binding transfer before the pottage is even given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. The birth order itself is overturned here: Jacob stands as elder in right, Esau is lowered from his dignity, the chapter's reversal in one line. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. 'With thee and bless thee.' The Lord will be with Isaac and bless him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. The stay in Gerar lasts three weeks of years, twenty-one years of dwelling among the Philistines before the expulsion that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 16. Prosperity itself becomes the provocation: Isaac's wealth among the Philistines sets up Abimelech's expulsion order in the verses that follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

Expelled for mightiness, Isaac departs in the first year of the seventh week and sojourns in the valleys of Gerar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. A valley well yields living water, Gerar's shepherds claim 'The water is ours,' and Isaac names the well Perversity, because they had been perverse, completed by the next verse.   • Verse 28. At the Well of the Oath the digging succeeds at once: living water where the Gerar wells brought strife, and where the next well finds none. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 31. Isaac himself names the pressure: the peace oath was sworn under constraint, not freely, and the chapter is careful to record that it was forced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 24. He arose from thence and they digged another well, and for that they strove not, and he called the name of it 'Room,' and Isaac said: 'Now the Lord hath made room for us, and we have increased in the land.' Matthew Henry on Genesis 26:22 notes Isaac cleaved to his peaceable principle, rather to fly than fight, preferring quietness to victory: those that follow peace, sooner or later, shall find peace; the two former wells show what the earth is, straitness and strife, while this well shows what heaven is, enlargement and peace, room enough there.
