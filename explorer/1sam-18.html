The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, when he had made an end of speaking unto Saul, that the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul.

2 And Saul took him that day, and would let him go no more home to his father’s house.

3 Then Jonathan and David made a covenant, because he loved him as his own soul.

4 And Jonathan stripped himself of the robe that was upon him, and gave it to David, and his garments, even to his sword, and to his bow, and to his girdle.

5 And David went out whithersoever Saul sent him, and behaved himself wisely: and Saul set him over the men of war, and he was accepted in the sight of all the people, and also in the sight of Saul’s servants.

6 And it came to pass as they came, when David was returned from the slaughter of the Philistine, that the women came out of all cities of Israel, singing and dancing, to meet king Saul, with tabrets, with joy, and with instruments of musick.

7 And the women answered one another as they played, and said, Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands.

8 And Saul was very wroth, and the saying displeased him; and he said, They have ascribed unto David ten thousands, and to me they have ascribed but thousands: and what can he have more but the kingdom?

9 And Saul eyed David from that day and forward.

10 And it came to pass on the morrow, that the evil spirit from God came upon Saul, and he prophesied in the midst of the house: and David played with his hand, as at other times: and there was a javelin in Saul’s hand.

11 And Saul cast the javelin; for he said, I will smite David even to the wall with it. And David avoided out of his presence twice.

12 And Saul was afraid of David, because the LORD was with him, and was departed from Saul.

13 Therefore Saul removed him from him, and made him his captain over a thousand; and he went out and came in before the people.

14 And David behaved himself wisely in all his ways; and the LORD was with him.

15 Wherefore when Saul saw that he behaved himself very wisely, he was afraid of him.

16 But all Israel and Judah loved David, because he went out and came in before them.

17 And Saul said to David, Behold my elder daughter Merab, her will I give thee to wife: only be thou valiant for me, and fight the LORD’s battles. For Saul said, Let not mine hand be upon him, but let the hand of the Philistines be upon him.

18 And David said unto Saul, Who am I? and what is my life, or my father’s family in Israel, that I should be son in law to the king?

19 But it came to pass at the time when Merab Saul’s daughter should have been given to David, that she was given unto Adriel the Meholathite to wife.

20 And Michal Saul’s daughter loved David: and they told Saul, and the thing pleased him.

21 And Saul said, I will give him her, that she may be a snare to him, and that the hand of the Philistines may be against him. Wherefore Saul said to David, Thou shalt this day be my son in law in the one of the twain.

22 And Saul commanded his servants, saying, Commune with David secretly, and say, Behold, the king hath delight in thee, and all his servants love thee: now therefore be the king’s son in law.

23 And Saul’s servants spake those words in the ears of David. And David said, Seemeth it to you a light thing to be a king’s son in law, seeing that I am a poor man, and lightly esteemed?

24 And the servants of Saul told him, saying, On this manner spake David.

25 And Saul said, Thus shall ye say to David, The king desireth not any dowry, but an hundred foreskins of the Philistines, to be avenged of the king’s enemies. But Saul thought to make David fall by the hand of the Philistines.

26 And when his servants told David these words, it pleased David well to be the king’s son in law: and the days were not expired.

27 Wherefore David arose and went, he and his men, and slew of the Philistines two hundred men; and David brought their foreskins, and they gave them in full tale to the king, that he might be the king’s son in law. And Saul gave him Michal his daughter to wife.

28 And Saul saw and knew that the LORD was with David, and that Michal Saul’s daughter loved him.

29 And Saul was yet the more afraid of David; and Saul became David’s enemy continually.

30 Then the princes of the Philistines went forth: and it came to pass, after they went forth, that David behaved himself more wisely than all the servants of Saul; so that his name was much set by.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

When David has made an end of speaking to Saul, the soul of Jonathan is knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loves him as his own soul; they make a covenant, and Jonathan strips himself of robe, garments, sword, bow and girdle and gives them to David. David goes out wherever Saul sends him and behaves wisely, is set over the men of war, and is accepted by all the people, until the women coming out to meet the returning army sing, Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands, and Saul eyes David from that day forward. The evil spirit comes on Saul, David plays as at other times, and Saul casts his javelin twice, saying, I will smite David even to the wall; David avoids him, is removed to captain over a thousand, and grows only more loved and more feared. Saul lays traps with marriage, first Merab, given instead to Adriel, then Michal, who loves David, for a dowry of a hundred Philistine foreskins meant to make David fall by Philistine hands; David brings two hundred, marries Michal, and Saul, seeing the LORD is with David, becomes his enemy continually, while David behaves more wisely than all Saul's servants and his name is much set by. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When David had made an end of speaking unto Saul, the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul. The friendship of David and Jonathan was the effect of Divine grace, Henry notes, which produces in true believers one heart and one soul; this union of souls comes from partaking in the Spirit of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Saul took David that day and would let him go no more home to his father's house. The victor was kept at court, in the king's sight and service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Then Jonathan and David made a covenant, because he loved him as his own soul. Where God unites hearts, Henry observes, carnal matters are too weak to separate them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Jonathan stripped himself of his robe and gave it to David, with his garments, his sword, his bow, and his girdle. The heir gave the shepherd his own arms and robes, a pledge of the covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. David went out whithersoever Saul sent him and behaved himself wisely, and Saul set him over the men of war, and he was accepted by all the people and by Saul's servants. It was a great proof of God's grace in David, Henry says, that he bore all this respect and honour without being lifted up above measure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. As they came, when David returned from the slaughter of the Philistine, the women came out of all Israel's cities singing and dancing to meet King Saul, with tabrets, joy, and instruments of music. Triumph brought a public song. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The women answered one another as they played, Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands. The song's arithmetic set the servant above the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Saul was very wroth, and the saying displeased him, and he said, They have ascribed to David ten thousands, and to me but thousands; and what can he have more but the kingdom? David's troubles, Henry notes, not only immediately followed his triumphs but arose from them — such is the vanity of what seems greatest in this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Saul eyed David from that day and forward. Envy fixed its eye on the young captain and never looked away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. On the morrow the evil spirit from God came upon Saul, and he prophesied in the midst of the house, while David played as at other times, and there was a javelin in Saul's hand. Compare David with his harp, aiming to serve Saul, and Saul with his javelin, aiming to slay David, Henry says, and observe the sweetness of God's persecuted people and the barbarity of their persecutors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Saul cast the javelin, saying, I will smite David even to the wall, and David avoided out of his presence twice. It is a sign the Spirit of God is departed from men, Henry observes, if, like Saul, they are peevish, envious, suspicious, and ill-natured; but David's safety must be ascribed to God's providence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Saul was afraid of David, because the LORD was with him and was departed from Saul. The very presence that had once drawn Saul to David now terrified him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Therefore Saul removed him from himself and made him captain over a thousand, and he went out and came in before the people. Exile from the court became a wider command in the field. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. David behaved himself wisely in all his ways, and the LORD was with him. For a long time David was kept in continual apprehension of falling by Saul's hand, Henry notes, yet he persevered in meek and respectful behaviour toward his persecutor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. When Saul saw that he behaved himself very wisely, he was afraid of him. Wisdom itself became a fresh cause of fear to the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. But all Israel and Judah loved David, because he went out and came in before them. The people's love grew as the king's fear grew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Saul said to David, Behold my elder daughter Merab, her will I give thee to wife; only be thou valiant for me and fight the LORD's battles. For Saul said, Let not mine hand be upon him, but let the hand of the Philistines be upon him. The offer of marriage was a trap baited with honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. David said, Who am I, and what is my life or my father's family in Israel, that I should be son-in-law to the king? If David magnified the honour of being son-in-law to King Saul, Henry asks, how should we magnify the honour of being sons to the King of kings? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. But at the time Merab should have been given to David, she was given to Adriel the Meholathite to wife. The promised bride was given to another, and the trap was reset. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Michal Saul's daughter loved David, and they told Saul, and the thing pleased him. A daughter's love became the king's new instrument. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Saul said, I will give him her, that she may be a snare to him, and that the hand of the Philistines may be against him; wherefore he said, Thou shalt this day be my son-in-law in the one of the twain. The snare was laid openly in Saul's own counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Saul commanded his servants to commune secretly with David, saying, Behold, the king hath delight in thee, and all his servants love thee; now therefore be the king's son-in-law. Flattery was sent ahead of the demand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Saul's servants spoke those words to David, and David said, Seemeth it to you a light thing to be a king's son-in-law, seeing I am a poor man and lightly esteemed? David's humility counted the honour too great for a poor shepherd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. The servants told Saul what David had spoken. The humble answer was carried back to the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Saul said, Thus shall ye say to David, The king desireth not any dowry, but an hundred foreskins of the Philistines, to be avenged of the king's enemies. But Saul thought to make David fall by the hand of the Philistines. The dowry was a death sentence disguised as a bride-price. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. When his servants told David these words, it pleased David well to be the king's son-in-law, and the days were not expired. David accepted the perilous terms without delay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. David arose and went, he and his men, and slew two hundred Philistines, and brought their foreskins in full tale to the king, that he might be the king's son-in-law, and Saul gave him Michal his daughter to wife. The demanded hundred was doubled, and the trap returned its maker empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Saul saw and knew that the LORD was with David, and that Michal his daughter loved him. The evidence was now undeniable, even to Saul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. Saul was yet the more afraid of David, and Saul became David's enemy continually. Knowledge of God's presence with David hardened, rather than softened, the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. Then the princes of the Philistines went forth, and after they went forth, David behaved himself more wisely than all Saul's servants, so that his name was much set by. How uncommon is such prudence under insults and provocations, Henry asks; opposition in others will not excuse wrong tempers in us, but should increase our care in the duties of our station. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
