Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Book of the Covenant 2 (J. Cooper & A. J. Maclean, The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1902), public domain (archive.org cu31924029296170))

1 If a fornicatress, or brothel keeper, or a drunkard, or a maker of idols,’ or a painter, or one engaged in shows, or a charioteer, or a wrestler,’ or one who goeth to the contest, or a combatant [in the games], or one who teacheth wrestling,” or a public huntsman, or a priest of idols, or a keeper of them, be [among those that come], let him not be received.

2 If any such desire to become faithful, let him cease from these [things]; and being in deed faithful, and being baptized,’ let him be received and let him partake. And if he do not cease, let him be rejected.

3 If any one be a teacher of boys in worldly wisdom, it is well if he cease. But if he have no other craft” by which to live, let him be excused.

4 If any one be a soldier or in authority, let him be taught not to oppress or to kill or to rob, or to be angry or to rage and afflict any one. But let those rations suffice him which are given to him. But if they wish to be baptized in the Lord, let them cease from military service or from the [post of] authority, and if not let them not be received.

5 Let a catechumen or a believer of the people, if he desire to be a soldier, either cease from his intention,‘ or if not let him be rejected. For he hath despised God by his thought, and leaving the things of the Spirit, he hath perfected himself in the flesh, and hath treated the faith with contempt.

6 If a fornicatress or a dissolute man or a drunkard do not [these things], and desire, believing, to become catechumens,’ they may [be admitted]. And if they make progress, let them be baptized ; but if not let them be rejected.

7 If a concubine of a man& be a servant, and desire to be faithful, if she educate those who are born [of her] and she separate from her master, or be joined’ to him alone in marriage, let her hear; and being baptized let her partake in the Offering, but if not let her be rejected.

8 He who doeth things which may not be spoken of,” or a diviner or a magician or a necromancer, these are defiled and do not come to judgment.

9 Let a charmer,’ or an astrologer, or an interpreter of dreams, or a sorcerer, or one who gathereth together the people, or a star-gazer, or a diviner by idols, either cease, and when he ceaseth let him be exorcised and baptized ; or if not let him be rejected.

10 If a man have a concubine, let him divorce her and marry in the law and hear the word of instruction.”

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Certain comers are not to be received at all while they continue in their callings: a fornicatress, brothel keeper, drunkard, maker of idols, painter, one engaged in shows, charioteer, wrestler, one who goes to the contest, combatant in the games, teacher of wrestling, public huntsman, or priest or keeper of idols. If any such desire to become faithful, they are to cease from these things, and, being in deed faithful and baptized, are to be received and partake; if they do not cease, they are to be rejected. A teacher of boys in worldly wisdom does well if he ceases, but if he has no other craft by which to live, he is to be excused. A soldier or a man in authority is to be taught not to oppress, kill, rob, be angry, rage or afflict anyone, and to let the rations given him suffice; but if they wish to be baptized in the Lord, they are to cease from military service or from the post of authority, and if not, they are not to be received. A catechumen or believer of the people who desires to be a soldier is either to cease from his intention or be rejected, for he has despised God by his thought, leaving the things of the Spirit and perfecting himself in the flesh, treating the faith with contempt. If a fornicatress, dissolute man or drunkard do not continue in these things and desire, believing, to become catechumens, they may be admitted, and if they make progress, be baptized, but if not, rejected. If a concubine of a man who is a servant desires to be faithful, and she educates the children born of her and separates from her master, or is joined to him alone in marriage, she is to hear, and being baptized, partake in the Offering; if not, she is to be rejected. He who does things which may not be spoken of, or a diviner, magician or necromancer, are defiled and do not come to judgment. A charmer, astrologer, interpreter of dreams, sorcerer, one who gathers together the people, star-gazer, or diviner by idols is either to cease — and when he ceases, to be exorcised and baptized — or to be rejected. If a man has a concubine, he is to divorce her, marry in the law, and hear the word of instruction.
