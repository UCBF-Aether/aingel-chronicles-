The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then all Israel gathered themselves to David unto Hebron, saying, Behold, we are thy bone and thy flesh.

2 And moreover in time past, even when Saul was king, thou wast he that leddest out and broughtest in Israel: and the LORD thy God said unto thee, Thou shalt feed my people Israel, and thou shalt be ruler over my people Israel.

3 Therefore came all the elders of Israel to the king to Hebron; and David made a covenant with them in Hebron before the LORD; and they anointed David king over Israel, according to the word of the LORD by Samuel.

4 And David and all Israel went to Jerusalem, which is Jebus; where the Jebusites were, the inhabitants of the land.

5 And the inhabitants of Jebus said to David, Thou shalt not come hither. Nevertheless David took the castle of Zion, which is the city of David.

6 And David said, Whosoever smiteth the Jebusites first shall be chief and captain. So Joab the son of Zeruiah went first up, and was chief.

7 And David dwelt in the castle; therefore they called it the city of David.

8 And he built the city round about, even from Millo round about: and Joab repaired the rest of the city.

9 So David waxed greater and greater: for the LORD of hosts was with him.

10 These also are the chief of the mighty men whom David had, who strengthened themselves with him in his kingdom, and with all Israel, to make him king, according to the word of the LORD concerning Israel.

11 And this is the number of the mighty men whom David had; Jashobeam, an Hachmonite, the chief of the captains: he lifted up his spear against three hundred slain by him at one time.

12 And after him was Eleazar the son of Dodo, the Ahohite, who was one of the three mighties.

13 He was with David at Pasdammim, and there the Philistines were gathered together to battle, where was a parcel of ground full of barley; and the people fled from before the Philistines.

14 And they set themselves in the midst of that parcel, and delivered it, and slew the Philistines; and the LORD saved them by a great deliverance.

15 Now three of the thirty captains went down to the rock to David, into the cave of Adullam; and the host of the Philistines encamped in the valley of Rephaim.

16 And David was then in the hold, and the Philistines’ garrison was then at Bethlehem.

17 And David longed, and said, Oh that one would give me drink of the water of the well of Bethlehem, that is at the gate!

18 And the three brake through the host of the Philistines, and drew water out of the well of Bethlehem, that was by the gate, and took it, and brought it to David: but David would not drink of it, but poured it out to the LORD.

19 And said, My God forbid it me, that I should do this thing: shall I drink the blood of these men that have put their lives in jeopardy? for with the jeopardy of their lives they brought it. Therefore he would not drink it. These things did these three mightiest.

20 And Abishai the brother of Joab, he was chief of the three: for lifting up his spear against three hundred, he slew them, and had a name among the three.

21 Of the three, he was more honourable than the two; for he was their captain: howbeit he attained not to the first three.

22 Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, the son of a valiant man of Kabzeel, who had done many acts; he slew two lionlike men of Moab: also he went down and slew a lion in a pit in a snowy day.

23 And he slew an Egyptian, a man of great stature, five cubits high; and in the Egyptian’s hand was a spear like a weaver’s beam; and he went down to him with a staff, and plucked the spear out of the Egyptian’s hand, and slew him with his own spear.

24 These things did Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, and had the name among the three mighties.

25 Behold, he was honourable among the thirty, but attained not to the first three: and David set him over his guard.

26 Also the valiant men of the armies were, Asahel the brother of Joab, Elhanan the son of Dodo of Bethlehem,

27 Shammoth the Harorite, Helez the Pelonite,

28 Ira the son of Ikkesh the Tekoite, Abiezer the Antothite,

29 Sibbecai the Hushathite, Ilai the Ahohite,

30 Maharai the Netophathite, Heled the son of Baanah the Netophathite,

31 Ithai the son of Ribai of Gibeah, that pertained to the children of Benjamin, Benaiah the Pirathonite,

32 Hurai of the brooks of Gaash, Abiel the Arbathite,

33 Azmaveth the Baharumite, Eliahba the Shaalbonite,

34 The sons of Hashem the Gizonite, Jonathan the son of Shage the Hararite,

35 Ahiam the son of Sacar the Hararite, Eliphal the son of Ur,

36 Hepher the Mecherathite, Ahijah the Pelonite,

37 Hezro the Carmelite, Naarai the son of Ezbai,

38 Joel the brother of Nathan, Mibhar the son of Haggeri,

39 Zelek the Ammonite, Naharai the Berothite, the armourbearer of Joab the son of Zeruiah,

40 Ira the Ithrite, Gareb the Ithrite,

41 Uriah the Hittite, Zabad the son of Ahlai,

42 Adina the son of Shiza the Reubenite, a captain of the Reubenites, and thirty with him,

43 Hanan the son of Maachah, and Joshaphat the Mithnite,

44 Uzzia the Ashterathite, Shama and Jehiel the sons of Hothan the Aroerite,

45 Jediael the son of Shimri, and Joha his brother, the Tizite,

46 Eliel the Mahavite, and Jeribai, and Joshaviah, the sons of Elnaam, and Ithmah the Moabite,

47 Eliel, and Obed, and Jasiel the Mesobaite.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells David's crowning at Hebron, his capture of Jerusalem from the Jebusites, and then lists his mighty men, including the three who broke through the Philistines to fetch him water from Bethlehem's well. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. All Israel gathered to David at Hebron, saying, we are thy bone and flesh. Henry notes David came to the throne after seven years over Judah only, in consideration of his kinship, former services, and divine designation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Even when Saul was king, David led Israel out and in, and the Lord said he should feed and rule Israel. Henry makes this divine designation the chief ground of his anointing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The elders came to Hebron, David made a covenant with them before the Lord, and they anointed him king according to Samuel's word. Henry stresses the original contract between prince and people: even David took a coronation oath to rule by law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. David and all Israel went to Jerusalem, which is Jebus, where the Jebusites lived. Henry notes Zion was held by Jebusites till David's time, and taking it was one of his first exploits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Jebus's inhabitants said, thou shalt not come hither, yet David took the castle of Zion, the city of David. Henry says opposition quickened David's resolution: defiance made him resolve to force it whatever it cost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. David promised chief and captain to whoever smote the Jebusites first, so Joab son of Zeruiah went first up and was chief. Henry notes prospect of preferment fired Joab: take honour from a soldier's eye and you cut the spurs from his heels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. David dwelt in the castle, therefore they called it the city of David. Henry links this to the psalm: God set his king upon his holy hill of Zion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He built the city round about, from Millo round about, and Joab repaired the rest. Henry treats the building as David settling his royal city after taking its stronghold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. David waxed greater and greater, for the Lord of hosts was with him. Henry insists David ascribed his increase not to his hosts but to the Lord of hosts, whose presence is all in all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. These are the chief mighty men who strengthened themselves with David in his kingdom, with all Israel, to make him king according to the Lord's word. Henry notes in strengthening David they strengthened themselves, since his advancement was theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Jashobeam the Hachmonite, chief of the captains, lifted his spear against three hundred slain at one time. Henry cites this as an exploit possible only by God's extraordinary presence, as one man chasing a thousand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. After him was Eleazar son of Dodo the Ahohite, one of the three mighties. Henry counts Eleazar among the first three whose deeds made David's catalogue honourable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. He was with David at Pas-dammim, where Philistines gathered to battle at a barley field, and the people fled. Henry sets this flight as the foil to the stand that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. They set themselves in the midst of the parcel, delivered it, slew the Philistines, and the Lord saved them by a great deliverance. Henry attributes the deliverance to God working by men and means, inclining hearts and giving victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Three of the thirty captains went down to the rock to David, into Adullam's cave, while Philistines encamped in Rephaim. Henry introduces the Bethlehem water exploit as David's own great act in this catalogue. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. David was then in the hold, and the Philistines' garrison was then at Bethlehem. Henry notes the longing that follows arose while David was hemmed in and Bethlehem held by the enemy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. David longed and said, oh that one would give me drink of Bethlehem's well water at the gate. Henry reads this as an unadvised wish, whose retraction by repentance became David's honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The three broke through the Philistine host, drew water from Bethlehem's well by the gate, and brought it to David, but he poured it out to the Lord. Henry praises his devotion: water too precious for his own drinking he gave God as a drink offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. David said, God forbid I drink the blood of men who jeopardised their lives, for with jeopardy of their lives they brought it, so he would not drink it. Henry adds tenderness to his servants: in his account the water had turned to blood, and great men must not be prodigal of their servants' blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Abishai brother of Joab was chief of the three, lifting his spear against three hundred and slaying them, gaining a name among the three. Henry pairs this 300 with Jashobeam's as proof of God's presence with the heroes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Of the three he was more honourable than the two and was their captain, yet he attained not to the first three. Henry keeps the ranking exact: honour among the three, but not equality with the first three. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Benaiah son of Jehoiada of Kabzeel did many acts: he slew two lionlike men of Moab, and slew a lion in a pit on a snowy day. Henry cites the lionlike men as another deed requiring God's extraordinary presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. He slew an Egyptian of great stature, five cubits high, whose spear was like a weaver's beam, going down with a staff, plucking the spear from the Egyptian's hand, and slaying him with it. Henry counts the giant-slaying among the wonderful achievements of the heroes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. These things did Benaiah, and he had a name among the three mighties. Henry closes Benaiah's exploits as earning a name, though not the first three's rank. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. He was honourable among the thirty but attained not to the first three, and David set him over his guard. Henry notes preferment followed service: the guard's command rewarded proven valour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. Zelek the Ammonite is named among the valiant men. Henry notes an Ammonite among David's worthies, though the law barred Ammonites from the congregation, likely dispensed with for hearty service, as a sign the Son of David would have worthies among the Gentiles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. Eliel the Mahavite, Jeribai, Joshaviah sons of Elnaam, and Ithmah the Moabite are named. Henry likewise notes a Moabite among the worthies, pointing to Gentiles gathered to David's Son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Millo. A place named in verse 8 in the building of Jerusalem round about. The study note does not explain the term further, so no fuller gloss is asserted here. Unresolved Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
