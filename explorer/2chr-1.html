The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Solomon the son of David was strengthened in his kingdom, and the LORD his God was with him, and magnified him exceedingly.

2 Then Solomon spake unto all Israel, to the captains of thousands and of hundreds, and to the judges, and to every governor in all Israel, the chief of the fathers.

3 So Solomon, and all the congregation with him, went to the high place that was at Gibeon; for there was the tabernacle of the congregation of God, which Moses the servant of the LORD had made in the wilderness.

4 But the ark of God had David brought up from Kirjathjearim to the place which David had prepared for it: for he had pitched a tent for it at Jerusalem.

5 Moreover the brasen altar, that Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, had made, he put before the tabernacle of the LORD: and Solomon and the congregation sought unto it.

6 And Solomon went up thither to the brasen altar before the LORD, which was at the tabernacle of the congregation, and offered a thousand burnt offerings upon it.

7 In that night did God appear unto Solomon, and said unto him, Ask what I shall give thee.

8 And Solomon said unto God, Thou hast shewed great mercy unto David my father, and hast made me to reign in his stead.

9 Now, O LORD God, let thy promise unto David my father be established: for thou hast made me king over a people like the dust of the earth in multitude.

10 Give me now wisdom and knowledge, that I may go out and come in before this people: for who can judge this thy people, that is so great?

11 And God said to Solomon, Because this was in thine heart, and thou hast not asked riches, wealth, or honour, nor the life of thine enemies, neither yet hast asked long life; but hast asked wisdom and knowledge for thyself, that thou mayest judge my people, over whom I have made thee king:

12 Wisdom and knowledge is granted unto thee; and I will give thee riches, and wealth, and honour, such as none of the kings have had that have been before thee, neither shall there any after thee have the like.

13 Then Solomon came from his journey to the high place that was at Gibeon to Jerusalem, from before the tabernacle of the congregation, and reigned over Israel.

14 And Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen: and he had a thousand and four hundred chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen, which he placed in the chariot cities, and with the king at Jerusalem.

15 And the king made silver and gold at Jerusalem as plenteous as stones, and cedar trees made he as the sycomore trees that are in the vale for abundance.

16 And Solomon had horses brought out of Egypt, and linen yarn: the king’s merchants received the linen yarn at a price.

17 And they fetched up, and brought forth out of Egypt a chariot for six hundred shekels of silver, and an horse for an hundred and fifty: and so brought they out horses for all the kings of the Hittites, and for the kings of Syria, by their means.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Solomon's strengthening in his kingdom, his great sacrifice at Gibeon, God's gift of wisdom in answer to his prayer, and the wealth, horses, and chariots that marked his reign. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Solomon was strengthened in his kingdom because the Lord his God was with him and magnified him exceedingly. Henry notes that though his title was contested, God's presence strengthened his heart, his hands and his interest in the people, and God's presence will be our strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Solomon spoke to all Israel, to the captains, judges, governors and chief of the fathers, summoning them to worship with him. Henry notes that authority is well bestowed when used for God's glory, and that magistrates are likely to do well when they take God along with them at their setting out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Solomon and all the congregation went to the high place at Gibeon, where the tabernacle of the congregation was. Henry notes Solomon began his reign with this public, pious visit to God's altar, a very good omen, and that it is desirable to have many join in solemn worship, the more the better. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The ark of God, however, David had brought up to Jerusalem, to the tent he had prepared for it. Henry notes the division: David kept mostly to the ark at Jerusalem, while Solomon honoured the tabernacle and its altars, and neither man's zeal should be censured for differing in its instance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The bronze altar Bezaleel had made stood before the tabernacle of the Lord at Gibeon. Henry notes Solomon brought his sacrifices thither because it is the altar that sanctifies every gift, and JFB adds that this was the legally appointed spot, on the time-honoured altar made in the wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Solomon went up to the bronze altar and offered a thousand burnt offerings upon it. Henry notes the abundance: where God sows plentifully he expects to reap accordingly, and Solomon gave God his dues out of the flocks and herds David had left him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. In that night God appeared to Solomon and said, Ask what I shall give thee. Henry notes God both put him in the way of receiving and tried him: men's characters appear in their choices, and asking what we would have tries a man as much as asking what he would do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Solomon answered by recalling God's great mercy to David his father, and that God had made him reign in his stead. Henry notes the plea: God who put him in David's place could in faith be asked for grace to do its duty, and the eminence of those before us should quicken prayer for wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Solomon prayed that the promise to David his father be established, since he was made king over a people like the dust of the earth in multitude. Henry notes God's promises are our best pleas in prayer: wisdom was not itself promised, but was necessary for building the house and establishing the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Give me now wisdom and knowledge, that I may go out and come in before this people, Solomon prayed, for who can judge so great a people? Henry notes he chose spiritual blessings rather than temporal ones, owning wisdom a desirable gift and God its giver, to be sought for the work God calls us to. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. God answered that because this was in Solomon's heart, and he had not asked for riches, wealth, honour or the life of his enemies, his request would be granted. Henry notes wisdom is given freely to those who value it and resolve to use it, and God upbraids not petitioners with their folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Wisdom and knowledge were granted, and with them riches, wealth and honour such as no king before or after had. Henry notes God gave the wealth and honour Solomon did not ask for precisely because he asked not for them: those who make the other world their end gain this world as well, in good measure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Then Solomon came from Gibeon to Jerusalem, from before the tabernacle, and reigned over Israel. Henry's concise summary frames the chapter's second movement: having honoured God by sacrifice and prayer, Solomon now honoured Israel by increasing its strength, wealth and trade. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Solomon gathered fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, stationed in chariot cities and with the king at Jerusalem. JFB notes his passion for horses exceeded any Israelite monarch's, a prohibited indulgence whether for luxury or power, and that the establishment served trade as well as show. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The king made silver and gold at Jerusalem as plentiful as stones, and cedar trees as the sycamore trees of the valley. Henry's concise point covers the whole passage: Solomon's choice of wisdom was blessed with strength and wealth, and those who refer themselves to God's providence have the most comfort in what they have. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Solomon had horses brought out of Egypt, and linen yarn, the king's merchants receiving the yarn at a price. JFB notes the Egyptian breed was highly valued, as fine as the Arabian but larger and more powerful, and that Solomon perceived the commercial advantage of controlling this trade through Judea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. A chariot was brought up out of Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver, and a horse for a hundred and fifty, and so for the kings of the Hittites and of Syria. JFB notes the chariot cost four times a horse, and that Solomon's agents bought in Egyptian markets and sold onward, establishing a monopoly through his chariot cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
