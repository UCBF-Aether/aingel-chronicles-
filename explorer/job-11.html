The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then answered Zophar the Naamathite, and said,

2 Should not the multitude of words be answered? and should a man full of talk be justified?

3 Should thy lies make men hold their peace? and when thou mockest, shall no man make thee ashamed?

4 For thou hast said, My doctrine is pure, and I am clean in thine eyes.

5 But oh that God would speak, and open his lips against thee;

6 And that he would shew thee the secrets of wisdom, that they are double to that which is! Know therefore that God exacteth of thee less than thine iniquity deserveth.

7 Canst thou by searching find out God? canst thou find out the Almighty unto perfection?

8 It is as high as heaven; what canst thou do? deeper than hell; what canst thou know?

9 The measure thereof is longer than the earth, and broader than the sea.

10 If he cut off, and shut up, or gather together, then who can hinder him?

11 For he knoweth vain men: he seeth wickedness also; will he not then consider it?

12 For vain men would be wise, though man be born like a wild ass’s colt.

13 If thou prepare thine heart, and stretch out thine hands toward him;

14 If iniquity be in thine hand, put it far away, and let not wickedness dwell in thy tabernacles.

15 For then shalt thou lift up thy face without spot; yea, thou shalt be stedfast, and shalt not fear:

16 Because thou shalt forget thy misery, and remember it as waters that pass away:

17 And thine age shall be clearer than the noonday: thou shalt shine forth, thou shalt be as the morning.

18 And thou shalt be secure, because there is hope; yea, thou shalt dig about thee, and thou shalt take thy rest in safety.

19 Also thou shalt lie down, and none shall make thee afraid; yea, many shall make suit unto thee.

20 But the eyes of the wicked shall fail, and they shall not escape, and their hope shall be as the giving up of the ghost.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Zophar, the third friend, answers harshly: Job’s multitude of words must be answered, and a man full of talk is not thereby justified. God’s wisdom, he says, is higher than heaven and deeper than hell, longer than the earth and broader than the sea — and God exacts less than Job’s iniquity deserves. If Job prepares his heart, puts iniquity far away, and stretches his hands toward God, his face will lift without spot and his age will be clearer than noonday. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Zophar the Naamathite answers, opening the first and harshest speech of the third friend. His attack falls on Job's words as much as on his sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Should not the multitude of words be answered, and should a man full of talk be justified? JFB notes Zophar assails Job's empty words and indirectly the two friends' weak replies, since taciturnity is highly prized among Orientals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Should Job's lies make men hold their peace, and when he mocks shall no man make him ashamed? JFB renders lies as vain boasting, and mocking as upbraiding God by complaints. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Job has said his doctrine is pure and he is clean in God's eyes. JFB notes doctrine is purposely used of speeches that sounded like lessons, and that Job maintained his sincerity against suspicion, not faultlessness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Oh that God would speak and open his lips against Job! Zophar wishes for a divine verdict to settle the dispute his own words cannot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. God would show Job the secrets of wisdom, that they are double to what is, and that God exacts less than Job's iniquity deserves. JFB renders it as God's ways, if their secret wisdom were shown, vastly exceeding men's wisdom, and exacting as consigning much guilt to oblivion in Job's favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Can Job by searching find out God, or find out the Almighty unto perfection? JFB renders it as penetrating to the perfections of the Almighty, which no searching reaches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. God's wisdom is as high as heaven — what can Job do? Deeper than hell — what can he know? JFB notes the forcible abruptness: the heights of heaven, what can you do to attain them with your gaze? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The measure of God's wisdom is longer than the earth and broader than the sea. No line Job drops can sound it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. If God cuts off, shuts up, or gathers together, who can hinder him? JFB renders cut off as passing over like a storm, shut up as imprisoning for trial, and gather together as holding a judicial assembly to pass sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. God knows vain men, and sees wickedness and will consider it. JFB notes God's wisdom detects sin where man's eye cannot reach, seeing wickedness man does not perceive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Vain man would be wise, though man be born like a wild ass's colt. JFB explains vain as hollow, and the wild ass's colt as a proverb for untamed wildness: man wishes to appear wisely obedient, but is unsubdued in spirit from birth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. If Job prepares his heart and stretches out his hands toward God, the way to restoration opens. JFB notes the apodosis comes at verse 15, and preparation of heart is obtained by stretching out hands in prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. If iniquity is in Job's hand, let him put it far away, and let not wickedness dwell in his tabernacles. JFB renders it as putting far away the iniquity in his hand, as Zacchaeus did, before the then of verse 15 follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Then Job shall lift up his face without spot, be steadfast, and not fear. JFB notes Zophar answers Job's own words, that he would not lift up his head: if he follows this advice he may lift up his face, steadfast like metals made firm by fusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Job shall forget his misery, remembering it as waters that pass away. JFB compares the dried stream: as its threatened waves are forgotten when it runs dry, so misery will be remembered only as passed water. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Job's age shall be clearer than noonday, and he shall shine forth, with darkness as the morning. JFB explains the age as his days of life, and the noonday as his former prosperity at its height, with any new darkness reduced to mere morning twilight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Job shall be secure because there is hope, and shall dig about him and take his rest in safety. JFB notes experience will teach hope in every trial, and dig may mean digging wells, the chief necessity in the East. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Job shall lie down and none shall make him afraid, and many shall make suit unto him. JFB notes the oriental images of prosperity, and making suit as literally stroking his face or caressing him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. But the eyes of the wicked shall fail, they shall not escape, and their hope shall be as the giving up of the ghost. JFB calls this a warning if Job will not turn: the obdurate look in vain for relief, every refuge vanishes, and hope leaves them as breath leaves the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
