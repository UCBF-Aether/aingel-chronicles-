The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Esther 1 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–10; King James Version, Apocrypha (the Rest of Esther) for the Greek additions)

1 Now it came to pass in the days of Ahasuerus, (this is Ahasuerus which reigned, from India even unto Ethiopia, over an hundred and seven and twenty provinces:)

2 That in those days, when the king Ahasuerus sat on the throne of his kingdom, which was in Shushan the palace,

3 In the third year of his reign, he made a feast unto all his princes and his servants; the power of Persia and Media, the nobles and princes of the provinces, being before him:

4 When he shewed the riches of his glorious kingdom and the honour of his excellent majesty many days, even an hundred and fourscore days.

5 And when these days were expired, the king made a feast unto all the people that were present in Shushan the palace, both unto great and small, seven days, in the court of the garden of the king’s palace;

6 Where were white, green, and blue, hangings, fastened with cords of fine linen and purple to silver rings and pillars of marble: the beds were of gold and silver, upon a pavement of red, and blue, and white, and black, marble.

7 And they gave them drink in vessels of gold, (the vessels being diverse one from another,) and royal wine in abundance, according to the state of the king.

8 And the drinking was according to the law; none did compel: for so the king had appointed to all the officers of his house, that they should do according to every man’s pleasure.

9 Also Vashti the queen made a feast for the women in the royal house which belonged to king Ahasuerus.

10 On the seventh day, when the heart of the king was merry with wine, he commanded Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, and Abagtha, Zethar, and Carcas, the seven chamberlains that served in the presence of Ahasuerus the king,

11 To bring Vashti the queen before the king with the crown royal, to shew the people and the princes her beauty: for she was fair to look on.

12 But the queen Vashti refused to come at the king’s commandment by his chamberlains: therefore was the king very wroth, and his anger burned in him.

13 Then the king said to the wise men, which knew the times, (for so was the king’s manner toward all that knew law and judgment:

14 And the next unto him was Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena, and Memucan, the seven princes of Persia and Media, which saw the king’s face, and which sat the first in the kingdom;)

15 What shall we do unto the queen Vashti according to law, because she hath not performed the commandment of the king Ahasuerus by the chamberlains?

16 And Memucan answered before the king and the princes, Vashti the queen hath not done wrong to the king only, but also to all the princes, and to all the people that are in all the provinces of the king Ahasuerus.

17 For this deed of the queen shall come abroad unto all women, so that they shall despise their husbands in their eyes, when it shall be reported, The king Ahasuerus commanded Vashti the queen to be brought in before him, but she came not.

18 Likewise shall the ladies of Persia and Media say this day unto all the king’s princes, which have heard of the deed of the queen. Thus shall there arise too much contempt and wrath.

19 If it please the king, let there go a royal commandment from him, and let it be written among the laws of the Persians and the Medes, that it be not altered, That Vashti come no more before king Ahasuerus; and let the king give her royal estate unto another that is better than she.

20 And when the king’s decree which he shall make shall be published throughout all his empire, (for it is great,) all the wives shall give to their husbands honour, both to great and small.

21 And the saying pleased the king and the princes; and the king did according to the word of Memucan:

22 For he sent letters into all the king’s provinces, into every province according to the writing thereof, and to every people after their language, that every man should bear rule in his own house, and that it should be published according to the language of every people.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

King Ahasuerus, who reigns from India to Ethiopia over a hundred and twenty-seven provinces, holds a hundred-and-eighty-day display of his kingdom's riches, followed by a seven-day feast for everyone present in Shushan. On the seventh day he sends for Queen Vashti to show her beauty to the people and princes; she refuses to come, and on Memucan's advice the king deposes her by decree, with letters to every province affirming that every man should bear rule in his own house. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the days of Ahasuerus, who reigned from India to Ethiopia over a hundred and twenty-seven provinces, the story opens. Henry notes the vast empire is the stage: a decree from such a king touches the whole known world, and a deliverance there touches it too. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When Ahasuerus sat on the throne of his kingdom in Shushan the palace, his power was settled. JFB notes Shushan was the winter capital, where the court and its records lay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. In the third year of his reign he made a feast for all his princes and servants, the power of Persia and Media, nobles and princes of the provinces before him. Henry notes the feast gathers the empire's strength around one throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. For a hundred and eighty days he showed the riches of his glorious kingdom and the honour of his excellent majesty. JFB notes the long display was not one feast but successive entertainments of province after province, all to exhibit wealth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. When these days expired, the king made a feast for all the people present in Shushan, great and small, seven days, in the court of the palace garden. Henry contrasts the two feasts: first the great, then all, so that none in the capital is left out of the king's bounty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. White, green and blue hangings, fastened with fine linen and purple cords to silver rings and marble pillars, gold and silver beds on a pavement of red, blue, white and black marble, decked the court. JFB notes the colours and stones are Persian luxury at its height, the setting for what follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They gave drink in diverse vessels of gold, and royal wine in abundance, according to the state of the king. Henry notes the abundance itself is the point: a king's feast is measured by excess. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The drinking was according to law; none did compel, for the king appointed his officers to do according to every man's pleasure. JFB notes this Persian custom of free drinking is named because it explains the king's later merry command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Also Vashti the queen made a feast for the women in the royal house belonging to Ahasuerus. Henry notes the separate women's feast follows the custom of the court, and keeps Vashti apart from the king's company until she is summoned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. On the seventh day, when the king's heart was merry with wine, he commanded Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar and Carcas, the seven chamberlains that served before him. JFB notes the seven chamberlains are the king's personal attendants, sent on an errand that wine had made reckless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. To bring Vashti the queen before the king with the royal crown, to show the people and princes her beauty, for she was fair to look on. Henry notes the summons treats the queen as an ornament of the feast, to be displayed before a drinking company. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. But queen Vashti refused to come at the king's commandment by his chamberlains: therefore the king was very wroth, and his anger burned in him. JFB notes the refusal is stated without its reasons, and the king's wrath is the passion of a man crossed in public. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Then the king said to the wise men which knew the times, for so was the king's manner toward all that knew law and judgment. Henry notes even a drunken king turns to counsellors, and the question put to them is legal, not personal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. And next unto him was Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena and Memucan, the seven princes of Persia and Media, which saw the king's face and sat first in the kingdom. JFB notes these are the king's inner council, whose advice in such a case becomes law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. What shall we do unto queen Vashti according to law, because she has not performed the king's commandment by the chamberlains? The question is framed as law, though the offence is a wife's refusal, showing how a household matter is raised to affairs of state. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. And Memucan answered before the king and princes, Vashti has not done wrong to the king only, but also to all the princes and all the people in all the provinces. Henry notes the argument widens a private refusal into a public example, the usual plea for making an example. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For this deed of the queen shall come abroad unto all women, so that they shall despise their husbands, when it shall be reported, Ahasuerus commanded Vashti to be brought in, but she came not. JFB notes the fear is imitation: one queen's refusal, once known, will be every wife's excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Likewise shall the ladies of Persia and Media say this day unto all the king's princes, which have heard of the deed of the queen. Thus shall there arise too much contempt and wrath. Henry notes the council foresees a kingdom of household quarrels following one court quarrel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. If it please the king, let a royal commandment go forth, written among the laws of the Persians and Medes, that it be not altered, that Vashti come no more before Ahasuerus; and let the king give her royal estate unto another that is better than she. JFB notes the irrevocable law is invoked for a divorce, and Henry adds that God's providence served its own purpose by it, making way for Esther to the crown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. And when the king's decree shall be published throughout all his empire, for it is great, all wives shall give their husbands honour, both great and small. The decree's stated aim is domestic order in every house, great and small alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. And the saying pleased the king and the princes; and the king did according to the word of Memucan. Henry notes how easily a king in anger adopts a counsellor's severity as his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. For he sent letters into all the provinces, each in its own writing and to every people in its own language, that every man should bear rule in his own house, and that it should be published in the language of every people. JFB notes the multilingual letters show the empire's machinery at work for a household rule, and Clarke adds the parade of enactment served chiefly to deprive Vashti of her crown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke

Unfamiliar terms

  • Shushan the palace. The royal capital where the king sits on his throne; the study note, following JFB, calls it the winter capital, where the court and its records lay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Chamberlains. Court servants who serve in the king's presence, here the seven who are sent to bring Vashti. Documented Chapter text, verses 10-11
  • The drinking was according to the law; none did compel. The king's order for the feast: each guest drinks as he pleases, rather than by compulsion. Documented Chapter text, verse 8
