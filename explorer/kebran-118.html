Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Kebra Nagast 118 (E. A. Wallis Budge, The Queen of Sheba and Her Only Son Menyelek (1922), public domain, for chs 1–118; Ge'ez text for ch. 119)

*COLOPHON*

1 In the Arabic text it is said: "We have turned [this book] into Arabic from a Coptic manuscript [belonging to] the throne of Mark the Evangelist, the teacher, the Father of us all. We have translated it in the four hundred and ninth year of mercy in the country of Ethiopia, in the days of Gabra Masḳal the king, who is called Lâlîbalâ, in the days of Abbâ George, the good bishop. And God neglected to have it translated and interpreted into the speech of Abyssinia. And when I had pondered this—Why did not ’Abal‘ez and Abalfarog who edited (or, copied) the book translate it? I said this: It went out in the days of Zâguâ, and they did not translate it because this book says: Those who reign not being Israelites are transgressors of the Law. Had they been of the kingdom of Israel they would have edited (or, translated) it. And it was found in Nâzrêt."

2 "And pray ye for me, your servant Isaac the poor man. And chide ye me not because of the incorrectness of the speech of the tongue. For I have toiled much for the glory of the country of Ethiopia, and for the going forth of the heavenly Zion, and for the glory of the King of Ethiopia. And I consulted the upright and God-loving governor Yâ‘ebîka ’Egzî’ĕ, and he approved and said unto me, 'Work.' And I worked, God helping me, and He did not requite me according to my sins. And pray ye for your servant Isaac, and for those

3 who toiled with me in the going out (i.e. production) of this book, for we were in sore tribulation, I, and Yamharana-’Ab, and Ḥezba-Krestôs, and Andrew, and Philip, and Maḥârî-’ab. May God have mercy upon them, and may He write their names in the Book of Life in the kingdom of heaven, with those of all the saints and martyrs for ever and ever! Amen."

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The colophon gives the book's own account of itself: in the Arabic text it is said that the book was turned into Arabic from a Coptic manuscript belonging to the throne of Mark the Evangelist, and translated in the four hundred and ninth year of mercy in Ethiopia, in the days of Gabra Maskal the king, who is called Lalibala. It closes with a prayer for those who toiled in producing the book — Yamharana-Ab, Hezba-Krestos, Andrew, Philip and Mahari-Ab — that God write their names in the Book of Life with the saints and martyrs. Unfamiliar terms

  • Zion. In this book, “Zion” is the name given to the Tabernacle of the Law of God — the Ark and its Law — not only the hill in Jerusalem. The book itself calls her “our Lady Zion, the holy and heavenly Tabernacle of the Law of God.” Interpreted Chapter text above
