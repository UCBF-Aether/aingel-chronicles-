The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 26 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the Ziphites came unto Saul to Gibeah, saying, Doth not David hide himself in the hill of Hachilah, which is before Jeshimon?

2 Then Saul arose, and went down to the wilderness of Ziph, having three thousand chosen men of Israel with him, to seek David in the wilderness of Ziph.

3 And Saul pitched in the hill of Hachilah, which is before Jeshimon, by the way. But David abode in the wilderness, and he saw that Saul came after him into the wilderness.

4 David therefore sent out spies, and understood that Saul was come in very deed.

5 And David arose, and came to the place where Saul had pitched: and David beheld the place where Saul lay, and Abner the son of Ner, the captain of his host: and Saul lay in the trench, and the people pitched round about him.

6 Then answered David and said to Ahimelech the Hittite, and to Abishai the son of Zeruiah, brother to Joab, saying, Who will go down with me to Saul to the camp? And Abishai said, I will go down with thee.

7 So David and Abishai came to the people by night: and, behold, Saul lay sleeping within the trench, and his spear stuck in the ground at his bolster: but Abner and the people lay round about him.

8 Then said Abishai to David, God hath delivered thine enemy into thine hand this day: now therefore let me smite him, I pray thee, with the spear even to the earth at once, and I will not smite him the second time.

9 And David said to Abishai, Destroy him not: for who can stretch forth his hand against the LORD’s anointed, and be guiltless?

10 David said furthermore, As the LORD liveth, the LORD shall smite him; or his day shall come to die; or he shall descend into battle, and perish.

11 The LORD forbid that I should stretch forth mine hand against the LORD’s anointed: but, I pray thee, take thou now the spear that is at his bolster, and the cruse of water, and let us go.

12 So David took the spear and the cruse of water from Saul’s bolster; and they gat them away, and no man saw it, nor knew it, neither awaked: for they were all asleep; because a deep sleep from the LORD was fallen upon them.

13 Then David went over to the other side, and stood on the top of an hill afar off; a great space being between them:

14 And David cried to the people, and to Abner the son of Ner, saying, Answerest thou not, Abner? Then Abner answered and said, Who art thou that criest to the king?

15 And David said to Abner, Art not thou a valiant man? and who is like to thee in Israel? wherefore then hast thou not kept thy lord the king? for there came one of the people in to destroy the king thy lord.

16 This thing is not good that thou hast done. As the LORD liveth, ye are worthy to die, because ye have not kept your master, the LORD’s anointed. And now see where the king’s spear is, and the cruse of water that was at his bolster.

17 And Saul knew David’s voice, and said, Is this thy voice, my son David? And David said, It is my voice, my lord, O king.

18 And he said, Wherefore doth my lord thus pursue after his servant? for what have I done? or what evil is in mine hand?

19 Now therefore, I pray thee, let my lord the king hear the words of his servant. If the LORD have stirred thee up against me, let him accept an offering: but if they be the children of men, cursed be they before the LORD; for they have driven me out this day from abiding in the inheritance of the LORD, saying, Go, serve other gods.

20 Now therefore, let not my blood fall to the earth before the face of the LORD: for the king of Israel is come out to seek a flea, as when one doth hunt a partridge in the mountains.

21 Then said Saul, I have sinned: return, my son David: for I will no more do thee harm, because my soul was precious in thine eyes this day: behold, I have played the fool, and have erred exceedingly.

22 And David answered and said, Behold the king’s spear! and let one of the young men come over and fetch it.

23 The LORD render to every man his righteousness and his faithfulness; for the LORD delivered thee into my hand to day, but I would not stretch forth mine hand against the LORD’s anointed.

24 And, behold, as thy life was much set by this day in mine eyes, so let my life be much set by in the eyes of the LORD, and let him deliver me out of all tribulation.

25 Then Saul said to David, Blessed be thou, my son David: thou shalt both do great things, and also shalt still prevail. So David went on his way, and Saul returned to his place.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The Ziphites betray David again, saying he hides in the hill of Hachilah before Jeshimon, and Saul goes down with three thousand chosen men, pitching in the hill itself. David sends spies, comes in person to where Saul lies in the trench with Abner and the people round about him, and asks who will go down with him into the camp; Abishai volunteers, and by night they find Saul asleep, his spear stuck in the ground at his bolster, a deep sleep from the LORD fallen on all. Abishai urges one stroke of the spear to the earth, and David refuses again, who can stretch forth his hand against the LORD's anointed and be guiltless, for the LORD shall smite him, or his day shall come to die, or he shall descend into battle and perish; instead he takes the spear and the cruse of water from Saul's bolster. From a hill afar off he cries to Abner that his watch is not good, worthy to die for not keeping the LORD's anointed, and shows the missing spear and cruse; Saul knows his voice, confesses, I have sinned, I have played the fool and erred exceedingly, and blesses David, thou shalt both do great things and also shalt still prevail, yet David returns the spear and does not return with it, and they part to meet no more. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Ziphites came unto Saul to Gibeah, saying, Doth not David hide himself in the hill of Hachilah, which is before Jeshimon? The betrayal of chapter 23 was repeated, and the hunt renewed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Then Saul arose and went down to the wilderness of Ziph, having three thousand chosen men with him, to seek David there. How soon do unholy hearts lose the good impressions convictions have made upon them, Henry observes: the weeping confession at Engedi was already forgotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Saul pitched in the hill of Hachilah before Jeshimon, by the way, but David abode in the wilderness, and saw that Saul came after him. The hunter camped on the very hill the betrayers had named. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. David sent out spies and understood that Saul was come in very deed. The fugitive verified the report before he moved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. David arose and came to the place where Saul had pitched, and beheld where Saul lay, and Abner son of Ner, captain of his host; Saul lay in the trench, and the people pitched round about him. David reconnoitred the royal camp in person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. David said to Ahimelech the Hittite and to Abishai son of Zeruiah, Who will go down with me to Saul to the camp? And Abishai said, I will go down with thee. Two men volunteered for the night venture into the enemy camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. So David and Abishai came to the people by night, and behold, Saul lay sleeping within the trench, his spear stuck in the ground at his bolster, with Abner and the people round about him. How helpless were Saul and all his men, Henry says: all as though disarmed and chained, yet nothing was done to them; they were only asleep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Then said Abishai, God hath delivered thine enemy into thine hand this day; now let me smite him with the spear even to the earth at once, and I will not smite him the second time. The temptation was very strong, Henry notes, and Abishai offered to do the deed in one stroke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. David said to Abishai, Destroy him not, for who can stretch forth his hand against the LORD's anointed, and be guiltless? David still resolved to wait until God thought fit to avenge him on Saul, Henry says, and would by no means force his way to the promised crown by wrong methods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. David said furthermore, As the LORD liveth, the LORD shall smite him, or his day shall come to die, or he shall descend into battle and perish. If David yielded, Henry observes, he would sin against God; therefore he resisted the temptation and trusted God with the event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The LORD forbid that I should stretch forth mine hand against the LORD's anointed, but take now the spear at his bolster, and the cruse of water, and let us go. The spear and the water-cruse would be the evidence, as the skirt had been before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. So David took the spear and the cruse of water from Saul's bolster, and they gat them away, and no man saw it, nor knew it, neither awaked, for they were all asleep, because a deep sleep from the LORD was fallen upon them. How easily can God weaken the strongest, befool the wisest, and baffle the most watchful, Henry reflects. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Then David went over to the other side and stood on the top of a hill afar off, a great space being between them. From a safe height, David would reason with the camp he had just left asleep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. David cried to the people and to Abner son of Ner, Answerest thou not, Abner? Then Abner answered, Who art thou that criest to the king? The voice from the hill woke the captain before it woke the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. David challenged Abner as a valiant man without peer in Israel, asking why he had not kept his lord the king when one of the people had come in to destroy him. David reasoned seriously and affectionately with Saul, Henry notes, beginning with the captain's failed watch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. David pronounced Abner's watch not good and said the guards were worthy to die for not keeping their master, the LORD's anointed, and pointed to the missing spear and water-cruse at Saul's bolster. The missing spear and cruse proved the charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Saul knew David's voice and said, Is this thy voice, my son David? And David said, It is my voice, my lord, O king. The king woke to his enemy's voice and his own spear gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. David asked why his lord pursued his servant, and what he had done or what evil was in his hand. His question again exposed the pursuit as causeless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Now therefore, let my lord hear the words of his servant: if the LORD have stirred thee up against me, let him accept an offering; but if they be the children of men, cursed be they before the LORD, for they have driven me out this day from abiding in the inheritance of the LORD, saying, Go, serve other gods. Those who forbid our attendance on God's ordinances, Henry observes, do what they can to estrange us from God and make us heathens; and we are to reckon that which exposes us to sin the greatest injury that can be done us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Now therefore, let not my blood fall to the earth before the face of the LORD, for the king of Israel is come out to seek a flea, as when one doth hunt a partridge in the mountains. The king's hunt was again a chase after a flea and a partridge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Then said Saul, I have sinned: return, my son David, for I will no more do thee harm, because my soul was precious in thine eyes this day; behold, I have played the fool, and have erred exceedingly. Saul repeated his good words and good wishes, Henry notes, but showed no evidence of true repentance towards God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. David answered, Behold the king's spear! And let one of the young men come over and fetch it. The spear was returned, but David did not return with it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. The LORD render to every man his righteousness and his faithfulness, for the LORD delivered thee into my hand today, but I would not stretch forth mine hand against the LORD's anointed. In sinning against God, Henry says, men play the fool and err exceedingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. And behold, as thy life was much set by this day in mine eyes, so let my life be much set by in the eyes of the LORD, and let him deliver me out of all tribulation. David prayed his own mercy back upon himself as a pattern of God's mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Then Saul said to David, Blessed be thou, my son David; thou shalt both do great things, and also shalt still prevail. So David went on his way, and Saul returned to his place. David and Saul parted to meet no more, Henry notes; no reconciliation among men is firm which is not founded in and cemented by peace with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
