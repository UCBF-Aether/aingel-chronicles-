The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 33 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Manasseh was twelve years old when he began to reign, and he reigned fifty and five years in Jerusalem:

2 But did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, like unto the abominations of the heathen, whom the LORD had cast out before the children of Israel.

3 For he built again the high places which Hezekiah his father had broken down, and he reared up altars for Baalim, and made groves, and worshipped all the host of heaven, and served them.

4 Also he built altars in the house of the LORD, whereof the LORD had said, In Jerusalem shall my name be for ever.

5 And he built altars for all the host of heaven in the two courts of the house of the LORD.

6 And he caused his children to pass through the fire in the valley of the son of Hinnom: also he observed times, and used enchantments, and used witchcraft, and dealt with a familiar spirit, and with wizards: he wrought much evil in the sight of the LORD, to provoke him to anger.

7 And he set a carved image, the idol which he had made, in the house of God, of which God had said to David and to Solomon his son, In this house, and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen before all the tribes of Israel, will I put my name for ever:

8 Neither will I any more remove the foot of Israel from out of the land which I have appointed for your fathers; so that they will take heed to do all that I have commanded them, according to the whole law and the statutes and the ordinances by the hand of Moses.

9 So Manasseh made Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem to err, and to do worse than the heathen, whom the LORD had destroyed before the children of Israel.

10 And the LORD spake to Manasseh, and to his people: but they would not hearken.

11 Wherefore the LORD brought upon them the captains of the host of the king of Assyria, which took Manasseh among the thorns, and bound him with fetters, and carried him to Babylon.

12 And when he was in affliction, he besought the LORD his God, and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers,

13 And prayed unto him: and he was intreated of him, and heard his supplication, and brought him again to Jerusalem into his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that the LORD he was God.

14 Now after this he built a wall without the city of David, on the west side of Gihon, in the valley, even to the entering in at the fish gate, and compassed about Ophel, and raised it up a very great height, and put captains of war in all the fenced cities of Judah.

15 And he took away the strange gods, and the idol out of the house of the LORD, and all the altars that he had built in the mount of the house of the LORD, and in Jerusalem, and cast them out of the city.

16 And he repaired the altar of the LORD, and sacrificed thereon peace offerings and thank offerings, and commanded Judah to serve the LORD God of Israel.

17 Nevertheless the people did sacrifice still in the high places, yet unto the LORD their God only.

18 Now the rest of the acts of Manasseh, and his prayer unto his God, and the words of the seers that spake to him in the name of the LORD God of Israel, behold, they are written in the book of the kings of Israel.

19 His prayer also, and how God was intreated of him, and all his sins, and his trespass, and the places wherein he built high places, and set up groves and graven images, before he was humbled: behold, they are written among the sayings of the seers.

20 So Manasseh slept with his fathers, and they buried him in his own house: and Amon his son reigned in his stead.

21 Amon was two and twenty years old when he began to reign, and reigned two years in Jerusalem.

22 But he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, as did Manasseh his father: for Amon sacrificed unto all the carved images which Manasseh his father had made, and served them;

23 And humbled not himself before the LORD, as Manasseh his father had humbled himself; but Amon trespassed more and more.

24 And his servants conspired against him, and slew him in his own house.

25 But the people of the land slew all them that had conspired against king Amon; and the people of the land made Josiah his son king in his stead.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Manasseh's idolatry and his captivity and repentance in Babylon, his son Amon's evil reign and slaying, and the making of Josiah king. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Manasseh was twelve years old when he began to reign, and he reigned fifty and five years in Jerusalem. Henry notes Manasseh reigned long, and his story begins with wretched apostasy, a foolish young prince contradicting his father's good example and education. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. But did evil in the sight of the Lord, like unto the abominations of the heathen, whom the Lord had cast out before Israel's children. Henry notes he abandoned himself to all impiety, transcribing the heathen's abominations, and from this learn that it is no new thing, though sad, for godly parents' children to turn aside from the good way in which they were trained, for parents may give many good things but cannot give grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. For he built again the high places which Hezekiah his father had broken down, and he reared up altars for Baalim, and made groves, and worshipped all the host of heaven, and served them. Henry notes he ruined the established religion and unravelled his father's glorious reformation, rebuilding high places, rearing Baalim's altars, making groves and worshipping heaven's host, for corruptions in worship are diseases the church is apt to relapse into even when seemingly cured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Also he built altars in the house of the Lord, whereof the Lord had said, In Jerusalem shall my name be for ever. Henry notes he profaned God's house with idolatry, in contempt of God's choice of Zion to be his rest for ever and Israel his covenant people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And he built altars for all the host of heaven in the two courts of the house of the Lord. Henry notes he profaned God's chosen temple with altars for heaven's host in both its courts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And he caused his children to pass through the fire in the valley of the son of Hinnom, also he observed times, and used enchantments, and used witchcraft, and dealt with a familiar spirit, and with wizards: he wrought much evil in the sight of the Lord, to provoke him to anger. Henry notes he dedicated his children to Moloch and made the devil's lying oracles his guides and counsellors, observing times, enchantments and witchcraft, dealing with familiar spirits and wizards, for the god of this world has strangely blinded men's minds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And he set a carved image, the idol which he had made, in the house of God, of which God had said to David and to Solomon his son, In this house, and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen before all the tribes of Israel, will I put my name for ever. Henry notes he set his carved idol in God's house with solemnity, as if master of that house, in contempt of God's chosen house and Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Neither will I any more remove the foot of Israel from out of the land which I have appointed for your fathers, so that they will take heed to do all that I have commanded them, according to the whole law and the statutes and the ordinances by the hand of Moses. Henry notes he embraced other gods in contempt of the fair terms he stood upon with God, Israel's foot not to be removed if they took heed to do all commanded by Moses' law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. So Manasseh made Judah and Jerusalem's inhabitants to err, and to do worse than the heathen, whom the Lord had destroyed before Israel's children. Henry notes he made them err and do worse than the heathen, for if the unclean spirit returns, he brings seven other spirits more wicked than himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And the Lord spake to Manasseh, and to his people, but they would not hearken. Henry notes God spoke to him and his people by prophets, but they would not hearken, and we may admire God's grace in speaking and their obstinacy in turning a deaf ear, that badness did not quite turn away his goodness, but still he waited to be gracious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Wherefore the Lord brought upon them the captains of the host of Assyria's king, which took Manasseh among the thorns, and bound him with fetters, and carried him to Babylon. Henry notes God brought a foreign enemy upon him: Babylon's king, who had courted his father, invaded him now he had departed from God, and his captains took Manasseh among the thorns, perhaps hiding in a bush or garden, or figuratively perplexed in counsels and embarrassed in affairs, in the briers, and bound him, held before with his own iniquity's cords, and carried him prisoner to Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And when he was in affliction, he besought the Lord his God, and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers. Henry notes in distress he did not, like Ahaz, trespass yet more, but humbled himself and returned to God, for sanctified afflictions often prove happy means of conversion, and he humbled himself greatly, truly sorry, ashamed and afraid of God's wrath, accepting punishment of iniquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And prayed unto him, and he was intreated of him, and heard his supplication, and brought him again to Jerusalem into his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that the Lord he was God. Henry notes he was convinced Jehovah is the only living true God, applied to him as his God renouncing all others, prayed for pardon and favour's return, and God was entreated and heard him, making a way for escape, for afflictions continue no longer than till they have done their work, and when Manasseh returned to God and duty he was soon returned to his kingdom, a pattern of long-suffering for great sinners not to despair. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Now after this he built a wall without the city of David, on the west side of Gihon, in the valley, even to the entering in at the fish gate, and compassed about Ophel, and raised it up a very great height, and put captains of war in all the fenced cities of Judah. Henry notes in prosperity after repentance he built a wall about David's city, for by sin he had unwalled and exposed it, and put captains of war in fenced cities for the country's security, and Josephus says all his rest of time he was so changed that he was looked upon as a very happy man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And he took away the strange gods, and the idol out of the house of the Lord, and all the altars that he had built in the mount of the house of the Lord, and in Jerusalem, and cast them out of the city. Henry notes the fruits meet for repentance: he turned from sins, taking away strange gods, images and the idol set up with solemnity in the Lord's house, casting out all idolatrous altars in the mount and Jerusalem as detestable, loathing them as much as ever he loved them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And he repaired the altar of the Lord, and sacrificed thereon peace offerings and thank offerings, and commanded Judah to serve the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes he returned to duty, repairing the Lord's altar, abused or neglected, sacrificing peace-offerings to implore favour and thank-offerings to praise deliverance, and using his power to reform his people as before he had abused it to corrupt them, commanding Judah to serve the Lord God of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Nevertheless the people did sacrifice still in the high places, yet unto the Lord their God only. Henry notes he prevailed to bring them off false gods but not from high places, for they still sacrificed in them, yet to the Lord only, and he could not carry reformation so far as he had carried corruption, for it is easy to debauch manners but not so easy to reform them again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Now the rest of the acts of Manasseh, and his prayer unto his God, and the words of the seers that spake to him in the name of the Lord God of Israel, behold, they are written in the book of the kings of Israel. Henry notes the heads for a full narrative are referred to other writings then extant, including the seers' words that spoke to him in the Lord's name to reprove sin and exhort repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. His prayer also, and how God was intreated of him, and all his sins, and his trespass, and the places wherein he built high places, and set up groves and graven images, before he was humbled, behold, they are written among the sayings of the seers. Henry notes a particular account was kept of all his sin and trespass, his high places, groves and images before humbling, probably from his own confession left on record in the book entitled The words of the seers, sent to his reprovers as gratitude, for penitents should take shame, thank reprovers and warn others, and his prayer and God's being entreated are twice mentioned as remarkable, written for generations to come that people might praise the Lord for readiness to receive returning prodigals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. So Manasseh slept with his fathers, and they buried him in his own house, and Amon his son reigned in his stead. Henry notes notice is taken of his burial, not in kings' sepulchres but in his own house, buried privately with none of his father's honours at death, for penitents may recover comfort sooner than credit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Amon was two and twenty years old when he began to reign, and reigned two years in Jerusalem. Henry notes Amon reigned very wickedly and soon ended his days unhappily, reigning but two years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. But he did evil in the sight of the Lord, as did Manasseh his father, for Amon sacrificed unto all the carved images which Manasseh his father had made, and served them. Henry notes he did as Manasseh had done in apostasy, and Manasseh was defective in that when he cast out images he did not utterly deface and destroy them according to the law requiring burning, so Amon knew where to find them, soon set them up and sacrificed to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And humbled not himself before the Lord, as Manasseh his father had humbled himself, but Amon trespassed more and more. Henry notes he outdid his father in sinning, trespassing more and more, but came short of his father in repenting, humbling not himself as his father had, and it is not so much sin as impenitence that ruins men, not the disease but neglect of the remedy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And his servants conspired against him, and slew him in his own house. Henry notes he reigned but two years and then his servants conspired and slew him, perhaps promising himself he should repent as his father did in latter days, but cut off young, for he rebelled against God and his own servants rebelled against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. But the people of the land slew all them that had conspired against king Amon, and the people of the land made Josiah his son king in his stead. Henry notes God was righteous herein, but the conspirators wicked, and justly did the land's people put them to death as traitors, for kings' lives are particularly under Providence's and laws' protection, and they made Josiah his son king in his stead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
