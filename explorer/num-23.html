The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Balaam said unto Balak, Build me here seven altars, and prepare me here seven oxen and seven rams.

2 And Balak did as Balaam had spoken; and Balak and Balaam offered on every altar a bullock and a ram.

3 And Balaam said unto Balak, Stand by thy burnt offering, and I will go: peradventure the LORD will come to meet me: and whatsoever he sheweth me I will tell thee. And he went to an high place.

4 And God met Balaam: and he said unto him, I have prepared seven altars, and I have offered upon every altar a bullock and a ram.

5 And the LORD put a word in Balaam’s mouth, and said, Return unto Balak, and thus thou shalt speak.

6 And he returned unto him, and, lo, he stood by his burnt sacrifice, he, and all the princes of Moab.

7 And he took up his parable, and said, Balak the king of Moab hath brought me from Aram, out of the mountains of the east, saying, Come, curse me Jacob, and come, defy Israel.

8 How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed? or how shall I defy, whom the LORD hath not defied?

9 For from the top of the rocks I see him, and from the hills I behold him: lo, the people shall dwell alone, and shall not be reckoned among the nations.

10 Who can count the dust of Jacob, and the number of the fourth part of Israel? Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his!

11 And Balak said unto Balaam, What hast thou done unto me? I took thee to curse mine enemies, and, behold, thou hast blessed them altogether.

12 And he answered and said, Must I not take heed to speak that which the LORD hath put in my mouth?

13 And Balak said unto him, Come, I pray thee, with me unto another place, from whence thou mayest see them: thou shalt see but the utmost part of them, and shalt not see them all: and curse me them from thence.

14 And he brought him into the field of Zophim, to the top of Pisgah, and built seven altars, and offered a bullock and a ram on every altar.

15 And he said unto Balak, Stand here by thy burnt offering, while I meet the LORD yonder.

16 And the LORD met Balaam, and put a word in his mouth, and said, Go again unto Balak, and say thus.

17 And when he came to him, behold, he stood by his burnt offering, and the princes of Moab with him. And Balak said unto him, What hath the LORD spoken?

18 And he took up his parable, and said, Rise up, Balak, and hear; hearken unto me, thou son of Zippor:

19 God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?

20 Behold, I have received commandment to bless: and he hath blessed; and I cannot reverse it.

21 He hath not beheld iniquity in Jacob, neither hath he seen perverseness in Israel: the LORD his God is with him, and the shout of a king is among them.

22 God brought them out of Egypt; he hath as it were the strength of an unicorn.

23 Surely there is no enchantment against Jacob, neither is there any divination against Israel: according to this time it shall be said of Jacob and of Israel, What hath God wrought!

24 Behold, the people shall rise up as a great lion, and lift up himself as a young lion: he shall not lie down until he eat of the prey, and drink the blood of the slain.

25 And Balak said unto Balaam, Neither curse them at all, nor bless them at all.

26 But Balaam answered and said unto Balak, Told not I thee, saying, All that the LORD speaketh, that I must do?

27 And Balak said unto Balaam, Come, I pray thee, I will bring thee unto another place; peradventure it will please God that thou mayest curse me them from thence.

28 And Balak brought Balaam unto the top of Peor, that looketh toward Jeshimon.

29 And Balaam said unto Balak, Build me here seven altars, and prepare me here seven bullocks and seven rams.

30 And Balak did as Balaam had said, and offered a bullock and a ram on every altar.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives Balaam's first two oracles. On the high places of Baal and then on Pisgah, Balak builds seven altars and offers a bullock and a ram on each, but the word put in Balaam's mouth is blessing, not curse. The first oracle says Israel dwells alone, cannot be counted, and cannot be cursed whom God has not cursed. The second says God is not a man that he should lie, he has blessed and Balaam cannot reverse it, no iniquity is seen in Jacob, and there is no enchantment against Jacob nor divination against Israel. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Balaam said to Balak, build me here seven altars, and prepare me here seven bullocks and seven rams. JFB notes Balak, a heathen, would suppose the altars honoured Baal, though they were prepared for the true God's worship, while Balaam blended superstition with divine worship by choosing Baal's high places and many altars instead of the one God appointed, and by trading on the heathen mystery attached to seven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Balak did as Balaam had spoken, and Balak and Balaam offered on every altar a bullock and a ram. The king and the prophet sacrificed together, fourteen animals on seven altars, in the full view of Israel's camps, as if magnitude of offering could purchase a curse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Balaam said to Balak, stand by your burnt offering, and I will go, peradventure the Lord will come to meet me, and whatsoever he shows me I will tell you, and he went to a high place. JFB notes standing by the offering was the posture of one expecting an important favour, and the high place apart was where Balaam practised rites to obtain an oracle's response. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. God met Balaam, and he said to him, I have prepared seven altars, and I have offered upon every altar a bullock and a ram. JFB stresses God met him not in compliance with his incantations, but to frustrate his wicked designs and compel him, contrary to his desires and interests, to pronounce a blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Lord put a word in Balaam's mouth, and said, return to Balak, and thus you shall speak. Henry notes the miracle was as great as opening the ass's mouth: he who opened the beast's mouth caused this wicked man's mouth to speak words as contrary to his heart's desire as the ass's were to brute powers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He returned to him, and lo, he stood by his burnt sacrifice, he, and all the princes of Moab. Balak waited where Balaam had left him, with all his princes, expecting a curse and about to hear the opposite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. He took up his parable, and said, Balak king of Moab has brought me from Aram, out of the mountains of the east, saying, come, curse me Jacob, and come, defy Israel. JFB notes a parable means speech under inspiration in a highly poetical, figurative, oracular style, and Aram with the eastern mountains denotes upper Mesopotamia east of Moab, a region infamous for magicians and soothsayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. How shall I curse, whom God has not cursed? Or how shall I defy, whom the Lord has not defied? JFB notes a divine blessing had been pronounced over Jacob's posterity, so whatever prodigies charms could achieve, all magical skill and human power were utterly impotent to counteract God's decree. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. For from the top of the rocks I see him, and from the hills I behold him: lo, the people shall dwell alone, and shall not be reckoned among the nations. JFB notes the bare place on the rocks was chosen because seeing the people was deemed necessary to devote them to destruction, yet Israel's destiny was to be a distinct, peculiar people, separated in government, religion, customs and divine protection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Who can count the dust of Jacob, and the number of the fourth part of Israel? Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his! JFB notes the dust is an Oriental hyperbole for a populous nation as promised to Jacob, and the fourth part means the camp's four divisions, each formidable in numbers, while the death of the righteous, Hebrew Jeshurun, means a people happy in life and at death through knowledge of the true God and hope through his grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Balak said to Balaam, what have you done to me? I took you to curse my enemies, and behold, you have blessed them altogether. Henry notes a confession of God's overruling power was thus extorted from a wicked prophet to the confusion of a wicked prince, and the curse was turned into blessing by God's overruling power in love to Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. He answered and said, must I not take heed to speak that which the Lord has put in my mouth? The prophet's defence was compulsion itself: the word put in his mouth was not his to edit, and heeding it was the only faithfulness left to a man hired to do the opposite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Balak said to him, come, I pray you, with me to another place, from whence you may see them: you shall see but the utmost part of them, and shall not see them all, and curse me them from thence. JFB notes Balak, surprised and disappointed at the eulogy, hoped a different point of observation would produce different feelings, as if a partial view could change God's verdict. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He brought him into the field of Zophim, to the top of Pisgah, and built seven altars, and offered a bullock and a ram on every altar. JFB describes Zophim as a flat surface on the mountain summit, cultivated land, or, in another rendering, the field of sentinels, where Balak's guards were posted to give signals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He said to Balak, stand here by your burnt offering, while I meet the Lord yonder. The second attempt repeated the first exactly: the king by his offering, the prophet apart to meet the Lord, as if repetition could succeed where the first rite had failed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The Lord met Balaam, put a word in his mouth, and said, go again to Balak, and say thus. The second meeting matched the first: God met the prophet again, and again the word given was not the word Balak had paid for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. When he came to him, behold, he stood by his burnt offering, and the princes of Moab with him, and Balak said to him, what has the Lord spoken? Balak waited a second time with his princes, and his question assumed the Lord might at last have changed his answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. He took up his parable, and said, rise up, Balak, and hear, hearken to me, you son of Zippor. JFB notes rise up, since Balak was already standing, means now attend to me, and the counsel and promises of God respecting Israel are unchangeable, so no attempt to prevail on him to reverse them will succeed as it might with a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. God is not a man, that he should lie, neither the son of man, that he should repent: has he said, and shall he not do it? Or has he spoken, and shall he not make it good? Henry notes men change their minds and break their words, but God never changes his mind and therefore never recalls his promise, and when Scripture says he repents, it means a change of his way, not of his mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Behold, I have received commandment to bless, and he has blessed, and I cannot reverse it. The commandment to bless was itself the reversal Balak feared: what God had blessed, no prophet's word could unbless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. He has not beheld iniquity in Jacob, neither has he seen perverseness in Israel: the Lord his God is with him, and the shout of a king is among them. JFB notes many sins were observed and punished in this people, but no universal and hopeless apostasy had yet appeared to induce God to abandon or destroy them, and the shout of a king is the joyful acclamation of a people rejoicing in a victorious prince's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. God brought them out of Egypt; he has as it were the strength of a unicorn. JFB notes Israel was no longer the poor, feeble, spiritless horde of the exodus, but powerful and invincible as a reem, that is, a rhinoceros. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Surely there is no enchantment against Jacob, neither is there any divination against Israel: according to this time it shall be said of Jacob and of Israel, what has God wrought! JFB notes no art can prevail against a people under the shield of Omnipotence, for whom miracles have been and yet shall be performed, a theme of admiration in succeeding ages. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Behold, the people shall rise up as a great lion, and lift up himself as a young lion: he shall not lie down until he eat of the prey, and drink the blood of the slain. Henry notes this second blessing was both larger and stronger than the former: Israel rises like a lion that does not rest until its prey is eaten, a picture of conquest still to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Balak said to Balaam, neither curse them at all, nor bless them at all. Since Balaam could not say what Balak would have him say, Balak wished him to say nothing, yet, as Henry notes, though there are many devices in man's heart, God's counsels shall stand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Balaam answered Balak, told not I you, saying, all that the Lord speaks, that I must do? JFB calls this a remarkable confession: Balaam was divinely constrained to utter words different from what it was his purpose and inclination to do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Balak said to Balaam, come, I pray you, I will bring you to another place; peradventure it will please God that you may curse me them from thence. Henry notes they resolved on another attempt, though they had no promise on which to build their hopes, unlike those who have a promise that the vision at the end shall speak and not lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Balak brought Balaam to the top of Peor, that looks toward Jeshimon. JFB identifies Peor, or Beth-peor, as the eminence on which a temple of Baal stood, looking toward Jeshimon, the desert tract in the south of Palestine on both sides of the Dead Sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Balaam said to Balak, build me here seven altars, and prepare me here seven bullocks and seven rams. The third set of altars repeated the first two exactly: seven altars, seven bullocks, seven rams, as if the third repetition could move a God who had spoken twice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Balak did as Balaam had said, and offered a bullock and a ram on every altar. The king obeyed a third time, and the sacrifices stood ready a third time, for the oracle that would bless Israel a third time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
