The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when Athaliah the mother of Ahaziah saw that her son was dead, she arose and destroyed all the seed royal.

2 But Jehosheba, the daughter of king Joram, sister of Ahaziah, took Joash the son of Ahaziah, and stole him from among the king’s sons which were slain; and they hid him, even him and his nurse, in the bedchamber from Athaliah, so that he was not slain.

3 And he was with her hid in the house of the LORD six years. And Athaliah did reign over the land.

4 And the seventh year Jehoiada sent and fetched the rulers over hundreds, with the captains and the guard, and brought them to him into the house of the LORD, and made a covenant with them, and took an oath of them in the house of the LORD, and shewed them the king’s son.

5 And he commanded them, saying, This is the thing that ye shall do; A third part of you that enter in on the sabbath shall even be keepers of the watch of the king’s house;

6 And a third part shall be at the gate of Sur; and a third part at the gate behind the guard: so shall ye keep the watch of the house, that it be not broken down.

7 And two parts of all you that go forth on the sabbath, even they shall keep the watch of the house of the LORD about the king.

8 And ye shall compass the king round about, every man with his weapons in his hand: and he that cometh within the ranges, let him be slain: and be ye with the king as he goeth out and as he cometh in.

9 And the captains over the hundreds did according to all things that Jehoiada the priest commanded: and they took every man his men that were to come in on the sabbath, with them that should go out on the sabbath, and came to Jehoiada the priest.

10 And to the captains over hundreds did the priest give king David’s spears and shields, that were in the temple of the LORD.

11 And the guard stood, every man with his weapons in his hand, round about the king, from the right corner of the temple to the left corner of the temple, along by the altar and the temple.

12 And he brought forth the king’s son, and put the crown upon him, and gave him the testimony; and they made him king, and anointed him; and they clapped their hands, and said, God save the king.

13 And when Athaliah heard the noise of the guard and of the people, she came to the people into the temple of the LORD.

14 And when she looked, behold, the king stood by a pillar, as the manner was, and the princes and the trumpeters by the king, and all the people of the land rejoiced, and blew with trumpets: and Athaliah rent her clothes, and cried, Treason, Treason.

15 But Jehoiada the priest commanded the captains of the hundreds, the officers of the host, and said unto them, Have her forth without the ranges: and him that followeth her kill with the sword. For the priest had said, Let her not be slain in the house of the LORD.

16 And they laid hands on her; and she went by the way by the which the horses came into the king’s house: and there was she slain.

17 And Jehoiada made a covenant between the LORD and the king and the people, that they should be the LORD’s people; between the king also and the people.

18 And all the people of the land went into the house of Baal, and brake it down; his altars and his images brake they in pieces thoroughly, and slew Mattan the priest of Baal before the altars. And the priest appointed officers over the house of the LORD.

19 And he took the rulers over hundreds, and the captains, and the guard, and all the people of the land; and they brought down the king from the house of the LORD, and came by the way of the gate of the guard to the king’s house. And he sat on the throne of the kings.

20 And all the people of the land rejoiced, and the city was in quiet: and they slew Athaliah with the sword beside the king’s house.

21 Seven years old was Jehoash when he began to reign.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Athaliah, Ahab's daughter, seizes Judah's throne and kills the royal seed, but baby Joash is hidden six years in the temple by Jehosheba. Jehoiada the priest crowns the seven-year-old, Athaliah is slain, and the covenant is renewed and Baal's temple broken down. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When Athaliah mother of Ahaziah saw her son was dead, she arose and destroyed all the seed royal. JFB explains she was incited partly to keep David's family from outliving hers, partly in self-defence against Jehu, who was bent on destroying Ahab's posterity to which she belonged, but chiefly from personal ambition to rule and establish Baal worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Jehosheba, daughter of King Joram and sister of Ahaziah, took Joash son of Ahaziah and stole him from among the king's sons being slain, hiding him and his nurse in the bedchamber from Athaliah. JFB notes Jehosheba was not Athaliah's daughter but a secondary wife's, and the bedchamber was a priests' closet in the temple of which Jehoiada and his wife had sole charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Joash was hidden with Jehosheba in the house of the LORD six years while Athaliah reigned over the land. JFB's section "Jehoash saved from Athaliah's massacre" frames these six hidden years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. In the seventh year Jehoiada sent and fetched the rulers over hundreds, the captains, and the guard, brought them into the LORD's house, made a covenant with them, took an oath, and showed them the king's son. JFB notes such a general convocation could scarcely have been obtained except at a public solemn festival, and Jehoiada's conspiracy admits full justification, since Athaliah was a usurper of a race destined for destruction, while the young prince held an inherent right to David's throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Jehoiada commanded that a third part entering on the sabbath should keep the watch of the king's house. JFB's account of Jehoiada's plan covers this division of the guard for the plot's execution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. A third part was to be at the gate of Sur, and a third part at the gate behind the guard, for the watch of the house, that it be not broken down. JFB notes Jehoiada arranged with the rulers the plan and time of carrying the plot into execution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Two parts of those going forth on the sabbath were to keep the watch of the LORD's house about the king. JFB presents this as part of Jehoiada's arranged plan, supported by the chief civil and ecclesiastical authorities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. They were to compass the king round about, every man with weapons in hand, and whoever came within the ranges was to be slain, and they were to be with the king as he went out and came in. JFB notes Jehoiada, most probably high priest, acted under an impulse of the Divine Spirit through the prophets' counsels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The captains over the hundreds did according to all Jehoiada the priest commanded, each taking his men, those entering and those going out on the sabbath, and coming to Jehoiada. JFB's section "He is made king" covers this obedient muster. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The priest gave the captains King David's spears and shields that were in the temple of the LORD. JFB's account of the coronation preparations includes arming the guard from the temple's stores. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The guard stood, every man with weapons in hand, round about the king, from the right corner of the temple to the left, by the altar and the temple. JFB's section describes this armed ring around the young king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Jehoiada brought forth the king's son, put the crown on him, gave him the testimony, and they made him king and anointed him, clapping hands and saying, "God save the king." JFB's section "He is made king" records this coronation as the restoration of David's heir. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. When Athaliah heard the noise of the guard and people, she came to the people into the temple of the LORD. JFB notes the profound secrecy of the conspiracy made the vast crowd's unusual acclamations startling, rousing the tyrant's suspicions, and she was permitted into the courts by Jehoiada's directions so she might be secured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Athaliah looked and saw the king standing by a pillar, as the manner was, with princes and trumpeters, and all the land rejoicing and blowing trumpets, and she rent her clothes crying, "Treason, Treason." JFB notes the king stood by a pillar, or on a platform erected for that purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Jehoiada commanded the captains to bring Athaliah out within the ranges, and whoever followed her was to be slain with the sword, for the priest said not to slay her in the LORD's house. JFB explains "without the ranges" meant beyond the fences, so the sacred place might not be stained with human blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. They laid hands on her, and she went by the way horses came into the king's house, and there she was slain. JFB's section "Athaliah slain" closes the usurper's reign with her execution outside the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Jehoiada made a covenant between the LORD and the king and the people, that they should be the LORD's people, and likewise between the king and the people. JFB explains the covenant with the LORD was a renewal of the national covenant, and the covenant between king and people bound the king to rule by divine law while the people gave allegiance to the LORD's anointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. All the people went into Baal's house, broke it down, broke his altars and images, and slew Mattan Baal's priest before the altars, while the priest appointed officers over the LORD's house. JFB notes the immediate fruit of the covenant's renewal was the destruction of Baal's temple and the slaughter of his priests, restoring pure worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Jehoiada took the rulers, captains, guard, and all the people, and brought the king down from the LORD's house through the guard's gate to the king's house, where he sat on the throne of the kings. JFB notes the covenant's fruit included the establishment of the young king on Judah's hereditary throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. All the people of the land rejoiced, the city was quiet, and they had slain Athaliah with the sword beside the king's house. JFB's section closes with the land's rejoicing at the restoration of God's worship and David's heir. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
