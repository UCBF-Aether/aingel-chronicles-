The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And there was given me a reed like unto a rod: and the angel stood, saying, Rise, and measure the temple of God, and the altar, and them that worship therein.

2 But the court which is without the temple leave out, and measure it not; for it is given unto the Gentiles: and the holy city shall they tread under foot forty and two months.

3 And I will give power unto my two witnesses, and they shall prophesy a thousand two hundred and threescore days, clothed in sackcloth.

4 These are the two olive trees, and the two candlesticks standing before the God of the earth.

5 And if any man will hurt them, fire proceedeth out of their mouth, and devoureth their enemies: and if any man will hurt them, he must in this manner be killed.

6 These have power to shut heaven, that it rain not in the days of their prophecy: and have power over waters to turn them to blood, and to smite the earth with all plagues, as often as they will.

7 And when they shall have finished their testimony, the beast that ascendeth out of the bottomless pit shall make war against them, and shall overcome them, and kill them.

8 And their dead bodies shall lie in the street of the great city, which spiritually is called Sodom and Egypt, where also our Lord was crucified.

9 And they of the people and kindreds and tongues and nations shall see their dead bodies three days and an half, and shall not suffer their dead bodies to be put in graves.

10 And they that dwell upon the earth shall rejoice over them, and make merry, and shall send gifts one to another; because these two prophets tormented them that dwelt on the earth.

11 And after three days and an half the spirit of life from God entered into them, and they stood upon their feet; and great fear fell upon them which saw them.

12 And they heard a great voice from heaven saying unto them, Come up hither. And they ascended up to heaven in a cloud; and their enemies beheld them.

13 And the same hour was there a great earthquake, and the tenth part of the city fell, and in the earthquake were slain of men seven thousand: and the remnant were affrighted, and gave glory to the God of heaven.

14 The second woe is past; and, behold, the third woe cometh quickly.

15 And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.

16 And the four and twenty elders, which sat before God on their seats, fell upon their faces, and worshipped God,

17 Saying, We give thee thanks, O LORD God Almighty, which art, and wast, and art to come; because thou hast taken to thee thy great power, and hast reigned.

18 And the nations were angry, and thy wrath is come, and the time of the dead, that they should be judged, and that thou shouldest give reward unto thy servants the prophets, and to the saints, and them that fear thy name, small and great; and shouldest destroy them which destroy the earth.

19 And the temple of God was opened in heaven, and there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament: and there were lightnings, and voices, and thunderings, and an earthquake, and great hail.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

John is given a reed to measure the temple, altar and worshippers, while the outer court is left to the Gentiles for forty-two months, and two witnesses — the two olive trees and candlesticks — prophesy in sackcloth, are killed by the beast, lie unburied while the earth rejoices, then rise and ascend to heaven amid an earthquake. The seventh angel sounds, great voices proclaim that the kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord and of his Christ, the elders give thanks, the dead are judged and God's servants rewarded, and the temple in heaven is opened with the ark of his testament seen within. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John was given a reed like a rod and told, "Rise, and measure the temple of God, and the altar, and them that worship therein." Henry notes the measuring, after Ezekiel's pattern, may be for preservation in danger, for trial against the true model, or for reformation of what is redundant, deficient, or changed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The court outside the temple was to be left out and not measured, for it was given to the Gentiles, and the holy city would be trodden under foot forty-two months. Henry explains the outer court was no part of the temple by Solomon's or Zerubbabel's model, so God abandoned it to pagan ceremonies, while the desolations of the church are limited in time and she shall be delivered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Christ will give power to his two witnesses, who shall prophesy a thousand two hundred and threescore days, clothed in sackcloth. Henry notes the number is small yet sufficient, since every cause is established by two witnesses, and that they are God's eminent faithful ministers preaching in the worst of times, deeply affected by the church's distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. These are the two olive trees and the two candlesticks standing before the God of the earth. Henry compares them to Zerubbabel and Joshua in Zechariah's vision: God gave them the oil of holy zeal, courage, strength, and comfort, so their lamps of profession burned by inward gracious principles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. If anyone would hurt them, fire proceeds out of their mouth and devours their enemies, and whoever would hurt them must in this manner be killed. Henry notes God's words in the prophets' mouths are fire devouring enemies as wood, and that persecutors are galled and self-condemned by faithful preaching, praying, and suffering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. They have power to shut heaven so it does not rain in the days of their prophecy, power over waters to turn them to blood, and power to smite the earth with all plagues as often as they will. Henry compares the shut heaven to Elijah's prayer and the bloodied waters to Pharaoh's plagues, showing God inflicts judgments on enemies at his witnesses' prayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. When they have finished their testimony, the beast ascending out of the bottomless pit shall make war against them, overcome them, and kill them. Henry notes they are immortal and invulnerable until their work is done, and that antichrist would prevail against them for a time by subtle learning and chiefly by open force. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Their dead bodies shall lie in the street of the great city, spiritually called Sodom and Egypt, where also our Lord was crucified. Henry notes the malice pursued even their corpses, denied a quiet grave in Babylon's high street, where Christ in his mystical body suffered more than anywhere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. People, kindreds, tongues, and nations shall see their dead bodies three days and a half, and not suffer their bodies to be put in graves. Henry treats the short exposure as part of the enemies' insult, contrasting with the long prophecy that preceded it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Those dwelling on the earth shall rejoice over them, make merry, and send gifts to one another, because these two prophets tormented those who dwelt on the earth. Henry notes the world's gladness at being rid of witnesses whose doctrine and example teased, terrified, and tormented guilty consciences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. After three days and a half the Spirit of life from God entered into them, and they stood on their feet, and great fear fell on those who saw them. Henry notes God's witnesses may be slain but shall rise again in their successors, as God revives his work when it seems dead, putting life and courage into them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. They heard a great voice from heaven saying, "Come up hither," and ascended up to heaven in a cloud, while their enemies beheld them. Henry notes the witnesses did not grasp at advancement until God called them, and that persecutors seeing the faithful honoured is itself part of their punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. In the same hour there was a great earthquake, a tenth part of the city fell, seven thousand were slain, and the remnant were affrighted and gave glory to the God of heaven. Henry refers this to the Reformation, when princes and states fell from Rome, many were slain by war, and others, convicted by the Spirit's sword, repented and glorified God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The second woe is past, and behold, the third woe comes quickly. Henry notes the seventh trumpet had been suspended until John was acquainted with these intervening occurrences of great moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. The seventh angel sounded, and great voices in heaven said, "The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever." Henry notes the kingdoms were always his by creation and purchase in title, and now title turns into possession, a reign that shall never end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The four and twenty elders who sat before God on their seats fell on their faces and worshipped God. Henry notes the manner of their adoration: they rose from their seats and fell on their faces with reverence and humility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. They said, "We give thee thanks, O Lord God Almighty, which art, and wast, and art to come; because thou hast taken to thee thy great power, and hast reigned." Henry notes they thank God for asserting his rights and exerting his power, turning title into actual possession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The nations were angry, and God's wrath is come, with the time of the dead to be judged, of rewarding his servants the prophets, saints, and those who fear his name, small and great, and of destroying those who destroy the earth. Henry notes the nations met God's wrath with their own anger, fretting against the just rewarding of his people and increasing their guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The temple of God was opened in heaven, and the ark of his testament was seen in his temple, with lightnings, voices, thunderings, an earthquake, and great hail. Henry notes the opened temple means freer communication between heaven and earth, and the ark, long locked away under antichrist, now brought to view as a token of God's returned presence and favour in Christ the propitiation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Two witnesses. The two olive trees and two candlesticks standing before the God of the earth, who prophesy, are killed, rise and ascend. Interpreted Study note, verse 4, Matthew Henry
