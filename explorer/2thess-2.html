The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Thessalonians 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now we beseech you, brethren, by the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, and by our gathering together unto him,

2 That ye be not soon shaken in mind, or be troubled, neither by spirit, nor by word, nor by letter as from us, as that the day of Christ is at hand.

3 Let no man deceive you by any means: for that day shall not come, except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition;

4 Who opposeth and exalteth himself above all that is called God, or that is worshipped; so that he as God sitteth in the temple of God, shewing himself that he is God.

5 Remember ye not, that, when I was yet with you, I told you these things?

6 And now ye know what withholdeth that he might be revealed in his time.

7 For the mystery of iniquity doth already work: only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way.

8 And then shall that Wicked be revealed, whom the Lord shall consume with the spirit of his mouth, and shall destroy with the brightness of his coming:

9 Even him, whose coming is after the working of Satan with all power and signs and lying wonders,

10 And with all deceivableness of unrighteousness in them that perish; because they received not the love of the truth, that they might be saved.

11 And for this cause God shall send them strong delusion, that they should believe a lie:

12 That they all might be damned who believed not the truth, but had pleasure in unrighteousness.

13 But we are bound to give thanks alway to God for you, brethren beloved of the Lord, because God hath from the beginning chosen you to salvation through sanctification of the Spirit and belief of the truth:

14 Whereunto he called you by our gospel, to the obtaining of the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ.

15 Therefore, brethren, stand fast, and hold the traditions which ye have been taught, whether by word, or our epistle.

16 Now our Lord Jesus Christ himself, and God, even our Father, which hath loved us, and hath given us everlasting consolation and good hope through grace,

17 Comfort your hearts, and stablish you in every good word and work.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Do not be shaken, Paul urges, by claims that the day of Christ has already come. First, he says, must come a falling away and the revealing of the man of sin, who opposes and exalts himself above all worship; a restraining force holds him back for now, but the Lord will destroy him at his coming. Readers are therefore to stand fast and hold the teachings they were given. Interpreted Editorial overview of the chapter text; verse-by-verse study-note summaries follow

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul beseeches them by Christ's coming and by the gathering of believers to him, so that the hope itself steadies them rather than unsettles them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. They must not be soon shaken or troubled by spirit, word, or forged letter claiming the day of Christ is at hand, for error spreads fastest by alarm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. No one should deceive them, because that day will not come until a falling away comes first and the man of sin is revealed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The man of sin opposes and exalts himself above all called God or worshipped, usurping divine honours in the very place of God's worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Paul reminds them he told them these things while still with them, so this warning is no new doctrine but a repetition of his oral teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. They already know what withholds the man of sin, so that he can only be revealed in his own appointed time, not before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The mystery of iniquity already works in secret, while the restrainer holds it back until he is taken out of the way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Then the Wicked will be revealed, only to be consumed by the spirit of Christ's mouth and destroyed by the brightness of his coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. His coming works after Satan, with all power, signs, and lying wonders, so that his miracles are wonders of deceit, not proofs of truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. His deceit works in those who perish because they received not the love of the truth, and a heart that rejects truth is left open to lies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. For this cause God sends strong delusion that they believe a lie, not as author of sin but in righteous judgment withdrawing grace from wilful unbelief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. All are thus damned who believed not the truth but had pleasure in unrighteousness, since an erroneous mind and a vicious life go together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Against that terror Paul gives thanks for them, beloved of the Lord, chosen from the beginning to salvation through sanctification and belief of the truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. God called them by the gospel to obtain the glory of the Lord Jesus Christ, so their hope rests on his call, not on their own strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Therefore they must stand fast and hold the traditions taught by word or epistle, since steadfastness is the guard against both error and apostasy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Christ himself and God the Father have loved them and given everlasting consolation and good hope through grace, a comfort no trouble can cancel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Paul prays that God would comfort their hearts and establish them in every good word and work, for comfort is itself a means of establishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • man of sin. the chapter’s figure of lawless opposition, revealed before the day of the Lord; who or what this figure is in history is not settled by this chapter Unresolved No source consulted for this page identifies the figure beyond the chapter text
  • letteth. in this older wording, restrains or holds back, here the force holding the lawless one back until its time (verse 7) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
