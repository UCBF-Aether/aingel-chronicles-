The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place.

2 And suddenly there came a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind, and it filled all the house where they were sitting.

3 And there appeared unto them cloven tongues like as of fire, and it sat upon each of them.

4 And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.

5 And there were dwelling at Jerusalem Jews, devout men, out of every nation under heaven.

6 Now when this was noised abroad, the multitude came together, and were confounded, because that every man heard them speak in his own language.

7 And they were all amazed and marvelled, saying one to another, Behold, are not all these which speak Galilaeans?

8 And how hear we every man in our own tongue, wherein we were born?

9 Parthians, and Medes, and Elamites, and the dwellers in Mesopotamia, and in Judaea, and Cappadocia, in Pontus, and Asia,

10 Phrygia, and Pamphylia, in Egypt, and in the parts of Libya about Cyrene, and strangers of Rome, Jews and proselytes,

11 Cretes and Arabians, we do hear them speak in our tongues the wonderful works of God.

12 And they were all amazed, and were in doubt, saying one to another, What meaneth this?

13 Others mocking said, These men are full of new wine.

14 But Peter, standing up with the eleven, lifted up his voice, and said unto them, Ye men of Judaea, and all ye that dwell at Jerusalem, be this known unto you, and hearken to my words:

15 For these are not drunken, as ye suppose, seeing it is but the third hour of the day.

16 But this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel;

17 And it shall come to pass in the last days, saith God, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams:

18 And on my servants and on my handmaidens I will pour out in those days of my Spirit; and they shall prophesy:

19 And I will shew wonders in heaven above, and signs in the earth beneath; blood, and fire, and vapour of smoke:

20 The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the great and notable day of the Lord come:

21 And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved.

22 Ye men of Israel, hear these words; Jesus of Nazareth, a man approved of God among you by miracles and wonders and signs, which God did by him in the midst of you, as ye yourselves also know:

23 Him, being delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God, ye have taken, and by wicked hands have crucified and slain:

24 Whom God hath raised up, having loosed the pains of death: because it was not possible that he should be holden of it.

25 For David speaketh concerning him, I foresaw the Lord always before my face, for he is on my right hand, that I should not be moved:

26 Therefore did my heart rejoice, and my tongue was glad; moreover also my flesh shall rest in hope:

27 Because thou wilt not leave my soul in hell, neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption.

28 Thou hast made known to me the ways of life; thou shalt make me full of joy with thy countenance.

29 Men and brethren, let me freely speak unto you of the patriarch David, that he is both dead and buried, and his sepulchre is with us unto this day.

30 Therefore being a prophet, and knowing that God had sworn with an oath to him, that of the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up Christ to sit on his throne;

31 He seeing this before spake of the resurrection of Christ, that his soul was not left in hell, neither his flesh did see corruption.

32 This Jesus hath God raised up, whereof we all are witnesses.

33 Therefore being by the right hand of God exalted, and having received of the Father the promise of the Holy Ghost, he hath shed forth this, which ye now see and hear.

34 For David is not ascended into the heavens: but he saith himself, The Lord said unto my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand,

35 Until I make thy foes thy footstool.

36 Therefore let all the house of Israel know assuredly, that God hath made the same Jesus, whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Christ.

37 Now when they heard this, they were pricked in their heart, and said unto Peter and to the rest of the apostles, Men and brethren, what shall we do?

38 Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost.

39 For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the LORD our God shall call.

40 And with many other words did he testify and exhort, saying, Save yourselves from this untoward generation.

41 Then they that gladly received his word were baptized: and the same day there were added unto them about three thousand souls.

42 And they continued stedfastly in the apostles’ doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.

43 And fear came upon every soul: and many wonders and signs were done by the apostles.

44 And all that believed were together, and had all things common;

45 And sold their possessions and goods, and parted them to all men, as every man had need.

46 And they, continuing daily with one accord in the temple, and breaking bread from house to house, did eat their meat with gladness and singleness of heart,

47 Praising God, and having favour with all the people. And the Lord added to the church daily such as should be saved.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

On the day of Pentecost the Holy Spirit comes on the disciples, who speak in other languages, and Peter preaches that this fulfils Joel's prophecy and that Jesus, crucified and risen, is Lord and Christ. About three thousand people are baptized, and the new community shares its life, teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, and prayer. Interpreted Overview of this chapter's text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When the day of Pentecost was fully come they were all with one accord in one place. Henry contrasts this with their earlier strifes over who should be greatest, now ended by praying together, and draws the lesson that where brethren dwell in unity, there the Lord commands his blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. A sound like a rushing mighty wind filled the house where they sat. Henry reads the wind as signifying the powerful influences and working of the Spirit upon the minds of men and thereby upon the world, with convictions making way for comforts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Cloven tongues like fire appeared and sat upon each of them. Henry explains the fire: the Spirit melts the heart, burns up the dross, and kindles pious and devout affections, in which the spiritual sacrifices are offered up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They were all filled with the Holy Ghost and began to speak with other tongues as the Spirit gave them utterance. Henry distinguishes the filling: graces of the Spirit, comforts in Christ's love and the hope of heaven, and miraculous gifts for furthering the gospel, speaking not from previous meditation but as the Spirit gave utterance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Devout Jews from every nation under heaven were dwelling in Jerusalem for the feast. Henry's point on the whole passage is that the first instruments of spreading Christianity could have made no progress without this gift of tongues, which proved their authority was from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The multitude came together and were confounded because each heard them speak in his own language. Henry frames this against Babel: the difference of languages that had much hindered the spread of knowledge and religion was now overcome by the gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. They asked how it was that each heard in his own tongue wherein he was born. Henry's answer for the passage is the same reversal of Babel: the gift removed the language hindrance to the gospel and proved the speakers' authority was from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Peter, standing up with the eleven, lifted up his voice to the mockers and the amazed alike. Henry reads the sermon as proof Peter was thoroughly recovered from his fall and restored to divine favour: he who had denied Christ now boldly confessed him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Peter declares this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel. Henry stresses that Peter, though filled with the Spirit and speaking with tongues, did not set aside the Scriptures: the pouring forth of the Spirit was the fulfilling of Scripture and the fruit of Christ's resurrection and ascension. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. In the last days God would pour out his Spirit upon all flesh, and sons and daughters would prophesy. Henry treats this pouring forth as the fulfilling of Scripture and the fruit of Christ's resurrection and ascension, given so hearers would embrace the faith of Christ and join his church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. God's servants and handmaidens would prophesy in those days as the Spirit was poured out. Henry presents this as part of the same scriptural fulfilment, the miraculous pouring forth that proves Christ's resurrection and ascension. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved. Henry draws the warning plainly: none will escape the condemnation of the great day except those who call upon the name of the Lord, in and through his Son Jesus Christ, as Saviour of sinners and Judge of all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Peter preaches Jesus of Nazareth, a man approved of God by miracles, wonders and signs. Henry calls this the history of Christ, beginning from the gift of the Holy Ghost and moving to the death and sufferings the hearers had witnessed but a few weeks before. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Jesus was delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God, yet taken and crucified by wicked hands. Henry holds both sides together: as God's act the death was of wonderful grace and wisdom under an eternal counsel, and as the people's act it was awful sin and folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. God raised Jesus up, having loosed the pains of death, because it was not possible he should be holden of it. Henry says Christ's resurrection did away the reproach of his death, and Peter speaks largely upon it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. David's words are applied to Christ: God would not leave his soul in hell, neither suffer his Holy One to see corruption. Henry identifies Christ as God's Holy One, sanctified and set apart to the work of redemption, whose death becomes for all his people the entrance to a blessed life for evermore. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Thou hast made known to me the ways of life, and wilt make me full of joy with thy countenance. Henry draws the encouragement: through the Saviour the ways of life are made known, and believers are encouraged to expect God's presence and favour for evermore. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. David, seeing this before, spake of the resurrection of Christ, that his soul was not left in hell, neither his flesh did see corruption. Henry says this event had taken place as foretold, and the resurrection did away the reproach of Christ's death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. This Jesus hath God raised up, whereof we all are witnesses. Henry stresses that the resurrection did not rest on prophecy alone: Christ had poured upon his disciples the miraculous gifts and divine influences whose effects the hearers themselves witnessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Exalted by the right hand of God, Jesus received the promise of the Holy Ghost and shed forth what the crowd now saw and heard. Henry treats this outpouring as proof of both resurrection and ascension, the fruit of Christ's exaltation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Therefore let all the house of Israel know assuredly that God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye crucified, both Lord and Christ. Henry sums the sermon up here: all springs from assured belief that Jesus is the Lord and the anointed Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. When they heard this they were pricked in their heart and asked what they should do. Henry says neither Peter's words nor the miracle could have produced such effects without the Holy Spirit: sinners whose eyes are opened cannot but be pricked for sin and feel an inward uneasiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Peter exhorted them to repent and be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and they would receive the gift of the Holy Ghost. Henry reads baptism as openly avowing belief in Jesus as Messiah, with remission of sins and a share in the Spirit's gifts and graces following. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. The promise is unto you and your children and to all that are afar off, as many as the Lord shall call. Henry extends the encouragement: repentance and remission are still preached to the chief of sinners, the promises are to us and our children, and the blessings are offered to all that are afar off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. With many other words Peter testified and exhorted them to save themselves from this untoward generation. Henry explains the separation: to break off from wicked people, avoiding them with dread and holy fear, is the only way to save ourselves from them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. They that gladly received his word were baptized, and about three thousand souls were added that day. Henry is careful about what gift they received: the Spirit of adoption, converting, guiding and sanctifying grace bestowed on all members of the heavenly Father's family, from which no true believer is shut out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. They continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, in breaking of bread and in prayers. Henry calls this the truly primitive church in its infancy and greatest innocence, keeping close to holy ordinances and abounding in piety and devotion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. All that believed were together and had all things common, selling possessions and parting them as every man had need. Henry explains that this did not destroy property but did away selfishness: love made every one to be to another as to himself, and God, who moved them to it, knew they were quickly to be driven from their possessions in Judea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. They sold their possessions and goods and parted them to all men as every man had need. Henry's point is charity over ownership: the Holy Ghost filled them with such love that selfishness gave way, and distribution followed need rather than claim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. Praising God and having favour with all the people, they saw the Lord add to the church daily such as should be saved. Henry stresses these were not merely professors but such as were actually brought into acceptance with God, partakers of regenerating grace, effectually brought to Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • Pentecost. The Jewish feast at which the events of this chapter take place, when devout Jews from many nations were in Jerusalem. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • cloven tongues. Tongues parted or divided, like fire, appearing over each person; Henry reads the fire as the Spirit's melting and kindling work. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • untoward generation. A crooked or perverse generation; Peter urges his hearers to separate from it. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
