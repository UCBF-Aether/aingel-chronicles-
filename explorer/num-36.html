The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 36 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the chief fathers of the families of the children of Gilead, the son of Machir, the son of Manasseh, of the families of the sons of Joseph, came near, and spake before Moses, and before the princes, the chief fathers of the children of Israel:

2 And they said, The LORD commanded my lord to give the land for an inheritance by lot to the children of Israel: and my lord was commanded by the LORD to give the inheritance of Zelophehad our brother unto his daughters.

3 And if they be married to any of the sons of the other tribes of the children of Israel, then shall their inheritance be taken from the inheritance of our fathers, and shall be put to the inheritance of the tribe whereunto they are received: so shall it be taken from the lot of our inheritance.

4 And when the jubile of the children of Israel shall be, then shall their inheritance be put unto the inheritance of the tribe whereunto they are received: so shall their inheritance be taken away from the inheritance of the tribe of our fathers.

5 And Moses commanded the children of Israel according to the word of the LORD, saying, The tribe of the sons of Joseph hath said well.

6 This is the thing which the LORD doth command concerning the daughters of Zelophehad, saying, Let them marry to whom they think best; only to the family of the tribe of their father shall they marry.

7 So shall not the inheritance of the children of Israel remove from tribe to tribe: for every one of the children of Israel shall keep himself to the inheritance of the tribe of his fathers.

8 And every daughter, that possesseth an inheritance in any tribe of the children of Israel, shall be wife unto one of the family of the tribe of her father, that the children of Israel may enjoy every man the inheritance of his fathers.

9 Neither shall the inheritance remove from one tribe to another tribe; but every one of the tribes of the children of Israel shall keep himself to his own inheritance.

10 Even as the LORD commanded Moses, so did the daughters of Zelophehad:

11 For Mahlah, Tirzah, and Hoglah, and Milcah, and Noah, the daughters of Zelophehad, were married unto their father’s brothers’ sons:

12 And they were married into the families of the sons of Manasseh the son of Joseph, and their inheritance remained in the tribe of the family of their father.

13 These are the commandments and the judgments, which the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses unto the children of Israel in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter closes Numbers by finishing the case of Zelophehad's daughters. The heads of Manasseh point out that if the heiresses marry outside the tribe, their inheritance will pass to another tribe when the jubilee comes, diminishing Manasseh's portion. Moses commands that they marry only within their father's tribe, so that no inheritance moves from tribe to tribe, and the five daughters marry their father's brothers' sons. The book ends where its laws were given: these are the commandments and judgments commanded by Moses to Israel in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Inheritance of the tribe. The family's share of land, which must not pass from tribe to tribe; heiresses therefore marry within their father's tribe. Interpreted Chapter text in the Scripture panel above

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And the chief fathers of the families of Gilead, son of Machir, son of Manasseh, of the families of Joseph, came near, and spoke before Moses, and before the princes, the chief fathers of Israel. JFB notes these tribal governors in Manasseh consulted Moses on a case affecting the public honour and interests of their tribe, relating once more to the daughters of Zelophehad, formerly applicants in their own instance for recognition as heirs, now the subject of an application on the tribe's behalf to prevent alienation of their patrimony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And they said, the Lord commanded my lord to give the land for an inheritance by lot to Israel, and my lord was commanded by the Lord to give the inheritance of Zelophehad our brother to his daughters. The chief fathers rehearsed the two commands together: the land by lot, and Zelophehad's inheritance to his daughters, whose interaction now needed settling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And if they be married to any of the sons of the other tribes of Israel, then shall their inheritance be taken from the inheritance of our fathers, and shall be put to the inheritance of the tribe whereunto they are received: so shall it be taken from the lot of our inheritance. JFB notes the unrestricted marriages of daughters in such circumstances threatened seriously to affect the tenure of land in Israel, since their inheritance would go to their children, who by the father's side would belong to another tribe, leading through complication of interests and confusion of families to an evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. And when the jubile of Israel shall be, then shall their inheritance be put to the inheritance of the tribe whereunto they are received: so shall their inheritance be taken away from the inheritance of the tribe of our fathers. Henry notes the heads of Manasseh sought to preserve the divine appointment of inheritances and prevent contests and quarrels among those who should come afterwards, and JFB adds the evil was one for which even the jubilee could not afford a remedy, since the jubilee would fix the alienation permanently. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Moses commanded Israel according to the word of the Lord, saying, the tribe of the sons of Joseph have said well. JFB notes the plea appeared just and reasonable, and accordingly an enactment was made, an instance of progressive legislation in Israel, whose enactments were suggested by circumstances, though additions or modifications of the law were confined to civil affairs, while the slightest change was inadmissible in laws relating to worship or religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. This is the thing which the Lord does command concerning the daughters of Zelophehad, saying, let them marry to whom they think best, only to the family of the tribe of their father shall they marry. JFB notes the daughters, while left to free choice of husbands, were restricted to marry not only within their own tribe but within the family of their father's tribe, that is, one of their cousins, and this restriction was imposed only on heiresses, since other daughters might marry into another tribe but were liable to forfeit their patrimonial inheritance, which on the father's or brothers' death went to the nearest family kinsman. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. So shall not the inheritance of Israel remove from tribe to tribe: for every one of Israel shall keep himself to the inheritance of the tribe of his fathers. Henry notes God would not have one tribe enriched at the expense of another, and each tribe was to keep to its own inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And every daughter, that possesses an inheritance in any tribe of Israel, shall be wife to one of the family of the tribe of her father, that Israel may enjoy every man the inheritance of his fathers. The rule was generalized to every heiress daughter in any tribe, so that every man of Israel might enjoy his fathers' inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Neither shall the inheritance remove from one tribe to another tribe, but every one of the tribes of Israel shall keep himself to his own inheritance. The rule was restated as a standing guard: no inheritance was to remove from tribe to tribe, and every tribe was to keep its own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Even as the Lord commanded Moses, so did the daughters of Zelophehad. The daughters submitted to the appointment, and Henry asks how they could fail to marry well when God himself directed them, adding that all our intentions and inclinations ought to be subjected to God's will when it is made known, especially in contracting marriage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. For Mahlah, Tirzah, and Hoglah, and Milcah, and Noah, the daughters of Zelophehad, were married to their father's brothers' sons. The five daughters married their father's brothers' sons, their cousins within Manasseh, exactly as the command required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And they were married into the families of the sons of Manasseh son of Joseph, and their inheritance remained in the tribe of the family of their father. Married within Manasseh's families, their inheritance remained in the tribe of their father's family, and the tribe's patrimony was preserved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. These are the commandments and the judgments, which the Lord commanded by the hand of Moses to Israel in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho. JFB notes the encampment stood on an extensive plateau north of the Arnon, wrested from the Moabites by Sihon and Og yet retaining its original possessors' name, the site now called El-Koura, a large plain near Nebo between the Arnon and the Wael, a desert plain on the eastern bank marked by groves of wild thorny acacia, while Henry closes that most of these judgments related to the settlement in Canaan into which Israel was now entering, and whatever new condition God's providence brings us into, we must beg him to teach us its duties, that we may do the work of the day in its day, the duty of a place in its place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
