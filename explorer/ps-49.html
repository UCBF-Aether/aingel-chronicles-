The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 49 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Hear this, all ye people; give ear, all ye inhabitants of the world:

2 Both low and high, rich and poor, together.

3 My mouth shall speak of wisdom; and the meditation of my heart shall be of understanding.

4 I will incline mine ear to a parable: I will open my dark saying upon the harp.

5 Wherefore should I fear in the days of evil, when the iniquity of my heels shall compass me about?

6 They that trust in their wealth, and boast themselves in the multitude of their riches;

7 None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him:

8 (For the redemption of their soul is precious, and it ceaseth for ever:)

9 That he should still live for ever, and not see corruption.

10 For he seeth that wise men die, likewise the fool and the brutish person perish, and leave their wealth to others.

11 Their inward thought is, that their houses shall continue for ever, and their dwelling places to all generations; they call their lands after their own names.

12 Nevertheless man being in honour abideth not: he is like the beasts that perish.

13 This their way is their folly: yet their posterity approve their sayings. Selah.

14 Like sheep they are laid in the grave; death shall feed on them; and the upright shall have dominion over them in the morning; and their beauty shall consume in the grave from their dwelling.

15 But God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave: for he shall receive me. Selah.

16 Be not thou afraid when one is made rich, when the glory of his house is increased;

17 For when he dieth he shall carry nothing away: his glory shall not descend after him.

18 Though while he lived he blessed his soul: and men will praise thee, when thou doest well to thyself.

19 He shall go to the generation of his fathers; they shall never see light.

20 Man that is in honour, and understandeth not, is like the beasts that perish.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A wisdom psalm for all people, low and high, rich and poor: wealth cannot redeem a brother or pay God a ransom, for the soul's redemption is too precious. Wise, fool, and brutish alike die and leave wealth to others, like sheep laid in the grave, but God will redeem my soul from the grave, for he shall receive me, and when a man dies he carries nothing away. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hear this, all you people; give ear, all inhabitants of the world: the audience is universal — all peoples and all the world's inhabitants are summoned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Both low and high, rich and poor, together: low and high, rich and poor together — no rank is exempt from the lesson. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. My mouth shall speak of wisdom, and the meditation of my heart shall be of understanding: mouth and heart agree — spoken wisdom from meditated understanding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. I will incline my ear to a parable; I will open my dark saying upon the harp: a parable and a dark saying, opened on the harp, are the form of the wisdom — truth sung, not merely stated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Wherefore should I fear in the days of evil, when the iniquity of my heels shall compass me about? Fear in evil days, when heel-iniquity — sins dogging his steps, or supplanters at his heels — compasses him, is questioned as needless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They that trust in their wealth, and boast themselves in the multitude of their riches: wealth-trust and riches-boasting are the folly to be exposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him: no wealth can redeem a brother or pay God a ransom — redemption is beyond purchase. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For the redemption of their soul is precious, and it ceases for ever: the soul's redemption is so precious that the price ceases for ever — it cannot be paid. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. That he should still live for ever, and not see corruption: the unattainable aim is stated — to live for ever and never see corruption, which wealth cannot buy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For he sees that wise men die, likewise the fool and the brutish person perish, and leave their wealth to others: wise, fool, and brutish alike die and leave wealth to others — death levels wisdom and folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Their inward thought is that their houses shall continue for ever, and their dwelling places to all generations; they call their lands after their own names: inwardly they dream of everlasting houses and named lands — a counterfeit eternity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Nevertheless man being in honour abides not; he is like the beasts that perish: honour without abiding makes man like perishing beasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. This their way is their folly, yet their posterity approve their sayings: the way is folly, yet posterity approves — folly is inherited as wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Like sheep they are laid in the grave; death shall feed on them; and the upright shall have dominion over them in the morning; and their beauty shall consume in the grave from their dwelling: sheep in the grave, death as shepherd feeding on them, the upright ruling in the morning, beauty consumed — the reversal is total. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. But God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave, for he shall receive me: against wealth's failure stands God's redemption from the grave's power, and his receiving — the psalm's hope of life beyond death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Be not afraid when one is made rich, when the glory of his house is increased: fear at another's riches and increased house-glory is forbidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For when he dies he shall carry nothing away; his glory shall not descend after him: death strips him — nothing carried, glory not descending. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Though while he lived he blessed his soul, and men will praise you when you do well to yourself: self-blessing in life and men's praise for self-service are the rich man's portion, and it ends at death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. He shall go to the generation of his fathers; they shall never see light: he joins his fathers' generation, who never see light — darkness is the end of the unredeemed rich. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Man that is in honour, and understands not, is like the beasts that perish: the refrain returns with its key — honour without understanding is beast-like perishing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
