The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Didascalia 27 (J. M. Harden, The Ethiopic Didascalia (London: SPCK, 1920), public domain (Cornell University Library scan, archive.org cu31924096083336))

*THAT CHRISTIANS OUGHT NOT TO ENTER A HOUSE OF REVELRY AND DANCING, BUT (OUGHT) RATHER TO GIVE PRAISE AND SING IN THE CHURCH.'*

1 BEHOLD, beloved brethren, we give you good counsel, that ye keep far from impure speech and hearing of songs, and from dainty meats and luxury and from all works of vanity wherein is no profit Work not the pleasure of the desire of your flesh on the Sabbath day, for the prophet saith, ‘Serve the Lord with fear and rejoice unto Him with trembling.” For it beseemeth not a Christian to listen to singing and music and playing on instruments, as the foolish people do.

2 And moreover, let them not take on their lips the names of the (false) gods and idols, nor name the name of Satan and his angels, for, when they name his name, the Holy Spirit departeth from them, and in His stead an unclean spirit abideth on them. ;

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In plain language: this short chapter counsels Christians to keep far from houses of revelry and dancing, impure speech, songs, dainty meats, luxury and all works of vanity with no profit, and not to work the pleasure of the flesh on the Sabbath day. Instead they should give praise and sing in the Church, and not take the names of false gods, idols, Satan or his angels on their lips, lest, it says, the Holy Spirit depart and an unclean spirit abide in their stead.
