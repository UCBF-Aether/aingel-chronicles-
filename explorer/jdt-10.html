The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judith 10 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Now after that she had ceased to cry unto the God of Israel, and bad made an end of all these words.

2 She rose where she had fallen down, and called her maid, and went down into the house in the which she abode in the sabbath days, and in her feast days,

3 And pulled off the sackcloth which she had on, and put off the garments of her widowhood, and washed her body all over with water, and anointed herself with precious ointment, and braided the hair of her head, and put on a tire upon it, and put on her garments of gladness, wherewith she was clad during the life of Manasses her husband.

4 And she took sandals upon her feet, and put about her her bracelets, and her chains, and her rings, and her earrings, and all her ornaments, and decked herself bravely, to allure the eyes of all men that should see her.

5 Then she gave her maid a bottle of wine, and a cruse of oil, and filled a bag with parched corn, and lumps of figs, and with fine bread; so she folded all these things together, and laid them upon her.

6 Thus they went forth to the gate of the city of Bethulia, and found standing there Ozias and the ancients of the city, Chabris and Charmis.

7 And when they saw her, that her countenance was altered, and her apparel was changed, they wondered at her beauty very greatly, and said unto her.

8 The God, the God of our fathers give thee favour, and accomplish thine enterprizes to the glory of the children of Israel, and to the exaltation of Jerusalem. Then they worshipped God.

9 And she said unto them, Command the gates of the city to be opened unto me, that I may go forth to accomplish the things whereof ye have spoken with me. So they commanded the young men to open unto her, as she had spoken.

10 And when they had done so, Judith went out, she, and her maid with her; and the men of the city looked after her, until she was gone down the mountain, and till she had passed the valley, and could see her no more.

11 Thus they went straight forth in the valley: and the first watch of the Assyrians met her,

12 And took her, and asked her, Of what people art thou? and whence comest thou? and whither goest thou? And she said, I am a woman of the Hebrews, and am fled from them: for they shall be given you to be consumed:

13 And I am coming before Holofernes the chief captain of your army, to declare words of truth; and I will shew him a way, whereby he shall go, and win all the hill country, without losing the body or life of any one of his men.

14 Now when the men heard her words, and beheld her countenance, they wondered greatly at her beauty, and said unto her,

15 Thou hast saved thy life, in that thou hast hasted to come down to the presence of our lord: now therefore come to his tent, and some of us shall conduct thee, until they have delivered thee to his hands.

16 And when thou standest before him, be not afraid in thine heart, but shew unto him according to thy word; and he will entreat thee well.

17 Then they chose out of them an hundred men to accompany her and her maid; and they brought her to the tent of Holofernes.

18 Then was there a concourse throughout all the camp: for her coming was noised among the tents, and they came about her, as she stood without the tent of Holofernes, till they told him of her.

19 And they wondered at her beauty, and admired the children of Israel because of her, and every one said to his neighbour, Who would despise this people, that have among them such women? surely it is not good that one man of them be left who being let go might deceive the whole earth.

20 And they that lay near Holofernes went out, and all his servants and they brought her into the tent.

21 Now Holofernes rested upon his bed under a canopy, which was woven with purple, and gold, and emeralds, and precious stones.

22 So they shewed him of her; and he came out before his tent with silver lamps going before him.

23 And when Judith was come before him and his servants they all marvelled at the beauty of her countenance; and she fell down upon her face, and did reverence unto him: and his servants took her up.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

Having ended her prayer, Judith lays aside her widow's garments and sackcloth, washes, anoints herself, dresses in her finest apparel, and, with her maid carrying provisions, passes the gates and the guards, who marvel at her beauty. She is brought before Holofernes, falls on her face and does him reverence, and his servants lift her up as all marvel at the beauty of her countenance. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Widow's garments. the sackcloth and mourning dress Judith lays aside before going out to the camp (verse 2). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Maid. Judith's waiting-woman, who carries her provisions and accompanies her (verse 5). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. She rose where she had fallen down, and called her maid, and went down into the house in which she abode in the sabbath days and in her feast days; the Greek adds, "where she spent the sabbath and festival days, she rolled up her" sackcloth and widow's garments — she remained in her oratory therefore only on other days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. And pulled off the sackcloth which she had on, and put off the garments of her widowhood, and washed her body all over with water, and anointed herself with precious ointment, and combed her hair, and put on her bonnet; "body" is in Complutensian Greek "mouth," but other editions seem more accurate, and "best" ointment is in Greek "thick," probably the myrobalanum of Pliny. The bonnet, or mitre, was tied with ribbands hanging down behind, like those of bishops. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. And she took sandals upon her feet, and put about her her bracelets, and her chains, and her rings, and her earrings, and all her ornaments, and decked herself to be admired; sandals were highly ornamented and worn by people of quality, and lilies hung pendent from the neck. The Greek reads, "and she was richly adorned, so that she might captivate (literally, deceive) the eyes of whatever men should behold her." The fathers highly extol her virtue, and she was no doubt actuated by the purest motives, yet she might be guilty of some indiscretion; God inspired her with the laudable design of freeing her country, but the means were left to her own choice. The making use of rich attire was not, however, sinful in itself, and we have no right to suspect that Judith gave way either to vanity or to sensuality; provided they observe due modesty, who will blame ladies of quality for rich attire or perfumes? Judith might lawfully desire to marry Holofernes for the good of her nation, as Esther became the wife of Assuerus, though an infidel, and the general seemed even willing to become a convert (chapter 11:21). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. Then she gave her maid a bottle of wine, and a cruse of oil, and filled a bag with parched corn, and lumps of figs, and with fine bread; the bottle was made of leather, used for carrying wine, the corn was eaten either steeped in water or mixed with oil, and "cheese" is so read in Syriac while Greek reads "pure loaves." Why she took her provisions with her, she explains in chapter 12:2. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. And when they saw her, that her countenance was altered, and her apparel was changed, they wondered at her beauty very greatly; the Greek adds, "with her countenance and robes changed." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. The God, the God of our fathers give thee favour, and accomplish thine enterprises to the glory of the children of Israel, and to the exaltation of Jerusalem; the Greek reads, "And they said to her, May God, the God of" our fathers give thee favour, "For the glory of the Israelites, and the exaltation of Jerusalem; and they adored God." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. And she said unto them, "Command the gates of the city to be opened unto me, that I may go forth to accomplish the things whereof ye have spoken with me"; the Greek continues, "And she said to them, Order the gate of the city to be opened for me, and I will go out to accomplish what you were talking about to me. And they commanded the young men to open for her; and they did so." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. And when they had done so, Judith went out, she, and her maid with her, and the men of the city looked after her, until she was gone down the mountain, and had passed the valley; the Greek continues, "But Judith and her maid went out. And the citizens looked at her while she descended the hill, till she had passed the valley, when they lost sight of her; and they passed along straight through the vale." It appears that there was still day-light, or the moon shone brightly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. And took her, and asked her, "Of what people art thou? and whence comest thou? and whither goest thou?" And she said, "I am a woman of the Hebrews, and am fled from them"; the Greek reads, "Because they are about to be given up a prey to you. Therefore, I come to Holofernes, general in chief of your army, to inform him of the truth." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. And I am coming before Holofernes the chief captain of your army, to declare words of truth, and I will show him a way whereby he shall go and win all the hill country; in this and the following chapter, some things related to have been said by Judith seem hard to reconcile with truth, but all that is related in Scripture of God's servants is not approved by Scripture, and even saints in their enterprises may sometimes slip into venial sins. By means of mental reservation we may exculpate her from lying, but this expedient is bad and childish; she might speak ironically or prophetically, or it might be an untruth to be excused like stratagems in war — how often does an enemy give out that he is going to march to some place, when he intends to go quite another way! She spoke many things certainly true, and others which would probably have taken place if God had not sent relief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. Thou hast saved thy life, in that thou hast hasted to come down to the presence of our lord: now therefore come to his tent, and some of us shall conduct thee unto him; the Greek adds, "and now proceed to his tent, and some of us shall give thee into his hands." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. And when thou standest before him, be not afraid in thine heart, but show unto him according to thy word, and he will entreat thee well; the Greek continues, "But if thou stand before him, let not thy heart fear, but tell him this, and he will treat thee well." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 17. Then they chose out of them an hundred men to accompany her and her maid, and they brought her to the tent of Holofernes; the Greek continues, "And they selected 100 men, and surrounded her and her servant-maid, and conducted her to the tent of Holofernes." Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 18. Then was there a concourse throughout all the camp, for her coming was noised among the tents, and they came about her, as she stood without the tent of Holofernes, till they had told him of her; the Greek continues, "And they ran from all parts of the camp, as soon as her coming was made known, and they came round her as she stood without the tent of Holofernes, till they had told him of her. And they were astonished at her beauty, and at the Israelites, and said to each other, Who," and so on. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 19. And they wondered at her beauty, and admired the children of Israel because of her, and every one said to his neighbour, "Who would despise this people, that have among them such women?"; the Greek reads, "For it is not expedient to leave one man of them alive, lest they should delude all the earth" (the Old Vulgate omits the negation). Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. And they that lay near Holofernes went out, and all his servants, and they brought her into the tent; the Greek reads, "And all who slept near Holofernes, and all his servants, came out and introduced her into the tent," which was divided into two apartments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 21. Now Holofernes rested upon his bed under a canopy, which was woven with purple, and gold, and emeralds, and precious stones; the Greek reads that Holofernes "was reposing on a bed in the canopy (or inner room)," and the curtain was intended to keep off gnats — a delicacy Mark Antony imitated, as Horace notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. So they showed him of her, and he came out before his tent with silver lamps going before him; the Greek reads, "And they told him of her, and he came out to the forepart of the tent, and silver lamps preceded him," either for grandeur, as they did the kings of Persia and the emperors of Rome and of the Turks, or because it was still dark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 23. And when Judith was come before him and his servants they all marvelled at the beauty of her countenance, and she fell down upon her face, and did reverence unto him; the Greek reads, "But as soon as Judith came in sight of him and of his ministers, they all were astonished at the beauty of her face; and falling prostrate, she adored him (with civil respect) and his servants lifted her up." St. Fulgentius judges, with great probability, that Judith was now about 40 years old. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
