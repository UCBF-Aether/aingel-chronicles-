The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jeremiah 14 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 The word of the LORD that came to Jeremiah concerning the dearth.

2 Judah mourneth, and the gates thereof languish; they are black unto the ground; and the cry of Jerusalem is gone up.

3 And their nobles have sent their little ones to the waters: they came to the pits, and found no water; they returned with their vessels empty; they were ashamed and confounded, and covered their heads.

4 Because the ground is chapt, for there was no rain in the earth, the plowmen were ashamed, they covered their heads.

5 Yea, the hind also calved in the field, and forsook it, because there was no grass.

6 And the wild asses did stand in the high places, they snuffed up the wind like dragons; their eyes did fail, because there was no grass.

7 O LORD, though our iniquities testify against us, do thou it for thy name’s sake: for our backslidings are many; we have sinned against thee.

8 O the hope of Israel, the saviour thereof in time of trouble, why shouldest thou be as a stranger in the land, and as a wayfaring man that turneth aside to tarry for a night?

9 Why shouldest thou be as a man astonied, as a mighty man that cannot save? yet thou, O LORD, art in the midst of us, and we are called by thy name; leave us not.

10 Thus saith the LORD unto this people, Thus have they loved to wander, they have not refrained their feet, therefore the LORD doth not accept them; he will now remember their iniquity, and visit their sins.

11 Then said the LORD unto me, Pray not for this people for their good.

12 When they fast, I will not hear their cry; and when they offer burnt offering and an oblation, I will not accept them: but I will consume them by the sword, and by the famine, and by the pestilence.

13 Then said I, Ah, Lord GOD! behold, the prophets say unto them, Ye shall not see the sword, neither shall ye have famine; but I will give you assured peace in this place.

14 Then the LORD said unto me, The prophets prophesy lies in my name: I sent them not, neither have I commanded them, neither spake unto them: they prophesy unto you a false vision and divination, and a thing of nought, and the deceit of their heart.

15 Therefore thus saith the LORD concerning the prophets that prophesy in my name, and I sent them not, yet they say, Sword and famine shall not be in this land; By sword and famine shall those prophets be consumed.

16 And the people to whom they prophesy shall be cast out in the streets of Jerusalem because of the famine and the sword; and they shall have none to bury them, them, their wives, nor their sons, nor their daughters: for I will pour their wickedness upon them.

17 Therefore thou shalt say this word unto them; Let mine eyes run down with tears night and day, and let them not cease: for the virgin daughter of my people is broken with a great breach, with a very grievous blow.

18 If I go forth into the field, then behold the slain with the sword! and if I enter into the city, then behold them that are sick with famine! yea, both the prophet and the priest go about into a land that they know not.

19 Hast thou utterly rejected Judah? hath thy soul lothed Zion? why hast thou smitten us, and there is no healing for us? we looked for peace, and there is no good; and for the time of healing, and behold trouble!

20 We acknowledge, O LORD, our wickedness, and the iniquity of our fathers: for we have sinned against thee.

21 Do not abhor us, for thy name’s sake, do not disgrace the throne of thy glory: remember, break not thy covenant with us.

22 Are there any among the vanities of the Gentiles that can cause rain? or can the heavens give showers? art not thou he, O LORD our God? therefore we will wait upon thee: for thou hast made all these things.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The word concerning the dearth opens with Judah mourning and Jerusalem's cry going up as drought empties the land. False prophets promise peace while sword and famine are at work, Jeremiah intercedes, and the chapter closes by asking whether any vanity of the Gentiles can cause rain — only the Lord can give showers. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The word of the Lord that came to Jeremiah concerning the dearth. Henry's chapter is a drought upon the land of Judah: the word concerning the dearth opens tears, confession, and supplication over a land without rain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Judah mourns, and the gates thereof languish; they are black unto the ground; and the cry of Jerusalem is gone up. Henry notes the people were in tears, but it was rather the cry of their trouble, and of their sin, than of their prayer: Judah mourns, gates languish, and Jerusalem's cry goes up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. And their nobles have sent their little ones to the waters: they came to the pits, and found no water; they returned with their vessels empty; they were ashamed and confounded, and covered their heads. Henry urges us to be thankful for the mercy of water, that we may not be taught to value it by feeling the want of it: nobles' children return with empty vessels, ashamed and confounded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Because the ground is chapt, for there was no rain in the earth, the plowmen were ashamed, they covered their heads. Henry notes the dependence husbandmen have upon divine providence: they cannot plough nor sow in hope, unless God water their furrows, and with chapt ground the plowmen are ashamed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Yea, the hind also calved in the field, and forsook it, because there was no grass. Henry notes the case even of the wild beasts was very pitiable: the hind forsakes her newborn for want of grass, and the drought reaches beyond men to beasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. And the wild asses did stand in the high places, they snuffed up the wind like dragons; their eyes did fail, because there was no grass. Henry's wild asses, snuffing wind with failing eyes for want of grass, complete the pitiable case of the wild beasts under the dearth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. O Lord, though our iniquities testify against us, do thou it for thy name's sake: for our backslidings are many; we have sinned against thee. Henry notes the people are not forward to pray, but the prophet prays for them: sin is humbly confessed, and our sins not only accuse us, but answer against us, yet the plea is for God's name's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. O the hope of Israel, the saviour thereof in time of trouble, why shouldest thou be as a stranger in the land, and as a wayfaring man that turneth aside to tarry for a night? Henry notes our best pleas in prayer are those fetched from the glory of God's own name, and we should dread God's departure more than the removal of our creature-comforts: the hope of Israel must not become a stranger in his own land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Why shouldest thou be as a man astonied, as a mighty man that cannot save? Yet thou, O Lord, art in the midst of us, and we are called by thy name; leave us not. Henry notes he has given Israel his word to hope in, and it becomes us in prayer to show ourselves more concerned for God's glory than for our own comfort: the Lord in the midst of his people, whose name they bear, must not leave them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Thus says the Lord unto this people: Thus have they loved to wander, they have not refrained their feet, therefore the Lord doth not accept them; he will now remember their iniquity, and visit their sins. Henry notes the Lord calls the Jews 'this people,' not 'his people': they had forsaken his service, therefore he would punish them according to their sins, for they loved to wander and would not refrain their feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Then said the Lord unto me: Pray not for this people for their good. Henry notes he forbade Jeremiah to plead for them: the prophet who prayed for them in the drought is now forbidden to pray for their good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. When they fast, I will not hear their cry; and when they offer burnt offering and an oblation, I will not accept them: but I will consume them by the sword, and by the famine, and by the pestilence. Henry's divine purpose to punish is declared: fasting unheard, offerings unaccepted, and consumption by sword, famine, and pestilence instead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Then said I: Ah, Lord God! Behold, the prophets say unto them, Ye shall not see the sword, neither shall ye have famine; but I will give you assured peace in this place. Henry notes false teachers encourage men to expect peace and salvation, without repentance, faith, conversion, and holiness of life: the prophets' assured peace is the lie Jeremiah lays before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Then the Lord said unto me: The prophets prophesy lies in my name: I sent them not, neither have I commanded them, neither spake unto them: they prophesy unto you a false vision and divination, and a thing of nought, and the deceit of their heart. Henry notes the false prophets were the most criminal: the Lord pronounces condemnation on them, for they prophesy lies in his name, unsent and uncommanded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Therefore thus says the Lord concerning the prophets that prophesy in my name, and I sent them not, yet they say, Sword and famine shall not be in this land: By sword and famine shall those prophets be consumed. Henry warns those who believe a lie must not plead it for an excuse, and they shall feel what they say they will not fear: the prophets of no sword and no famine shall themselves be consumed by sword and famine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. And the people to whom they prophesy shall be cast out in the streets of Jerusalem because of the famine and the sword; and they shall have none to bury them, them, their wives, nor their sons, nor their daughters: for I will pour their wickedness upon them. Henry notes that as the people loved to have it so, they were not to escape judgements: the deceived, loving the lie, share the casting out, the famine, and the sword. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Therefore thou shalt say this word unto them: Let mine eyes run down with tears night and day, and let them not cease: for the virgin daughter of my people is broken with a great breach, with a very grievous blow. Henry notes Jeremiah acknowledged his own sins, and those of the people, but pleaded with the Lord to remember his covenant: instead of interceding only, he is told to lament, with tears night and day for the broken virgin daughter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. If I go forth into the field, then behold the slain with the sword! And if I enter into the city, then behold them that are sick with famine! Yea, both the prophet and the priest go about into a land that they know not. Henry's supplication sees sword in the field and famine in the city, with prophet and priest wandering in a land they know not: the distress is total, and no idol of the Gentiles could help them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Hast thou utterly rejected Judah? Hath thy soul lothed Zion? Why hast thou smitten us, and there is no healing for us? We looked for peace, and there is no good; and for the time of healing, and behold trouble! Henry's plea remembers the covenant even while asking whether Judah is utterly rejected: looked-for peace has become trouble, and no healing has come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. We acknowledge, O Lord, our wickedness, and the iniquity of our fathers: for we have sinned against thee. Henry notes the Lord will heal every truly repenting sinner: the acknowledgement of our wickedness and our fathers' iniquity is the confession that opens healing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Do not abhor us, for thy name's sake, do not disgrace the throne of thy glory: remember, break not thy covenant with us. Henry notes Jeremiah pleaded with the Lord to remember his covenant: the plea is again for God's name's sake and his throne of glory, that the covenant not be broken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Are there any among the vanities of the Gentiles that can cause rain? Or can the heavens give showers? Art not thou he, O Lord our God? Therefore we will wait upon thee: for thou hast made all these things. Henry closes: in their distress none of the idols of the Gentiles could help them, nor could the heavens give rain of themselves; the Lord will always have a people to plead with him at his mercy-seat, and should he not see fit to hear our prayers on behalf of our guilty land, he will certainly bless with salvation all who confess their sins and seek his mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
