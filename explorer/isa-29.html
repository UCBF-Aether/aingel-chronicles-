The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Isaiah 29 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Woe to Ariel, to Ariel, the city where David dwelt! add ye year to year; let them kill sacrifices.

2 Yet I will distress Ariel, and there shall be heaviness and sorrow: and it shall be unto me as Ariel.

3 And I will camp against thee round about, and will lay siege against thee with a mount, and I will raise forts against thee.

4 And thou shalt be brought down, and shalt speak out of the ground, and thy speech shall be low out of the dust, and thy voice shall be, as of one that hath a familiar spirit, out of the ground, and thy speech shall whisper out of the dust.

5 Moreover the multitude of thy strangers shall be like small dust, and the multitude of the terrible ones shall be as chaff that passeth away: yea, it shall be at an instant suddenly.

6 Thou shalt be visited of the LORD of hosts with thunder, and with earthquake, and great noise, with storm and tempest, and the flame of devouring fire.

7 And the multitude of all the nations that fight against Ariel, even all that fight against her and her munition, and that distress her, shall be as a dream of a night vision.

8 It shall even be as when an hungry man dreameth, and, behold, he eateth; but he awaketh, and his soul is empty: or as when a thirsty man dreameth, and, behold, he drinketh; but he awaketh, and, behold, he is faint, and his soul hath appetite: so shall the multitude of all the nations be, that fight against mount Zion.

9 Stay yourselves, and wonder; cry ye out, and cry: they are drunken, but not with wine; they stagger, but not with strong drink.

10 For the LORD hath poured out upon you the spirit of deep sleep, and hath closed your eyes: the prophets and your rulers, the seers hath he covered.

11 And the vision of all is become unto you as the words of a book that is sealed, which men deliver to one that is learned, saying, Read this, I pray thee: and he saith, I cannot; for it is sealed:

12 And the book is delivered to him that is not learned, saying, Read this, I pray thee: and he saith, I am not learned.

13 Wherefore the Lord said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men:

14 Therefore, behold, I will proceed to do a marvellous work among this people, even a marvellous work and a wonder: for the wisdom of their wise men shall perish, and the understanding of their prudent men shall be hid.

15 Woe unto them that seek deep to hide their counsel from the LORD, and their works are in the dark, and they say, Who seeth us? and who knoweth us?

16 Surely your turning of things upside down shall be esteemed as the potter’s clay: for shall the work say of him that made it, He made me not? or shall the thing framed say of him that framed it, He had no understanding?

17 Is it not yet a very little while, and Lebanon shall be turned into a fruitful field, and the fruitful field shall be esteemed as a forest?

18 And in that day shall the deaf hear the words of the book, and the eyes of the blind shall see out of obscurity, and out of darkness.

19 The meek also shall increase their joy in the LORD, and the poor among men shall rejoice in the Holy One of Israel.

20 For the terrible one is brought to nought, and the scorner is consumed, and all that watch for iniquity are cut off:

21 That make a man an offender for a word, and lay a snare for him that reproveth in the gate, and turn aside the just for a thing of nought.

22 Therefore thus saith the LORD, who redeemed Abraham, concerning the house of Jacob, Jacob shall not now be ashamed, neither shall his face now wax pale.

23 But when he seeth his children, the work of mine hands, in the midst of him, they shall sanctify my name, and sanctify the Holy One of Jacob, and shall fear the God of Israel.

24 They also that erred in spirit shall come to understanding, and they that murmured shall learn doctrine.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Woe to Ariel, Jerusalem, the city where David dwelt: siege and distress are coming, yet in an instant the enemy host will vanish like a dream. The deliverance opens blind eyes and deaf ears, the meek increase their joy, and those who erred in spirit come to understanding. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Woe to Ariel, to Ariel, the city where David dwelt! Add year to year, and let them kill sacrifices. This chapter opens the series on Sennacherib's invasion of Judea and its deliverance. Ariel is Jerusalem, meaning Lion of God, the city rendered by God invincible, the lion being emblem of a mighty hero, or Hearth of God, the place where altar-fire continually burns to God. Add year to year is ironical: suffer one year after another to glide on in the round of formal, heartless sacrifices, or add yet another year to the one just closed, and let the beasts of another year go round — then God will distress Ariel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Yet God will distress Ariel, and there will be heaviness and sorrow, and it will be unto him as Ariel. Then, after the year's round, God will distress Ariel with groaning and moaning, preserving the Hebrew paronomasia, and it will be as Ariel: either the city will be as a lion of God, emerging unvanquished, or as the altar of burnt offering consuming the besiegers with fire, or best, since verse 3 continues the threat and deliverance does not come till verse 4, like a hearth of burning, a scene of devastation by fire. The prophecy ultimately contemplates besides Sennacherib's affliction and deliverance, Rome's destruction of Jerusalem, the Jews' dispersion, their restoration, their besiegers' destruction, and Israel's final glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God will camp against you round about, and lay siege against you with a mount, and raise forts against you. The I is Jehovah acting through the Assyrian, his instrument; the mount is an artificial mound formed to out-top high walls, or a station of warriors for the siege; round about was not fully realised under Sennacherib but in the Roman siege, and the forts are siege-towers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. You will be brought down, and speak out of the ground, and your speech will be low out of the dust, and your voice will be, as of one that has a familiar spirit, out of the ground, and your speech will whisper out of the dust. Jerusalem will be as a captive humbled to the dust, her voice coming from the earth as that of spirit-charmers or necromancers, faint and shrill as the voice of the dead was supposed to be, with ventriloquism making the voice appear to come from the earth; it is an appropriate retribution that Jerusalem, which consulted necromancers, should be made like them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Moreover the multitude of your strangers will be like small dust, and the multitude of the terrible ones will be as chaff that passes away, and it will be at an instant suddenly. Yet — yet in this extremity help will come and the enemy be scattered: the strangers are foreign invaders, and their destruction will be at an instant, in a moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. You will be visited of the Lord of hosts with thunder, and with earthquake, and great noise, with storm and tempest, and the flame of devouring fire. You is the Assyrian army, and the thunder is not literal in the Assyrians' case but figurative for an awful judgment, though the ulterior fulfilment against the Jews' foes in the last days may be more literal, including earthquake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The multitude of all the nations that fight against Ariel, even all that fight against her and her munition, and that distress her, will be as a dream of a night vision. The munition is the fortress, and the whole multitude fighting against Ariel will prove as insubstantial as a night dream. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. It will be even as when a hungry man dreams, and behold, he eats, but he awakes, and his soul is empty; or as when a thirsty man dreams, and behold, he drinks, but he awakes, and behold, he is faint, and his soul has appetite: so will the multitude of all the nations be, that fight against Mount Zion. Their disappointment in the very height of confident expectation of taking Jerusalem will be as great as the hungry man's dream of eating who awakes still hungry, and the thirsty man's dream of drinking who awakes still thirsty; their dream will be dissipated on the fatal morning of the host's destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Stay yourselves, and wonder; cry out, and cry: they are drunken, but not with wine; they stagger, but not with strong drink. Stay means rather be astounded, expressing the stupid and amazed incredulity with which the Jews received Isaiah's announcement, and wonder is a threat: be astounded, since you choose to be so, at the prophecy, and soon you will be amazed at the sight of the actual event. Cry out means be blinded, since you choose to be so though light shines all round you, and soon you will be blinded in good earnest to your sorrow; they are drunken not with wine but with spiritual paralysis, and the change from speaking to them to speaking of them intimates the prophet turning away from them because of their stupid unbelief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For the Lord has poured out upon you the spirit of deep sleep, and has closed your eyes: the prophets and your rulers, the seers, has he covered. Jehovah gives them up judicially to their own hardness of heart, as Paul quotes it, and the eyes closed are the prophets, and the heads covered are the seers: Orientals cover the head to sleep, so covered parallels closed eyes, and covering the face was also preparatory to execution. This cannot apply to Isaiah's own time but to subsequent times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The vision of all is become unto you as the words of a book that is sealed, which men deliver to one that is learned, saying: Read this, I pray you, and he says: I cannot, for it is sealed. The whole vision is the same as revelation or law, and sealed recalls chapter 8: God seals up truth so that even the learned, lacking believing docility, cannot discern it, and prophecy remained comparatively a sealed volume until Jesus, who alone is worthy, opened the seals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And the book is delivered to him that is not learned, saying: Read this, I pray you, and he says: I am not learned. The unlearned succeed no better than the learned, not from want of human learning as they fancy, but from not having the teaching of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Therefore the Lord said: Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men. The precept of men replaces God's precepts given by His prophets, and their worship is external and by rule, not heartfelt as God requires, as Christ quotes this verse from the Septuagint. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Therefore, behold, I will proceed to do a marvellous work among this people, even a marvellous work and a wonder, for the wisdom of their wise men will perish, and the understanding of their prudent men will be hid. The marvellous work is one of unparalleled vengeance on the hypocrites, comparable to the strange work of chapter 28, and judgment will visit the wise in that respect in which they most pride themselves: their wisdom will be hid, no longer appearing to help the nation in distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Woe unto them that seek deep to hide their counsel from the Lord, and their works are in the dark, and they say: Who sees us? And who knows us? Seek deep to hide is rather that seek to hide deeply, and the reference is to the secret plan of many Jewish nobles to seek Egyptian aid against Assyria contrary to Isaiah's advice; at the same time the hypocrite in general is described, who under a plausible exterior tries to hide his real character not only from men but even from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Surely your turning of things upside down will be esteemed as the potter's clay, for will the work say of him that made it: He made me not? Or will the thing framed say of him that framed it: He had no understanding? Rather, Ah, your perverseness! Just as if the potter should be esteemed as the clay, for they invert the order of things, putting themselves instead of God and vice versa, as if the potter were esteemed as the clay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Is it not yet a very little while, and Lebanon will be turned into a fruitful field, and the fruitful field will be esteemed as a forest? Turned stands against their turnings of things upside down: there will be other and better turnings, the outpouring of the Spirit in the latter days, first on the Jews, followed by their national restoration, then on the Gentiles. The fruitful field is literally a Carmel, and the moral change in the Jewish nation will be as great as if wooded Lebanon became a fruitful field and vice versa: men's hearts, once a moral desert, will be reclaimed to bear fruits of righteousness, while the ungodly who seemed prosperous will be exhibited in their real barrenness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. In that day the deaf will hear the words of the book, and the eyes of the blind will see out of obscurity, and out of darkness. The blind are chiefly the spiritually blind, and the book, as Revelation is called pre-eminently, will no longer be sealed as in verse 11, but the most unintelligent will hear and see. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The meek also will increase their joy in the Lord, and the poor among men will rejoice in the Holy One of Israel. The meek are rather the afflicted godly, virtuous suffering, and the poor among men are the poorest of men, namely the pious poor, who will rejoice when they see their oppressors punished and Jehovah exhibited as their protector and rewarder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. For the terrible one is brought to nothing, and the scorner is consumed, and all that watch for iniquity are cut off. The terrible one is the persecutors among the Jewish nobles, the scorner is the scorner of chapter 28, and those who watch for iniquity not only commit it but watch for opportunities of committing it and make it their whole study. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Those that make a man an offender for a word, and lay a snare for him that reproves in the gate, and turn aside the just for a thing of naught. The reprover is rather one who pleads, one who has a suit at issue, the gate is the place of concourse where courts of justice were held, and the just is one who has a just cause, or Jesus Christ the Just One; they turn him aside through falsehood, by a decision null in justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Therefore thus says the Lord, who redeemed Abraham, concerning the house of Jacob: Jacob will not now be ashamed, neither will his face now wax pale. The Lord who redeemed Abraham out of Ur, a land of idolaters, says that after the moral revolution of verse 17, Jacob's children will no longer give cause to their forefathers to blush for them; waxing pale was with shame and disappointment at the wicked degeneracy of his posterity and fear as to their punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. But when he sees his children, the work of my hands, in the midst of him, they will sanctify my name, and sanctify the Holy One of Jacob, and fear the God of Israel. But — rather for — when Jacob sees his children, the work of God's hands spiritually as well as physically, in the midst of him, that is, in his land, or his children the Gentiles adopted among the Israelites, they will sanctify God's name; by Jehovah's agency Israel will be cleansed of corruptions and consist wholly of pious men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. They also that erred in spirit will come to understanding, and they that murmured will learn doctrine. Those who erred in spirit, as in chapter 28, will come to understanding, and those who murmured — murmuring being characteristic of Israel's rebellion — will learn doctrine, rather receive discipline or instruction, so that chastisements and the Gentiles' piety provoking the Jews to holy jealousy will produce the desired effect, and this shall be so no more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Those who erred in spirit, as in chapter 28, will come to understanding, and those who murmured — murmuring being characteristic of Israel's rebellion — will learn doctrine, rather receive discipline or instruction, so that chastisements and the Gentiles' piety provoking the Jews to holy jealousy will produce the desired effect, and this shall be so no more.
