The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Thessalonians 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Paul, and Silvanus, and Timotheus, unto the church of the Thessalonians in God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ:

2 Grace unto you, and peace, from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

3 We are bound to thank God always for you, brethren, as it is meet, because that your faith groweth exceedingly, and the charity of every one of you all toward each other aboundeth;

4 So that we ourselves glory in you in the churches of God for your patience and faith in all your persecutions and tribulations that ye endure:

5 Which is a manifest token of the righteous judgment of God, that ye may be counted worthy of the kingdom of God, for which ye also suffer:

6 Seeing it is a righteous thing with God to recompense tribulation to them that trouble you;

7 And to you who are troubled rest with us, when the Lord Jesus shall be revealed from heaven with his mighty angels,

8 In flaming fire taking vengeance on them that know not God, and that obey not the gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ:

9 Who shall be punished with everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power;

10 When he shall come to be glorified in his saints, and to be admired in all them that believe (because our testimony among you was believed) in that day.

11 Wherefore also we pray always for you, that our God would count you worthy of this calling, and fulfil all the good pleasure of his goodness, and the work of faith with power:

12 That the name of our Lord Jesus Christ may be glorified in you, and ye in him, according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul, with Silvanus and Timothy, thanks God for the Thessalonians' growing faith and love under persecution and affliction. Their endurance is presented as evidence of God's righteous judgment: rest for the afflicted when Christ is revealed, and punishment for those who do not know God or obey the gospel, while Paul prays God will count them worthy of their calling. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul writes with Silvanus and Timothy to a church that stands in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, not merely in a city or a sect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Grace comes first and peace follows it, both from the Father and from Christ, so the greeting itself teaches the order of salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Thanksgiving is a debt here, because their faith grew exceedingly and their love abounded, and where grace truly is, it increases. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Paul glories in them before other churches, since their patience and faith held under every persecution and tribulation they endured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Their patient suffering is a manifest token of God's righteous judgment, fitting them to be counted worthy of the kingdom for which they suffer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. It is righteous with God to repay tribulation to those who trouble his people, since his justice will not leave wrongdoing unanswered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Rest is promised to the troubled, together with the apostles, at the revelation of the Lord Jesus from heaven with his mighty angels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. That day brings vengeance in flaming fire on those who know not God and do not obey the gospel, so ignorance and disobedience are both judged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Their punishment is everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord, a ruin made dreadful above all by separation from his glory and power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. On that day Christ will be glorified in his saints and admired in all who believe, his grace and power shining in what he has wrought in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Paul therefore prays always that God would count them worthy of their calling and fulfil all the good pleasure of his goodness in them with power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The end of it all is that Christ's name be glorified in them and they in him, according to the grace of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
