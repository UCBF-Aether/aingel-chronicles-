The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty man of valour, and he was the son of an harlot: and Gilead begat Jephthah.

2 And Gilead’s wife bare him sons; and his wife’s sons grew up, and they thrust out Jephthah, and said unto him, Thou shalt not inherit in our father’s house; for thou art the son of a strange woman.

3 Then Jephthah fled from his brethren, and dwelt in the land of Tob: and there were gathered vain men to Jephthah, and went out with him.

4 And it came to pass in process of time, that the children of Ammon made war against Israel.

5 And it was so, that when the children of Ammon made war against Israel, the elders of Gilead went to fetch Jephthah out of the land of Tob:

6 And they said unto Jephthah, Come, and be our captain, that we may fight with the children of Ammon.

7 And Jephthah said unto the elders of Gilead, Did not ye hate me, and expel me out of my father’s house? and why are ye come unto me now when ye are in distress?

8 And the elders of Gilead said unto Jephthah, Therefore we turn again to thee now, that thou mayest go with us, and fight against the children of Ammon, and be our head over all the inhabitants of Gilead.

9 And Jephthah said unto the elders of Gilead, If ye bring me home again to fight against the children of Ammon, and the LORD deliver them before me, shall I be your head?

10 And the elders of Gilead said unto Jephthah, The LORD be witness between us, if we do not so according to thy words.

11 Then Jephthah went with the elders of Gilead, and the people made him head and captain over them: and Jephthah uttered all his words before the LORD in Mizpeh.

12 And Jephthah sent messengers unto the king of the children of Ammon, saying, What hast thou to do with me, that thou art come against me to fight in my land?

13 And the king of the children of Ammon answered unto the messengers of Jephthah, Because Israel took away my land, when they came up out of Egypt, from Arnon even unto Jabbok, and unto Jordan: now therefore restore those lands again peaceably.

14 And Jephthah sent messengers again unto the king of the children of Ammon:

15 And said unto him, Thus saith Jephthah, Israel took not away the land of Moab, nor the land of the children of Ammon:

16 But when Israel came up from Egypt, and walked through the wilderness unto the Red sea, and came to Kadesh;

17 Then Israel sent messengers unto the king of Edom, saying, Let me, I pray thee, pass through thy land: but the king of Edom would not hearken thereto. And in like manner they sent unto the king of Moab: but he would not consent: and Israel abode in Kadesh.

18 Then they went along through the wilderness, and compassed the land of Edom, and the land of Moab, and came by the east side of the land of Moab, and pitched on the other side of Arnon, but came not within the border of Moab: for Arnon was the border of Moab.

19 And Israel sent messengers unto Sihon king of the Amorites, the king of Heshbon; and Israel said unto him, Let us pass, we pray thee, through thy land into my place.

20 But Sihon trusted not Israel to pass through his coast: but Sihon gathered all his people together, and pitched in Jahaz, and fought against Israel.

21 And the LORD God of Israel delivered Sihon and all his people into the hand of Israel, and they smote them: so Israel possessed all the land of the Amorites, the inhabitants of that country.

22 And they possessed all the coasts of the Amorites, from Arnon even unto Jabbok, and from the wilderness even unto Jordan.

23 So now the LORD God of Israel hath dispossessed the Amorites from before his people Israel, and shouldest thou possess it?

24 Wilt not thou possess that which Chemosh thy god giveth thee to possess? So whomsoever the LORD our God shall drive out from before us, them will we possess.

25 And now art thou any thing better than Balak the son of Zippor, king of Moab? did he ever strive against Israel, or did he ever fight against them,

26 While Israel dwelt in Heshbon and her towns, and in Aroer and her towns, and in all the cities that be along by the coasts of Arnon, three hundred years? why therefore did ye not recover them within that time?

27 Wherefore I have not sinned against thee, but thou doest me wrong to war against me: the LORD the Judge be judge this day between the children of Israel and the children of Ammon.

28 Howbeit the king of the children of Ammon hearkened not unto the words of Jephthah which he sent him.

29 Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jephthah, and he passed over Gilead, and Manasseh, and passed over Mizpeh of Gilead, and from Mizpeh of Gilead he passed over unto the children of Ammon.

30 And Jephthah vowed a vow unto the LORD, and said, If thou shalt without fail deliver the children of Ammon into mine hands,

31 Then it shall be, that whatsoever cometh forth of the doors of my house to meet me, when I return in peace from the children of Ammon, shall surely be the LORD’s, and I will offer it up for a burnt offering.

32 So Jephthah passed over unto the children of Ammon to fight against them; and the LORD delivered them into his hands.

33 And he smote them from Aroer, even till thou come to Minnith, even twenty cities, and unto the plain of the vineyards, with a very great slaughter. Thus the children of Ammon were subdued before the children of Israel.

34 And Jephthah came to Mizpeh unto his house, and, behold, his daughter came out to meet him with timbrels and with dances: and she was his only child; beside her he had neither son nor daughter.

35 And it came to pass, when he saw her, that he rent his clothes, and said, Alas, my daughter! thou hast brought me very low, and thou art one of them that trouble me: for I have opened my mouth unto the LORD, and I cannot go back.

36 And she said unto him, My father, if thou hast opened thy mouth unto the LORD, do to me according to that which hath proceeded out of thy mouth; forasmuch as the LORD hath taken vengeance for thee of thine enemies, even of the children of Ammon.

37 And she said unto her father, Let this thing be done for me: let me alone two months, that I may go up and down upon the mountains, and bewail my virginity, I and my fellows.

38 And he said, Go. And he sent her away for two months: and she went with her companions, and bewailed her virginity upon the mountains.

39 And it came to pass at the end of two months, that she returned unto her father, who did with her according to his vow which he had vowed: and she knew no man. And it was a custom in Israel,

40 That the daughters of Israel went yearly to lament the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite four days in a year.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jephthah, a mighty man of valour thrust out by his half-brothers for his birth, gathers a band in Tob and is fetched back by Gilead's elders on his own terms, to be head as well as captain. Before fighting he sends a careful embassy to Ammon's king: Israel took no Ammonite or Moabite land, took the Amorites' land after Sihon refused passage, and has held it three hundred years by the LORD's grant. The Spirit comes on him, he vows that whatever first meets him on his return shall be the LORD's, wins twenty cities, and is met by his only daughter with timbrels. The chapter's own text, beyond the study notes, tells that she is given two months to bewail her virginity, the vow is performed, and Israel's daughters lament her four days a year. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

  • Verse 1. Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty man of valour, son of a harlot, or concubine or foreigner in an inferior marriage, and JFB notes that whatever dishonour attached to his birth, his high and energetic character made him early a person of note, his father belonging to Manasseh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Gilead's wife's sons thrust Jephthah out, saying he should not inherit, since as son of a secondary wife he had no share while legitimate children lived, and JFB notes the brothers' rude violence suggests secret ill-will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Jephthah fled to the land of Tob north of Gilead, where vain, idle and desperate men gathered to him and went out with him, leading a freebooting life against Ammonites and neighbours, like David when driven from Saul's court. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. In process of time the Ammonites made war against Israel, and JFB notes the historian here resumes the thread from chapter 10, the Ammonites having invaded so that active hostilities were inevitable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The elders of Gilead went to fetch Jephthah from Tob, since all eyes were directed to him as the only man with the qualities needed to preserve the country, and a deputation was sent from the camp at Mizpeh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They asked him to come and be their captain to fight Ammon, the very office his exile had kept from him now offered by necessity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Jephthah answered, Did ye not hate me and expel me, and why come ye now in distress, giving them at first a haughty and cold reception, and JFB notes he was now in circumstances to make his own terms, binding them to invest him with unlimited authority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The elders replied that they therefore turned again to him, that he might go with them, fight Ammon, and be head over all Gilead, conceding the terms his question had forced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Jephthah asked whether, if he returned and the Lord delivered Ammon, he would indeed be their head, making the bargain explicit before he risked his life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The elders swore, The Lord be witness between us that we will do according to thy words, and JFB notes the solemn oath was afterwards ratified in a general assembly at Mizpeh, where Jephthah uttered all his words before the Lord, his inauguration as judge solemnised by prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Jephthah went with the elders, and the people made him head and captain, and he uttered all his words before the Lord in Mizpeh, completing the covenant the deputation had sworn in Tob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Jephthah sent messengers to Ammon's king asking what quarrel he had with Israel that he came to fight their land, and JFB calls this first judicial act a credit to his prudence, moderation, justice, and humanity, since the bravest officers are averse to war and he sought to show hostilities were forced upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Ammon's king claimed Israel had taken his land from Arnon to Jabbok and Jordan when they came from Egypt, and JFB summarises Jephthah's unanswerable reply in three arguments: the land was not Ammon's when Israel took it from the Amorites, three hundred years of possession gave prescriptive right, and Israel held it by the Lord's grant as Ammon held its own by Chemosh's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 18. They then went through the wilderness, compassed Edom and Moab, came by the east side, pitched on the other side of Arnon, and came not within Moab's border, a route that deliberately avoided Ammonite and Moabite land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Israel sent to Sihon king of the Amorites at Heshbon, asking only to pass through his land to their own place, a request for transit, not for territory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Sihon trusted Israel not, gathered his people, pitched at Jahaz, and fought Israel, so that the war with the Amorites was begun by Sihon's refusal, not Israel's aggression. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The Lord delivered Sihon and all his people into Israel's hand, and Israel possessed all the Amorites' land, the conquest JFB cites as the first of Jephthah's three arguments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Israel possessed all the Amorites' coasts from Arnon to Jabbok and from the wilderness to Jordan, defining exactly the land Ammon now claimed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The Lord God of Israel dispossessed the Amorites before his people, Jephthah argued, and shall Ammon now possess it, pressing the divine grant as the ground of Israel's title. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. But Ammon's king hearkened not to Jephthah's words, and JFB notes the remonstrances were disregarded, war became inevitable, and preparations were made for determined resistance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. The Spirit of the Lord came upon Jephthah, and he passed over Gilead and Manasseh to levy troops, then returned to Mizpeh and marched against Ammon, and JFB notes the calm wisdom and energy displayed were evidence he was qualified by higher resources than his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. The vow pointed not to an animal, which might be an unclean dog, but to a person, and JFB notes Jephthah, bred beyond Jordan and near heathen sacrifice, may have imagined a human immolation acceptable, while the alternative reading offers dedication to sanctuary service instead of burnt offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Jephthah passed over to Ammon, fought, and the Lord delivered them into his hand, meeting them at Aroer on the Arnon, and JFB notes the decisive victory and pursuit over about sixty miles to Abel, the plain of vineyards. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. He smote them from Aroer to Minnith, twenty cities, and to Abel, with a very great slaughter, and Ammon was subdued before Israel, completing the victory the vow had preceded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Jephthah came to Mizpeh to his house, and his daughter came out to meet him with timbrels and dances, leading the female band that hailed returning victors, and JFB notes the vow was full in his mind and had not been communicated, or precautions would have been taken, while her grief indicates her life was to be forfeited. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Investigation (editorial — a question, not a conclusion)

This section investigates a parallel that a study note itself raises; it does not conclude anything about dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty. Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, on Judges 11:3, describes Jephthah in Tob, where vain, idle and desperate men gathered to him and went out with him, as like David when driven from Saul's court. Interpreted Study note on Judges 11:3, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Parallels. Both men are rejected insiders who gather a band of marginal men outside respectable society, and both are later recalled to leadership in a crisis: Jephthah by Gilead's elders, who swear he shall be head over all Gilead, and David by the distressed, indebted and discontented who gather to him at Adullam (1 Samuel 22). Interpreted Study notes on Judges 11:3, 11:8-11 and 1 Samuel 22:1-2

Differences. Jephthah is thrust out by his own half-brothers over inheritance, is fetched back by formal deputation on negotiated terms, argues his case by embassy before fighting, and binds himself with a vow that ends in his daughter's fate. David is driven from a king's court, gathers men without any elders' bargain or embassy, and makes no vow of this kind at Adullam; his Adullam chapter turns instead to sheltering his parents and receiving the escaped priest Abiathar.
