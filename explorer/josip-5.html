Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter argues its case from Scripture itself, quoting passages as it goes: Adam and the tree, one tongue at Babel with 'let us go down', fire and brimstone rained on Sodom and Gomorrah from the Lord, the Psalms' 'Thou art my Son' and 'Sit at my right hand', and Isaiah's vision of the Holy One. In the chapter's own reading, each passage shows the Son and the Holy Spirit with the Father, and Isaiah's prophecy is said to be fulfilled concerning the manifestation of the Holy Trinity. Investigation (editorial — investigating, not concluding)

This section investigates a question raised by the chapter itself; it does not conclude it. The chapter itself builds its argument by quoting Scripture: Adam and the tree, Babel's “let us go down”, fire rained on Sodom and Gomorrah from the Lord, the Psalms' “Thou art my Son” and “Sit at my right hand”, and Isaiah's vision of the Holy One. Parallels

The quoted passages do share a surface feature the chapter seizes on: divine speech in the plural (“let us”), a Lord acting from the Lord at Sodom, and royal and prophetic language later applied to a Son and to the Holy One. Those are genuine textual points of contact between the chapter and the passages it cites. Interpreted Chapter text as printed above; passages as received in the canon

Differences

In their own narrative contexts, those passages do not name a Trinity, and their plural speech, the Sodom theophany, and Isaiah's vision have been read in other ways as well. The conclusion that they manifest Father, Son, and Holy Spirit together is the chapter's interpretation, not a claim the quoted verses state in those terms.
