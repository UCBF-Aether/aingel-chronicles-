The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron, saying,

2 Every man of the children of Israel shall pitch by his own standard, with the ensign of their father’s house: far off about the tabernacle of the congregation shall they pitch.

3 And on the east side toward the rising of the sun shall they of the standard of the camp of Judah pitch throughout their armies: and Nahshon the son of Amminadab shall be captain of the children of Judah.

4 And his host, and those that were numbered of them, were threescore and fourteen thousand and six hundred.

5 And those that do pitch next unto him shall be the tribe of Issachar: and Nethaneel the son of Zuar shall be captain of the children of Issachar.

6 And his host, and those that were numbered thereof, were fifty and four thousand and four hundred.

7 Then the tribe of Zebulun: and Eliab the son of Helon shall be captain of the children of Zebulun.

8 And his host, and those that were numbered thereof, were fifty and seven thousand and four hundred.

9 All that were numbered in the camp of Judah were an hundred thousand and fourscore thousand and six thousand and four hundred, throughout their armies. These shall first set forth.

10 On the south side shall be the standard of the camp of Reuben according to their armies: and the captain of the children of Reuben shall be Elizur the son of Shedeur.

11 And his host, and those that were numbered thereof, were forty and six thousand and five hundred.

12 And those which pitch by him shall be the tribe of Simeon: and the captain of the children of Simeon shall be Shelumiel the son of Zurishaddai.

13 And his host, and those that were numbered of them, were fifty and nine thousand and three hundred.

14 Then the tribe of Gad: and the captain of the sons of Gad shall be Eliasaph the son of Reuel.

15 And his host, and those that were numbered of them, were forty and five thousand and six hundred and fifty.

16 All that were numbered in the camp of Reuben were an hundred thousand and fifty and one thousand and four hundred and fifty, throughout their armies. And they shall set forth in the second rank.

17 Then the tabernacle of the congregation shall set forward with the camp of the Levites in the midst of the camp: as they encamp, so shall they set forward, every man in his place by their standards.

18 On the west side shall be the standard of the camp of Ephraim according to their armies: and the captain of the sons of Ephraim shall be Elishama the son of Ammihud.

19 And his host, and those that were numbered of them, were forty thousand and five hundred.

20 And by him shall be the tribe of Manasseh: and the captain of the children of Manasseh shall be Gamaliel the son of Pedahzur.

21 And his host, and those that were numbered of them, were thirty and two thousand and two hundred.

22 Then the tribe of Benjamin: and the captain of the sons of Benjamin shall be Abidan the son of Gideoni.

23 And his host, and those that were numbered of them, were thirty and five thousand and four hundred.

24 All that were numbered of the camp of Ephraim were an hundred thousand and eight thousand and an hundred, throughout their armies. And they shall go forward in the third rank.

25 The standard of the camp of Dan shall be on the north side by their armies: and the captain of the children of Dan shall be Ahiezer the son of Ammishaddai.

26 And his host, and those that were numbered of them, were threescore and two thousand and seven hundred.

27 And those that encamp by him shall be the tribe of Asher: and the captain of the children of Asher shall be Pagiel the son of Ocran.

28 And his host, and those that were numbered of them, were forty and one thousand and five hundred.

29 Then the tribe of Naphtali: and the captain of the children of Naphtali shall be Ahira the son of Enan.

30 And his host, and those that were numbered of them, were fifty and three thousand and four hundred.

31 All they that were numbered in the camp of Dan were an hundred thousand and fifty and seven thousand and six hundred. They shall go hindmost with their standards.

32 These are those which were numbered of the children of Israel by the house of their fathers: all those that were numbered of the camps throughout their hosts were six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty.

33 But the Levites were not numbered among the children of Israel; as the LORD commanded Moses.

34 And the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses: so they pitched by their standards, and so they set forward, every one after their families, according to the house of their fathers.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter arranges the camp and the march by standards. Four camps of three tribes each surround the tabernacle at a distance: Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun on the east; Reuben, Simeon, and Gad on the south; Ephraim, Manasseh, and Benjamin on the west; Dan, Asher, and Naphtali on the north. The tabernacle, with the Levites around it, sets forward in the midst of the camps, and each tribe pitches by its own standard and journeys by its families, exactly as commanded. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Standard (ensign). The banner of a father's house by which each tribe pitched and marched, at a reverential distance from the tabernacle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. Every man pitched by his own standard, with the ensign of his father's house, at a reverential distance from the tabernacle. JFB explains the standards as visible rallying signs, in three kinds: great tribal standards, standards of subdivided portions, and family ensigns, probably like Egyptian banners stripped of idolatrous symbols. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Judah's camp, with Issachar and Zebulun under its standard, pitched on the east toward the sunrise and led the march, which ran mostly eastward. JFB notes the twelve census superintendents were also made captains of their tribes, a dignity owed to their hereditary office as tribal heads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Issachar pitched next to Judah, on one side. Henry's general lesson for the whole chapter is contentment with one's allotted place: Israel took their posts without murmuring or disputing, and that order was both their safety and their beauty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Zebulun pitched on Judah's other side, completing a strong and imposing van, since Judah was the most numerous tribe and Issachar and Zebulun were also very numerous. The leading camp was thus the strongest, fit to break the way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The whole camp of Judah numbered a hundred and fourscore and six thousand and four hundred. The van's great strength, with the tabernacle at the centre, shows a host arranged for both protection and advance, not a crowd wandering at random. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Reuben's camp held the south side, Ephraim's the west and Dan's the north, with Judah on the east, forming a great square around the tabernacle, computed at more than twelve square miles for the fighting men alone, besides families, tents and baggage. JFB adds the march order: Judah first, then Reuben's division, then the Levites bearing the tabernacle, then Ephraim's division, with Dan's bringing up the rear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The camp of Reuben numbered a hundred and fifty-one thousand four hundred and fifty. JFB notes the wisdom of the placement: the strongest camps, Judah and Dan, formed van and rear, while the smallest and weakest, Reuben's and Ephraim's, marched in the centre, guarded on both sides. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The tabernacle of the congregation set forward in the midst of the camps, with the Levites around it. As the chief's tent stands at the centre of a nomad encampment, so the tent of Israel's Divine King stood at the centre of Israel, with his own tribe guarding it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Ephraim's camp held the west side, forming the third great division on the march, after the Levites. Its position kept the sacred centre protected by a full division on either side of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The camp of Ephraim numbered a hundred and eight thousand and a hundred. Ephraim's division, with Manasseh and Benjamin, was the smallest of the four camps, and its central place on the march matched its lesser strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Dan's camp held the north side and brought up the rear of the march. JFB notes Dan was next in force to Judah, so the rearguard, like the van, was a strong camp, guarding the host from behind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. The camp of Dan numbered a hundred and fifty-seven thousand and six hundred, the largest camp after Judah's. The rear was therefore no weak tail but a second great strength, closing the host as Judah opened it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. These are those numbered of Israel by their fathers' houses, six hundred and three thousand five hundred and fifty in all. The four camps together repeat the census total of chapter 1, now arranged into a marching and encamping order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. The Levites were not numbered among Israel, as the Lord commanded Moses, because their service was the tabernacle, not war. Their separate place, camped around the sanctuary itself, is the exception that proves the order of the whole chapter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Israel did according to all the Lord commanded: they pitched by their standards and journeyed by their families. Henry draws the church lesson: the gospel church ought to be compact after this model, everyone knowing and keeping his place, so that beholders rejoice at its order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Connections to other passages
