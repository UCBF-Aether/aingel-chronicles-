The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Habakkuk 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower, and will watch to see what he will say unto me, and what I shall answer when I am reproved.

2 And the LORD answered me, and said, Write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it.

3 For the vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie: though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry.

4 Behold, his soul which is lifted up is not upright in him: but the just shall live by his faith.

5 Yea also, because he transgresseth by wine, he is a proud man, neither keepeth at home, who enlargeth his desire as hell, and is as death, and cannot be satisfied, but gathereth unto him all nations, and heapeth unto him all people:

6 Shall not all these take up a parable against him, and a taunting proverb against him, and say, Woe to him that increaseth that which is not his! how long? and to him that ladeth himself with thick clay!

7 Shall they not rise up suddenly that shall bite thee, and awake that shall vex thee, and thou shalt be for booties unto them?

8 Because thou hast spoiled many nations, all the remnant of the people shall spoil thee; because of men’s blood, and for the violence of the land, of the city, and of all that dwell therein.

9 Woe to him that coveteth an evil covetousness to his house, that he may set his nest on high, that he may be delivered from the power of evil!

10 Thou hast consulted shame to thy house by cutting off many people, and hast sinned against thy soul.

11 For the stone shall cry out of the wall, and the beam out of the timber shall answer it.

12 Woe to him that buildeth a town with blood, and stablisheth a city by iniquity!

13 Behold, is it not of the LORD of hosts that the people shall labour in the very fire, and the people shall weary themselves for very vanity?

14 For the earth shall be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea.

15 Woe unto him that giveth his neighbour drink, that puttest thy bottle to him, and makest him drunken also, that thou mayest look on their nakedness!

16 Thou art filled with shame for glory: drink thou also, and let thy foreskin be uncovered: the cup of the LORD’s right hand shall be turned unto thee, and shameful spewing shall be on thy glory.

17 For the violence of Lebanon shall cover thee, and the spoil of beasts, which made them afraid, because of men’s blood, and for the violence of the land, of the city, and of all that dwell therein.

18 What profiteth the graven image that the maker thereof hath graven it; the molten image, and a teacher of lies, that the maker of his work trusteth therein, to make dumb idols?

19 Woe unto him that saith to the wood, Awake; to the dumb stone, Arise, it shall teach! Behold, it is laid over with gold and silver, and there is no breath at all in the midst of it.

20 But the LORD is in his holy temple: let all the earth keep silence before him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Habakkuk takes his stand on the watchtower to see what God will say and what answer he will give when reproved, and the answer is: write the vision, make it plain upon tables, that he may run who reads it, for it is yet for an appointed time, and though it tarry, wait for it. At its centre stands the line that the just shall live by his faith, set against the proud man enlarged as hell and never satisfied. Five woes follow, on plunder, covetousness, blood-built cities, making neighbours drunk to gaze on their nakedness, and trusting a dumb idol, and the chapter closes in silence before the LORD in his holy temple. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower, and will watch to see what he will say unto me, and what I shall answer when I am reproved. When tossed and perplexed with doubts about Providence, Henry says, we must watch against temptations to impatience; when we have poured out complaints and requests before God, we must observe the answers God gives by his word, his Spirit, and providences. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The LORD answered me, and said, Write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it. All are concerned in the truths of God's word, Henry notes, and the vision was to be written plainly so that it could be read at speed and carried to others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. For the vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie; though it tarry, wait for it, because it will surely come, it will not tarry. Though the promised favour be deferred long, Henry says, it will come at last and abundantly recompense us for waiting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Behold, his soul which is lifted up is not upright in him; but the just shall live by his faith. God will not disappoint the believing expectations of those who wait to hear what he will say, Henry observes; the humble, broken-hearted, repenting sinner alone seeks an interest in this salvation, resting his soul on the promise and on Christ, and thus he walks and works, as well as lives, by faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Yea also, because he transgresseth by wine, he is a proud man, neither keepeth at home, who enlargeth his desire as hell, and is as death, and cannot be satisfied, but gathereth unto him all nations, and heapeth unto him all people. The prophet reads the doom of all proud and oppressive powers that bear hard upon God's people, Henry notes; the lusts of the flesh, the lust of the eye, and the pride of life are the entangling snares of men, and he that led Israel captive is himself led captive by each of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. All the oppressed were to take up a parable and taunting proverb against the plunderer, crying woe to him that increaseth what is not his and ladeth himself with thick clay. No more of what we have is to be reckoned ours than what we come honestly by, Henry says, for riches are but clay, thick clay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Shall they not rise up suddenly that shall bite thee, and awake that shall vex thee, and thou shalt be for booties unto them? Those who travel through thick clay are hindered and dirtied in their journey, Henry observes; so are those who go through the world in the midst of abundance of wealth, and they overload themselves with thick clay and sink into destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Because thou hast spoiled many nations, all the remnant of the people shall spoil thee, because of men's blood, and for the violence of the land, of the city, and of all that dwell therein. See what will be the end hereof, Henry says: what is gotten by violence from others, others shall take away by violence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Woe to him that coveteth an evil covetousness to his house, that he may set his nest on high, that he may be delivered from the power of evil! Covetousness brings disquiet into a family, Henry notes; he that is greedy of gain troubles his own house, and what is worse, brings the curse of God upon all its affairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Thou hast consulted shame to thy house by cutting off many people, and hast sinned against thy soul. There is a lawful gain which by God's blessing may be a comfort to a house, Henry says, but what is got by fraud and injustice will bring poverty and ruin upon a family — and that is not the worst: thou hast sinned against thine own soul and endangered it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. For the stone shall cry out of the wall, and the beam out of the timber shall answer it. Those who wrong their neighbours do much greater wrong to their own souls, Henry observes, and if the sinner thinks he has managed his frauds with art, the very riches he heaped together will witness against him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Woe to him that buildeth a town with blood, and stablisheth a city by iniquity! A city founded on blood carries its own condemnation in its foundations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Behold, is it not of the LORD of hosts that the people shall labour in the very fire, and the people shall weary themselves for very vanity? There are not greater drudges in the world, Henry says, than slaves to mere worldly pursuits; they find themselves disappointed of it and disappointed in it, and own it is worse than vanity — vexation of spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. For the earth shall be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea. By staining and sinking earthly glory, Henry observes, God manifests and magnifies his own glory, and fills the earth with the knowledge of it, as plentifully as waters cover the sea, deep and spread far and wide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Woe unto him that giveth his neighbour drink, that puttest thy bottle to him, and makest him drunken also, that thou mayest look on their nakedness! A severe woe is pronounced against drunkenness, Henry says, fearful against all guilty of it at any time and in any place, from the stately palace to the paltry ale-house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Thou art filled with shame for glory: drink thou also, and let thy foreskin be uncovered; the cup of the LORD's right hand shall be turned unto thee, and shameful spewing shall be on thy glory. There is woe to him, and punishment answering to the sin, Henry notes; to give a neighbour drink so that he may expose himself, disclose secrets, or be drawn into a bad bargain is wickedness, and to take pleasure in it is to do what we can towards the murder both of soul and body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. For the violence of Lebanon shall cover thee, and the spoil of beasts, which made them afraid, because of men's blood, and for the violence of the land, of the city, and of all that dwell therein. The violence done to Lebanon and its beasts returns upon the violent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. What profiteth the graven image that the maker thereof hath graven it; the molten image, and a teacher of lies, that the maker of his work trusteth therein, to make dumb idols? The folly of worshipping idols is exposed, Henry says: the maker trusts his own work, and the work is a teacher of lies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Woe unto him that saith to the wood, Awake; to the dumb stone, Arise, it shall teach! Behold, it is laid over with gold and silver, and there is no breath at all in the midst of it. Gold and silver cannot breathe life into wood and stone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. But the LORD is in his holy temple: let all the earth keep silence before him. The Lord is in his holy temple in heaven, Henry concludes, where we have access to him in the way he has appointed; may we welcome his salvation and worship him in his earthly temples, through Christ Jesus and by the Holy Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
