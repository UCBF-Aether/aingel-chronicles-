The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Mark 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he said unto them, Verily I say unto you, That there be some of them that stand here, which shall not taste of death, till they have seen the kingdom of God come with power.

2 And after six days Jesus taketh with him Peter, and James, and John, and leadeth them up into an high mountain apart by themselves: and he was transfigured before them.

3 And his raiment became shining, exceeding white as snow; so as no fuller on earth can white them.

4 And there appeared unto them Elias with Moses: and they were talking with Jesus.

5 And Peter answered and said to Jesus, Master, it is good for us to be here: and let us make three tabernacles; one for thee, and one for Moses, and one for Elias.

6 For he wist not what to say; for they were sore afraid.

7 And there was a cloud that overshadowed them: and a voice came out of the cloud, saying, This is my beloved Son: hear him.

8 And suddenly, when they had looked round about, they saw no man any more, save Jesus only with themselves.

9 And as they came down from the mountain, he charged them that they should tell no man what things they had seen, till the Son of man were risen from the dead.

10 And they kept that saying with themselves, questioning one with another what the rising from the dead should mean.

11 And they asked him, saying, Why say the scribes that Elias must first come?

12 And he answered and told them, Elias verily cometh first, and restoreth all things; and how it is written of the Son of man, that he must suffer many things, and be set at nought.

13 But I say unto you, That Elias is indeed come, and they have done unto him whatsoever they listed, as it is written of him.

14 And when he came to his disciples, he saw a great multitude about them, and the scribes questioning with them.

15 And straightway all the people, when they beheld him, were greatly amazed, and running to him saluted him.

16 And he asked the scribes, What question ye with them?

17 And one of the multitude answered and said, Master, I have brought unto thee my son, which hath a dumb spirit;

18 And wheresoever he taketh him, he teareth him: and he foameth, and gnasheth with his teeth, and pineth away: and I spake to thy disciples that they should cast him out; and they could not.

19 He answereth him, and saith, O faithless generation, how long shall I be with you? how long shall I suffer you? bring him unto me.

20 And they brought him unto him: and when he saw him, straightway the spirit tare him; and he fell on the ground, and wallowed foaming.

21 And he asked his father, How long is it ago since this came unto him? And he said, Of a child.

22 And ofttimes it hath cast him into the fire, and into the waters, to destroy him: but if thou canst do any thing, have compassion on us, and help us.

23 Jesus said unto him, If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth.

24 And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.

25 When Jesus saw that the people came running together, he rebuked the foul spirit, saying unto him, Thou dumb and deaf spirit, I charge thee, come out of him, and enter no more into him.

26 And the spirit cried, and rent him sore, and came out of him: and he was as one dead; insomuch that many said, He is dead.

27 But Jesus took him by the hand, and lifted him up; and he arose.

28 And when he was come into the house, his disciples asked him privately, Why could not we cast him out?

29 And he said unto them, This kind can come forth by nothing, but by prayer and fasting.

30 And they departed thence, and passed through Galilee; and he would not that any man should know it.

31 For he taught his disciples, and said unto them, The Son of man is delivered into the hands of men, and they shall kill him; and after that he is killed, he shall rise the third day.

32 But they understood not that saying, and were afraid to ask him.

33 And he came to Capernaum: and being in the house he asked them, What was it that ye disputed among yourselves by the way?

34 But they held their peace: for by the way they had disputed among themselves, who should be the greatest.

35 And he sat down, and called the twelve, and saith unto them, If any man desire to be first, the same shall be last of all, and servant of all.

36 And he took a child, and set him in the midst of them: and when he had taken him in his arms, he said unto them,

37 Whosoever shall receive one of such children in my name, receiveth me: and whosoever shall receive me, receiveth not me, but him that sent me.

38 And John answered him, saying, Master, we saw one casting out devils in thy name, and he followeth not us: and we forbad him, because he followeth not us.

39 But Jesus said, Forbid him not: for there is no man which shall do a miracle in my name, that can lightly speak evil of me.

40 For he that is not against us is on our part.

41 For whosoever shall give you a cup of water to drink in my name, because ye belong to Christ, verily I say unto you, he shall not lose his reward.

42 And whosoever shall offend one of these little ones that believe in me, it is better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and he were cast into the sea.

43 And if thy hand offend thee, cut it off: it is better for thee to enter into life maimed, than having two hands to go into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched:

44 Where their worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched.

45 And if thy foot offend thee, cut it off: it is better for thee to enter halt into life, than having two feet to be cast into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched:

46 Where their worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched.

47 And if thine eye offend thee, pluck it out: it is better for thee to enter into the kingdom of God with one eye, than having two eyes to be cast into hell fire:

48 Where their worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched.

49 For every one shall be salted with fire, and every sacrifice shall be salted with salt.

50 Salt is good: but if the salt have lost his saltness, wherewith will ye season it? Have salt in yourselves, and have peace one with another.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jesus says some standing here will not taste death till they see the kingdom of God come with power (v. 1). Six days later Peter, James, and John see Jesus transfigured on a high mountain, talking with Moses and Elijah, and hear the voice: “This is my beloved Son: hear him” (vv. 2–13). Down the mountain the disciples fail to cast out a deaf and dumb spirit, and Jesus teaches that this kind comes out only by prayer and fasting (vv. 14–29). He predicts his death a second time, corrects a dispute about greatness with a child, and forbids stopping a stranger who casts out devils in his name, before stern warnings against causing the little ones to stumble (vv. 30–50). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Mark 9

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He said to them, Verily, there are some standing here who shall not taste death till they have seen the kingdom of God come with power. The transfiguration, within days, would let some disciples see the kingdom's power in a glimpse before death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. After six days Jesus took Peter, James, and John, and led them up into a high mountain apart by themselves, and he was transfigured before them. Three witnesses were chosen for a private glory, and the mountain apart became a sanctuary for what the crowd could not yet bear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. His raiment became shining, exceeding white as snow, so as no fuller on earth can whiten them. His clothes shone with a whiteness no earthly fuller could produce, for the glory within broke through his garments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. There appeared to them Elijah with Moses, and they were talking with Jesus. The law and the prophets, in Moses and Elijah, stood with Christ and talked with him, owning him as the end of both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Peter answered and said, Master, it is good for us to be here, and let us make three tabernacles, one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah. Peter would prolong the vision with tabernacles, putting Moses and Elijah on a level with Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For he knew not what to say, for they were sore afraid. Fear excused his words but did not justify them, for terror often speaks before it understands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. There was a cloud that overshadowed them, and a voice came out of the cloud, saying, This is my beloved Son: hear him. The cloud and the voice repeat the baptism, adding the command to hear him above Moses and Elijah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Suddenly, when they looked round, they saw no man any more, save Jesus only with themselves. Moses and Elijah vanished, leaving Jesus only, for the law and prophets having witnessed, the Son alone remains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. As they came down from the mountain, he charged them to tell no man what they had seen, till the Son of man were risen from the dead. The vision was sealed until the resurrection should give it its meaning, lest glory without a cross mislead the hearers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. They kept that saying with themselves, questioning what the rising from the dead should mean. Obedience kept the secret, but their question shows they had not yet understood resurrection, though they had heard it foretold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They asked him, Why do the scribes say that Elijah must first come? The scribes' doctrine of Elijah's coming seemed to clash with a Messiah already come, and the disciples sought the reconciliation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. He answered, Elijah truly comes first and restores all things, and how it is written of the Son of man that he must suffer many things and be set at nothing. Elijah's restoring work stands beside the Son's suffering, and both are written: glory for the forerunner does not cancel shame for the Messiah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But I say to you, Elijah is indeed come, and they have done to him whatever they listed, as it is written of him. John the Baptist was the Elijah who came, and men's treatment of him, ending in Herod's prison, fulfilled what was written. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. When he came to his disciples, he saw a great multitude about them, and the scribes questioning with them. From the mountain's glory he descended to a disputing crowd, and the scribes were pressing the disciples' failure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Straightway all the people, when they beheld him, were greatly amazed, and running to him saluted him. His coming, perhaps with some remaining brightness, amazed them, and the crowd ran to the master the scribes could not answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He asked the scribes, What question you with them? Christ takes up his disciples' cause and turns the question back on the questioners, requiring them to state their charge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. One of the multitude answered, Master, I have brought to you my son, who has a dumb spirit. A father's case replaces the scribes' dispute: a dumb spirit had silenced his son, and he brought the boy himself when the disciples could not help. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Wherever it takes him, it tears him, and he foams, gnashes with his teeth, and pines away, and I spoke to your disciples that they should cast him out, and they could not. The spirit's violence and the disciples' failure are both stated plainly, for the father's hope now rests on Christ alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. He answered, O faithless generation, how long shall I be with you? How long shall I suffer you? Bring him to me. The rebuke falls on the whole generation, scribes, crowd, and disciples alike, yet it ends in the gracious command to bring the boy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. They brought him to him, and when the spirit saw him, straightway it tore him, and he fell on the ground and wallowed foaming. The devil's last rage at Christ's presence made the boy's misery worse before it ended, as if to prove its power by its fury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. He asked his father, How long is it since this came to him? And he said, Of a child. The question draws out a history of lifelong affliction, that the cure may be known as no sudden fancy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Ofttimes it has cast him into the fire and into the waters to destroy him, but if you can do anything, have compassion on us and help us. The father's 'if you can' doubts Christ's power even while begging his compassion, and his prayer joins himself with his son in one 'us'. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Jesus said to him, If you can believe, all things are possible to him that believes. Christ turns the 'if' back on the father: the question is not his power but the father's faith, and faith opens all things to possibility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Straightway the father cried out and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help my unbelief. Tears join belief and unbelief in one cry, and this is the truest prayer of weak faith: it confesses its lack and begs Christ to supply it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. When Jesus saw the people come running together, he rebuked the foul spirit, saying, You dumb and deaf spirit, I charge you, come out of him, and enter no more into him. Before the gathering crowd, Christ commands by name, and adds 'enter no more' to secure a lasting deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The spirit cried and rent him sore and came out of him, and he was as one dead, so that many said, He is dead. The departure looked like death, and the bystanders' verdict shows how like death deliverance can appear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. But Jesus took him by the hand and lifted him up, and he arose. The same hand that commanded the spirit lifted the boy, and rising proved the cure complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. When he came into the house, his disciples asked him privately, Why could not we cast him out? Private failure again sought private explanation, and the disciples did not hide their defeat from their master. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. He said, This kind can come forth by nothing but by prayer and fasting. Some victories require extraordinary means: prayer and fasting are the appointed weapons against stubborn powers, and the disciples' failure was a failure of dependence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. They departed from there and passed through Galilee, and he would not that any man should know it. He travelled privately through Galilee, avoiding crowds to teach his disciples alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. For he taught his disciples and said, The Son of man is delivered into the hands of men, and they shall kill him, and after he is killed, he shall rise the third day. The second prediction is plainer than the first: delivered, killed, and risen the third day, a lesson repeated because it was not yet learned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. But they understood not that saying, and were afraid to ask him. They neither understood nor dared to ask, and fear of reproof kept them from the explanation they needed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. He came to Capernaum, and being in the house he asked them, What was it that you disputed among yourselves by the way? In the house, Christ examined the road's dispute, for private questioning follows public teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. But they held their peace, for by the way they had disputed who should be the greatest. Silence confessed the shame of the dispute: ambition among disciples is exposed by the very question that names it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. He sat down, called the twelve, and said, If any man desires to be first, the same shall be last of all and servant of all. Sitting as a teacher, Christ reverses ambition: the way to first place is last place, and greatness is service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. He took a child, set him in the midst of them, and when he had taken him in his arms, he said to them. A child in the midst, embraced by Christ, becomes the living lesson that ambition must unlearn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Whoever shall receive one of such children in my name receives me, and whoever receives me receives not me but him that sent me. Receiving a lowly child in Christ's name is receiving Christ himself, and receiving Christ is receiving the Father who sent him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. John answered, Master, we saw one casting out devils in your name, and he follows not us, and we forbade him because he follows not us. John's report shows party spirit among the disciples: they forbade a man for not following them, though he followed Christ's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. But Jesus said, Forbid him not, for there is no man who shall do a miracle in my name that can lightly speak evil of me. A miracle in Christ's name is a bond to Christ, and such a man will not quickly turn to revile him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. For he that is not against us is on our part. Neutrality toward Christ's cause is counted friendship, and the disciples must not make enemies of those who are not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. For whoever shall give you a cup of water to drink in my name, because you belong to Christ, verily, he shall not lose his reward. Even a cup of water, given for Christ's sake, is remembered and rewarded, for God measures the motive, not the gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. Whoever shall offend one of these little ones that believe in me, it is better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck and he were cast into the sea. Offending a believing child is so great an evil that drowning with a millstone is the lesser misery, a warning against causing the weak to stumble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. If your hand offends you, cut it off: it is better to enter into life maimed than having two hands to go into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched. Hand, foot, and eye are to be sacrificed rather than kept to sin, for maimed life is better than whole damnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. Where their worm dies not and the fire is not quenched. The worm and fire are undying, for hell's punishment has no end, and its repetition in each verse stresses what men most wish to forget. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. If your foot offends you, cut it off: it is better to enter halt into life than having two feet to be cast into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched. The foot that walks into sin must be stopped at any cost, for halting into life is better than running into hell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. Where their worm dies not and the fire is not quenched. The repeated sentence is itself the sermon: judgment without end answers every excuse for keeping a sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. If your eye offends you, pluck it out: it is better to enter into the kingdom of God with one eye than having two eyes to be cast into hell fire. The eye that lusts must be plucked in resolve, for one-eyed entrance into the kingdom is better than two-eyed damnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. Where their worm dies not and the fire is not quenched. A third repetition seals the warning, that no reader may pass it as a figure of speech only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. For everyone shall be salted with fire, and every sacrifice shall be salted with salt. As sacrifices were salted, so disciples are seasoned by trial, and fire purifies what it does not consume in the godly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 50. Salt is good, but if the salt has lost its saltness, with what will you season it? Have salt in yourselves, and have peace one with another. Salt that has lost its savour is useless, and disciples must keep grace's seasoning within, which shows itself in peace among themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Jesus said to him, If you can believe, all things are possible to him that believes. Christ turns the 'if' back on the father: the question is not his power but the father's faith, and faith opens all things to possibility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Straightway the father cried out and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help my unbelief. Tears join belief and unbelief in one cry, and this is the truest prayer of weak faith: it confesses its lack and begs Christ to supply it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. When Jesus saw the people come running together, he rebuked the foul spirit, saying, You dumb and deaf spirit, I charge you, come out of him, and enter no more into him. Before the gathering crowd, Christ commands by name, and adds 'enter no more' to secure a lasting deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The spirit cried and rent him sore and came out of him, and he was as one dead, so that many said, He is dead. The departure looked like death, and the bystanders' verdict shows how like death deliverance can appear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. But Jesus took him by the hand and lifted him up, and he arose. The same hand that commanded the spirit lifted the boy, and rising proved the cure complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. When he came into the house, his disciples asked him privately, Why could not we cast him out? Private failure again sought private explanation, and the disciples did not hide their defeat from their master. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. He said, This kind can come forth by nothing but by prayer and fasting. Some victories require extraordinary means: prayer and fasting are the appointed weapons against stubborn powers, and the disciples' failure was a failure of dependence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. They departed from there and passed through Galilee, and he would not that any man should know it. He travelled privately through Galilee, avoiding crowds to teach his disciples alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. For he taught his disciples and said, The Son of man is delivered into the hands of men, and they shall kill him, and after he is killed, he shall rise the third day. The second prediction is plainer than the first: delivered, killed, and risen the third day, a lesson repeated because it was not yet learned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. But they understood not that saying, and were afraid to ask him. They neither understood nor dared to ask, and fear of reproof kept them from the explanation they needed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. He came to Capernaum, and being in the house he asked them, What was it that you disputed among yourselves by the way? In the house, Christ examined the road's dispute, for private questioning follows public teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. But they held their peace, for by the way they had disputed who should be the greatest. Silence confessed the shame of the dispute: ambition among disciples is exposed by the very question that names it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. He sat down, called the twelve, and said, If any man desires to be first, the same shall be last of all and servant of all. Sitting as a teacher, Christ reverses ambition: the way to first place is last place, and greatness is service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. He took a child, set him in the midst of them, and when he had taken him in his arms, he said to them. A child in the midst, embraced by Christ, becomes the living lesson that ambition must unlearn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Whoever shall receive one of such children in my name receives me, and whoever receives me receives not me but him that sent me. Receiving a lowly child in Christ's name is receiving Christ himself, and receiving Christ is receiving the Father who sent him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. John answered, Master, we saw one casting out devils in your name, and he follows not us, and we forbade him because he follows not us. John's report shows party spirit among the disciples: they forbade a man for not following them, though he followed Christ's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. But Jesus said, Forbid him not, for there is no man who shall do a miracle in my name that can lightly speak evil of me. A miracle in Christ's name is a bond to Christ, and such a man will not quickly turn to revile him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. For he that is not against us is on our part. Neutrality toward Christ's cause is counted friendship, and the disciples must not make enemies of those who are not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. For whoever shall give you a cup of water to drink in my name, because you belong to Christ, verily, he shall not lose his reward. Even a cup of water, given for Christ's sake, is remembered and rewarded, for God measures the motive, not the gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. Whoever shall offend one of these little ones that believe in me, it is better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck and he were cast into the sea. Offending a believing child is so great an evil that drowning with a millstone is the lesser misery, a warning against causing the weak to stumble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. If your hand offends you, cut it off: it is better to enter into life maimed than having two hands to go into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched. Hand, foot, and eye are to be sacrificed rather than kept to sin, for maimed life is better than whole damnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. Where their worm dies not and the fire is not quenched. The worm and fire are undying, for hell's punishment has no end, and its repetition in each verse stresses what men most wish to forget. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. If your foot offends you, cut it off: it is better to enter halt into life than having two feet to be cast into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched. The foot that walks into sin must be stopped at any cost, for halting into life is better than running into hell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. Where their worm dies not and the fire is not quenched. The repeated sentence is itself the sermon: judgment without end answers every excuse for keeping a sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. If your eye offends you, pluck it out: it is better to enter into the kingdom of God with one eye than having two eyes to be cast into hell fire. The eye that lusts must be plucked in resolve, for one-eyed entrance into the kingdom is better than two-eyed damnation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 48. Where their worm dies not and the fire is not quenched. A third repetition seals the warning, that no reader may pass it as a figure of speech only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 49. For everyone shall be salted with fire, and every sacrifice shall be salted with salt. As sacrifices were salted, so disciples are seasoned by trial, and fire purifies what it does not consume in the godly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Salt that has lost its savour is useless, and disciples must keep grace's seasoning within, which shows itself in peace among themselves. Investigation (editorial — a parallel examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a possible parallel. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

The transfiguration — Mark 9 and Luke 9

Mark 9:2–8 and Luke 9:28–36 agree on the core: Peter, James, and John taken up a high mountain apart, Jesus transfigured before them, Moses and Elijah appearing, and the voice from the cloud, “This is my beloved Son: hear him.” The differences are characteristic: Mark dates it “after six days,” Luke “about an eight days after these sayings” and says he went up to pray; Luke adds that Moses and Elijah spoke of Jesus’ decease to be accomplished at Jerusalem, and that the disciples were heavy with sleep. Whether the day-count difference reflects counting methods or separate recollection is not settled by the sources consulted for this page.
