The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 32 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when the people saw that Moses delayed to come down out of the mount, the people gathered themselves together unto Aaron, and said unto him, Up, make us gods, which shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him.

2 And Aaron said unto them, Break off the golden earrings, which are in the ears of your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters, and bring them unto me.

3 And all the people brake off the golden earrings which were in their ears, and brought them unto Aaron.

4 And he received them at their hand, and fashioned it with a graving tool, after he had made it a molten calf: and they said, These be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.

5 And when Aaron saw it, he built an altar before it; and Aaron made proclamation, and said, To morrow is a feast to the LORD.

6 And they rose up early on the morrow, and offered burnt offerings, and brought peace offerings; and the people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play.

7 And the LORD said unto Moses, Go, get thee down; for thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves:

8 They have turned aside quickly out of the way which I commanded them: they have made them a molten calf, and have worshipped it, and have sacrificed thereunto, and said, These be thy gods, O Israel, which have brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.

9 And the LORD said unto Moses, I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people:

10 Now therefore let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot against them, and that I may consume them: and I will make of thee a great nation.

11 And Moses besought the LORD his God, and said, LORD, why doth thy wrath wax hot against thy people, which thou hast brought forth out of the land of Egypt with great power, and with a mighty hand?

12 Wherefore should the Egyptians speak, and say, For mischief did he bring them out, to slay them in the mountains, and to consume them from the face of the earth? Turn from thy fierce wrath, and repent of this evil against thy people.

13 Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, thy servants, to whom thou swarest by thine own self, and saidst unto them, I will multiply your seed as the stars of heaven, and all this land that I have spoken of will I give unto your seed, and they shall inherit it for ever.

14 And the LORD repented of the evil which he thought to do unto his people.

15 And Moses turned, and went down from the mount, and the two tables of the testimony were in his hand: the tables were written on both their sides; on the one side and on the other were they written.

16 And the tables were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, graven upon the tables.

17 And when Joshua heard the noise of the people as they shouted, he said unto Moses, There is a noise of war in the camp.

18 And he said, It is not the voice of them that shout for mastery, neither is it the voice of them that cry for being overcome: but the noise of them that sing do I hear.

19 And it came to pass, as soon as he came nigh unto the camp, that he saw the calf, and the dancing: and Moses’ anger waxed hot, and he cast the tables out of his hands, and brake them beneath the mount.

20 And he took the calf which they had made, and burnt it in the fire, and ground it to powder, and strawed it upon the water, and made the children of Israel drink of it.

21 And Moses said unto Aaron, What did this people unto thee, that thou hast brought so great a sin upon them?

22 And Aaron said, Let not the anger of my lord wax hot: thou knowest the people, that they are set on mischief.

23 For they said unto me, Make us gods, which shall go before us: for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him.

24 And I said unto them, Whosoever hath any gold, let them break it off. So they gave it me: then I cast it into the fire, and there came out this calf.

25 And when Moses saw that the people were naked; (for Aaron had made them naked unto their shame among their enemies:)

26 Then Moses stood in the gate of the camp, and said, Who is on the LORD’s side? let him come unto me. And all the sons of Levi gathered themselves together unto him.

27 And he said unto them, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Put every man his sword by his side, and go in and out from gate to gate throughout the camp, and slay every man his brother, and every man his companion, and every man his neighbour.

28 And the children of Levi did according to the word of Moses: and there fell of the people that day about three thousand men.

29 For Moses had said, Consecrate yourselves today to the LORD, even every man upon his son, and upon his brother; that he may bestow upon you a blessing this day.

30 And it came to pass on the morrow, that Moses said unto the people, Ye have sinned a great sin: and now I will go up unto the LORD; peradventure I shall make an atonement for your sin.

31 And Moses returned unto the LORD, and said, Oh, this people have sinned a great sin, and have made them gods of gold.

32 Yet now, if thou wilt forgive their sin--; and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book which thou hast written.

33 And the LORD said unto Moses, Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book.

34 Therefore now go, lead the people unto the place of which I have spoken unto thee: behold, mine Angel shall go before thee: nevertheless in the day when I visit I will visit their sin upon them.

35 And the LORD plagued the people, because they made the calf, which Aaron made.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

While Moses lingers on the mountain, the people ask Aaron for gods to go before them, and he makes a golden calf from their earrings, which they worship with feasting. Moses breaks the tables, grinds the calf to powder, and intercedes until God turns from destroying Israel; the Levites then execute judgment in the camp, and Moses pleads again for atonement. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When the people saw that Moses delayed to come down out of the mount, they gathered themselves together unto Aaron, and said, Up, make us gods, which shall go before us, for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him. Henry notes while Moses was in the mount receiving the law, the people made a tumultuous address to Aaron, for this giddy multitude were weary of waiting, and weariness in waiting betrays to many temptations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Aaron said unto them, Break off the golden earrings, which are in the ears of your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters, and bring them unto me. Henry notes their readiness to part with their earrings to make an idol should shame our niggardliness in the service of the true God, for they did not draw back on account of the cost of their idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And all the people brake off the golden earrings which were in their ears, and brought them unto Aaron. Henry observes the people obeyed at once, stripping themselves of gold for a god they could see. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And he received them at their hand, and fashioned it with a graving tool, after he had made it a molten calf, and they said, These be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt. Henry notes Aaron produced the shape of an ox or calf, giving it some finish with a graving tool, and they changed the truth of God into a lie, calling the calf the god that had brought them out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And when Aaron saw it, he built an altar before it, and Aaron made proclamation, and said, To morrow is a feast to the LORD. Henry observes Aaron built an altar to the idol and proclaimed a feast to the LORD, mingling the true God's name with false worship, which made the sin worse, not better. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And they rose up early on the morrow, and offered burnt offerings, and brought peace offerings, and the people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play. Henry notes they offered sacrifice to this idol, and having set up an image, their sacrifices were abomination, for they had, only days before, heard the voice of God speaking out of the fire, Thou shalt not make to thyself any graven image. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And the LORD said unto Moses, Go, get thee down, for thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. Henry notes God says the Israelites had corrupted themselves, for sin is the corruption of the sinner, and it is a self-corruption, every man being tempted when drawn aside of his own lust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. They have turned aside quickly out of the way which I commanded them, and they have made them a molten calf, and have worshipped it, and have sacrificed thereunto, and said, These be thy gods, O Israel, which have brought thee up out of the land of Egypt. Henry observes they turned aside out of the way, for sin is a departing from the way of duty into a by-path, and they soon forgot God's works, though they had so lately covenanted to do all he had said. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And the LORD said unto Moses, I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people. Henry notes God sees what men cannot discover, nor is any wickedness of the world hid from him, and we could not bear to see the thousandth part of the evil which God sees every day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Now therefore let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot against them, and that I may consume them, and that I may make of thee a great nation. Henry observes God expressed his just displeasure after the manner of men, as if Moses's prayer could save them from ruin, and thus Moses was a type of Christ, by whose mediation alone God would reconcile the world to himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And Moses besought the LORD his God, and said, LORD, why doth thy wrath wax hot against thy people, which thou hast brought forth out of the land of Egypt with great power, and with a mighty hand? Henry notes Moses pleaded God's glory, for the glorifying of God's name, as it ought to be our first petition, so it ought to be our great plea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Wherefore should the Egyptians speak, and say, For mischief did he bring them out, to slay them in the mountains, and to consume them from the face of the earth? Turn from thy fierce wrath, and repent of this evil against thy people. Henry observes Moses pleaded that Egypt would blaspheme God's name if Israel perished, for God's honour among the nations was at stake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, thy servants, to whom thou swarest by thine own self, and saidst unto them, I will multiply your seed as the stars of heaven, and all this land that I have spoken of will I give unto your seed, and they shall inherit it forever. Henry notes God's promises are to be our pleas in prayer, for what he has promised he is able to perform. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And the LORD repented of the evil which he thought to do unto his people. Henry observes that in answer to Moses's prayers, God showed his purpose of sparing the people, as he had before seemed determined on their destruction, which change of the outward discovery of his purpose is called repenting of the evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And Moses turned, and went down from the mount, and the two tables of the testimony were in his hand, the tables were written on both their sides, on the one side and on the other were they written. Henry notes what a change it is to come down from the mount of communion with God to converse with a wicked world: in God we see nothing but what is pure and pleasing, in the world nothing but what is sinful and provoking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And the tables were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, graven upon the tables. Henry observes the tables were God's work and God's writing, graven, not painted, for the law was meant to last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And when Joshua heard the noise of the people as they shouted, he said unto Moses, There is a noise of war in the camp. Henry notes Joshua, waiting below, mistook the noise for war, for he had not seen the camp's revelry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And he said, It is not the voice of them that shout for mastery, neither is it the voice of them that cry for being overcome, but the noise of them that sing do I hear. Henry observes Moses distinguished the sound at once: not war, not defeat, but singing, the noise of idolatrous feasting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And it came to pass, as soon as he came nigh unto the camp, that he saw the calf, and the dancing, and Moses's anger waxed hot, and he cast the tables out of his hands, and brake them beneath the mount. Henry notes the sight broke the tables: the law was broken in the camp before it was broken beneath the mount, for Israel had already broken the covenant the tables contained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And he took the calf which they had made, and burnt it in the fire, and ground it to powder, and strewed it upon the water, and made the children of Israel drink of it. Henry notes that it might appear an idol is nothing in the world, Moses ground the calf to dust, and mixing the powder with their drink signified that the backslider in heart should be filled with his own ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And Moses said unto Aaron, What did this people unto thee, that thou hast brought so great a sin upon them? Henry observes the question put the blame where it belonged: Aaron, set apart by divine appointment to the priesthood, had shamed himself so far as to build an altar to a golden calf, and must own himself unworthy of attending at God's altar, indebted to free grace alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And Aaron said, Let not the anger of my lord wax hot, for thou knowest the people, that they are set on mischief. Henry notes Aaron began with the people's mischief, as if their pressure excused his compliance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. For they said unto me, Make us gods, which shall go before us, for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him. Henry observes Aaron repeated the people's demand as his defence, but men can only tempt us to sin, they cannot force us, and we must never be drawn into sin by anything man can say or do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And I said unto them, Whosoever hath any gold, let them break it off. So they gave it me, then I cast it into the fire, and there came out this calf. Henry notes never did any wise man make a more frivolous and foolish excuse than Aaron's, for the calf did not come out of the fire by itself, but by his graving tool. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And when Moses saw that the people were naked, for Aaron had made them naked unto their shame among their enemies, the approach of Moses turned their dancing into trembling. Henry notes they were exposed to shame by their sin, and Moses's course to roll away the reproach was not by concealing the sin or putting a false colour upon it, but by punishing it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Then Moses stood in the gate of the camp, and said, Who is on the LORD's side? Let him come unto me. And all the sons of Levi gathered themselves together unto him. Henry observes the call divided the camp: those on the LORD's side gathered to Moses, and the Levites answered first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And he said unto them, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Put every man his sword by his side, and go in and out from gate to gate throughout the camp, and slay every man his brother, and every man his companion, and every man his neighbour. Henry notes the Levites were to slay the ringleaders in this wickedness, yet none were executed but those who openly stood forth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And the children of Levi did according to the word of Moses, and there fell of the people that day about three thousand men. Henry observes those who in the morning were shouting and dancing, before night were dying, for such sudden changes do the Lord's judgments sometimes make with sinners secure and jovial in their sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For Moses had said, Consecrate yourselves today to the LORD, even every man upon his son, and upon his brother, that he may bestow upon you a blessing this day. Henry notes the Levites' consecration came through judgment on their own kin, for loyalty to God outranked blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And it came to pass on the morrow, that Moses said unto the people, Ye have sinned a great sin, and now I will go up unto the LORD, peradventure I shall make an atonement for your sin. Henry notes Moses called it a great sin, for the work of ministers is to show people the greatness of their sins, and the great evil of sin appears in the price of pardon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And Moses returned unto the LORD, and said, Oh, this people have sinned a great sin, and have made them gods of gold. Henry observes Moses came not to make excuses but to make atonement, confessing the sin in full before he pleaded for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Yet now, if thou wilt forgive their sin, and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book which thou hast written. Henry notes we are not to suppose Moses meant he would be willing to perish forever for the people's sake, for we are to love our neighbour as ourselves, and not more than ourselves, but having that mind which was in Christ, he was willing to lay down his life in the most painful manner, if he might thereby preserve the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And the LORD said unto Moses, Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book. Henry observes the answer guarded substitution: each sinner stood in his own name, and Moses could not wholly turn away God's wrath, which shows the law of Moses was not able to reconcile men to God and perfect our peace with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Therefore now go, lead the people unto the place of which I have spoken unto thee, and behold, mine Angel shall go before thee, and nevertheless in the day when I visit I will visit their sin upon them. Henry notes the march would resume, but with a reservation: the sin was not forgotten, and visitation would come in its day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And the LORD plagued the people, because they made the calf, which Aaron made. Henry closes on the sober end: no unhumbled, carnal heart can long endure the holy precepts and spiritual worship of God, but a god, a priest, a worship, and a doctrine suited to the carnal mind will ever meet with abundance of worshippers, and well is it for us that the Prophet like unto Moses has made atonement for our souls and now intercedes in our behalf. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 28. And the children of Levi did according to the word of Moses, and there fell of the people that day about three thousand men. Henry observes those who in the morning were shouting and dancing, before night were dying, for such sudden changes do the Lord's judgments sometimes make with sinners secure and jovial in their sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. For Moses had said, Consecrate yourselves today to the LORD, even every man upon his son, and upon his brother, that he may bestow upon you a blessing this day. Henry notes the Levites' consecration came through judgment on their own kin, for loyalty to God outranked blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And it came to pass on the morrow, that Moses said unto the people, Ye have sinned a great sin, and now I will go up unto the LORD, peradventure I shall make an atonement for your sin. Henry notes Moses called it a great sin, for the work of ministers is to show people the greatness of their sins, and the great evil of sin appears in the price of pardon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And Moses returned unto the LORD, and said, Oh, this people have sinned a great sin, and have made them gods of gold. Henry observes Moses came not to make excuses but to make atonement, confessing the sin in full before he pleaded for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Yet now, if thou wilt forgive their sin, and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book which thou hast written. Henry notes we are not to suppose Moses meant he would be willing to perish forever for the people's sake, for we are to love our neighbour as ourselves, and not more than ourselves, but having that mind which was in Christ, he was willing to lay down his life in the most painful manner, if he might thereby preserve the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And the LORD said unto Moses, Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book. Henry observes the answer guarded substitution: each sinner stood in his own name, and Moses could not wholly turn away God's wrath, which shows the law of Moses was not able to reconcile men to God and perfect our peace with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Therefore now go, lead the people unto the place of which I have spoken unto thee, and behold, mine Angel shall go before thee, and nevertheless in the day when I visit I will visit their sin upon them. Henry notes the march would resume, but with a reservation: the sin was not forgotten, and visitation would come in its day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. And the LORD plagued the people, because they made the calf, which Aaron made. Henry closes on the sober end: no unhumbled, carnal heart can long endure the holy precepts and spiritual worship of God, but a god, a priest, a worship, and a doctrine suited to the carnal mind will ever meet with abundance of worshippers, and well is it for us that the Prophet like unto Moses has made atonement for our souls and now intercedes in our behalf. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
