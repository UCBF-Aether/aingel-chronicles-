The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD said unto Noah, Come thou and all thy house into the ark; for thee have I seen righteous before me in this generation.

2 Of every clean beast thou shalt take to thee by sevens, the male and his female: and of beasts that are not clean by two, the male and his female.

3 Of fowls also of the air by sevens, the male and the female; to keep seed alive upon the face of all the earth.

4 For yet seven days, and I will cause it to rain upon the earth forty days and forty nights; and every living substance that I have made will I destroy from off the face of the earth.

5 And Noah did according unto all that the LORD commanded him.

6 And Noah was six hundred years old when the flood of waters was upon the earth.

7 And Noah went in, and his sons, and his wife, and his sons’ wives with him, into the ark, because of the waters of the flood.

8 Of clean beasts, and of beasts that are not clean, and of fowls, and of every thing that creepeth upon the earth,

9 There went in two and two unto Noah into the ark, the male and the female, as God had commanded Noah.

10 And it came to pass after seven days, that the waters of the flood were upon the earth.

11 In the six hundredth year of Noah’s life, in the second month, the seventeenth day of the month, the same day were all the fountains of the great deep broken up, and the windows of heaven were opened.

12 And the rain was upon the earth forty days and forty nights.

13 In the selfsame day entered Noah, and Shem, and Ham, and Japheth, the sons of Noah, and Noah’s wife, and the three wives of his sons with them, into the ark;

14 They, and every beast after his kind, and all the cattle after their kind, and every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind, and every fowl after his kind, every bird of every sort.

15 And they went in unto Noah into the ark, two and two of all flesh, wherein is the breath of life.

16 And they that went in, went in male and female of all flesh, as God had commanded him: and the LORD shut him in.

17 And the flood was forty days upon the earth; and the waters increased, and bare up the ark, and it was lift up above the earth.

18 And the waters prevailed, and were increased greatly upon the earth; and the ark went upon the face of the waters.

19 And the waters prevailed exceedingly upon the earth; and all the high hills, that were under the whole heaven, were covered.

20 Fifteen cubits upward did the waters prevail; and the mountains were covered.

21 And all flesh died that moved upon the earth, both of fowl, and of cattle, and of beast, and of every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth, and every man:

22 All in whose nostrils was the breath of life, of all that was in the dry land, died.

23 And every living substance was destroyed which was upon the face of the ground, both man, and cattle, and the creeping things, and the fowl of the heaven; and they were destroyed from the earth: and Noah only remained alive, and they that were with him in the ark.

24 And the waters prevailed upon the earth an hundred and fifty days.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Noah, his household and the animals enter the ark, and the flood comes: rain for forty days and waters that prevail for a hundred and fifty days, covering the high hills. Every living thing outside the ark perishes, and only Noah and those with him in the ark remain alive. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God told Noah to come into the ark, with all his house, for Noah alone had God seen righteous in this generation. Righteousness here is the faithfulness already described in chapter 6, not a claim of sinlessness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Of every clean beast Noah was to take seven pairs, male and female, and of unclean beasts one pair. The larger number of clean animals anticipates sacrifice after the flood, and provision for worship as well as survival. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Of birds also seven pairs, to keep seed alive on the earth. The point is continuity: life is preserved in kinds, so the world after the flood can be repopulated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. In seven days God would send rain for forty days and forty nights and destroy every living substance he had made from the earth. The seven days are a final warning period, and the forty days mark a complete, deliberate judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Noah did according to all God commanded him. As in chapter 6, obedience is total and unhurried: he does what he is told, not a selection of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Noah was 600 years old when the flood of waters came on the earth. The age anchors the flood in Noah's lifetime and in the genealogy, not in vague mythic time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Noah, his sons, his wife and his sons' wives entered the ark because of the flood waters. The household enters together: salvation here is a family preserved by one man's faithfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Of clean and unclean beasts, birds and creeping things, pairs came to Noah. The animals arrive as God directed, reinforcing that this rescue is God's ordering, not Noah's roundup alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. They entered two by two, male and female, as God commanded Noah. The pairing preserves reproduction after the flood, and the repetition stresses obedience to the pattern given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. After seven days, the waters of the flood came on the earth. The delay ends exactly as announced, showing the warning period was real and the judgment was not hasty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. In Noah's 600th year, on the seventeenth day of the second month, the fountains of the great deep broke up and the windows of heaven opened. JFB and Henry read this as water from below and above at once: a total undoing of the ordered waters of creation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Rain fell on the earth forty days and forty nights. The steady duration matters: this is not a brief storm but a sustained judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. On that same day Noah, Shem, Ham and Japheth, Noah's wife and his sons' wives entered the ark. The verse repeats the entry to fix the date: the household was inside before the waters rose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. With them entered every beast, cattle, creeping thing and bird after its kind. The ark's company is complete by kinds, matching the command of chapter 6. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They came to Noah into the ark, two by two, of all flesh with the breath of life. Life itself, in all its kinds, is carried through judgment inside the one vessel God appointed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Those that entered, male and female of all flesh, went in as God commanded, and the LORD shut him in. Henry stresses the shutting: God himself seals the ark, so safety depends on God, not on Noah's latch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The flood lasted forty days, the waters increased and bore up the ark, and it was lifted above the earth. The vessel built to float, not sail, now does exactly its work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The waters prevailed and increased greatly, and the ark moved on the face of the waters. The ark rides above a world being unmade, a picture of preservation through judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The waters prevailed exceedingly, and all the high hills under heaven were covered. The language stresses totality: no high ground offered escape, so deliverance could only be in the ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The waters rose fifteen cubits upward, and the mountains were covered. The measurement makes the point concrete: even the highest landmarks disappeared under water. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. All flesh died that moved on the earth: birds, cattle, beasts, creeping things and every man. The judgment announced in chapter 6 falls exactly on the order it named. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. All in whose nostrils was the breath of life, of all that was on dry land, died. Land life perishes; only what was in the ark, and water life not named here, remains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Every living substance on the earth was destroyed, man and beast and creeping thing and fowl, and only Noah and those with him in the ark remained alive. The verse sums the flood as total judgment with a single exception: the household God preserved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The waters prevailed on the earth 150 days. The long prevailing shows the flood was not a momentary event but a sustained state, before any abating begins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages

  • Genesis 6 — Noah's righteousness is the faithfulness already described in chapter 6; his total obedience, the ark's company by kinds, and the judgment itself all match the command and announcement of chapter 6.
