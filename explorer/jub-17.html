The Text — Jubilees 17 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the first year of the fifth week Isaac was weaned in this jubilee, and Abraham made a great banquet in the third month, on the day his son Isaac was weaned.

2 And Ishmael, the son of Hagar, the Egyptian, was before the face of Abraham, his father, in his place, and Abraham rejoiced and blessed God because he had seen his sons 3 and had not died childless.

3 And he remembered the words which He had spoken to him on the day on which Lot had parted from him, and he rejoiced because the Lord had given him seed upon the earth to inherit the earth, and he blessed with all his mouth the Creator of all things. And Sarah saw Ishmael playing and dancing and Abraham rejoicing with great joy, and she became jealous of Ishmael and said to Abraham, " Cast out this bondwoman and her son ; for the son of this bondwoman will not be heir with my son, Isaac." 5.

4 And the thing was grievous in Abraham's sight, because of his maidservant and because of his son, that he should drive them from him.

5 And God said to Abraham " Let it not be grievous in thy sight, because of the child and because of the bondwoman ; in all that Sarah hath said unto thee, hearken to her words and do (them) ; for in Isaac shall thy name and seed be called.

6 But as for the son of this bondwoman I will make him a great nation, because he is of thy seed." 8.

7 And Abraham rose up early in the morning

8 and took bread and a bottle of water, and placed them on the shoulders of Hagar and the child, and sent her away.

9 And she departed and wandered in the wilderness of Beersheba, and the water in the bottle was spent, and the child. thirsted, and was not able to go on, and fell down.

10 And his mother took him and cast him under an olive tree and went and sat her down over against him, at the distance of a bow-shot ; for she said, " Let me not see the death of my child," and as she sat she wept.

11 And an angel of God, one of the holy ones, said unto her, " Why weepest thou, Hagar?

12 Arise, take the child, and hold him in thine hand ; for God hath heard thy voice, and hath seen the child." 12.

13 And she opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water, and she went and filled her bottle with water, and she gave her child to drink, and she arose and went towards the wilderness of Paran.

14 And the child grew and became an archer, and God was with him ; and his mother took him a wife from among the daughters of Egypt.

15 And she bare him a son, and he called his "name Nebaioth ; for she said, " The Lord was nigh to me when I called upon him."

16 Mastema proposes to God that Abraham shall be put to the Proof.

17 And it came to pass in the seventh week, in the first year thereof, in the first month in this jubilee on the twelfth of this month, there were voices in heaven regarding Abraham, that he was faithful in all that He told him, and that he loved the Lord, and that in every affliction he was faithful.

21 And the Lord knew that Abraham was faithful in all his afflictions ; for He had tried him through his country and with famine, and had tried him with the wealth

22 of kings, and had tried him again through his wife, when she was torn (from him), and with circumcision, and had tried him through Ishmael and Hagar, his maid-servant, when he sent them away.

23 And in everything wherein He had tried him, he was found faithful, and his soul was not impatient, and he was not slow to act ; for he was faithful and a lover of the Lord.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

In plain language: In the first year of the fifth week Isaac was weaned in this jubilee, and Abraham made a great banquet in the third month, on the day his son Isaac was weaned. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the first year of the fifth week Isaac was weaned in this jubilee, and Abraham made a great banquet in the third month, on the day his son Isaac was weaned. JFB on Genesis 21:8 notes children are suckled longer in the East, boys usually for two or three years, and weaning is always a season of domestic festivity, the newly weaned child being formally brought before assembled relatives and friends, Isaac attired in the symbolic robe, the badge of birthright, admitted heir of the tribe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Ishmael, son of Hagar the Egyptian, was before the face of Abraham his father in his place, and Abraham rejoiced and blessed God because he had seen his sons and had not died childless. Both sons are present at the banquet, and Abraham's joy answers his old complaint of going childless in chapter 14. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 3. He remembered the words spoken to him on the day Lot had parted from him, and rejoiced because the Lord had given him seed upon the earth to inherit the earth, and blessed with all his mouth the Creator of all things. And Sarah saw Ishmael playing and dancing and Abraham rejoicing with great joy, and she became jealous of Ishmael and said to Abraham: 'Cast out this bondwoman and her son; for the son of this bondwoman will not be heir with my son, Isaac.' JFB on Genesis 21:9 notes Ishmael, aware of the great change in his prospects, under irritated and resentful feelings, joined probably by his mother, treated the young heir with derision and probably some violence; Sarah's demand follows the play and the joy, with heirship as the stated reason. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The thing was grievous in Abraham's sight, because of his maidservant and because of his son, that he should drive them from him. Expulsion grieves Abraham doubly: for the maidservant and for the son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 5. God said to Abraham: 'Let it not be grievous in thy sight, because of the child and because of the bondwoman; in all that Sarah hath said unto thee, hearken to her words and do them; for in Isaac shall thy name and seed be called.' JFB on Genesis 21:10, 12 notes nothing but the expulsion of both could now preserve harmony in the household, and Abraham's perplexity was relieved by an announcement of the divine will, which all who fear God will promptly obey, however painful to flesh and blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. 'But as for the son of this bondwoman I will make him a great nation, because he is of thy seed.' JFB on Genesis 21:13 notes Providence overruled a family brawl to give rise to two great and extraordinary peoples; Ishmael's nationhood rests on Abraham's seed, not on the covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. This verse opens the expulsion: Abraham rose up early in the morning. The sentence is completed by the bread and water of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. He took bread and a bottle of water, and placed them on the shoulders of Hagar and the child, and sent her away. JFB on Genesis 21:14 notes the early rising, that the wanderers might reach an asylum before noon; bread includes all sorts of victuals, and the bottle is a leathern vessel of a whole lamb or kid skin, carried over the shoulder, while Ishmael, a lad of seventeen, was sent out at the age when Arab chiefs customarily send sons to do for themselves with a few days' provisions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. She departed and wandered in the wilderness of Beersheba, and the water in the bottle was spent, and the child thirsted, and was not able to go on, and fell down. JFB on Genesis 21:14-15 notes the wandering in the southern border's wide desert, out of the common direction, where they lost their way, and Ishmael sank exhausted from fatigue and thirst. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. His mother took him and cast him under an olive tree and went and sat down over against him, at the distance of a bow-shot, saying, 'Let me not see the death of my child,' and as she sat she wept. JFB on Genesis 21:15 notes the mother laid his head under a bush to smell the damp, sitting a little distance away in hopeless sorrow, unable to witness his distress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. An angel of God, one of the holy ones, said unto her: 'Why weepest thou, Hagar?' The angel's question opens the deliverance, addressing Hagar's weeping directly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. 'Arise, take the child, and hold him in thine hand; for God hath heard thy voice, and hath seen the child.' God has heard Hagar's voice and seen the child, fulfilling Ishmael's name, 'God hears,' as JFB notes on Genesis 16:11. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. She opened her eyes and saw a well of water, and went and filled her bottle with water, and gave her child to drink, and arose and went towards the wilderness of Paran. JFB on Genesis 21:19 notes God opened her eyes, and whether she looked to God or not, he regarded her and directed her to a fountain close beside her, probably hidden amid brushwood, by whose waters her almost expiring son was revived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The child grew and became an archer, and God was with him, and his mother took him a wife from among the daughters of Egypt. JFB on Genesis 21:20-21 notes God was with the lad in Paran, Arabia, where his posterity ever dwelt, and his mother, on his virtual deprivation of his father, arranged his marriage among her own relatives in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. She bare him a son, and he called his name Nebaioth, for she said: 'The Lord was nigh to me when I called upon him.' Nebaioth's name is explained by his mother's confession: the Lord was near when she called. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 16. This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: Mastema proposes to God that Abraham shall be put to the proof. It divides the expulsion from the heavenly proposal that leads to the Akedah in chapter 18. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 17. In the seventh week, in the first year thereof, in the first month in this jubilee, on the twelfth of this month, there were voices in heaven regarding Abraham, that he was faithful in all that God told him, and that he loved the Lord, and that in every affliction he was faithful. Heavenly voices testify to Abraham's faithfulness in every affliction, the premise on which the coming test will turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. This verse opens Mastema's approach: and the prince Mastema came. The sentence is completed by his proposal in verse 20. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. The prince Mastema came and said before God: 'Behold, Abraham loveth Isaac his son, and he delighteth in him above all things else; bid him offer him as a burnt-offering on the altar, and thou wilt see if he will do this command, and thou wilt know if he is faithful in everything wherein thou dost try him.' Mastema proposes the Akedah as proof: command the offering of the beloved son, and faithfulness will be known by doing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. The Lord knew that Abraham was faithful in all his afflictions, for he had tried him through his country and with famine, and had tried him with the wealth of kings. The trials begin with country and famine, then the wealth of kings, the long schooling of Abraham's faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. He had tried him again through his wife, when she was torn from him, and with circumcision, and had tried him through Ishmael and Hagar his maid-servant, when he sent them away. The trials continue: the wife torn away, circumcision, and the expulsion of Ishmael and Hagar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. In everything wherein he had tried him, Abraham was found faithful, and his soul was not impatient, and he was not slow to act, for he was faithful and a lover of the Lord. The verdict on all the trials: faithful, not impatient, not slow to act, a lover of the Lord, ready for the greatest trial of chapter 18. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
