The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Song of Songs 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 I am the rose of Sharon, and the lily of the valleys.

2 As the lily among thorns, so is my love among the daughters.

3 As the apple tree among the trees of the wood, so is my beloved among the sons. I sat down under his shadow with great delight, and his fruit was sweet to my taste.

4 He brought me to the banqueting house, and his banner over me was love.

5 Stay me with flagons, comfort me with apples: for I am sick of love.

6 His left hand is under my head, and his right hand doth embrace me.

7 I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, and by the hinds of the field, that ye stir not up, nor awake my love, till he please.

8 The voice of my beloved! behold, he cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping upon the hills.

9 My beloved is like a roe or a young hart: behold, he standeth behind our wall, he looketh forth at the windows, shewing himself through the lattice.

10 My beloved spake, and said unto me, Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away.

11 For, lo, the winter is past, the rain is over and gone;

12 The flowers appear on the earth; the time of the singing of birds is come, and the voice of the turtle is heard in our land;

13 The fig tree putteth forth her green figs, and the vines with the tender grape give a good smell. Arise, my love, my fair one, and come away.

14 O my dove, that art in the clefts of the rock, in the secret places of the stairs, let me see thy countenance, let me hear thy voice; for sweet is thy voice, and thy countenance is comely.

15 Take us the foxes, the little foxes, that spoil the vines: for our vines have tender grapes.

16 My beloved is mine, and I am his: he feedeth among the lilies.

17 Until the day break, and the shadows flee away, turn, my beloved, and be thou like a roe or a young hart upon the mountains of Bether.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Spring has come and the beloved calls his love to rise and come away, past winter rain, flowering vines and singing birds. Between the lovers' exchanges sit two refrains: do not stir up love until it pleases, and catch the little foxes that spoil the tender vines. The chapter closes with the mutual claim, my beloved is mine and I am his. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. I am the rose of Sharon, and the lily of the valleys. Believers are beautiful as clothed in the righteousness of Christ, Henry says, and fragrant as adorned with the graces of his Spirit, thriving under the refreshing beams of the Sun of righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. As the lily among thorns, so is my love among the daughters. The church is weak in herself, Henry notes, yet strong in him that supports her, while the wicked, who have no love to Christ, are as thorns, worthless and hurtful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. As the apple tree among the trees of the wood, so is my beloved among the sons; I sat down under his shadow with great delight, and his fruit was sweet to my taste. The world is a barren tree to the soul, Henry says, but Christ is a fruitful one, and weary souls may find rest in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. He brought me to the banqueting house, and his banner over me was love. Christ brings the soul to seek and find comforts through his ordinances, Henry says, which are as a banqueting-house where his saints feast with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Stay me with flagons, comfort me with apples, for I am sick of love. How much better it is with the soul when sick from love to Christ, Henry observes, than when surfeited with the love of this world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. His left hand is under my head, and his right hand doth embrace me. Though Christ seemed to have withdrawn, Henry notes, he was even then a very present help, and all his saints are in his hand, which tenderly holds their aching heads. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, that ye stir not up nor awake my love till he please. Finding Christ thus nigh, Henry says, the soul is in great care that her communion with him is not interrupted, for we easily grieve the Spirit by wrong tempers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The voice of my beloved! Behold, he comes leaping upon the mountains, skipping upon the hills. The church pleases herself with thoughts of further communion with Christ, Henry says, for none besides can speak to the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. My beloved is like a roe or a young hart; behold, he stands behind our wall, looking forth at the windows, showing himself through the lattice. He comes speedily, Henry notes, and even when Christ seems to forsake, it is but for a moment; he will soon return with everlasting loving-kindness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. My beloved spoke and said unto me, Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away. Christ invites the new convert to arise from sloth and despondency, Henry says, and to leave sin and worldly vanities for union and communion with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. For lo, the winter is past, the rain is over and gone. The winter may mean years passed in ignorance and sin, unfruitful and miserable, Henry explains, or storms that accompanied conviction of guilt and danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The flowers appear on the earth, the time of the singing of birds is come, and the voice of the turtle is heard in our land. Even the unripe fruits of holiness are pleasant unto him whose grace has produced them, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The fig tree puts forth her green figs, and the vines with the tender grape give a good smell. Arise, my love, my fair one, and come away. All these encouraging tokens of divine favour, Henry says, are motives to follow Christ more fully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. O my dove, in the clefts of the rock, in the secret places of the stairs, let me see thy countenance, let me hear thy voice, for sweet is thy voice and thy countenance is comely. The church is Christ's dove, Henry says; she returns to him as her Noah, and Christ is the Rock in whom alone she can think herself safe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Take us the foxes, the little foxes, that spoil the vines, for our vines have tender grapes. The first risings of sinful thoughts, trifling pursuits, and small departures from truth are little foxes which must be removed, Henry says, for they destroy graces and comforts and crush good beginnings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. My beloved is mine, and I am his; he feeds among the lilies. He feeds among the lilies, Henry notes, showing Christ's gracious presence among believers, and it becomes them to believe this when under desertion and absence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Until the day break and the shadows flee away, turn, my beloved, and be thou like a roe upon the mountains of Bether. The shadows of the Jewish dispensation were dispelled by the dawning of the gospel day, Henry says, and a day of comfort will come after a night of desertion; Christ will come over every separating mountain to take us home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
