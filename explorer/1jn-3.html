The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 John 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called the sons of God: therefore the world knoweth us not, because it knew him not.

2 Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.

3 And every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as he is pure.

4 Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law.

5 And ye know that he was manifested to take away our sins; and in him is no sin.

6 Whosoever abideth in him sinneth not: whosoever sinneth hath not seen him, neither known him.

7 Little children, let no man deceive you: he that doeth righteousness is righteous, even as he is righteous.

8 He that committeth sin is of the devil; for the devil sinneth from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil.

9 Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remaineth in him: and he cannot sin, because he is born of God.

10 In this the children of God are manifest, and the children of the devil: whosoever doeth not righteousness is not of God, neither he that loveth not his brother.

11 For this is the message that ye heard from the beginning, that we should love one another.

12 Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother’s righteous.

13 Marvel not, my brethren, if the world hate you.

14 We know that we have passed from death unto life, because we love the brethren. He that loveth not his brother abideth in death.

15 Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer: and ye know that no murderer hath eternal life abiding in him.

16 Hereby perceive we the love of God, because he laid down his life for us: and we ought to lay down our lives for the brethren.

17 But whoso hath this world’s good, and seeth his brother have need, and shutteth up his bowels of compassion from him, how dwelleth the love of God in him?

18 My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth.

19 And hereby we know that we are of the truth, and shall assure our hearts before him.

20 For if our heart condemn us, God is greater than our heart, and knoweth all things.

21 Beloved, if our heart condemn us not, then have we confidence toward God.

22 And whatsoever we ask, we receive of him, because we keep his commandments, and do those things that are pleasing in his sight.

23 And this is his commandment, That we should believe on the name of his Son Jesus Christ, and love one another, as he gave us commandment.

24 And he that keepeth his commandments dwelleth in him, and he in him. And hereby we know that he abideth in us, by the Spirit which he hath given us.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Behold what love the Father has given, that believers are called children of God, the writer marvels, though what they will be has not yet appeared; when Christ appears they will be like him, seeing him as he is, and everyone with this hope purifies himself. Sin is lawlessness, Christ appeared to take sins away and destroy the devil's works, and the children of God are distinguished from the devil's children by righteousness and love, not like Cain who murdered his brother, but in deeds and truth, with hearts assured before God. Interpreted Editorial overview of the chapter text; verse-by-verse study-note summaries follow

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Behold the surpassing love bestowed on us, that we should be called sons of God, and be so in reality. JFB notes that with God to call is to make really to be, and that doing righteousness is a sign of adoption, not the price that purchases it; the world does not know us because it did not know the Father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Beloved, we are sons of God now, though what we shall be has not yet been visibly manifested. When he appears we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is, and continual beholding generates likeness, as the moon reflects the sun it faces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Everyone who has this hope resting on Christ purifies himself, even as he is pure. Henry warns that a hope which makes allowance for gratifying impure desires is the hope of hypocrites, not of the sons of God, and JFB adds that purification is worked by Christ's Spirit dwelling in us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Sin is defined here as transgression of the law: committing sin is going against God's law of purity, and so shows a person has no such hope of being pure as God is pure. John often states negatively what he has stated positively, showing where sin is, birth from God is not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Christ was manifested to take away our sins entirely, as the scapegoat did typically, and in him is no sin. Henry notes he took the sinless weaknesses that follow the fall, the infirmities that expose us to suffering and temptation, but never our moral proneness to sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Whoever abides in him, as the branch in the vine, does not go on sinning, and whoever sins has not seen him nor known him. JFB qualifies the ideal carefully: believers do fall into sins, but such sins are alien to the life of God and need Christ's cleansing blood at once, and in so far as a person abides in Christ, he is free from sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Let no man deceive you, as antinomians try to do: he who does righteousness is righteous, even as he is righteous. JFB explains the logic is reversed from earning: his doing does not make him righteous, but his being righteous through faith makes him do righteousness, as good fruit shows a good tree. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He who commits sin is of the devil, who sins from the beginning, and for this purpose the Son of God was manifested, to destroy the works of the devil. Christians cannot practise what Christ came to destroy, and Henry adds that a man cannot act like the devil and at the same time be a disciple of Christ Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Whoever is born of God does not commit sin, because his seed, the living word of God made by the Spirit the seed of new life, remains in him. JFB explains this as a present, energetic abiding: so long as the new life principle rules, sin can have no place, though the regenerate are sometimes surprised into sins when that principle lies dormant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. In this the children of God and the children of the devil are manifest: whoever does not do righteousness is not of God, nor whoever does not love his brother. There is no middle class between the two families, and love of the brethren is the token that distinguishes them, not loud professions or seemingly good works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The message heard from the beginning is that we should love one another, the gospel announcement of him who loved us. JFB notes this love is aimed first at brethren in Christ, children of the same family of God, rather than mankind in the abstract. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Cain is the counter-example: he was of the wicked one and slew his brother because his own works were evil and his brother's righteous. Envy and hatred of a brother's piety, because God accepted Abel's offering and rejected Cain's, is the seed of the woman's seed and the serpent's seed at enmity from the first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Do not marvel if the world hates you, for Cain is its representative and the marvel would be if the world loved you. Henry observes that none who rightly know the heart of man can wonder at the contempt and enmity of ungodly people against the children of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. We know we have passed from death to life because we love the brethren, and whoever does not love his brother abides in death. Henry treats this love as the special fruit of faith and a certain sign of being born again, not party zeal or affection for those of the same name and sentiments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Whoever hates his brother is a murderer in heart, and no murderer has eternal life abiding in him. The life of grace in a regenerate heart is the beginning of a life of glory, of which haters of their brethren must be destitute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Hereby we perceive the love of God, because Christ laid down his life for us, and we ought to lay down our lives for the brethren. Henry calls this the condescension, miracle, and mystery of divine love, that God would redeem the church with his own blood, and we should love those whom God so loved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Whoever has this world's goods, sees his brother in need, and shuts up compassion from him shows the love of God does not dwell in him. Henry warns that when the love of the world and its goods overcomes compassion for a perishing brother, every instance weakens the evidences of conversion, and habitual selfishness decides against a person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Let us not love in word or in tongue only, but in deed and in truth. The test of a true sense of Christ's love is practical compassion, not profession, and the Spirit is grieved at selfishness, leaving the selfish heart without comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Hereby we know we are of the truth and shall assure our hearts before him: deeds of love, not words alone, give the conscience its quiet ground. A well-informed conscience, heard and diligently attended to, is the way assurance is settled before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. If our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart and knows all things, so that condemnation must be weighed against his fuller knowledge, not merely our own feeling. Henry's counsel is to let conscience be well-informed and heard, since God also condemns known sin and neglected duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. If our heart does not condemn us, then we have confidence toward God. This confidence belongs to believers who, through the Spirit of adoption and faith in the great High Priest, can approach their reconciled Father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Whatever we ask, we receive, because we keep his commandments and do what is pleasing in his sight. Henry adds the condition that prayer is answered if it is good for us, and that good-will toward others, especially the brethren, must fill the hearts of those who go to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. His commandment is twofold and one: believe on the name of his Son Jesus Christ, and love one another, as he gave commandment. Faith and love belong together here, and obedience to both is the shape of the Christian life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Whoever keeps his commandments dwells in him and he in him, and we know he abides in us by the Spirit he has given us. Henry pictures this union as dwelling in Christ as an ark, refuge, and rest, and in the Father through him, a union worked by the Spirit who changes us from the power of Satan to the power of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • seed. the living word of God made by the Spirit the seed of new life, remaining in the one born of God (verse 9) Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
