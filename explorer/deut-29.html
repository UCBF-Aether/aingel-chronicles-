The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Deuteronomy 29 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 These are the words of the covenant, which the LORD commanded Moses to make with the children of Israel in the land of Moab, beside the covenant which he made with them in Horeb.

2 And Moses called unto all Israel, and said unto them, Ye have seen all that the LORD did before your eyes in the land of Egypt unto Pharaoh, and unto all his servants, and unto all his land;

3 The great temptations which thine eyes have seen, the signs, and those great miracles:

4 Yet the LORD hath not given you an heart to perceive, and eyes to see, and ears to hear, unto this day.

5 And I have led you forty years in the wilderness: your clothes are not waxen old upon you, and thy shoe is not waxen old upon thy foot.

6 Ye have not eaten bread, neither have ye drunk wine or strong drink: that ye might know that I am the LORD your God.

7 And when ye came unto this place, Sihon the king of Heshbon, and Og the king of Bashan, came out against us unto battle, and we smote them:

8 And we took their land, and gave it for an inheritance unto the Reubenites, and to the Gadites, and to the half tribe of Manasseh.

9 Keep therefore the words of this covenant, and do them, that ye may prosper in all that ye do.

10 Ye stand this day all of you before the LORD your God; your captains of your tribes, your elders, and your officers, with all the men of Israel,

11 Your little ones, your wives, and thy stranger that is in thy camp, from the hewer of thy wood unto the drawer of thy water:

12 That thou shouldest enter into covenant with the LORD thy God, and into his oath, which the LORD thy God maketh with thee this day:

13 That he may establish thee to day for a people unto himself, and that he may be unto thee a God, as he hath said unto thee, and as he hath sworn unto thy fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.

14 Neither with you only do I make this covenant and this oath;

15 But with him that standeth here with us this day before the LORD our God, and also with him that is not here with us this day:

16 (For ye know how we have dwelt in the land of Egypt; and how we came through the nations which ye passed by;

17 And ye have seen their abominations, and their idols, wood and stone, silver and gold, which were among them:)

18 Lest there should be among you man, or woman, or family, or tribe, whose heart turneth away this day from the LORD our God, to go and serve the gods of these nations; lest there should be among you a root that beareth gall and wormwood;

19 And it come to pass, when he heareth the words of this curse, that he bless himself in his heart, saying, I shall have peace, though I walk in the imagination of mine heart, to add drunkenness to thirst:

20 The LORD will not spare him, but then the anger of the LORD and his jealousy shall smoke against that man, and all the curses that are written in this book shall lie upon him, and the LORD shall blot out his name from under heaven.

21 And the LORD shall separate him unto evil out of all the tribes of Israel, according to all the curses of the covenant that are written in this book of the law:

22 So that the generation to come of your children that shall rise up after you, and the stranger that shall come from a far land, shall say, when they see the plagues of that land, and the sicknesses which the LORD hath laid upon it;

23 And that the whole land thereof is brimstone, and salt, and burning, that it is not sown, nor beareth, nor any grass groweth therein, like the overthrow of Sodom, and Gomorrah, Admah, and Zeboim, which the LORD overthrew in his anger, and in his wrath:

24 Even all nations shall say, Wherefore hath the LORD done thus unto this land? what meaneth the heat of this great anger?

25 Then men shall say, Because they have forsaken the covenant of the LORD God of their fathers, which he made with them when he brought them forth out of the land of Egypt:

26 For they went and served other gods, and worshipped them, gods whom they knew not, and whom he had not given unto them:

27 And the anger of the LORD was kindled against this land, to bring upon it all the curses that are written in this book:

28 And the LORD rooted them out of their land in anger, and in wrath, and in great indignation, and cast them into another land, as it is this day.

29 The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In Moab, Moses renews the covenant with everyone present — leaders, elders, children, strangers, even those not yet born — grounding it in what their eyes have seen in Egypt and the wilderness. The warning is aimed at the heart that turns away in secret, imagining peace while following its own stubbornness; and the chapter ends with the famous boundary: secret things belong to the Lord, but the revealed words are Israel's to do. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These are the words of the covenant which the Lord commanded Moses to make with the children of Israel in the land of Moab, beside the covenant he made with them in Horeb; the discourse continues, its subject being Israel's covenant, its privileges and its obligations, renewed now in different circumstances after they had violated its conditions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And Moses called all Israel, and said, You have seen all that the Lord did before your eyes in Egypt to Pharaoh, his servants, and all his land; the appeal was to those who had been very young at the Exodus and remembered the marvellous transactions that preceded and followed it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The temptations, signs and miracles are recalled as seen things: Moses appeals to eyes that watched Egypt and the wilderness, so the covenant rests on witnessed wonders, not on report. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Yet the Lord has not given you a heart to perceive, and eyes to see, and ears to hear, unto this day; those wonderful events made no good impression on them, for they were strangers to the grace of wisdom given liberally to all who ask. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. And I have led you forty years in the wilderness; your clothes are not waxen old upon you, and your shoe is not waxen old upon your foot, miracles continued without interruption, proclaiming the immediate hand of God and training them to habitual confidence in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. You have not eaten bread, neither have you drunk wine or strong drink, that you might know that I am the Lord your God; the daily manna and water were themselves the training. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The victories over Sihon and Og are the recent proof before the renewal: Israel has already seen two kings fall, so the covenant is made by a people who know God's power in battle, not only in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. And you took their land, and gave it for an inheritance to the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh, brilliant successes won with little exertion or loss on Israel's part. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Keep therefore the words of this covenant, and do them, that you may prosper in all that you do; faithful adherence to the covenant is the direct and sure means of obtaining its promised blessings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The whole assembly stands: captains, elders, officers and all men together, so the renewal binds the nation as one body, with no rank standing apart from the oath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Your little ones, your wives, and your stranger in your camp, from the hewer of your wood to the drawer of your water; the whole congregation, of all ages and conditions, native and naturalized stranger alike, was assembled to renew the Sinaitic covenant, none exempt from its terms. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Entering the covenant means entering its oath: the bond is sworn, not merely agreed, and the oath is what makes breaking it a matter of curses, not of changed minds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Establishment runs both ways: Israel becomes a people to God, and God becomes their God, on the ground of what he said and swore to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, for the Moab covenant renews the patriarchal oath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. But with him who stands here with us this day before the Lord, and also with him who is not here with us this day, the covenant binding future generations as well as the present assembly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Knowledge is firsthand: Israel dwelt in Egypt and passed through the nations, so the idolatries warned against are not hearsay but sights the people themselves walked past. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The idols are catalogued by material, wood, stone, silver, gold, to show their worthlessness: gods made of the same stuff as furniture and coin cannot keep a covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Lest there should be among you man, woman, family, or tribe, whose heart turns away this day from the Lord our God, to go and serve the gods of these nations, lest there should be among you a root that bears gall and wormwood; any lapsing into idolatry might prove a root of bitterness, spreading its corrupt influence all around. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. And it comes to pass, when he hears the words of this curse, that he blesses himself in his heart, saying, I shall have peace, though I walk in the imagination of my heart, to add drunkenness to thirst; some might delude themselves that taking the oath would secure the blessings even while following their own heart's devices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The Lord will not spare him, but then the anger of the Lord and his jealousy shall smoke against that man, and all the curses written in this book shall lie upon him, and the Lord shall blot out his name from under heaven; the covenant of grace has curses in reserve for transgressors, as certain, lasting, and severe as its blessings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Separation to evil is the reverse of election: the self-blessing man is cut out from all the tribes by the very curses of the covenant he swore, singled out for judgment as Israel was singled out for blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The warning is written for onlookers: a later generation and a stranger from far off will see the plagues and sicknesses and ask, so desolation itself becomes a witness that preaches the covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The land's ruin is pictured as Sodom's overthrow: brimstone, salt and burning leave nothing sown and no grass growing, so apostasy ends in a country as sterile as the cities of the plain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Even all nations ask the question: why this land, why this heat of anger, and the question itself forces the answer onto Israel's forsaken covenant rather than onto chance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The answer the nations receive names the cause plainly: forsaking the covenant made at the Exodus, not bad fortune, is why the land lies under anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. The gods served were unknown and ungiven: Israel traded a God who had revealed himself and given himself to them for deities with no history and no gift behind them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Anger is not formless: it brings upon the land exactly the curses written in this book, so judgment follows the text Israel swore rather than inventing new penalties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. And the Lord rooted them out of their land in anger, wrath, and great indignation, and cast them into another land, as it is this day; travellers in later ages, seeing Palestine desolate, would read in its ruined cities the execution of this threatening. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. The secret things belong to the Lord our God, but those things which are revealed belong to us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law; this verse answers astonishment at whether Israel could ever be so wicked, for God's providence combines goodness and severity in mystery too profound for our capacities, yet the revealed part is enough for obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
