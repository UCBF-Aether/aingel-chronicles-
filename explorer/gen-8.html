The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And God remembered Noah, and every living thing, and all the cattle that was with him in the ark: and God made a wind to pass over the earth, and the waters asswaged;

2 The fountains also of the deep and the windows of heaven were stopped, and the rain from heaven was restrained;

3 And the waters returned from off the earth continually: and after the end of the hundred and fifty days the waters were abated.

4 And the ark rested in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, upon the mountains of Ararat.

5 And the waters decreased continually until the tenth month: in the tenth month, on the first day of the month, were the tops of the mountains seen.

6 And it came to pass at the end of forty days, that Noah opened the window of the ark which he had made:

7 And he sent forth a raven, which went forth to and fro, until the waters were dried up from off the earth.

8 Also he sent forth a dove from him, to see if the waters were abated from off the face of the ground;

9 But the dove found no rest for the sole of her foot, and she returned unto him into the ark, for the waters were on the face of the whole earth: then he put forth his hand, and took her, and pulled her in unto him into the ark.

10 And he stayed yet other seven days; and again he sent forth the dove out of the ark;

11 And the dove came in to him in the evening; and, lo, in her mouth was an olive leaf pluckt off: so Noah knew that the waters were abated from off the earth.

12 And he stayed yet other seven days; and sent forth the dove; which returned not again unto him any more.

13 And it came to pass in the six hundredth and first year, in the first month, the first day of the month, the waters were dried up from off the earth: and Noah removed the covering of the ark, and looked, and, behold, the face of the ground was dry.

14 And in the second month, on the seven and twentieth day of the month, was the earth dried.

15 And God spake unto Noah, saying,

16 Go forth of the ark, thou, and thy wife, and thy sons, and thy sons’ wives with thee.

17 Bring forth with thee every living thing that is with thee, of all flesh, both of fowl, and of cattle, and of every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth; that they may breed abundantly in the earth, and be fruitful, and multiply upon the earth.

18 And Noah went forth, and his sons, and his wife, and his sons’ wives with him:

19 Every beast, every creeping thing, and every fowl, and whatsoever creepeth upon the earth, after their kinds, went forth out of the ark.

20 And Noah builded an altar unto the LORD; and took of every clean beast, and of every clean fowl, and offered burnt offerings on the altar.

21 And the LORD smelled a sweet savour; and the LORD said in his heart, I will not again curse the ground any more for man’s sake; for the imagination of man’s heart is evil from his youth; neither will I again smite any more every thing living, as I have done.

22 While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God remembers Noah, the waters recede in stages, and the ark rests on the mountains of Ararat. After the raven and the dove test the land, Noah leaves the ark, builds an altar, and God promises that seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease while the earth remains. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God remembered Noah and all the living things with him in the ark, and sent a wind over the earth, and the waters subsided. Remembered does not mean God had forgotten, but that he now acted on his covenant promise to preserve them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The fountains of the deep and the windows of heaven were stopped, and the rain was restrained. The sources of judgment are closed in reverse order: what was opened in chapter 7 is now shut. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The waters returned from off the earth continually, and after 150 days they were abated. The slow receding matches the slow prevailing: restoration, like judgment, takes time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. In the seventh month, on the seventeenth day, the ark rested on the mountains of Ararat. Resting on high ground is the first sign that the world can be inhabited again, though the household does not yet leave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The waters decreased continually until the tenth month, when the tops of the mountains were seen. The appearing of mountain tops marks steady progress, but the land is still far from dry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. After forty days Noah opened the window of the ark he had made. Noah tests conditions before acting, rather than rushing out at the first sign of land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He sent out a raven, which went forth to and fro until the waters dried from the earth. The raven, able to feed on carrion and rest elsewhere, does not return with news, so it proves little about dry land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He sent out a dove to see if the waters had abated from the ground. The dove, a cleaner bird that needs dry resting ground, is a better test of habitability. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The dove found no rest for her foot and returned to the ark, for waters were still on the whole earth, so Noah took her in. The return is itself information: the earth is not yet ready. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. He waited another seven days and sent the dove out again. Noah's patience is deliberate: he tests again after a full week rather than forcing an exit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. In the evening the dove returned with a freshly plucked olive leaf in her mouth, so Noah knew the waters had abated. The leaf is the first sign of new plant life and of land emerging enough for trees to show. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. He waited another seven days, sent the dove out, and she did not return. Her staying away signals the earth can now support her, and Noah reads the absence as permission to prepare to leave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. In Noah's 601st year, in the first month, on the first day, the waters were dried from the earth, and Noah removed the ark's covering and saw the ground was dry. Seeing dry ground is not yet leaving: he still waits for God's command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. In the second month, on the twenty-seventh day, the earth was dried. The exact date closes the flood year: from entry to full drying, more than a year has passed inside the ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. God spoke to Noah, telling him to go out of the ark. After all the signs, the exit itself comes by command, not by Noah's calculation alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The command names Noah, his wife, his sons and his sons' wives. The same household that entered together leaves together, preserved as a family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. God told him to bring out every living thing, that they might breed abundantly and multiply on the earth. The purpose of preservation is now stated: life is to fill the renewed earth again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Noah went out, with his sons, his wife and his sons' wives. Obedience completes the pattern: entry by command, waiting by patience, exit by command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Every beast, creeping thing, bird and moving thing went out of the ark after their kinds. The whole company leaves in order, kind by kind, as it entered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Noah built an altar to the LORD and offered burnt offerings of every clean beast and clean bird. Henry notes this is the first altar named, and Noah worships before he builds a house or plants a field, using the clean animals set apart for this purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. The LORD smelled a sweet savour and said he would not again curse the ground for man's sake, though man's heart is evil from youth, nor again smite every living thing. Henry stresses God does not deny human evil; he promises mercy despite it, on the ground of acceptable sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. While the earth remains, seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night shall not cease. God's promise restores the regular order of seasons, so human life can plan and plant in trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages

  • Genesis 7 — The shutting of the flood's sources reverses what was opened in chapter 7: judgment is closed in reverse order. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 8:2, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
