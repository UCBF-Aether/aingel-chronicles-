The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ecclesiastes 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Who is as the wise man? and who knoweth the interpretation of a thing? a man’s wisdom maketh his face to shine, and the boldness of his face shall be changed.

2 I counsel thee to keep the king’s commandment, and that in regard of the oath of God.

3 Be not hasty to go out of his sight: stand not in an evil thing; for he doeth whatsoever pleaseth him.

4 Where the word of a king is, there is power: and who may say unto him, What doest thou?

5 Whoso keepeth the commandment shall feel no evil thing: and a wise man’s heart discerneth both time and judgment.

6 Because to every purpose there is time and judgment, therefore the misery of man is great upon him.

7 For he knoweth not that which shall be: for who can tell him when it shall be?

8 There is no man that hath power over the spirit to retain the spirit; neither hath he power in the day of death: and there is no discharge in that war; neither shall wickedness deliver those that are given to it.

9 All this have I seen, and applied my heart unto every work that is done under the sun: there is a time wherein one man ruleth over another to his own hurt.

10 And so I saw the wicked buried, who had come and gone from the place of the holy, and they were forgotten in the city where they had so done: this is also vanity.

11 Because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, therefore the heart of the sons of men is fully set in them to do evil.

12 Though a sinner do evil an hundred times, and his days be prolonged, yet surely I know that it shall be well with them that fear God, which fear before him:

13 But it shall not be well with the wicked, neither shall he prolong his days, which are as a shadow; because he feareth not before God.

14 There is a vanity which is done upon the earth; that there be just men, unto whom it happeneth according to the work of the wicked; again, there be wicked men, to whom it happeneth according to the work of the righteous: I said that this also is vanity.

15 Then I commended mirth, because a man hath no better thing under the sun, than to eat, and to drink, and to be merry: for that shall abide with him of his labour the days of his life, which God giveth him under the sun.

16 When I applied mine heart to know wisdom, and to see the business that is done upon the earth: (for also there is that neither day nor night seeth sleep with his eyes:)

17 Then I beheld all the work of God, that a man cannot find out the work that is done under the sun: because though a man labour to seek it out, yet he shall not find it; yea farther; though a wise man think to know it, yet shall he not be able to find it.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Counsel for living under a king whose word is power, followed by the hard cases: no one has power over death, delayed punishment emboldens evildoers, and just and wicked sometimes receive each other's due. The counsel stands anyway: fear God, enjoy life, and accept that God's work cannot be found out. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Who is as the wise man, and who knows the interpretation of a thing, for a man's wisdom makes his face to shine, and the boldness of his face is changed, because wisdom gives a man both understanding and a gracious bearing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Keep the king's commandment, and that in regard of the oath of God, because obedience to the ruler is part of obedience to God, by whose ordinance the ruler stands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Be not hasty to go out of his sight, and stand not in an evil thing, for he does whatsoever pleases him, because open defiance of a king who can do as he pleases only invites ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Where the word of a king is, there is power, and who may say unto him, what doest thou, because a king's command carries authority that a subject cannot call to account. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Whoso keeps the commandment shall feel no evil thing, and a wise man's heart discerns both time and judgment, because obedience keeps a man safe, and wisdom knows when and how to act. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Because to every purpose there is time and judgment, therefore the misery of man is great upon him, since man knows there is a right season and judgment, yet cannot master either. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For he knows not that which shall be, and who can tell him when it shall be, because the future is hidden, and no man can be told the hour of what is coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. There is no man that has power over the spirit to retain the spirit, neither has he power in the day of death, and there is no discharge in that war, neither shall wickedness deliver those given to it, because death is a war from which no man is discharged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. All this he saw, and applied his heart to every work done under the sun, where one man rules over another to his own hurt, because dominion misused harms the ruler as well as the ruled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. So he saw the wicked buried, who had come and gone from the place of the holy, and they were forgotten in the city where they had done so, which is also vanity, because even a splendid funeral cannot preserve a wicked memory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, therefore the heart of the sons of men is fully set in them to do evil, because delay in punishment is mistaken for impunity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Though a sinner do evil a hundred times and his days be prolonged, yet surely he knows it shall be well with them that fear God, which fear before him, because God's patience is not God's approval. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But it shall not be well with the wicked, neither shall he prolong his days, which are as a shadow, because he fears not before God, and a life lived without that fear has no true length. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. There is a vanity done upon the earth, that there are just men unto whom it happens according to the work of the wicked, and wicked men unto whom it happens according to the work of the righteous, which he says is vanity, because present providence does not sort men visibly by desert. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Then he commended mirth, because a man has no better thing under the sun than to eat and drink and be merry, for that shall abide with him of his labour all the days of his life which God gives him under the sun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. When he applied his heart to know wisdom and to see the business done upon the earth, even one who neither day nor night sees sleep with his eyes, he found the labour of knowledge without rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Then he beheld all the work of God, that a man cannot find out the work done under the sun, because though a man labour to seek it out, yet he shall not find it, and though a wise man think to know it, yet shall he not be able to find it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
