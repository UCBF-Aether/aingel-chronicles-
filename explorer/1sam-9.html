The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now there was a man of Benjamin, whose name was Kish, the son of Abiel, the son of Zeror, the son of Bechorath, the son of Aphiah, a Benjamite, a mighty man of power.

2 And he had a son, whose name was Saul, a choice young man, and a goodly: and there was not among the children of Israel a goodlier person than he: from his shoulders and upward he was higher than any of the people.

3 And the asses of Kish Saul’s father were lost. And Kish said to Saul his son, Take now one of the servants with thee, and arise, go seek the asses.

4 And he passed through mount Ephraim, and passed through the land of Shalisha, but they found them not: then they passed through the land of Shalim, and there they were not: and he passed through the land of the Benjamites, but they found them not.

5 And when they were come to the land of Zuph, Saul said to his servant that was with him, Come, and let us return; lest my father leave caring for the asses, and take thought for us.

6 And he said unto him, Behold now, there is in this city a man of God, and he is an honourable man; all that he saith cometh surely to pass: now let us go thither; peradventure he can shew us our way that we should go.

7 Then said Saul to his servant, But, behold, if we go, what shall we bring the man? for the bread is spent in our vessels, and there is not a present to bring to the man of God: what have we?

8 And the servant answered Saul again, and said, Behold, I have here at hand the fourth part of a shekel of silver: that will I give to the man of God, to tell us our way.

9 (Beforetime in Israel, when a man went to enquire of God, thus he spake, Come, and let us go to the seer: for he that is now called a Prophet was beforetime called a Seer.)

10 Then said Saul to his servant, Well said; come, let us go. So they went unto the city where the man of God was.

11 And as they went up the hill to the city, they found young maidens going out to draw water, and said unto them, Is the seer here?

12 And they answered them, and said, He is; behold, he is before you: make haste now, for he came to day to the city; for there is a sacrifice of the people to day in the high place:

13 As soon as ye be come into the city, ye shall straightway find him, before he go up to the high place to eat: for the people will not eat until he come, because he doth bless the sacrifice; and afterwards they eat that be bidden. Now therefore get you up; for about this time ye shall find him.

14 And they went up into the city: and when they were come into the city, behold, Samuel came out against them, for to go up to the high place.

15 Now the LORD had told Samuel in his ear a day before Saul came, saying,

16 To morrow about this time I will send thee a man out of the land of Benjamin, and thou shalt anoint him to be captain over my people Israel, that he may save my people out of the hand of the Philistines: for I have looked upon my people, because their cry is come unto me.

17 And when Samuel saw Saul, the LORD said unto him, Behold the man whom I spake to thee of! this same shall reign over my people.

18 Then Saul drew near to Samuel in the gate, and said, Tell me, I pray thee, where the seer’s house is.

19 And Samuel answered Saul, and said, I am the seer: go up before me unto the high place; for ye shall eat with me to day, and to morrow I will let thee go, and will tell thee all that is in thine heart.

20 And as for thine asses that were lost three days ago, set not thy mind on them; for they are found. And on whom is all the desire of Israel? Is it not on thee, and on all thy father’s house?

21 And Saul answered and said, Am not I a Benjamite, of the smallest of the tribes of Israel? and my family the least of all the families of the tribe of Benjamin? wherefore then speakest thou so to me?

22 And Samuel took Saul and his servant, and brought them into the parlour, and made them sit in the chiefest place among them that were bidden, which were about thirty persons.

23 And Samuel said unto the cook, Bring the portion which I gave thee, of which I said unto thee, Set it by thee.

24 And the cook took up the shoulder, and that which was upon it, and set it before Saul. And Samuel said, Behold that which is left! set it before thee, and eat: for unto this time hath it been kept for thee since I said, I have invited the people. So Saul did eat with Samuel that day.

25 And when they were come down from the high place into the city, Samuel communed with Saul upon the top of the house.

26 And they arose early: and it came to pass about the spring of the day, that Samuel called Saul to the top of the house, saying, Up, that I may send thee away. And Saul arose, and they went out both of them, he and Samuel, abroad.

27 And as they were going down to the end of the city, Samuel said to Saul, Bid the servant pass on before us, (and he passed on), but stand thou still a while, that I may shew thee the word of God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Kish of Benjamin, a mighty man of power, loses his asses and sends his son Saul, a choice young man taller from the shoulders up than any of the people, with a servant to seek them. The search carries Saul through Ephraim, Shalisha, Shalim and Benjamin to the land of Zuph, where the servant suggests consulting the man of God in the city, and the fourth part of a shekel is found for a present. Maidens drawing water direct them to the seer, who is coming that day for a sacrifice at the high place; the LORD had told Samuel a day before that tomorrow he would send a man out of Benjamin to be anointed captain over Israel. Samuel meets Saul at the gate, seats him in the chiefest place among about thirty guests, gives him the reserved shoulder portion, communes with him on the housetop, and at dawn sends the servant ahead so that he may show Saul the word of God. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. There was a man of Benjamin named Kish, a mighty man of power, whose family line is given in full. From this ordinary Benjamite house God would raise Israel's first king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Kish had a son named Saul, a choice young man, goodly beyond all Israel, taller from the shoulders up than any of the people. The people's king would first commend himself to their eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Kish's asses were lost, and he told Saul to take a servant and go seek them. Saul readily went, and his obedience to his father was praiseworthy, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. He passed through Mount Ephraim, Shalisha, and Shalim, and through Benjamin, but found them not. The long, fruitless search carried him steadily toward Ramah and Samuel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. In the land of Zuph, Saul said to his servant, let us return, lest his father stop caring for the asses and start worrying for them. Saul's thoughtfulness for his father shows the same obedient character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. The servant said, there is in this city a man of God, an honourable man whose every word comes to pass; let us go to him, and perhaps he can show us our way. Wherever we are, Henry counsels, we should use our opportunities of acquainting ourselves with those who are wise and good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Saul asked what they could bring the man, since their bread was spent and they had no present for the man of God. Many, Henry observes, will consult a man of God if he comes in their way who would not go a step out of their way to get wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The servant had at hand the fourth part of a shekel of silver, which he would give the man of God to tell them their way. Samuel needed not their money, Henry notes, nor would he have denied advice without it; the gift was a token of respect for his office, according to the usage of those times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Beforetime in Israel, one who went to enquire of God said, Come, let us go to the seer, for he that is now called a Prophet was beforetime called a Seer. The note explains the older name for the office Samuel held. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Saul said, Well said, come, let us go, and they went to the city where the man of God was. A small errand and a servant's suggestion were turning Saul's steps toward his destiny. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. As they went up the hill to the city, they found young maidens going out to draw water and asked, Is the seer here? Even the maid-servants of the city could direct them to the prophet — it is no small benefit, Henry says, to live in religious and holy places, and we should always be ready to help those seeking God's prophets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The maidens answered that he was before them and to make haste, for he had come that day to the city because there was a sacrifice of the people that day in the high place. They had heard of the sacrifice and could tell of the necessity for Samuel's presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. As soon as they entered the city they would find him before he went up to eat, for the people would not eat until he blessed the sacrifice, and afterwards those bidden would eat. The maidens' directions were exact, and Saul was to hurry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. They went up into the city, and as they entered, Samuel came out against them to go up to the high place. The seeker and the prophet met at the very gate, by God's ordering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Now the LORD had told Samuel in his ear a day before Saul came. God had prepared his prophet before the king arrived. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Though God had granted Israel's request in displeasure, Henry notes, he still sent them a man to save them from the Philistines, listening graciously to their cry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. When Samuel saw Saul, the LORD said, Behold the man whom I spake to thee of! This same shall reign over my people. The sign and the man arrived together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Saul drew near to Samuel in the gate and asked where the seer's house was, not knowing he spoke to the seer himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Samuel answered, I am the seer; go up before me to the high place, for ye shall eat with me today, and tomorrow I will let thee go and tell thee all that is in thine heart. Samuel, so far from envying Saul, Henry observes, was the first and most forward to do him honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

The lost asses were already found, and a far greater matter — the desire of Israel — now rested on Saul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Saul answered, Am not I a Benjamite, of the smallest tribe, and my family the least of Benjamin's families? Wherefore speakest thou so to me? His humility at the word was genuine; the smallest tribe's least family was chosen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 24. The cook set the shoulder before Saul, and Samuel said, Behold that which is left, set it before thee and eat, for it has been kept for thee since I said I had invited the people. So Saul ate with Samuel that day, the reserved portion marking him out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. When they came down from the high place into the city, Samuel communed with Saul upon the top of the house. That evening, and early the next morning, Samuel communed with Saul on the flat roof, Henry notes, convincing him that he was the person God had fixed upon for the government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. They arose early, and about the spring of the day Samuel called Saul to the top of the house, saying, Up, that I may send thee away, and they went out together. How different are the Lord's purposes for us from our own intentions, Henry reflects: Saul went out to seek asses and literally found a kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. As they went down to the end of the city, Samuel told Saul to bid the servant pass on, but to stand still a while, that he might show him the word of God. The private word would come only after the servant had gone ahead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
