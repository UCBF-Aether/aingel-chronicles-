The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Titus 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Put them in mind to be subject to principalities and powers, to obey magistrates, to be ready to every good work,

2 To speak evil of no man, to be no brawlers, but gentle, shewing all meekness unto all men.

3 For we ourselves also were sometimes foolish, disobedient, deceived, serving divers lusts and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, and hating one another.

4 But after that the kindness and love of God our Saviour toward man appeared,

5 Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;

6 Which he shed on us abundantly through Jesus Christ our Saviour;

7 That being justified by his grace, we should be made heirs according to the hope of eternal life.

8 This is a faithful saying, and these things I will that thou affirm constantly, that they which have believed in God might be careful to maintain good works. These things are good and profitable unto men.

9 But avoid foolish questions, and genealogies, and contentions, and strivings about the law; for they are unprofitable and vain.

10 A man that is an heretick after the first and second admonition reject;

11 Knowing that he that is such is subverted, and sinneth, being condemned of himself.

12 When I shall send Artemas unto thee, or Tychicus, be diligent to come unto me to Nicopolis: for I have determined there to winter.

13 Bring Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their journey diligently, that nothing be wanting unto them.

14 And let ours also learn to maintain good works for necessary uses, that they be not unfruitful.

15 All that are with me salute thee. Greet them that love us in the faith. Grace be with you all. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Remind them, Paul says, to be subject to rulers and authorities, obedient, gentle and courteous to all, remembering they themselves were once foolish, disobedient and enslaved to passions. But God's kindness appeared, and he saved them not by works of righteousness but by mercy, through washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit, so that, justified by grace, they might become heirs of eternal life and devote themselves to good works, avoiding foolish controversies and a divisive person after two warnings. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Believers are to be reminded to submit to rulers and authorities, obey magistrates, and be ready for every good work. Spiritual privilege does not cancel civil duty; it confirms it, and Cretan proneness to riot made the reminder necessary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. They must speak evil of no one, avoid quarrelling, and be gentle, showing all meekness to all people. Such conduct toward all follows from the universality of God's grace toward all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. We too were once foolish, disobedient, deceived, enslaved to various lusts, living in malice and envy, hateful and hating one another. Remembering our own past sins should make us lenient toward others, for grace alone remedied our folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. But then the kindness and love of God our Saviour toward mankind appeared. Salvation is owed to God's benign goodness, not to any merit in us that would let us despise others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He saved us not because of righteous works we had done, but according to his mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit. Henry stresses that works are found in the saved but are not among the causes of salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. That Spirit he poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Saviour, the channel and Mediator of the gift. The pouring out covers grace received before, in, and after baptism. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The purpose was that, being justified by his grace, we should become heirs according to the hope of eternal life. Justification is free forgiveness through Christ's righteousness received by faith, not anything merited by the sinner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. This is a faithful saying, and Titus must affirm it constantly, so that believers will be careful to maintain good works, which are good and profitable to people. Declared grace presses the necessity of good works rather than excusing their absence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Foolish questions, genealogies, contentions, and strivings about the law must be avoided, for they are unprofitable and vain. They yield no moral fruit and distract from the practical duties grace teaches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. A divisive, self-willed heretic is to be rejected after a first and second admonition. Rejection here means having nothing more to do with him in admonition or intercourse, not yet formal excommunication. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Such a person is perverted and sins wilfully against knowledge, being condemned by himself. After repeated warning he cannot claim that no one told him better. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. When Paul sends Artemas or Tychicus to take Titus's place in Crete, Titus is to come diligently to Paul at Nicopolis, where Paul has determined to winter. Tychicus was well qualified, having twice carried Paul's letters from Rome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Titus is to help Zenas the lawyer and Apollos forward on their journey diligently, so that nothing is lacking to them. Supplying travellers' needs was a concrete form of the hospitality and good works urged throughout the letter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. And our people also must learn to maintain good works for necessary uses, so that they are not unfruitful. Christianity obliges believers to honest labour and to meeting the real needs of fellow workers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. All with Paul salute Titus, who is to greet those who love them in the faith, and grace is wished to them all. Grace, Henry notes, is the chief thing to be wished and prayed for, being all good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
