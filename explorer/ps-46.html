The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 46 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.

2 Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea;

3 Though the waters thereof roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with the swelling thereof. Selah.

4 There is a river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacles of the most High.

5 God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved: God shall help her, and that right early.

6 The heathen raged, the kingdoms were moved: he uttered his voice, the earth melted.

7 The LORD of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge. Selah.

8 Come, behold the works of the LORD, what desolations he hath made in the earth.

9 He maketh wars to cease unto the end of the earth; he breaketh the bow, and cutteth the spear in sunder; he burneth the chariot in the fire.

10 Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth.

11 The LORD of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge. Selah.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God our refuge: though earth be removed and mountains carried into the sea, waters roaring, a river gladdens the city of God, where he dwells and she shall not be moved. Nations rage and melt at his voice, wars cease, bows broken, chariots burned, 'Be still, and know that I am God,' with the refrain: the Lord of hosts is with us. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble: refuge, strength, and a help very present — not distant — in trouble are the threefold ground of fearlessness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Therefore we will not fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea: even earth's removal and mountains in the sea cannot move those whose refuge is God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Though the waters roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with the swelling thereof: roaring, troubled waters and shaking mountains complete the picture of cosmic upheaval met without fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. There is a river, whose streams shall make glad the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacles of the Most High: against the roaring sea stands a gladdening river — God's quiet provision for his city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved; God shall help her, and that right early: God's indwelling presence makes the city unmovable, and his help comes early, at dawn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The heathen raged, the kingdoms were moved; he uttered his voice, the earth melted: raging nations and moved kingdoms dissolve at a single utterance — the earth melts before his voice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The Lord of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge: the refrain names him Lord of hosts, with us, and God of Jacob, our refuge — covenant and armies together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Come, behold the works of the Lord, what desolations he has made in the earth: the invitation is to behold desolations — God's judgments on the earth are to be seen, not hidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. He makes wars to cease to the end of the earth; he breaks the bow, cuts the spear in sunder, and burns the chariot in the fire: war's weapons are broken, cut, and burned — peace is made by disarming, to earth's end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Be still, and know that I am God; I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth: stillness is commanded as the way to know God, whose exaltation among nations and in the earth is certain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The Lord of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge: the refrain returns, closing the psalm as it steadied its middle — presence and refuge are the last words. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
