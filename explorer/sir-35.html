The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Sirach 35 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 He that keepeth the law bringeth offerings enough: he that taketh heed to the commandment offereth a peace offering.

2 He that requiteth a goodturn offereth fine flour; and he that giveth alms sacrificeth praise.

3 To depart from wickedness is a thing pleasing to the Lord; and to forsake unrighteousness is a propitiation.

4 Thou shalt not appear empty before the Lord.

5 For all these things [are to be done] because of the commandment.

6 The offering of the righteous maketh the altar fat, and the sweet savour thereof is before the most High.

7 The sacrifice of a just man is acceptable. and the memorial thereof shall never be forgotten.

8 Give the Lord his honour with a good eye, and diminish not the firstfruits of thine hands.

9 In all thy gifts shew a cheerful countenance, and dedicate thy tithes with gladness.

10 Give unto the most High according as he hath enriched thee; and as thou hast gotten, give with a cheerful eye.

11 For the Lord recompenseth, and will give thee seven times as much.

12 Do not think to corrupt with gifts; for such he will not receive: and trust not to unrighteous sacrifices; for the Lord is judge, and with him is no respect of persons.

13 He will not accept any person against a poor man, but will hear the prayer of the oppressed.

14 He will not despise the supplication of the fatherless; nor the widow, when she poureth out her complaint.

15 Do not the tears run down the widow's cheeks? and is not her cry against him that causeth them to fall?

16 He that serveth the Lord shall be accepted with favour, and his prayer shall reach unto the clouds.

17 The prayer of the humble pierceth the clouds: and till it come nigh, he will not be comforted; and will not depart, till the most High shall behold to judge righteously, and execute judgment.

18 For the Lord will not be slack, neither will the Mighty be patient toward them, till he have smitten in sunder the loins of the unmerciful, and repayed vengeance to the heathen; till he have taken away the multitude of the proud, and broken the sceptre of the unrighteous;

19 Till he have rendered to every man according to his deeds, and to the works of men according to their devices; till he have judged the cause of his people, and made them to rejoice in his mercy.

20 Mercy is seasonable in the time of affliction, as clouds of rain in the time of drought.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Keeping the law brings offerings enough, and heeding the commandment offers a peace offering. The Lord recompenses and will give seven times as much, and the chapter closes with mercy seasonable in affliction, as clouds of rain in the time of drought. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. He who keeps the law brings offerings enough; he who takes heed to the commandment offers a peace offering. Law-keeping is offering enough, and heeding the commandment is itself a peace offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. He who requites a good turn offers fine flour, and he who gives alms sacrifices praise. Repaying kindness is a fine-flour offering, and almsgiving is a sacrifice of praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. To depart from wickedness is pleasing to the Lord, and to forsake unrighteousness is a propitiation. Departing from wickedness pleases God, and forsaking unrighteousness atones. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. You will not appear empty before the Lord. No one should come before God empty-handed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. For all these things are to be done because of the commandment. Offerings and departures from evil are all done because God commanded them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. The offering of the righteous makes the altar fat, and the sweet savour thereof is before the Most High. Righteous offerings fatten the altar, and their sweet savour rises before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. The sacrifice of a just man is acceptable, and the memorial thereof will never be forgotten. A just man's sacrifice is accepted, and its memorial is never forgotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. Give the Lord his honour with a good eye, and do not diminish the firstfruits of your hands. God's honour should be given generously, with a good eye, and firstfruits must not be diminished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. In all your gifts show a cheerful countenance, and dedicate your tithes with gladness. Gifts should be given with a cheerful face, and tithes dedicated gladly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Give to the Most High according as he has enriched you, and as you have gotten, give with a cheerful eye. Giving should match God's enrichment of you, given with a cheerful eye in proportion to what was gotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. For the Lord recompenses, and will give you seven times as much. God repays, returning seven times as much as given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. Do not think to corrupt with gifts, for such he will not receive; and do not trust to unrighteous sacrifices, for the Lord is judge, and with him is no respect of persons. God cannot be bribed with gifts and unrighteous sacrifices cannot be trusted, since he judges without respect of persons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. He will not accept any person against a poor man, but will hear the prayer of the oppressed. God does not accept persons against the poor, but hears the oppressed man's prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. He will not despise the supplication of the fatherless, nor the widow, when she pours out her complaint. Fatherless supplication and a widow's poured-out complaint are not despised by God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. Do not the tears run down the widow's cheeks? And is not her cry against him who causes them to fall? The widow's tears run down her cheeks, and her cry stands against whoever caused them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. He who serves the Lord will be accepted with favour, and his prayer will reach to the clouds. The Lord's servant is accepted with favour, and his prayer reaches the clouds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. The prayer of the humble pierces the clouds, and till it comes near, he will not be comforted, and will not depart, till the Most High beholds to judge righteously and execute judgment. Humble prayer pierces the clouds and persists uncomforted until God looks down to judge righteously. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. For the Lord will not be slack, nor will the Mighty be patient toward them, till he has smitten in sunder the loins of the unmerciful, repaid vengeance to the heathen, taken away the multitude of the proud, and broken the sceptre of the unrighteous. God will not delay or be patient toward the unmerciful, heathen, proud, and unrighteous until he breaks them and their sceptres. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 19. Till he has rendered to every man according to his deeds, and to the works of men according to their devices; till he has judged the cause of his people, and made them rejoice in his mercy. Judgment renders to each according to deeds and devices, judges his people's cause, and makes them rejoice in his mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 20. Mercy is seasonable in the time of affliction, as clouds of rain in the time of drought. Mercy in affliction is as timely as rain clouds in drought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
