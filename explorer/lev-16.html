The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 16 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses after the death of the two sons of Aaron, when they offered before the LORD, and died;

2 And the LORD said unto Moses, Speak unto Aaron thy brother, that he come not at all times into the holy place within the vail before the mercy seat, which is upon the ark; that he die not: for I will appear in the cloud upon the mercy seat.

3 Thus shall Aaron come into the holy place: with a young bullock for a sin offering, and a ram for a burnt offering.

4 He shall put on the holy linen coat, and he shall have the linen breeches upon his flesh, and shall be girded with a linen girdle, and with the linen mitre shall he be attired: these are holy garments; therefore shall he wash his flesh in water, and so put them on.

5 And he shall take of the congregation of the children of Israel two kids of the goats for a sin offering, and one ram for a burnt offering.

6 And Aaron shall offer his bullock of the sin offering, which is for himself, and make an atonement for himself, and for his house.

7 And he shall take the two goats, and present them before the LORD at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

8 And Aaron shall cast lots upon the two goats; one lot for the LORD, and the other lot for the scapegoat.

9 And Aaron shall bring the goat upon which the LORD’s lot fell, and offer him for a sin offering.

10 But the goat, on which the lot fell to be the scapegoat, shall be presented alive before the LORD, to make an atonement with him, and to let him go for a scapegoat into the wilderness.

11 And Aaron shall bring the bullock of the sin offering, which is for himself, and shall make an atonement for himself, and for his house, and shall kill the bullock of the sin offering which is for himself:

12 And he shall take a censer full of burning coals of fire from off the altar before the LORD, and his hands full of sweet incense beaten small, and bring it within the vail:

13 And he shall put the incense upon the fire before the LORD, that the cloud of the incense may cover the mercy seat that is upon the testimony, that he die not:

14 And he shall take of the blood of the bullock, and sprinkle it with his finger upon the mercy seat eastward; and before the mercy seat shall he sprinkle of the blood with his finger seven times.

15 Then shall he kill the goat of the sin offering, that is for the people, and bring his blood within the vail, and do with that blood as he did with the blood of the bullock, and sprinkle it upon the mercy seat, and before the mercy seat:

16 And he shall make an atonement for the holy place, because of the uncleanness of the children of Israel, and because of their transgressions in all their sins: and so shall he do for the tabernacle of the congregation, that remaineth among them in the midst of their uncleanness.

17 And there shall be no man in the tabernacle of the congregation when he goeth in to make an atonement in the holy place, until he come out, and have made an atonement for himself, and for his household, and for all the congregation of Israel.

18 And he shall go out unto the altar that is before the LORD, and make an atonement for it; and shall take of the blood of the bullock, and of the blood of the goat, and put it upon the horns of the altar round about.

19 And he shall sprinkle of the blood upon it with his finger seven times, and cleanse it, and hallow it from the uncleanness of the children of Israel.

20 And when he hath made an end of reconciling the holy place, and the tabernacle of the congregation, and the altar, he shall bring the live goat:

21 And Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Israel, and all their transgressions in all their sins, putting them upon the head of the goat, and shall send him away by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness:

22 And the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited: and he shall let go the goat in the wilderness.

23 And Aaron shall come into the tabernacle of the congregation, and shall put off the linen garments, which he put on when he went into the holy place, and shall leave them there:

24 And he shall wash his flesh with water in the holy place, and put on his garments, and come forth, and offer his burnt offering, and the burnt offering of the people, and make an atonement for himself, and for the people.

25 And the fat of the sin offering shall he burn upon the altar.

26 And he that let go the goat for the scapegoat shall wash his clothes, and bathe his flesh in water, and afterward come into the camp.

27 And the bullock for the sin offering, and the goat for the sin offering, whose blood was brought in to make atonement in the holy place, shall one carry forth without the camp; and they shall burn in the fire their skins, and their flesh, and their dung.

28 And he that burneth them shall wash his clothes, and bathe his flesh in water, and afterward he shall come into the camp.

29 And this shall be a statute for ever unto you: that in the seventh month, on the tenth day of the month, ye shall afflict your souls, and do no work at all, whether it be one of your own country, or a stranger that sojourneth among you:

30 For on that day shall the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD.

31 It shall be a sabbath of rest unto you, and ye shall afflict your souls, by a statute for ever.

32 And the priest, whom he shall anoint, and whom he shall consecrate to minister in the priest’s office in his father’s stead, shall make the atonement, and shall put on the linen clothes, even the holy garments:

33 And he shall make an atonement for the holy sanctuary, and he shall make an atonement for the tabernacle of the congregation, and for the altar, and he shall make an atonement for the priests, and for all the people of the congregation.

34 And this shall be an everlasting statute unto you, to make an atonement for the children of Israel for all their sins once a year. And he did as the LORD commanded Moses.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the yearly atonement rite, set after the death of Nadab and Abihu. Aaron the high priest alone, and only on this day, enters within the veil, in plain linen, first offering a bullock for himself and his house. Two goats are then presented: one lot for the LORD is sacrificed as the people's sin offering and its blood sprinkled on and before the mercy seat, and the live goat has all Israel's sins confessed over it and is sent away into the wilderness. The day is kept in the seventh month, on the tenth day, as a sabbath of rest on which souls are afflicted and no work is done, by a perpetual statute. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Mercy seat. The cover of the ark, within the veil, before which the high priest sprinkles the blood of the sin offerings on the yearly atonement day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Scapegoat. The live goat on which the lot for Azazel falls: Aaron confesses all Israel's sins over it and sends it away into the wilderness, bearing their iniquities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD speaks to Moses after Nadab and Abihu's death, a catastrophe that filled Aaron with apprehension lest guilt be entailed on his house, and this law secures the Aaronic order by due observance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Aaron shall not come at all times into the holy place within the veil, lest he die, for the LORD will appear in the cloud upon the mercy seat, and only the high priest may enter there, once a year, to inspire reverence for the most holy place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Aaron shall come with a young bullock for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering, victims brought alive but not sacrificed until the ceremonies between verses 3 and 11 are complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. He shall put on holy linen coat, breeches, girdle, and mitre, washing his flesh first, a plain linen dress like common Levites, since he atones for his own sins as a humble suppliant, highest and lowest being on a level as sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He shall take from Israel two kids of the goats for a sin offering and one ram for a burnt offering, the goats, though used differently, constituting only one offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Aaron shall offer his bullock of the sin offering and make atonement for himself and his house, the priests, whose sins are judicially transferred by laying hands on the victim's head. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He shall take the two goats and present them before the LORD at the tabernacle door, both presented before their disposal is decided. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He shall cast lots on the two goats, one lot for the LORD and the other for the scapegoat, the lots being gold pieces inscribed for the LORD and for Azazel, shaken in an urn and placed on each goat's head. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The goat on which the LORD's lot falls he shall bring and offer for a sin offering, as the people's sin offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The goat on which the scapegoat lot falls shall be presented alive before the LORD to make atonement with him and to let him go into the wilderness, the two goats together forming one atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Aaron shall bring his bullock, make atonement for himself and his house, and kill it with his own hands, the first part of the service solemnising his mind and the people's by sacrifices for their sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. He shall take a censer full of burning coals from the altar and his hands full of sweet incense beaten small and bring it within the veil, amid the solemn attention and anxious prayers of the multitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He shall put the incense on the fire before the LORD, that the cloud of incense may cover the mercy seat, that he die not, the fragrant smoke preventing presumptuous gazing at the LORD's throne and being the cloud in which He appears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. He shall take the bullock's blood and sprinkle it with his finger once upon the mercy seat eastward, on the side next to himself, and seven times before the mercy seat, on the front of the ark. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. He shall kill the goat of the people's sin offering, bring its blood within the veil, and do with it as with the bullock's blood, sprinkling it upon and before the mercy seat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He shall make atonement for the holy place because of Israel's uncleanness and transgressions in all their sins, and so for the tabernacle remaining among them in their uncleanness, the whole tabernacle being stained by a guilty people's sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. No man shall be in the tabernacle when he goes in to make atonement until he comes out, having made atonement for himself, his household, and all Israel, the exclusion marking the priests' unworthiness and the impurities of their service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He shall go out to the altar before the LORD, make atonement for it, and put blood of the bullock and goat on its horns round about, the mingled blood showing priests and people equally need atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. He shall sprinkle blood on the altar with his finger seven times, and cleanse and hallow it from Israel's uncleanness, the sanctuary thus purified so the LORD may continue to dwell among them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. When he has finished reconciling holy place, tabernacle, and altar, he shall bring the live goat, already presented before the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Aaron shall lay both hands on the live goat's head and confess over it all Israel's iniquities, transgressions, and sins, putting them on the goat, and send it away by a fit man into the wilderness, transferring them to the goat as substitute, a typical reference to Christ who bore away our sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The goat shall bear all their iniquities to a land not inhabited, and he shall let it go in the wilderness, in early times loosed to escape, in Christ's time carried to a high rock twelve miles from Jerusalem and thrust over it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Aaron shall come into the tabernacle, put off the linen garments worn in the holy place, and leave them there, laying aside plain linen for the remaining service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. He shall wash his flesh in water in the holy place, put on his pontifical garments, come forth, and offer his burnt offering and the people's burnt offering, making atonement for himself and the people, the two reserved rams. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The fat of the sin offering he shall burn upon the altar, the altar portion of the sin offerings whose carcasses are dealt with outside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. He who lets go the scapegoat shall wash his clothes, bathe his flesh in water, and afterward come into the camp, as with those who burn the sin-offering carcasses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. The bullock and goat for sin offerings, whose blood was brought in to make atonement in the holy place, shall be carried forth without the camp, and their skins, flesh, and dung burnt in the fire, in conformity with the general sin-offering law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. He who burns them shall wash his clothes, bathe his flesh in water, and afterward come into the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. This shall be a perpetual statute, that in the seventh month, on the tenth day, they shall afflict their souls and do no work, whether native or sojourning stranger, this annual expiation being observed as a solemn fast and sabbath, with death for labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. For on that day the priest shall make atonement for you, to cleanse you, that you may be clean from all your sins before the LORD, the year's sins, irreverences, and impurities being expiated at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. It shall be a sabbath of rest to you, and you shall afflict your souls, by a perpetual statute, kept as a holy convocation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The priest who is anointed and consecrated to minister in his father's stead shall make the atonement and put on the linen clothes, the holy garments, so the office continues in succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. He shall make atonement for the holy sanctuary, the tabernacle, the altar, the priests, and all the people of the congregation, the whole order of atonement being repeated by each successor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. This shall be an everlasting statute, to make atonement for Israel for all their sins once a year, and Aaron did as the LORD commanded Moses, the public reading of this chapter with Leviticus 23 producing salutary impressions of sin and duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Connections to other passages

  • The study note for verse 34 connects this passage to Leviticus 23: This chapter's yearly atonement statute is read publicly with Leviticus 23, the feast calendar that appoints the day of atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
