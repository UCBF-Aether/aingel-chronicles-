The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the three and twentieth year of Joash the son of Ahaziah king of Judah Jehoahaz the son of Jehu began to reign over Israel in Samaria, and reigned seventeen years.

2 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, and followed the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, which made Israel to sin; he departed not therefrom.

3 And the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel, and he delivered them into the hand of Hazael king of Syria, and into the hand of Benhadad the son of Hazael, all their days.

4 And Jehoahaz besought the LORD, and the LORD hearkened unto him: for he saw the oppression of Israel, because the king of Syria oppressed them.

5 (And the LORD gave Israel a saviour, so that they went out from under the hand of the Syrians: and the children of Israel dwelt in their tents, as beforetime.

6 Nevertheless they departed not from the sins of the house of Jeroboam, who made Israel sin, but walked therein: and there remained the grove also in Samaria.)

7 Neither did he leave of the people to Jehoahaz but fifty horsemen, and ten chariots, and ten thousand footmen; for the king of Syria had destroyed them, and had made them like the dust by threshing.

8 Now the rest of the acts of Jehoahaz, and all that he did, and his might, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

9 And Jehoahaz slept with his fathers; and they buried him in Samaria: and Joash his son reigned in his stead.

10 In the thirty and seventh year of Joash king of Judah began Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz to reign over Israel in Samaria, and reigned sixteen years.

11 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD; he departed not from all the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel sin: but he walked therein.

12 And the rest of the acts of Joash, and all that he did, and his might wherewith he fought against Amaziah king of Judah, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

13 And Joash slept with his fathers; and Jeroboam sat upon his throne: and Joash was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel.

14 Now Elisha was fallen sick of his sickness whereof he died. And Joash the king of Israel came down unto him, and wept over his face, and said, O my father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof.

15 And Elisha said unto him, Take bow and arrows. And he took unto him bow and arrows.

16 And he said to the king of Israel, Put thine hand upon the bow. And he put his hand upon it: and Elisha put his hands upon the king’s hands.

17 And he said, Open the window eastward. And he opened it. Then Elisha said, Shoot. And he shot. And he said, The arrow of the LORD’s deliverance, and the arrow of deliverance from Syria: for thou shalt smite the Syrians in Aphek, till thou have consumed them.

18 And he said, Take the arrows. And he took them. And he said unto the king of Israel, Smite upon the ground. And he smote thrice, and stayed.

19 And the man of God was wroth with him, and said, Thou shouldest have smitten five or six times; then hadst thou smitten Syria till thou hadst consumed it: whereas now thou shalt smite Syria but thrice.

20 And Elisha died, and they buried him. And the bands of the Moabites invaded the land at the coming in of the year.

21 And it came to pass, as they were burying a man, that, behold, they spied a band of men; and they cast the man into the sepulchre of Elisha: and when the man was let down, and touched the bones of Elisha, he revived, and stood up on his feet.

22 But Hazael king of Syria oppressed Israel all the days of Jehoahaz.

23 And the LORD was gracious unto them, and had compassion on them, and had respect unto them, because of his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and would not destroy them, neither cast he them from his presence as yet.

24 So Hazael king of Syria died; and Benhadad his son reigned in his stead.

25 And Jehoash the son of Jehoahaz took again out of the hand of Benhadad the son of Hazael the cities, which he had taken out of the hand of Jehoahaz his father by war. Three times did Joash beat him, and recovered the cities of Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jehoahaz's and Joash's reigns in Israel pass under Syrian oppression, eased when God hears their cry; Elisha dies, and a dead man tossed into his grave revives at touching his bones. The chapter closes with Joash recovering Israel's cities from Ben-hadad three times, as Elisha had promised. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the twenty-third year of Joash son of Ahaziah king of Judah, Jehoahaz son of Jehu began to reign over Israel in Samaria for seventeen years. JFB notes under him Israel's apostasy grew greater and more confirmed than under Jehu, as he pursued his predecessors' policy of supporting calf worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Jehoahaz did evil in the LORD's sight and followed the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, departing not from them. JFB presents this continued calf worship as the cause of the severer national chastisement that followed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The LORD's anger was kindled against Israel, and he delivered them into the hand of Hazael king of Syria and Benhadad his son all their days. JFB identifies Hazael and Benhadad as the instruments the LORD employed in scourging the revolted nation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Jehoahaz besought the LORD, and the LORD hearkened to him, for he saw Israel's oppression under Syria. JFB explains he was heard not on his own account but for the ancient covenant with the patriarchs, and because divine honour required deliverance to check the idolatrous enemy's blasphemous taunts that God had forsaken Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The LORD gave Israel a saviour, so they went out from under Syria's hand and dwelt in their tents as before. JFB explains this saviour was not one patriotic defender but the deliverance obtained through Jehoahaz's two successors, Joash and Jeroboam. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Nevertheless they departed not from the sins of Jeroboam's house, but walked therein, and the grove remained in Samaria. JFB identifies the grove as Asherah, the idol set up by Ahab, which ought to have been demolished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Jehoahaz was left only fifty horsemen, ten chariots, and ten thousand footmen, for Syria's king had destroyed them and made them like dust in threshing. JFB explains threshing imagery: grain beaten in the open air with straw shattered to pieces, a figure of national suffering little short of extermination, originating in a barbarous war custom Hazael literally followed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The rest of Jehoahaz's acts and his might are written in the chronicles of Israel's kings. JFB notes his might is particularly mentioned to show the grievous oppression was not due to the king's cowardice but solely to God's righteous judgment for foul apostasy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Jehoahaz slept with his fathers, was buried in Samaria, and Joash his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section "Joash succeeds him" opens the second half of the chapter with this succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. In the thirty-seventh year of Joash king of Judah, Jehoash son of Jehoahaz began to reign over Israel in Samaria for sixteen years. JFB's section on Joash's succession frames his reign as the beginning of Israel's deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Joash did evil in the LORD's sight, departing not from all the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, but walking therein. JFB's section notes even the delivering king continued in Jeroboam's sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The rest of Joash's acts, his might, and how he fought against Amaziah king of Judah are written in the chronicles of Israel's kings. JFB notes this summary occurs early and is repeated in the account of Judah's king, cross-referring to the war with Amaziah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Joash slept with his fathers, Jeroboam sat upon his throne, and Joash was buried in Samaria with Israel's kings. JFB's section closes Joash's reign with this succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Elisha fell sick of the sickness whereof he died, and Joash king of Israel came down to him, weeping over his face, "My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof." JFB contrasts Elisha's death by disease with Elijah, who did not die, and explains the king's sorrow as grief for the incalculable loss to the kingdom, with "my father" as a complimentary phrase for an eminent guardian and deliverer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Elisha told Joash to take bow and arrows, and he took them. JFB explains hostilities were proclaimed by a formal discharge of an arrow into enemy country, and Elisha directed this symbolic act to intimate the victories promised over the Syrians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Elisha told the king to put his hand upon the bow, and Elisha put his hands upon the king's hands. JFB explains this represented the power imparted to the bow shot as coming from the LORD through the prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Elisha told him to open the eastward window, and when he opened it, Elisha said to shoot, and he shot, and Elisha declared it the arrow of the LORD's deliverance from Syria, to smite the Syrians in Aphek till consumed. JFB notes shooting eastward declared war for the territory east of Samaria the Syrians had taken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Elisha told Joash to take the arrows, and he took them, and Elisha said to smite upon the ground, and Joash smote thrice and stayed. JFB explains shooting arrows into the ground tokened the number of victories he would gain, so stopping at the third betrayed weakness of faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The man of God was wroth, saying Joash should have smitten five or six times, and then he would have smitten Syria till consumed, but now he would smite Syria only thrice. JFB notes that as each discharged arrow signified a victory, the more arrows shot, the more victories gained, and stopping so soon made his conquests incomplete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Elisha died and was buried, and Moabite bands invaded the land at the coming in of the year. JFB notes Elisha enjoyed a happier life than Elijah, with a milder character, and the spring was the usual season for campaigns, when Moabite bands made incursions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. As men were burying a man, they spied a band, cast the man into Elisha's sepulchre, and when he touched Elisha's bones he revived and stood on his feet. JFB explains both bodies were swathed, not coffined, so they could contact, and the miracle's object was to stimulate Israel's faith in Elisha's still unaccomplished predictions about the Syrian war. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Hazael king of Syria oppressed Israel all the days of Jehoahaz. JFB's account of the Syrian scourge frames this oppression as the judgment under which Israel groaned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. But the LORD was gracious to Israel, had compassion, and had respect to them because of his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and would not destroy or cast them from his presence. JFB notes Jehoahaz was heard for the ancient covenant's sake, not his own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Hazael king of Syria died, and Benhadad his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section records this succession as the turn toward Israel's deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Jehoash son of Jehoahaz took again from Benhadad the cities Hazael had taken from his father in war, smiting him three times and recovering Israel's cities. JFB notes this fulfils the prediction: Joash regained all the cities the Syrians had taken from his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
