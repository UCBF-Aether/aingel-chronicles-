The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hosea 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 When Ephraim spake trembling, he exalted himself in Israel; but when he offended in Baal, he died.

2 And now they sin more and more, and have made them molten images of their silver, and idols according to their own understanding, all of it the work of the craftsmen: they say of them, Let the men that sacrifice kiss the calves.

3 Therefore they shall be as the morning cloud and as the early dew that passeth away, as the chaff that is driven with the whirlwind out of the floor, and as the smoke out of the chimney.

4 Yet I am the LORD thy God from the land of Egypt, and thou shalt know no god but me: for there is no saviour beside me.

5 I did know thee in the wilderness, in the land of great drought.

6 According to their pasture, so were they filled; they were filled, and their heart was exalted; therefore have they forgotten me.

7 Therefore I will be unto them as a lion: as a leopard by the way will I observe them:

8 I will meet them as a bear that is bereaved of her whelps, and will rend the caul of their heart, and there will I devour them like a lion: the wild beast shall tear them.

9 O Israel, thou hast destroyed thyself; but in me is thine help.

10 I will be thy king: where is any other that may save thee in all thy cities? and thy judges of whom thou saidst, Give me a king and princes?

11 I gave thee a king in mine anger, and took him away in my wrath.

12 The iniquity of Ephraim is bound up; his sin is hid.

13 The sorrows of a travailing woman shall come upon him: he is an unwise son; for he should not stay long in the place of the breaking forth of children.

14 I will ransom them from the power of the grave; I will redeem them from death: O death, I will be thy plagues; O grave, I will be thy destruction: repentance shall be hid from mine eyes.

15 Though he be fruitful among his brethren, an east wind shall come, the wind of the LORD shall come up from the wilderness, and his spring shall become dry, and his fountain shall be dried up: he shall spoil the treasure of all pleasant vessels.

16 Samaria shall become desolate; for she hath rebelled against her God: they shall fall by the sword: their infants shall be dashed in pieces, and their women with child shall be ripped up.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Ephraim once spoke with trembling and was exalted, but offending in Baal he died, and now the people sin more and more, making molten images and kissing the calves. Prosperity made them full, and being full they forgot God, so judgment comes as lion, leopard, and bereaved bear; yet God says he will ransom them from the grave and redeem them from death, even as Samaria faces desolation for rebellion. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When Ephraim spoke trembling, he exalted himself in Israel, but when he offended in Baal, he died, because Ephraim, once honoured while humble, died spiritually when Baal worship became his offence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And now they sin more and more, and have made them molten images of their silver, and idols according to their own understanding, all of it the work of the craftsmen, they say of them, let the men that sacrifice kiss the calves, because idolatry grows by its own progress, and men are told to kiss the calves their own craftsmen made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Therefore they shall be as the morning cloud, and as the early dew that passes away, as the chaff that is driven with the whirlwind out of the floor, and as the smoke out of the chimney, because all four images agree, that idolaters vanish swiftly and leave nothing behind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Yet I am the Lord thy God from the land of Egypt, and thou shalt know no god but me, for there is no saviour beside me, because the exodus God remains the only God and the only Saviour, whatever idols have been added since. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. I did know thee in the wilderness, in the land of great drought, because God's knowledge of Israel was proved in the wilderness, where drought made every provision a direct gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. According to their pasture, so were they filled, they were filled, and their heart was exalted, therefore have they forgotten me, because full pasture bred a proud heart, and the proud heart forgot the God who gave the pasture. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Therefore I will be unto them as a lion, as a leopard by the way will I observe them, because God, who fed them, will now meet them as a lion on the road and as a leopard watching the way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. I will meet them as a bear that is bereaved of her whelps, and will rend the caul of their heart, and there will I devour them like a lion, the wild beast shall tear them, because the bereaved bear is the fiercest image of all, and the rending reaches the very caul of the heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. O Israel, thou hast destroyed thyself, but in me is thine help, because destruction is Israel's own work, while help remains in God alone, which is the whole gospel of the chapter in one sentence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. I will be thy king, where is any other that may save thee in all thy cities, and thy judges of whom thou saidst, give me a king and princes, because the king they demanded cannot save, and God himself must be king if any saving is to be done. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. I gave thee a king in mine anger, and took him away in my wrath, because the monarchy itself, given in anger for asking, will be taken away in wrath for abusing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. The iniquity of Ephraim is bound up, his sin is hid, because the sin is not forgotten but stored up, bound like a sealed bundle, waiting for the day of reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The sorrows of a travailing woman shall come upon him, he is an unwise son, for he should not stay long in the place of the breaking forth of children, because Ephraim is in birth pangs, yet like an unwise child he stays at the birth passage and will not come forth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. I will ransom them from the power of the grave, I will redeem them from death, O death, I will be thy plagues, O grave, I will be thy destruction, repentance shall be hid from mine eyes, because God promises a ransom from death itself, and speaks to death and the grave as enemies he will plague and destroy, a promise Henry reads as reaching beyond Israel's restoration to resurrection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Though he be fruitful among his brethren, an east wind shall come, the wind of the Lord shall come up from the wilderness, and his spring shall become dry, and his fountain shall be dried up, he shall spoil the treasure of all pleasant vessels, because fruitfulness will wither before the east wind from the wilderness, and the Assyrian will spoil every pleasant vessel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Samaria shall become desolate, for she has rebelled against her God, they shall fall by the sword, their infants shall be dashed in pieces, and their women with child shall be ripped up, because rebellion against God ends in the desolation of Samaria, with the horrors of a sacked city named in full. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Baal and Baalim. the Canaanite god, and the plural form for the Baals, whose worship the prophets charge God's people with, crediting idols with gifts God himself gave. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Ephraim. the leading tribe of the northern kingdom, used in the prophets for the northern kingdom of Israel itself, as distinct from Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Samaria. the capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, standing in the prophets for the ten tribes as Jerusalem stands for Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
