The Text — Genesis 50 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Joseph fell upon his father’s face, and wept upon him, and kissed him.

2 And Joseph commanded his servants the physicians to embalm his father: and the physicians embalmed Israel.

3 And forty days were fulfilled for him; for so are fulfilled the days of those which are embalmed: and the Egyptians mourned for him threescore and ten days.

4 And when the days of his mourning were past, Joseph spake unto the house of Pharaoh, saying, If now I have found grace in your eyes, speak, I pray you, in the ears of Pharaoh, saying,

5 My father made me swear, saying, Lo, I die: in my grave which I have digged for me in the land of Canaan, there shalt thou bury me. Now therefore let me go up, I pray thee, and bury my father, and I will come again.

6 And Pharaoh said, Go up, and bury thy father, according as he made thee swear.

7 And Joseph went up to bury his father: and with him went up all the servants of Pharaoh, the elders of his house, and all the elders of the land of Egypt,

8 And all the house of Joseph, and his brethren, and his father’s house: only their little ones, and their flocks, and their herds, they left in the land of Goshen.

9 And there went up with him both chariots and horsemen: and it was a very great company.

10 And they came to the threshingfloor of Atad, which is beyond Jordan, and there they mourned with a great and very sore lamentation: and he made a mourning for his father seven days.

11 And when the inhabitants of the land, the Canaanites, saw the mourning in the floor of Atad, they said, This is a grievous mourning to the Egyptians: wherefore the name of it was called Abelmizraim, which is beyond Jordan.

12 And his sons did unto him according as he commanded them:

13 For his sons carried him into the land of Canaan, and buried him in the cave of the field of Machpelah, which Abraham bought with the field for a possession of a buryingplace of Ephron the Hittite, before Mamre.

14 And Joseph returned into Egypt, he, and his brethren, and all that went up with him to bury his father, after he had buried his father.

15 And when Joseph’s brethren saw that their father was dead, they said, Joseph will peradventure hate us, and will certainly requite us all the evil which we did unto him.

16 And they sent a messenger unto Joseph, saying, Thy father did command before he died, saying,

17 So shall ye say unto Joseph, Forgive, I pray thee now, the trespass of thy brethren, and their sin; for they did unto thee evil: and now, we pray thee, forgive the trespass of the servants of the God of thy father. And Joseph wept when they spake unto him.

18 And his brethren also went and fell down before his face; and they said, Behold, we be thy servants.

19 And Joseph said unto them, Fear not: for am I in the place of God?

20 But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive.

21 Now therefore fear ye not: I will nourish you, and your little ones. And he comforted them, and spake kindly unto them.

22 And Joseph dwelt in Egypt, he, and his father’s house: and Joseph lived an hundred and ten years.

23 And Joseph saw Ephraim’s children of the third generation: the children also of Machir the son of Manasseh were brought up upon Joseph’s knees.

24 And Joseph said unto his brethren, I die: and God will surely visit you, and bring you out of this land unto the land which he sware to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.

25 And Joseph took an oath of the children of Israel, saying, God will surely visit you, and ye shall carry up my bones from hence.

26 So Joseph died, being an hundred and ten years old: and they embalmed him, and he was put in a coffin in Egypt.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Joseph mourns and embalms his father, and a great company carries Jacob to Machpelah for burial as he had sworn. Back in Egypt, the brothers fear Joseph will repay them now their father is dead, but Joseph weeps, reassures them that what they meant for evil God meant for good, and dies at a hundred and ten, charging that his bones be carried up when God visits them. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Joseph falls on his father's face, weeps over him, and kisses him, performing as principal member of the family the duty of closing his eyes and giving the farewell kiss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Joseph commands his servants the physicians to embalm his father, since in Egypt embalmers were a distinct class who infused resinous substances, dried the body, and wrapped it in linen for a chest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Forty days are fulfilled for embalming, and the Egyptians mourn seventy days, a period of public mourning as for a royal personage, including the whole embalming time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. When mourning is past, Joseph speaks to Pharaoh's house through others, since as a mourner he is unfit to appear in the royal presence, and he asks them to speak in Pharaoh's ears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. He reports his father's oath, that he must be buried in the grave dug in Canaan, and asks leave to go up and bury him and return, so the request is framed as duty, not flight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Pharaoh grants leave, saying go up and bury your father as he made you swear, honouring both the oath and Joseph's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Joseph goes up with all Pharaoh's servants, the elders of his house, and the elders of Egypt, a funeral cavalcade of court attachés and state officers over a journey of some three hundred miles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. All Joseph's house, his brothers, and his father's house go too, leaving only their little ones and flocks in Goshen, so the family is present while its base remains in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Chariots and horsemen go up as well, making a very great company, an imposing escort that honours both Jacob and Joseph. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. At the threshing floor of Atad beyond Jordan they mourn with a great and sore lamentation for seven days, the last and most violent halt for grief before the burial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The Canaanites, seeing the mourning, call the place Abel-mizraim, the mourning of the Egyptians, so the spot keeps the name of that grief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Jacob's sons do as he commanded them, carrying out the burial charge faithfully, so the father's last orders are obeyed by the whole family. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. His sons carry him into Canaan and bury him in Machpelah's cave, bought by Abraham from Ephron before Mamre, though Egyptian attendants could not join that ceremony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. After burying his father, Joseph returns to Egypt with his brothers and all who went up with him, keeping the promise made to Pharaoh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. With their father dead, the brothers fear Joseph will hate them and requite the evil they did, a fear that shows guilt still unquiet even after years of kindness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. They send a message to Joseph, saying their father commanded before his death that Joseph should forgive their trespass and sin, appealing to Jacob's authority for pardon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. They ask Joseph to forgive the servants of his father's God, and Joseph weeps when they speak to him, deeply affected by their fear and their plea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The brothers themselves come, fall down before him, and say they are his servants, renewing the bowing of the dreams in humble submission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Joseph tells them not to fear, asking whether he is in God's place, so vengeance is refused as a prerogative that belongs to God alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Joseph explains that though they meant evil against him, God meant it for good, to bring about the saving of many people alive, a summary of providence over the whole story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. He promises not to fear, for he will nourish them and their little ones, and he comforts them and speaks kindly to them, so assurance follows theology. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Joseph dwells in Egypt with his father's household and lives one hundred and ten years, eighty years after his elevation, witnessing the growth of his family and kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Joseph sees Ephraim's children to the third generation, and Manasseh's son Machir's children are brought up on his knees, a patriarch's long view of his house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Dying, Joseph says God will surely visit them and bring them out of Egypt to the land sworn to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, holding the promise firm at the end of his life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Joseph takes an oath from Israel that when God visits them they shall carry up his bones from Egypt, the strongest proof of his faith, since he asks for no burial in Canaan now but for a share in the future return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Joseph dies at one hundred and ten and is embalmed and put in a coffin in Egypt, closing Genesis with a coffin in Egypt and a promise pointing back to Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
