The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Nehemiah 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now in the twenty and fourth day of this month the children of Israel were assembled with fasting, and with sackclothes, and earth upon them.

2 And the seed of Israel separated themselves from all strangers, and stood and confessed their sins, and the iniquities of their fathers.

3 And they stood up in their place, and read in the book of the law of the LORD their God one fourth part of the day; and another fourth part they confessed, and worshipped the LORD their God.

4 Then stood up upon the stairs, of the Levites, Jeshua, and Bani, Kadmiel, Shebaniah, Bunni, Sherebiah, Bani, and Chenani, and cried with a loud voice unto the LORD their God.

5 Then the Levites, Jeshua, and Kadmiel, Bani, Hashabniah, Sherebiah, Hodijah, Shebaniah, and Pethahiah, said, Stand up and bless the LORD your God for ever and ever: and blessed be thy glorious name, which is exalted above all blessing and praise.

6 Thou, even thou, art LORD alone; thou hast made heaven, the heaven of heavens, with all their host, the earth, and all things that are therein, the seas, and all that is therein, and thou preservest them all; and the host of heaven worshippeth thee.

7 Thou art the LORD the God, who didst choose Abram, and broughtest him forth out of Ur of the Chaldees, and gavest him the name of Abraham;

8 And foundest his heart faithful before thee, and madest a covenant with him to give the land of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, and the Perizzites, and the Jebusites, and the Girgashites, to give it, I say, to his seed, and hast performed thy words; for thou art righteous:

9 And didst see the affliction of our fathers in Egypt, and heardest their cry by the Red sea;

10 And shewedst signs and wonders upon Pharaoh, and on all his servants, and on all the people of his land: for thou knewest that they dealt proudly against them. So didst thou get thee a name, as it is this day.

11 And thou didst divide the sea before them, so that they went through the midst of the sea on the dry land; and their persecutors thou threwest into the deeps, as a stone into the mighty waters.

12 Moreover thou leddest them in the day by a cloudy pillar; and in the night by a pillar of fire, to give them light in the way wherein they should go.

13 Thou camest down also upon mount Sinai, and spakest with them from heaven, and gavest them right judgments, and true laws, good statutes and commandments:

14 And madest known unto them thy holy sabbath, and commandedst them precepts, statutes, and laws, by the hand of Moses thy servant:

15 And gavest them bread from heaven for their hunger, and broughtest forth water for them out of the rock for their thirst, and promisedst them that they should go in to possess the land which thou hadst sworn to give them.

16 But they and our fathers dealt proudly, and hardened their necks, and hearkened not to thy commandments,

17 And refused to obey, neither were mindful of thy wonders that thou didst among them; but hardened their necks, and in their rebellion appointed a captain to return to their bondage: but thou art a God ready to pardon, gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and forsookest them not.

18 Yea, when they had made them a molten calf, and said, This is thy God that brought thee up out of Egypt, and had wrought great provocations;

19 Yet thou in thy manifold mercies forsookest them not in the wilderness: the pillar of the cloud departed not from them by day, to lead them in the way; neither the pillar of fire by night, to shew them light, and the way wherein they should go.

20 Thou gavest also thy good spirit to instruct them, and withheldest not thy manna from their mouth, and gavest them water for their thirst.

21 Yea, forty years didst thou sustain them in the wilderness, so that they lacked nothing; their clothes waxed not old, and their feet swelled not.

22 Moreover thou gavest them kingdoms and nations, and didst divide them into corners: so they possessed the land of Sihon, and the land of the king of Heshbon, and the land of Og king of Bashan.

23 Their children also multipliedst thou as the stars of heaven, and broughtest them into the land, concerning which thou hadst promised to their fathers, that they should go in to possess it.

24 So the children went in and possessed the land, and thou subduedst before them the inhabitants of the land, the Canaanites, and gavest them into their hands, with their kings, and the people of the land, that they might do with them as they would.

25 And they took strong cities, and a fat land, and possessed houses full of all goods, wells digged, vineyards, and oliveyards, and fruit trees in abundance: so they did eat, and were filled, and became fat, and delighted themselves in thy great goodness.

26 Nevertheless they were disobedient, and rebelled against thee, and cast thy law behind their backs, and slew thy prophets which testified against them to turn them to thee, and they wrought great provocations.

27 Therefore thou deliveredst them into the hand of their enemies, who vexed them: and in the time of their trouble, when they cried unto thee, thou heardest them from heaven; and according to thy manifold mercies thou gavest them saviours, who saved them out of the hand of their enemies.

28 But after they had rest, they did evil again before thee: therefore leftest thou them in the land of their enemies, so that they had the dominion over them: yet when they returned, and cried unto thee, thou heardest them from heaven; and many times didst thou deliver them according to thy mercies;

29 And testifiedst against them, that thou mightest bring them again unto thy law: yet they dealt proudly, and hearkened not unto thy commandments, but sinned against thy judgments, (which if a man do, he shall live in them;) and withdrew the shoulder, and hardened their neck, and would not hear.

30 Yet many years didst thou forbear them, and testifiedst against them by thy spirit in thy prophets: yet would they not give ear: therefore gavest thou them into the hand of the people of the lands.

31 Nevertheless for thy great mercies’ sake thou didst not utterly consume them, nor forsake them; for thou art a gracious and merciful God.

32 Now therefore, our God, the great, the mighty, and the terrible God, who keepest covenant and mercy, let not all the trouble seem little before thee, that hath come upon us, on our kings, on our princes, and on our priests, and on our prophets, and on our fathers, and on all thy people, since the time of the kings of Assyria unto this day.

33 Howbeit thou art just in all that is brought upon us; for thou hast done right, but we have done wickedly:

34 Neither have our kings, our princes, our priests, nor our fathers, kept thy law, nor hearkened unto thy commandments and thy testimonies, wherewith thou didst testify against them.

35 For they have not served thee in their kingdom, and in thy great goodness that thou gavest them, and in the large and fat land which thou gavest before them, neither turned they from their wicked works.

36 Behold, we are servants this day, and for the land that thou gavest unto our fathers to eat the fruit thereof and the good thereof, behold, we are servants in it:

37 And it yieldeth much increase unto the kings whom thou hast set over us because of our sins: also they have dominion over our bodies, and over our cattle, at their pleasure, and we are in great distress.

38 And because of all this we make a sure covenant, and write it; and our princes, Levites, and priests, seal unto it.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

On the twenty-fourth day of the month the people assemble fasting, in sackcloth with earth on their heads, separate themselves from strangers, and confess their sins. The Levites pray a long prayer retelling the whole story: creation, Abraham, the exodus, Sinai, the wilderness, the conquest, repeated rebellion and mercy, kings, and exile. Because of all this, they make a sure covenant, write it, and their princes, Levites, and priests seal it. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Sackcloth and earth. signs of mourning and humiliation worn at the fast: sackcloth garments, and earth upon the head (verse 1). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Sure covenant. the written agreement the people seal, binding themselves to the law they have just heard rehearsed (verse 38). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. On the twenty-fourth day of the seventh month the children of Israel assembled fasting, in sackcloth, with earth on their heads. Henry notes this occasional fast, a fortnight after the feasts, was a day to afflict the soul and loose the bands of wickedness: outward humiliation that gave glory to God and stirred one another to repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The seed of Israel separated themselves from all strangers, then stood and confessed their sins and the iniquities of their fathers. Henry explains the separation: those who would join themselves to God by prayer and covenant must separate from sin and sinners, for light has no communion with darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. A fourth part of the day they read in the book of the law, and another fourth part they confessed and worshipped the LORD. Henry observes the time was equally divided, three hours reading and three hours prayer, so the word furnished matter for prayer and prayer made the word profitable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Jeshua, Bani, Kadmiel, Shebaniah, Bunni, Sherebiah, Bani, and Chenani stood on the stairs of the Levites and cried with a loud voice to the LORD. Henry notes they prayed successively, crying aloud not so God might hear better but that the people might, to excite their fervency. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The Levites called the people to stand up and bless the LORD their God for ever, for his glorious name is exalted above all blessing and praise. Henry notes even the angels' praises fall infinitely short of what God's name deserves, and our goodness extends not to him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. The prayer adores God as Jehovah alone, maker of heaven, the heaven of heavens with all their host, the earth, the seas, and all in them, who preserves them all while the host of heaven worships him. Henry makes this the first article of praise: the only living and true God, Creator and great Protector of all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. God chose Abram, brought him out of Ur of the Chaldees, and gave him the name Abraham. Henry reads this as distinguishing free grace: God chose him, brought him out, and honoured him as father of many nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. God found Abraham's heart faithful, made covenant to give his seed the land of the Canaanites and other nations, and performed his words, for he is righteous. Henry notes God found the heart faithful because he made it so, and wherever he finds a faithful heart he proves a faithful God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. God saw Israel's affliction in Egypt and heard their cry by the Red Sea. Henry notes the deliverance was seasonable now that they sought the perfecting of their deliverance from Babylon, and came in compassion to their affliction and in answer to their cry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. God showed signs and wonders upon Pharaoh, his servants, and his people, for he knew they dealt proudly, and so got himself a name. Henry notes that wherein the proud dealt proudly, God showed himself above them, and to this day his name is glorified for that work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. God divided the sea before them so they passed through on dry land, and cast their persecutors into the deeps as a stone into mighty waters. Henry stresses the miracle: deliverance for Israel was destruction for their enemies, irrecoverable as a stone sunk in water. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. By day God led them with a pillar of cloud and by night with a pillar of fire, to give them light in the way they should go. Henry notes the pillar both directed every stage and step and stood as a visible token of God's guiding, guarding presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. God came down on Mount Sinai, spoke with them from heaven, and gave right judgments, true laws, and good statutes and commandments. Henry calls this the greatest favour of all: no nation had such laws, and even the ceremonial institutions were tokens of God's goodness and types of gospel grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. God made known his holy sabbath and commanded precepts, statutes, and laws by Moses his servant. Henry notes the sabbath was a token of particular favour, distinguishing Israel from nations that had lost that ancient part of revealed religion, and a means of keeping communion with God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. God gave bread from heaven for their hunger and water out of the rock for their thirst, and promised they should go in and possess the land he had sworn to give. Henry notes they had food convenient on the way and the good land at journey's end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. But their fathers dealt proudly, hardened their necks, and did not hearken to God's commandments. Henry traces the obstinacy to pride: they thought it below them to bow their necks to God's yoke and set up their own will against God's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. They refused to obey and were not mindful of God's wonders, hardening their necks and appointing a captain to return to bondage, yet God, ready to pardon, gracious, merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, did not forsake them. Henry notes they heard God's word but did not hearken, and saw his works but did not consider them as miracles or mercies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. They made a molten calf and said, "This is thy God, that brought thee up out of Egypt," working great provocations. Henry names the two wilderness returns: to Egyptian slavery for garlic and onions, and to Egyptian idolatry, sottishly calling a calf their deliverer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Yet in his manifold mercies God did not forsake them in the wilderness: the pillar of cloud did not depart by day nor the pillar of fire by night to show them the way. Henry stresses the doubling of thanks owed when mercies, though forfeited, are continued. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. God gave his good Spirit to instruct them, did not withhold manna from their mouth, and gave them water for their thirst. Henry notes that besides Sinai, the books of Moses were constant instruction, and men like Bezaleel, Joshua, and Caleb were filled with God's Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Forty years God sustained them in the wilderness, so they lacked nothing, their clothes did not wear out, and their feet did not swell. Henry marvels that never was a people so long and so tenderly nursed, carried as on eagles' wings over a rough and tedious way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. God gave them kingdoms and nations, dividing them, so they possessed the land of Sihon king of Heshbon and Og king of Bashan. Henry counts this among the favours: they were made numerous enough to replenish the land and victorious enough to master it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Their children were multiplied as the stars of heaven, and God brought them into the land he had promised their fathers they should possess. Henry notes the settlement was happy: strong cities, furnished houses, and a fat land all made ready to their hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. The children went in and possessed the land, subduing the Canaanites and their kings and nations, given into their hands to do with them as they would. Henry observes they set their feet, if they pleased, on the necks of kings, gaining a settlement they could not have wished easier or happier. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. They took strong cities and a fat land, houses full of goods, wells dug, vineyards, oliveyards, and fruit trees in abundance, ate, were filled, became fat, and delighted in God's great goodness. Henry notes they found everything prepared, and could not wish to be more easy or happy had it not been their own fault. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Nevertheless they were disobedient and rebelled, cast God's law behind their backs, slew his prophets who testified to turn them to him, and wrought great provocations. Henry calls this returning the greatest injury for the greatest kindness, abusing both God's prophets and his favours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Therefore God gave them into the hand of enemies who vexed them, but in their trouble they cried to him, and he heard from heaven and, according to his manifold mercies, gave them saviours who saved them. Henry notes these deliverances came not for any merit but purely according to God's mercies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. After they had rest they did evil again, so God left them in the hand of their enemies, but when they returned and cried, he heard from heaven and delivered them many times according to his mercies. Henry observes neither troubles nor deliverances held them to duty; neither fear nor love sufficed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. God testified against them to bring them again to his law, but they dealt proudly, did not hearken, sinned against his judgments, in which a man shall live if he does them, withdrew the shoulder, hardened their necks, and would not hear. Henry notes the design of all prophetic testimony was to lay their necks under the law's yoke and walk by its rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Many years God forbore them and testified against them by his Spirit in his prophets, yet they would not give ear, so he gave them into the hand of the peoples of the lands. Henry notes God's patience lengthened out as he waited to see if they would repent, and that refusing the Spirit's teaching leaves his word to accuse and judge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Yet for his great mercies' sake God did not utterly consume them nor forsake them, for he is a gracious and merciful God. Henry explains: had he forsaken them they would have been consumed, but he stirred not up all his wrath, designing their reformation, not their destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The prayer turns to petition, its only request: let not all the trouble seem little before God, great, mighty, and terrible, who keeps covenant and mercy, trouble that has lain on kings, princes, priests, prophets, fathers, and all the people since the kings of Assyria until this day. Henry notes they prescribe nothing to God, only asking him to take cognizance of it, as he did in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. They own God is just in all that is brought upon them, for he has done right, but they have done wickedly. Henry's rule for sharp and long rebukes: justify God and judge ourselves, for he will be clear when he judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Their kings, princes, priests, and fathers did not keep God's law nor hearken to his commandments and testimonies. Henry charges two contempts as the cause of their troubles: contempt of the good law and contempt of the good land God gave them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Their kings did not serve God in their kingdom, nor did the people serve him in the use of his great goodness and the large, fat land set before them, nor turn from their wicked works. Henry notes the irony: those who would not serve God in their own land were made to serve their enemies in a strange land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Now they are servants in the very land given to their fathers to eat its fruit and good, behold, servants in it. Henry calls it a sad change: free-born Israelites holding by as base a tenure as villenage under the kings of Persia, a work of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. The land yields much increase to the kings set over them because of their sins, who have dominion over their bodies and cattle at their pleasure, leaving them in great distress. Henry notes the land-tax amounted to a rack-rent, so that rents and profits accrued to the king and bare subsistence remained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Because of all this, they make a sure covenant and write it, and their princes, Levites, and priests seal it. Henry notes the covenant was made with serious consideration, great solemnity, joint consent, and fixed resolution, without reserving a power of revocation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Yet for his great mercies' sake God did not utterly consume them nor forsake them, for he is a gracious and merciful God. Henry explains: had he forsaken them they would have been consumed, but he stirred not up all his wrath, designing their reformation, not their destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. The prayer turns to petition, its only request: let not all the trouble seem little before God, great, mighty, and terrible, who keeps covenant and mercy, trouble that has lain on kings, princes, priests, prophets, fathers, and all the people since the kings of Assyria until this day. Henry notes they prescribe nothing to God, only asking him to take cognizance of it, as he did in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. They own God is just in all that is brought upon them, for he has done right, but they have done wickedly. Henry's rule for sharp and long rebukes: justify God and judge ourselves, for he will be clear when he judges. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. Their kings, princes, priests, and fathers did not keep God's law nor hearken to his commandments and testimonies. Henry charges two contempts as the cause of their troubles: contempt of the good law and contempt of the good land God gave them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Their kings did not serve God in their kingdom, nor did the people serve him in the use of his great goodness and the large, fat land set before them, nor turn from their wicked works. Henry notes the irony: those who would not serve God in their own land were made to serve their enemies in a strange land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. Now they are servants in the very land given to their fathers to eat its fruit and good, behold, servants in it. Henry calls it a sad change: free-born Israelites holding by as base a tenure as villenage under the kings of Persia, a work of sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. The land yields much increase to the kings set over them because of their sins, who have dominion over their bodies and cattle at their pleasure, leaving them in great distress. Henry notes the land-tax amounted to a rack-rent, so that rents and profits accrued to the king and bare subsistence remained. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Because of all this, they make a sure covenant and write it, and their princes, Levites, and priests seal it. Henry notes the covenant was made with serious consideration, great solemnity, joint consent, and fixed resolution, without reserving a power of revocation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
