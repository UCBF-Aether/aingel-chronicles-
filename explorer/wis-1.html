The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Wisdom of Solomon 1 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Love righteousness, ye that be judges of the earth: think of the Lord with a good (heart,) and in simplicity of heart seek him.

2 For he will be found of them that tempt him not; and sheweth himself unto such as do not distrust him.

3 For froward thoughts separate from God: and his power, when it is tried, reproveth the unwise.

4 For into a malicious soul wisdom shall not enter; nor dwell in the body that is subject unto sin.

5 For the holy spirit of discipline will flee deceit, and remove from thoughts that are without understanding, and will not abide when unrighteousness cometh in.

6 For wisdom is a loving spirit; and will not acquit a blasphemer of his words: for God is witness of his reins, and a true beholder of his heart, and a hearer of his tongue.

7 For the Spirit of the Lord filleth the world: and that which containeth all things hath knowledge of the voice.

8 Therefore he that speaketh unrighteous things cannot be hid: neither shall vengeance, when it punisheth, pass by him.

9 For inquisition shall be made into the counsels of the ungodly: and the sound of his words shall come unto the Lord for the manifestation of his wicked deeds.

10 For the ear of jealousy heareth all things: and the noise of murmurings is not hid.

11 Therefore beware of murmuring, which is unprofitable; and refrain your tongue from backbiting: for there is no word so secret, that shall go for nought: and the mouth that belieth slayeth the soul.

12 Seek not death in the error of your life: and pull not upon yourselves destruction with the works of your hands.

13 For God made not death: neither hath he pleasure in the destruction of the living.

14 For he created all things, that they might have their being: and the generations of the world were healthful; and there is no poison of destruction in them, nor the kingdom of death upon the earth:

15 (For righteousness is immortal:)

16 But ungodly men with their works and words called it to them: for when they thought to have it their friend, they consumed to nought, and made a covenant with it, because they are worthy to take part with it.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Judges of the earth are told to love righteousness and seek the Lord with a sincere heart, because wisdom will not enter a malicious soul or a body subject to sin. God hears every word, even murmuring in secret, and the chapter's ground is stated plainly: God made not death, righteousness is immortal, and the ungodly invite death to themselves by their own works and words. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Love righteousness, you judges of the earth, and think of the Lord with a good heart, seeking him in simplicity. Haydock notes the book's opening truth, pressed through the first five chapters, is that God must be approached with just sentiments and without duplicity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. He will be found by those who do not tempt him, and shows himself to those who do not distrust him. Haydock's reading is that a sincere, undoubting heart is the condition on which God reveals himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. Froward thoughts separate from God, and his power, when tried, reproves the unwise. Haydock observes that such thoughts are not mere folly; tested power exposes them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. Wisdom will not enter a malicious soul, nor dwell in a body subject to sin. Haydock notes soul and body are so joined that the defilement of one banishes wisdom from the other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. The holy spirit of discipline flees deceit, removes from unwise thoughts, and is put to confusion at approaching iniquity. Haydock calls this spirit the instructive light of the Holy Ghost, which abandons those who deny God or live wickedly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. Wisdom's spirit is loving to man, yet will not acquit a blasphemer, for God witnesses his reins and heart and hears his tongue. Haydock stresses that the same spirit which instructs will not acquit the guilty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. The Spirit of the Lord fills the world, and what contains all things knows every voice. Haydock notes that this Spirit, having made and filled all things, must be perfectly acquainted with every action. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. Therefore he who speaks unrighteous things cannot be hidden, nor shall vengeance pass him by. Haydock's point is that the all-containing Spirit makes secret sin an impossibility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. Inquisition shall be made into the ungodly man's counsels, and his words shall come to God for correction of his iniquities. Haydock reads this as the judgment in which words themselves are brought forward as evidence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. God's ear of jealousy hears all things, and no muttering escapes it. Haydock notes that God, strong and jealous, examines even the smallest deviation from rectitude. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. Beware then of murmuring, which is unprofitable, and refrain the tongue from backbiting, for no secret speech goes unpunished. Haydock's warning is that murmuring, though whispered, is heard and judged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. Do not seek death by the error of your life, nor draw destruction by your own hands. Haydock warns against eagerly seeking one's own ruin by an evil life, and against blaming death as if sin were necessity; God made man to be immortal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 13. God did not make death, nor does he delight in the destruction of the living. Haydock's point is that death entered by sin, not by God's making. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. God created all things that they might have their being, and the generations of the world are healthful, with no poison of destruction in them. Haydock notes creation was pronounced good, and that poison and wild beasts harm man only after sin, while Christ is the antidote. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 15. Righteousness is immortal. Haydock treats this as the chapter's sum: the righteous life partakes of the immortality God intended. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 16. The ungodly, with hands and words, call death to themselves, deem it a friend, and make a covenant with it. Haydock notes they strive to draw upon themselves the second death, refusing repentance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words in this chapter that modern readers may trip over. They are editorial explanation, not Scripture.

  • froward. Turned aside and perverse in thought, set against the simplicity of heart the chapter commends. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • reins. The inward parts, the seat of thought and feeling, of which God is said to be witness. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • backbiting. Secret slander spoken behind another's back, which the chapter warns is heard and judged. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing George Leo Haydock
