The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Sirach 12 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 When thou wilt do good know to whom thou doest it; so shalt thou be thanked for thy benefits.

2 Do good to the godly man, and thou shalt find a recompence; and if not from him, yet from the most High.

3 There can no good come to him that is always occupied in evil, nor to him that giveth no alms.

4 Give to the godly man, and help not a sinner.

5 Do well unto him that is lowly, but give not to the ungodly: hold back thy bread, and give it not unto him, lest he overmaster thee thereby: for [else] thou shalt receive twice as much evil for all the good thou shalt have done unto him.

6 For the most High hateth sinners, and will repay vengeance unto the ungodly, and keepeth them against the mighty day of their punishment.

7 Give unto the good, and help not the sinner.

8 A friend cannot be known in prosperity: and an enemy cannot be hidden in adversity.

9 In the prosperity of a man enemies will be grieved: but in his adversity even a friend will depart.

10 Never trust thine enemy: for like as iron rusteth, so is his wickedness.

11 Though he humble himself, and go crouching, yet take good heed and beware of him, and thou shalt be unto him as if thou hadst wiped a lookingglass, and thou shalt know that his rust hath not been altogether wiped away.

12 Set him not by thee, lest, when he hath overthrown thee, he stand up in thy place; neither let him sit at thy right hand, lest he seek to take thy seat, and thou at the last remember my words, and be pricked therewith.

13 Who will pity a charmer that is bitten with a serpent, or any such as come nigh wild beasts?

14 So one that goeth to a sinner, and is defiled with him in his sins, who will pity?

15 For a while he will abide with thee, but if thou begin to fall, he will not tarry.

16 An enemy speaketh sweetly with his lips, but in his heart he imagineth how to throw thee into a pit: he will weep with his eyes, but if he find opportunity, he will not be satisfied with blood.

17 If adversity come upon thee, thou shalt find him there first; and though he pretend to help thee, yet shall he undermine thee.

18 He will shake his head, and clap his hands, and whisper much, and change his countenance.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Good deeds should be done with discernment about who receives them, and an enemy should never be trusted, since his wickedness is like rust in iron, constant and corrosive. The chapter ends by picturing the enemy's gloating in shaking head, clapping hands, and whispering. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When you will do good, know to whom you do it, and you will be thanked for your benefits. Good deeds should be directed with knowledge of the recipient, and thanks follow well-placed benefits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 2. Do good to the godly man, and you will find a recompense; and if not from him, yet from the Most High. Good done to the godly is repaid, by the man if possible, and otherwise by God himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 3. No good can come to him who is always occupied in evil, nor to him who gives no alms. A life occupied in evil and a life without almsgiving are both barren of good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 4. Give to the godly man, and do not help a sinner. Giving should be directed to the godly, not to strengthening a sinner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 5. Do well to him who is lowly, but do not give to the ungodly; hold back your bread and do not give it to him, lest he overmaster you thereby, for else you will receive twice as much evil for all the good you have done him. Help for the lowly is urged, but giving to the ungodly arms him against you, returning double evil for good. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 6. The Most High hates sinners, and will repay vengeance to the ungodly, keeping them against the mighty day of their punishment. God's hatred of sinners is not forgetfulness; vengeance is kept for the great day of punishment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 7. Give to the good, and do not help the sinner. The rule is repeated plainly: give to the good, not to the sinner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 8. A friend cannot be known in prosperity, and an enemy cannot be hidden in adversity. Prosperity hides the difference between friend and flatterer, while adversity exposes enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 9. In a man's prosperity enemies will be grieved, but in his adversity even a friend will depart. Prosperity grieves enemies, yet adversity can scatter even friends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 10. Never trust your enemy, for as iron rusts, so does his wickedness. An enemy's wickedness is like rust in iron: constant, corrosive, and never to be trusted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 11. Though he humble himself and go crouching, take good heed and beware of him, and you will be to him as one who has wiped a looking-glass, and you will know his rust has not been altogether wiped away. A crouching, humbled enemy still carries rust beneath the polish, and the wise man sees through the wiped surface. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 12. Do not set him by you, lest when he has overthrown you he stand up in your place; nor let him sit at your right hand, lest he seek to take your seat, and you at last remember these words and be pricked with them. Giving an enemy a place beside you or at your right hand invites him to seize your seat, leaving only regret for ignored warning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 13. Who will pity a charmer bitten by a serpent, or any such as come near wild beasts? One who handles serpents and wild beasts and is bitten has invited the danger and earns little pity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 14. So one who goes to a sinner and is defiled with him in his sins, who will pity? Going to a sinner and sharing his defilement is like handling serpents: pity is not owed to a chosen danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 15. For a while he will abide with you, but if you begin to fall, he will not tarry. The sinner's companion stays only while things stand; at the first fall he leaves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 16. An enemy speaks sweetly with his lips, but in his heart imagines how to throw you into a pit; he will weep with his eyes, but if he finds opportunity he will not be satisfied with blood. Sweet lips and weeping eyes mask a heart plotting a pit, and given opportunity the enemy wants blood, not tears. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 17. If adversity comes upon you, you will find him there first, and though he pretends to help you, yet he will undermine you. In adversity the enemy arrives first, and his pretended help is really undermining. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
  • Verse 18. He will shake his head, clap his hands, whisper much, and change his countenance. The enemy's gloating shows in shaking head, clapping hands, whispering, and a changing face. Interpreted Study note, summarizing W. O. E. Oesterley
