The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the ark of the LORD was in the country of the Philistines seven months.

2 And the Philistines called for the priests and the diviners, saying, What shall we do to the ark of the LORD? tell us wherewith we shall send it to his place.

3 And they said, If ye send away the ark of the God of Israel, send it not empty; but in any wise return him a trespass offering: then ye shall be healed, and it shall be known to you why his hand is not removed from you.

4 Then said they, What shall be the trespass offering which we shall return to him? They answered, Five golden emerods, and five golden mice, according to the number of the lords of the Philistines: for one plague was on you all, and on your lords.

5 Wherefore ye shall make images of your emerods, and images of your mice that mar the land; and ye shall give glory unto the God of Israel: peradventure he will lighten his hand from off you, and from off your gods, and from off your land.

6 Wherefore then do ye harden your hearts, as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts? when he had wrought wonderfully among them, did they not let the people go, and they departed?

7 Now therefore make a new cart, and take two milch kine, on which there hath come no yoke, and tie the kine to the cart, and bring their calves home from them:

8 And take the ark of the LORD, and lay it upon the cart; and put the jewels of gold, which ye return him for a trespass offering, in a coffer by the side thereof; and send it away, that it may go.

9 And see, if it goeth up by the way of his own coast to Bethshemesh, then he hath done us this great evil: but if not, then we shall know that it is not his hand that smote us: it was a chance that happened to us.

10 And the men did so; and took two milch kine, and tied them to the cart, and shut up their calves at home:

11 And they laid the ark of the LORD upon the cart, and the coffer with the mice of gold and the images of their emerods.

12 And the kine took the straight way to the way of Bethshemesh, and went along the highway, lowing as they went, and turned not aside to the right hand or to the left; and the lords of the Philistines went after them unto the border of Bethshemesh.

13 And they of Bethshemesh were reaping their wheat harvest in the valley: and they lifted up their eyes, and saw the ark, and rejoiced to see it.

14 And the cart came into the field of Joshua, a Bethshemite, and stood there, where there was a great stone: and they clave the wood of the cart, and offered the kine a burnt offering unto the LORD.

15 And the Levites took down the ark of the LORD, and the coffer that was with it, wherein the jewels of gold were, and put them on the great stone: and the men of Bethshemesh offered burnt offerings and sacrificed sacrifices the same day unto the LORD.

16 And when the five lords of the Philistines had seen it, they returned to Ekron the same day.

17 And these are the golden emerods which the Philistines returned for a trespass offering unto the LORD; for Ashdod one, for Gaza one, for Askelon one, for Gath one, for Ekron one;

18 And the golden mice, according to the number of all the cities of the Philistines belonging to the five lords, both of fenced cities, and of country villages, even unto the great stone of Abel, whereon they set down the ark of the LORD: which stone remaineth unto this day in the field of Joshua, the Bethshemite.

19 And he smote the men of Bethshemesh, because they had looked into the ark of the LORD, even he smote of the people fifty thousand and threescore and ten men: and the people lamented, because the LORD had smitten many of the people with a great slaughter.

20 And the men of Bethshemesh said, Who is able to stand before this holy LORD God? and to whom shall he go up from us?

21 And they sent messengers to the inhabitants of Kirjathjearim, saying, The Philistines have brought again the ark of the LORD; come ye down, and fetch it up to you.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After seven months in Philistine country, the Philistines consult their priests and diviners, who advise not to send the ark empty but with a trespass offering of five golden emerods and five golden mice, one for each lord, and to give glory to the God of Israel. The test is set on a new cart drawn by two milch kine that have never borne a yoke, their calves shut up at home: if they go straight to Bethshemesh, the LORD has done this great evil, and if not, it was chance. The kine go straight along the highway, lowing, turning neither right nor left, while the lords follow to the border; Bethshemesh rejoices at the ark's return, offers the cart and kine, but men who look into the ark are smitten, and the ark is sent on to Kirjathjearim. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The ark of the LORD was in Philistine country seven months. Seven months the Philistines were punished by the ark's presence, Henry notes, because they would not send it home sooner; sinners lengthen their own miseries by refusing to part with their sins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The Philistines called for their priests and diviners, asking what to do with the ark and how to send it to its place. Their own religious experts were consulted to undo the trouble their capture had caused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The priests and diviners said, if you send away the ark, send it not empty but return a trespass offering, and then you shall be healed and know why God's hand was not removed. Even Philistine diviners understood that restitution, not mere removal, was required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Asked what trespass offering to return, they answered five golden emerods and five golden mice, according to the number of the Philistine lords — one for each lord, since the same plague was on all. The offering confessed the plague in golden images of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. They were to make images of their emerods and of the mice that marred the land, and give glory to the God of Israel, in case he would lighten his hand from them, their gods, and their land. Giving glory to God, not merely escaping pain, was the counsel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Why harden your hearts, they asked, as Egypt and Pharaoh hardened theirs, who let Israel go only after God had wrought wonderfully among them? How willing bad men are, Henry observes, to shift off their convictions and believe a judgment is mere chance, a rod with no voice to heed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Now make a new cart and take two milch kine that have never borne a yoke, tie them to the cart, and bring their calves home from them. The untrained, bereaved cows would be the test of God's hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Take the ark and lay it on the cart, and put the golden jewels returned as a trespass offering in a coffer beside it, and send it away. The ark would travel unescorted, with its tribute beside it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. And see: if it goes up by the way of its own coast to Bethshemesh, then the LORD has done this great evil; but if not, then it was chance that happened to them. The test was designed to distinguish God's hand from chance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The men did so, taking two milch kine, tying them to the cart, and shutting up their calves at home. Every natural pull of the cows would be toward home, to their calves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. They laid the ark on the cart, and the coffer with the golden mice and the images of the emerods beside it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The kine took the straight way to Bethshemesh, going along the highway lowing as they went, turning neither right nor left, while the Philistine lords followed to the border. These two kine knew their Owner, Henry notes — the great Owner whom Hophni and Phinehas had not known — and God's providence serves its purposes even by brute creatures. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The men of Bethshemesh were reaping wheat harvest in the valley, and when they lifted their eyes and saw the ark, they rejoiced to see it. Their joy at the ark's return was greater than the joy of harvest, Henry says, as the revival of holy ordinances after restraint and trouble is a matter of great joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. The cart came into the field of Joshua the Bethshemite and stood by a great stone, and they split the cart's wood and offered the kine as a burnt offering to the LORD. The cart that carried the ark became its own sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The Levites took down the ark and the coffer with its golden jewels and put them on the great stone, and the men of Bethshemesh offered burnt offerings and sacrifices that day. The return was met with proper priestly handling and worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. When the five Philistine lords had seen it, they returned to Ekron the same day. Their test was complete: the ark had gone home by God's hand, not by chance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. These are the golden emerods returned as a trespass offering: one each for Ashdod, Gaza, Askelon, Gath, and Ekron. The record names each lord's city, confessing the plague city by city. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. And the golden mice, according to the number of all the Philistine cities belonging to the five lords, both fenced cities and country villages, as far as the great stone of Abel where the ark was set. The offering covered the whole land the mice had marred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. He smote the men of Bethshemesh because they had looked into the ark, smiting fifty thousand and threescore and ten men, and the people lamented because the LORD had smitten many with a great slaughter. It is a great affront to God, Henry warns, for vain men to pry into secret things that belong not to them; God will not suffer his ark to be profaned, and those who will not reverently use the tokens of his grace shall feel his justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. The men of Bethshemesh said, Who is able to stand before this holy LORD God, and to whom shall he go up from us? Instead of asking how they might have peace with God and recover his favour, they desired to be rid of the ark — foolish men running from one extreme to the other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. They sent messengers to Kirjathjearim, saying the Philistines had brought back the ark, come down and fetch it up to you. When God's word works terror on sinners' consciences, Henry observes, many instead of taking blame to themselves quarrel with the word and put it from them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
