The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 115 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Not unto us, O LORD, not unto us, but unto thy name give glory, for thy mercy, and for thy truth’s sake.

2 Wherefore should the heathen say, Where is now their God?

3 But our God is in the heavens: he hath done whatsoever he hath pleased.

4 Their idols are silver and gold, the work of men’s hands.

5 They have mouths, but they speak not: eyes have they, but they see not:

6 They have ears, but they hear not: noses have they, but they smell not:

7 They have hands, but they handle not: feet have they, but they walk not: neither speak they through their throat.

8 They that make them are like unto them; so is every one that trusteth in them.

9 O Israel, trust thou in the LORD: he is their help and their shield.

10 O house of Aaron, trust in the LORD: he is their help and their shield.

11 Ye that fear the LORD, trust in the LORD: he is their help and their shield.

12 The LORD hath been mindful of us: he will bless us; he will bless the house of Israel; he will bless the house of Aaron.

13 He will bless them that fear the LORD, both small and great.

14 The LORD shall increase you more and more, you and your children.

15 Ye are blessed of the LORD which made heaven and earth.

16 The heaven, even the heavens, are the LORD’s: but the earth hath he given to the children of men.

17 The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence.

18 But we will bless the LORD from this time forth and for evermore. Praise the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

"Not unto us, O LORD, not unto us, but unto thy name give glory, for thy mercy, and for thy truth's sake" — lest the heathen ask, where is now their God? The answer is that our God is in the heavens and has done whatever he pleased, while their idols are silver and gold, the work of men's hands, with mouths that speak not, eyes that see not, ears that hear not, hands that handle not, feet that walk not; and those who make them are like them, as is everyone who trusts in them. Against that, Israel, the house of Aaron, and all who fear the LORD are each called to trust in the LORD, their help and shield, who has been mindful of them and will bless them, small and great, and increase them and their children. The heavens are the LORD's, the earth he has given to the children of men; the dead praise not the LORD, but we will bless him from this time forth and for evermore. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Henry excludes boasting for ever: no opinion of our own merits may enter prayer or praise, for all good we do is by grace and all good we have is mere mercy, so every crown is cast at the throne — and even unanswered prayer must end, whatever becomes of us, unto thy name give glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. Henry hears the heathen taunt as both complaint and plea: why should they ask where God is, when he is everywhere by providence and nigh by promise — and why is Israel brought so low as to give the taunt colour, unless God appear to vindicate his own name? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Henry answers directly: our God is in the heavens, where heathen gods never were, and his power fills the lower world, for he has done whatsoever he pleased by sovereign counsel; ask where he is and he is at the beginning and end of everything, not far from any of us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Henry calls idols senseless things made of silver and gold dug poor and dirty from a mine — proper stuff for money, not for gods — the work of men's hands and vain imaginations, so that the argument is irrefragable: the workmen made it, therefore it is not God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Henry notes the idols' painted completeness mocks them: mouths and eyes were carved well enough, yet no life could be put in, so they speak not in answer to consulters — the crafty priest must speak for them, as Baal's image had no voice that answered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. Henry adds ears that hear no prayer however loud, and noses that smell no incense however strong or sweet: the image is dead after pretended consecration exactly as before, with not so much as a breath of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. Henry completes the jest: hands that handle no gifts and can bestow none, feet that cannot stir a step for relief, and not even a mutter through the throat — fitter for a toy shop than a temple, for children to play with than for men to pray to. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Henry infers the worshippers' sottishness: makers of images may be ingenious, but makers of gods are senseless like them, blind to God's invisible things in creation and deaf to day and night declaring his glory, sinking deeper into sense until God gives them up to a reprobate mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 9. Henry exhorts Israel as a body and each Israelite in private concerns to trust the living God, leaving God to dispose of all for the best; folly trusts dead images, wisdom trusts him who is a help to forward good and a shield against evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 10. Henry turns to Aaron's house: priests, most maligned and struck at, are under God's particular care and ought to be examples of cheerful confidence and faithful adherence in the worst of times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 11. Henry includes the proselytes who fear the Lord though not of Israel's seed: he will not fail nor forsake them, for wherever there is awful fear of God there may be cheerful faith, and those who reverence his word may rely on it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 12. Henry grounds trust in experience: the Lord has been mindful of us constantly and remarkably — of our case, wants, prayers, promises and covenant — and all our comforts flow from his thoughts toward us, though we have forgotten him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 13. Henry extends the blessing beyond Israel and Aaron to all who fear the Lord in every nation, small and great, young and old, poor and figured, weak and strong in grace: the greatest need his blessing and it shall not be denied to the meanest that fear him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 14. Henry reads increase as God's own blessing: increase in number, wealth and honour, but especially spiritual increase — knowledge, wisdom, grace, holiness and joy, constant and more and more till perfection, and hereditary, you in your children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 15. Henry says the blessed of the Lord have encouragement enough to trust him, for their blesser made heaven and earth: his blessings are free, needing nothing himself, and rich, having all things at command for those who fear and trust him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 16. Henry contrasts high glory with large goodness: the heaven of heavens is the Lord's own palace of light and bliss, yet the earth he has given to men for meat, drink and lodging — still his in chief, let out as a vineyard whose rents and account he will require. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 17. Henry distinguishes soul from body: faithful souls delivered from the flesh still praise God in perfect light, but the dead body cannot praise him here, death ending our glorifying service in this world of trial, and the grave being darkness and silence with no work or device. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 18. Henry closes with resolve born of that limit: others are dead and their service ended, so we will do the more to fill the gap, and we ourselves must shortly go to silence, so while we live we will bless the Lord, engage others, and praise him when we are gone, for evermore. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
