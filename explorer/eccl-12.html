The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ecclesiastes 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Remember now thy Creator in the days of thy youth, while the evil days come not, nor the years draw nigh, when thou shalt say, I have no pleasure in them;

2 While the sun, or the light, or the moon, or the stars, be not darkened, nor the clouds return after the rain:

3 In the day when the keepers of the house shall tremble, and the strong men shall bow themselves, and the grinders cease because they are few, and those that look out of the windows be darkened,

4 And the doors shall be shut in the streets, when the sound of the grinding is low, and he shall rise up at the voice of the bird, and all the daughters of musick shall be brought low;

5 Also when they shall be afraid of that which is high, and fears shall be in the way, and the almond tree shall flourish, and the grasshopper shall be a burden, and desire shall fail: because man goeth to his long home, and the mourners go about the streets:

6 Or ever the silver cord be loosed, or the golden bowl be broken, or the pitcher be broken at the fountain, or the wheel broken at the cistern.

7 Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it.

8 Vanity of vanities, saith the preacher; all is vanity.

9 And moreover, because the preacher was wise, he still taught the people knowledge; yea, he gave good heed, and sought out, and set in order many proverbs.

10 The preacher sought to find out acceptable words: and that which was written was upright, even words of truth.

11 The words of the wise are as goads, and as nails fastened by the masters of assemblies, which are given from one shepherd.

12 And further, by these, my son, be admonished: of making many books there is no end; and much study is a weariness of the flesh.

13 Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man.

14 For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good, or whether it be evil.

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Remember your Creator in youth, before the failing body pictured as a trembling house, dimming windows, a broken silver cord and golden bowl, and dust returning to earth. The book closes by returning to vanity, praising the Preacher's collected proverbs, and giving its conclusion: fear God, keep his commandments, for God brings every work into judgment. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Remember now thy Creator in the days of thy youth, while the evil days come not, nor the years draw nigh when thou shalt say, I have no pleasure in them, because youth is the season for choosing God, before age makes obedience hard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. While the sun, or the light, or the moon, or the stars be not darkened, nor the clouds return after the rain, because the decay of age is pictured as a darkening sky, in which brightness fails and troubles return in succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. In the day when the keepers of the house shall tremble, and the strong men shall bow themselves, and the grinders cease because they are few, and those that look out of the windows be darkened, because the body itself is the house, and its keepers, strength, teeth and eyes all fail together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. And the doors shall be shut in the streets, when the sound of the grinding is low, and he shall rise up at the voice of the bird, and all the daughters of music shall be brought low, because age shuts the senses, breaks sleep, and dulls delight in sound. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. When they shall be afraid of that which is high, and fears shall be in the way, and the almond tree shall flourish, and the grasshopper shall be a burden, and desire shall fail, because the aged fear heights and stumbling, grow grey like almond blossom, and lose strength and appetite. Because man goes to his long home, and the mourners go about the streets, since death is a homecoming to the grave, and the street is filled with those who lament the departure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Or ever the silver cord be loosed, or the golden bowl be broken, or the pitcher be broken at the fountain, or the wheel broken at the cistern, because life is pictured as a lamp hung by a cord and water drawn by a wheel, and death breaks the whole apparatus at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was, and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it, because the body and the soul part at death, each to its own origin, which grounds the whole book's call to fear God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Vanity of vanities, says the Preacher, all is vanity, because the closing sentence repeats the opening text, now proved by the whole survey of life under the sun. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Because the Preacher was wise, he still taught the people knowledge, and gave good heed, and sought out, and set in order many proverbs, because wisdom is not kept for oneself but ordered for the teaching of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The Preacher sought to find out acceptable words, and that which was written was upright, even words of truth, because true teaching must be both pleasing in form and upright in matter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The words of the wise are as goads, and as nails fastened by the masters of assemblies, which are given from one shepherd, because wise words prick the conscience into action and fasten truth firmly, all from one Shepherd. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And further, by these, my son, be admonished, for of making many books there is no end, and much study is a weariness of the flesh, because human learning without limit wearies, and the reader is warned to rest in the words already given. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man, because the entire inquiry into vanity ends in reverence and obedience, which alone give life its duty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good or whether it be evil, because the final judgment reaches what no human court can reach, and this is the book's last answer to vanity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
