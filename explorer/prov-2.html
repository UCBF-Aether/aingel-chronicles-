The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 My son, if thou wilt receive my words, and hide my commandments with thee;

2 So that thou incline thine ear unto wisdom, and apply thine heart to understanding;

3 Yea, if thou criest after knowledge, and liftest up thy voice for understanding;

4 If thou seekest her as silver, and searchest for her as for hid treasures;

5 Then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God.

6 For the LORD giveth wisdom: out of his mouth cometh knowledge and understanding.

7 He layeth up sound wisdom for the righteous: he is a buckler to them that walk uprightly.

8 He keepeth the paths of judgment, and preserveth the way of his saints.

9 Then shalt thou understand righteousness, and judgment, and equity; yea, every good path.

10 When wisdom entereth into thine heart, and knowledge is pleasant unto thy soul;

11 Discretion shall preserve thee, understanding shall keep thee:

12 To deliver thee from the way of the evil man, from the man that speaketh froward things;

13 Who leave the paths of uprightness, to walk in the ways of darkness;

14 Who rejoice to do evil, and delight in the frowardness of the wicked;

15 Whose ways are crooked, and they froward in their paths:

16 To deliver thee from the strange woman, even from the stranger which flattereth with her words;

17 Which forsaketh the guide of her youth, and forgetteth the covenant of her God.

18 For her house inclineth unto death, and her paths unto the dead.

19 None that go unto her return again, neither take they hold of the paths of life.

20 That thou mayest walk in the way of good men, and keep the paths of the righteous.

21 For the upright shall dwell in the land, and the perfect shall remain in it.

22 But the wicked shall be cut off from the earth, and the transgressors shall be rooted out of it.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Seek wisdom as silver and hidden treasure, and the search ends in the fear of the LORD and the knowledge of God, who gives wisdom and guards the paths of judgment. Wisdom then does double duty as a deliverer: from the evil man who speaks perverse things and walks in darkness, and from the flattering strange woman whose house inclines to death, so that the upright remain in the land and the wicked are cut off. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Receiving Wisdom's words and hiding the commandments within is the earnest seeking that Henry promises will never be complained of as lost labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Inclining the ear to wisdom and applying the heart to understanding is the diligence that goes with the freeness of the gift: seek and find, ask and receive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Crying after knowledge and lifting up the voice for understanding marks those whom God favours: the righteous, on whom his image is renewed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Seeking wisdom as silver and searching for it as hid treasures is the earnestness that God answers, without doing away with our diligence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Then the seeker shall understand the fear of the LORD and find the knowledge of God, for if we depend on God and seek wisdom from him, he will enable us to keep the paths of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. For the LORD gives wisdom, and from his mouth come knowledge and understanding, the gift that rewards earnest seeking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. He lays up sound wisdom for the righteous and is a buckler to them that walk uprightly, keeping them in the paths of judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. He keeps the paths of judgment and preserves the way of his saints, so that those who depend on God are enabled to walk uprightly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Then the seeker shall understand righteousness, judgment and equity, every good path, since wisdom renewed in righteousness guides the whole life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. When wisdom enters into the heart and knowledge is pleasant to the soul, it not only fills the head but rules the heart and preserves from within and without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Discretion shall preserve and understanding shall keep the wise, guarding against corruptions within and temptations without. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Wisdom delivers from the way of the evil man who speaks froward things, for the wise man will shun such company and its ways of darkness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Those who leave the paths of uprightness to walk in darkness choose ways that are uncomfortable and unsafe, leaving plain, pleasant, lightsome paths for folly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. They rejoice to do evil and delight in the frowardness of the wicked, taking pleasure both in committing sin and in seeing others commit it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Their ways are crooked and they froward in their paths, the very darkness from which true wisdom preserves its possessor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Wisdom also delivers from the strange woman who flatters with her words, a snare of fleshly lusts that defile the body, the living temple, and war against the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. She forsakes the guide of her youth and forgets the covenant of her God, and Henry adds that this caution also warns against idolatry and subjecting the soul to the body. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Her house inclines unto death and her paths unto the dead, a fatal snare from which it is very rare that any caught recover themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. None that go unto her return again or take hold of the paths of life, so hardened is the heart and blinded the mind by the deceitfulness of this sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. The purpose of this deliverance is that the wise may walk in the way of good men and keep the paths of the righteous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. The upright shall dwell in the land and the perfect shall remain in it, for to the righteous the earth is a place of preparation for heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. But the wicked shall be cut off from the earth and transgressors rooted out of it, for to the wicked this earth is all the heaven they shall ever have, and they must share in the miseries of their latter end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
