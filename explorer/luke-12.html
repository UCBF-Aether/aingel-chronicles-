The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the mean time, when there were gathered together an innumerable multitude of people, insomuch that they trode one upon another, he began to say unto his disciples first of all, Beware ye of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy.

2 For there is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed; neither hid, that shall not be known.

3 Therefore whatsoever ye have spoken in darkness shall be heard in the light; and that which ye have spoken in the ear in closets shall be proclaimed upon the housetops.

4 And I say unto you my friends, Be not afraid of them that kill the body, and after that have no more that they can do.

5 But I will forewarn you whom ye shall fear: Fear him, which after he hath killed hath power to cast into hell; yea, I say unto you, Fear him.

6 Are not five sparrows sold for two farthings, and not one of them is forgotten before God?

7 But even the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Fear not therefore: ye are of more value than many sparrows.

8 Also I say unto you, Whosoever shall confess me before men, him shall the Son of man also confess before the angels of God:

9 But he that denieth me before men shall be denied before the angels of God.

10 And whosoever shall speak a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him: but unto him that blasphemeth against the Holy Ghost it shall not be forgiven.

11 And when they bring you unto the synagogues, and unto magistrates, and powers, take ye no thought how or what thing ye shall answer, or what ye shall say:

12 For the Holy Ghost shall teach you in the same hour what ye ought to say.

13 And one of the company said unto him, Master, speak to my brother, that he divide the inheritance with me.

14 And he said unto him, Man, who made me a judge or a divider over you?

15 And he said unto them, Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man’s life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth.

16 And he spake a parable unto them, saying, The ground of a certain rich man brought forth plentifully:

17 And he thought within himself, saying, What shall I do, because I have no room where to bestow my fruits?

18 And he said, This will I do: I will pull down my barns, and build greater; and there will I bestow all my fruits and my goods.

19 And I will say to my soul, Soul, thou hast much goods laid up for many years; take thine ease, eat, drink, and be merry.

20 But God said unto him, Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee: then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided?

21 So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God.

22 And he said unto his disciples, Therefore I say unto you, Take no thought for your life, what ye shall eat; neither for the body, what ye shall put on.

23 The life is more than meat, and the body is more than raiment.

24 Consider the ravens: for they neither sow nor reap; which neither have storehouse nor barn; and God feedeth them: how much more are ye better than the fowls?

25 And which of you with taking thought can add to his stature one cubit?

26 If ye then be not able to do that thing which is least, why take ye thought for the rest?

27 Consider the lilies how they grow: they toil not, they spin not; and yet I say unto you, that Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these.

28 If then God so clothe the grass, which is to day in the field, and to morrow is cast into the oven; how much more will he clothe you, O ye of little faith?

29 And seek not ye what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink, neither be ye of doubtful mind.

30 For all these things do the nations of the world seek after: and your Father knoweth that ye have need of these things.

31 But rather seek ye the kingdom of God; and all these things shall be added unto you.

32 Fear not, little flock; for it is your Father’s good pleasure to give you the kingdom.

33 Sell that ye have, and give alms; provide yourselves bags which wax not old, a treasure in the heavens that faileth not, where no thief approacheth, neither moth corrupteth.

34 For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.

35 Let your loins be girded about, and your lights burning;

36 And ye yourselves like unto men that wait for their lord, when he will return from the wedding; that when he cometh and knocketh, they may open unto him immediately.

37 Blessed are those servants, whom the lord when he cometh shall find watching: verily I say unto you, that he shall gird himself, and make them to sit down to meat, and will come forth and serve them.

38 And if he shall come in the second watch, or come in the third watch, and find them so, blessed are those servants.

39 And this know, that if the goodman of the house had known what hour the thief would come, he would have watched, and not have suffered his house to be broken through.

40 Be ye therefore ready also: for the Son of man cometh at an hour when ye think not.

41 Then Peter said unto him, Lord, speakest thou this parable unto us, or even to all?

42 And the Lord said, Who then is that faithful and wise steward, whom his lord shall make ruler over his household, to give them their portion of meat in due season?

43 Blessed is that servant, whom his lord when he cometh shall find so doing.

44 Of a truth I say unto you, that he will make him ruler over all that he hath.

45 But and if that servant say in his heart, My lord delayeth his coming; and shall begin to beat the menservants and maidens, and to eat and drink, and to be drunken;

46 The lord of that servant will come in a day when he looketh not for him, and at an hour when he is not aware, and will cut him in sunder, and will appoint him his portion with the unbelievers.

47 And that servant, which knew his lord’s will, and prepared not himself, neither did according to his will, shall be beaten with many stripes.

48 But he that knew not, and did commit things worthy of stripes, shall be beaten with few stripes. For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required: and to whom men have committed much, of him they will ask the more.

49 I am come to send fire on the earth; and what will I, if it be already kindled?

50 But I have a baptism to be baptized with; and how am I straitened till it be accomplished!

51 Suppose ye that I am come to give peace on earth? I tell you, Nay; but rather division:

52 For from henceforth there shall be five in one house divided, three against two, and two against three.

53 The father shall be divided against the son, and the son against the father; the mother against the daughter, and the daughter against the mother; the mother in law against her daughter in law, and the daughter in law against her mother in law.

54 And he said also to the people, When ye see a cloud rise out of the west, straightway ye say, There cometh a shower; and so it is.

55 And when ye see the south wind blow, ye say, There will be heat; and it cometh to pass.

56 Ye hypocrites, ye can discern the face of the sky and of the earth; but how is it that ye do not discern this time?

57 Yea, and why even of yourselves judge ye not what is right?

58 When thou goest with thine adversary to the magistrate, as thou art in the way, give diligence that thou mayest be delivered from him; lest he hale thee to the judge, and the judge deliver thee to the officer, and the officer cast thee into prison.

59 I tell thee, thou shalt not depart thence, till thou hast paid the very last mite.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Before an innumerable multitude Jesus first warns his disciples against the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy, and against fearing men rather than God (vv. 1–12). A dispute over an inheritance becomes the parable of the rich fool who builds bigger barns and dies that night (vv. 13–21), followed by teaching against anxiety — ravens, lilies, and a Father who knows what his children need — and the call to sell, give alms, and keep lamps burning for a master who may return at any watch (vv. 22–48). The chapter ends with fire and division, with discerning the signs of the times, and with settling quickly with an adversary on the way (vv. 49–59). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 12

  • Verse 22. Therefore I say unto you, take no thought for your life, what ye shall eat, nor for the body, what ye shall put on. Henry notes Christ largely insisted on this caution against disquieting, perplexing cares, urging us to cast care upon God, the right way to get ease. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. The life is more than meat, and the body is more than raiment. Life and body exceed their provisions, so anxiety about provisions mistakes the lesser for the greater. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Consider the ravens: they neither sow nor reap, have neither storehouse nor barn, and God feeds them; how much more are ye better than the fowls? Provision without storehouse is the ravens' lesson. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Which of you with taking thought can add to his stature one cubit? Anxiety cannot add even the least measure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. If ye then cannot do that which is least, why take ye thought for the rest? Inability in the least exposes anxiety's uselessness in the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Consider the lilies how they grow: they toil not, they spin not, yet Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these. Beauty without toil outshines royal glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. If God so clothes the grass, today in the field and tomorrow cast into the oven, how much more will he clothe you, O ye of little faith? Short-lived grass argues for lasting care of disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Seek not what ye shall eat or drink, neither be ye of doubtful mind. Seeking food as life's aim breeds a doubtful, tossed mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. For all these things do the nations of the world seek after, and your Father knows that ye have need of these things. Gentile seeking is not the disciples' pattern, and the Father already knows the need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. But rather seek ye the kingdom of God, and all these things shall be added unto you. Seeking the kingdom first reorders the rest as addition, not aim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Fear not, little flock, for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom. Henry urges us to examine whether we belong to this little flock, whose Master gives the kingdom by good pleasure, not by purchase. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Sell that ye have, and give alms; provide yourselves bags which wax not old, a treasure in the heavens that fails not, where no thief approaches, neither moth corrupts. Treasure is moved, not merely renounced, to a place thieves and moth cannot reach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also. Treasure locates the heart, whether on earth or in heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Let your loins be girded about, and your lights burning. Readiness is pictured as dressed and lit, not idle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And ye yourselves like men that wait for their lord, when he will return from the wedding, that when he comes and knocks, they may open immediately. Henry notes we are waiting servants, sitting up while he stays out late, ready to receive him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Blessed are those servants, whom the lord when he comes shall find watching: verily, he shall gird himself, make them sit down to meat, and come forth and serve them. Watchfulness is rewarded by a reversal: the lord serves the servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. If he comes in the second watch, or in the third watch, and finds them so, blessed are those servants. Late watches do not excuse sleep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. This know, if the goodman of the house had known what hour the thief would come, he would have watched, and not suffered his house to be broken through. Henry notes if men thus care for houses, we should be thus wise for souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Be ye therefore ready also, for the Son of man comes at an hour when ye think not. Readiness is constant because the hour is unknown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Then Peter said, Lord, speakest thou this parable unto us, or even to all? The question asks whether watchfulness is for apostles or for everyone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Who then is that faithful and wise steward, whom his lord shall make ruler over his household, to give them their portion of meat in due season? Henry notes all are to take Christ's word to themselves and inquire concerning it; no one is left so ignorant as not to know many wrongs done and rights neglected, so all are without excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Blessed is that servant, whom his lord when he comes shall find so doing. Blessing rests on being found at the assigned work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Of a truth, he will make him ruler over all that he has. Faithful stewardship in a household leads to rule over all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. But if that servant says in his heart, my lord delays his coming, and begins to beat menservants and maidens, and to eat and drink and be drunken, delay becomes cruelty and indulgence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. The lord of that servant will come in a day when he looks not for him, and at an hour when he is not aware, and will cut him in sunder, and appoint him his portion with the unbelievers. The servant who counted on delay is caught by the very coming he discounted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. That servant, who knew his lord's will, and prepared not himself, neither did according to his will, shall be beaten with many stripes. Knowledge without preparation increases stripes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. But he that knew not, and did things worthy of stripes, shall be beaten with few stripes. For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required, and to whom men have committed much, of him they will ask the more. Ignorance lessens but does not remove accountability, and gift measures requirement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. I am come to send fire on the earth, and what will I, if it be already kindled? Henry notes the gospel's coming would occasion desolations, not because Christ's religion tends that way, being pure, peaceable, and loving, but because it contradicts men's pride and lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. But I have a baptism to be baptized with, and how am I straitened till it be accomplished! Henry notes before the gospel's wide publication, Christ had a baptism far different from water and Spirit: he must endure sufferings and death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. Suppose ye that I am come to give peace on earth? I tell you, Nay, but rather division. Peace is not the immediate effect where pride resists the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. For from henceforth there shall be five in one house divided, three against two, and two against three. Division reaches inside the household itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. The father shall be divided against the son, and the son against the father; the mother against the daughter, and the daughter against the mother; the mother in law against her daughter in law, and the daughter in law against her mother in law. Every household bond is named, because none is exempt from gospel division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. When ye see a cloud rise out of the west, straightway ye say, there comes a shower, and so it is. Weather wisdom is accurate in its own sphere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. When ye see the south wind blow, ye say, there will be heat, and it comes to pass. The second sign confirms the first: they read signs well. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Ye hypocrites, ye can discern the face of sky and earth, but how is it that ye do not discern this time? Henry notes Christ would have people as wise in soul concerns as in outward affairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 57. Yea, and why even of yourselves judge ye not what is right? Discernment of the time should lead to self-judgment about what is right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 58. When thou goest with thine adversary to the magistrate, as thou art in the way, give diligence that thou mayest be delivered from him, lest he hale thee to the judge, and the judge deliver thee to the officer, and the officer cast thee into prison. Henry urges hastening to obtain peace with God before it is too late, applying to God in Christ reconciling the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 59. I tell thee, thou shalt not depart thence, till thou hast paid the very last mite. The way with an adversary ends in exact payment, so settlement must come before judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 29. Seek not what ye shall eat or drink, neither be ye of doubtful mind. Seeking food as life's aim breeds a doubtful, tossed mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. For all these things do the nations of the world seek after, and your Father knows that ye have need of these things. Gentile seeking is not the disciples' pattern, and the Father already knows the need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. But rather seek ye the kingdom of God, and all these things shall be added unto you. Seeking the kingdom first reorders the rest as addition, not aim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Fear not, little flock, for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom. Henry urges us to examine whether we belong to this little flock, whose Master gives the kingdom by good pleasure, not by purchase. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Sell that ye have, and give alms; provide yourselves bags which wax not old, a treasure in the heavens that fails not, where no thief approaches, neither moth corrupts. Treasure is moved, not merely renounced, to a place thieves and moth cannot reach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also. Treasure locates the heart, whether on earth or in heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. Let your loins be girded about, and your lights burning. Readiness is pictured as dressed and lit, not idle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. And ye yourselves like men that wait for their lord, when he will return from the wedding, that when he comes and knocks, they may open immediately. Henry notes we are waiting servants, sitting up while he stays out late, ready to receive him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. Blessed are those servants, whom the lord when he comes shall find watching: verily, he shall gird himself, make them sit down to meat, and come forth and serve them. Watchfulness is rewarded by a reversal: the lord serves the servants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. If he comes in the second watch, or in the third watch, and finds them so, blessed are those servants. Late watches do not excuse sleep. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. This know, if the goodman of the house had known what hour the thief would come, he would have watched, and not suffered his house to be broken through. Henry notes if men thus care for houses, we should be thus wise for souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Be ye therefore ready also, for the Son of man comes at an hour when ye think not. Readiness is constant because the hour is unknown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. Then Peter said, Lord, speakest thou this parable unto us, or even to all? The question asks whether watchfulness is for apostles or for everyone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Who then is that faithful and wise steward, whom his lord shall make ruler over his household, to give them their portion of meat in due season? Henry notes all are to take Christ's word to themselves and inquire concerning it; no one is left so ignorant as not to know many wrongs done and rights neglected, so all are without excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Blessed is that servant, whom his lord when he comes shall find so doing. Blessing rests on being found at the assigned work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Of a truth, he will make him ruler over all that he has. Faithful stewardship in a household leads to rule over all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. But if that servant says in his heart, my lord delays his coming, and begins to beat menservants and maidens, and to eat and drink and be drunken, delay becomes cruelty and indulgence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. The lord of that servant will come in a day when he looks not for him, and at an hour when he is not aware, and will cut him in sunder, and appoint him his portion with the unbelievers. The servant who counted on delay is caught by the very coming he discounted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. That servant, who knew his lord's will, and prepared not himself, neither did according to his will, shall be beaten with many stripes. Knowledge without preparation increases stripes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. But he that knew not, and did things worthy of stripes, shall be beaten with few stripes. For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required, and to whom men have committed much, of him they will ask the more. Ignorance lessens but does not remove accountability, and gift measures requirement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. I am come to send fire on the earth, and what will I, if it be already kindled? Henry notes the gospel's coming would occasion desolations, not because Christ's religion tends that way, being pure, peaceable, and loving, but because it contradicts men's pride and lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. But I have a baptism to be baptized with, and how am I straitened till it be accomplished! Henry notes before the gospel's wide publication, Christ had a baptism far different from water and Spirit: he must endure sufferings and death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. Suppose ye that I am come to give peace on earth? I tell you, Nay, but rather division. Peace is not the immediate effect where pride resists the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. For from henceforth there shall be five in one house divided, three against two, and two against three. Division reaches inside the household itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. The father shall be divided against the son, and the son against the father; the mother against the daughter, and the daughter against the mother; the mother in law against her daughter in law, and the daughter in law against her mother in law. Every household bond is named, because none is exempt from gospel division. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. When ye see a cloud rise out of the west, straightway ye say, there comes a shower, and so it is. Weather wisdom is accurate in its own sphere. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. When ye see the south wind blow, ye say, there will be heat, and it comes to pass. The second sign confirms the first: they read signs well. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. Ye hypocrites, ye can discern the face of sky and earth, but how is it that ye do not discern this time? Henry notes Christ would have people as wise in soul concerns as in outward affairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 57. Yea, and why even of yourselves judge ye not what is right? Discernment of the time should lead to self-judgment about what is right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 58. When thou goest with thine adversary to the magistrate, as thou art in the way, give diligence that thou mayest be delivered from him, lest he hale thee to the judge, and the judge deliver thee to the officer, and the officer cast thee into prison. Henry urges hastening to obtain peace with God before it is too late, applying to God in Christ reconciling the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 59. I tell thee, thou shalt not depart thence, till thou hast paid the very last mite. The way with an adversary ends in exact payment, so settlement must come before judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
