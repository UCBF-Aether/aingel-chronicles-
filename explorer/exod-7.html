The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD said unto Moses, See, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh: and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet.

2 Thou shalt speak all that I command thee: and Aaron thy brother shall speak unto Pharaoh, that he send the children of Israel out of his land.

3 And I will harden Pharaoh’s heart, and multiply my signs and my wonders in the land of Egypt.

4 But Pharaoh shall not hearken unto you, that I may lay my hand upon Egypt, and bring forth mine armies, and my people the children of Israel, out of the land of Egypt by great judgments.

5 And the Egyptians shall know that I am the LORD, when I stretch forth mine hand upon Egypt, and bring out the children of Israel from among them.

6 And Moses and Aaron did as the LORD commanded them, so did they.

7 And Moses was fourscore years old, and Aaron fourscore and three years old, when they spake unto Pharaoh.

8 And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron, saying,

9 When Pharaoh shall speak unto you, saying, Shew a miracle for you: then thou shalt say unto Aaron, Take thy rod, and cast it before Pharaoh, and it shall become a serpent.

10 And Moses and Aaron went in unto Pharaoh, and they did so as the LORD had commanded: and Aaron cast down his rod before Pharaoh, and before his servants, and it became a serpent.

11 Then Pharaoh also called the wise men and the sorcerers: now the magicians of Egypt, they also did in like manner with their enchantments.

12 For they cast down every man his rod, and they became serpents: but Aaron’s rod swallowed up their rods.

13 And he hardened Pharaoh’s heart, that he hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.

14 And the LORD said unto Moses, Pharaoh’s heart is hardened, he refuseth to let the people go.

15 Get thee unto Pharaoh in the morning; lo, he goeth out unto the water; and thou shalt stand by the river’s brink against he come; and the rod which was turned to a serpent shalt thou take in thine hand.

16 And thou shalt say unto him, The LORD God of the Hebrews hath sent me unto thee, saying, Let my people go, that they may serve me in the wilderness: and, behold, hitherto thou wouldest not hear.

17 Thus saith the LORD, In this thou shalt know that I am the LORD: behold, I will smite with the rod that is in mine hand upon the waters which are in the river, and they shall be turned to blood.

18 And the fish that is in the river shall die, and the river shall stink; and the Egyptians shall lothe to drink of the water of the river.

19 And the LORD spake unto Moses, Say unto Aaron, Take thy rod, and stretch out thine hand upon the waters of Egypt, upon their streams, upon their rivers, and upon their ponds, and upon all their pools of water, that they may become blood; and that there may be blood throughout all the land of Egypt, both in vessels of wood, and in vessels of stone.

20 And Moses and Aaron did so, as the LORD commanded; and he lifted up the rod, and smote the waters that were in the river, in the sight of Pharaoh, and in the sight of his servants; and all the waters that were in the river were turned to blood.

21 And the fish that was in the river died; and the river stank, and the Egyptians could not drink of the water of the river; and there was blood throughout all the land of Egypt.

22 And the magicians of Egypt did so with their enchantments: and Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, neither did he hearken unto them; as the LORD had said.

23 And Pharaoh turned and went into his house, neither did he set his heart to this also.

24 And all the Egyptians digged round about the river for water to drink; for they could not drink of the water of the river.

25 And seven days were fulfilled, after that the LORD had smitten the river.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Moses and Aaron, now old men, confront Pharaoh: Aaron's rod becomes a serpent, but Pharaoh's magicians do the same and his heart is hardened. The first plague follows, when the Nile and all Egypt's waters turn to blood and the fish die. Pharaoh's magicians imitate the wonder, and Pharaoh turns away unmoved. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God said, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh, and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet. Henry explains the strange title: Moses would speak with divine authority, and Aaron would utter what Moses received, as a prophet utters God's word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Moses was to speak all God commanded, and Aaron was to speak to Pharaoh, that he send Israel out. Henry notes the division of labour was God's own appointment, answering Moses's plea of uncircumcised lips. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. God would harden Pharaoh's heart and multiply his signs and wonders in Egypt. Henry explains the hardening as judicial: Pharaoh's repeated refusals were given over to their own course, that God's wonders might be multiplied. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Pharaoh would not hearken, that God might lay his hand upon Egypt and bring forth his armies by great judgments. Henry notes the plagues were not accidents but judgments, each aimed at Egypt's gods and pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The Egyptians would know that God is the LORD when he stretched out his hand upon Egypt and brought out Israel. Henry observes the plagues were also revelation: Egypt would learn the name Pharaoh had scorned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Moses and Aaron did as the LORD commanded them, so did they. Henry notes the simple obedience recorded here contrasts with all their earlier excuses, and it is the pattern of the whole deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Moses was eighty years old and Aaron eighty-three when they spoke to Pharaoh. Henry observes God called old men to this work, that the power might plainly be his and not theirs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The LORD spake to Moses and Aaron, directing them before they entered Pharaoh's court again. Henry notes every step was directed: the messengers did not improvise before the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. When Pharaoh demanded a miracle, Aaron was to cast his rod before him, and it would become a serpent. Henry notes the sign was fitted to the demand: Pharaoh asked for proof, and proof was given at once. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Aaron cast down his rod before Pharaoh and his servants, and it became a serpent. Henry observes the wonder was done openly in court, where it could neither be hidden nor denied. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Pharaoh called the wise men and sorcerers, and the magicians of Egypt did in like manner with their enchantments. Henry notes Satan's counterfeits appear wherever God's true wonders are wrought, to keep unbelief supplied with excuses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Each magician's rod became a serpent, but Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods. Henry reads the swallowing as the verdict on the contest: God's power devoured the imitation, leaving Pharaoh without a rival wonder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he hearkened not, as the LORD had said. Henry notes the miracle convinced no one who had resolved not to be convinced; the heart, not the eye, was the seat of the refusal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The LORD told Moses Pharaoh's heart was hardened and he refused to let the people go. Henry observes the diagnosis preceded the first plague: the disease was named before the cure was applied. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Moses was to meet Pharaoh in the morning as he went out to the water, standing by the river's brink with the rod in his hand. Henry notes the place was chosen: Pharaoh at his river-god, confronted where his pride and worship met. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Moses was to say, The LORD God of the Hebrews hath sent me, saying, Let my people go, that they may serve me in the wilderness, but hitherto thou wouldest not hear. Henry observes the demand was now backed by warning: past refusal would be answered by judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. In this thou shalt know that I am the LORD: Moses would smite the river's waters with the rod, and they would be turned to blood. Henry notes the sign answered Pharaoh's question, Who is the LORD? with a deed, not a debate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The fish would die, the river would stink, and the Egyptians would loathe to drink of it. Henry observes the plague struck Egypt's life-source and its god at once: the Nile that fed them became undrinkable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Aaron was to stretch his hand over all Egypt's waters, rivers, streams, and pools, and over vessels of wood and stone, that all might become blood. Henry notes the universality left no refuge: stored water as well as flowing water turned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Moses and Aaron did so, and Aaron lifted the rod and smote the waters in the sight of Pharaoh and his servants, and all the waters became blood. Henry observes the wonder was wrought before witnesses, that none could call it a tale. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The fish died, the river stank, the Egyptians could not drink, and there was blood throughout all the land of Egypt. Henry notes the completeness of the stroke: a nation's drink became its horror for seven days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The magicians did so with their enchantments, and Pharaoh's heart was hardened, neither did he hearken. Henry observes the counterfeit only deepened the plague, for it added blood without removing any, yet Pharaoh took it as excuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Pharaoh turned and went into his house, neither did he set his heart to this also. Henry notes the most damning response of all: not rage, but indifference, walking away from a judgment as if it were nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. All the Egyptians digged round about the river for water to drink, for they could not drink of the river. Henry observes the people felt what their king ignored, digging for life beside a river of death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Seven days were fulfilled after the LORD had smitten the river. Henry notes the plague was given its full week, that Egypt might feel the duration as well as the stroke, before the next demand came. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
