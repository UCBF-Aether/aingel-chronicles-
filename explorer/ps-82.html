The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 82 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 God standeth in the congregation of the mighty; he judgeth among the gods.

2 How long will ye judge unjustly, and accept the persons of the wicked? Selah.

3 Defend the poor and fatherless: do justice to the afflicted and needy.

4 Deliver the poor and needy: rid them out of the hand of the wicked.

5 They know not, neither will they understand; they walk on in darkness: all the foundations of the earth are out of course.

6 I have said, Ye are gods; and all of you are children of the most High.

7 But ye shall die like men, and fall like one of the princes.

8 Arise, O God, judge the earth: for thou shalt inherit all nations.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God stands in judgment over the mighty, addressed here as "gods," and charges them with judging unjustly and favouring the wicked. The command is to defend the poor and fatherless, do justice to the afflicted and needy, and deliver them from the wicked. Those judged walk on in darkness, yet are told they shall die like men; the psalm closes by asking God himself to arise, judge the earth, and inherit all nations. Interpreted Psalm text above; study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God stands in the congregation of the mighty and judges among the gods — rulers sit in his court, not above it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 2. How long will ye judge unjustly and accept the persons of the wicked? Partial justice is itself indicted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 3. Magistrates are charged to use entrusted power for those who cannot pay for it: the poor, who have no money to make friends or fee counsel, and the fatherless, who have lost the guides of their youth. Henry makes the point sharp — men called gods must be fathers of the fatherless, as Job was, and a poor man's honest cause must suffer no prejudice from his poverty, however great his adversary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 4. Deliver the poor and needy, rid them out of the hand of the wicked, is the magistrate's charge to rescue: they are to rescue those who have already fallen into the hands of oppressors and deliver them — rid them out of the hand of the wicked; avenge them of their adversary. These are clients whom there is nothing to be got by, no pay for serving them, no interest by obliging them; yet these are those whom judges and magistrates must concern themselves for, whose comfort they must consult and whose cause they must espouse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 5. Ignorance here is willed, not innocent: judges who will not understand walk on in darkness because interest, not reason or justice, governs them, and public miscarriage becomes public mischief, with foundations out of course. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 6. I have said, Ye are gods, and all of you children of the Most High — their office was godlike in authority, not in nature. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 7. But ye shall die like men and fall like one of the princes; high office does not exempt from death and judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
  • Verse 8. Reasoning with proud oppressors has proved useless, so the psalmist appeals upward: God must take to himself his great power and judge the earth. Henry reads this two ways at once — in providence, God has reserved power to himself and overrules wicked rulers, and in the Messiah, Christ shall inherit all nations and set these disorders right at his coming. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Complete)
