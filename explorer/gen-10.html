The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now these are the generations of the sons of Noah, Shem, Ham, and Japheth: and unto them were sons born after the flood.

2 The sons of Japheth; Gomer, and Magog, and Madai, and Javan, and Tubal, and Meshech, and Tiras.

3 And the sons of Gomer; Ashkenaz, and Riphath, and Togarmah.

4 And the sons of Javan; Elishah, and Tarshish, Kittim, and Dodanim.

5 By these were the isles of the Gentiles divided in their lands; every one after his tongue, after their families, in their nations.

6 And the sons of Ham; Cush, and Mizraim, and Phut, and Canaan.

7 And the sons of Cush; Seba, and Havilah, and Sabtah, and Raamah, and Sabtechah: and the sons of Raamah; Sheba, and Dedan.

8 And Cush begat Nimrod: he began to be a mighty one in the earth.

9 He was a mighty hunter before the LORD: wherefore it is said, Even as Nimrod the mighty hunter before the LORD.

10 And the beginning of his kingdom was Babel, and Erech, and Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar.

11 Out of that land went forth Asshur, and builded Nineveh, and the city Rehoboth, and Calah,

12 And Resen between Nineveh and Calah: the same is a great city.

13 And Mizraim begat Ludim, and Anamim, and Lehabim, and Naphtuhim,

14 And Pathrusim, and Casluhim, (out of whom came Philistim,) and Caphtorim.

15 And Canaan begat Sidon his first born, and Heth,

16 And the Jebusite, and the Amorite, and the Girgasite,

17 And the Hivite, and the Arkite, and the Sinite,

18 And the Arvadite, and the Zemarite, and the Hamathite: and afterward were the families of the Canaanites spread abroad.

19 And the border of the Canaanites was from Sidon, as thou comest to Gerar, unto Gaza; as thou goest, unto Sodom, and Gomorrah, and Admah, and Zeboim, even unto Lasha.

20 These are the sons of Ham, after their families, after their tongues, in their countries, and in their nations.

21 Unto Shem also, the father of all the children of Eber, the brother of Japheth the elder, even to him were children born.

22 The children of Shem; Elam, and Asshur, and Arphaxad, and Lud, and Aram.

23 And the children of Aram; Uz, and Hul, and Gether, and Mash.

24 And Arphaxad begat Salah; and Salah begat Eber.

25 And unto Eber were born two sons: the name of one was Peleg; for in his days was the earth divided; and his brother’s name was Joktan.

26 And Joktan begat Almodad, and Sheleph, and Hazarmaveth, and Jerah,

27 And Hadoram, and Uzal, and Diklah,

28 And Obal, and Abimael, and Sheba,

29 And Ophir, and Havilah, and Jobab: all these were the sons of Joktan.

30 And their dwelling was from Mesha, as thou goest unto Sephar a mount of the east.

31 These are the sons of Shem, after their families, after their tongues, in their lands, after their nations.

32 These are the families of the sons of Noah, after their generations, in their nations: and by these were the nations divided in the earth after the flood.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This is the table of the peoples after the flood: the sons of Shem, Ham and Japheth and their families spread into their nations and languages. Verses 3, 4, 7, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 23, 26, 27, 28, 29 and 30 are name lists carried in the Text panel, on which the study notes are silent. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These are the generations of Shem, Ham and Japheth after the flood, to whom sons were born. The chapter is a table of peoples, showing how the one household of chapter 9 spread into nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 2. The sons of Japheth are Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech and Tiras. JFB and Clarke identify these names with peoples spreading north and west, toward Asia Minor and Europe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 5. From these the isles of the Gentiles were divided in their lands, each after tongue, family and nation. The verse summarises Japheth's spread: peoples divided by language and kin, not yet by separate kingdoms alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 6. The sons of Ham are Cush, Mizraim, Phut and Canaan. Ham's line spreads south and west into Africa and Canaan, the regions that will dominate much of the story ahead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 8. Cush fathered Nimrod, who began to be a mighty one on the earth. Nimrod is the first figure in the table given a personal character, not just a name in a list. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 9. He was a mighty hunter before the LORD, so that it became a proverb. JFB notes mighty hunter can mean a hunter of men as well as beasts: a founder of power through conquest, whose fame passed into saying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 10. The beginning of his kingdom was Babel, Erech, Accad and Calneh, in the land of Shinar. Nimrod's kingdom starts in Mesopotamia, and Babel will become the centre of the next chapter's story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 11. Out of that land went Asshur and built Nineveh, Rehoboth, Calah and Resen. The Assyrian cities show Nimrod's power spreading north, founding the great cities later known as Assyria's capitals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 12. Resen is named as a great city lying between Nineveh and Calah. JFB notes it completes the Assyrian group founded from Nimrod's kingdom, showing that power's cities, not just its founder, spread north. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 15. Canaan fathered Sidon his firstborn, and Heth. The Canaanite list begins with the coastal city Sidon and the Hittites, peoples Israel will later meet in the promised land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 20. These are the sons of Ham, after families, tongues, countries and nations. The summary closes Ham's section as it closed Japheth's: a people divided by kin, language and land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 21. To Shem also, the elder brother of Japheth, children were born, and Shem is the father of all the children of Eber. Shem's line is introduced last and with honour, because the Hebrews, Eber's children, come through him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 22. The children of Shem are Elam, Asshur, Arphaxad, Lud and Aram. These names point to peoples east and north of Mesopotamia, including Elam and Aram, known in later history. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 24. Arphaxad fathered Salah, and Salah fathered Eber. This short chain is the direct line toward Abraham: through Eber, the name Hebrew will later be traced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 25. To Eber were born Peleg, for in his days the earth was divided, and his brother Joktan. JFB and Gill read the division as the scattering at Babel, so Peleg's name itself memorialises that event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 31. These are the sons of Shem, after families, tongues, lands and nations. Shem's section closes with the same formula, completing the threefold table. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill
  • Verse 32. These are the families of Noah's sons after their generations, in their nations, and from these the nations were divided on the earth after the flood. The chapter ends by stating its purpose: all nations after the flood descend from Noah's three sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill

Connections to other passages
