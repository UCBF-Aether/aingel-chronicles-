The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ezekiel 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then I looked, and, behold, in the firmament that was above the head of the cherubims there appeared over them as it were a sapphire stone, as the appearance of the likeness of a throne.

2 And he spake unto the man clothed with linen, and said, Go in between the wheels, even under the cherub, and fill thine hand with coals of fire from between the cherubims, and scatter them over the city. And he went in in my sight.

3 Now the cherubims stood on the right side of the house, when the man went in; and the cloud filled the inner court.

4 Then the glory of the LORD went up from the cherub, and stood over the threshold of the house; and the house was filled with the cloud, and the court was full of the brightness of the LORD’s glory.

5 And the sound of the cherubims’ wings was heard even to the outer court, as the voice of the Almighty God when he speaketh.

6 And it came to pass, that when he had commanded the man clothed with linen, saying, Take fire from between the wheels, from between the cherubims; then he went in, and stood beside the wheels.

7 And one cherub stretched forth his hand from between the cherubims unto the fire that was between the cherubims, and took thereof, and put it into the hands of him that was clothed with linen: who took it, and went out.

8 And there appeared in the cherubims the form of a man’s hand under their wings.

9 And when I looked, behold the four wheels by the cherubims, one wheel by one cherub, and another wheel by another cherub: and the appearance of the wheels was as the colour of a beryl stone.

10 And as for their appearances, they four had one likeness, as if a wheel had been in the midst of a wheel.

11 When they went, they went upon their four sides; they turned not as they went, but to the place whither the head looked they followed it; they turned not as they went.

12 And their whole body, and their backs, and their hands, and their wings, and the wheels, were full of eyes round about, even the wheels that they four had.

13 As for the wheels, it was cried unto them in my hearing, O wheel.

14 And every one had four faces: the first face was the face of a cherub, and the second face was the face of a man, and the third the face of a lion, and the fourth the face of an eagle.

15 And the cherubims were lifted up. This is the living creature that I saw by the river of Chebar.

16 And when the cherubims went, the wheels went by them: and when the cherubims lifted up their wings to mount up from the earth, the same wheels also turned not from beside them.

17 When they stood, these stood; and when they were lifted up, these lifted up themselves also: for the spirit of the living creature was in them.

18 Then the glory of the LORD departed from off the threshold of the house, and stood over the cherubims.

19 And the cherubims lifted up their wings, and mounted up from the earth in my sight: when they went out, the wheels also were beside them, and every one stood at the door of the east gate of the LORD’s house; and the glory of the God of Israel was over them above.

20 This is the living creature that I saw under the God of Israel by the river of Chebar; and I knew that they were the cherubims.

21 Every one had four faces apiece, and every one four wings; and the likeness of the hands of a man was under their wings.

22 And the likeness of their faces was the same faces which I saw by the river of Chebar, their appearances and themselves: they went every one straight forward.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Above the cherubim Ezekiel again sees a sapphire throne, and the man clothed in linen is told to take coals of fire from between the cherubim and scatter them over the city. Wheels, wings, and eyes move together in the vision, and the glory of God rises from the cherubim to the threshold of the house, as judgment on Jerusalem is set in motion. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Above the cherubim Ezekiel sees, as it were, a sapphire stone like a throne, opening the vision of the city's burning. Henry places the whole first section under that heading: the fire to be taken from between the cherubim signifies God's wrath to be executed upon Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The man clothed with linen is told to go between the wheels, under the cherub, fill his hand with coals of fire from between the cherubim, and scatter them over the city. Henry notes the fire of divine wrath that kindles judgment on a people is just and holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The cherubim stand on the right side of the house as the man goes in, and the cloud fills the inner court. Henry's section point stands behind the scene: the cloud of God's presence fills the house even as the fire of his wrath is being fetched for Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The glory of the Lord goes up from the cherub and stands over the threshold, while the house is filled with the cloud and the court with the brightness of his glory. Henry reads the movement as judgment prepared: the glory pauses at the threshold as the orders for the city's burning go out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The sound of the cherubim's wings is heard even to the outer court, as the voice of Almighty God when he speaks. Henry's first section gives the sound its meaning: it heralds the just and holy fire of divine wrath about to fall on Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. When the man clothed with linen is commanded to take fire from between the wheels, from between the cherubim, he goes in and stands beside the wheels. Henry notes the fire taken there signifies wrath to be executed, and in the great day the earth and all its works will be burnt up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. One cherub stretches his hand to the fire between the cherubim, takes some, and puts it into the hands of the man clothed with linen, who takes it and goes out. Henry's point is that the fire is no accident: it is God's wrath, deliberately fetched and deliberately carried out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. In the cherubim appears the form of a man's hand under their wings, the directing hand behind the vision. Henry's second section sees here the working of divine providence: the Spirit of life directs all creatures, in heaven and on earth, to serve the divine purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Four wheels stand by the cherubim, one wheel by one cherub, in the appearance of beryl. Henry reads the wheels as providence at work in the government of the lower world and its affairs, moving as God directs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The four wheels have one likeness, as if a wheel were in the midst of a wheel. Henry's providence point applies: however intricate their motions appear, all creatures and events are directed by one Spirit to one divine purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. The wheels go upon their four sides and turn not as they go, following wherever the head looks. Henry notes that when God is leaving a people in displeasure, angels above and all events below further his departure, each moving exactly as directed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The whole body, backs, hands, wings, and wheels are full of eyes round about. Henry's providence reading gives the eyes their sense: nothing in the lower world escapes the directing Spirit of God, who sees and orders all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. The wheels are cried to in Ezekiel's hearing, 'O wheel,' addressed as instruments in the vision. Henry sees providence itself summoned: the wheels are not blind fortune but servants of the divine purpose in the affairs of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Each cherub has four faces: a cherub, a man, a lion, and an eagle. Henry's section treats the whole living creature as part of providence's government, every face turned to the work the Spirit of life directs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The cherubim are lifted up, and Ezekiel recognises them as the living creature he saw by the river Chebar. Henry's departure theme begins here: the same creatures that bore God's glory at Chebar now rise to carry it away from the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. When the cherubim go, the wheels go by them, and when the cherubim lift their wings to mount up, the wheels lift up too. Henry states the concurrence plainly: angels above and events below further God's departure together, because the Spirit of the living creature is in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. When the cherubim stand, the wheels stand, and when they are lifted up, the wheels lift up themselves also, for the spirit of the living creature is in them. Henry's point is that the Spirit of God directs all creatures alike, so none moves apart from the divine purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. The glory of the Lord departs from the threshold of the house and stands over the cherubim. Henry warns that God removes by degrees from a provoking people, and this staged departure is the visible sign of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. The cherubim lift their wings and mount up from the earth in Ezekiel's sight, the wheels beside them, and they stand at the door of the east gate, with the glory of the God of Israel over them. Henry notes that when God is leaving a people, angels and events concur to further his going, yet he would return to a repenting, praying people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. This is the living creature Ezekiel saw under the God of Israel by the river Chebar, and he knows now that they are the cherubim. Henry's providence section closes the identification: the same directed creatures govern the departure as governed the first vision. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Each cherub has four faces and four wings, with the likeness of a man's hands under their wings. Henry reads the whole array as providence equipped for its work, every part directed by the Spirit of life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. The likeness of their faces is the same as those seen by the river Chebar, and they go every one straight forward. Henry's closing warning applies: let this warn sinners to seek the Lord while he may be found, and cause us all to walk humbly and watchfully with our God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

Connections to other passages

  • Ezekiel 1 — the Chebar vision. The living creatures seen here are identified in this chapter itself with the creatures by the river Chebar: “This is the living creature that I saw by the river of Chebar; and I knew that they were the cherubims,” and their faces are “the same faces which I saw by the river of Chebar.” Documented Ezekiel 10:15, 20, 22 (chapter text)
