The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Hebrews 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 For every high priest taken from among men is ordained for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins:

2 Who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way; for that he himself also is compassed with infirmity.

3 And by reason hereof he ought, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins.

4 And no man taketh this honour unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron.

5 So also Christ glorified not himself to be made an high priest; but he that said unto him, Thou art my Son, to day have I begotten thee.

6 As he saith also in another place, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec.

7 Who in the days of his flesh, when he had offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears unto him that was able to save him from death, and was heard in that he feared;

8 Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered;

9 And being made perfect, he became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him;

10 Called of God an high priest after the order of Melchisedec.

11 Of whom we have many things to say, and hard to be uttered, seeing ye are dull of hearing.

12 For when for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye have need that one teach you again which be the first principles of the oracles of God; and are become such as have need of milk, and not of strong meat.

13 For every one that useth milk is unskilful in the word of righteousness: for he is a babe.

14 But strong meat belongeth to them that are of full age, even those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Every high priest is taken from among men, the writer explains, able to deal gently with the ignorant because he too is beset with weakness, and called by God rather than taking the honour himself. So Christ, who did not glorify himself but was called God's Son and priest forever after the order of Melchisedec, learned obedience through suffering, yet the readers, who should be teachers by now, still need milk rather than solid food. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. For every high priest taken from among men is ordained for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins — the High Priest must be a man, a partaker of our nature, because sinful man cannot come to God alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way; for that he himself also is compassed with infirmity — a priest who knows infirmity can intercede with tenderness for those out of the way of truth, duty, and happiness, and lead them back from by-paths of error, sin, and misery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. And by reason hereof he ought, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins — the Aaronic priest's own infirmity required offerings for himself as well as for the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. And no man taketh this honour unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron — only those called of God can expect his assistance, acceptance, and blessing on their services. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. So also Christ glorified not himself to be made an high priest; but he that said unto him, Thou art my Son, to day have I begotten thee — Christ's priesthood, like Aaron's, rests on God's call, not on self-appointment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. As he saith also in another place, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec — Christ's priesthood is not Aaronic but Melchizedekian, and it is for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Who in the days of his flesh, when he had offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears unto him that was able to save him from death, and was heard in that he feared — Christ set an example not only to pray, but to be fervent in prayer; how many dry prayers, how few wetted with tears, do we offer up to God! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered — Christ, in the days of his flesh a tempted, suffering, dying Jesus, learned humble obedience to God's will by affliction, and we need affliction to teach us submission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. And being made perfect, he became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him — raised and exalted, he was given power to save to the uttermost all sinners who come unto God through him; but are we of that number? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Called of God an high priest after the order of Melchisedec — God himself confirms Christ in this everlasting priesthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Of whom we have many things to say, and hard to be uttered, seeing ye are dull of hearing — dull hearers make the preaching of the gospel difficult, and even those who have some faith may be dull hearers and slow to believe. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. For when for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye have need that one teach you again which be the first principles of the oracles of God; and are become such as have need of milk, and not of strong meat — much is looked for from those to whom much is given, and long-standing Christians still needing first principles are reproved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. For every one that useth milk is unskilful in the word of righteousness: for he is a babe — to be unskilful denotes want of experience in the things of the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. But strong meat belongeth to them that are of full age, even those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil — Christian experience is a spiritual sense, taste, or relish of the goodness and excellence of gospel truths, and no tongue can express the soul's satisfaction in a sense of Divine goodness in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

  • milk and strong meat. elementary teaching and mature teaching: readers who should be teachers still need milk, being unskilled in the word of righteousness (verses 12–14) Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
