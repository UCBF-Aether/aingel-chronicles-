The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Esther 8 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–10; King James Version, Apocrypha (the Rest of Esther) for the Greek additions)

1 On that day did the king Ahasuerus give the house of Haman the Jews’ enemy unto Esther the queen. And Mordecai came before the king; for Esther had told what he was unto her.

2 And the king took off his ring, which he had taken from Haman, and gave it unto Mordecai. And Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman.

3 And Esther spake yet again before the king, and fell down at his feet, and besought him with tears to put away the mischief of Haman the Agagite, and his device that he had devised against the Jews.

4 Then the king held out the golden sceptre toward Esther. So Esther arose, and stood before the king,

5 And said, If it please the king, and if I have favour in his sight, and the thing seem right before the king, and I be pleasing in his eyes, let it be written to reverse the letters devised by Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, which he wrote to destroy the Jews which are in all the king’s provinces:

6 For how can I endure to see the evil that shall come unto my people? or how can I endure to see the destruction of my kindred?

7 Then the king Ahasuerus said unto Esther the queen and to Mordecai the Jew, Behold, I have given Esther the house of Haman, and him they have hanged upon the gallows, because he laid his hand upon the Jews.

8 Write ye also for the Jews, as it liketh you, in the king’s name, and seal it with the king’s ring: for the writing which is written in the king’s name, and sealed with the king’s ring, may no man reverse.

9 Then were the king’s scribes called at that time in the third month, that is, the month Sivan, on the three and twentieth day thereof; and it was written according to all that Mordecai commanded unto the Jews, and to the lieutenants, and the deputies and rulers of the provinces which are from India unto Ethiopia, an hundred twenty and seven provinces, unto every province according to the writing thereof, and unto every people after their language, and to the Jews according to their writing, and according to their language.

10 And he wrote in the king Ahasuerus’ name, and sealed it with the king’s ring, and sent letters by posts on horseback, and riders on mules, camels, and young dromedaries:

11 Wherein the king granted the Jews which were in every city to gather themselves together, and to stand for their life, to destroy, to slay and to cause to perish, all the power of the people and province that would assault them, both little ones and women, and to take the spoil of them for a prey,

12 Upon one day in all the provinces of king Ahasuerus, namely, upon the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is the month Adar.

13 The copy of the writing for a commandment to be given in every province was published unto all people, and that the Jews should be ready against that day to avenge themselves on their enemies.

14 So the posts that rode upon mules and camels went out, being hastened and pressed on by the king’s commandment. And the decree was given at Shushan the palace.

15 And Mordecai went out from the presence of the king in royal apparel of blue and white, and with a great crown of gold, and with a garment of fine linen and purple: and the city of Shushan rejoiced and was glad.

16 The Jews had light, and gladness, and joy, and honour.

17 And in every province, and in every city, whithersoever the king’s commandment and his decree came, the Jews had joy and gladness, a feast and a good day. And many of the people of the land became Jews; for the fear of the Jews fell upon them.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Haman's house is given to Esther and the king's ring to Mordecai, but Haman's decree cannot simply be reversed, since a writing written in the king's name and sealed with the king's ring may no man reverse. Mordecai therefore writes a counter-decree in the king's name, sealed with the ring and sent by posts through the hundred and twenty-seven provinces, licensing the Jews to gather themselves together and stand for their life on the thirteenth day of Adar; Shushan rejoices as Mordecai goes out in royal apparel of blue and white with a great crown of gold. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. On that day did the king Ahasuerus give the house of Haman the Jews' enemy unto Esther the queen. And Mordecai came before the king; for Esther had told what he was unto her. JFB notes the transfer is immediate: Haman's estate passes to the queen, and Mordecai, now known as her kinsman, is admitted to the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And the king took off his ring, which he had taken from Haman, and gave it unto Mordecai. And Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman. Henry notes the ring passes from enemy to deliverer: the seal that signed the Jews' death now seals their deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And Esther spake yet again before the king, and fell down at his feet, and besought him with tears to put away the mischief of Haman the Agagite, and his device that he had devised against the Jews. JFB notes Esther's work is half done: Haman is dead, but his decree lives, and she weeps for a law, not only for a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Then the king held out the golden sceptre toward Esther. So Esther arose, and stood before the king. The sceptre is held out a second time, and Esther, who once risked death to enter, now stands by grace to plead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. And said, If it please the king, and if I have favour in his sight, and the thing seem right before the king, and I be pleasing in his eyes, let it be written to reverse the letters devised by Haman, which he wrote to destroy the Jews in all the king's provinces. Henry notes Esther asks for reversal in the full language of court deference, though she knows Persian law cannot be reversed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For how can I endure to see the evil that shall come unto my people? or how can I endure to see the destruction of my kindred? JFB notes the plea is personal and unanswerable: a queen cannot watch her kindred perish and call herself queen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Then the king Ahasuerus said unto Esther the queen and to Mordecai the Jew, Behold, I have given Esther the house of Haman, and him they have hanged upon the gallows, because he laid his hand upon the Jews. Henry notes the king recites what is done, as if that settled the matter, while the decree still stands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Write ye also for the Jews, as it likes you, in the king's name, and seal it with the king's ring: for the writing written in the king's name, and sealed with the king's ring, may no man reverse. JFB notes the solution: since the first writing cannot be undone, a second writing, equally royal, arms the Jews to defend themselves on the same day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Then were the king's scribes called in the third month, Sivan, on the twenty-third day; and it was written according to all that Mordecai commanded unto the Jews, and to the lieutenants, deputies and rulers of the provinces from India to Ethiopia, a hundred and twenty-seven provinces, each in its own writing and language, and to the Jews according to their writing and language. Henry notes Mordecai now commands the scribes, and the Jews receive the decree in their own tongue, a detail the first decree lacked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. And he wrote in king Ahasuerus's name, and sealed it with the king's ring, and sent letters by posts on horseback, and riders on mules, camels, and young dromedaries. JFB notes the swiftest posts of the empire carry the counter-decree, as they once carried the decree of death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Wherein the king granted the Jews in every city to gather themselves together, and to stand for their life, to destroy, to slay and to cause to perish, all the power of the people and province that would assault them, both little ones and women, and to take the spoil of them for a prey. Henry notes the grant mirrors the first decree's language, turning its weapons back on the attackers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Upon one day in all the provinces of king Ahasuerus, namely, upon the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month Adar. The same day is named, so that deliverance and danger meet on a single date. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The copy of the writing for a commandment to be given in every province was published unto all people, and that the Jews should be ready against that day to avenge themselves on their enemies. JFB notes publication matters: enemies must know the Jews are now lawfully armed, not merely privately warned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. So the posts that rode upon mules and camels went out, being hastened and pressed on by the king's commandment. And the decree was given at Shushan the palace. Henry notes the haste matches the first decree's haste, but now it carries life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And Mordecai went out from the presence of the king in royal apparel of blue and white, and with a great crown of gold, and with a garment of fine linen and purple: and the city of Shushan rejoiced and was glad. JFB notes Mordecai's dress is the visible sign of the reversal: the Jew at the gate now walks out in royal colours, and the city that was perplexed rejoices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The Jews had light, and gladness, and joy, and honour. Henry notes the fourfold summary: light after darkness, gladness after mourning, joy after fear, honour after contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. And in every province, and in every city, whithersoever the king's commandment and decree came, the Jews had joy and gladness, a feast and a good day. And many of the people of the land became Jews; for the fear of the Jews fell upon them. JFB notes the counter-decree's effect spreads like the first one's mourning, and fear now draws converts, as mourning once drew only pity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Sealed with the king's ring. The rule that binds this chapter: a writing written in the king's name and sealed with the king's ring may no man reverse, so Haman's decree must be met with a counter-decree rather than a repeal. Documented Chapter text, verse 8
  • Sivan. The month named with the third month, on whose twenty-third day the scribes are called to write Mordecai's counter-decree. Documented Chapter text, verse 9
