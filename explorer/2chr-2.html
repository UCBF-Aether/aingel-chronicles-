The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Solomon determined to build an house for the name of the LORD, and an house for his kingdom.

2 And Solomon told out threescore and ten thousand men to bear burdens, and fourscore thousand to hew in the mountain, and three thousand and six hundred to oversee them.

3 And Solomon sent to Huram the king of Tyre, saying, As thou didst deal with David my father, and didst send him cedars to build him an house to dwell therein, even so deal with me.

4 Behold, I build an house to the name of the LORD my God, to dedicate it to him, and to burn before him sweet incense, and for the continual shewbread, and for the burnt offerings morning and evening, on the sabbaths, and on the new moons, and on the solemn feasts of the LORD our God. This is an ordinance for ever to Israel.

5 And the house which I build is great: for great is our God above all gods.

6 But who is able to build him an house, seeing the heaven and heaven of heavens cannot contain him? who am I then, that I should build him an house, save only to burn sacrifice before him?

7 Send me now therefore a man cunning to work in gold, and in silver, and in brass, and in iron, and in purple, and crimson, and blue, and that can skill to grave with the cunning men that are with me in Judah and in Jerusalem, whom David my father did provide.

8 Send me also cedar trees, fir trees, and algum trees, out of Lebanon: for I know that thy servants can skill to cut timber in Lebanon; and, behold, my servants shall be with thy servants,

9 Even to prepare me timber in abundance: for the house which I am about to build shall be wonderful great.

10 And, behold, I will give to thy servants, the hewers that cut timber, twenty thousand measures of beaten wheat, and twenty thousand measures of barley, and twenty thousand baths of wine, and twenty thousand baths of oil.

11 Then Huram the king of Tyre answered in writing, which he sent to Solomon, Because the LORD hath loved his people, he hath made thee king over them.

12 Huram said moreover, Blessed be the LORD God of Israel, that made heaven and earth, who hath given to David the king a wise son, endued with prudence and understanding, that might build an house for the LORD, and an house for his kingdom.

13 And now I have sent a cunning man, endued with understanding, of Huram my father’s,

14 The son of a woman of the daughters of Dan, and his father was a man of Tyre, skilful to work in gold, and in silver, in brass, in iron, in stone, and in timber, in purple, in blue, and in fine linen, and in crimson; also to grave any manner of graving, and to find out every device which shall be put to him, with thy cunning men, and with the cunning men of my lord David thy father.

15 Now therefore the wheat, and the barley, the oil, and the wine, which my lord hath spoken of, let him send unto his servants:

16 And we will cut wood out of Lebanon, as much as thou shalt need: and we will bring it to thee in floats by sea to Joppa; and thou shalt carry it up to Jerusalem.

17 And Solomon numbered all the strangers that were in the land of Israel, after the numbering wherewith David his father had numbered them; and they were found an hundred and fifty thousand and three thousand and six hundred.

18 And he set threescore and ten thousand of them to be bearers of burdens, and fourscore thousand to be hewers in the mountain, and three thousand and six hundred overseers to set the people a work.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Solomon's determination to build the temple and his own palace, his agreement with Huram of Tyre for cedar timber and workmen, and the numbering of the labourers for the work. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Solomon determined to build first a house for the name of the Lord, and then a house for his kingdom. Henry notes the order matters: he who is first should be served first, and in both buildings Solomon aimed at the public good, not merely his own convenience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Solomon told out seventy thousand burden-bearers, eighty thousand hewers in the mountain, and thirty-six hundred overseers. Henry notes the vast distribution, one hundred and fifty thousand in all, and that so large a fabric, begun and ended in seven years, found work for so many hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Solomon sent to Huram king of Tyre, asking him to deal with him as he had dealt with David, who received cedars for his house. Henry notes we may expect kindness from our fathers' friends, and should cultivate correspondence with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The house was to be dedicated to God for sweet incense, continual showbread, and burnt offerings morning and evening, on sabbaths, new moons and feasts, an ordinance for ever. Henry notes the house was built to be used in God's service, and that the mysteries of true religion, unlike Gentile superstition, coveted not concealment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The house Solomon built would be great, for great is our God above all gods. Henry notes the house must be great in proportion to the esteem held for God, though between finite and infinite there can be no true proportion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Yet who is able to build God a house, seeing the heaven of heavens cannot contain him? Henry notes Solomon guarded against misapprehension: God does not dwell in temples made with hands, and the house was only for the convenience of priests and worshippers to burn sacrifice before him, with Solomon counting himself unworthy of the work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Solomon asked Huram to send a cunning man to work in gold, silver, brass, iron, purple, crimson and blue, and to engrave with the cunning men David had provided. Henry notes that since temple-work must be the best of its kind, Solomon sought the best workmen that could be got, even from Tyre. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He also asked for cedar, fir and algum trees out of Lebanon, since Huram's servants could skill to cut timber there, with his own servants working alongside. Henry notes no cost or contrivance was to be spared, for the house must be wonderfully great. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Timber was to be prepared in abundance, for the house Solomon was about to build would be wonderful great. Henry's point is that magnificence here was not vanity but proportion: a great God deserved a stately house, built without sparing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Solomon engaged to give the hewers twenty thousand measures each of beaten wheat and barley, and twenty thousand baths each of wine and oil. Henry notes he fed his workmen not with bread and water but plentifully, and that employers should see labourers well paid and well provided for. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Huram answered in writing that because the Lord loved his people, he had made Solomon king over them. Henry notes princes must see themselves preferred for the public good, not personal satisfaction, and rule so as to prove they were given in love and not in anger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Huram blessed the Lord God of Israel, maker of heaven and earth, who had given David a wise son endued with prudence and understanding. Henry notes Huram seems proselyted to Israel's religion, and that when Israel kept close to God's law, neighbouring nations were willing to be instructed by them in true religion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Huram sent a cunning man endued with understanding, of Huram his father's. Henry notes he would not fail Solomon's expectations in anything, a workman answering every requirement of the temple work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. The workman was the son of a woman of Dan, his father a Tyrian, skilful in gold, silver, brass, iron, stone, timber, purple, blue, fine linen and crimson, and in all engraving. Henry notes the mingling of Jewish and Gentile blood as a good omen of Jew and Gentile united in the gospel temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Huram asked that the wheat, barley, oil and wine Solomon had spoken of be sent to his servants. Henry notes Huram signified his dependence on Solomon for the maintenance of the workmen, as Solomon had promised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Huram undertook to cut as much wood out of Lebanon as Solomon needed and bring it in floats by sea to Joppa, whence Solomon would carry it up to Jerusalem. Henry notes this agreement matches the account in Kings, with timber delivered at Joppa for the building. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Solomon numbered all the strangers in Israel, after David's numbering, and they were a hundred and fifty-three thousand six hundred. Henry notes these were proselyted strangers without lands of inheritance, who applied themselves to trades and were bound in gratitude to serve the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. He set seventy thousand to bear burdens, eighty thousand to hew in the mountain, and thirty-six hundred overseers to set the people to work. Henry notes free-born Israelites were not employed in this drudgery, and that the strangers, though bound to serve, were well paid and not to be oppressed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Connections to other passages
