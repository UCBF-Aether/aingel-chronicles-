The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Esther 2 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–10; King James Version, Apocrypha (the Rest of Esther) for the Greek additions)

1 After these things, when the wrath of king Ahasuerus was appeased, he remembered Vashti, and what she had done, and what was decreed against her.

2 Then said the king’s servants that ministered unto him, Let there be fair young virgins sought for the king:

3 And let the king appoint officers in all the provinces of his kingdom, that they may gather together all the fair young virgins unto Shushan the palace, to the house of the women, unto the custody of Hege the king’s chamberlain, keeper of the women; and let their things for purification be given them:

4 And let the maiden which pleaseth the king be queen instead of Vashti. And the thing pleased the king; and he did so.

5 Now in Shushan the palace there was a certain Jew, whose name was Mordecai, the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish, a Benjamite;

6 Who had been carried away from Jerusalem with the captivity which had been carried away with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon had carried away.

7 And he brought up Hadassah, that is, Esther, his uncle’s daughter: for she had neither father nor mother, and the maid was fair and beautiful; whom Mordecai, when her father and mother were dead, took for his own daughter.

8 So it came to pass, when the king’s commandment and his decree was heard, and when many maidens were gathered together unto Shushan the palace, to the custody of Hegai, that Esther was brought also unto the king’s house, to the custody of Hegai, keeper of the women.

9 And the maiden pleased him, and she obtained kindness of him; and he speedily gave her her things for purification, with such things as belonged to her, and seven maidens, which were meet to be given her, out of the king’s house: and he preferred her and her maids unto the best place of the house of the women.

10 Esther had not shewed her people nor her kindred: for Mordecai had charged her that she should not shew it.

11 And Mordecai walked every day before the court of the women’s house, to know how Esther did, and what should become of her.

12 Now when every maid’s turn was come to go in to king Ahasuerus, after that she had been twelve months, according to the manner of the women, (for so were the days of their purifications accomplished, to wit, six months with oil of myrrh, and six months with sweet odours, and with other things for the purifying of the women;)

13 Then thus came every maiden unto the king; whatsoever she desired was given her to go with her out of the house of the women unto the king’s house.

14 In the evening she went, and on the morrow she returned into the second house of the women, to the custody of Shaashgaz, the king’s chamberlain, which kept the concubines: she came in unto the king no more, except the king delighted in her, and that she were called by name.

15 Now when the turn of Esther, the daughter of Abihail the uncle of Mordecai, who had taken her for his daughter, was come to go in unto the king, she required nothing but what Hegai the king’s chamberlain, the keeper of the women, appointed. And Esther obtained favour in the sight of all them that looked upon her.

16 So Esther was taken unto king Ahasuerus into his house royal in the tenth month, which is the month Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign.

17 And the king loved Esther above all the women, and she obtained grace and favour in his sight more than all the virgins; so that he set the royal crown upon her head, and made her queen instead of Vashti.

18 Then the king made a great feast unto all his princes and his servants, even Esther’s feast; and he made a release to the provinces, and gave gifts, according to the state of the king.

19 And when the virgins were gathered together the second time, then Mordecai sat in the king’s gate.

20 Esther had not yet shewed her kindred nor her people; as Mordecai had charged her: for Esther did the commandment of Mordecai, like as when she was brought up with him.

21 In those days, while Mordecai sat in the king’s gate, two of the king’s chamberlains, Bigthan and Teresh, of those which kept the door, were wroth, and sought to lay hands on the king Ahasuerus.

22 And the thing was known to Mordecai, who told it unto Esther the queen; and Esther certified the king thereof in Mordecai’s name.

23 And when inquisition was made of the matter, it was found out; therefore they were both hanged on a tree: and it was written in the book of the chronicles before the king.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After Vashti's removal, maidens are gathered from across the empire to Shushan, and Esther, a Jewess brought up by her cousin Mordecai, is chosen queen in Vashti's place, keeping her people secret at Mordecai's charge. Mordecai, sitting in the king's gate, learns of a plot by two chamberlains against the king, reports it through Esther, and the matter is investigated, punished, and written in the book of the chronicles before the king. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After these things, when the king's wrath was appeased, he remembered Vashti, and what she had done, and what was decreed against her. Henry notes the king's cooling anger turns to regret, but the irrevocable decree cannot be undone, so a new queen must be sought. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Then said the king's servants that ministered unto him, Let there be fair young virgins sought for the king. JFB notes the servants propose a remedy that flatters the king and fills the vacant place without revisiting the divorce. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And let the king appoint officers in all the provinces, that they may gather all the fair young virgins unto Shushan, to the house of the women, under Hege the chamberlain, keeper of the women; and let their things for purification be given them. Henry notes the empire-wide search, with a year of purification, shows how seriously the court took the choice of a queen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. And let the maiden which pleases the king be queen instead of Vashti. And the thing pleased the king; and he did so. The plan is adopted at once, and the search for Vashti's successor begins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Now in Shushan there was a certain Jew, whose name was Mordecai, son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjamite. JFB traces the line to Kish, Saul's father, setting a Benjamite of Saul's house against the Agagite who will soon rise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Who had been carried away from Jerusalem with the captivity carried away with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar had carried away. Henry notes Mordecai belongs to the exile generation's children: a Jew in Persia, yet still counted by his Jerusalem captivity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And he brought up Hadassah, that is, Esther, his uncle's daughter: for she had neither father nor mother, and the maid was fair and beautiful; whom Mordecai, when her father and mother were dead, took for his own daughter. JFB notes Hadassah means myrtle, Esther perhaps star; the orphan is raised by a kinsman who becomes a father to her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. So when the king's commandment and decree was heard, and many maidens were gathered to Shushan to Hegai's custody, Esther was brought also unto the king's house, to Hegai's custody. Henry notes Esther enters the same course as the rest, with no privilege yet, only the providence that placed her there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And the maiden pleased him, and she obtained kindness of him; and he speedily gave her her things for purification, with such things as belonged to her, and seven maidens out of the king's house: and he preferred her and her maids unto the best place of the house of the women. JFB notes Hegai's favour is the first mark of Esther's rise, given before the king ever sees her. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Esther had not showed her people nor her kindred: for Mordecai had charged her that she should not show it. Henry notes the concealment is Mordecai's counsel, not Esther's shame: her Jewishness is kept back until the hour when it can save her people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. And Mordecai walked every day before the court of the women's house, to know how Esther did, and what should become of her. JFB notes the daily walk of a foster-father, anxious and watchful, yet unable to enter where she is kept. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Now when every maid's turn was come to go in to the king, after she had been twelve months, according to the manner of the women, for so were the days of their purifications accomplished, six months with oil of myrrh, and six months with sweet odours and other purifying things. Henry notes the long purification is court ritual, not law, and it delays every maiden a full year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Then thus came every maiden unto the king; whatsoever she desired was given her to go with her out of the house of the women unto the king's house. JFB notes each maiden chose her own adornment for the single night that might win a crown. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. In the evening she went, and on the morrow she returned into the second house of the women, to Shaashgaz's custody, who kept the concubines: she came in unto the king no more, except the king delighted in her, and she were called by name. Henry notes the bleak arithmetic: most maidens pass one night and then wait, unnamed, in a second house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Now when Esther's turn, the daughter of Abihail, Mordecai's uncle, who had taken her for his daughter, was come to go in to the king, she required nothing but what Hegai the king's chamberlain appointed. And Esther obtained favour in the sight of all them that looked upon her. JFB notes her restraint is her distinction: she takes no extra adornment, and favour follows her before she reaches the king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. So Esther was taken unto king Ahasuerus into his house royal in the tenth month, the month Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign. The date fixes her crowning four years after Vashti's fall, in the winter month. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. And the king loved Esther above all the women, and she obtained grace and favour in his sight more than all the virgins; so that he set the royal crown upon her head, and made her queen instead of Vashti. Henry notes the crown passes from Vashti to Esther by the same providence that removed Vashti, and love, not merely choice, seals it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Then the king made a great feast unto all his princes and servants, even Esther's feast; and he made a release to the provinces, and gave gifts, according to the state of the king. JFB notes the feast and tax-release celebrate the new queen, and the gifts spread her favour through the provinces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. And when the virgins were gathered together the second time, then Mordecai sat in the king's gate. Henry notes Mordecai's place at the gate is a post of service and observation, where he will hear what the court hides. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Esther had not yet showed her kindred nor her people; as Mordecai had charged her: for Esther did the commandment of Mordecai, like as when she was brought up with him. JFB notes a queen still obeys her foster-father as a child did: obedience learned in obscurity holds in honour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. In those days, while Mordecai sat in the king's gate, two of the king's chamberlains, Bigthan and Teresh, keepers of the door, were wroth, and sought to lay hand on king Ahasuerus. Henry notes the plot comes from the door-keepers themselves, the men closest to the king's person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. And the thing was known to Mordecai, who told it unto Esther the queen; and Esther certified the king thereof in Mordecai's name. JFB notes the chain of loyalty: Mordecai to Esther, Esther to the king, with Mordecai's name honestly given, not hidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. And when inquisition was made, it was found out; therefore they were both hanged on a tree: and it was written in the book of the chronicles before the king. Henry notes the deed is recorded but not yet rewarded, a delay on which the whole later deliverance turns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Hadassah, that is, Esther. The chapter itself gives both names for the same maiden: Hadassah is her name, Esther the name she bears at court. Documented Chapter text, verse 7
  • House of the women. The part of the king's house where the maidens are kept under the keeper of the women, with a year of purification before going to the king. Documented Chapter text, verses 3, 12
  • King's gate. The place where Mordecai sits and where the king's servants gather; the seat from which Mordecai learns of the plot against the king. Documented Chapter text, verses 19, 21
