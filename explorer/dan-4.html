The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Daniel 4 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–12; King James Version, Apocrypha for the additions)

1 Nebuchadnezzar the king, unto all people, nations, and languages, that dwell in all the earth; Peace be multiplied unto you.

2 I thought it good to shew the signs and wonders that the high God hath wrought toward me.

3 How great are his signs! and how mighty are his wonders! his kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and his dominion is from generation to generation.

4 I Nebuchadnezzar was at rest in mine house, and flourishing in my palace:

5 I saw a dream which made me afraid, and the thoughts upon my bed and the visions of my head troubled me.

6 Therefore made I a decree to bring in all the wise men of Babylon before me, that they might make known unto me the interpretation of the dream.

7 Then came in the magicians, the astrologers, the Chaldeans, and the soothsayers: and I told the dream before them; but they did not make known unto me the interpretation thereof.

8 But at the last Daniel came in before me, whose name was Belteshazzar, according to the name of my God, and in whom is the spirit of the holy gods: and before him I told the dream, saying,

9 O Belteshazzar, master of the magicians, because I know that the spirit of the holy gods is in thee, and no secret troubleth thee, tell me the visions of my dream that I have seen, and the interpretation thereof.

10 Thus were the visions of mine head in my bed; I saw, and behold a tree in the midst of the earth, and the height thereof was great.

11 The tree grew, and was strong, and the height thereof reached unto heaven, and the sight thereof to the end of all the earth:

12 The leaves thereof were fair, and the fruit thereof much, and in it was meat for all: the beasts of the field had shadow under it, and the fowls of the heaven dwelt in the boughs thereof, and all flesh was fed of it.

13 I saw in the visions of my head upon my bed, and, behold, a watcher and an holy one came down from heaven;

14 He cried aloud, and said thus, Hew down the tree, and cut off his branches, shake off his leaves, and scatter his fruit: let the beasts get away from under it, and the fowls from his branches:

15 Nevertheless leave the stump of his roots in the earth, even with a band of iron and brass, in the tender grass of the field; and let it be wet with the dew of heaven, and let his portion be with the beasts in the grass of the earth:

16 Let his heart be changed from man’s, and let a beast’s heart be given unto him; and let seven times pass over him.

17 This matter is by the decree of the watchers, and the demand by the word of the holy ones: to the intent that the living may know that the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men, and giveth it to whomsoever he will, and setteth up over it the basest of men.

18 This dream I king Nebuchadnezzar have seen. Now thou, O Belteshazzar, declare the interpretation thereof, forasmuch as all the wise men of my kingdom are not able to make known unto me the interpretation: but thou art able; for the spirit of the holy gods is in thee.

19 Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was astonied for one hour, and his thoughts troubled him. The king spake, and said, Belteshazzar, let not the dream, or the interpretation thereof, trouble thee. Belteshazzar answered and said, My lord, the dream be to them that hate thee, and the interpretation thereof to thine enemies.

20 The tree that thou sawest, which grew, and was strong, whose height reached unto the heaven, and the sight thereof to all the earth;

21 Whose leaves were fair, and the fruit thereof much, and in it was meat for all; under which the beasts of the field dwelt, and upon whose branches the fowls of the heaven had their habitation:

22 It is thou, O king, that art grown and become strong: for thy greatness is grown, and reacheth unto heaven, and thy dominion to the end of the earth.

23 And whereas the king saw a watcher and an holy one coming down from heaven, and saying, Hew the tree down, and destroy it; yet leave the stump of the roots thereof in the earth, even with a band of iron and brass, in the tender grass of the field; and let it be wet with the dew of heaven, and let his portion be with the beasts of the field, till seven times pass over him;

24 This is the interpretation, O king, and this is the decree of the most High, which is come upon my lord the king:

25 That they shall drive thee from men, and thy dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field, and they shall make thee to eat grass as oxen, and they shall wet thee with the dew of heaven, and seven times shall pass over thee, till thou know that the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men, and giveth it to whomsoever he will.

26 And whereas they commanded to leave the stump of the tree roots; thy kingdom shall be sure unto thee, after that thou shalt have known that the heavens do rule.

27 Wherefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable unto thee, and break off thy sins by righteousness, and thine iniquities by shewing mercy to the poor; if it may be a lengthening of thy tranquillity.

28 All this came upon the king Nebuchadnezzar.

29 At the end of twelve months he walked in the palace of the kingdom of Babylon.

30 The king spake, and said, Is not this great Babylon, that I have built for the house of the kingdom by the might of my power, and for the honour of my majesty?

31 While the word was in the king’s mouth, there fell a voice from heaven, saying, O king Nebuchadnezzar, to thee it is spoken; The kingdom is departed from thee.

32 And they shall drive thee from men, and thy dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field: they shall make thee to eat grass as oxen, and seven times shall pass over thee, until thou know that the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men, and giveth it to whomsoever he will.

33 The same hour was the thing fulfilled upon Nebuchadnezzar: and he was driven from men, and did eat grass as oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, till his hairs were grown like eagles’ feathers, and his nails like birds’ claws.

34 And at the end of the days I Nebuchadnezzar lifted up mine eyes unto heaven, and mine understanding returned unto me, and I blessed the most High, and I praised and honoured him that liveth for ever, whose dominion is an everlasting dominion, and his kingdom is from generation to generation:

35 And all the inhabitants of the earth are reputed as nothing: and he doeth according to his will in the army of heaven, and among the inhabitants of the earth: and none can stay his hand, or say unto him, What doest thou?

36 At the same time my reason returned unto me; and for the glory of my kingdom, mine honour and brightness returned unto me; and my counsellors and my lords sought unto me; and I was established in my kingdom, and excellent majesty was added unto me.

37 Now I Nebuchadnezzar praise and extol and honour the King of heaven, all whose works are truth, and his ways judgment: and those that walk in pride he is able to abase.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Nebuchadnezzar himself proclaims the wonders God has done for him, then tells his dream of a great tree cut down to a stump, banded with iron and brass, until seven times pass over it. Daniel interprets: the tree is the king, who will be driven from men to eat grass like oxen until he knows that the Most High rules in the kingdom of men. The dream comes true, the king is restored, and he ends by praising the King of heaven, whose works are truth and who abases those who walk in pride. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 25. That they shall drive thee from men, and thy dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field, and they shall make thee to eat grass as oxen, and they shall wet thee with the dew of heaven, and seven times shall pass over thee, till thou know that the most High rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomsoever he will. Whatever outward affliction God lays upon us, Henry says, we have cause to bear it patiently and be thankful he continues the use of our reason. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. And whereas they commanded to leave the stump of the tree roots, thy kingdom shall be sure unto thee, after that thou shalt have known that the heavens do rule. The stump preserved, Henry's reading shows, meant restoration after acknowledgment that heaven rules. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Wherefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable unto thee, and break off thy sins by righteousness, and thine iniquities by showing mercy to the poor, if it may be a lengthening of thy tranquillity. It is necessary in repentance, Henry says, not only to cease to do evil, but to learn to do good; though it might not wholly prevent the judgment, the trouble may be longer before it comes or shorter when it does. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. All this came upon the king Nebuchadnezzar. The warning was not heeded in time, Henry's account shows, and the sentence fell as spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. At the end of twelve months he walked in the palace of the kingdom of Babylon. A full year of tranquillity passed, Henry implies, before the proud word was spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. The king spoke and said: Is not this great Babylon, that I have built for the house of the kingdom by the might of my power, and for the honour of my majesty? While the proud word was in the king's mouth, Henry says, the powerful word came from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. While the word was in the king's mouth, there fell a voice from heaven, saying: O king Nebuchadnezzar, to thee it is spoken; The kingdom is departed from thee. His understanding and memory were gone, Henry says, and all the powers of the rational soul were broken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. And they shall drive thee from men, and thy dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field; they shall make thee to eat grass as oxen, and seven times shall pass over thee, until thou know that the most High rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomsoever he will. Nebuchadnezzar would be more than a man, Henry says, but God justly makes him less than a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. The same hour was the thing fulfilled upon Nebuchadnezzar, and he was driven from men, and did eat grass as oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, till his hairs were grown like eagles' feathers, and his nails like birds' claws. How careful we ought to be, Henry warns, not to do anything which may provoke God to put us out of our senses! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. And at the end of the days I Nebuchadnezzar lifted up mine eyes unto heaven, and mine understanding returned unto me, and I blessed the most High, and I praised and honoured him that lives for ever, whose dominion is an everlasting dominion, and his kingdom is from generation to generation. We may learn to believe concerning God, Henry says, that the most high God lives for ever, and his kingdom is like himself, everlasting and universal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. And all the inhabitants of the earth are reputed as nothing, and he does according to his will in the army of heaven, and among the inhabitants of the earth, and none can stay his hand, or say unto him, What doest thou? His power cannot be resisted, Henry says. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. At the same time my reason returned unto me, and for the glory of my kingdom, mine honour and brightness returned unto me, and my counsellors and my lords sought unto me, and I was established in my kingdom, and excellent majesty was added unto me. When men are brought to honour God by confession of sin and acknowledging his sovereignty, Henry says, then, and not till then, they may expect God will honour them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. Now I Nebuchadnezzar praise and extol and honour the King of heaven, all whose works are truth, and his ways judgment, and those that walk in pride he is able to abase. There can be no reasonable doubt, Henry closes, that Nebuchadnezzar was a true penitent and an accepted believer; the Lord knows how to abase those that walk in pride, but gives grace to the humble, broken-hearted sinner who calls upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

That they shall drive thee from men, and thy dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field, and they shall make thee to eat grass as oxen, and they shall wet thee with the dew of heaven, and seven times shall pass over thee, till thou know that the most High rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomsoever he will. Whatever outward affliction God lays upon us, Henry says, we have cause to bear it patiently and be thankful he continues the use of our reason. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. And whereas they commanded to leave the stump of the tree roots, thy kingdom shall be sure unto thee, after that thou shalt have known that the heavens do rule. The stump preserved, Henry's reading shows, meant restoration after acknowledgment that heaven rules. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Wherefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable unto thee, and break off thy sins by righteousness, and thine iniquities by showing mercy to the poor, if it may be a lengthening of thy tranquillity. It is necessary in repentance, Henry says, not only to cease to do evil, but to learn to do good; though it might not wholly prevent the judgment, the trouble may be longer before it comes or shorter when it does. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. All this came upon the king Nebuchadnezzar. The warning was not heeded in time, Henry's account shows, and the sentence fell as spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. At the end of twelve months he walked in the palace of the kingdom of Babylon. A full year of tranquillity passed, Henry implies, before the proud word was spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. The king spoke and said: Is not this great Babylon, that I have built for the house of the kingdom by the might of my power, and for the honour of my majesty? While the proud word was in the king's mouth, Henry says, the powerful word came from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. While the word was in the king's mouth, there fell a voice from heaven, saying: O king Nebuchadnezzar, to thee it is spoken; The kingdom is departed from thee. His understanding and memory were gone, Henry says, and all the powers of the rational soul were broken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. And they shall drive thee from men, and thy dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field; they shall make thee to eat grass as oxen, and seven times shall pass over thee, until thou know that the most High rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomsoever he will. Nebuchadnezzar would be more than a man, Henry says, but God justly makes him less than a man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. The same hour was the thing fulfilled upon Nebuchadnezzar, and he was driven from men, and did eat grass as oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, till his hairs were grown like eagles' feathers, and his nails like birds' claws. How careful we ought to be, Henry warns, not to do anything which may provoke God to put us out of our senses! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. And at the end of the days I Nebuchadnezzar lifted up mine eyes unto heaven, and mine understanding returned unto me, and I blessed the most High, and I praised and honoured him that lives for ever, whose dominion is an everlasting dominion, and his kingdom is from generation to generation. We may learn to believe concerning God, Henry says, that the most high God lives for ever, and his kingdom is like himself, everlasting and universal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. And all the inhabitants of the earth are reputed as nothing, and he does according to his will in the army of heaven, and among the inhabitants of the earth, and none can stay his hand, or say unto him, What doest thou? His power cannot be resisted, Henry says. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. At the same time my reason returned unto me, and for the glory of my kingdom, mine honour and brightness returned unto me, and my counsellors and my lords sought unto me, and I was established in my kingdom, and excellent majesty was added unto me. When men are brought to honour God by confession of sin and acknowledging his sovereignty, Henry says, then, and not till then, they may expect God will honour them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. Now I Nebuchadnezzar praise and extol and honour the King of heaven, all whose works are truth, and his ways judgment, and those that walk in pride he is able to abase. There can be no reasonable doubt, Henry closes, that Nebuchadnezzar was a true penitent and an accepted believer; the Lord knows how to abase those that walk in pride, but gives grace to the humble, broken-hearted sinner who calls upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
