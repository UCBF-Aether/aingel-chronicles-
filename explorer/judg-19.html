The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Judges 19 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass in those days, when there was no king in Israel, that there was a certain Levite sojourning on the side of mount Ephraim, who took to him a concubine out of Bethlehemjudah.

2 And his concubine played the whore against him, and went away from him unto her father’s house to Bethlehemjudah, and was there four whole months.

3 And her husband arose, and went after her, to speak friendly unto her, and to bring her again, having his servant with him, and a couple of asses: and she brought him into her father’s house: and when the father of the damsel saw him, he rejoiced to meet him.

4 And his father in law, the damsel’s father, retained him; and he abode with him three days: so they did eat and drink, and lodged there.

5 And it came to pass on the fourth day, when they arose early in the morning, that he rose up to depart: and the damsel’s father said unto his son in law, Comfort thine heart with a morsel of bread, and afterward go your way.

6 And they sat down, and did eat and drink both of them together: for the damsel’s father had said unto the man, Be content, I pray thee, and tarry all night, and let thine heart be merry.

7 And when the man rose up to depart, his father in law urged him: therefore he lodged there again.

8 And he arose early in the morning on the fifth day to depart; and the damsel’s father said, Comfort thine heart, I pray thee. And they tarried until afternoon, and they did eat both of them.

9 And when the man rose up to depart, he, and his concubine, and his servant, his father in law, the damsel’s father, said unto him, Behold, now the day draweth toward evening, I pray you tarry all night: behold, the day groweth to an end, lodge here, that thine heart may be merry; and to morrow get you early on your way, that thou mayest go home.

10 But the man would not tarry that night, but he rose up and departed, and came over against Jebus, which is Jerusalem; and there were with him two asses saddled, his concubine also was with him.

11 And when they were by Jebus, the day was far spent; and the servant said unto his master, Come, I pray thee, and let us turn in into this city of the Jebusites, and lodge in it.

12 And his master said unto him, We will not turn aside hither into the city of a stranger, that is not of the children of Israel; we will pass over to Gibeah.

13 And he said unto his servant, Come, and let us draw near to one of these places to lodge all night, in Gibeah, or in Ramah.

14 And they passed on and went their way; and the sun went down upon them when they were by Gibeah, which belongeth to Benjamin.

15 And they turned aside thither, to go in and to lodge in Gibeah: and when he went in, he sat him down in a street of the city: for there was no man that took them into his house to lodging.

16 And, behold, there came an old man from his work out of the field at even, which was also of mount Ephraim; and he sojourned in Gibeah: but the men of the place were Benjamites.

17 And when he had lifted up his eyes, he saw a wayfaring man in the street of the city: and the old man said, Whither goest thou? and whence comest thou?

18 And he said unto him, We are passing from Bethlehemjudah toward the side of mount Ephraim; from thence am I: and I went to Bethlehemjudah, but I am now going to the house of the LORD; and there is no man that receiveth me to house.

19 Yet there is both straw and provender for our asses; and there is bread and wine also for me, and for thy handmaid, and for the young man which is with thy servants: there is no want of any thing.

20 And the old man said, Peace be with thee; howsoever let all thy wants lie upon me; only lodge not in the street.

21 So he brought him into his house, and gave provender unto the asses: and they washed their feet, and did eat and drink.

22 Now as they were making their hearts merry, behold, the men of the city, certain sons of Belial, beset the house round about, and beat at the door, and spake to the master of the house, the old man, saying, Bring forth the man that came into thine house, that we may know him.

23 And the man, the master of the house, went out unto them, and said unto them, Nay, my brethren, nay, I pray you, do not so wickedly; seeing that this man is come into mine house, do not this folly.

24 Behold, here is my daughter a maiden, and his concubine; them I will bring out now, and humble ye them, and do with them what seemeth good unto you: but unto this man do not so vile a thing.

25 But the men would not hearken to him: so the man took his concubine, and brought her forth unto them; and they knew her, and abused her all the night until the morning: and when the day began to spring, they let her go.

26 Then came the woman in the dawning of the day, and fell down at the door of the man’s house where her lord was, till it was light.

27 And her lord rose up in the morning, and opened the doors of the house, and went out to go his way: and, behold, the woman his concubine was fallen down at the door of the house, and her hands were upon the threshold.

28 And he said unto her, Up, and let us be going. But none answered. Then the man took her up upon an ass, and the man rose up, and gat him unto his place.

29 And when he was come into his house, he took a knife, and laid hold on his concubine, and divided her, together with her bones, into twelve pieces, and sent her into all the coasts of Israel.

30 And it was so, that all that saw it said, There was no such deed done nor seen from the day that the children of Israel came up out of the land of Egypt unto this day: consider of it, take advice, and speak your minds.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A Levite sojourning in Mount Ephraim takes a concubine from Bethlehem-judah; she leaves him, he follows to speak friendly to her, and his father-in-law's hospitality delays their departure day after day until late on the fifth day. Refusing shelter in Jebus, a city of strangers, they press on to Gibeah of Benjamin, where no man takes them in and an old Ephraimite sojourner houses them. That night men of Belial beset the house, the concubine is abused all night and found dead at the threshold in the morning, and her lord divides her into twelve pieces and sends them through all the coasts of Israel, with the summons to consider, take advice and speak. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In those days, when there was no king in Israel, a Levite sojourning on the side of Mount Ephraim took a concubine out of Bethlehemjudah, and JFB notes this episode belongs to the same early period of anarchy and disorder, and that a concubine wife, though a secondary relationship, possessed the true essence of marriage and was lawful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. His concubine went away from him to her father's house at Bethlehemjudah and was there four whole months, and JFB notes other versions, with Josephus, represent the flight as disgust at frequent brawls, rather than the cause assigned in the translation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Her husband arose and went after her to speak friendly to her, in Hebrew to speak to her heart, and bring her again, and JFB notes the father rejoiced to meet him, hoping for complete reconciliation, and the Levite yielded to hospitable importunities and prolonged his stay for days. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Her father retained him, and they ate and drank and lodged three days, the reconciliation sealed by days of hospitality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. On the fourth day they arose early, and the damsel's father said to his son-in-law, Comfort thine heart with a morsel of bread, and afterward go your way, hospitality again delaying departure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They sat down, ate and drank together, and the father said, Be content and tarry all night, so that the morning's departure became another night's stay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. When the man rose to depart, his father-in-law urged him again to tarry, and he lodged there again that night, the pattern of detention repeating. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. On the fifth day he arose early to depart, but the father said, Comfort thine heart, and tarry until afternoon, and JFB notes he tarried with reluctance until the decline of day, since Eastern breakfast, taken late, was purposely protracted to argue for a further stay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The man rose to depart, but the father said the day drew toward evening and tarry all night, and JFB notes the pitching time of day was when travellers usually halted, so it was too late to begin a journey, though duty perhaps obliged the Levite to delay no further. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. The man would not tarry that night, but departed with his concubine, servant, and two asses, and came over against Jebus, which is Jerusalem, and JFB notes the Levite resisted his servant's advice to enter Jebus, still in old inhabitants' hands, preferring to press on to Gibeah, occupied by Israelites, though the event showed it would have been better to trust aliens than his own countrymen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. When they were by Jebus the day was far spent, and the servant said, Let us turn into this city of the Jebusites and lodge in it, proposing the nearer shelter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. His master answered they would not turn aside to a city of strangers not of Israel, but pass over to Gibeah, choosing Israelite lodging over alien safety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. He said they would lodge in Gibeah or Ramah, Gibeah five miles northeast of Jerusalem and Ramah four or five miles north, as JFB locates them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. They passed on, and the sun went down upon them when they were by Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin, so that night found them at the very place the Levite had chosen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They turned aside to lodge in Gibeah, and when he went in he sat down in a street of the city, and no man took them into his house, and JFB notes Palestine towns then had no inns, and travellers without introduction spread bedding in the streets, while in Gibeah even the usual urgent invitation failed, owing to the people's bad character. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. An old man came from his work in the field at evening, himself of Mount Ephraim and sojourning in Gibeah, and JFB notes his hospitality was perhaps quickened by learning the stranger's occupation and his return to duties at Shiloh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The old man saw the wayfaring man in the street and asked whence he came and whither he went, breaking the city's silence with a question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The Levite answered that they were passing from Bethlehemjudah toward Mount Ephraim, whence he was, and had been to Bethlehemjudah, and were going to the house of the Lord, but no man received them into his house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. He added that they had straw and provender for their asses and bread and wine for himself, the maid, and the young man, and lacked nothing, and JFB notes Eastern travellers always carried provisions, since even khans afforded only rest and shelter, and used chopped straw with barley or beans for provender. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The old man said, Peace be with thee, let all thy wants lie upon me, only lodge not in the street, and JFB notes his earnest dissuasive arose from acquaintance with the infamous practices of the place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. He brought them into his house, gave provender to the asses, washed their feet, and they ate and drank, so that the Levite's own provisions were kept for emergencies while the old man's hospitality supplied the night. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. As they were making their hearts merry, certain sons of Belial beset the house and beat at the door, demanding the man be brought out that they might know him, and JFB notes the narrative discloses a morality that would seem incredible if not on sacred testimony, and that both men ought to have protected the women at the expense of their lives, though the guilt is not fastened on Gibeah's general population. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The old man went out and pleaded, Nay, my brethren, do not so wickedly, seeing this man is come into mine house, do not this folly, appealing to hospitality against the demand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. He offered his virgin daughter and the man's concubine, saying, humble them and do with them what seemeth good, but unto this man do not so vile a thing, a proposal JFB's note on the whole outrage leaves standing as part of the horror, not as a justification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. But the men would not hearken, so the Levite took his concubine and brought her out to them, and they knew her and abused her all night until morning, and let her go at daybreak. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Then the woman came in the dawning of the day and fell down at the door of the man's house where her lord was, till it was light, and died there at the threshold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Her lord rose in the morning, opened the doors, and went out to go his way, and behold the woman was fallen at the door with her hands upon the threshold. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. He said, Up, and let us be going, but none answered, so he took her up upon the ass and rose to his place, carrying the dead woman home in silence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. When he came to his house he took a knife, laid hold on his concubine, and divided her into twelve pieces with her bones, and sent her into all the coasts of Israel, and JFB notes the want of regular government warranted this extraordinary step, and no method could more certainly rouse universal horror and indignation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. He sent the pieces throughout Israel, and all who saw said there had been no such deed since Israel came up from Egypt, bidding all Israel consider, take advice, and speak, so that the summons itself became the nation's call to judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
