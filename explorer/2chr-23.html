The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And in the seventh year Jehoiada strengthened himself, and took the captains of hundreds, Azariah the son of Jeroham, and Ishmael the son of Jehohanan, and Azariah the son of Obed, and Maaseiah the son of Adaiah, and Elishaphat the son of Zichri, into covenant with him.

2 And they went about in Judah, and gathered the Levites out of all the cities of Judah, and the chief of the fathers of Israel, and they came to Jerusalem.

3 And all the congregation made a covenant with the king in the house of God. And he said unto them, Behold, the king’s son shall reign, as the LORD hath said of the sons of David.

4 This is the thing that ye shall do; A third part of you entering on the sabbath, of the priests and of the Levites, shall be porters of the doors;

5 And a third part shall be at the king’s house; and a third part at the gate of the foundation: and all the people shall be in the courts of the house of the LORD.

6 But let none come into the house of the LORD, save the priests, and they that minister of the Levites; they shall go in, for they are holy: but all the people shall keep the watch of the LORD.

7 And the Levites shall compass the king round about, every man with his weapons in his hand; and whosoever else cometh into the house, he shall be put to death: but be ye with the king when he cometh in, and when he goeth out.

8 So the Levites and all Judah did according to all things that Jehoiada the priest had commanded, and took every man his men that were to come in on the sabbath, with them that were to go out on the sabbath: for Jehoiada the priest dismissed not the courses.

9 Moreover Jehoiada the priest delivered to the captains of hundreds spears, and bucklers, and shields, that had been king David’s, which were in the house of God.

10 And he set all the people, every man having his weapon in his hand, from the right side of the temple to the left side of the temple, along by the altar and the temple, by the king round about.

11 Then they brought out the king’s son, and put upon him the crown, and gave him the testimony, and made him king. And Jehoiada and his sons anointed him, and said, God save the king.

12 Now when Athaliah heard the noise of the people running and praising the king, she came to the people into the house of the LORD:

13 And she looked, and, behold, the king stood at his pillar at the entering in, and the princes and the trumpets by the king: and all the people of the land rejoiced, and sounded with trumpets, also the singers with instruments of musick, and such as taught to sing praise. Then Athaliah rent her clothes, and said, Treason, Treason.

14 Then Jehoiada the priest brought out the captains of hundreds that were set over the host, and said unto them, Have her forth of the ranges: and whoso followeth her, let him be slain with the sword. For the priest said, Slay her not in the house of the LORD.

15 So they laid hands on her; and when she was come to the entering of the horse gate by the king’s house, they slew her there.

16 And Jehoiada made a covenant between him, and between all the people, and between the king, that they should be the LORD’s people.

17 Then all the people went to the house of Baal, and brake it down, and brake his altars and his images in pieces, and slew Mattan the priest of Baal before the altars.

18 Also Jehoiada appointed the offices of the house of the LORD by the hand of the priests the Levites, whom David had distributed in the house of the LORD, to offer the burnt offerings of the LORD, as it is written in the law of Moses, with rejoicing and with singing, as it was ordained by David.

19 And he set the porters at the gates of the house of the LORD, that none which was unclean in any thing should enter in.

20 And he took the captains of hundreds, and the nobles, and the governors of the people, and all the people of the land, and brought down the king from the house of the LORD: and they came through the high gate into the king’s house, and set the king upon the throne of the kingdom.

21 And all the people of the land rejoiced: and the city was quiet, after that they had slain Athaliah with the sword.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells Jehoiada the priest's rising in the seventh year, the crowning of the boy Joash, the slaying of Athaliah, and the covenant renewed between God, king, and people. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And in the seventh year Jehoiada strengthened himself, and took the captains of hundreds, Azariah son of Jeroham, Ishmael son of Jehohanan, Azariah son of Obed, Maaseiah son of Adaiah, and Elishaphat son of Zichri, into covenant with him. Henry notes Jehoiada was a man of great prudence, reserving the prince till fit to appear and the nation weary of the usurper, preparing beforehand and effecting it with admirable secrecy and expedition, and a man of great interest, for captains joined him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And they went about in Judah, and gathered the Levites out of all Judah's cities, and the chief of the fathers of Israel, and they came to Jerusalem. Henry notes Levites and chief fathers came at Jehoiada's call, ready to receive orders, showing what command wisdom and virtue give men, and all entrusted with the secret kept counsel till execution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And all the congregation made a covenant with the king in the house of God. And he said unto them, Behold, the king's son shall reign, as the Lord hath said of the sons of David. Henry notes Jehoiada was a man of great faith: not common equity or his wife's relation moved him, but regard to God's word and the divine entail of the crown, for the king's son shall reign, must reign, as the Lord hath said. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. This is the thing that ye shall do: A third part of you entering on the sabbath, of the priests and Levites, shall be porters of the doors. Henry notes Jehoiada prepared the people, armed them and appointed their posts, dividing the priests and Levites entering on the sabbath among the doors, the king's house and the foundation gate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And a third part shall be at the king's house, and a third part at the gate of the foundation, and all the people shall be in the courts of the house of the Lord. Henry's posting account applies: a third at the king's house, a third at the foundation gate, and the people in the temple courts, each in his appointed post. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. But let none come into the house of the Lord, save the priests, and they that minister of the Levites; they shall go in, for they are holy, but all the people shall keep the watch of the Lord. Henry notes Jehoiada was a man of great religion: though the matter was done in the temple, he ordered none but holy priests and ministering Levites to enter, on pain of death, for sacred things must never be profaned, not even for civil rights. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And the Levites shall compass the king round about, every man with his weapons in his hand, and whosoever else cometh into the house, he shall be put to death, but be ye with the king when he cometh in, and when he goeth out. Henry notes the Levites were to compass the king armed, guarding his coming in and going out, with death for intruders, securing the young king in the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. So the Levites and all Judah did according to all things Jehoiada commanded, and took every man his men that were to come in on the sabbath, with them that were to go out on the sabbath, for Jehoiada dismissed not the courses. Henry notes Levites and all Judah did as Jehoiada commanded, and strangely, all entrusted with the secret kept their counsel, for the words of the wise are heard in quiet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Moreover Jehoiada delivered to the captains of hundreds spears, bucklers and shields, that had been King David's, which were in the house of God. Henry notes Jehoiada armed the captains with David's spears, bucklers and shields kept in God's house, fitting the restoration with David's own weapons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And he set all the people, every man having his weapon in hand, from the right side of the temple to the left side, along by the altar and the temple, by the king round about. Henry notes Jehoiada appointed the people their posts, armed and set round the temple and altar, compassing the king on every side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Then they brought out the king's son, and put upon him the crown, and gave him the testimony, and made him king. And Jehoiada and his sons anointed him, and said, God save the king. Henry notes Jehoiada was a man of great resolution: having undertaken the business he went through with it, brought out the king, crowned him and gave him the testimony, venturing his head in a good cause, and his sons joined in anointing, one likely being Zechariah whom Joash later slew for reproving him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Now when Athaliah heard the noise of the people running and praising the king, she came to the people into the house of the Lord. Henry notes Athaliah, imagining her interest better than it was, ventured into the Lord's house at the noise of praise, running upon the sword of justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And she looked, and behold, the king stood at his pillar at the entering in, and the princes and trumpets by the king, and all the people rejoiced, and sounded with trumpets, also singers with instruments, and such as taught to sing praise. Then Athaliah rent her clothes, and said, Treason, Treason. Henry notes the people were pleased: the king stood at his pillar by right, and all rejoiced to see a rod sprung from Jesse's stem, praising king and God, while Athaliah, seconded by none, cried treason, deceived by pride. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Then Jehoiada brought out the captains of hundreds set over the host, and said unto them, Have her forth of the ranges, and whoso followeth her, let him be slain with the sword. For the priest said, Slay her not in the house of the Lord. Henry notes Jehoiada, as protector in the king's minority, ordered her slain, but care was taken she should not be slain in the Lord's house, lest that sacred place be disgraced or that wicked woman honoured. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. So they laid hands on her, and when she was come to the entering of the horse gate by the king's house, they slew her there. Henry notes the order was done immediately at the horse gate, outside the temple, ending six years of bloody tyranny. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And Jehoiada made a covenant between him, and between all the people, and between the king, that they should be the Lord's people. Henry notes the original contract was tripartite: Jehoiada as God's priest and mediator, the people and the king, with the true intent that they should be the Lord's people, God covenanting to take them, king and people covenanting to be his, and king and people covenanting with each other in God's fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Then all the people went to the house of Baal, and brake it down, and brake his altars and images in pieces, and slew Mattan the priest of Baal before the altars. Henry notes they would not have done half their work if they had only destroyed the usurper of the king's right and not of God's right: Baal's house, altars and images must come down, and his priest slain, for God commanded seducers to idolatry be put to death, since Baal's worship was Athaliah's reign's greatest grievance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Also Jehoiada appointed the offices of the house of the Lord by the hand of the priests the Levites, whom David had distributed in the house of the Lord, to offer burnt offerings as written in Moses' law, with rejoicing and singing, as ordained by David. Henry notes Jehoiada restored temple offices to proper courses and hands: priests for sacrifices according to Moses, singers according to David, offered with rejoicing and singing, for we joy in God when we receive atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And he set the porters at the gates of the house of the Lord, that none which was unclean in anything should enter in. Henry notes porters were put in their posts as David ordered, to take care that none ceremonially unclean be admitted into the temple courts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And he took the captains of hundreds, and nobles, and governors, and all the people, and brought down the king from the house of the Lord, and they came through the high gate into the king's house, and set the king upon the throne of the kingdom. Henry notes civil government was re-established: the king was brought in state to his palace and set on the throne, to give law and judgment in person or by Jehoiada his tutor. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And all the people of the land rejoiced, and the city was quiet, after they had slain Athaliah with the sword. Henry notes the generality rejoiced and the rest were quiet with no opposition, for when the Son of David is enthroned in the soul all is quiet and springs of joy are opened. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
