The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Zechariah 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass in the fourth year of king Darius, that the word of the LORD came unto Zechariah in the fourth day of the ninth month, even in Chisleu;

2 When they had sent unto the house of God Sherezer and Regemmelech, and their men, to pray before the LORD,

3 And to speak unto the priests which were in the house of the LORD of hosts, and to the prophets, saying, Should I weep in the fifth month, separating myself, as I have done these so many years?

4 Then came the word of the LORD of hosts unto me, saying,

5 Speak unto all the people of the land, and to the priests, saying, When ye fasted and mourned in the fifth and seventh month, even those seventy years, did ye at all fast unto me, even to me?

6 And when ye did eat, and when ye did drink, did not ye eat for yourselves, and drink for yourselves?

7 Should ye not hear the words which the LORD hath cried by the former prophets, when Jerusalem was inhabited and in prosperity, and the cities thereof round about her, when men inhabited the south and the plain?

8 And the word of the LORD came unto Zechariah, saying,

9 Thus speaketh the LORD of hosts, saying, Execute true judgment, and shew mercy and compassions every man to his brother:

10 And oppress not the widow, nor the fatherless, the stranger, nor the poor; and let none of you imagine evil against his brother in your heart.

11 But they refused to hearken, and pulled away the shoulder, and stopped their ears, that they should not hear.

12 Yea, they made their hearts as an adamant stone, lest they should hear the law, and the words which the LORD of hosts hath sent in his spirit by the former prophets: therefore came a great wrath from the LORD of hosts.

13 Therefore it is come to pass, that as he cried, and they would not hear; so they cried, and I would not hear, saith the LORD of hosts:

14 But I scattered them with a whirlwind among all the nations whom they knew not. Thus the land was desolate after them, that no man passed through nor returned: for they laid the pleasant land desolate.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In the fourth year of Darius, men sent to the house of God ask the priests and prophets whether they should still weep in the fifth month, as they have done for so many years. God's answer goes behind the question: when you fasted and mourned in the fifth and seventh months for seventy years, did you fast at all unto me, and when you ate and drank, did you not eat and drink for yourselves? The former prophets had cried for true judgment, mercy, and compassion, and for not oppressing widow, fatherless, stranger, or poor, but the fathers stopped their ears and made their hearts as adamant stone, so they were scattered with a whirlwind among nations they did not know and the pleasant land was left desolate. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And it came to pass in the fourth year of king Darius, that the word of the Lord came unto Zechariah in the fourth day of the ninth month, even in Chisleu, because two years after the visions, a dated question arrives about the fasts, once the temple is rising again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. When they had sent unto the house of God Sherezer and Regemmelech, and their men, to pray before the Lord, because the men of Bethel send a deputation to pray before the Lord, seeking an answer about their fasting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And to speak unto the priests which were in the house of the Lord of hosts, and to the prophets, saying, should I weep in the fifth month, separating myself, as I have done these so many years, because the question put to priests and prophets is whether the fast of the fifth month, kept through the captivity, should continue now that the temple is being rebuilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Then came the word of the Lord of hosts unto me, saying, because the answer comes not from the priests but from the Lord of hosts himself, turning the question back upon the questioners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Speak unto all the people of the land, and to the priests, saying, when ye fasted and mourned in the fifth and seventh month, even those seventy years, did ye at all fast unto me, even to me, because God asks whether seventy years of fasting were ever fasting unto him, or only mourning for their own losses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And when ye did eat, and when ye did drink, did not ye eat for yourselves, and drink for yourselves, because the feasting was as self-serving as the fasting, both done for themselves rather than for God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Should ye not hear the words which the Lord has cried by the former prophets, when Jerusalem was inhabited and in prosperity, and the cities thereof round about her, when men inhabited the south and the plain, because the former prophets had already answered this question in days of prosperity, before Jerusalem fell. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. And the word of the Lord came unto Zechariah, saying, because a second word follows, giving the substance of what the former prophets cried. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Thus speaks the Lord of hosts, saying, execute true judgment, and shew mercy and compassions every man to his brother, because the required religion is summed up in true judgment between men, with mercy and compassion toward every brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. And oppress not the widow, nor the fatherless, the stranger, nor the poor, and let none of you imagine evil against his brother in your heart, because the four defenceless classes are named, widow, fatherless, stranger and poor, and even imagined evil against a brother is forbidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. But they refused to hearken, and pulled away the shoulder, and stopped their ears, that they should not hear, because the fathers refused like a stubborn ox pulling away the shoulder from the yoke, and stopped their ears against the word. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Yea, they made their hearts as an adamant stone, lest they should hear the law, and the words which the Lord of hosts has sent in his spirit by the former prophets, therefore came a great wrath from the Lord of hosts, because hearts made like adamant against law and prophets brought great wrath from the Lord of hosts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Therefore it is come to pass, that as he cried, and they would not hear, so they cried, and I would not hear, says the Lord of hosts, because the retribution is exact, that as God cried and was not heard, so they cried and were not heard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. But I scattered them with a whirlwind among all the nations whom they knew not, thus the land was desolate after them, that no man passed through nor returned, for they laid the pleasant land desolate, because the scattering was like a whirlwind among unknown nations, and the pleasant land was left desolate, with none passing through or returning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Fasts of the fifth and seventh months. fasts kept through the seventy years of exile, which Zechariah 7 questions: were they ever fasting unto God, or only mourning for the people's own losses?
