The Text — Psalms 147 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Praise ye the LORD: for it is good to sing praises unto our God; for it is pleasant; and praise is comely.

2 The LORD doth build up Jerusalem: he gathereth together the outcasts of Israel.

3 He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.

4 He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names.

5 Great is our Lord, and of great power: his understanding is infinite.

6 The LORD lifteth up the meek: he casteth the wicked down to the ground.

7 Sing unto the LORD with thanksgiving; sing praise upon the harp unto our God:

8 Who covereth the heaven with clouds, who prepareth rain for the earth, who maketh grass to grow upon the mountains.

9 He giveth to the beast his food, and to the young ravens which cry.

10 He delighteth not in the strength of the horse: he taketh not pleasure in the legs of a man.

11 The LORD taketh pleasure in them that fear him, in those that hope in his mercy.

12 Praise the LORD, O Jerusalem; praise thy God, O Zion.

13 For he hath strengthened the bars of thy gates; he hath blessed thy children within thee.

14 He maketh peace in thy borders, and filleth thee with the finest of the wheat.

15 He sendeth forth his commandment upon earth: his word runneth very swiftly.

16 He giveth snow like wool: he scattereth the hoarfrost like ashes.

17 He casteth forth his ice like morsels: who can stand before his cold?

18 He sendeth out his word, and melteth them: he causeth his wind to blow, and the waters flow.

19 He sheweth his word unto Jacob, his statutes and his judgments unto Israel.

20 He hath not dealt so with any nation: and as for his judgments, they have not known them. Praise ye the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A praise psalm in three movements, each returning to Jerusalem and to God's care. First, it is good, pleasant and comely to sing praises, because the LORD builds up Jerusalem, gathers the outcasts of Israel, heals the broken in heart and binds their wounds, tells the number of the stars and calls them all by name — great in power, infinite in understanding — lifting the meek and casting the wicked to the ground. Second, thanksgiving for providence: he covers heaven with clouds, prepares rain, makes grass grow on mountains, gives beasts their food and young ravens their cry, delighting not in horse strength or man's legs but in those who fear him and hope in his mercy. Third, praise from Jerusalem and Zion, whose gates he has strengthened and children blessed, whose borders he makes peaceful and fills with the finest wheat; his commandment runs swiftly over the earth, giving snow like wool, scattering hoarfrost like ashes, casting ice like morsels, then sending his word to melt them, wind to blow and waters to flow. The close is particular: he shows his word to Jacob, his statutes and judgments to Israel, and has not dealt so with any nation.
