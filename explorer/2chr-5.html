The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Thus all the work that Solomon made for the house of the LORD was finished: and Solomon brought in all the things that David his father had dedicated; and the silver, and the gold, and all the instruments, put he among the treasures of the house of God.

2 Then Solomon assembled the elders of Israel, and all the heads of the tribes, the chief of the fathers of the children of Israel, unto Jerusalem, to bring up the ark of the covenant of the LORD out of the city of David, which is Zion.

3 Wherefore all the men of Israel assembled themselves unto the king in the feast which was in the seventh month.

4 And all the elders of Israel came; and the Levites took up the ark.

5 And they brought up the ark, and the tabernacle of the congregation, and all the holy vessels that were in the tabernacle, these did the priests and the Levites bring up.

6 Also king Solomon, and all the congregation of Israel that were assembled unto him before the ark, sacrificed sheep and oxen, which could not be told nor numbered for multitude.

7 And the priests brought in the ark of the covenant of the LORD unto his place, to the oracle of the house, into the most holy place, even under the wings of the cherubims:

8 For the cherubims spread forth their wings over the place of the ark, and the cherubims covered the ark and the staves thereof above.

9 And they drew out the staves of the ark, that the ends of the staves were seen from the ark before the oracle; but they were not seen without. And there it is unto this day.

10 There was nothing in the ark save the two tables which Moses put therein at Horeb, when the LORD made a covenant with the children of Israel, when they came out of Egypt.

11 And it came to pass, when the priests were come out of the holy place: (for all the priests that were present were sanctified, and did not then wait by course:

12 Also the Levites which were the singers, all of them of Asaph, of Heman, of Jeduthun, with their sons and their brethren, being arrayed in white linen, having cymbals and psalteries and harps, stood at the east end of the altar, and with them an hundred and twenty priests sounding with trumpets:)

13 It came even to pass, as the trumpeters and singers were as one, to make one sound to be heard in praising and thanking the LORD; and when they lifted up their voice with the trumpets and cymbals and instruments of musick, and praised the LORD, saying, For he is good; for his mercy endureth for ever: that then the house was filled with a cloud, even the house of the LORD;

14 So that the priests could not stand to minister by reason of the cloud: for the glory of the LORD had filled the house of God.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the bringing of the ark into the temple, the Levites' music, and the cloud of God's glory filling the house so that the priests could not stand to minister. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Thus all the work Solomon made for the house of the Lord was finished, and he brought in all the things David his father had dedicated, silver, gold and instruments, among the treasures of the house of God. Henry notes Solomon was just both to God and to his father: dedicated things must not be alienated, for it is sacrilege to do so, and the temple sanctified the gold, not the reverse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Then Solomon assembled the elders of Israel, all heads of tribes and chief of the fathers, to Jerusalem, to bring up the ark of the covenant from the city of David, which is Zion. Henry notes the ark was brought in attended by a very great assembly, to grace the solemnity with a sumptuous appearance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Wherefore all the men of Israel assembled to the king in the feast of the seventh month. Henry notes this was done with great joy, keeping a holy feast on the occasion, for the establishment of public worship with tokens of God's presence ought to be matter of great joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And all the elders of Israel came, and the Levites took up the ark. Henry notes the ark was carried by the priests and Levites in solemn procession, attended by the elders who came to grace the occasion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And they brought up the ark, and the tabernacle of the congregation, and all the holy vessels in the tabernacle, which the priests and Levites brought up. Henry notes these were not alienated, altered or melted down, though there was no need of them, but carefully laid up as monuments of antiquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Also King Solomon and all the congregation assembled before the ark sacrificed sheep and oxen which could not be told nor numbered for multitude. Henry notes that whatever comfort we have, we must by the sacrifice of praise give God the glory, and not be straitened therein, for with such sacrifices God is well pleased. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And the priests brought the ark into its place, to the oracle of the house, into the most holy place, even under the wings of the cherubim. Henry notes the ark, with mercy-seat and cherubim, was the same as in the tabernacle, for God's presence and grace are the same in little assemblies as in large ones. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. For the cherubim spread forth their wings over the place of the ark, and covered the ark and its staves above. Henry notes the ark was put under the wings of the great cherubim Solomon had set up there, the token of God's presence overshadowed in the holy place. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And they drew out the staves, so that their ends were seen from the ark before the oracle, but not seen without; and there it is unto this day. Henry notes this phrase marks the day of the source from which the story was transcribed, not the post-captivity day of the book's writing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. There was nothing in the ark save the two tables Moses put therein at Horeb, when the Lord made a covenant with Israel coming out of Egypt. Henry's ark typology applies: the ark was a type of Christ and token of God's presence, and at its heart lay the covenant law, the promise that God is with his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And it came to pass, when the priests were come out of the holy place, for all the priests present were sanctified and did not then wait by course. Henry notes the whole family of priests attended, not one course only, because there was work enough for all, and all should be eye-witnesses of the solemnity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Also the Levite singers, of Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun, with sons and brethren, arrayed in white linen, with cymbals, psalteries and harps, stood at the east end of the altar, with a hundred and twenty priests sounding trumpets. Henry notes all three families of singers and musicians were employed, with 120 trumpet priests completing the concert at the east end toward the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. It came to pass, as trumpeters and singers were as one, to make one sound praising and thanking the Lord, saying, For he is good, for his mercy endureth for ever, that the house was filled with a cloud. Henry notes it was not at the sacrifices but at the praises that God gave this token, for the sacrifice of praise pleases the Lord better than an ox, and it came when they were unanimous, fervent, and celebrating God's everlasting mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. So the priests could not stand to minister by reason of the cloud, for the glory of the Lord had filled the house of God. Henry notes the cloud signified God's acceptance and his condescension to human weakness, and also intimated that the Levitical priesthood, made of men with infirmity, should cease when the Messiah came, in whom the fullness of God dwelt bodily. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • oracle. The most holy place itself: verse 7 names the ark’s place as “the oracle of the house, into the most holy place.” Documented Chapter text, verse 7
