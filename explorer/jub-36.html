Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 36 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the sixth year of this week Isaac called his two sons, Esau and Jacob, and they came to him, and he said unto them : " My sons, I am going the way of my fathers, to the eternal house 2 where my fathers are.

2 Wherefore bury me near Abraham my father, in the double cave in the field of Ephron the Ilittite, where Abraham purchased a sepulchre to bury in ; in the sepulchre which I digged for myself, there bury me.

3 And this I command you, my sons, that ye practise righteousness and uprightness on the earth, so that the Lord may bring upon you all that the Lord said that he would do to Abraham and to his seed.

4 And love one another, my sons, your brothers as a man who loveth his own soul, and let each seek in what he may benefit his brother, and act together on the earth ; and let them love each other as their own souls.

5 And concerning the question of idols, I command and admonish you to reject them and hate them, and love them not; for they are full of deception for those that worship them and for those that bow down to them.

6 Remember ye, my sons, the Lord God of Abraham your father, and how I too worshipped Him and served Him in righteousness and in joy, that He might multiply you and increase your seed as the stars of heaven in multitude, and establish you on the earth as the plant of righteousness 4 which will not be rooted out unto all the generations for ever.

7 And now 1 shall make you swear a great oath — for there is no oath which is greater than it by the name glorious and honoured

8 and great and splendid and wonderful and mighty, which created the heavens and the earth and all things together — that ye will fear Him and worship Him.

9 And that each will love his brother with affection and righteousness, and that neither will desire evil against his brother from henceforth for ever all the days of your life, so that ye may prosper in all your deeds and not be destroyed.

10 And if either of you deviseth evil against his brother, know that from henceforth every one that deviseth evil against his brother will fall into his hand, and will be rooted out of the land of the living, and his seed will be destroyed from under heaven.

11 But on the day of turbulence and execration and indignation and anger, with flaming devouring fire as He burnt Sodom, so likewise will He burn his land and his city and all that is his, and he will be blotted out of the book of the discipline of the children of men, and not be recorded in the book of life but in that which is appointed to destruction, and he will depart into eternal execration ; so that their condemnation may be always renewed in hate and in execration and in wrath and in torment and in indignation and in plagues and in disease for ever. n.

12 I say and testify to you, my sons, according to the judgment which will come upon the man who wisheth to injure his brother." 12.

13 And he divided all his possessions between the two on that flay, and he gave the larger portion to him that was the first-born, and the tower and all that was about it. and all that Abraham possessed at the Well of the Oath.

14 And he said, " This larger portion I shall give to the first-born." 14.

15 And Esau said, " I have sold to Jacob and given my birthright to Jacob; to him let it be given, and I have not a single word to say regarding it, for it is his." 15.

16 And Isaac said, " May a blessing rest upon you, my sons, and upon your seed this day, for ye have given me rest, and my heart is not pained concerning the birthright, lest thou shouldest work wickedness on account

17 of it.

18 May the Most High God bless the man that worketh righteousness, him and his seed for ever." 17.

19 And he ended commanding them and blessing them, and they ate and drank together before him, and he rejoiced because there was one mind between them, and they went forth from him and rested that day and slept.

20 And Isaac slept on his bed that day rejoicing; and he slept the eternal sleep, and died one hundred and eighty years old.

21 He completed twenty-five weeks and five years ; and his two sons Esau and Jacob buried him.

22 And Esau went to the land of Edom, to the mountains of Seir, and dwelt there.

23 And Jacob dwelt in the mountains of Hebron, in the tower of the land of the sojournings of his father Abraham, and he worshipped the Lord with all his heart and according to the visible commands according as He had divided the days of his generations.

24 And Leah his wife died in the fourth year of the second week of the fortyfifth jubilee, and he buried her in the double cave near Rebecca his mother, to the left of the grave of Sarah, his father's mother.

25 And all her sons and his sons came to mourn over Leah his wife with him, and to comfort him regarding her, for he was lamenting her.

26 For he loved her exceedingly after Rachel her sister died ; for she was perfect and upright in all her ways and honoured Jacob, and all the days that she lived with him he did not hear from her mouth a harsh word, for she was gentle and peaceable and upright and honourable.

27 And he remembered all her deeds which she had done during her life, and he lamented her exceedingly; for he loved her with all his heart and with all his soul.

28 Esau and his Sons wage War with Jacob

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the sixth year of this week Isaac called his two sons, Esau and Jacob, and told them he was going the way of his fathers, to the eternal house where his fathers are. Henry notes that a dying patriarch's charge and blessing finish his testimony, and that Isaac's death, though recorded out of strict order, closes his long life of faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Isaac charged them to bury him near Abraham in the double cave in Ephron's field, in the sepulchre he had dug for himself. Henry notes that Jacob spoke of his burial-place from faith in the promise that Canaan should be his seed's inheritance in due time, and Isaac's charge breathes the same faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Isaac commanded his sons to practise righteousness and uprightness on the earth, so that the Lord might bring upon them all he had promised to Abraham and his seed. The promise is tied to conduct: the covenant blessings follow a righteous walk. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Isaac charged them to love one another as a man loves his own soul, each seeking his brother's benefit, acting together, and loving each other as their own souls. The charge answers the brothers' whole history: the rivalry that began in the womb must end in mutual love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Isaac commanded them concerning idols, to reject, hate, and not love them, for they are full of deception for worshippers and for those who bow down to them. Henry's account of Jacob's household agrees: strange gods must be put away and buried out of sight before the family can worship purely. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Isaac bade his sons remember the Lord God of Abraham, whom he too had worshipped and served in righteousness and joy, that God might multiply them as the stars of heaven and establish them as a plant of righteousness never rooted out. The memory of the fathers' worship is the ground of the sons' hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Isaac said he would make his sons swear a great oath, for there is no greater oath, by the glorious, honoured, great, splendid, wonderful, and mighty name. The oath's greatness matches the charge it seals: brotherly peace for life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The oath was sworn by the name that created the heavens and the earth and all things together, that the brothers would fear and worship God. Creation is invoked as the witness of the covenant between the brothers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The oath bound each brother to love the other with affection and righteousness, and never to desire evil against his brother all the days of his life, so that they might prosper and not be destroyed. Prosperity and peace are joined: the brothers stand or fall together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Isaac warned that if either brother devised evil against the other, he would fall into his brother's hand, be rooted out of the land of the living, and his seed destroyed from under heaven. The warning is the sanction of the oath, and it foreshadows the war that Esau's sons will force upon Jacob. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Isaac said and testified to his sons the judgment that would come upon the man who wished to injure his brother. The testimony closes the charge: the oath is witnessed, and its breach carries a named judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Isaac divided all his possessions between the two that day, giving the larger portion to the first-born, with the tower and all about it, and all that Abraham possessed at the Well of the Oath. Henry's account of Esau's portion notes the same pattern: the children of this world have their possession in hand, while the children of God have theirs in promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Isaac said the larger portion would go to the first-born, the outward honour of primogeniture, even though the covenant blessing had gone to Jacob. The division separates the father's goods from the promise, giving Esau the goods and Jacob the blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Esau answered that he had sold his birthright to Jacob and given it to him, that it was Jacob's, and that he had not a single word to say regarding it. The acknowledgment settles the old grievance at last: the birthright was sold, not stolen, and Esau owns the sale. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Isaac blessed his sons, saying a blessing should rest on them and their seed that day, for they had given him rest, and his heart was not pained concerning the birthright, lest Esau should work wickedness on account of it. The father's rest is the peace of brothers reconciled before his death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Isaac prayed that the Most High God would bless the man who works righteousness, him and his seed for ever. The blessing is conditional in form and universal in scope: righteousness, wherever it is worked, carries the blessing with it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Isaac ended his commanding and blessing, and the brothers ate and drank together before him, rejoicing because there was one mind between them, then went forth and rested that day and slept. Henry notes the same agreement at the father's funeral: God had wonderfully changed Esau's mind, and the brothers stood together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Isaac slept on his bed that day rejoicing, slept the eternal sleep, and died one hundred and eighty years old. Henry records Isaac's death with the same quiet: he had set his house in order, blessed his sons, and died in peace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Isaac completed twenty-five weeks and five years, and his two sons Esau and Jacob buried him. Henry takes particular notice of the agreement of Esau and Jacob at their father's funeral, to show how God had changed Esau's mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Esau went to the land of Edom, to the mountains of Seir, and dwelt there. Henry notes that Mount Seir is called the land of Edom's possession, while Canaan was still only the land of promise for Jacob's seed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Jacob dwelt in the mountains of Hebron, in the tower of his father Abraham's sojournings, worshipping the Lord with all his heart according to the visible commands, as God had divided the days of his generations. The tower at Hebron becomes Jacob's seat, the place of the fathers' worship continued by the son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Leah died in the fourth year of the second week of the forty-fifth jubilee, and Jacob buried her in the double cave near Rebecca his mother, to the left of Sarah's grave. Henry's account of the burial cave notes that Leah was buried there with the patriarchs, the family grave gathering its members across generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. All Leah's sons and Jacob's sons came to mourn over Leah with him and to comfort him, for he was lamenting her. The gathered sons mourn the mother whose children had built the house of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Jacob loved Leah exceedingly after Rachel her sister died, for she was perfect and upright in all her ways, honoured Jacob, and never spoke a harsh word to him all their life, being gentle, peaceable, upright, and honourable. The eulogy reverses Leah's early story: the unloved wife became the honoured wife, and Henry's account of the sisters agrees that God's providence in hearing Leah must be owned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Jacob remembered all Leah's deeds during her life and lamented her exceedingly, loving her with all his heart and soul. The lament is the husband's testimony to a life of quiet faithfulness, remembered deed by deed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
