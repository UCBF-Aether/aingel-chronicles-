The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Ezekiel 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And he said unto me, Son of man, stand upon thy feet, and I will speak unto thee.

2 And the spirit entered into me when he spake unto me, and set me upon my feet, that I heard him that spake unto me.

3 And he said unto me, Son of man, I send thee to the children of Israel, to a rebellious nation that hath rebelled against me: they and their fathers have transgressed against me, even unto this very day.

4 For they are impudent children and stiffhearted. I do send thee unto them; and thou shalt say unto them, Thus saith the Lord GOD.

5 And they, whether they will hear, or whether they will forbear, (for they are a rebellious house,) yet shall know that there hath been a prophet among them.

6 And thou, son of man, be not afraid of them, neither be afraid of their words, though briers and thorns be with thee, and thou dost dwell among scorpions: be not afraid of their words, nor be dismayed at their looks, though they be a rebellious house.

7 And thou shalt speak my words unto them, whether they will hear, or whether they will forbear: for they are most rebellious.

8 But thou, son of man, hear what I say unto thee; Be not thou rebellious like that rebellious house: open thy mouth, and eat that I give thee.

9 And when I looked, behold, an hand was sent unto me; and, lo, a roll of a book was therein;

10 And he spread it before me; and it was written within and without: and there was written therein lamentations, and mourning, and woe.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God calls Ezekiel “son of man,” the Spirit sets him on his feet, and he is sent to a rebellious Israel to speak God’s words whether they listen or not. The measure of his work is faithfulness, not success: even if they refuse, they will know a prophet has been among them. The chapter ends with a scroll covered in lamentation, mourning, and woe being handed to him. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Called son of man, a weak mortal creature, Ezekiel is told to stand, a posture of readiness for the business God gives him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Having no strength of his own, the Spirit entered him and set him on his feet, for God works in us whatever he requires of us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. He is sent to Israel, a rebellious nation whose fathers and they themselves have transgressed against God to this very day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. To impudent, stiffhearted children he must say, Thus saith the Lord GOD, speaking God's words whether they are welcome or not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Whether they hear or forbear, they shall know a prophet has been among them, and God's word will be made honourable either way. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. He must not fear their words or looks, though briers and thorns and scorpions surround him, for wicked men are near to cursing and burning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. He must speak God's words whether they hear or forbear, faithful to their souls, for they are most rebellious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. He himself must not be rebellious like that rebellious house, but open his mouth and eat what God gives him, obeying God's voice first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. A hand was sent to him holding a roll of a book, the written message he was to receive and deliver. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The roll, written within and without with lamentations, mourning, and woe, shows God's word is filled with woe to impenitent sinners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
