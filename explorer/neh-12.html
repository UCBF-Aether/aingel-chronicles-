The Text — Nehemiah 12 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now these are the priests and the Levites that went up with Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and Jeshua: Seraiah, Jeremiah, Ezra,

2 Amariah, Malluch, Hattush,

3 Shechaniah, Rehum, Meremoth,

4 Iddo, Ginnetho, Abijah,

5 Miamin, Maadiah, Bilgah,

6 Shemaiah, and Joiarib, Jedaiah,

7 Sallu, Amok, Hilkiah, Jedaiah. These were the chief of the priests and of their brethren in the days of Jeshua.

8 Moreover the Levites: Jeshua, Binnui, Kadmiel, Sherebiah, Judah, and Mattaniah, which was over the thanksgiving, he and his brethren.

9 Also Bakbukiah and Unni, their brethren, were over against them in the watches.

10 And Jeshua begat Joiakim, Joiakim also begat Eliashib, and Eliashib begat Joiada,

11 And Joiada begat Jonathan, and Jonathan begat Jaddua.

12 And in the days of Joiakim were priests, the chief of the fathers: of Seraiah, Meraiah; of Jeremiah, Hananiah;

13 Of Ezra, Meshullam; of Amariah, Jehohanan;

14 Of Melicu, Jonathan; of Shebaniah, Joseph;

15 Of Harim, Adna; of Meraioth, Helkai;

16 Of Iddo, Zechariah; of Ginnethon, Meshullam;

17 Of Abijah, Zichri; of Miniamin, of Moadiah, Piltai:

18 Of Bilgah, Shammua; of Shemaiah, Jehonathan;

19 And of Joiarib, Mattenai; of Jedaiah, Uzzi;

20 Of Sallai, Kallai; of Amok, Eber;

21 Of Hilkiah, Hashabiah; of Jedaiah, Nethaneel.

22 The Levites in the days of Eliashib, Joiada, and Johanan, and Jaddua, were recorded chief of the fathers: also the priests, to the reign of Darius the Persian.

23 The sons of Levi, the chief of the fathers, were written in the book of the chronicles, even until the days of Johanan the son of Eliashib.

24 And the chief of the Levites: Hashabiah, Sherebiah, and Jeshua the son of Kadmiel, with their brethren over against them, to praise and to give thanks, according to the commandment of David the man of God, ward over against ward.

25 Mattaniah, and Bakbukiah, Obadiah, Meshullam, Talmon, Akkub, were porters keeping the ward at the thresholds of the gates.

26 These were in the days of Joiakim the son of Jeshua, the son of Jozadak, and in the days of Nehemiah the governor, and of Ezra the priest, the scribe.

27 And at the dedication of the wall of Jerusalem they sought the Levites out of all their places, to bring them to Jerusalem, to keep the dedication with gladness, both with thanksgivings, and with singing, with cymbals, psalteries, and with harps.

28 And the sons of the singers gathered themselves together, both out of the plain country round about Jerusalem, and from the villages of Netophathi;

29 Also from the house of Gilgal, and out of the fields of Geba and Azmaveth: for the singers had builded them villages round about Jerusalem.

30 And the priests and the Levites purified themselves, and purified the people, and the gates, and the wall.

31 Then I brought up the princes of Judah upon the wall, and appointed two great companies of them that gave thanks, whereof one went on the right hand upon the wall toward the dung gate:

32 And after them went Hoshaiah, and half of the princes of Judah,

33 And Azariah, Ezra, and Meshullam,

34 Judah, and Benjamin, and Shemaiah, and Jeremiah,

35 And certain of the priests’ sons with trumpets; namely, Zechariah the son of Jonathan, the son of Shemaiah, the son of Mattaniah, the son of Michaiah, the son of Zaccur, the son of Asaph:

36 And his brethren, Shemaiah, and Azarael, Milalai, Gilalai, Maai, Nethaneel, and Judah, Hanani, with the musical instruments of David the man of God, and Ezra the scribe before them.

37 And at the fountain gate, which was over against them, they went up by the stairs of the city of David, at the going up of the wall, above the house of David, even unto the water gate eastward.

38 And the other company of them that gave thanks went over against them, and I after them, and the half of the people upon the wall, from beyond the tower of the furnaces even unto the broad wall;

39 And from above the gate of Ephraim, and above the old gate, and above the fish gate, and the tower of Hananeel, and the tower of Meah, even unto the sheep gate: and they stood still in the prison gate.

40 So stood the two companies of them that gave thanks in the house of God, and I, and the half of the rulers with me:

41 And the priests; Eliakim, Maaseiah, Miniamin, Michaiah, Elioenai, Zechariah, and Hananiah, with trumpets;

42 And Maaseiah, and Shemaiah, and Eleazar, and Uzzi, and Jehohanan, and Malchijah, and Elam, and Ezer. And the singers sang loud, with Jezrahiah their overseer.

43 Also that day they offered great sacrifices, and rejoiced: for God had made them rejoice with great joy: the wives also and the children rejoiced: so that the joy of Jerusalem was heard even afar off.

44 And at that time were some appointed over the chambers for the treasures, for the offerings, for the firstfruits, and for the tithes, to gather into them out of the fields of the cities the portions of the law for the priests and Levites: for Judah rejoiced for the priests and for the Levites that waited.

45 And both the singers and the porters kept the ward of their God, and the ward of the purification, according to the commandment of David, and of Solomon his son.

46 For in the days of David and Asaph of old there were chief of the singers, and songs of praise and thanksgiving unto God.

47 And all Israel in the days of Zerubbabel, and in the days of Nehemiah, gave the portions of the singers and the porters, every day his portion: and they sanctified holy things unto the Levites; and the Levites sanctified them unto the children of Aaron.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

What the chapter says, in plain language

The chapter first registers the priests and Levites who came up with Zerubbabel and Jeshua, and their successors in the days of Joiakim, Eliashib, Joiada, Jonathan, and Jaddua. Then comes the dedication of the wall: the Levites are sought out, priests and Levites purify themselves, the people, the gates, and the wall, and two great companies process in opposite directions along the wall, meeting at the house of God with singing, trumpets, and great joy, after which daily portions for singers and porters are restored. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

Unfamiliar terms

  • Dedication. the formal setting apart of the finished wall, with purification, processions, sacrifices, and rejoicing (verses 27-43). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
  • Singers and porters. the temple musicians and gatekeepers, whose daily portions the people again provide (verse 47). Interpreted Study note for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The priests and Levites who came up with Zerubbabel, when Jeshua was high priest, are here recorded by name. Henry explains why the register is preserved: to keep faithful ministers in everlasting remembrance, to stir their posterity to imitate their courage and fidelity, and to acknowledge that the righteous shall be remembered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Among the Levites, one is noted as being over the thanksgiving, entrusted to see the thanksgiving psalms constantly sung in the temple in due time and manner. Henry notes the Levites kept their turns in their watches, relieving one another as brethren, fellow-labourers, and fellow-soldiers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The succession of high priests runs from Jeshua through Joiakim, Eliashib, Joiada, and Jonathan to Jaddua. Henry traces the line through the Persian monarchy, from Jeshua at the restoration to Jaddua, who met Alexander the Great in his pontifical habit and showed him Daniel's prophecy of his conquests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Joiada begat Jonathan, and Jonathan begat Jaddua, completing the high-priestly succession. Henry notes the succession shows God's care for his church: as one generation of ministers passes away, another comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. In the days of Eliashib, the Levites recorded as chief of the fathers are named. Henry contrasts the generations: the priestly families of Joiakim's days began to degenerate, but a generation of Levites was raised up, eminently serviceable, and their service was no less acceptable for being of the lower rank of ministers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Some Levites were singers to praise and give thanks, according to the commandment of David the man of God. Henry notes these singers, like the porters, served by David's order, and that regard was had to personal qualifications more than to order, since the Levites expounded and prayed while priests grew remiss. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Mattaniah, Bakbukiah, Obadiah, Meshullam, Talmon, and Akkub were porters keeping the ward at the thresholds of the gates. Henry notes both singers and porters served according to David's command, the porters guarding the temple's thresholds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. These Levites served in the days of Joiakim son of Jeshua, and in the days of Nehemiah the governor and Ezra the priest and scribe. Henry frames the chapter's later generation by these days, when Levites, not priests, led in expounding and prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. At the dedication of the wall, the Levites were sought from all their places and brought to Jerusalem to keep the dedication with gladness, thanksgivings, singing, cymbals, psalteries, and harps. Henry explains the dedication: a solemn thanksgiving for the perfected work, a fresh devoting of the holy city to God, and a committing of it to divine protection, since unless the Lord kept the city the walls were built in vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The sons of the singers gathered from the plain country round Jerusalem and from the villages of Netophathi. Henry notes the general rendezvous: the Levites had gone home to their country posts after the feasts of chapters 8 and 9, but were summoned again because the city's surrender to God must pass through his ministers' hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. They came also from the house of Gilgal and the fields of Geba and Azmaveth, for the singers had built villages round about Jerusalem. Henry includes these gathered singers in the dedication's solemnity, with musical instruments signifying the dedication of the whole circuit of the wall to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. The priests and Levites purified themselves, and purified the people, the gates, and the wall. Henry stresses the order: those who would sanctify others must sanctify themselves first, and the purification, probably by the water of separation, was a type of Christ's blood purging consciences for God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Nehemiah brought the princes of Judah up on the wall and appointed two great companies to give thanks, one going on the right hand toward the dung gate. Henry describes the procession: the companies walked the whole circuit with musical instruments, singing psalms as they went, to signify the dedication of it all to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. The first company's brethren followed with the musical instruments of David the man of God, and Ezra the scribe before them. Henry notes Ezra led this company's van, while the other company went to the left with Nehemiah bringing up the rear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. The other company of thanksgivers went opposite, with Nehemiah after them and half the people on the wall, from beyond the tower of the furnaces to the broad wall. Henry notes Nehemiah brought up the rear of this company, so the two processions would meet in the temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. So the two companies of thanksgivers stood in the house of God, with Nehemiah and half the rulers. Henry notes that at length both companies met in the temple and joined their thanksgivings, the crowd walking below within and without the wall to be affected by the mercy and to perpetuate its remembrance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. The singers sang loud, with Jezrahiah their overseer. Henry counts this loud song among the dedication's testimonies of joy, with great sacrifices, trumpets, instruments, and songs of praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. That day they offered great sacrifices and rejoiced, for God had made them rejoice with great joy, wives and children rejoicing too, so that Jerusalem's joy was heard afar off. Henry notes God gave both the matter for joy and hearts to rejoice, and that the honest shouts of common people, women, and children were recorded to their praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. At that time some were appointed over the chambers for treasures, offerings, firstfruits, and tithes, to gather from the fields of the cities the portions of the law for priests and Levites, for Judah rejoiced for the priests and Levites who waited. Henry notes the people, seeing diligent ministers, thought nothing too much to do for them, and appointed collectors so portions would not be lost for want of being demanded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. The singers kept the ward of their God and the porters kept the ward of the purification, according to the commandment of David and Solomon his son. Henry notes the dedication's joy left ministers more careful: singers attended their office in due time, and porters preserved the temple's purity by denying admission to the ceremonially unclean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 46. In the days of David and Asaph of old there were chief singers, with songs of praise and thanksgiving to God. Henry traces the singers' and porters' daily portions to David's and Solomon's appointments, with a fund settled for their further encouragement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. All Israel, in the days of Zerubbabel and Nehemiah, gave the singers' and porters' portions every day, sanctified holy things to the Levites, and the Levites sanctified them to the priests. Henry notes both gifts and receivers were sanctified when given with an eye to God's honour, bringing blessing on the house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
