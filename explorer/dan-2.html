The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Daniel 2 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–12; King James Version, Apocrypha for the additions)

1 And in the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar Nebuchadnezzar dreamed dreams, wherewith his spirit was troubled, and his sleep brake from him.

2 Then the king commanded to call the magicians, and the astrologers, and the sorcerers, and the Chaldeans, for to shew the king his dreams. So they came and stood before the king.

3 And the king said unto them, I have dreamed a dream, and my spirit was troubled to know the dream.

4 Then spake the Chaldeans to the king in Syriack, O king, live for ever: tell thy servants the dream, and we will shew the interpretation.

5 The king answered and said to the Chaldeans, The thing is gone from me: if ye will not make known unto me the dream, with the interpretation thereof, ye shall be cut in pieces, and your houses shall be made a dunghill.

6 But if ye shew the dream, and the interpretation thereof, ye shall receive of me gifts and rewards and great honour: therefore shew me the dream, and the interpretation thereof.

7 They answered again and said, Let the king tell his servants the dream, and we will shew the interpretation of it.

8 The king answered and said, I know of certainty that ye would gain the time, because ye see the thing is gone from me.

9 But if ye will not make known unto me the dream, there is but one decree for you: for ye have prepared lying and corrupt words to speak before me, till the time be changed: therefore tell me the dream, and I shall know that ye can shew me the interpretation thereof.

10 The Chaldeans answered before the king, and said, There is not a man upon the earth that can shew the king’s matter: therefore there is no king, lord, nor ruler, that asked such things at any magician, or astrologer, or Chaldean.

11 And it is a rare thing that the king requireth, and there is none other that can shew it before the king, except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh.

12 For this cause the king was angry and very furious, and commanded to destroy all the wise men of Babylon.

13 And the decree went forth that the wise men should be slain; and they sought Daniel and his fellows to be slain.

14 Then Daniel answered with counsel and wisdom to Arioch the captain of the king’s guard, which was gone forth to slay the wise men of Babylon:

15 He answered and said to Arioch the king’s captain, Why is the decree so hasty from the king? Then Arioch made the thing known to Daniel.

16 Then Daniel went in, and desired of the king that he would give him time, and that he would shew the king the interpretation.

17 Then Daniel went to his house, and made the thing known to Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, his companions:

18 That they would desire mercies of the God of heaven concerning this secret; that Daniel and his fellows should not perish with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.

19 Then was the secret revealed unto Daniel in a night vision. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

20 Daniel answered and said, Blessed be the name of God for ever and ever: for wisdom and might are his:

21 And he changeth the times and the seasons: he removeth kings, and setteth up kings: he giveth wisdom unto the wise, and knowledge to them that know understanding:

22 He revealeth the deep and secret things: he knoweth what is in the darkness, and the light dwelleth with him.

23 I thank thee, and praise thee, O thou God of my fathers, who hast given me wisdom and might, and hast made known unto me now what we desired of thee: for thou hast now made known unto us the king’s matter.

24 Therefore Daniel went in unto Arioch, whom the king had ordained to destroy the wise men of Babylon: he went and said thus unto him; Destroy not the wise men of Babylon: bring me in before the king, and I will shew unto the king the interpretation.

25 Then Arioch brought in Daniel before the king in haste, and said thus unto him, I have found a man of the captives of Judah, that will make known unto the king the interpretation.

26 The king answered and said to Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, Art thou able to make known unto me the dream which I have seen, and the interpretation thereof?

27 Daniel answered in the presence of the king, and said, The secret which the king hath demanded cannot the wise men, the astrologers, the magicians, the soothsayers, shew unto the king;

28 But there is a God in heaven that revealeth secrets, and maketh known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days. Thy dream, and the visions of thy head upon thy bed, are these;

29 As for thee, O king, thy thoughts came into thy mind upon thy bed, what should come to pass hereafter: and he that revealeth secrets maketh known to thee what shall come to pass.

30 But as for me, this secret is not revealed to me for any wisdom that I have more than any living, but for their sakes that shall make known the interpretation to the king, and that thou mightest know the thoughts of thy heart.

31 Thou, O king, sawest, and behold a great image. This great image, whose brightness was excellent, stood before thee; and the form thereof was terrible.

32 This image’s head was of fine gold, his breast and his arms of silver, his belly and his thighs of brass,

33 His legs of iron, his feet part of iron and part of clay.

34 Thou sawest till that a stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image upon his feet that were of iron and clay, and brake them to pieces.

35 Then was the iron, the clay, the brass, the silver, and the gold, broken to pieces together, and became like the chaff of the summer threshingfloors; and the wind carried them away, that no place was found for them: and the stone that smote the image became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth.

36 This is the dream; and we will tell the interpretation thereof before the king.

37 Thou, O king, art a king of kings: for the God of heaven hath given thee a kingdom, power, and strength, and glory.

38 And wheresoever the children of men dwell, the beasts of the field and the fowls of the heaven hath he given into thine hand, and hath made thee ruler over them all. Thou art this head of gold.

39 And after thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee, and another third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth.

40 And the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron: forasmuch as iron breaketh in pieces and subdueth all things: and as iron that breaketh all these, shall it break in pieces and bruise.

41 And whereas thou sawest the feet and toes, part of potters’ clay, and part of iron, the kingdom shall be divided; but there shall be in it of the strength of the iron, forasmuch as thou sawest the iron mixed with miry clay.

42 And as the toes of the feet were part of iron, and part of clay, so the kingdom shall be partly strong, and partly broken.

43 And whereas thou sawest iron mixed with miry clay, they shall mingle themselves with the seed of men: but they shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay.

44 And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed: and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever.

45 Forasmuch as thou sawest that the stone was cut out of the mountain without hands, and that it brake in pieces the iron, the brass, the clay, the silver, and the gold; the great God hath made known to the king what shall come to pass hereafter: and the dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure.

46 Then the king Nebuchadnezzar fell upon his face, and worshipped Daniel, and commanded that they should offer an oblation and sweet odours unto him.

47 The king answered unto Daniel, and said, Of a truth it is, that your God is a God of gods, and a Lord of kings, and a revealer of secrets, seeing thou couldest reveal this secret.

48 Then the king made Daniel a great man, and gave him many great gifts, and made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon, and chief of the governors over all the wise men of Babylon.

49 Then Daniel requested of the king, and he set Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, over the affairs of the province of Babylon: but Daniel sat in the gate of the king.

How to read the labels

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Nebuchadnezzar dreams a dream he cannot remember, and his wise men cannot recover or interpret it on pain of death. Daniel, given the dream and its meaning in a night vision, tells the king of a great image — head of gold, breast of silver, belly of brass, legs of iron, feet of iron and clay — smashed by a stone cut out without hands, which becomes a great mountain filling the earth. The image is the succession of kingdoms; the stone is a kingdom God himself sets up, which shall stand for ever. Interpreted Chapter text; study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Praying friends are valuable friends, Henry says, and it well becomes the greatest and best men to desire the prayers of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Then Daniel went to his house, and made the thing known to Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, his companions. Let us show that we value our friends and their prayers, Henry urges, as Daniel shared the peril with his companions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. That they would desire mercies of the God of heaven concerning this secret, that Daniel and his fellows should not perish with the rest of the wise men of Babylon. They were particular in prayer, Henry notes, and their plea with God was the peril they were in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Then was the secret revealed unto Daniel in a night vision. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven. The mercy Daniel and his fellows prayed for was bestowed, Henry says, and the fervent prayers of righteous men avail much. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Daniel answered, blessed be the name of God for ever and ever, for wisdom and might are his. Daniel was thankful to God for making known that to him which saved the lives of himself and his fellows, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. He changes the times and the seasons, removes kings and sets up kings, gives wisdom unto the wise and knowledge to them that know understanding. Whatever we pray for, Henry says, we can expect nothing but as the gift of God's mercies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. He reveals the deep and secret things, knows what is in the darkness, and the light dwells with him. How much more should we be thankful, Henry asks, for God making known the great salvation of the soul to those who are not among the worldly wise and prudent! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. I thank thee and praise thee, O thou God of my fathers, who hast given me wisdom and might, and hast made known unto me now what we desired of thee, for thou hast now made known unto us the king's matter. Daniel's thanksgiving, Henry notes, names God as the giver of the wisdom that saved them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Therefore Daniel went in unto Arioch, whom the king had ordained to destroy the wise men, and said, bring me in before the king, and I will show unto the king the interpretation. Daniel takes away the king's opinion of his magicians and soothsayers, Henry says, directing attention to the God who reveals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Then Arioch brought in Daniel before the king in haste, and said, I have found a man of the captives of Judah, that will make known unto the king the interpretation. The insufficiency of creatures should drive us to the all-sufficiency of the Creator, Henry says. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. The king answered Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, art thou able to make known unto me the dream which I have seen, and the interpretation thereof? Daniel confirmed the king in his opinion that the dream was of great consequence, Henry notes, relating to the affairs and changes of this lower world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Daniel answered, the secret which the king has demanded cannot the wise men, the astrologers, the magicians, the soothsayers, show unto the king. There is One who can do that for us, Henry says, which none on earth can, particularly the work of redemption and the secret designs of God's love therein. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. But there is a God in heaven that reveals secrets, and makes known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days. Let those whom God has highly favoured and honoured lay aside all opinion of their own wisdom and worthiness, Henry urges, that the Lord alone may be praised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. As for thee, O king, thy thoughts came into thy mind upon thy bed, what should come to pass hereafter, and he that reveals secrets makes known to thee what shall come to pass. Daniel confirmed that the dream concerned what should come to pass hereafter, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. But as for me, this secret is not revealed to me for any wisdom that I have more than any living, but for their sakes that shall make known the interpretation to the king, and that thou mightest know the thoughts of thy heart. Daniel disclaimed personal wisdom, Henry's reading stresses, so that the Lord alone might be praised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. Thou, O king, sawest, and behold a great image, whose brightness was excellent, stood before thee, and the form thereof was terrible. This image represented the kingdoms of the earth, Henry says, that should successively rule the nations and influence the affairs of the Jewish church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. This image's head was of fine gold, his breast and his arms of silver, his belly and his thighs of brass. The head of gold signified the Chaldean empire, then in being, Henry explains, and the breast and arms of silver the empire of the Medes and Persians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. His legs of iron, his feet part of iron and part of clay. The belly and thighs of brass signified the Grecian empire founded by Alexander, Henry says, and the legs and feet of iron the Roman empire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. Thou sawest till that a stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image upon his feet that were of iron and clay, and broke them to pieces. The stone cut out without hands represented the kingdom of our Lord Jesus Christ, Henry says, set up in the kingdoms of the world upon the ruins of Satan's kingdom in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. Then was the iron, the clay, the brass, the silver, and the gold, broken to pieces together, and became like the chaff of the summer threshingfloors, and the wind carried them away, and no place was found for them, and the stone that smote the image became a great mountain and filled the whole earth. Of the increase of Christ's government and peace there shall be no end, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. This is the dream, and we will tell the interpretation thereof before the king. Daniel now turns from the dream to its interpretation, Henry's outline shows, having proved the revelation genuine by telling the dream itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. Thou, O king, art a king of kings, for the God of heaven has given thee a kingdom, power, strength, and glory. The head of gold is the Chaldean empire then in being, Henry says, and Nebuchadnezzar himself its head. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. And wheresoever the children of men dwell, the beasts of the field and the fowls of the heaven has he given into thine hand, and has made thee ruler over them all. Thou art this head of gold. The image's successive kingdoms, Henry explains, should rule the nations in turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. And after thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee, and another third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth. The second kingdom of silver is the Medes and Persians, Henry says, and the third of brass the Grecian empire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. And the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron, forasmuch as iron breaks in pieces and subdues all things. The legs and feet of iron signified the Roman empire, Henry explains, which branched into ten kingdoms, as the toes of these feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 41. And whereas thou sawest the feet and toes, part of potters' clay and part of iron, the kingdom shall be divided, but there shall be in it of the strength of the iron. Some of the ten kingdoms were weak as clay, Henry notes, others strong as iron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 42. And as the toes of the feet were part of iron and part of clay, so the kingdom shall be partly strong and partly broken. Endeavours have often been used to unite them for strengthening the empire, Henry says, but in vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 43. And whereas thou sawest iron mixed with miry clay, they shall mingle themselves with the seed of men, but they shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay. The mingling without cleaving, Henry's reading shows, marks the divided kingdom's incurable weakness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 44. And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed, and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever. This was the Stone which the builders refused, Henry says, because it was not cut out by their hands, but it is become the head stone of the corner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 45. Forasmuch as thou sawest that the stone was cut out of the mountain without hands, and that it broke in pieces the iron, the brass, the clay, the silver, and the gold, the great God has made known to the king what shall come to pass hereafter, and the dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure. As far as events have gone, Henry says, the fulfilling of this prophetic vision has been most exact and undeniable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 46. Then the king Nebuchadnezzar fell upon his face, and worshipped Daniel, and commanded that they should offer an oblation and sweet odours unto him. It is our business to direct attention to the Lord as the Author and Giver of every good gift, Henry says, and many have thoughts of the divine power who do not think of serving God themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 47. The king answered Daniel, of a truth it is, that your God is a God of gods, and a Lord of kings, and a revealer of secrets, seeing thou couldest reveal this secret. The king confessed the God of heaven, Henry's account shows, though confession alone is not service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 48. Then the king made Daniel a great man, and gave him many great gifts, and made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon, and chief of the governors over all the wise men of Babylon. All should strive, Henry says, that God may be glorified and the best interests of mankind furthered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 49. Then Daniel requested of the king, and he set Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, over the affairs of the province of Babylon, but Daniel sat in the gate of the king. Daniel used his honour for his friends' good, Henry's account implies, and remained himself at the king's gate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Daniel humbly sought God rather than raging against the decree, Henry's account shows, answering with counsel and wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Daniel asked Arioch, why is the decree so hasty from the king? Then Arioch made the thing known to Daniel. Daniel's calm inquiry, Henry implies, opened the way to the mercy that followed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Then Daniel went in, and desired of the king that he would give him time, and that he would show the king the interpretation. Praying friends are valuable friends, Henry says, and it well becomes the greatest and best men to desire the prayers of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Then Daniel went to his house, and made the thing known to Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, his companions. Let us show that we value our friends and their prayers, Henry urges, as Daniel shared the peril with his companions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. That they would desire mercies of the God of heaven concerning this secret, that Daniel and his fellows should not perish with the rest of the wise men of Babylon. They were particular in prayer, Henry notes, and their plea with God was the peril they were in. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Then was the secret revealed unto Daniel in a night vision. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven. The mercy Daniel and his fellows prayed for was bestowed, Henry says, and the fervent prayers of righteous men avail much. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Daniel answered, blessed be the name of God for ever and ever, for wisdom and might are his. Daniel was thankful to God for making known that to him which saved the lives of himself and his fellows, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. He changes the times and the seasons, removes kings and sets up kings, gives wisdom unto the wise and knowledge to them that know understanding. Whatever we pray for, Henry says, we can expect nothing but as the gift of God's mercies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. He reveals the deep and secret things, knows what is in the darkness, and the light dwells with him. How much more should we be thankful, Henry asks, for God making known the great salvation of the soul to those who are not among the worldly wise and prudent! Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. I thank thee and praise thee, O thou God of my fathers, who hast given me wisdom and might, and hast made known unto me now what we desired of thee, for thou hast now made known unto us the king's matter. Daniel's thanksgiving, Henry notes, names God as the giver of the wisdom that saved them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Therefore Daniel went in unto Arioch, whom the king had ordained to destroy the wise men, and said, bring me in before the king, and I will show unto the king the interpretation. Daniel takes away the king's opinion of his magicians and soothsayers, Henry says, directing attention to the God who reveals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Then Arioch brought in Daniel before the king in haste, and said, I have found a man of the captives of Judah, that will make known unto the king the interpretation. The insufficiency of creatures should drive us to the all-sufficiency of the Creator, Henry says. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. The king answered Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, art thou able to make known unto me the dream which I have seen, and the interpretation thereof? Daniel confirmed the king in his opinion that the dream was of great consequence, Henry notes, relating to the affairs and changes of this lower world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. Daniel answered, the secret which the king has demanded cannot the wise men, the astrologers, the magicians, the soothsayers, show unto the king. There is One who can do that for us, Henry says, which none on earth can, particularly the work of redemption and the secret designs of God's love therein. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. But there is a God in heaven that reveals secrets, and makes known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days. Let those whom God has highly favoured and honoured lay aside all opinion of their own wisdom and worthiness, Henry urges, that the Lord alone may be praised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. As for thee, O king, thy thoughts came into thy mind upon thy bed, what should come to pass hereafter, and he that reveals secrets makes known to thee what shall come to pass. Daniel confirmed that the dream concerned what should come to pass hereafter, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. But as for me, this secret is not revealed to me for any wisdom that I have more than any living, but for their sakes that shall make known the interpretation to the king, and that thou mightest know the thoughts of thy heart. Daniel disclaimed personal wisdom, Henry's reading stresses, so that the Lord alone might be praised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 31. Thou, O king, sawest, and behold a great image, whose brightness was excellent, stood before thee, and the form thereof was terrible. This image represented the kingdoms of the earth, Henry says, that should successively rule the nations and influence the affairs of the Jewish church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 32. This image's head was of fine gold, his breast and his arms of silver, his belly and his thighs of brass. The head of gold signified the Chaldean empire, then in being, Henry explains, and the breast and arms of silver the empire of the Medes and Persians. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 33. His legs of iron, his feet part of iron and part of clay. The belly and thighs of brass signified the Grecian empire founded by Alexander, Henry says, and the legs and feet of iron the Roman empire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 34. Thou sawest till that a stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image upon his feet that were of iron and clay, and broke them to pieces. The stone cut out without hands represented the kingdom of our Lord Jesus Christ, Henry says, set up in the kingdoms of the world upon the ruins of Satan's kingdom in them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 35. Then was the iron, the clay, the brass, the silver, and the gold, broken to pieces together, and became like the chaff of the summer threshingfloors, and the wind carried them away, and no place was found for them, and the stone that smote the image became a great mountain and filled the whole earth. Of the increase of Christ's government and peace there shall be no end, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 36. This is the dream, and we will tell the interpretation thereof before the king. Daniel now turns from the dream to its interpretation, Henry's outline shows, having proved the revelation genuine by telling the dream itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 37. Thou, O king, art a king of kings, for the God of heaven has given thee a kingdom, power, strength, and glory. The head of gold is the Chaldean empire then in being, Henry says, and Nebuchadnezzar himself its head. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 38. And wheresoever the children of men dwell, the beasts of the field and the fowls of the heaven has he given into thine hand, and has made thee ruler over them all. Thou art this head of gold. The image's successive kingdoms, Henry explains, should rule the nations in turn. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 39. And after thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee, and another third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth. The second kingdom of silver is the Medes and Persians, Henry says, and the third of brass the Grecian empire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 40. And the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron, forasmuch as iron breaks in pieces and subdues all things. The legs and feet of iron signified the Roman empire, Henry explains, which branched into ten kingdoms, as the toes of these feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 41. And whereas thou sawest the feet and toes, part of potters' clay and part of iron, the kingdom shall be divided, but there shall be in it of the strength of the iron. Some of the ten kingdoms were weak as clay, Henry notes, others strong as iron. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 42. And as the toes of the feet were part of iron and part of clay, so the kingdom shall be partly strong and partly broken. Endeavours have often been used to unite them for strengthening the empire, Henry says, but in vain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 43. And whereas thou sawest iron mixed with miry clay, they shall mingle themselves with the seed of men, but they shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay. The mingling without cleaving, Henry's reading shows, marks the divided kingdom's incurable weakness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 44. And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed, and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever. This was the Stone which the builders refused, Henry says, because it was not cut out by their hands, but it is become the head stone of the corner. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 45. Forasmuch as thou sawest that the stone was cut out of the mountain without hands, and that it broke in pieces the iron, the brass, the clay, the silver, and the gold, the great God has made known to the king what shall come to pass hereafter, and the dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure. As far as events have gone, Henry says, the fulfilling of this prophetic vision has been most exact and undeniable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 46. Then the king Nebuchadnezzar fell upon his face, and worshipped Daniel, and commanded that they should offer an oblation and sweet odours unto him. It is our business to direct attention to the Lord as the Author and Giver of every good gift, Henry says, and many have thoughts of the divine power who do not think of serving God themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 47. The king answered Daniel, of a truth it is, that your God is a God of gods, and a Lord of kings, and a revealer of secrets, seeing thou couldest reveal this secret. The king confessed the God of heaven, Henry's account shows, though confession alone is not service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 48. Then the king made Daniel a great man, and gave him many great gifts, and made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon, and chief of the governors over all the wise men of Babylon. All should strive, Henry says, that God may be glorified and the best interests of mankind furthered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 49. Then Daniel requested of the king, and he set Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, over the affairs of the province of Babylon, but Daniel sat in the gate of the king. Daniel used his honour for his friends' good, Henry's account implies, and remained himself at the king's gate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
