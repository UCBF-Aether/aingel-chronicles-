The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 26 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Judge me, O LORD; for I have walked in mine integrity: I have trusted also in the LORD; therefore I shall not slide.

2 Examine me, O LORD, and prove me; try my reins and my heart.

3 For thy lovingkindness is before mine eyes: and I have walked in thy truth.

4 I have not sat with vain persons, neither will I go in with dissemblers.

5 I have hated the congregation of evil doers; and will not sit with the wicked.

6 I will wash mine hands in innocency: so will I compass thine altar, O LORD:

7 That I may publish with the voice of thanksgiving, and tell of all thy wondrous works.

8 LORD, I have loved the habitation of thy house, and the place where thine honour dwelleth.

9 Gather not my soul with sinners, nor my life with bloody men:

10 In whose hands is mischief, and their right hand is full of bribes.

11 But as for me, I will walk in mine integrity: redeem me, and be merciful unto me.

12 My foot standeth in an even place: in the congregations will I bless the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Integrity examined: the speaker offers heart and reins for God's searching, refuses the company of vain and dissembling men, washes his hands in innocence to compass the altar with thanksgiving, loves God's house, and prays not to share the bribe-stained end of sinners. His foot stands on even ground, blessing God in the congregations. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Judge me, O Lord, for I have walked in my integrity, and trusted in the Lord, therefore I shall not slide: he asks for judgment on his walk, with trust as its ground and steadfastness as its fruit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Examine me, Lord, and prove me; try my reins and my heart: the inward parts — affections and thoughts — are offered for God's searching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For your lovingkindness is before my eyes, and I have walked in your truth: God's mercy kept in view produces a walk in truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. I have not sat with vain persons, nor will I go in with dissemblers: company is a moral choice, and he refuses both the empty and the hypocritical. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. I have hated the congregation of evildoers, and will not sit with the wicked: hatred of the assembly, not of persons as such, keeps him from their seat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. I will wash my hands in innocency, and so compass your altar, O Lord: cleansed hands are the preparation for approaching God's altar in worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. That I may publish with the voice of thanksgiving and tell of all your wondrous works: worship at the altar issues in public thanksgiving and testimony. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Lord, I have loved the habitation of your house, and the place where your honour dwells: love for God's house is love for the place of his manifested glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Gather not my soul with sinners, nor my life with bloody men: he prays not to share the sinners' end, as he has not shared their ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. In whose hands is mischief, and their right hand full of bribes: the sinners are described by bribery and scheming hands — corruption that perverts justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. But as for me, I will walk in my integrity; redeem me and be merciful to me: his resolve is joined to a plea for redemption and mercy, not to self-righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. My foot stands in an even place; in the congregations I will bless the Lord: firm footing now becomes public blessing — the even place leads to the congregation's praise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
