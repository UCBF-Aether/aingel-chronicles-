The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Romans 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 What shall we say then that Abraham our father, as pertaining to the flesh, hath found?

2 For if Abraham were justified by works, he hath whereof to glory; but not before God.

3 For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness.

4 Now to him that worketh is the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt.

5 But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness.

6 Even as David also describeth the blessedness of the man, unto whom God imputeth righteousness without works,

7 Saying, Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered.

8 Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin.

9 Cometh this blessedness then upon the circumcision only, or upon the uncircumcision also? for we say that faith was reckoned to Abraham for righteousness.

10 How was it then reckoned? when he was in circumcision, or in uncircumcision? Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision.

11 And he received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had yet being uncircumcised: that he might be the father of all them that believe, though they be not circumcised; that righteousness might be imputed unto them also:

12 And the father of circumcision to them who are not of the circumcision only, but who also walk in the steps of that faith of our father Abraham, which he had being yet uncircumcised.

13 For the promise, that he should be the heir of the world, was not to Abraham, or to his seed, through the law, but through the righteousness of faith.

14 For if they which are of the law be heirs, faith is made void, and the promise made of none effect:

15 Because the law worketh wrath: for where no law is, there is no transgression.

16 Therefore it is of faith, that it might be by grace; to the end the promise might be sure to all the seed; not to that only which is of the law, but to that also which is of the faith of Abraham; who is the father of us all,

17 (As it is written, I have made thee a father of many nations,) before him whom he believed, even God, who quickeneth the dead, and calleth those things which be not as though they were.

18 Who against hope believed in hope, that he might become the father of many nations, according to that which was spoken, So shall thy seed be.

19 And being not weak in faith, he considered not his own body now dead, when he was about an hundred years old, neither yet the deadness of Sarah’s womb:

20 He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief; but was strong in faith, giving glory to God;

21 And being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform.

22 And therefore it was imputed to him for righteousness.

23 Now it was not written for his sake alone, that it was imputed to him;

24 But for us also, to whom it shall be imputed, if we believe on him that raised up Jesus our Lord from the dead;

25 Who was delivered for our offences, and was raised again for our justification.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Abraham, Paul argues, was counted righteous by faith before he was circumcised and before the law, so he is the father of all who believe, circumcised or not. David's psalm confirms the same blessing of the man to whom God counts righteousness without works, and the promise of heirship came through faith, resting on the God who gives life to the dead and calls things that are not as though they were. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. What shall we say that Abraham our father, as pertaining to the flesh, has found? Henry notes that Abraham is the test case for justification. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. If Abraham were justified by works, he would have something to glory in, but not before God. JFB stresses that glorying before men is not glorying before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. What does Scripture say? Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness. Henry notes that faith, not labour, was reckoned as righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. To him who works, the reward is reckoned not of grace but of debt. JFB stresses that wages and gifts belong to opposite economies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. To him who works not, but believes on him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness. Henry observes the scandal and comfort of the phrase: God justifies the ungodly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. David also describes the blessedness of the man to whom God imputes righteousness without works. JFB notes that David's psalm confirms Abraham's story. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven and whose sins are covered. Henry stresses that forgiveness is the first half of imputed blessedness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin. JFB notes that non-imputation of sin and imputation of righteousness are two sides of one gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Does this blessedness come on the circumcised only, or on the uncircumcised also? For faith was reckoned to Abraham for righteousness. Henry observes that the timing of Abraham's justification settles the question. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. How was it reckoned? When Abraham was in circumcision or in uncircumcision? Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision. JFB stresses the chronology: justification preceded the rite by years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Abraham received circumcision as a sign and seal of the righteousness of the faith he had while uncircumcised, that he might be father of all who believe though uncircumcised. Henry notes that a seal confirms a gift; it does not create it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. He is also father of the circumcised who not only are circumcised but walk in the steps of Abraham's faith. JFB stresses that descent is traced by faith, not by blood or rite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The promise that Abraham would be heir of the world came not through the law but through the righteousness of faith. Henry observes that a promise so wide could never rest on so narrow a ground as law-keeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. If those of the law are heirs, faith is made void and the promise made of no effect. JFB notes that law and promise as grounds of inheritance exclude each other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The law works wrath, for where there is no law there is no transgression. Henry stresses that law, by defining transgression, exposes man to wrath. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Therefore the promise is of faith, that it might be by grace, to the end it might be sure to all the seed, not only to those of the law but to those of Abraham's faith. JFB notes that grace makes the promise certain, because it does not depend on man's performance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. As it is written, Abraham is father of many nations, before God whom he believed, who gives life to the dead and calls things that are not as though they were. Henry observes that Abraham's faith rested on God's life-giving power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Against hope Abraham believed in hope, that he might become father of many nations, according to what was spoken: so shall your seed be. JFB notes that faith hopes when nature gives no ground for hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Being not weak in faith, he did not consider his own body now dead, being about a hundred years old, nor the deadness of Sarah's womb. Henry stresses that faith looks at God's promise, not at the body's decay. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. He staggered not at God's promise through unbelief, but was strong in faith, giving glory to God. JFB notes that unwavering trust itself glorifies God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. He was fully persuaded that what God had promised, he was able also to perform. Henry observes that faith measures God's ability, not the difficulty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Therefore it was imputed to him for righteousness. JFB notes the therefore: this kind of faith is what God counts as righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. It was not written for his sake alone that it was imputed to him. Henry notes that Scripture records Abraham's justification as a pattern, not a private privilege. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. It was written also for us, to whom it shall be imputed if we believe on him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead. JFB stresses that the same faith, directed to the same God, receives the same reckoning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Jesus was delivered for our offences and raised again for our justification. Henry notes that his death pays for sin and his resurrection seals the acquittal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • imputed. Counted or reckoned to someone's account; Abraham's faith was counted to him for righteousness. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • heir of the world. Paul's description of the promise to Abraham and his seed, received not through law but through the righteousness of faith. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • circumcision as a seal. Paul's account of Abraham's circumcision: a sign and seal of the righteousness he already had by faith. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
