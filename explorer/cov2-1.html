The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Book of the Covenant 1 (J. Cooper & A. J. Maclean, The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1902), public domain (archive.org cu31924029296170))

*OF LAYMEN THUS:*

1 Let those who first come‘ to hear the Word, before they enter among all the people, first come to teachers at home,’ and let them be examined as to all the cause [of their coming] with all accuracy, so that their teachers may know for what they have come, or with what will, And if they have come with a good will and love, let them be diligently taught.

2 But let those who bring them be such as are well on in years, faithful who are known by the Church; and let them bear witness about them, if they are able to hear [the word]. Also let their life and conversation be asked about: if they be not contentious, if quiet, if meek, not speaking vain things or despisers or foul speakers, or buffoons or leaders astray, or ridicule mongers.

3 Also if any of them have a wife or not; and if of his own free will he have not [a wife], let him be instructed carefully and diligently and persuaded with all kindness to amend his failings.

4 And let the bishop provide for him in the Lord with prophetical instructions which lead him to purity; and if he maketh progress, also with apostolic doctrines and then with Gospel [doctrines] and with the perfect word of doctrine; and if he be worthy, let him be baptized. And if thus he be worthy of the hidden things, let him hear [them] by himself, and also make progress in that which is hidden.

5 Let there be no obstacle at all to him who desireth to marry, so that he be not caught by the Evil One with fornication. But let him marry a Christian, a faithful [woman] of the race of the Christians, who is able to keep her husband in the faith; at the bidding of the bishop, he thus providing for him.

6 And also let him who cometh’ be asked if he be a slave or free; and if the slave of one who is faithful, and if also his master permit him, let him hear. But if his master be not faithful and do not permit him, let him be persuaded to permit him.

7 And if [his master] say truly about him that he! wisheth to become a Christian because he hateth his masters, let him be cast out. But if no cause be shewn of hatred of servitude, but [if] he [really] wish to be a Christian, let him hear. But if his master be faithful and do not bear witness to him, let him be cast out.

8 Similarly if [a woman] be the wife of a man,” let the woman be taught to please her husband in the fear of God. But if both of them desire to serve purity in piety, they have “ a reward.

9 Let him who is unmarried! not commit fornication, but let him marry in the law. But if he desire to persevere thus, let him abide in the Lord.

10 If any one be tormented with a devil, let him not hear the Word from a teacher until he be cleansed. For the intelligence, when consumed with a material spirit, doth not receive the immaterial and holy Word. But if he be cleansed, let him be instructed in the Word.

Overview

The book now turns to laymen: those who first come to hear the Word are, before entering among all the people, first to come to teachers at home and be examined with all accuracy as to the cause of their coming, so that their teachers may know for what they have come and with what will; if they have come with good will and love, they are to be diligently taught. Those who bring them are to be well on in years, faithful and known by the Church, bearing witness about them if they are able to hear the word; their life and conversation are to be asked about — whether they are contentious, quiet, meek, speaking vain things, despisers, foul speakers, buffoons, leaders astray or ridicule mongers. They are also to be asked whether they have a wife; if of his own free will he has not a wife, he is to be instructed carefully and diligently and persuaded with all kindness to amend his failings. The bishop is to provide for him in the Lord with prophetical instructions leading him to purity, and if he makes progress, with apostolic doctrines and then with Gospel doctrines and the perfect word of doctrine; if he is worthy, he is to be baptized, and if worthy of the hidden things, he is to hear them by himself and make progress in what is hidden. There is to be no obstacle at all to him who desires to marry, so that he is not caught by the Evil One with fornication; he is to marry a Christian, a faithful woman of the race of Christians, able to keep her husband in the faith, at the bidding of the bishop, who thus provides for him. He who comes is also to be asked if he is a slave or free: if the slave of one who is faithful and his master permits him, he is to hear; if his master is not faithful and does not permit him, he is to be persuaded to permit him. If the master says truly that he wishes to become a Christian because he hates his masters, he is to be cast out; but if no cause of hatred of servitude is shown and he really wishes to be a Christian, he is to hear; if his master is faithful and does not bear witness to him, he is to be cast out. Similarly, if a woman is the wife of a man, she is to be taught to please her husband in the fear of God; but if both desire to serve purity in piety, they have a reward. He who is unmarried is not to commit fornication but to marry in the law; if he desires to persevere thus, let him abide in the Lord. If anyone is tormented with a devil, he is not to hear the Word from a teacher until he is cleansed, for the intelligence, when consumed with a material spirit, does not receive the immaterial and holy Word; but if he is cleansed, he is to be instructed in the Word.
