The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Likewise this is the law of the trespass offering: it is most holy.

2 In the place where they kill the burnt offering shall they kill the trespass offering: and the blood thereof shall he sprinkle round about upon the altar.

3 And he shall offer of it all the fat thereof; the rump, and the fat that covereth the inwards,

4 And the two kidneys, and the fat that is on them, which is by the flanks, and the caul that is above the liver, with the kidneys, it shall he take away:

5 And the priest shall burn them upon the altar for an offering made by fire unto the LORD: it is a trespass offering.

6 Every male among the priests shall eat thereof: it shall be eaten in the holy place: it is most holy.

7 As the sin offering is, so is the trespass offering: there is one law for them: the priest that maketh atonement therewith shall have it.

8 And the priest that offereth any man’s burnt offering, even the priest shall have to himself the skin of the burnt offering which he hath offered.

9 And all the meat offering that is baken in the oven, and all that is dressed in the fryingpan, and in the pan, shall be the priest’s that offereth it.

10 And every meat offering, mingled with oil, and dry, shall all the sons of Aaron have, one as much as another.

11 And this is the law of the sacrifice of peace offerings, which he shall offer unto the LORD.

12 If he offer it for a thanksgiving, then he shall offer with the sacrifice of thanksgiving unleavened cakes mingled with oil, and unleavened wafers anointed with oil, and cakes mingled with oil, of fine flour, fried.

13 Besides the cakes, he shall offer for his offering leavened bread with the sacrifice of thanksgiving of his peace offerings.

14 And of it he shall offer one out of the whole oblation for an heave offering unto the LORD, and it shall be the priest’s that sprinkleth the blood of the peace offerings.

15 And the flesh of the sacrifice of his peace offerings for thanksgiving shall be eaten the same day that it is offered; he shall not leave any of it until the morning.

16 But if the sacrifice of his offering be a vow, or a voluntary offering, it shall be eaten the same day that he offereth his sacrifice: and on the morrow also the remainder of it shall be eaten:

17 But the remainder of the flesh of the sacrifice on the third day shall be burnt with fire.

18 And if any of the flesh of the sacrifice of his peace offerings be eaten at all on the third day, it shall not be accepted, neither shall it be imputed unto him that offereth it: it shall be an abomination, and the soul that eateth of it shall bear his iniquity.

19 And the flesh that toucheth any unclean thing shall not be eaten; it shall be burnt with fire: and as for the flesh, all that be clean shall eat thereof.

20 But the soul that eateth of the flesh of the sacrifice of peace offerings, that pertain unto the LORD, having his uncleanness upon him, even that soul shall be cut off from his people.

21 Moreover the soul that shall touch any unclean thing, as the uncleanness of man, or any unclean beast, or any abominable unclean thing, and eat of the flesh of the sacrifice of peace offerings, which pertain unto the LORD, even that soul shall be cut off from his people.

22 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

23 Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, Ye shall eat no manner of fat, of ox, or of sheep, or of goat.

24 And the fat of the beast that dieth of itself, and the fat of that which is torn with beasts, may be used in any other use: but ye shall in no wise eat of it.

25 For whosoever eateth the fat of the beast, of which men offer an offering made by fire unto the LORD, even the soul that eateth it shall be cut off from his people.

26 Moreover ye shall eat no manner of blood, whether it be of fowl or of beast, in any of your dwellings.

27 Whatsoever soul it be that eateth any manner of blood, even that soul shall be cut off from his people.

28 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

29 Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, He that offereth the sacrifice of his peace offerings unto the LORD shall bring his oblation unto the LORD of the sacrifice of his peace offerings.

30 His own hands shall bring the offerings of the LORD made by fire, the fat with the breast, it shall he bring, that the breast may be waved for a wave offering before the LORD.

31 And the priest shall burn the fat upon the altar: but the breast shall be Aaron’s and his sons’.

32 And the right shoulder shall ye give unto the priest for an heave offering of the sacrifices of your peace offerings.

33 He among the sons of Aaron, that offereth the blood of the peace offerings, and the fat, shall have the right shoulder for his part.

34 For the wave breast and the heave shoulder have I taken of the children of Israel from off the sacrifices of their peace offerings, and have given them unto Aaron the priest and unto his sons by a statute for ever from among the children of Israel.

35 This is the portion of the anointing of Aaron, and of the anointing of his sons, out of the offerings of the LORD made by fire, in the day when he presented them to minister unto the LORD in the priest’s office;

36 Which the LORD commanded to be given them of the children of Israel, in the day that he anointed them, by a statute for ever throughout their generations.

37 This is the law of the burnt offering, of the meat offering, and of the sin offering, and of the trespass offering, and of the consecrations, and of the sacrifice of the peace offerings;

38 Which the LORD commanded Moses in mount Sinai, in the day that he commanded the children of Israel to offer their oblations unto the LORD, in the wilderness of Sinai.

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter completes the priests' duty laws. It gives the trespass offering's rite and the priests' portions, then the peace offering rules: a thanksgiving offering must be eaten the same day, a vow or voluntary offering may be eaten a second day, and flesh kept to the third day must be burnt. It restates the bans on eating reserved fat and blood, assigns the wave breast and heave shoulder to the priests, and closes with a summary of all the offering laws given on Mount Sinai. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Wave offering. A portion, such as the breast, waved to and fro before the LORD as devoted to God, then given to the priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Heave offering. A portion, such as the right shoulder, lifted up and given to the officiating priest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. This chapter continues the priestly duty laws for trespass offerings, which are most holy, with part consumed on the altar and part as priests' perquisite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The trespass offering is killed where the burnt offering is killed and its blood sprinkled round about the altar, under the same preliminary regulations as burnt offerings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. All its fat is offered from it, the fat tail and the fat covering the entrails, as the fatty parts are consumed on the altar as in sin and peace offerings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The kidneys with their fat and the fatty lobe above the liver are removed with the rest of the fat portion, following the established fat pattern. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The priest burns these fat portions on the altar as an offering made by fire, which is what makes it a trespass offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Every male among the priests may eat the flesh in a holy place, as the common share of the priestly order who live upon these provisions and meet at a common table. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The trespass offering follows one law with the sin offering, and the priest who makes atonement with it shall have it, as the officiating minister's fee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The officiating priest keeps for himself the skin of the burnt offering he has offered, since all flesh and fat are consumed and only the skin remains, a valuable perquisite thought to continue a patriarchal usage from Adam. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Every meat offering baked in the oven, prepared in a covered pan, or in a pan belongs to the priest who offers it, as the officiating minister's exclusive fee. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Every other meat offering, whether mixed with oil or dry, belongs to all Aaron's sons equally, as the common provision of the priestly order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The law of peace offerings now follows, with leavened bread offered besides the usual accompaniments, as thanksgiving bread common at feasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. For a thanksgiving peace offering, unleavened cakes mingled with oil, wafers anointed with oil, and cakes of fried flour are brought with the sacrifice, together with leavened bread as part of the festive thanksgiving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Leavened bread is offered with the sacrifice of thanksgiving peace offerings, because such bread is common at feasts and marks the offering as a thanksgiving meal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Of the whole oblation one cake from each kind is offered as a heave offering to the LORD, belonging to the priest who sprinkles the blood of the peace offerings, as the officiating minister's portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The flesh of a thanksgiving peace offering shall be eaten the same day it is offered, with none left until morning, keeping the feast within the day of offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. A vow or voluntary peace offering may be eaten on the day of offering and on the day following, a wider window than the thanksgiving offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. If any flesh of the sacrifice remains until the third day, it shall be burnt with fire, a prohibition thought to prevent a superstitious notion that some virtue or holiness belonged to the kept flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Flesh eaten on the third day makes the sacrifice unacceptable to God and not profitable to the offerer, since the appointed time has been violated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Flesh that has touched any unclean thing shall not be eaten but burnt with fire, while flesh that is clean may be eaten by all who are clean, preserving the holiness of the shared feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. A person unclean who eats peace-offering flesh shall be cut off from his people, that is, excluded from Israelite privileges under a sentence of excommunication. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Whoever touches an unclean thing, whether the uncleanness of man, an unclean beast, or any abominable unclean thing, some copies reading reptile, and then eats peace-offering flesh shall likewise be cut off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The prohibition of fat is now restated, as explained at Leviticus 3:17, that the reserved fat belongs to the LORD and is not to be eaten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. No fat of ox, sheep, or goat shall be eaten, since these are the sacrificial animals whose fat is reserved for the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Fat of a beast that dies of itself or is torn may be used for other purposes, but shall in no wise be eaten, maintaining the distinction between altar fat and ordinary use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Whoever eats the fat of a beast offered by fire to the LORD shall be cut off from his people, because he has taken the LORD's portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. No manner of blood shall be eaten, whether of fowl or of beast, in any dwelling, the blood prohibition resting on reverence for the atoning blood, as at Leviticus 3:17. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Whoever eats any blood shall be cut off from his people, under the same exclusion as for eating reserved fat or eating while unclean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. The priests' portion from peace offerings is now prescribed, completing the summing up of priestly privileges and duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. The offerer himself must bring his peace offering to the LORD, with his own hands, in order to show that the sacrifice is voluntary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. His own hands bring the offerings made by fire, the fat with the breast, so the breast may be waved as a wave offering before the LORD. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. The priest burns the fat on the altar, but the breast, having been waved to and fro as devoted to God, is given to Aaron and his sons generally. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. The right shoulder, or thigh, is given to the priest as a heave offering from the peace offerings, as the officiating priest's particular perquisite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. The son of Aaron who offers the blood and the fat of the peace offering shall have the right thigh for his part, since he is the officiating minister. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. The wave breast and heave shoulder are taken from Israel's peace offerings and given to Aaron and his sons by a perpetual statute, the breast for the order generally and the shoulder for the officiant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. This is the anointing portion of Aaron and his sons from the offerings made by fire, from the day Moses presented them to minister as priests, anointing here meaning office or dignity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. The LORD commanded this portion to be given them by Israel on the day He anointed them, by a statute forever throughout their generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. These verses sum up the laws of burnt offering, meat offering, sin offering, trespass offering, consecrations, and peace offering, as the general law regulating priestly privileges and duties. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. All these laws were commanded by the LORD to Moses on Mount Sinai, on the day He commanded Israel to offer their offerings in the wilderness of Sinai. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
