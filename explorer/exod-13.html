The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Sanctify unto me all the firstborn, whatsoever openeth the womb among the children of Israel, both of man and of beast: it is mine.

3 And Moses said unto the people, Remember this day, in which ye came out from Egypt, out of the house of bondage; for by strength of hand the LORD brought you out from this place: there shall no leavened bread be eaten.

4 This day came ye out in the month Abib.

5 And it shall be when the LORD shall bring thee into the land of the Canaanites, and the Hittites, and the Amorites, and the Hivites, and the Jebusites, which he sware unto thy fathers to give thee, a land flowing with milk and honey, that thou shalt keep this service in this month.

6 Seven days thou shalt eat unleavened bread, and in the seventh day shall be a feast to the LORD.

7 Unleavened bread shall be eaten seven days; and there shall no leavened bread be seen with thee, neither shall there be leaven seen with thee in all thy quarters.

8 And thou shalt shew thy son in that day, saying, This is done because of that which the LORD did unto me when I came forth out of Egypt.

9 And it shall be for a sign unto thee upon thine hand, and for a memorial between thine eyes, that the LORD’s law may be in thy mouth: for with a strong hand hath the LORD brought thee out of Egypt.

10 Thou shalt therefore keep this ordinance in his season from year to year.

11 And it shall be when the LORD shall bring thee into the land of the Canaanites, as he sware unto thee and to thy fathers, and shall give it thee,

12 That thou shalt set apart unto the LORD all that openeth the matrix, and every firstling that cometh of a beast which thou hast; the males shall be the LORD’s.

13 And every firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem with a lamb; and if thou wilt not redeem it, then thou shalt break his neck: and all the firstborn of man among thy children shalt thou redeem.

14 And it shall be when thy son asketh thee in time to come, saying, What is this? that thou shalt say unto him, By strength of hand the LORD brought us out from Egypt, from the house of bondage:

15 And it came to pass, when Pharaoh would hardly let us go, that the LORD slew all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both the firstborn of man, and the firstborn of beast: therefore I sacrifice to the LORD all that openeth the matrix, being males; but all the firstborn of my children I redeem.

16 And it shall be for a token upon thine hand, and for frontlets between thine eyes: for by strength of hand the LORD brought us forth out of Egypt.

17 And it came to pass, when Pharaoh had let the people go, that God led them not through the way of the land of the Philistines, although that was near; for God said, Lest peradventure the people repent when they see war, and they return to Egypt:

18 But God led the people about, through the way of the wilderness of the Red sea: and the children of Israel went up harnessed out of the land of Egypt.

19 And Moses took the bones of Joseph with him: for he had straitly sworn the children of Israel, saying, God will surely visit you; and ye shall carry up my bones away hence with you.

20 And they took their journey from Succoth, and encamped in Etham, in the edge of the wilderness.

21 And the LORD went before them by day in a pillar of a cloud, to lead them the way; and by night in a pillar of fire, to give them light; to go by day and night:

22 He took not away the pillar of the cloud by day, nor the pillar of fire by night, from before the people.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The firstborn of man and beast are consecrated to the LORD, and Israel is to keep the feast of unleavened bread year by year as a memorial of coming out of Egypt. God leads them not by the short road through Philistia but round by the wilderness, carrying Joseph's bones, guided by a pillar of cloud by day and fire by night. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD spake to Moses, giving the law of the firstborn before the people marched. Henry notes the law followed the judgment at once: the firstborn spared were to be claimed by the God who spared them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Sanctify unto me all the firstborn, whatsoever openeth the womb among Israel, both of man and beast, for it is mine. Henry observes the claim rested on redemption: their lives were preserved through a ransom, and ransomed lives are to be consecrated to God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Moses told the people, Remember this day, in which ye came out from Egypt, out of the house of bondage, for by strength of hand the LORD brought you out, and no leavened bread was to be eaten. Henry notes the remembrance was commanded, not left to feeling: the day of deliverance must be kept up every year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. This day came ye out in the month Abib. Henry notes the month itself was named for the season of new life, that the calendar might preach the Exodus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. When the LORD brought them into the land of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Hivites, and Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey, they were to keep this service in this month. Henry observes the ordinance was given for the land of rest, not only for the night of flight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Seven days they were to eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there would be a feast to the LORD. Henry notes the week of unleavened bread taught sincerity, and under the gospel we must not only remember Christ but observe his holy supper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Unleavened bread was to be eaten seven days, and no leavened bread was to be seen with them, nor leaven in all their quarters. Henry observes the thoroughness: leaven was not merely uneaten but unseen, for hypocrisy hidden at home spoils the feast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. They were to show their son in that day, saying, This is done because of that which the LORD did unto me when I came forth out of Egypt. Henry calls this an old law for catechising: children must be acquainted betimes with the histories of the Bible, and parents must have the story on their lips. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. It would be for a sign upon the hand and a memorial between the eyes, that the LORD's law might be in their mouth, for with a strong hand the LORD brought them out. Henry notes those who have God's law in their heart should have it in their mouth, speaking of it often to affect themselves and teach others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. They were therefore to keep this ordinance in its season from year to year. Henry observes the yearly return kept the memory from fading: old mercies must not be forgotten, that God may be praised and faith encouraged. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. When the LORD brought them into the land of the Canaanites, as he swore to them and their fathers, and gave it to them, the law of the firstborn would take effect. Henry notes the promise and the duty arrived together: the land given was the land where the firstborn were claimed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. They were to set apart unto the LORD all that openeth the matrix, and every firstling that cometh of a beast which is male, for it would be the LORD's. Henry observes the claim covered the whole firstborn order, man and beast alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Every firstling of an ass was to be redeemed with a lamb, and if not redeemed, its neck was to be broken; and all the firstborn of man among their children were to be redeemed. Henry notes the unclean beast could not be sacrificed, so it was ransomed or destroyed, a figure of souls forfeited to justice unless ransomed by Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. When a son asked in time to come, What is this? the father was to answer by the story of the Exodus. Henry observes the question was expected and the answer commanded: the institutions existed to make children ask, and parents answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. When Pharaoh would hardly let them go, the LORD slew all the firstborn in Egypt, both man and beast, therefore Israel sacrificed the males that opened the matrix and redeemed all their firstborn sons. Henry notes the reason was recited with the rite, lest the custom harden into empty form. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. It would be for a token upon the hand and for frontlets between the eyes, for by strength of hand the LORD brought them out of Egypt. Henry observes the repetition seals the point: redemption must mark both work and sight, hand and forehead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. When Pharaoh had let the people go, God led them not through the way of the Philistines, although that was near, lest seeing war they should repent and return to Egypt. Henry notes God's way is the right way though it seems about, and he proportions his people's trials to their strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. God led the people about, through the way of the wilderness of the Red Sea, and they went up harnessed out of Egypt. Henry notes they went up in good order, in ranks, in expectation of Canaan, carrying Joseph's bones with them through the desert. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Moses took the bones of Joseph with him, for Joseph had straitly sworn Israel that God would surely visit them, and they were to carry his bones up hence. Henry observes the bones were a pledge carried through the wilderness: Joseph's faith expected the promise, and Israel carried its proof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. They took their journey from Succoth and encamped in Etham, in the edge of the wilderness. Henry notes the first stages were orderly marches, each camp a step from bondage toward the sea. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. The LORD went before them by day in a pillar of cloud, to lead them the way, and by night in a pillar of fire, to give them light, that they might go by day and night. Henry notes the pillar was the appearance of the Divine Majesty itself: it sheltered by day from heat and gave light by night, and the Bible is to us what the pillar was to Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. He took not away the pillar of cloud by day, nor the pillar of fire by night, from before the people. Henry closes on the constancy: the guide that led them out never left them, and Christ will not leave nor lose his church in the wilderness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
