The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now these be the last words of David. David the son of Jesse said, and the man who was raised up on high, the anointed of the God of Jacob, and the sweet psalmist of Israel, said,

2 The Spirit of the LORD spake by me, and his word was in my tongue.

3 The God of Israel said, the Rock of Israel spake to me, He that ruleth over men must be just, ruling in the fear of God.

4 And he shall be as the light of the morning, when the sun riseth, even a morning without clouds; as the tender grass springing out of the earth by clear shining after rain.

5 Although my house be not so with God; yet he hath made with me an everlasting covenant, ordered in all things, and sure: for this is all my salvation, and all my desire, although he make it not to grow.

6 But the sons of Belial shall be all of them as thorns thrust away, because they cannot be taken with hands:

7 But the man that shall touch them must be fenced with iron and the staff of a spear; and they shall be utterly burned with fire in the same place.

8 These be the names of the mighty men whom David had: The Tachmonite that sat in the seat, chief among the captains; the same was Adino the Eznite: he lift up his spear against eight hundred, whom he slew at one time.

9 And after him was Eleazar the son of Dodo the Ahohite, one of the three mighty men with David, when they defied the Philistines that were there gathered together to battle, and the men of Israel were gone away:

10 He arose, and smote the Philistines until his hand was weary, and his hand clave unto the sword: and the LORD wrought a great victory that day; and the people returned after him only to spoil.

11 And after him was Shammah the son of Agee the Hararite. And the Philistines were gathered together into a troop, where was a piece of ground full of lentiles: and the people fled from the Philistines.

12 But he stood in the midst of the ground, and defended it, and slew the Philistines: and the LORD wrought a great victory.

13 And three of the thirty chief went down, and came to David in the harvest time unto the cave of Adullam: and the troop of the Philistines pitched in the valley of Rephaim.

14 And David was then in an hold, and the garrison of the Philistines was then in Bethlehem.

15 And David longed, and said, Oh that one would give me drink of the water of the well of Bethlehem, which is by the gate!

16 And the three mighty men brake through the host of the Philistines, and drew water out of the well of Bethlehem, that was by the gate, and took it, and brought it to David: nevertheless he would not drink thereof, but poured it out unto the LORD.

17 And he said, Be it far from me, O LORD, that I should do this: is not this the blood of the men that went in jeopardy of their lives? therefore he would not drink it. These things did these three mighty men.

18 And Abishai, the brother of Joab, the son of Zeruiah, was chief among three. And he lifted up his spear against three hundred, and slew them, and had the name among three.

19 Was he not most honourable of three? therefore he was their captain: howbeit he attained not unto the first three.

20 And Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, the son of a valiant man, of Kabzeel, who had done many acts, he slew two lionlike men of Moab: he went down also and slew a lion in the midst of a pit in time of snow:

21 And he slew an Egyptian, a goodly man: and the Egyptian had a spear in his hand; but he went down to him with a staff, and plucked the spear out of the Egyptian’s hand, and slew him with his own spear.

22 These things did Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, and had the name among three mighty men.

23 He was more honourable than the thirty, but he attained not to the first three. And David set him over his guard.

24 Asahel the brother of Joab was one of the thirty; Elhanan the son of Dodo of Bethlehem,

25 Shammah the Harodite, Elika the Harodite,

26 Helez the Paltite, Ira the son of Ikkesh the Tekoite,

27 Abiezer the Anethothite, Mebunnai the Hushathite,

28 Zalmon the Ahohite, Maharai the Netophathite,

29 Heleb the son of Baanah, a Netophathite, Ittai the son of Ribai out of Gibeah of the children of Benjamin,

30 Benaiah the Pirathonite, Hiddai of the brooks of Gaash,

31 Abialbon the Arbathite, Azmaveth the Barhumite,

32 Eliahba the Shaalbonite, of the sons of Jashen, Jonathan,

33 Shammah the Hararite, Ahiam the son of Sharar the Hararite,

34 Eliphelet the son of Ahasbai, the son of the Maachathite, Eliam the son of Ahithophel the Gilonite,

35 Hezrai the Carmelite, Paarai the Arbite,

36 Igal the son of Nathan of Zobah, Bani the Gadite,

37 Zelek the Ammonite, Nahari the Beerothite, armourbearer to Joab the son of Zeruiah,

38 Ira an Ithrite, Gareb an Ithrite,

39 Uriah the Hittite: thirty and seven in all.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

David's last words celebrate the just ruler and God's everlasting covenant with his house, and the chapter then lists his mighty men and their deeds. The long roll of names ends with Uriah the Hittite, a quiet reminder of the cost of David's greatest sin. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These are the last words of David, son of Jesse, the man raised up on high, the anointed of the God of Jacob, the sweet psalmist of Israel. Those with long experience of God's goodness, Henry says, should bear testimony to the promise when they finish their course, and these words are very worthy of regard. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The Spirit of the Lord spoke by him, and his word was in his tongue. David avows his divine inspiration, Henry notes: he and other holy men spoke and wrote as moved by the Holy Ghost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The God of Israel says, the Rock of Israel speaks: he that rules over men must be just, ruling in the fear of God. Justice joined to the fear of God is the rule laid down for every ruler, and David's last words begin with government, not with himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Such a ruler shall be as the light of the morning when the sun rises, a morning without clouds, as tender grass springing by clear shining after rain. Just rule brings the freshness of a cloudless morning after rain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Although my house is not so with God, yet he has made with David an everlasting covenant, ordered in all things and sure, all his salvation and all his desire, though God makes it not to grow. David comforts himself, Henry says, in the covenant of mercy and peace made with him as a sinner believing in the promised Saviour; it was all his salvation, and in comparison all earthly objects lost their attractions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. But the sons of Belial shall all be as thorns thrust away, because they cannot be taken with hands. The wicked are not to be handled but thrust aside, dangerous to touch. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The man who touches them must be fenced with iron and the staff of a spear, and they shall be utterly burned with fire in the same place. Judgment on the worthless is complete and consuming, not partial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. These are the names of David's mighty men: the Tachmonite who sat in the seat, chief among the captains, Adino the Eznite, who lifted his spear against eight hundred and slew them at one time. The chief's single feat sets the standard for the whole roll. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. After him Eleazar son of Dodo the Ahohite, one of the three mighty men with David when they defied the Philistines gathered to battle and Israel's men had gone away.   • Verse 10. Eleazar arose and smote the Philistines until his hand was weary and clave to the sword, and the Lord wrought a great victory, and the people returned only to spoil.   • Verse 11. After him Shammah son of Agee the Hararite, when the Philistines gathered in a troop where a piece of ground was full of lentils, and the people fled from them. One man's stand over a lentil field becomes a test of whether Israel's ground is held or abandoned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Shammah stood in the midst of the ground, defended it, slew the Philistines, and the Lord wrought a great victory. Holding one field, like holding one truth, can turn a rout into a victory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Three of the thirty chief went down to David at harvest time in the cave of Adullam, while the Philistine troop pitched in the valley of Rephaim. The chiefs seek their king in his hiding place at harvest, when the enemy holds the valley. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. David was then in the hold, and the Philistine garrison was then in Bethlehem. The king is confined while the enemy holds his own birthplace town. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. David longs and says, Oh that one would give me drink of the water of the well of Bethlehem by the gate! Henry calls it an instance of weakness: thirsty, remembering the well of his youth, he speaks without due thought, and his men take the wish as a command. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The three mighty men break through the Philistine host, draw water from Bethlehem's well by the gate, and bring it to David. Were valiant men so forward to expose themselves on the least hint of their prince's mind, Henry asks, shall we not long to approve ourselves to our Lord Jesus by ready compliance with his will?   • Verse 17. David will not drink it, but pours it out to the Lord, saying, Be it far from me; is not this the blood of men who went in jeopardy of their lives? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 19. Was he not most honourable of three, and therefore their captain, yet he attained not to the first three? Honour has degrees even among the mighty, and Abishai's place is just below the highest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Benaiah son of Jehoiada, a valiant man of Kabzeel who did many acts, slew two lionlike men of Moab, and went down and slew a lion in a pit in time of snow. The lion in the pit on a snowy day is the deed that marks a man who seeks danger rather than waiting for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. He slew an Egyptian, a goodly man, who had a spear, but Benaiah went down to him with a staff, plucked the spear from the Egyptian's hand, and slew him with his own spear. The weapon of the enemy becomes the instrument of his defeat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. These things Benaiah did, and had a name among three mighty men. The catalogue of his acts earns him a name among the three, as Abishai's did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. He was more honourable than the thirty, but attained not to the first three, and David set him over his guard. The king's own guard is entrusted to the man just below the highest rank. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
