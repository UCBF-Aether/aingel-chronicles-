The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 18 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD said unto Aaron, Thou and thy sons and thy father’s house with thee shall bear the iniquity of the sanctuary: and thou and thy sons with thee shall bear the iniquity of your priesthood.

2 And thy brethren also of the tribe of Levi, the tribe of thy father, bring thou with thee, that they may be joined unto thee, and minister unto thee: but thou and thy sons with thee shall minister before the tabernacle of witness.

3 And they shall keep thy charge, and the charge of all the tabernacle: only they shall not come nigh the vessels of the sanctuary and the altar, that neither they, nor ye also, die.

4 And they shall be joined unto thee, and keep the charge of the tabernacle of the congregation, for all the service of the tabernacle: and a stranger shall not come nigh unto you.

5 And ye shall keep the charge of the sanctuary, and the charge of the altar: that there be no wrath any more upon the children of Israel.

6 And I, behold, I have taken your brethren the Levites from among the children of Israel: to you they are given as a gift for the LORD, to do the service of the tabernacle of the congregation.

7 Therefore thou and thy sons with thee shall keep your priest’s office for everything of the altar, and within the vail; and ye shall serve: I have given your priest’s office unto you as a service of gift: and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death.

8 And the LORD spake unto Aaron, Behold, I also have given thee the charge of mine heave offerings of all the hallowed things of the children of Israel; unto thee have I given them by reason of the anointing, and to thy sons, by an ordinance for ever.

9 This shall be thine of the most holy things, reserved from the fire: every oblation of theirs, every meat offering of theirs, and every sin offering of theirs, and every trespass offering of theirs which they shall render unto me, shall be most holy for thee and for thy sons.

10 In the most holy place shalt thou eat it; every male shall eat it: it shall be holy unto thee.

11 And this is thine; the heave offering of their gift, with all the wave offerings of the children of Israel: I have given them unto thee, and to thy sons and to thy daughters with thee, by a statute for ever: every one that is clean in thy house shall eat of it.

12 All the best of the oil, and all the best of the wine, and of the wheat, the firstfruits of them which they shall offer unto the LORD, them have I given thee.

13 And whatsoever is first ripe in the land, which they shall bring unto the LORD, shall be thine; every one that is clean in thine house shall eat of it.

14 Every thing devoted in Israel shall be thine.

15 Every thing that openeth the matrix in all flesh, which they bring unto the LORD, whether it be of men or beasts, shall be thine: nevertheless the firstborn of man shalt thou surely redeem, and the firstling of unclean beasts shalt thou redeem.

16 And those that are to be redeemed from a month old shalt thou redeem, according to thine estimation, for the money of five shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary, which is twenty gerahs.

17 But the firstling of a cow, or the firstling of a sheep, or the firstling of a goat, thou shalt not redeem; they are holy: thou shalt sprinkle their blood upon the altar, and shalt burn their fat for an offering made by fire, for a sweet savour unto the LORD.

18 And the flesh of them shall be thine, as the wave breast and as the right shoulder are thine.

19 All the heave offerings of the holy things, which the children of Israel offer unto the LORD, have I given thee, and thy sons and thy daughters with thee, by a statute for ever: it is a covenant of salt for ever before the LORD unto thee and to thy seed with thee.

20 And the LORD spake unto Aaron, Thou shalt have no inheritance in their land, neither shalt thou have any part among them: I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel.

21 And, behold, I have given the children of Levi all the tenth in Israel for an inheritance, for their service which they serve, even the service of the tabernacle of the congregation.

22 Neither must the children of Israel henceforth come nigh the tabernacle of the congregation, lest they bear sin, and die.

23 But the Levites shall do the service of the tabernacle of the congregation, and they shall bear their iniquity: it shall be a statute for ever throughout your generations, that among the children of Israel they have no inheritance.

24 But the tithes of the children of Israel, which they offer as an heave offering unto the LORD, I have given to the Levites to inherit: therefore I have said unto them, Among the children of Israel they shall have no inheritance.

25 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

26 Thus speak unto the Levites, and say unto them, When ye take of the children of Israel the tithes which I have given you from them for your inheritance, then ye shall offer up an heave offering of it for the LORD, even a tenth part of the tithe.

27 And this your heave offering shall be reckoned unto you, as though it were the corn of the threshingfloor, and as the fulness of the winepress.

28 Thus ye also shall offer an heave offering unto the LORD of all your tithes, which ye receive of the children of Israel; and ye shall give thereof the LORD’s heave offering to Aaron the priest.

29 Out of all your gifts ye shall offer every heave offering of the LORD, of all the best thereof, even the hallowed part thereof out of it.

30 Therefore thou shalt say unto them, When ye have heaved the best thereof from it, then it shall be counted unto the Levites as the increase of the threshingfloor, and as the increase of the winepress.

31 And ye shall eat it in every place, ye and your households: for it is your reward for your service in the tabernacle of the congregation.

32 And ye shall bear no sin by reason of it, when ye have heaved from it the best of it: neither shall ye pollute the holy things of the children of Israel, lest ye die.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter defines what priests and Levites bear and what they receive. Aaron, his sons, and his father's house bear the iniquity of the sanctuary and of the priesthood, and the Levites are given to them as a gift to keep the charge of the tabernacle, while no stranger may come near. In return the priests receive the heave offerings, firstfruits, devoted things, and firstborn redeemed at five shekels, and the Levites receive all the tithes of Israel, from which they in turn offer a tithe of the best to Aaron. The chapter closes by warning that no priest or Levite may pollute the holy things or bear sin and die. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The Lord said to Aaron: you, your sons and your father's house with you shall bear the iniquity of the sanctuary, and you and your sons shall bear the iniquity of your priesthood. Henry notes Aaron would see reason not to be proud of his preferment when he considered the great care and charge upon him: be not high-minded, but fear, for the greater the trust committed, the greater the danger of betraying it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Your brethren of Levi, the tribe of your father, bring with you, that they may be joined to you and minister to you, while you and your sons minister before the tabernacle of witness. The Levites were joined to Aaron's house as helpers, not rivals: their ministry supported the priesthood it did not replace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. They shall keep your charge and the charge of all the tabernacle, only they shall not come nigh the vessels of the sanctuary and the altar, that neither they nor you die. The boundary protected both sides: Levites who overstepped it endangered priests as well as themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They shall be joined to you, and keep the charge of the tabernacle of the congregation, and no stranger shall come nigh to you. The joining was permanent and exclusive: the tabernacle's charge belonged to Levi alone, and strangers were kept from it by death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. You shall keep the charge of the sanctuary and the charge of the altar, that there be no wrath any more upon the children of Israel. The priests' charge was itself Israel's protection: faithful keeping at sanctuary and altar kept wrath from the whole people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. I, behold, I have taken your brethren the Levites from among Israel, to you they are given as a gift for the Lord, to do the service of the tabernacle. The Levites were God's gift twice over: taken by God, then given to Aaron, so that their service was grace, not a tribal claim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Therefore you and your sons with you shall keep your priest's office for everything of the altar and within the veil, and you shall serve: I have given your priest's office to you as a service of gift, and the stranger that comes nigh shall be put to death. Henry calls the priest's service a warfare, and asks who goes a warfare at his own charges: the office was a gift, and its boundary was death, because God's institutions must not be invaded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Behold, I also have given you the charge of my heave offerings, of all the hallowed things of Israel: to you have I given them by reason of the anointing, and to your sons, by an ordinance for ever. Henry notes the provision was given by an ordinance for ever, that the lamp might not go out for want of oil, and so a gospel ministry should continue till Christ comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. This shall be yours of the most holy things, reserved from the fire: every oblation of theirs, every meat offering, every sin offering, and every trespass offering, which they render to me, shall be most holy for you and for your sons. The priests lived on the most holy portion itself: what was reserved from the altar fire became their food, because they served at the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. In the most holy place shall you eat it; every male shall eat it: it shall be holy to you. The eating was itself holy and restricted: only males of the priestly house, and only in the most holy place, so that provision never became common use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And this is yours: the heave offering of their gift, with all the wave offerings of Israel, I have given to you, and to your sons and daughters with you, by a statute for ever, and everyone clean in your house shall eat of it. The heave and wave offerings, unlike the most holy things, fed the whole priestly household, sons and daughters, if clean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. All the best of the oil, and all the best of the wine, and of the wheat, the firstfruits of them which they offer to the Lord, them have I given you. Henry stresses the best should be offered for firstfruits: those who think to save by putting God off with the refuse deceive themselves, for God is not mocked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Whatsoever is first ripe in the land, which they bring to the Lord, shall be yours, and everyone clean in your house shall eat of it. The first ripe fruit, like the best oil and wine, went to the priests, so that God's portion was first in time as well as in quality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Everything devoted in Israel shall be yours. Devoted things, given wholly to the Lord without redemption, passed to his priests, the human hands of the divine ownership. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Everything that opens the matrix in all flesh, which they bring to the Lord, whether of men or beasts, shall be yours: nevertheless the firstborn of man you shall surely redeem, and the firstling of unclean beasts you shall redeem. The firstborn belonged to God by the exodus claim, but man and unclean beast were redeemed rather than sacrificed, while clean firstlings were offered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Those to be redeemed from a month old you shall redeem, according to your estimation, for five shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary, which is twenty gerahs. The redemption price was fixed and early: a month old, five sanctuary shekels, so that no priest could bargain over the firstborn's ransom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. But the firstling of a cow, or a sheep, or a goat, you shall not redeem: they are holy: you shall sprinkle their blood on the altar, and burn their fat for an offering made by fire, for a sweet savour to the Lord. Clean firstlings were not redeemed because they were holy in themselves, fit for altar blood and fire, not for ransom money. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And the flesh of them shall be yours, as the wave breast and as the right shoulder are yours. After blood and fat went to the altar, the flesh fed the priests, on the same pattern as the peace offering's breast and shoulder. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. All the heave offerings of the holy things, which Israel offers to the Lord, have I given to you and your sons and daughters with you, by a statute for ever: it is a covenant of salt for ever before the Lord to you and your seed with you.   • Verse 20. You shall have no inheritance in their land, neither shall you have any part among them: I am your part and your inheritance among the children of Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

The tithe was the inheritance in place of land: God, whose the whole is, gave a tenth of all the land's produce to the tribe that owned no land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
