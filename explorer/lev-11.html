The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 11 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses and to Aaron, saying unto them,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, These are the beasts which ye shall eat among all the beasts that are on the earth.

3 Whatsoever parteth the hoof, and is clovenfooted, and cheweth the cud, among the beasts, that shall ye eat.

4 Nevertheless these shall ye not eat of them that chew the cud, or of them that divide the hoof: as the camel, because he cheweth the cud, but divideth not the hoof; he is unclean unto you.

5 And the coney, because he cheweth the cud, but divideth not the hoof; he is unclean unto you.

6 And the hare, because he cheweth the cud, but divideth not the hoof; he is unclean unto you.

7 And the swine, though he divide the hoof, and be clovenfooted, yet he cheweth not the cud; he is unclean to you.

8 Of their flesh shall ye not eat, and their carcase shall ye not touch; they are unclean to you.

9 These shall ye eat of all that are in the waters: whatsoever hath fins and scales in the waters, in the seas, and in the rivers, them shall ye eat.

10 And all that have not fins and scales in the seas, and in the rivers, of all that move in the waters, and of any living thing which is in the waters, they shall be an abomination unto you:

11 They shall be even an abomination unto you; ye shall not eat of their flesh, but ye shall have their carcases in abomination.

12 Whatsoever hath no fins nor scales in the waters, that shall be an abomination unto you.

13 And these are they which ye shall have in abomination among the fowls; they shall not be eaten, they are an abomination: the eagle, and the ossifrage, and the ospray,

14 And the vulture, and the kite after his kind;

15 Every raven after his kind;

16 And the owl, and the night hawk, and the cuckow, and the hawk after his kind,

17 And the little owl, and the cormorant, and the great owl,

18 And the swan, and the pelican, and the gier eagle,

19 And the stork, the heron after her kind, and the lapwing, and the bat.

20 All fowls that creep, going upon all four, shall be an abomination unto you.

21 Yet these may ye eat of every flying creeping thing that goeth upon all four, which have legs above their feet, to leap withal upon the earth;

22 Even these of them ye may eat; the locust after his kind, and the bald locust after his kind, and the beetle after his kind, and the grasshopper after his kind.

23 But all other flying creeping things, which have four feet, shall be an abomination unto you.

24 And for these ye shall be unclean: whosoever toucheth the carcase of them shall be unclean until the even.

25 And whosoever beareth ought of the carcase of them shall wash his clothes, and be unclean until the even.

26 The carcases of every beast which divideth the hoof, and is not clovenfooted, nor cheweth the cud, are unclean unto you: every one that toucheth them shall be unclean.

27 And whatsoever goeth upon his paws, among all manner of beasts that go on all four, those are unclean unto you: whoso toucheth their carcase shall be unclean until the even.

28 And he that beareth the carcase of them shall wash his clothes, and be unclean until the even: they are unclean unto you.

29 These also shall be unclean unto you among the creeping things that creep upon the earth; the weasel, and the mouse, and the tortoise after his kind,

30 And the ferret, and the chameleon, and the lizard, and the snail, and the mole.

31 These are unclean to you among all that creep: whosoever doth touch them, when they be dead, shall be unclean until the even.

32 And upon whatsoever any of them, when they are dead, doth fall, it shall be unclean; whether it be any vessel of wood, or raiment, or skin, or sack, whatsoever vessel it be, wherein any work is done, it must be put into water, and it shall be unclean until the even; so it shall be cleansed.

33 And every earthen vessel, whereinto any of them falleth, whatsoever is in it shall be unclean; and ye shall break it.

34 Of all meat which may be eaten, that on which such water cometh shall be unclean: and all drink that may be drunk in every such vessel shall be unclean.

35 And every thing whereupon any part of their carcase falleth shall be unclean; whether it be oven, or ranges for pots, they shall be broken down: for they are unclean and shall be unclean unto you.

36 Nevertheless a fountain or pit, wherein there is plenty of water, shall be clean: but that which toucheth their carcase shall be unclean.

37 And if any part of their carcase fall upon any sowing seed which is to be sown, it shall be clean.

38 But if any water be put upon the seed, and any part of their carcase fall thereon, it shall be unclean unto you.

39 And if any beast, of which ye may eat, die; he that toucheth the carcase thereof shall be unclean until the even.

40 And he that eateth of the carcase of it shall wash his clothes, and be unclean until the even: he also that beareth the carcase of it shall wash his clothes, and be unclean until the even.

41 And every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth shall be an abomination; it shall not be eaten.

42 Whatsoever goeth upon the belly, and whatsoever goeth upon all four, or whatsoever hath more feet among all creeping things that creep upon the earth, them ye shall not eat; for they are an abomination.

43 Ye shall not make yourselves abominable with any creeping thing that creepeth, neither shall ye make yourselves unclean with them, that ye should be defiled thereby.

44 For I am the LORD your God: ye shall therefore sanctify yourselves, and ye shall be holy; for I am holy: neither shall ye defile yourselves with any manner of creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.

45 For I am the LORD that bringeth you up out of the land of Egypt, to be your God: ye shall therefore be holy, for I am holy.

46 This is the law of the beasts, and of the fowl, and of every living creature that moveth in the waters, and of every creature that creepeth upon the earth:

47 To make a difference between the unclean and the clean, and between the beast that may be eaten and the beast that may not be eaten.

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter sets out which creatures may be eaten and which are unclean. Land animals must both part the hoof and chew the cud; water creatures must have fins and scales; birds of prey and carrion-feeders are excluded, while leaping insects such as the locust may be eaten. It then gives the rules for contact with carcasses, for vessels, food, and seed, and for washing and being unclean until evening, with the stated purpose of distinguishing clean from unclean and of keeping Israel holy, as the LORD is holy. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Cheweth the cud. Ruminating: bringing food back up to chew again, one of the two marks, with a parted cloven hoof, of a clean land animal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Clovenfooted. Having a hoof fully parted into two; the camel is excluded because its division is not complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. These laws are addressed to both Moses and Aaron, civil and ecclesiastical rulers, indicating their twofold religious and sanitary purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Israel is told which beasts may be eaten, the distinction of meats serving above all to keep them a distinct and peculiar people, preventing familiarity with neighbouring peoples' idolatries and vices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Only beasts that both part the hoof, being cloven-footed, and chew the cud may be eaten, since ruminants digest food more fully and pass off poisonous properties through the salivary glands, while the parted hoof discharges foetid matter between the toes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The camel is excluded because, though its foot divides to a certain extent into two large parts, the division is not complete, its toes resting on an elastic pad, and its flesh is tough as a beast of burden; its prohibition also keeps Israel apart from Ishmael's descendants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The coney, not the rabbit but the hyrax of the rock clefts, is unclean because it only appears to chew the cud and does not divide the hoof. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The hare is unclean for the same reason, only appearing to ruminate by working its jaws, not being cloven-footed, and being subject to vermin and poisonous plants eaten to expel them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The swine, though it divides the hoof, does not chew the cud, lacking that natural provision for purifying its system, and in hot climates its flesh is liable to produce leprosy, scurvy, and eruptions; its prohibition also guards against neighbouring idolatries. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Neither the flesh of these unclean beasts shall be eaten nor their carcasses touched, since both eating and contact defile under the distinction of meats. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. In the waters, whatever has fins and scales may be eaten, fins and scales being the means by which a fish's excrescences are carried off, as by perspiration in animals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Whatever lacks fins and scales in seas or rivers, of all that move in the waters, is an abomination, since such creatures in hot climates cause malignant disorders and often prove mortal poison. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. These finless and scaleless creatures shall be an abomination, their flesh not eaten and their carcasses held in abhorrence, under the same sanitary and religious distinction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Whatever has no fins nor scales in the waters is an abomination, a class including frogs, eels, and shellfish of all descriptions, many of which are suspected of a poisonous nature when plentifully eaten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Among fowls, birds of prey and carrion-feeders are an abomination and shall not be eaten, no fewer than twenty species then known being listed, implying that unlisted birds, those subsisting on vegetable matter, are allowed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The vulture, here probably the kite or glede with its darting and hovering flight, and the kite after his kind are unclean, the prohibition extending to the whole species, since they devour carrion and putrefying flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Every raven after his kind, including crow and pie, is unclean as a carrion bird within the birds-of-prey class. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The owl, generally taken for the ostrich, the nighthawk with its nocturnal habits, the cuckoo, evidently another rapacious bird, and the hawk after his kind, including every falcon variety, are unclean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The little or horned owl, the cormorant supposed to be the gull, and the great owl, according to some the Egyptian ibis, are unclean within the same rapacious and aquatic grouping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The swan, frequenting marshy places and held sacred by Egyptians in heathen temples, the pelican with its pouch for catching and holding food, and the gier eagle, perhaps the kingfisher or the Egyptian vulture feeding on carrion, are unclean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The stork, declared unclean probably because it feeds on serpents and venomous reptiles, the heron, an aquatic bird of uncertain exact species, the lapwing or hoopoe, filthy from feeding on insects and worms, and the bat, noted for voracity and filthiness, are unclean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. All winged creeping things going upon all four are an abomination, fowls here meaning all winged creatures, including insects, reptiles, and worms with more than four feet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Flying creeping things with legs above their feet to leap may be eaten, a description that fits the locust exactly, which Arabs fry in olive oil and which, salted, dried, and fried, tastes not unlike red herrings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The locust, bald locust, beetle, and grasshopper after their kinds may be eaten, as lawful food within the leaping-insect exception. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. All other flying creeping things with four feet are an abomination, since they lack the leaping legs that mark the permitted locusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. For these creatures Israel shall be unclean, and whoever touches their carcass shall be unclean until evening, the touch rule enforcing cleanliness in a land where venomous reptiles creep into houses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Whoever bears any part of such a carcass shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening, the washing removing both ceremonial defilement and noxious matter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. Every beast that divides the hoof but is not cloven-footed nor chews the cud is unclean, a class including single-hoofed beasts like horses and asses and pawed beasts like lions and tigers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Whatever goes upon its paws among beasts that go on all four is unclean, and whoever touches its carcass is unclean until evening, under the same pawed-beast rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. He who bears the carcass of a pawed beast shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening, as with other unclean carcasses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Among creeping things on the earth, the weasel, rather the mole, the mouse, placed among reptiles for its diminutive size, and the tortoise, a lizard with hard pointed tail scales, are unclean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. The ferret, thought by some the newt or chameleon, the chameleon called warral by Arabs, the lizard, the snail, a sand lizard called chulca, and the mole, another lizard species, are unclean. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. These creeping things are unclean, and whoever touches them when dead shall be unclean until evening, a regulation that, though it often excluded people suddenly from society and worship, enforced attention to cleanliness against venom and noxious matter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. Whatever a dead creeping thing falls upon, whether vessel of wood, raiment, skin, or sack, shall be unclean, since a carcass might fall inadvertently on clothes, skin bottles, or domestic articles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. An earthen vessel into which one falls is wholly unclean and shall be broken, the porous vessel being unable to be cleansed of the defilement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. All food on which such water comes shall be unclean, and all drink in such a vessel shall be unclean, extending the carcass defilement through water to food and drink. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Everything on which part of their carcass falls, oven or ranges for pots, shall be unclean and broken down, the defilement reaching the very cooking vessels. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. A fountain or pit with plenty of water nevertheless remains clean, though what touches the carcass in it is unclean, since running water is not defiled as a vessel is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. Sowing seed on which part of a carcass falls remains clean, dry seed being protected from the defilement that water would carry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. If water has been put on the seed and a carcass falls on it, it shall be unclean, the water making the seed receptive to defilement as in the vessel rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. If a beast that may be eaten dies, whoever touches its carcass shall be unclean until evening, even though the animal itself was clean for food. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. He who eats of its carcass or bears it shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening, the same washing and evening limit as for unclean carcasses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Every creeping thing on the earth is an abomination and shall not be eaten, restating the whole creeping class under the distinction of meats. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. Whatever goes on its belly, on all four, or on many feet among creeping things shall not be eaten, for they are an abomination. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. Israel shall not make themselves abominable or unclean with any creeping thing, lest they be defiled by them, the food distinction being a guard against defilement itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. For the LORD is their God, therefore they shall sanctify themselves and be holy, for He is holy, and shall not defile themselves with creeping things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. The LORD who brought them up out of Egypt to be their God requires holiness, for He is holy, the exodus grounding the food laws in covenant identity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. This is the law of beasts, fowl, water creatures, and creeping things, the whole chapter being one law of distinction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. Its purpose is to make a difference between unclean and clean, and between the beast that may be eaten and that which may not, laws that probably gave divine sanction to ancient usages, some prohibited on physiological grounds and others for unwholesomeness in warm countries or connection with contemporary idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 40. He who eats of its carcass or bears it shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening, the same washing and evening limit as for unclean carcasses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. Every creeping thing on the earth is an abomination and shall not be eaten, restating the whole creeping class under the distinction of meats. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. Whatever goes on its belly, on all four, or on many feet among creeping things shall not be eaten, for they are an abomination. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 43. Israel shall not make themselves abominable or unclean with any creeping thing, lest they be defiled by them, the food distinction being a guard against defilement itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 44. For the LORD is their God, therefore they shall sanctify themselves and be holy, for He is holy, and shall not defile themselves with creeping things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 45. The LORD who brought them up out of Egypt to be their God requires holiness, for He is holy, the exodus grounding the food laws in covenant identity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 46. This is the law of beasts, fowl, water creatures, and creeping things, the whole chapter being one law of distinction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 47. Its purpose is to make a difference between unclean and clean, and between the beast that may be eaten and that which may not, laws that probably gave divine sanction to ancient usages, some prohibited on physiological grounds and others for unwholesomeness in warm countries or connection with contemporary idolatry.
