The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Corinthians 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Moreover, brethren, we do you to wit of the grace of God bestowed on the churches of Macedonia;

2 How that in a great trial of affliction the abundance of their joy and their deep poverty abounded unto the riches of their liberality.

3 For to their power, I bear record, yea, and beyond their power they were willing of themselves;

4 Praying us with much intreaty that we would receive the gift, and take upon us the fellowship of the ministering to the saints.

5 And this they did, not as we hoped, but first gave their own selves to the Lord, and unto us by the will of God.

6 Insomuch that we desired Titus, that as he had begun, so he would also finish in you the same grace also.

7 Therefore, as ye abound in every thing, in faith, and utterance, and knowledge, and in all diligence, and in your love to us, see that ye abound in this grace also.

8 I speak not by commandment, but by occasion of the forwardness of others, and to prove the sincerity of your love.

9 For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich.

10 And herein I give my advice: for this is expedient for you, who have begun before, not only to do, but also to be forward a year ago.

11 Now therefore perform the doing of it; that as there was a readiness to will, so there may be a performance also out of that which ye have.

12 For if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not.

13 For I mean not that other men be eased, and ye burdened:

14 But by an equality, that now at this time your abundance may be a supply for their want, that their abundance also may be a supply for your want: that there may be equality:

15 As it is written, He that had gathered much had nothing over; and he that had gathered little had no lack.

16 But thanks be to God, which put the same earnest care into the heart of Titus for you.

17 For indeed he accepted the exhortation; but being more forward, of his own accord he went unto you.

18 And we have sent with him the brother, whose praise is in the gospel throughout all the churches;

19 And not that only, but who was also chosen of the churches to travel with us with this grace, which is administered by us to the glory of the same Lord, and declaration of your ready mind:

20 Avoiding this, that no man should blame us in this abundance which is administered by us:

21 Providing for honest things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men.

22 And we have sent with them our brother, whom we have oftentimes proved diligent in many things, but now much more diligent, upon the great confidence which I have in you.

23 Whether any do enquire of Titus, he is my partner and fellowhelper concerning you: or our brethren be enquired of, they are the messengers of the churches, and the glory of Christ.

24 Wherefore shew ye to them, and before the churches, the proof of your love, and of our boasting on your behalf.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul holds up the Macedonian churches, who in deep poverty abounded in liberality for the Jerusalem saints, and urges Corinth, abundant in faith, utterance, knowledge, and love, to abound in this grace also. He sends Titus and two proven brothers to administer the collection honestly, so that no one can blame them, and asks Corinth to show before the churches the proof of their love and of his boasting about them. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Moreover, brethren, we do you to wit of the grace of God bestowed on the churches of Macedonia. Henry notes Paul makes the Macedonians' giving known as grace, not as human generosity alone: God bestowed it, and Corinth is told so that grace may stir grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. How that in a great trial of affliction the abundance of their joy and their deep poverty abounded unto the riches of their liberality. JFB notes the paradox: affliction and deep poverty, met by abundant joy, abound into rich liberality, as if poverty itself gave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For to their power, I bear record, yea, and beyond their power they were willing of themselves. Henry notes the giving passed both measure and compulsion: to their power, beyond their power, and willing of themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Praying us with much intreaty that we would receive the gift, and take upon us the fellowship of the ministering to the saints. JFB notes the begging is reversed: the poor beg to be allowed to give, counting the ministry to the saints a fellowship, not a burden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. And this they did, not as we hoped, but first gave their own selves to the Lord, and unto us by the will of God. Henry notes the order explains everything: self-giving to the Lord first, then to Paul by God's will, and only then the gift of money. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Insomuch that we desired Titus, that as he had begun, so he would also finish in you the same grace also. JFB notes Titus, who began the collection at Corinth, is asked to finish it, so that Corinth's grace matches Macedonia's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Therefore, as ye abound in every thing, in faith, and utterance, and knowledge, and in all diligence, and in your love to us, see that ye abound in this grace also. Henry notes Corinth abounds in gifts of speech and knowledge, and Paul asks that giving abound alongside them, lest the finest gifts lack the grace of liberality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. I speak not by commandment, but by occasion of the forwardness of others, and to prove the sincerity of your love. JFB notes the collection is not commanded: others' forwardness is the occasion, and the sincerity of Corinth's love is what will be proved. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich. Henry notes the motive is Christ's own poverty: rich, he became poor for our sakes, that through his poverty we might be rich, and giving follows that pattern. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. And herein I give my advice: for this is expedient for you, who have begun before, not only to do, but also to be forward a year ago. JFB notes advice, not command, and expediency: Corinth began a year ago, both in doing and in willingness, and finishing suits beginners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Now therefore perform the doing of it; that as there was a readiness to will, so there may be a performance also out of that which ye have. Henry notes readiness to will must become performance, and performance is measured by what a man has, not by what he wishes he had. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. For if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man has, and not according to that he has not. JFB notes the rule of acceptance: a willing mind, and a gift proportioned to what is possessed, not to what is lacking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. For I mean not that other men be eased, and ye burdened. Henry notes the collection is not meant to transfer burden: easing Jerusalem must not mean burdening Corinth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. But by an equality, that now at this time your abundance may be a supply for their want, that their abundance also may be a supply for your want: that there may be equality. JFB notes equality is the aim across time: now Corinth's abundance supplies Jerusalem's want, and another day Jerusalem's abundance may supply Corinth's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. As it is written, He that had gathered much had nothing over; and he that had gathered little had no lack. Henry notes the manna pattern: gathering much left nothing over, gathering little left no lack, and the collection aims at that same equality. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. But thanks be to God, which put the same earnest care into the heart of Titus for you. JFB notes Titus's care is God's gift, the same earnest care Paul himself feels, placed by God in Titus's heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. For indeed he accepted the exhortation; but being more forward, of his own accord he went unto you. Henry notes Titus does more than accept: of his own accord, more forward than exhortation required, he goes to Corinth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. And we have sent with him the brother, whose praise is in the gospel throughout all the churches. JFB notes the unnamed brother is praised in the gospel in all the churches, a reputation that vouches for the collection's handling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. And not that only, but who was also chosen of the churches to travel with us with this grace, which is administered by us to the glory of the same Lord, and declaration of your ready mind. Henry notes the brother is church-chosen, not Paul-chosen, so the administration is accountable to the churches, to the Lord's glory and to Corinth's ready mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Avoiding this, that no man should blame us in this abundance which is administered by us. JFB notes the precaution: abundance handled by one man invites blame, so Paul multiplies witnesses to avoid even the appearance of misuse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Providing for honest things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men. Henry notes honesty must be visible twice over: before God and before men, since the collection's credit depends on both. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. And we have sent with them our brother, whom we have oftentimes proved diligent in many things, but now much more diligent, upon the great confidence which I have in you. JFB notes a third brother is added, proved diligent often, and now more diligent still, because confidence in Corinth spurs him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Whether any do enquire of Titus, he is my partner and fellowhelper concerning you: or our brethren be enquired of, they are the messengers of the churches, and the glory of Christ. Henry notes the credentials: Titus is Paul's partner, the brethren are the churches' messengers and Christ's glory, and either title answers enquiry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Wherefore show ye to them, and before the churches, the proof of your love, and of our boasting on your behalf. JFB notes the proof is public: shown to the brethren and before the churches, it proves both Corinth's love and Paul's boasting true. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • Macedonia. The region whose churches, though poor and afflicted, gave beyond their power for the saints. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • liberality. Generous giving; Paul says the Macedonians' deep poverty abounded to the riches of their liberality. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • equality. Paul's aim for the collection: present abundance supplying present lack, so that there may be fairness. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
