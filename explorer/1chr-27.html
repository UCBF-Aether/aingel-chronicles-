The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 27 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the children of Israel after their number, to wit, the chief fathers and captains of thousands and hundreds, and their officers that served the king in any matter of the courses, which came in and went out month by month throughout all the months of the year, of every course were twenty and four thousand.

2 Over the first course for the first month was Jashobeam the son of Zabdiel: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

3 Of the children of Perez was the chief of all the captains of the host for the first month.

4 And over the course of the second month was Dodai an Ahohite, and of his course was Mikloth also the ruler: in his course likewise were twenty and four thousand.

5 The third captain of the host for the third month was Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, a chief priest: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

6 This is that Benaiah, who was mighty among the thirty, and above the thirty: and in his course was Ammizabad his son.

7 The fourth captain for the fourth month was Asahel the brother of Joab, and Zebadiah his son after him: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

8 The fifth captain for the fifth month was Shamhuth the Izrahite: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

9 The sixth captain for the sixth month was Ira the son of Ikkesh the Tekoite: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

10 The seventh captain for the seventh month was Helez the Pelonite, of the children of Ephraim: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

11 The eighth captain for the eighth month was Sibbecai the Hushathite, of the Zarhites: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

12 The ninth captain for the ninth month was Abiezer the Anetothite, of the Benjamites: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

13 The tenth captain for the tenth month was Maharai the Netophathite, of the Zarhites: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

14 The eleventh captain for the eleventh month was Benaiah the Pirathonite, of the children of Ephraim: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

15 The twelfth captain for the twelfth month was Heldai the Netophathite, of Othniel: and in his course were twenty and four thousand.

16 Furthermore over the tribes of Israel: the ruler of the Reubenites was Eliezer the son of Zichri: of the Simeonites, Shephatiah the son of Maachah:

17 Of the Levites, Hashabiah the son of Kemuel: of the Aaronites, Zadok:

18 Of Judah, Elihu, one of the brethren of David: of Issachar, Omri the son of Michael:

19 Of Zebulun, Ishmaiah the son of Obadiah: of Naphtali, Jerimoth the son of Azriel:

20 Of the children of Ephraim, Hoshea the son of Azaziah: of the half tribe of Manasseh, Joel the son of Pedaiah:

21 Of the half tribe of Manasseh in Gilead, Iddo the son of Zechariah: of Benjamin, Jaasiel the son of Abner:

22 Of Dan, Azareel the son of Jeroham. These were the princes of the tribes of Israel.

23 But David took not the number of them from twenty years old and under: because the LORD had said he would increase Israel like to the stars of the heavens.

24 Joab the son of Zeruiah began to number, but he finished not, because there fell wrath for it against Israel; neither was the number put in the account of the chronicles of king David.

25 And over the king’s treasures was Azmaveth the son of Adiel: and over the storehouses in the fields, in the cities, and in the villages, and in the castles, was Jehonathan the son of Uzziah:

26 And over them that did the work of the field for tillage of the ground was Ezri the son of Chelub:

27 And over the vineyards was Shimei the Ramathite: over the increase of the vineyards for the wine cellars was Zabdi the Shiphmite:

28 And over the olive trees and the sycomore trees that were in the low plains was Baalhanan the Gederite: and over the cellars of oil was Joash:

29 And over the herds that fed in Sharon was Shitrai the Sharonite: and over the herds that were in the valleys was Shaphat the son of Adlai:

30 Over the camels also was Obil the Ishmaelite: and over the asses was Jehdeiah the Meronothite:

31 And over the flocks was Jaziz the Hagerite. All these were the rulers of the substance which was king David’s.

32 Also Jonathan David’s uncle was a counsellor, a wise man, and a scribe: and Jehiel the son of Hachmoni was with the king’s sons:

33 And Ahithophel was the king’s counsellor: and Hushai the Archite was the king’s companion:

34 And after Ahithophel was Jehoiada the son of Benaiah, and Abiathar: and the general of the king’s army was Joab.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter lists the army's monthly courses of twenty-four thousand, the princes of the tribes, David's stewards over his substance, and his counsellors. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Now the children of Israel after their number, to wit, the chief fathers and captains of thousands and hundreds, and their officers that served the king in any matter of the courses, which came in and went out month by month throughout all the months of the year, of every course were twenty and four thousand. Henry praises David's prudent model: not a standing army burdening the country, nor disbandment leaving the kingdom naked, but 24,000 constantly in arms, changed monthly, totalling 288,000 trained militia. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Over the first course for the first month was Jashobeam the son of Zabdiel, and in his course were twenty and four thousand. Henry notes the twelve commanders were David's worthies, first signalised by great actions, then advanced: honour attending merit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And over the course of the second month was Dodai an Ahohite, and of his course was Mikloth also the ruler. Henry notes Dodai had Mikloth as substitute when absent or infirm, or successor when dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The third captain of the host for the third month was Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, a chief priest, and in his course were twenty and four thousand. Henry notes cohen can mean priest or prince, and here better chief ruler or principal officer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. This is that Benaiah, who was mighty among the thirty, and above the thirty, and in his course was Ammizabad his son. Henry notes Benaiah had his son under him in his course. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The fourth captain for the fourth month was Asahel the brother of Joab, and Zebadiah his son after him, and in his course were twenty and four thousand. Henry notes Asahel's son succeeding him shows the militia plan was laid in David's early reign, since Asahel was killed while David reigned in Hebron, then revived after the wars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Furthermore over the tribes of Israel: the ruler of the Reubenites was Eliezer, and of the Simeonites, Shephatiah. Henry notes Moses's ancient order continued, every tribe with its prince, whether by election or succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Of Judah, Elihu, one of the brethren of David. Henry notes Elihu, or Eliab, prince of Judah, was Jesse's eldest son, descended from Nahshon and Salmon, princes in Moses's time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Of Benjamin, Jaasiel the son of Abner. Henry notes though Abner opposed David's throne, David did not oppose his son's preferment, perhaps nominating him himself, teaching us to render good for evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. But David took not the number of them from twenty years old and under, because the Lord had said he would increase Israel like to the stars of the heavens. Henry notes David forbade numbering under twenty, thinking to save reflection on the promise of innumerability, yet it was a poor salvo, since under-twenty were never customarily numbered. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Joab the son of Zeruiah began to number, but he finished not, because there fell wrath for it against Israel, neither was the number put in the account of the chronicles of king David. Henry notes the prideful numbering turned to no good account: never perfected, never exact, never recorded as authentic, for Joab did it by halves and David was ashamed of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And over the king's treasures was Azmaveth the son of Adiel, and over the storehouses in the fields, cities, villages, and castles, was Jehonathan the son of Uzziah. Henry introduces the court officers as rulers of the king's substance, all for service, not state or sport, in the simplicity of those times. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And over the flocks was Jaziz the Hagerite. All these were the rulers of the substance which was king David's. Henry sums up the substance officers: David, great soldier, scholar, and prince, was yet a great husband of his estate, stocked for profit not pleasure, since the king himself is served of the field. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Also Jonathan David's uncle was a counsellor, a wise man, and a scribe, and Jehiel the son of Hachmoni was with the king's sons. Henry notes David's attendants were eminent for wisdom: his uncle, wise and scripturally read, was counsellor, and another excelled in learning as tutor to his children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. And Ahithophel was the king's counsellor, and Hushai the Archite was the king's companion. Henry distinguishes Ahithophel, very cunning, as counsellor, from Hushai, an honest man, as companion and confidant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. And after Ahithophel was Jehoiada the son of Benaiah, and Abiathar, and the general of the king's army was Joab. Henry closes the court list with Jehoiada and Abiathar succeeding Ahithophel, and Joab as general, noting much of a prince's wisdom is seen in choosing his ministry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • courses. Monthly divisions of the army, each of twenty-four thousand, coming in and going out month by month through the year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
