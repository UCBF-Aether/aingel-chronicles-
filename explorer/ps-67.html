The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 67 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 God be merciful unto us, and bless us; and cause his face to shine upon us; Selah.

2 That thy way may be known upon earth, thy saving health among all nations.

3 Let the people praise thee, O God; let all the people praise thee.

4 O let the nations be glad and sing for joy: for thou shalt judge the people righteously, and govern the nations upon earth. Selah.

5 Let the people praise thee, O God; let all the people praise thee.

6 Then shall the earth yield her increase; and God, even our own God, shall bless us.

7 God shall bless us; and all the ends of the earth shall fear him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A mission prayer: mercy, blessing, a shining face, that God's way be known on earth, his saving health among all nations. Let all peoples praise, nations glad for righteous judgment and governance, earth yielding increase, with the refrain twice, let the people praise you, all the people, ending with blessing and fear at earth's ends. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God be merciful unto us, and bless us, and cause his face to shine upon us: mercy, blessing, and a shining face are prayed for — the Aaronic blessing's shape, applied to the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. That your way may be known upon earth, your saving health among all nations: the purpose of blessing is mission — God's way known on earth, his saving health among all nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Let the people praise you, O God; let all the people praise you: the peoples' praise, all the peoples' praise, is prayed for twice in this psalm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. O let the nations be glad and sing for joy, for you shall judge the people righteously, and govern the nations upon earth: nations glad and singing, because God's judgment is righteous and his government is over the nations on earth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Let the people praise you, O God; let all the people praise you: the refrain returns — praise, all peoples, again. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Then shall the earth yield her increase, and God, even our own God, shall bless us: earth's increase follows the peoples' praise, and God, even our own God, blesses — harvest and blessing are joined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. God shall bless us, and all the ends of the earth shall fear him: blessing on us, fear at earth's ends — the psalm ends with blessing spreading to reverence worldwide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
