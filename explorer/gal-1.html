The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Galatians 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Paul, an apostle, (not of men, neither by man, but by Jesus Christ, and God the Father, who raised him from the dead;)

2 And all the brethren which are with me, unto the churches of Galatia:

3 Grace be to you and peace from God the Father, and from our Lord Jesus Christ,

4 Who gave himself for our sins, that he might deliver us from this present evil world, according to the will of God and our Father:

5 To whom be glory for ever and ever. Amen.

6 I marvel that ye are so soon removed from him that called you into the grace of Christ unto another gospel:

7 Which is not another; but there be some that trouble you, and would pervert the gospel of Christ.

8 But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed.

9 As we said before, so say I now again, if any man preach any other gospel unto you than that ye have received, let him be accursed.

10 For do I now persuade men, or God? or do I seek to please men? for if I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ.

11 But I certify you, brethren, that the gospel which was preached of me is not after man.

12 For I neither received it of man, neither was I taught it, but by the revelation of Jesus Christ.

13 For ye have heard of my conversation in time past in the Jews’ religion, how that beyond measure I persecuted the church of God, and wasted it:

14 And profited in the Jews’ religion above many my equals in mine own nation, being more exceedingly zealous of the traditions of my fathers.

15 But when it pleased God, who separated me from my mother’s womb, and called me by his grace,

16 To reveal his Son in me, that I might preach him among the heathen; immediately I conferred not with flesh and blood:

17 Neither went I up to Jerusalem to them which were apostles before me; but I went into Arabia, and returned again unto Damascus.

18 Then after three years I went up to Jerusalem to see Peter, and abode with him fifteen days.

19 But other of the apostles saw I none, save James the Lord’s brother.

20 Now the things which I write unto you, behold, before God, I lie not.

21 Afterwards I came into the regions of Syria and Cilicia;

22 And was unknown by face unto the churches of Judaea which were in Christ:

23 But they had heard only, That he which persecuted us in times past now preacheth the faith which once he destroyed.

24 And they glorified God in me.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul opens by defending the gospel he preaches: it came not from any man but by revelation from Jesus Christ. The Galatians, he marvels, are already drifting toward a different gospel, which he insists is no gospel at all, and he retells his own reversal from persecutor of the church to preacher among the Gentiles, a change Jerusalem barely knew except by report. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul is an apostle not of men nor by man, but by Jesus Christ and God the Father who raised him from the dead, expressly appointed by Christ and therefore by the Father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He writes with all the brethren with him to the churches of Galatia, churches formed partly of converted Jews and partly of Gentile converts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Grace and peace come from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, grace being God's good will and good work in us, and peace the inward comfort and outward prosperity truly needful, with grace first, since there is no true peace without grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Christ gave himself for our sins to deliver us from this present evil world, according to God's will, an atonement God's justice required and to which Christ freely submitted, redeeming from wrath and from wicked practices alike. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. To God be glory forever and ever, since the greatness of the price shows sin to be the most horrible thing that can be expressed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Paul marvels that they are so soon removed from him who called them into the grace of Christ unto another gospel, a guilt he presses with tenderness, as people drawn away by troublers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. That other gospel is not another at all; some trouble them and would pervert the gospel of Christ, setting up works of the law in place of Christ's righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Though Paul himself or an angel from heaven preached any other gospel than the one preached to them, let him be accursed, so solemnly does Paul denounce any false foundation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. As said before, so he says again: if any man preaches any other gospel than what they received, let him be accursed, since all other gospels are devices of Satan, whether flattering self-righteous pride or favoring worldly lusts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Paul does not seek to please men or persuade men rather than God, for if he still pleased men he would not be Christ's servant, and he will not alter Christ's doctrine to gain favor or avoid fury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He certifies that the gospel he preached is not after man, since in so important a matter he must not fear men's frowns or use men's wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. He neither received it of man nor was taught it, but by the revelation of Jesus Christ, not merely by education. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. They have heard of his former conduct in the Jews' religion, how beyond measure he persecuted the church of God and wasted it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He profited in the Jews' religion above many his equals, being exceedingly zealous for the traditions of his fathers, which makes his reversal the more evidently God's work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. But when it pleased God, who separated him from his mother's womb and called him by his grace, his conversion was wrought by God's power and grace working in him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. God revealed his Son in Paul, that he might preach him among the heathen, and Paul immediately conferred not with flesh and blood, obeying without hesitating over worldly interest, credit, ease, or life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Neither did he go up to Jerusalem to the apostles before him, but went into Arabia and returned to Damascus, showing his gospel came by revelation, not by apostolic instruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Then after three years he went up to Jerusalem to see Peter and stayed fifteen days, a visit of fellowship, not of instruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Other apostles he saw none, save James the Lord's brother, underscoring how little his doctrine owed to men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. What he writes, behold, before God, he lies not, solemnly affirming the account of his calling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Afterwards he came into the regions of Syria and Cilicia, preaching where he had not been known. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. He was unknown by face to the churches of Judea which were in Christ, so their praise of God for him rested on report alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. They had heard only that he who once persecuted them now preaches the faith he once destroyed, a reversal that is itself matter of thanksgiving. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And they glorified God in him, as churches do when they hear of such instances to the praise of the glory of his grace, whether they have seen the person or not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
