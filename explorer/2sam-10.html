The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Samuel 10 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass after this, that the king of the children of Ammon died, and Hanun his son reigned in his stead.

2 Then said David, I will shew kindness unto Hanun the son of Nahash, as his father shewed kindness unto me. And David sent to comfort him by the hand of his servants for his father. And David’s servants came into the land of the children of Ammon.

3 And the princes of the children of Ammon said unto Hanun their lord, Thinkest thou that David doth honour thy father, that he hath sent comforters unto thee? hath not David rather sent his servants unto thee, to search the city, and to spy it out, and to overthrow it?

4 Wherefore Hanun took David’s servants, and shaved off the one half of their beards, and cut off their garments in the middle, even to their buttocks, and sent them away.

5 When they told it unto David, he sent to meet them, because the men were greatly ashamed: and the king said, Tarry at Jericho until your beards be grown, and then return.

6 And when the children of Ammon saw that they stank before David, the children of Ammon sent and hired the Syrians of Bethrehob and the Syrians of Zoba, twenty thousand footmen, and of king Maacah a thousand men, and of Ishtob twelve thousand men.

7 And when David heard of it, he sent Joab, and all the host of the mighty men.

8 And the children of Ammon came out, and put the battle in array at the entering in of the gate: and the Syrians of Zoba, and of Rehob, and Ishtob, and Maacah, were by themselves in the field.

9 When Joab saw that the front of the battle was against him before and behind, he chose of all the choice men of Israel, and put them in array against the Syrians:

10 And the rest of the people he delivered into the hand of Abishai his brother, that he might put them in array against the children of Ammon.

11 And he said, If the Syrians be too strong for me, then thou shalt help me: but if the children of Ammon be too strong for thee, then I will come and help thee.

12 Be of good courage, and let us play the men for our people, and for the cities of our God: and the LORD do that which seemeth him good.

13 And Joab drew nigh, and the people that were with him, unto the battle against the Syrians: and they fled before him.

14 And when the children of Ammon saw that the Syrians were fled, then fled they also before Abishai, and entered into the city. So Joab returned from the children of Ammon, and came to Jerusalem.

15 And when the Syrians saw that they were smitten before Israel, they gathered themselves together.

16 And Hadarezer sent, and brought out the Syrians that were beyond the river: and they came to Helam; and Shobach the captain of the host of Hadarezer went before them.

17 And when it was told David, he gathered all Israel together, and passed over Jordan, and came to Helam. And the Syrians set themselves in array against David, and fought with him.

18 And the Syrians fled before Israel; and David slew the men of seven hundred chariots of the Syrians, and forty thousand horsemen, and smote Shobach the captain of their host, who died there.

19 And when all the kings that were servants to Hadarezer saw that they were smitten before Israel, they made peace with Israel, and served them. So the Syrians feared to help the children of Ammon any more.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

When the Ammonite king dies, David sends envoys to comfort his son Hanun, but Hanun humiliates them, and the insult becomes war with Ammon and its Syrian allies. Joab and Abishai win a hard double battle, and the Syrians make peace with David. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After this the king of Ammon dies, and his son Hanun reigns in his stead. A change of king brings a change of temper, and David's kindness will meet a very different reception. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. David resolves to show kindness to Hanun as his father Nahash showed kindness to him, sending servants to comfort him. Nahash had been an enemy to Israel, Henry notes, yet had shown David kindness, and David gratefully returns it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. Ammon's princes persuade Hanun that David's comforters are really spies come to search and overthrow the city. Those who bear ill-will to their neighbours, Henry observes, are resolved not to believe their neighbours bear any good-will to them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Hanun shaves off half the servants' beards, cuts their garments in the middle, and sends them away. By the usages of those days, Henry says, this was the most contemptuous treatment of ambassadors possible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. David, told of it, sends to meet the ashamed men and bids them tarry at Jericho until their beards grow. He shows much concern for his servants, and Henry draws the lesson not to lay unjust reproaches to heart: they wear off, and reputation wrongfully hurt grows again, as these beards did. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Seeing they stink before David, the Ammonites hire Syrians of Bethrehob and Zoba, twenty thousand footmen, with the king of Maacah and Ishtob. They that are at war with the Son of David, Henry notes, not only give provocation but begin the war. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. David hears of it and sends Joab and all the host of the mighty men. The insult to ambassadors is answered with the full army, not with a protest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. The Ammonites array at the gate, while the Syrians of Zoba, Rehob, Ishtob, and Maacah stand by themselves in the field. Israel faces a two-front battle, city before and field behind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Joab, seeing battle before and behind, chooses the choice men of Israel and arrays them against the Syrians. The best troops are set where the danger is greatest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. The rest he delivers to Abishai his brother, to array against Ammon. Command is divided so neither front is left without a captain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Joab pledges mutual help: if the Syrians are too strong for him, Abishai shall help him, and if Ammon is too strong for Abishai, Joab will come. Christ's soldiers, Henry says, should strengthen one another's hands in their spiritual warfare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Be of good courage, and let us play the men for our people and the cities of our God, and let the Lord do what seems good to him. When we make conscience of doing our duty, Henry notes, we may leave the event with God, hoping for his salvation in his own way and time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Joab draws near against the Syrians, and they flee before him. The first front collapses without a prolonged fight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. When Ammon sees the Syrians fled, they flee before Abishai into the city, and Joab returns to Jerusalem. The second front follows the first, and the campaign ends for the season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The Syrians, seeing they were smitten before Israel, gather themselves together again. Even a baffled cause, Henry observes, will make head as long as there is any life in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Hadarezer brings out the Syrians beyond the river to Helam, with Shobach captain of his host before them. The enemy regroups across the Euphrates for a second, larger attempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. David gathers all Israel, passes over Jordan to Helam, and the Syrians array against him. This time the king himself leads, and the battle is joined on the far side of the river. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. The Syrians flee before Israel; David slays men of seven hundred chariots and forty thousand horsemen, and smites Shobach, who dies there. With this the promise to Abraham and Joshua, that Israel's borders should extend to the Euphrates, is performed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. All the kings who served Hadarezer, seeing they were smitten, make peace with Israel and serve them, and the Syrians fear to help Ammon any more. Henry's lesson is that it is dangerous to help those who have God against them, for when they fall, their helpers fall with them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
