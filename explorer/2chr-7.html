Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now when Solomon had made an end of praying, the fire came down from heaven, and consumed the burnt offering and the sacrifices; and the glory of the LORD filled the house.

2 And the priests could not enter into the house of the LORD, because the glory of the LORD had filled the LORD’s house.

3 And when all the children of Israel saw how the fire came down, and the glory of the LORD upon the house, they bowed themselves with their faces to the ground upon the pavement, and worshipped, and praised the LORD, saying, For he is good; for his mercy endureth for ever.

4 Then the king and all the people offered sacrifices before the LORD.

5 And king Solomon offered a sacrifice of twenty and two thousand oxen, and an hundred and twenty thousand sheep: so the king and all the people dedicated the house of God.

6 And the priests waited on their offices: the Levites also with instruments of musick of the LORD, which David the king had made to praise the LORD, because his mercy endureth for ever, when David praised by their ministry; and the priests sounded trumpets before them, and all Israel stood.

7 Moreover Solomon hallowed the middle of the court that was before the house of the LORD: for there he offered burnt offerings, and the fat of the peace offerings, because the brasen altar which Solomon had made was not able to receive the burnt offerings, and the meat offerings, and the fat.

8 Also at the same time Solomon kept the feast seven days, and all Israel with him, a very great congregation, from the entering in of Hamath unto the river of Egypt.

9 And in the eighth day they made a solemn assembly: for they kept the dedication of the altar seven days, and the feast seven days.

10 And on the three and twentieth day of the seventh month he sent the people away into their tents, glad and merry in heart for the goodness that the LORD had shewed unto David, and to Solomon, and to Israel his people.

11 Thus Solomon finished the house of the LORD, and the king’s house: and all that came into Solomon’s heart to make in the house of the LORD, and in his own house, he prosperously effected.

12 And the LORD appeared to Solomon by night, and said unto him, I have heard thy prayer, and have chosen this place to myself for an house of sacrifice.

13 If I shut up heaven that there be no rain, or if I command the locusts to devour the land, or if I send pestilence among my people;

14 If my people, which are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land.

15 Now mine eyes shall be open, and mine ears attent unto the prayer that is made in this place.

16 For now have I chosen and sanctified this house, that my name may be there for ever: and mine eyes and mine heart shall be there perpetually.

17 And as for thee, if thou wilt walk before me, as David thy father walked, and do according to all that I have commanded thee, and shalt observe my statutes and my judgments;

18 Then will I stablish the throne of thy kingdom, according as I have covenanted with David thy father, saying, There shall not fail thee a man to be ruler in Israel.

19 But if ye turn away, and forsake my statutes and my commandments, which I have set before you, and shall go and serve other gods, and worship them;

20 Then will I pluck them up by the roots out of my land which I have given them; and this house, which I have sanctified for my name, will I cast out of my sight, and will make it to be a proverb and a byword among all nations.

21 And this house, which is high, shall be an astonishment to every one that passeth by it; so that he shall say, Why hath the LORD done thus unto this land, and unto this house?

22 And it shall be answered, Because they forsook the LORD God of their fathers, which brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, and laid hold on other gods, and worshipped them, and served them: therefore hath he brought all this evil upon them.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the fire falling on the sacrifices and the glory filling the house, the great feast that followed, and God's answer to Solomon, promising blessing for obedience and warning of exile for forsaking him. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Now when Solomon had made an end of praying, the fire came down from heaven and consumed the burnt offering and sacrifices, and the glory of the Lord filled the house. Henry notes the fire came not upon the killing of the sacrifices but upon the praying of the prayer, testifying acceptance as with Moses, Gideon, David and Elijah, and signifying both God's terrible glory and his grace, since the fire fastened on the sacrifice in their stead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And the priests could not enter into the house of the Lord, because the glory of the Lord had filled the Lord's house. Henry's account of the glory filling the house applies: the cloud that beautified the house more than gold was also the sign that human priests, men of infirmity, could not stand to minister before it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And when all Israel saw the fire come down and the glory upon the house, they bowed with faces to the ground on the pavement, and worshipped and praised the Lord, saying, For he is good, for his mercy endureth for ever. Henry notes they did not run away affrighted but kept their ground, adoring with reverence and thankfulness, a song never out of season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Then the king and all the people offered sacrifices before the Lord. Henry notes the tokens of God's favour enlarged their hearts in service, and the king's example stirred the people, for good work goes on when leaders lead in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And King Solomon offered twenty-two thousand oxen and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep, so the king and all the people dedicated the house of God. Henry notes they feasted the holy fire and bade it welcome, increasing their sacrifices after the token of favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. And the priests waited on their offices, the Levites with instruments of music of the Lord which David had made to praise the Lord, because his mercy endureth for ever, when David praised by their ministry, and priests sounded trumpets before them, and all Israel stood. Henry notes the singers and musicians did their part with David's instruments and hymn, and David is said to praise by their ministry because he employed, directed and encouraged them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Moreover Solomon hallowed the middle of the court before the house, for there he offered burnt offerings and the fat of peace offerings, because the bronze altar could not receive them all. Henry notes the sacrifices were so numerous the altar could not contain them, so the flesh and fat were burnt in the midst of the court, which Solomon hallowed for that service, and in necessity the pavement might be an altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Also at the same time Solomon kept the feast seven days, and all Israel with him, a very great congregation, from Hamath unto the river of Egypt. Henry notes they kept the feast of dedication seven days, from the second to the ninth, with the greatest joy and satisfaction imaginable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And in the eighth day they made a solemn assembly, for they kept the dedication of the altar seven days, and the feast seven days. Henry notes the sequence: dedication seven days, the tenth day the day of atonement to afflict their souls, not unseasonable amid rejoicing, and on the fifteenth the feast of tabernacles to the twenty-second. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And on the three and twentieth day of the seventh month he sent the people away to their tents, glad and merry in heart for the goodness the Lord had showed to David, Solomon and Israel. Henry notes they did not separate till the twenty-third, and we must never grudge time spent in worship and communion with God, nor think it long. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Thus Solomon finished the house of the Lord and the king's house, and all that came into his heart to make in God's house and his own he prosperously effected. Henry notes Solomon went on in his work and prospered: those who begin with God's service are likely to go on successfully in their own affairs, and his praise was that what he undertook he went through with. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And the Lord appeared to Solomon by night, and said, I have heard thy prayer, and have chosen this place to myself for an house of sacrifice. Henry notes a prayer may be accepted yet not answered in the letter, so God appeared in the night, as before after a day of sacrifice, and gave a peculiar answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. If I shut up heaven that there be no rain, or command locusts to devour the land, or send pestilence among my people. Henry notes national judgments are here supposed: famine, pestilence, and perhaps war, with locusts meaning enemies as greedy as locusts laying all waste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. If my people, called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways, then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land. Henry notes national repentance, prayer and reformation are required: humbling under God's hand, praying, seeking his face, and turning from wicked ways, for pardoning mercy makes way for healing mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Now mine eyes shall be open, and mine ears attent unto the prayer made in this place. Henry notes God promised to answer prayers made in that place, with eyes open and ears attentive to prayer there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. For now have I chosen and sanctified this house, that my name may be there for ever, and mine eyes and mine heart shall be there perpetually. Henry notes God promised to own this house for a house of sacrifice to Israel and prayer for all people: his name shall be there for ever, there he will make himself known and be called upon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. And as for thee, if thou wilt walk before me as David thy father walked, and do according to all I have commanded thee, and observe my statutes and judgments. Henry notes God promised to perpetuate Solomon's kingdom on condition he persevered in duty, imitating David's example if he hoped for the benefit of David's covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Then will I establish the throne of thy kingdom, according as I covenanted with David, saying, There shall not fail thee a man to be ruler in Israel. Henry notes the conditional promise: the throne established according to the covenant with David, on Solomon's walking as David walked. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. But if ye turn away, and forsake my statutes and commandments set before you, and go and serve other gods, and worship them. Henry notes God set death as well as life before them, supposing it possible that, despite the temple, they might be drawn aside to other gods, knowing their proneness to backslide. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Then will I pluck them up by the roots out of my land I have given them, and this house sanctified for my name will I cast out of my sight, and make it a proverb and byword among all nations. Henry notes this would be ruin of both church and state: the nation plucked up like weeds, and the sanctuary no protection to those who trusted in it as a charm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. And this house, which is high, shall be an astonishment to every one that passeth by, so that he shall say, Why hath the Lord done thus unto this land and unto this house? Henry notes the high house, magnificent in structure and designed ends, shall come down wonderfully, to the amazement of neighbours. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And it shall be answered, Because they forsook the Lord God of their fathers, who brought them out of Egypt, and laid hold on other gods, worshipped and served them, therefore hath he brought all this evil upon them. Henry's threatened ruin is explained here: forsaking the God who delivered them for other gods is the cause of all the evil brought upon them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
