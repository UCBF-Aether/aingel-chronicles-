The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 32 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now the children of Reuben and the children of Gad had a very great multitude of cattle: and when they saw the land of Jazer, and the land of Gilead, that, behold, the place was a place for cattle;

2 The children of Gad and the children of Reuben came and spake unto Moses, and to Eleazar the priest, and unto the princes of the congregation, saying,

3 Ataroth, and Dibon, and Jazer, and Nimrah, and Heshbon, and Elealeh, and Shebam, and Nebo, and Beon,

4 Even the country which the LORD smote before the congregation of Israel, is a land for cattle, and thy servants have cattle:

5 Wherefore, said they, if we have found grace in thy sight, let this land be given unto thy servants for a possession, and bring us not over Jordan.

6 And Moses said unto the children of Gad and to the children of Reuben, Shall your brethren go to war, and shall ye sit here?

7 And wherefore discourage ye the heart of the children of Israel from going over into the land which the LORD hath given them?

8 Thus did your fathers, when I sent them from Kadeshbarnea to see the land.

9 For when they went up unto the valley of Eshcol, and saw the land, they discouraged the heart of the children of Israel, that they should not go into the land which the LORD had given them.

10 And the LORD’s anger was kindled the same time, and he sware, saying,

11 Surely none of the men that came up out of Egypt, from twenty years old and upward, shall see the land which I sware unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob; because they have not wholly followed me:

12 Save Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenezite, and Joshua the son of Nun: for they have wholly followed the LORD.

13 And the LORD’s anger was kindled against Israel, and he made them wander in the wilderness forty years, until all the generation, that had done evil in the sight of the LORD, was consumed.

14 And, behold, ye are risen up in your fathers’ stead, an increase of sinful men, to augment yet the fierce anger of the LORD toward Israel.

15 For if ye turn away from after him, he will yet again leave them in the wilderness; and ye shall destroy all this people.

16 And they came near unto him, and said, We will build sheepfolds here for our cattle, and cities for our little ones:

17 But we ourselves will go ready armed before the children of Israel, until we have brought them unto their place: and our little ones shall dwell in the fenced cities because of the inhabitants of the land.

18 We will not return unto our houses, until the children of Israel have inherited every man his inheritance.

19 For we will not inherit with them on yonder side Jordan, or forward; because our inheritance is fallen to us on this side Jordan eastward.

20 And Moses said unto them, If ye will do this thing, if ye will go armed before the LORD to war,

21 And will go all of you armed over Jordan before the LORD, until he hath driven out his enemies from before him,

22 And the land be subdued before the LORD: then afterward ye shall return, and be guiltless before the LORD, and before Israel; and this land shall be your possession before the LORD.

23 But if ye will not do so, behold, ye have sinned against the LORD: and be sure your sin will find you out.

24 Build you cities for your little ones, and folds for your sheep; and do that which hath proceeded out of your mouth.

25 And the children of Gad and the children of Reuben spake unto Moses, saying, Thy servants will do as my lord commandeth.

26 Our little ones, our wives, our flocks, and all our cattle, shall be there in the cities of Gilead:

27 But thy servants will pass over, every man armed for war, before the LORD to battle, as my lord saith.

28 So concerning them Moses commanded Eleazar the priest, and Joshua the son of Nun, and the chief fathers of the tribes of the children of Israel:

29 And Moses said unto them, If the children of Gad and the children of Reuben will pass with you over Jordan, every man armed to battle, before the LORD, and the land shall be subdued before you; then ye shall give them the land of Gilead for a possession:

30 But if they will not pass over with you armed, they shall have possessions among you in the land of Canaan.

31 And the children of Gad and the children of Reuben answered, saying, As the LORD hath said unto thy servants, so will we do.

32 We will pass over armed before the LORD into the land of Canaan, that the possession of our inheritance on this side Jordan may be ours.

33 And Moses gave unto them, even to the children of Gad, and to the children of Reuben, and unto half the tribe of Manasseh the son of Joseph, the kingdom of Sihon king of the Amorites, and the kingdom of Og king of Bashan, the land, with the cities thereof in the coasts, even the cities of the country round about.

34 And the children of Gad built Dibon, and Ataroth, and Aroer,

35 And Atroth, Shophan, and Jaazer, and Jogbehah,

36 And Bethnimrah, and Bethharan, fenced cities: and folds for sheep.

37 And the children of Reuben built Heshbon, and Elealeh, and Kirjathaim,

38 And Nebo, and Baalmeon, (their names being changed,) and Shibmah: and gave other names unto the cities which they builded.

39 And the children of Machir the son of Manasseh went to Gilead, and took it, and dispossessed the Amorite which was in it.

40 And Moses gave Gilead unto Machir the son of Manasseh; and he dwelt therein.

41 And Jair the son of Manasseh went and took the small towns thereof, and called them Havothjair.

42 And Nobah went and took Kenath, and the villages thereof, and called it Nobah, after his own name.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter settles the request of Reuben and Gad, who have a great multitude of cattle and see that Jazer and Gilead are a place for cattle. They ask to settle east of the Jordan instead of crossing over, and Moses first rebukes them, fearing they will discourage Israel as the spies did. They answer that they will build folds and cities for their families, then go armed before Israel until the land is subdued, and only then return. Moses agrees on those terms, gives them and half the tribe of Manasseh the kingdoms of Sihon and Og, and their cities are built and named. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Now Reuben and Gad had a very great multitude of cattle, and when they saw the land of Jazer, and the land of Gilead, that, behold, the place was a place for cattle. JFB notes a complete conquest had been made east of Jordan, Jazer the southern district between Arnon and Jabbok and Gilead the middle region between Jabbok and Jarmouk including Bashan, a region always famous for rich and extensive pastures, still the Bedouin shepherds' favourite resort, and Reuben and Gad, pre-eminently pastoral and placed under the same standard, united in preferring a request for this trans-jordanic region so well suited to pastoral habits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The children of Gad and the children of Reuben came and spoke to Moses, and to Eleazar the priest, and to the princes of the congregation, saying. The two tribes brought their request jointly to Moses, Eleazar and the princes, the public authorities who could grant or refuse a tribal inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Ataroth, and Dibon, and Jazer, and Nimrah, and Heshbon, and Elealeh, and Shebam, and Nebo, and Beon. The tribes named the conquered cities one by one, the Amorite towns whose pasture lands they had surveyed and now sought as their portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Even the country which the Lord smote before the congregation of Israel, is a land for cattle, and your servants have cattle. Their argument was fitness: the country the Lord had already smitten before Israel was a land for cattle, and they were cattlemen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Wherefore, said they, if we have found grace in your sight, let this land be given to your servants for a possession, and bring us not over Jordan. Henry notes two common things in the world might lead these tribes to this choice, the lust of the eye and the pride of life, and there was much amiss in the principle they went upon, consulting their own private convenience more than the public good, as many still seek their own things more than the things of Christ and are led by worldly interests to take up short of the heavenly Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Moses said to Gad and to Reuben, shall your brethren go to war, and shall you sit here? JFB notes their language was ambiguous, and Moses, suspicious the proposal was an act of unbelief, a scheme of self-policy and indolence to escape the perils of warfare and live in ease and safety, addressed a reproachful and passionate remonstrance, adding that if they truly meant to co-operate, they ought to have been more explicit at first. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And wherefore discourage you the heart of Israel from going over into the land which the Lord has given them? Henry notes the proposal showed disregard to Canaan, distrust of the Lord's promise, and unwillingness to encounter the difficulties and dangers of conquering and driving out its inhabitants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Thus did your fathers, when I sent them from Kadesh-barnea to see the land. Moses reached back to the spies' mission from Kadesh-barnea, the last time Israel stood ready to enter and turned back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. For when they went up to the valley of Eshcol, and saw the land, they discouraged the heart of Israel, that they should not go into the land which the Lord had given them. The fathers' sight of the land produced discouragement rather than courage, and Moses warned the sons against repeating it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And the Lord's anger was kindled the same time, and he swore, saying, surely none of the men that came up out of Egypt, from twenty years old and upward, shall see the land which I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, because they have not wholly followed me. The oath that followed the spies' discouragement barred the whole generation from the land, because they had not wholly followed the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Surely none of the men that came up out of Egypt, from twenty years old and upward, shall see the land which I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, because they have not wholly followed me. The sentence fell on fighting men, twenty and upward, the same measure by which both censuses had counted Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Save Caleb son of Jephunneh the Kenezite, and Joshua son of Nun: for they have wholly followed the Lord. Two men escaped the sentence, Caleb and Joshua, because they had wholly followed the Lord when the rest had not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And the Lord's anger was kindled against Israel, and he made them wander in the wilderness forty years, until all the generation, that had done evil in the sight of the Lord, was consumed. The wandering lasted forty years, until the evil generation was consumed, a sentence the second census had just proved fulfilled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And, behold, you are risen up in your fathers' stead, an increase of sinful men, to augment yet the fierce anger of the Lord toward Israel. Moses charged the sons with rising in their fathers' stead as an increase of sinful men, whose discouragement would augment the very anger that had consumed their fathers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. For if you turn away from after him, he will yet again leave them in the wilderness, and you shall destroy all this people. The warning was corporate: turning away would leave not only the two tribes but all Israel in the wilderness, and destroy the whole people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And they came near to him, and said, we will build sheepfolds here for our cattle, and cities for our little ones. JFB notes the suppliants had shrunk back, dreading refusal from their leader's undisguised emotions, but perceiving his objection rested only on the supposition they would not cross Jordan to help their brethren, they grew emboldened to approach with assurances of goodwill, and build here means rebuild or repair, since within two months new cities could not have been founded, the Amorite cities not being absolutely demolished but consisting only of mud-built or dry-stone walls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. But we ourselves will go ready armed before Israel, until we have brought them to their place, and our little ones shall dwell in the fenced cities because of the inhabitants of the land. JFB notes good policy in leaving a sufficient force to protect the conquered region against reprisals, since only forty thousand of Reuben, Gad and half Manasseh passed over Jordan, leaving seventy thousand five hundred and eighty men besides women and children under twenty for the new possessions' security, and ready armed means in a collective body, or as many as deemed necessary, while the rest remained to sustain and protect families and flocks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. We will not return to our houses, until Israel have inherited every man his inheritance. The pledge was total: no return home until every man of Israel had inherited, not merely until the war was won. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. For we will not inherit with them on yonder side Jordan, or forward, because our inheritance is fallen to us on this side Jordan eastward. Their choice was now stated plainly: no inheritance west of Jordan, because theirs had fallen eastward, and the condition for it was the pledge just made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. Moses said to them, if you will do this thing, if you will go armed before the Lord to war. JFB notes go before the Lord alludes to the order of march in which Reuben and Gad immediately preceded the ark, or to the Jordan passage where the ark stood mid-channel while all tribes marched by, so that literally they passed before the Lord and before the rest of Israel, or generally denotes an expedition blessed with God's presence and destined to promote his glory, and Moses' displeasure disappeared on their solemn assurances, though a lurking suspicion still kept him in an admonitory strain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. And will go all of you armed over Jordan before the Lord, until he has driven out his enemies from before him. The condition was armed passage over Jordan before the Lord, until his enemies were driven out, a war fought under God's banner rather than the tribes' convenience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. And the land be subdued before the Lord: then afterward you shall return, and be guiltless before the Lord, and before Israel, and this land shall be your possession before the Lord. Only when the land was subdued before the Lord would return leave them guiltless before God and Israel, and only then would the east bank be their possession before the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. But if you will not do so, behold, you have sinned against the Lord: and be sure your sin will find you out. Henry warns sin will surely find out the sinner sooner or later, and it concerns us now to find our sins out, that we may repent and forsake them, lest they find us out to our ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Build you cities for your little ones, and folds for your sheep, and do that which has proceeded out of your mouth. Moses granted the request on the pledge's terms: build the cities and folds, and do what your mouth has spoken, the pledge now binding as a vow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And Gad and Reuben spoke to Moses, saying, your servants will do as my lord commands. The tribes answered in the language of servants to their lord, accepting Moses' terms without dispute, which Henry calls the good effect of plain dealing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Our little ones, our wives, our flocks, and all our cattle, shall be there in the cities of Gilead. Families, wives, flocks and cattle would remain in Gilead's cities while the fighting men crossed Jordan, the division of labour the pledge required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. But your servants will pass over, every man armed for war, before the Lord to battle, as my lord says. The pledge was repeated in full: every man armed for war, before the Lord to battle, as Moses had said. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. So concerning them Moses commanded Eleazar the priest, and Joshua son of Nun, and the chief fathers of the tribes of Israel. The arrangement, with its express terms, was announced to the public authorities, Eleazar, Joshua and the chief fathers, so that the conditional grant stood as public law. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Moses said to them, if Gad and Reuben will pass with you over Jordan, every man armed to battle, before the Lord, and the land shall be subdued before you, then you shall give them the land of Gilead for a possession. The grant was stated to the authorities in conditional form: armed passage and a subdued land, then Gilead for a possession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. But if they will not pass over with you armed, they shall have possessions among you in the land of Canaan. Failure meant forfeiture: they would be compelled to cross Jordan and fight for a settlement like the rest of their brethren, with possessions among them in Canaan instead of Gilead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. And Gad and Reuben answered, saying, as the Lord has said to your servants, so will we do. The tribes answered in the Lord's name, and JFB notes their final reply showed that now all was right, whether Moses' expostulation had driven them from an original withdrawal or they had always meant to co-operate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. We will pass over armed before the Lord into the land of Canaan, that the possession of our inheritance on this side Jordan may be ours. The final pledge tied the crossing to the possession: armed passage into Canaan was the price of keeping the inheritance east of Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Moses gave to them, even to Gad, and to Reuben, and to half the tribe of Manasseh son of Joseph, the kingdom of Sihon king of the Amorites, and the kingdom of Og king of Bashan, the land, with the cities thereof in the coasts, even the cities of the country round about. JFB notes it is nowhere explained how half Manasseh was incorporated with the two tribes or what broke the tribe in two, since they were not mentioned in the application nor in Moses' first directions, but as a pastoral people with immense flocks like the others, Moses invited half of them to remain, the region being more than sufficient for the others, perhaps preferring them for valorous conduct against the Amorites. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. And Gad built Dibon, and Ataroth, and Aroer. JFB identifies Dibon with Dheban, now in ruins an hour from the Arnon, Ataroth, Hebrew for crowns, a town of that name not identified in Gad, and Aroer with Arair, standing on a precipice on the Arnon's north bank, while Henry notes built means repaired, not founded anew. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. And Atroth, Shophan, and Jaazer, and Jogbehah. JFB notes Jaazer stood near a famed fountain whose waters flow into the Wady Schaib about fifteen miles from Heshbon, and the Gadite cities together formed the repaired strongholds of the southern east bank. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. And Beth-nimrah, and Beth-haran, fenced cities, and folds for sheep. Beth-nimrah, now Nimrin, and Beth-haran were fenced cities with folds for sheep, the pastoral infrastructure of Gad's inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. And Reuben built Heshbon, and Elealeh, and Kirjathaim. JFB identifies Heshbon with Hesban, Elealeh, Hebrew for the high, with Elaal, and Kirjathaim, Hebrew for the double city, as Reuben's repaired cities in the conquered Amorite country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. And Nebo, and Baal-meon, (their names being changed,) and Shibmah, and gave other names to the cities which they built. JFB notes Nebo is now Neba near the mountain of that name, Baal-meon now Myoun in ruins where a temple of Baal stood, and Shibmah, or Shebam, near Heshbon famous for vines, while the names were changed either by the general custom of conquerors or, more likely, because the prohibition on mentioning other gods' names made it expedient to obliterate all remembrance of those idols on Israel's first settlement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 39. And the children of Machir son of Manasseh went to Gilead, and took it, and dispossessed the Amorites which were therein. Machir's sons conquered Gilead itself, dispossessing the Amorites who remained there, a conquest within the conquest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. Moses gave Gilead to Machir son of Manasseh, and he dwelt therein. Gilead, now Jelud, was given to Machir, and he dwelt in the land his sons had taken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. And Jair son of Manasseh went and took the small towns thereof, and called them Havoth-jair. Havoth-jair means tent-villages, and Jair, who captured them, was a descendant of Manasseh on his mother's side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. And Nobah went and took Kenath, and the villages thereof, and called it Nobah, after his own name. Nobah, a distinguished person of eastern Manasseh, took Kenath and its villages and renamed the town after himself, as Jair had done with his. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

  • Verse 36. And Beth-nimrah, and Beth-haran, fenced cities, and folds for sheep. Beth-nimrah, now Nimrin, and Beth-haran were fenced cities with folds for sheep, the pastoral infrastructure of Gad's inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. And Reuben built Heshbon, and Elealeh, and Kirjathaim. JFB identifies Heshbon with Hesban, Elealeh, Hebrew for the high, with Elaal, and Kirjathaim, Hebrew for the double city, as Reuben's repaired cities in the conquered Amorite country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. And Nebo, and Baal-meon, (their names being changed,) and Shibmah, and gave other names to the cities which they built. JFB notes Nebo is now Neba near the mountain of that name, Baal-meon now Myoun in ruins where a temple of Baal stood, and Shibmah, or Shebam, near Heshbon famous for vines, while the names were changed either by the general custom of conquerors or, more likely, because the prohibition on mentioning other gods' names made it expedient to obliterate all remembrance of those idols on Israel's first settlement.   • Verse 39. And the children of Machir son of Manasseh went to Gilead, and took it, and dispossessed the Amorites which were therein. Machir's sons conquered Gilead itself, dispossessing the Amorites who remained there, a conquest within the conquest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 40. Moses gave Gilead to Machir son of Manasseh, and he dwelt therein. Gilead, now Jelud, was given to Machir, and he dwelt in the land his sons had taken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 41. And Jair son of Manasseh went and took the small towns thereof, and called them Havoth-jair. Havoth-jair means tent-villages, and Jair, who captured them, was a descendant of Manasseh on his mother's side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 42. And Nobah went and took Kenath, and the villages thereof, and called it Nobah, after his own name. Nobah, a distinguished person of eastern Manasseh, took Kenath and its villages and renamed the town after himself, as Jair had done with his. Matthew Henry

  • Verse 2. The children of Gad and the children of Reuben came and spoke to Moses, and to Eleazar the priest, and to the princes of the congregation, saying. The two tribes brought their request jointly to Moses, Eleazar and the princes, the public authorities who could grant or refuse a tribal inheritance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Even the country which the Lord smote before the congregation of Israel, is a land for cattle, and your servants have cattle. Their argument was fitness: the country the Lord had already smitten before Israel was a land for cattle, and they were cattlemen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Wherefore, said they, if we have found grace in your sight, let this land be given to your servants for a possession, and bring us not over Jordan. Henry notes two common things in the world might lead these tribes to this choice, the lust of the eye and the pride of life, and there was much amiss in the principle they went upon, consulting their own private convenience more than the public good, as many still seek their own things more than the things of Christ and are led by worldly interests to take up short of the heavenly Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And wherefore discourage you the heart of Israel from going over into the land which the Lord has given them? Henry notes the proposal showed disregard to Canaan, distrust of the Lord's promise, and unwillingness to encounter the difficulties and dangers of conquering and driving out its inhabitants. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Thus did your fathers, when I sent them from Kadesh-barnea to see the land. Moses reached back to the spies' mission from Kadesh-barnea, the last time Israel stood ready to enter and turned back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. For when they went up to the valley of Eshcol, and saw the land, they discouraged the heart of Israel, that they should not go into the land which the Lord had given them. The fathers' sight of the land produced discouragement rather than courage, and Moses warned the sons against repeating it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And the Lord's anger was kindled the same time, and he swore, saying, surely none of the men that came up out of Egypt, from twenty years old and upward, shall see the land which I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, because they have not wholly followed me. The oath that followed the spies' discouragement barred the whole generation from the land, because they had not wholly followed the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Surely none of the men that came up out of Egypt, from twenty years old and upward, shall see the land which I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, because they have not wholly followed me. The sentence fell on fighting men, twenty and upward, the same measure by which both censuses had counted Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Save Caleb son of Jephunneh the Kenezite, and Joshua son of Nun: for they have wholly followed the Lord. Two men escaped the sentence, Caleb and Joshua, because they had wholly followed the Lord when the rest had not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And the Lord's anger was kindled against Israel, and he made them wander in the wilderness forty years, until all the generation, that had done evil in the sight of the Lord, was consumed. The wandering lasted forty years, until the evil generation was consumed, a sentence the second census had just proved fulfilled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. And, behold, you are risen up in your fathers' stead, an increase of sinful men, to augment yet the fierce anger of the Lord toward Israel. Moses charged the sons with rising in their fathers' stead as an increase of sinful men, whose discouragement would augment the very anger that had consumed their fathers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. For if you turn away from after him, he will yet again leave them in the wilderness, and you shall destroy all this people. The warning was corporate: turning away would leave not only the two tribes but all Israel in the wilderness, and destroy the whole people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. We will not return to our houses, until Israel have inherited every man his inheritance. The pledge was total: no return home until every man of Israel had inherited, not merely until the war was won. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. For we will not inherit with them on yonder side Jordan, or forward, because our inheritance is fallen to us on this side Jordan eastward. Their choice was now stated plainly: no inheritance west of Jordan, because theirs had fallen eastward, and the condition for it was the pledge just made. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And the land be subdued before the Lord: then afterward you shall return, and be guiltless before the Lord, and before Israel, and this land shall be your possession before the Lord. Only when the land was subdued before the Lord would return leave them guiltless before God and Israel, and only then would the east bank be their possession before the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. But if you will not do so, behold, you have sinned against the Lord: and be sure your sin will find you out. Henry warns sin will surely find out the sinner sooner or later, and it concerns us now to find our sins out, that we may repent and forsake them, lest they find us out to our ruin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Build you cities for your little ones, and folds for your sheep, and do that which has proceeded out of your mouth. Moses granted the request on the pledge's terms: build the cities and folds, and do what your mouth has spoken, the pledge now binding as a vow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And Gad and Reuben spoke to Moses, saying, your servants will do as my lord commands. The tribes answered in the language of servants to their lord, accepting Moses' terms without dispute, which Henry calls the good effect of plain dealing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. But your servants will pass over, every man armed for war, before the Lord to battle, as my lord says. The pledge was repeated in full: every man armed for war, before the Lord to battle, as Moses had said. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. We will pass over armed before the Lord into the land of Canaan, that the possession of our inheritance on this side Jordan may be ours. The final pledge tied the crossing to the possession: armed passage into Canaan was the price of keeping the inheritance east of Jordan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
