The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Corinthians 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not charity, I am become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal.

2 And though I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries, and all knowledge; and though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not charity, I am nothing.

3 And though I bestow all my goods to feed the poor, and though I give my body to be burned, and have not charity, it profiteth me nothing.

4 Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up,

5 Doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil;

6 Rejoiceth not in iniquity, but rejoiceth in the truth;

7 Beareth all things, believeth all things, hopeth all things, endureth all things.

8 Charity never faileth: but whether there be prophecies, they shall fail; whether there be tongues, they shall cease; whether there be knowledge, it shall vanish away.

9 For we know in part, and we prophesy in part.

10 But when that which is perfect is come, then that which is in part shall be done away.

11 When I was a child, I spake as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child: but when I became a man, I put away childish things.

12 For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known.

13 And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul's hymn to love: without love, tongues of men and angels, prophecy, knowledge, faith that moves mountains, and giving everything away are nothing, sounding brass and tinkling cymbal. Love is patient and kind, does not envy or boast, bears, believes, hopes, and endures all things, and never fails, while prophecies, tongues, and knowledge pass away; now faith, hope, and love abide, and the greatest is love. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. This chapter is the New Testament psalm of love. Though Paul speaks with the tongues of men and of angels — with the eloquence admired at Corinth and command of various languages, abused there for mere ostentation — if he has not charity, the principle of the ordinary and more important gifts of the Spirit, he is only sounding brass and a tinkling cymbal: sound without soul or feeling, sharp and piercing but empty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Though he has prophecy and understands all mysteries — the deep counsels of God hitherto secret but now revealed — and all knowledge of truths long known, and faith that could remove mountains, the practical power of the will elevated by faith, yet without love these are not what they seem and fail of the heavenly reward. Paul, who teaches justification by faith only, thus agrees with James: by works — that is, by love, the spirit of faith — a man is justified, and not by faith only. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Though he dole out in food all his goods to the poor, one of the highest functions of helps, and give his body to be burned, as the three youths yielded their bodies, yet without love he is profited nothing. These are noble exemplifications of love in giving and suffering, but they may be without love, in which case goods and body are given but not the soul, which is love's sphere; without the soul God rejects all else. Men will fight and die for Christianity but not live in its spirit, which is love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Love suffers long under provocations of evil from others — its negative side — and is kind, extending good to others — its positive side, matching the wisdom from above. It does not envy, including jealousy, does not vaunt itself in words even of gifts it really possesses, an indirect rebuke of display with tongues, and is not puffed up with party zeal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Love does not behave itself unseemly — it is not uncourteous or inattentive to civility and propriety. It seeks not its own, and thinks no evil: it imputes not the evil that actually is there, but makes allowances for others' falls, puts a charitable construction on them, excuses the evil inflicted on it, does not meditate on evil done by another, and in doubtful cases takes the more charitable view. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Love does not rejoice in iniquity but rejoices with the truth — it exults not at others' perpetration of unrighteousness but sympathizes with truth in its triumphs. Truth is the gospel truth, the inseparable ally of love, and the false charity that compromises truth by glossing over iniquity is tacitly condemned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Love bears all things without speaking of what it bears, enduring without divulging personal distress, holding fast like a watertight vessel and containing itself in silence under hardship. It believes all things, unsuspiciously believing all that is not palpably false to another's credit; it hopes what is good of another even when others have ceased to hope; and it endures persecutions in a patient and loving spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Love never fails — it is never out of use and always holds its place. But prophecies shall be done away with and knowledge shall be done away with, superseded at the Lord's coming by more perfect heavenly analogues, knowledge by intuition, while tongues, still more temporary, shall cease. A primary fulfillment came when the church attained maturity and the collected New Testament Scriptures and ordinary preaching made the supernatural gifts no longer required. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. For we know in part and we prophesy in part — partially and imperfectly, in contrast to the perfect man, the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ. Our present knowledge and prophecy are fragmentary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. But when that which is perfect is come, then that which is in part — fragmentary and isolated — shall be done away. The partial gives way to the complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. When Paul was a child, he spoke as a child, alluding to tongues; he understood as a child, alluding to prophecy; he thought, or reasoned, as a child, alluding to knowledge. Now that he has become a man, he has done away with childish things — the gifts of the present age belong to spiritual childhood beside the maturity to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Now we see through a glass darkly — in a mirror, in enigma. Ancient mirrors of polished brass gave only a dim reflected image, and an enigma conveys only a shadow to the ear; neither eye nor ear can fully represent the things God has prepared for those who love him. Even the revealed word now is a dark discourse beside direct vision. Then we shall see face to face, and know fully even as we are fully known — now we are known by God rather than knowing him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. But now, the case being so, there abide faith, hope, and charity, these three — while prophecy, tongues, and knowledge fail, these three abide permanently as necessary and sufficient for salvation at all times. Faith is toward God, hope is on our own behalf, and charity is love to God creating love toward our neighbor. In one sense faith and hope will be done away, superseded by sight and fruition, but in another sense they abide even hereafter as trust in God and anticipation of ever new joys; love alone in every sense abides, and is therefore the greatest, since it presupposes faith, which without love and its works is dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • charity. The King James word for love in this chapter, Paul's more excellent way. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • sounding brass. A noisy gong; Paul's image for speech without love. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • through a glass, darkly. Paul's image for present, partial knowledge, seeing as in a dim mirror, until face to face. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
