The Text — Jubilees 7 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And in the seventh week in the first year thereof, in this jubilee, Noah planted vines on the mountain on which the ark had rested, named Lubar one of the Ararat Mountains, and they pro-

2 duced fruit in the fourth year and he guarded their

3 fruit, and gathered it in this year in the seventh month.

4 And he made wine therefrom and put it

5 into a vessel, and kept it until the fifth year, until the

6 first day, on the new moon of the first month.

7 And he celebrated with joy the day of this feast, and he made a burnt sacrifice unto the Lord, one young ox and one ram, and seven sheep, each a year old, and a kid of the goats, that he might make atonement thereby for himself and his sons. 4.

8 And he prepared the kid first, and placed some of its blood on the flesh that was on the altar which he had made, and all the

9 fat he laid on the altar where he made the burnt sacrifice, and the ox and the ram and the sheep, and he laid all their flesh upon the altar.

10 And he placed all their offerings mingled with oil upon it, and afterwards he sprinkled wine on the fire which he had previously made on the altar, and he placed incense on the altar and caused a sweet savour to ascend acceptable before the Lord his God.

11 And he rejoiced and drank of this wine, he and his children with joy 7.

12 And it was evening, and he went into his tent, and being drunken he lay down and slept, and was uncovered in his tent as he slept.

13 And Ham saw Noah his father naked, and went forth and told his two brethren without.

14 And Shem took his garment and arose, he and Japheth, and they placed the garment on their shoulders and went backward and covered the shame of their father, and their faces were backward.' 10.

15 And Noah awoke from his sleep and knew all that his younger son had done unto him, and he cursed his son and said : " Cursed be Canaan; an enslaved servant shall he be unto his brethren." 11.

16 And he blessed Shem, and said : " Blessed be the Lord God of Shem, and Canaan shall be his servant.

17 God shall enlarge Japheth, and God shall dwell in the dwelling of Shem, and Canaan shall be his servant." 13.

18 And Ham knew that his father had cursed his younger son, and he was displeased that he had cursed his son, and he parted from his father, he and his sons with him, Cush and Mizraim and Put and Canaan.

19 And he built for himself a city and called its name after the name of his wife Ne'elatama'uk.

20 And Japheth saw it, and became envious of his brother, and he too built for himself a city, and he called its name after the name of his wife 'Adataneses.

21 And Shem dwelt with his father Noah, and he built a city close to his father on the mountain, and he too called its name

22 after the name of his wife Sedeqetelebab.

23 And behold these three cities are near Mount Lubar; Sedeqetelebab fronting the mountain on its east ; and Na'eltama'uk on the south ; 'Adatan'eses towards the west.

24 And these are the sons of Shem : Elam. and Asshur, and Arpachshad — this (son) was born two years after the flood — and Lud, and Aram.' 19.

25 The sons of Japheth : Gomer and Magog and Madai and Javan, Tubal and Meshech and Tiras : these are the sons of Noah. 20.

26 And in the twenty-eighth jubilee Noah began to enjoin upon his sons' sons the

27 ordinances and commandments, and all the judgments that he knew, and he exhorted his sons to observe righteousness, and to cover the shame of their flesh, and to bless their Creator, and honour father and mother, and love their neighbour, and guard their souls from fornication and uncleanness and all iniquity.

28 For owing to these three things came the flood upon the earth, namely, owing to the fornication wherein the Watchers against the law of their ordinances went a whoring after the daughters of men, and took themselves wives of all which they chose : and they made the beginning of uncleanness.

29 And they begat sons the Naphidim, and they were all unlike, and they devoured one another : and the Giants slew the Naphil, and the Naphil slew the Eljo, and the Eljo slew mankind, and one man slew another.

30 And every one sold himself to work iniquity and to

31 shed much blood and the earth was filled with iniquity.

32 And after this they sinned against the

33 beasts and birds, and all that moveth and walketh on the earth : and much blood was shed on the earth, and every imagination and desire of men imagined vanity and evil continually.

34 And the Lord destroyed everything from off the face of the earth ; because of the wickedness of their deeds, and because of the blood which they had shed in the midst of the earth He destroyed everything.

35 'And we were left, I and you, my sons, and everything that entered with us into the ark, and behold I see your works before me that ye do not walk in righteousness : for in the path of destruction ye have begun to walk, and ye are parting one from another, and are envious one of another, and (so it comes) that ye are not in harmony, my sons, each with his brother.

36 For I see, and behold the demons have begun (their) seductions against you and against your children and now I fear on your behalf, that after my death ye will shed the blood of men upon the earth, and that ye, too, will be destroyed from the face of the earth. For whoso sheddeth man's blood, and whoso eateth the blood of any flesh, shall all be destroyed from the earth.

37 And there will not be left any man that eateth blood.

38 Or that sheddeth the blood of man on the earth,

39 Nor will there be left to him any seed or descendants living under heaven ;

40 For into Sheol will they go,

41 And into the place of condemnation will they descend.

42 And into the darkness of the deep will they all be removed by a violent death.

43 There shall be no blood seen upon you of all the blood there shall be all the days in which ye have killed any beasts or cattle or whatever flieth upon the earth, and work ye a good work to your souls by covering that which hath been shed on the face of the earth.

44 And ye shall not be like him who eateth with blood, but guard yourselves that none may eat blood before you : cover the blood, for thus have I been commanded to testify to you and your children, together with all flesh.

45 And suffer not the soul to be eaten with the flesh, that your blood, which is your life, may not be required at the hand of any flesh that sheddeth (it) on the earth.

46 For the earth will not be clean from the blood which hath been shed upon it ; for (only) through the blood of him that shed it will the earth be purified throughout all its generations.

47 And now, my children, hearken : work judgment and righteousness that ye may be planted in righteousness ' over the face of the whole earth, and your glory lifted up before my God, who saved me from the waters of the flood.

48 And behold, ye will go and build for yourselves cities, and plant in them all the plants that are upon the earth, and moreover all fruit-bearing trees.

49 For three years the fruit of everything that is eaten will not be gathered : and in the fourth year its fruit will be accounted holy [and they will offer the first-fruits],

50 acceptable before the Most High God, who created heaven and earth and all things.

51 Let them offer in abundance the first of the wine and oil (as) first-fruits on the altar of the Lord, who receiveth it, and what is left let the servants of the house of the Lord eat before the altar which receiveth (it).

52 And in the fifth year

53 make ye the release so that ye release it s in righteousness and uprightness, and ye shall be righteous, and all that you plant will prosper.

54 For thus did Enoch, the father of your father command Methuselah, his son, and Methuselah his son Lamech, and Lamech commanded me all the things which his fathers commanded him.

55 And I also will give you commandment, my sons, as Enoch commanded his son in the first jubilees : whilst still living, the seventh in his generation, he commanded and testified to his son and to his sons' sons until the day of his death."

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: In the seventh week, in the first year thereof, in this jubilee, Noah planted vines on the mountain where the ark had rested, named Lubar, one of the Ararat mountains. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. In the seventh week, in the first year thereof, in this jubilee, Noah planted vines on the mountain where the ark had rested, named Lubar, one of the Ararat mountains. The vineyard rises on the grounding mountain itself, and the planting is dated to week and year. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 2. The vines produced fruit in the fourth year, and Noah guarded their fruit. The fourth-year fruit matters: verse 49 will make fourth-year fruit holy, and Noah's vines follow that rule from the start. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 3. He gathered the fruit in this year, in the seventh month. The gathering falls in the seventh month, the harvest season of the book's calendar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 4. He made wine from the fruit and put it into a vessel. The first wine after the flood is made from Lubar's vines, kept for a set time before use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 5. He kept the wine until the fifth year, until the first day, on the new moon of the first month. The keeping runs to a new moon of the first month, the book's memorial new moon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 6. This verse completes the keeping notice: the wine was kept until the first day, on the new moon of the first month. The date fixes the feast that follows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 7. He celebrated with joy the day of this feast, and made a burnt sacrifice to the Lord, one young ox and one ram, and seven sheep each a year old, and a kid of the goats, to make atonement for himself and his sons. The firstfruits feast joins joy, burnt sacrifice and atonement for Noah and his sons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 8. He prepared the kid first, and placed some of its blood on the flesh that was on the altar he had made. The kid comes first, and its blood is placed on the altar's flesh before the rest of the offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. All the fat he laid on the altar where he made the burnt sacrifice, with the ox, ram and sheep, laying all their flesh upon the altar. The whole burnt offering follows the kid's blood rite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. He placed all their offerings mingled with oil upon it, afterwards sprinkled wine on the fire he had previously made on the altar, and placed incense on the altar, causing a sweet savour to ascend acceptable before the Lord his God. Oil, wine and incense complete the offering, as in chapter 6's sacrifice, and the savour is acceptable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. He rejoiced and drank of this wine, he and his children with joy. Feast joy is shared: Noah and his children drink the new wine together after the sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 12. It was evening, and he went into his tent, and being drunken he lay down and slept, and was uncovered in his tent as he slept. JFB on Genesis 9:21 calls this a solitary stain on the character of so eminently pious a man, believed to be the result of age or inadvertency, perhaps at the festivities of the vintage season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Ham saw Noah his father naked, and went forth and told his two brethren without. The offence is twofold: seeing and then publishing it to his brothers outside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. Shem took his garment and arose, he and Japheth, and they placed the garment on their shoulders and went backward and covered the shame of their father, their faces backward. Reverence is shown by averted faces: they cover what Ham exposed, without looking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 15. Noah awoke from his sleep and knew all that his younger son had done to him, and he cursed his son, saying, 'Cursed be Canaan; an enslaved servant shall he be unto his brethren.' JFB on Genesis 9:25 notes the doom was fulfilled in the destruction of the Canaanites, with Ham's other descendants degraded and enslaved; Jubilees directs the curse at Canaan, Ham's younger son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. He blessed Shem, saying, 'Blessed be the Lord God of Shem, and Canaan shall be his servant.' JFB on Genesis 9:26 reads this as an intimation that Shem's descendants would be peculiarly honoured in the service of the true God, his Church established among them, and Canaan's people made their servants by conquest or submission. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. 'God shall enlarge Japheth, and God shall dwell in the dwelling of Shem, and Canaan shall be his servant.' JFB on Genesis 9:27 reads the enlargement as a vast increase in posterity and possessions, and dwelling in Shem's tents as a prophecy fulfilled as the nations come under Shem's blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Ham knew his father had cursed his younger son, and was displeased that he had cursed his son, and parted from his father, he and his sons with him, Cush and Mizraim and Put and Canaan. The curse divides the family: Ham departs with all four sons, beginning the parting Noah will warn against in verse 35. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. Ham built for himself a city and called its name after the name of his wife Ne'elatama'uk. The departed son founds his own city, named for his wife. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. Japheth saw it, became envious of his brother, and he too built for himself a city, calling its name after his wife 'Adataneses. Envy drives imitation: Japheth's city answers Ham's, the very envy Noah names in verse 35. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. Shem dwelt with his father Noah, and built a city close to his father on the mountain. Unlike Ham who departed and Japheth who envied, Shem stays beside his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. Shem too called his city's name after the name of his wife Sedeqetelebab. All three cities are named for wives, but Shem's alone stands close to his father's mountain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. Behold these three cities are near Mount Lubar: Sedeqetelebab fronting the mountain on its east, Na'eltama'uk on the south, and 'Adatan'eses towards the west. The three cities ring Lubar on three sides, east, south and west, around the ark's mountain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 24. These are the sons of Shem: Elam, Asshur, and Arpachshad, this son born two years after the flood, and Lud, and Aram. JFB on Genesis 10 notes Shem is introduced with marked distinction as the father of Eber, ancestor of the Hebrews, and Aram's descendants received Armenia, Mesopotamia and Syria. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The sons of Japheth: Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech and Tiras; these are the sons of Noah. JFB on Genesis 10:1, 5 notes the catalogue is not arranged by seniority, and Japheth's descendants settled the isles of the Gentiles, the sea-accessible countries of Europe, Asia Minor and the east of the Euxine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. This verse opens Noah's testament: in the twenty-eighth jubilee Noah began to enjoin upon his sons' sons the ordinances, commandments and judgments he knew. The sentence is completed by the exhortation of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. He exhorted his sons to observe righteousness, cover the shame of their flesh, bless their Creator, honour father and mother, love their neighbour, and guard their souls from fornication, uncleanness and all iniquity. Noah's testament gathers the moral law: righteousness, modesty, worship, family honour, neighbour love, and purity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. For owing to these three things came the flood upon the earth, namely the fornication wherein the Watchers, against the law of their ordinances, went whoring after the daughters of men and took wives of all they chose, and they made the beginning of uncleanness. Noah names the flood's causes, beginning with the Watchers' fornication against their own ordinances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. They begat sons, the Naphidim, and they were all unlike, and they devoured one another: the Giants slew the Naphil, the Naphil slew the Eljo, and the Eljo slew mankind, and one man slew another. Charles names the three classes of giants, Giants, Naphil and Eljo, destroying each other in descending order until mankind itself is slain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. This verse continues the corruption: everyone sold himself to work iniquity and to shed much blood. The giants' mutual slaughter becomes mankind's universal self-sale to iniquity and bloodshed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. The earth was filled with iniquity. The sentence begun in verse 30 closes here: iniquity fills the earth as the waters soon will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. This verse opens the next corruption: after this they sinned against the beasts and birds, and all that moves and walks on the earth. The sentence is completed by the bloodshed and evil imagination of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. Much blood was shed on the earth, and every imagination and desire of men imagined vanity and evil continually. Corruption spreads from men to beasts, and inward imagination matches outward bloodshed, echoing chapter 5's account of the flood generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. The Lord destroyed everything from off the face of the earth, because of the wickedness of their deeds and because of the blood shed in the midst of the earth. Noah's retrospective names the flood's double cause: wicked deeds and shed blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. 'And we were left, I and you, my sons, and everything that entered with us into the ark, and behold I see your works before me that ye do not walk in righteousness: for in the path of destruction ye have begun to walk, and ye are parting one from another, and are envious one of another, and so it comes that ye are not in harmony, my sons, each with his brother.' Noah warns that parting and envy, already seen in verses 18-20, repeat the pre-flood path of destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. 'For I see, and behold the demons have begun their seductions against you and against your children, and now I fear on your behalf, that after my death ye will shed the blood of men upon the earth, and that ye, too, will be destroyed from the face of the earth. For whoso sheddeth man's blood, and whoso eateth the blood of any flesh, shall all be destroyed from the earth.' Demons' seduction succeeds the Watchers, and Noah's fear is specific: bloodshed and blood-eating will destroy his sons as the flood destroyed the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. There will not be left any man that eateth blood. The curse's first line: blood-eaters will be utterly removed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. Nor any that sheddeth the blood of man on the earth. The second line extends the removal to all manslayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. Nor will there be left to him any seed or descendants living under heaven. The third line cuts off the offender's seed and descendants from life under heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. For into Sheol will they go. The fourth line sends the offenders to Sheol, the realm of the dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. And into the place of condemnation will they descend. The fifth line names their destination as condemnation, not mere death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. And into the darkness of the deep will they all be removed by a violent death. The sixth line ends the curse in the darkness of the deep, by violent death matching their violent deeds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. 'There shall be no blood seen upon you of all the blood there shall be all the days in which ye have killed any beasts or cattle or whatever flieth upon the earth, and work ye a good work to your souls by covering that which hath been shed on the face of the earth.' Blood shed in lawful killing must be covered, and covering it is a good work for the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. 'And ye shall not be like him who eateth with blood, but guard yourselves that none may eat blood before you: cover the blood, for thus have I been commanded to testify to you and your children, together with all flesh.' Guarding against blood-eating and covering shed blood is Noah's commanded testimony to his children and to all flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. 'And suffer not the soul to be eaten with the flesh, that your blood, which is your life, may not be required at the hand of any flesh that sheddeth it on the earth.' The soul is the blood, the life itself, and eating soul with flesh brings bloodguilt under chapter 6's requirement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. 'For the earth will not be clean from the blood which hath been shed upon it; for only through the blood of him that shed it will the earth be purified throughout all its generations.' Shed blood pollutes the earth, and only the shedder's blood purifies it, generation after generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. 'And now, my children, hearken: work judgment and righteousness that ye may be planted in righteousness over the face of the whole earth, and your glory lifted up before my God, who saved me from the waters of the flood.' After warnings comes the positive charge: judgment and righteousness plant them over the whole earth, with glory before the God who saved Noah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 48. 'And behold, ye will go and build for yourselves cities, and plant in them all the plants that are upon the earth, and moreover all fruit-bearing trees.' Settlement and planting follow the testament: cities and orchards are the work of the obedient generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 49. 'For three years the fruit of everything that is eaten will not be gathered: and in the fourth year its fruit will be accounted holy, and they will offer the first-fruits, acceptable before the Most High God, who created heaven and earth and all things.' Three ungathered years and a holy fourth year of firstfruits order every planting, as Noah's own vines in verse 2 followed the fourth-year rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 50. This verse completes the fourth-year rule: the holy fruit is acceptable before the Most High God, creator of heaven and earth and all things. The offering's acceptability rests on the Creator's own claim over all planting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 51. 'Let them offer in abundance the first of the wine and oil as first-fruits on the altar of the Lord, who receiveth it, and what is left let the servants of the house of the Lord eat before the altar which receiveth it.' Wine and oil firstfruits go on the altar in abundance, and the remainder feeds the Lord's house servants before the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 52. This verse opens the fifth-year rule: and in the fifth year make the release. The sentence is completed by the next verse's righteous release. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 53. 'Make ye the release so that ye release it in righteousness and uprightness, and ye shall be righteous, and all that you plant will prosper.' The fifth-year release, made righteously, brings righteousness and prosperity to all planting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 54. 'For thus did Enoch, the father of your father, command Methuselah, his son, and Methuselah his son Lamech, and Lamech commanded me all the things which his fathers commanded him.' Noah's testament is not new: it is the chain of command from Enoch through Methuselah and Lamech to Noah himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 55. 'And I also will give you commandment, my sons, as Enoch commanded his son in the first jubilees: whilst still living, the seventh in his generation, he commanded and testified to his son and to his sons' sons until the day of his death.' Noah mirrors Enoch, seventh in his generation, giving living testimony to sons and sons' sons until death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse . This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the genealogy of Shem's descendants, and Noah and his sons dividing the earth. It divides Noah's testament from the division of the earth in chapters 8 and 9. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles

  • Verse 8. He prepared the kid first, and placed some of its blood on the flesh that was on the altar he had made. The kid comes first, and its blood is placed on the altar's flesh before the rest of the offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 9. All the fat he laid on the altar where he made the burnt sacrifice, with the ox, ram and sheep, laying all their flesh upon the altar. The whole burnt offering follows the kid's blood rite. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 10. He placed all their offerings mingled with oil upon it, afterwards sprinkled wine on the fire he had previously made on the altar, and placed incense on the altar, causing a sweet savour to ascend acceptable before the Lord his God. Oil, wine and incense complete the offering, as in chapter 6's sacrifice, and the savour is acceptable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 11. He rejoiced and drank of this wine, he and his children with joy. Feast joy is shared: Noah and his children drink the new wine together after the sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 13. Ham saw Noah his father naked, and went forth and told his two brethren without. The offence is twofold: seeing and then publishing it to his brothers outside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 14. Shem took his garment and arose, he and Japheth, and they placed the garment on their shoulders and went backward and covered the shame of their father, their faces backward. Reverence is shown by averted faces: they cover what Ham exposed, without looking. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 18. Ham knew his father had cursed his younger son, and was displeased that he had cursed his son, and parted from his father, he and his sons with him, Cush and Mizraim and Put and Canaan. The curse divides the family: Ham departs with all four sons, beginning the parting Noah will warn against in verse 35. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 19. Ham built for himself a city and called its name after the name of his wife Ne'elatama'uk. The departed son founds his own city, named for his wife. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 20. Japheth saw it, became envious of his brother, and he too built for himself a city, calling its name after his wife 'Adataneses. Envy drives imitation: Japheth's city answers Ham's, the very envy Noah names in verse 35. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 21. Shem dwelt with his father Noah, and built a city close to his father on the mountain. Unlike Ham who departed and Japheth who envied, Shem stays beside his father. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 22. Shem too called his city's name after the name of his wife Sedeqetelebab. All three cities are named for wives, but Shem's alone stands close to his father's mountain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 23. Behold these three cities are near Mount Lubar: Sedeqetelebab fronting the mountain on its east, Na'eltama'uk on the south, and 'Adatan'eses towards the west. The three cities ring Lubar on three sides, east, south and west, around the ark's mountain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 26. This verse opens Noah's testament: in the twenty-eighth jubilee Noah began to enjoin upon his sons' sons the ordinances, commandments and judgments he knew. The sentence is completed by the exhortation of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 27. He exhorted his sons to observe righteousness, cover the shame of their flesh, bless their Creator, honour father and mother, love their neighbour, and guard their souls from fornication, uncleanness and all iniquity. Noah's testament gathers the moral law: righteousness, modesty, worship, family honour, neighbour love, and purity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 28. For owing to these three things came the flood upon the earth, namely the fornication wherein the Watchers, against the law of their ordinances, went whoring after the daughters of men and took wives of all they chose, and they made the beginning of uncleanness. Noah names the flood's causes, beginning with the Watchers' fornication against their own ordinances. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 29. They begat sons, the Naphidim, and they were all unlike, and they devoured one another: the Giants slew the Naphil, the Naphil slew the Eljo, and the Eljo slew mankind, and one man slew another. Charles names the three classes of giants, Giants, Naphil and Eljo, destroying each other in descending order until mankind itself is slain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 30. This verse continues the corruption: everyone sold himself to work iniquity and to shed much blood. The giants' mutual slaughter becomes mankind's universal self-sale to iniquity and bloodshed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 31. The earth was filled with iniquity. The sentence begun in verse 30 closes here: iniquity fills the earth as the waters soon will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 32. This verse opens the next corruption: after this they sinned against the beasts and birds, and all that moves and walks on the earth. The sentence is completed by the bloodshed and evil imagination of the next verse. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 33. Much blood was shed on the earth, and every imagination and desire of men imagined vanity and evil continually. Corruption spreads from men to beasts, and inward imagination matches outward bloodshed, echoing chapter 5's account of the flood generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 34. The Lord destroyed everything from off the face of the earth, because of the wickedness of their deeds and because of the blood shed in the midst of the earth. Noah's retrospective names the flood's double cause: wicked deeds and shed blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 35. 'And we were left, I and you, my sons, and everything that entered with us into the ark, and behold I see your works before me that ye do not walk in righteousness: for in the path of destruction ye have begun to walk, and ye are parting one from another, and are envious one of another, and so it comes that ye are not in harmony, my sons, each with his brother.' Noah warns that parting and envy, already seen in verses 18-20, repeat the pre-flood path of destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 36. 'For I see, and behold the demons have begun their seductions against you and against your children, and now I fear on your behalf, that after my death ye will shed the blood of men upon the earth, and that ye, too, will be destroyed from the face of the earth. For whoso sheddeth man's blood, and whoso eateth the blood of any flesh, shall all be destroyed from the earth.' Demons' seduction succeeds the Watchers, and Noah's fear is specific: bloodshed and blood-eating will destroy his sons as the flood destroyed the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 37. There will not be left any man that eateth blood. The curse's first line: blood-eaters will be utterly removed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 38. Nor any that sheddeth the blood of man on the earth. The second line extends the removal to all manslayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 39. Nor will there be left to him any seed or descendants living under heaven. The third line cuts off the offender's seed and descendants from life under heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 40. For into Sheol will they go. The fourth line sends the offenders to Sheol, the realm of the dead. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 41. And into the place of condemnation will they descend. The fifth line names their destination as condemnation, not mere death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 42. And into the darkness of the deep will they all be removed by a violent death. The sixth line ends the curse in the darkness of the deep, by violent death matching their violent deeds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 43. 'There shall be no blood seen upon you of all the blood there shall be all the days in which ye have killed any beasts or cattle or whatever flieth upon the earth, and work ye a good work to your souls by covering that which hath been shed on the face of the earth.' Blood shed in lawful killing must be covered, and covering it is a good work for the soul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 44. 'And ye shall not be like him who eateth with blood, but guard yourselves that none may eat blood before you: cover the blood, for thus have I been commanded to testify to you and your children, together with all flesh.' Guarding against blood-eating and covering shed blood is Noah's commanded testimony to his children and to all flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 45. 'And suffer not the soul to be eaten with the flesh, that your blood, which is your life, may not be required at the hand of any flesh that sheddeth it on the earth.' The soul is the blood, the life itself, and eating soul with flesh brings bloodguilt under chapter 6's requirement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 46. 'For the earth will not be clean from the blood which hath been shed upon it; for only through the blood of him that shed it will the earth be purified throughout all its generations.' Shed blood pollutes the earth, and only the shedder's blood purifies it, generation after generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 47. 'And now, my children, hearken: work judgment and righteousness that ye may be planted in righteousness over the face of the whole earth, and your glory lifted up before my God, who saved me from the waters of the flood.' After warnings comes the positive charge: judgment and righteousness plant them over the whole earth, with glory before the God who saved Noah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 48. 'And behold, ye will go and build for yourselves cities, and plant in them all the plants that are upon the earth, and moreover all fruit-bearing trees.' Settlement and planting follow the testament: cities and orchards are the work of the obedient generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 49. 'For three years the fruit of everything that is eaten will not be gathered: and in the fourth year its fruit will be accounted holy, and they will offer the first-fruits, acceptable before the Most High God, who created heaven and earth and all things.' Three ungathered years and a holy fourth year of firstfruits order every planting, as Noah's own vines in verse 2 followed the fourth-year rule. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 50. This verse completes the fourth-year rule: the holy fruit is acceptable before the Most High God, creator of heaven and earth and all things. The offering's acceptability rests on the Creator's own claim over all planting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 51. 'Let them offer in abundance the first of the wine and oil as first-fruits on the altar of the Lord, who receiveth it, and what is left let the servants of the house of the Lord eat before the altar which receiveth it.' Wine and oil firstfruits go on the altar in abundance, and the remainder feeds the Lord's house servants before the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 52. This verse opens the fifth-year rule: and in the fifth year make the release. The sentence is completed by the next verse's righteous release. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 53. 'Make ye the release so that ye release it in righteousness and uprightness, and ye shall be righteous, and all that you plant will prosper.' The fifth-year release, made righteously, brings righteousness and prosperity to all planting. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 54. 'For thus did Enoch, the father of your father, command Methuselah, his son, and Methuselah his son Lamech, and Lamech commanded me all the things which his fathers commanded him.' Noah's testament is not new: it is the chain of command from Enoch through Methuselah and Lamech to Noah himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse 55. 'And I also will give you commandment, my sons, as Enoch commanded his son in the first jubilees: whilst still living, the seventh in his generation, he commanded and testified to his son and to his sons' sons until the day of his death.' Noah mirrors Enoch, seventh in his generation, giving living testimony to sons and sons' sons until death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
  • Verse . This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the genealogy of Shem's descendants, and Noah and his sons dividing the earth. It divides Noah's testament from the division of the earth in chapters 8 and 9. Interpreted Study note, summarizing R. H. Charles
