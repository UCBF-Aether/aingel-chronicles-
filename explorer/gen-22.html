The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass after these things, that God did tempt Abraham, and said unto him, Abraham: and he said, Behold, here I am.

2 And he said, Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah; and offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of.

3 And Abraham rose up early in the morning, and saddled his ass, and took two of his young men with him, and Isaac his son, and clave the wood for the burnt offering, and rose up, and went unto the place of which God had told him.

4 Then on the third day Abraham lifted up his eyes, and saw the place afar off.

5 And Abraham said unto his young men, Abide ye here with the ass; and I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you.

6 And Abraham took the wood of the burnt offering, and laid it upon Isaac his son; and he took the fire in his hand, and a knife; and they went both of them together.

7 And Isaac spake unto Abraham his father, and said, My father: and he said, Here am I, my son. And he said, Behold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?

8 And Abraham said, My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.

9 And they came to the place which God had told him of; and Abraham built an altar there, and laid the wood in order, and bound Isaac his son, and laid him on the altar upon the wood.

10 And Abraham stretched forth his hand, and took the knife to slay his son.

11 And the angel of the LORD called unto him out of heaven, and said, Abraham, Abraham: and he said, Here am I.

12 And he said, Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou any thing unto him: for now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me.

13 And Abraham lifted up his eyes, and looked, and behold behind him a ram caught in a thicket by his horns: and Abraham went and took the ram, and offered him up for a burnt offering in the stead of his son.

14 And Abraham called the name of that place Jehovahjireh: as it is said to this day, In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen.

15 And the angel of the LORD called unto Abraham out of heaven the second time,

16 And said, By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD, for because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son:

17 That in blessing I will bless thee, and in multiplying I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven, and as the sand which is upon the sea shore; and thy seed shall possess the gate of his enemies;

18 And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thou hast obeyed my voice.

19 So Abraham returned unto his young men, and they rose up and went together to Beersheba; and Abraham dwelt at Beersheba.

20 And it came to pass after these things, that it was told Abraham, saying, Behold, Milcah, she hath also born children unto thy brother Nahor;

21 Huz his firstborn, and Buz his brother, and Kemuel the father of Aram,

22 And Chesed, and Hazo, and Pildash, and Jidlaph, and Bethuel.

23 And Bethuel begat Rebekah: these eight Milcah did bear to Nahor, Abraham’s brother.

24 And his concubine, whose name was Reumah, she bare also Tebah, and Gaham, and Thahash, and Maachah.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

God tests Abraham by commanding him to offer Isaac, his only son, on a mountain in Moriah. Abraham obeys to the point of raising the knife, is stopped, offers a ram caught in a thicket instead, names the place Jehovah-jireh, and receives the oath renewing blessing and offspring. Verses 21, 22 and 24 list Nahor's family and are carried in the Text panel, on which the study notes are silent. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. God tested Abraham, not by tempting him to sin but by proving his faith, and Abraham answered at once, "ready at a moment's warning" for God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The command named Isaac in ever closer terms, his son, his only son, whom he loved, so that the cost of obedience was felt in full before it was obeyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Abraham rose early and prepared everything beforehand, wood, knife and servants, showing no delay or reluctance, and set out on the two-day journey to Moriah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. On the third day Abraham saw the place from afar, leaving the long journey behind and approaching the appointed mountain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Abraham told his servants to stay with the donkey while he and Isaac went to worship, adding that they would come back, a word of trust in the outcome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Isaac carried the wood and Abraham the fire and knife, and father and son walked together, Isaac bearing the load as a willing participant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Isaac asked where the lamb for the offering was, seeing fire and wood but no victim, a natural question from a son who knew the pattern of sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Abraham answered that God himself would provide a lamb, words spoken in confidence that God would find a way, even if he could not yet see how Isaac would be restored. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. At the place Abraham built an altar, arranged the wood, bound Isaac and laid him on it, an act Henry and JFB say required sustaining faith in both father and son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Abraham stretched out his hand and took the knife, carrying obedience to the very point of action. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The angel of the Lord called Abraham by name from heaven, stopping him at the decisive moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. God forbade any harm to Isaac, declaring that Abraham's willingness proved he feared God, since he had not withheld his only son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Abraham saw a ram caught by its horns in a thicket and offered it instead of his son, a substitution provided at exactly the needed moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Abraham named the place Jehovah-jireh, the Lord will provide, and the saying arose that in the mount of the Lord provision shall be seen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. The angel called to Abraham a second time from heaven, now to confirm blessing after the test had been passed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. God swore by himself, since he could swear by no greater, that because Abraham had not withheld his son, blessing would surely follow. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. God promised descendants as numerous as stars and sand, who would possess the gate of their enemies, a picture of fruitful and victorious posterity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. In Abraham's seed all nations would be blessed, because he had obeyed; JFB notes faith and obedience are presented together here, faith proved by works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Abraham returned to his young men, and they went together to Beersheba, where Abraham settled after the trial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. After these things Abraham was told that Milcah had also borne children to his brother Nahor, news that quietly prepares the way for Rebekah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Bethuel, one of Nahor's sons, became the father of Rebekah, the woman who would later become Isaac's wife and carry the promise forward. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Jehovah-jireh. The name Abraham gave the place, meaning the LORD will provide; from it arose the saying that in the mount of the LORD provision shall be seen. Interpreted Study note on Genesis 22:14, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
