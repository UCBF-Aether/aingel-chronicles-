The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Acts 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then said the high priest, Are these things so?

2 And he said, Men, brethren, and fathers, hearken; The God of glory appeared unto our father Abraham, when he was in Mesopotamia, before he dwelt in Charran,

3 And said unto him, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and come into the land which I shall shew thee.

4 Then came he out of the land of the Chaldaeans, and dwelt in Charran: and from thence, when his father was dead, he removed him into this land, wherein ye now dwell.

5 And he gave him none inheritance in it, no, not so much as to set his foot on: yet he promised that he would give it to him for a possession, and to his seed after him, when as yet he had no child.

6 And God spake on this wise, That his seed should sojourn in a strange land; and that they should bring them into bondage, and entreat them evil four hundred years.

7 And the nation to whom they shall be in bondage will I judge, said God: and after that shall they come forth, and serve me in this place.

8 And he gave him the covenant of circumcision: and so Abraham begat Isaac, and circumcised him the eighth day; and Isaac begat Jacob; and Jacob begat the twelve patriarchs.

9 And the patriarchs, moved with envy, sold Joseph into Egypt: but God was with him,

10 And delivered him out of all his afflictions, and gave him favour and wisdom in the sight of Pharaoh king of Egypt; and he made him governor over Egypt and all his house.

11 Now there came a dearth over all the land of Egypt and Chanaan, and great affliction: and our fathers found no sustenance.

12 But when Jacob heard that there was corn in Egypt, he sent out our fathers first.

13 And at the second time Joseph was made known to his brethren; and Joseph’s kindred was made known unto Pharaoh.

14 Then sent Joseph, and called his father Jacob to him, and all his kindred, threescore and fifteen souls.

15 So Jacob went down into Egypt, and died, he, and our fathers,

16 And were carried over into Sychem, and laid in the sepulchre that Abraham bought for a sum of money of the sons of Emmor the father of Sychem.

17 But when the time of the promise drew nigh, which God had sworn to Abraham, the people grew and multiplied in Egypt,

18 Till another king arose, which knew not Joseph.

19 The same dealt subtilly with our kindred, and evil entreated our fathers, so that they cast out their young children, to the end they might not live.

20 In which time Moses was born, and was exceeding fair, and nourished up in his father’s house three months:

21 And when he was cast out, Pharaoh’s daughter took him up, and nourished him for her own son.

22 And Moses was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and was mighty in words and in deeds.

23 And when he was full forty years old, it came into his heart to visit his brethren the children of Israel.

24 And seeing one of them suffer wrong, he defended him, and avenged him that was oppressed, and smote the Egyptian:

25 For he supposed his brethren would have understood how that God by his hand would deliver them: but they understood not.

26 And the next day he shewed himself unto them as they strove, and would have set them at one again, saying, Sirs, ye are brethren; why do ye wrong one to another?

27 But he that did his neighbour wrong thrust him away, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge over us?

28 Wilt thou kill me, as thou diddest the Egyptian yesterday?

29 Then fled Moses at this saying, and was a stranger in the land of Madian, where he begat two sons.

30 And when forty years were expired, there appeared to him in the wilderness of mount Sina an angel of the Lord in a flame of fire in a bush.

31 When Moses saw it, he wondered at the sight: and as he drew near to behold it, the voice of the LORD came unto him,

32 Saying, I am the God of thy fathers, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. Then Moses trembled, and durst not behold.

33 Then said the Lord to him, Put off thy shoes from thy feet: for the place where thou standest is holy ground.

34 I have seen, I have seen the affliction of my people which is in Egypt, and I have heard their groaning, and am come down to deliver them. And now come, I will send thee into Egypt.

35 This Moses whom they refused, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge? the same did God send to be a ruler and a deliverer by the hand of the angel which appeared to him in the bush.

36 He brought them out, after that he had shewed wonders and signs in the land of Egypt, and in the Red sea, and in the wilderness forty years.

37 This is that Moses, which said unto the children of Israel, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear.

38 This is he, that was in the church in the wilderness with the angel which spake to him in the mount Sina, and with our fathers: who received the lively oracles to give unto us:

39 To whom our fathers would not obey, but thrust him from them, and in their hearts turned back again into Egypt,

40 Saying unto Aaron, Make us gods to go before us: for as for this Moses, which brought us out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him.

41 And they made a calf in those days, and offered sacrifice unto the idol, and rejoiced in the works of their own hands.

42 Then God turned, and gave them up to worship the host of heaven; as it is written in the book of the prophets, O ye house of Israel, have ye offered to me slain beasts and sacrifices by the space of forty years in the wilderness?

43 Yea, ye took up the tabernacle of Moloch, and the star of your god Remphan, figures which ye made to worship them: and I will carry you away beyond Babylon.

44 Our fathers had the tabernacle of witness in the wilderness, as he had appointed, speaking unto Moses, that he should make it according to the fashion that he had seen.

45 Which also our fathers that came after brought in with Jesus into the possession of the Gentiles, whom God drave out before the face of our fathers, unto the days of David;

46 Who found favour before God, and desired to find a tabernacle for the God of Jacob.

47 But Solomon built him an house.

48 Howbeit the most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands; as saith the prophet,

49 Heaven is my throne, and earth is my footstool: what house will ye build me? saith the Lord: or what is the place of my rest?

50 Hath not my hand made all these things?

51 Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost: as your fathers did, so do ye.

52 Which of the prophets have not your fathers persecuted? and they have slain them which shewed before of the coming of the Just One; of whom ye have been now the betrayers and murderers:

53 Who have received the law by the disposition of angels, and have not kept it.

54 When they heard these things, they were cut to the heart, and they gnashed on him with their teeth.

55 But he, being full of the Holy Ghost, looked up stedfastly into heaven, and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God,

56 And said, Behold, I see the heavens opened, and the Son of man standing on the right hand of God.

57 Then they cried out with a loud voice, and stopped their ears, and ran upon him with one accord,

58 And cast him out of the city, and stoned him: and the witnesses laid down their clothes at a young man’s feet, whose name was Saul.

59 And they stoned Stephen, calling upon God, and saying, Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.

60 And he kneeled down, and cried with a loud voice, Lord, lay not this sin to their charge. And when he had said this, he fell asleep.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Stephen answers the charge against him by retelling Israel's story: Abraham called out of Mesopotamia, Joseph sold yet raised up in Egypt, Moses refused yet sent as deliverer, and the people turning to idols in the wilderness. He argues that God does not dwell only in temples made with hands, charges his hearers with resisting the Spirit as their fathers did, and is stoned while praying for his killers' forgiveness. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then said the high priest, Are these things so? Henry frames Stephen's whole defence as answering the charge of blasphemy and apostasy by showing that he is a son of Abraham and values himself on it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The God of glory appeared unto our father Abraham when he was in Mesopotamia, before he dwelt in Charran. Henry reads Abraham's calling as showing the power and freeness of divine grace and the nature of conversion, and adds that outward forms and distinctions are as nothing compared with separation from the world and devotedness to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Get thee out of thy country and from thy kindred, and come into the land which I shall shew thee. Henry's lesson from the call is separation: outward forms are nothing beside leaving the world behind and being devoted to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Then came he out of the land of the Chaldaeans and dwelt in Charran, and from thence, when his father was dead, he removed him into the land of Canaan. Henry notes the slow steps by which the promise made to Abraham advanced toward performance, plainly showing it had a spiritual meaning and that the land intended was the heavenly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He gave him none inheritance in it, no, not so much as to set his foot on, yet he promised that he would give it to him for a possession, and to his seed after him. Henry's point is the same slow advance: the promise moved by steps that show its spiritual meaning, with the heavenly land in view. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. God spake on this wise, that his seed should sojourn in a strange land, and that they should bring them into bondage and entreat them evil four hundred years. Henry warns not to be discouraged at the slowness of the fulfilling of God's promises, for suffering times often are growing times with the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The nation to whom they shall be in bondage will I judge, said God, and after that shall they come forth and serve me in this place. Henry adds that God is preparing for his people's deliverance when their day is darkest and their distress deepest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. He gave him the covenant of circumcision, and so Abraham begat Isaac, and Isaac begat Jacob, and Jacob begat the twelve patriarchs. Henry notes it is well to recur to the first rise of usages or sentiments which have been perverted, and the covenant sign stands at the head of the line Stephen traces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The patriarchs, moved with envy, sold Joseph into Egypt, but God was with him. Henry reminds the Jews of the wickedness of the patriarchs of their tribes in envying their brother Joseph, and says the same spirit was still working in them toward Christ and his ministers, while God owned Joseph in his troubles and was with him by the power of his Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And delivered him out of all his afflictions, and gave him favour and wisdom in the sight of Pharaoh king of Egypt, who made him governor over Egypt and all his house. Henry's gloss is that God was with Joseph by his Spirit, both on his own mind by giving him comfort, and on those he was concerned with, by giving him favour in their eyes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The faith of the patriarchs in desiring to be buried in the land of Canaan is Henry's point here: carried over into Sychem and laid in the sepulchre Abraham bought, they plainly showed they had regard to the heavenly country. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. But when the time of the promise drew nigh, which God had sworn to Abraham, the people grew and multiplied in Egypt. Henry's encouragement fits the growth in bondage: suffering times often are growing times with the church, and we should not be discouraged at the slowness of God's promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Till another king arose, which knew not Joseph. Henry's pattern is that God is preparing for his people's deliverance when their day is darkest, and a king who knew not Joseph marks that darkening. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The same dealt subtilly with our kindred, and evil entreated our fathers, so that they cast out their young children, to the end they might not live. Henry says God prepares deliverance when distress is deepest, and this subtil cruelty is the depth from which deliverance would come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. In which time Moses was born, and was exceeding fair, and nourished up in his father's house three months. Henry explains the fairness: Moses was exceeding fair, fair toward God, and it is the beauty of holiness which is in God's sight of great price, and he was wonderfully preserved in his infancy, for God takes special care of those of whom he designs to make special use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. When he was cast out, Pharaoh's daughter took him up and nourished him for her own son. Henry's preservation point continues: God will take special care of those of whom he designs to make special use, and the rescue by Pharaoh's daughter is that care at work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. For he supposed his brethren would have understood how that God by his hand would deliver them, but they understood not. Henry turns the misunderstanding toward Stephen's hearers: if they do not wilfully shut their eyes, they may understand that God will, by this Jesus, deliver them out of a worse slavery than that of Egypt, and though men prolong their own miseries, the Lord will effect his own designs of mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. But he that did his neighbour wrong thrust him away, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge over us? Henry's later point anticipates this refusal: this Moses whom they refused, even this same has God advanced to be a Prince and Saviour, just as the Jesus they now refuse has been advanced. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. When forty years were expired, there appeared to him in the wilderness of mount Sina an angel of the Lord in a flame of fire in a bush. Henry says men deceive themselves if they think God cannot do what he sees to be good anywhere, for he can bring his people into a wilderness and there speak comfortably to them, and the bush not consumed represented Israel in the fire of affliction, yet not consumed, and may be a type of Christ taking human nature upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Saying, I am the God of thy fathers, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. Henry draws the resurrection proof from the name: the death of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob cannot break the covenant relation between God and them, for Abraham is dead, yet God is still his God, therefore Abraham is still alive, and this is the life and immortality brought to light by the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. I have seen, I have seen the affliction of my people which is in Egypt, and I have heard their groaning, and am come down to deliver them. Henry says God has compassion for the troubles of his church and the groans of his persecuted people, and their deliverance takes rise from his pity, and that deliverance was typical of what Christ did when, for us men and for our salvation, he came down from heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. This Moses whom they refused, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge? the same did God send to be a ruler and a deliverer by the hand of the angel which appeared to him in the bush. Henry states the type plainly: Moses was an eminent type of Christ as Israel's deliverer, and this Jesus, whom they now refused as their fathers did Moses, even this same has God advanced to be a Prince and Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. He brought them out, after that he had shewed wonders and signs in the land of Egypt, and in the Red sea, and in the wilderness forty years. Henry traces the deliverance to its rise in God's pity for his persecuted people, a deliverance typical of Christ's coming down from heaven for our salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. This is that Moses, which said unto the children of Israel, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear. Henry defends Stephen against the blasphemy charge here: in asserting that Jesus should change the customs of the ceremonial law, Stephen was so far from blaspheming Moses that he honoured him, by showing how the prophecy of Moses was come to pass, and God who gave those customs by Moses might no doubt change the custom by his Son Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. To whom our fathers would not obey, but thrust him from them, and in their hearts turned back again into Egypt. Henry's application is direct: Israel thrust Moses from them and would have returned to their bondage, so men in general will not obey Jesus, because they love this present evil world and rejoice in their own works and devices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Saying unto Aaron, Make us gods to go before us, for as for this Moses, which brought us out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him. Henry reads the demand for gods as the same turning back: they thrust Moses away and chose their own works and devices over obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. They made a calf in those days, and offered sacrifice unto the idol, and rejoiced in the works of their own hands. Henry joins this to the fathers' idolatry, to which God gave them up as a punishment for their early forsaking of him, and notes men rejoice in their own works and devices instead of obeying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 42. Then God turned, and gave them up to worship the host of heaven, as it is written in the book of the prophets. Henry's charge is that Stephen upbraids the Jews with the idolatry of their fathers, to which God gave them up as a punishment for their early forsaking him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 43. Yea, ye took up the tabernacle of Moloch, and the star of your god Remphan, figures which ye made to worship them, and I will carry you away beyond Babylon. Henry's point stands over the whole idolatry passage: God gave them up to the worship they chose, as punishment for forsaking him early. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Our fathers had the tabernacle of witness in the wilderness, as he had appointed, speaking unto Moses, that he should make it according to the fashion that he had seen. Henry's succession argument begins here: it was no dishonour but an honour to God that the tabernacle gave way to the temple, so it is now that the earthly temple gives way to the spiritual one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 45. Which also our fathers that came after brought in with Jesus into the possession of the Gentiles, whom God drave out before the face of our fathers, unto the days of David. Henry continues the succession: the tabernacle gave way to the temple without dishonour to God, as the earthly temple now gives way to the spiritual. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. But Solomon built him an house. Henry's point is the succession itself: the tabernacle gave way to the temple, the earthly temple gives way to the spiritual one, and at last the spiritual shall give way to the eternal one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Howbeit the most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands, as saith the prophet. Henry asks with Stephen: the whole world is God's temple, in which he is everywhere present and fills it with his glory, so what occasion has he for a temple to manifest himself in? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 49. Heaven is my throne, and earth is my footstool: what house will ye build me? saith the Lord: or what is the place of my rest? Henry adds that as heaven is his throne and the earth his footstool, none of our services can profit him who made all things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Hath not my hand made all these things? Henry closes the temple argument: none of our services can profit him who made all things, and next to the human nature of Christ, the broken and spiritual heart is his most valued temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost: as your fathers did, so do ye. Henry explains the break in Stephen's defence: perceiving they would not bear the argument about the temple, he broke off and by the Spirit of wisdom, courage and power sharply rebuked his persecutors, for there is in our sinful hearts that which always resists the Holy Ghost, though in God's elect this resistance is overcome when the fulness of time comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Which of the prophets have not your fathers persecuted? and they have slain them which shewed before of the coming of the Just One, of whom ye have been now the betrayers and murderers. Henry says their guilt stung them to the heart, and they sought relief in murdering their reprover instead of sorrow and supplication for mercy, and when plain truths provoke opposers of the gospel, they should be shown their guilt and danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. Who have received the law by the disposition of angels, and have not kept it. Henry contrasts the manner of giving: the gospel was offered now, not by angels, but from the Holy Ghost, yet they did not embrace it, for they were resolved not to comply with God either in his law or in his gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. When they heard these things, they were cut to the heart, and they gnashed on him with their teeth. Henry's diagnosis is that their guilt stung them to the heart, and they sought relief in murdering their reprover instead of sorrow and supplication for mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. But he, being full of the Holy Ghost, looked up stedfastly into heaven, and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God. Henry says nothing is so comfortable to dying saints, or so encouraging to suffering saints, as to see Jesus at the right hand of God, and blessed be God, by faith we may see him there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. And said, Behold, I see the heavens opened, and the Son of man standing on the right hand of God. Henry repeats the comfort: to see Jesus at the right hand of God is the encouragement of suffering saints, and by faith believers may see him there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 59. And they stoned Stephen, calling upon God, and saying, Lord Jesus, receive my spirit. Henry notes Stephen offered up two short prayers in his dying moments, and that our Lord Jesus is God, to whom we are to seek and in whom we are to trust and comfort ourselves, living and dying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 60. And he kneeled down, and cried with a loud voice, Lord, lay not this sin to their charge. And when he had said this, he fell asleep. Henry calls this the prayer for his persecutors: though the sin was very great, yet if they would lay it to their hearts, God would not lay it to their charge, and Stephen died with as much composure as if he had been going to sleep, to awake again in the morning of the resurrection into the presence of the Lord, where is fulness of joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry turns the misunderstanding toward Stephen's hearers: if they do not wilfully shut their eyes, they may understand that God will, by this Jesus, deliver them out of a worse slavery than that of Egypt, and though men prolong their own miseries, the Lord will effect his own designs of mercy.   • Verse 27. But he that did his neighbour wrong thrust him away, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge over us? Henry's later point anticipates this refusal: this Moses whom they refused, even this same has God advanced to be a Prince and Saviour, just as the Jesus they now refuse has been advanced.   • Verse 30. When forty years were expired, there appeared to him in the wilderness of mount Sina an angel of the Lord in a flame of fire in a bush. Henry says men deceive themselves if they think God cannot do what he sees to be good anywhere, for he can bring his people into a wilderness and there speak comfortably to them, and the bush not consumed represented Israel in the fire of affliction, yet not consumed, and may be a type of Christ taking human nature upon him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Saying, I am the God of thy fathers, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. Henry draws the resurrection proof from the name: the death of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob cannot break the covenant relation between God and them, for Abraham is dead, yet God is still his God, therefore Abraham is still alive, and this is the life and immortality brought to light by the gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. I have seen, I have seen the affliction of my people which is in Egypt, and I have heard their groaning, and am come down to deliver them. Henry says God has compassion for the troubles of his church and the groans of his persecuted people, and their deliverance takes rise from his pity, and that deliverance was typical of what Christ did when, for us men and for our salvation, he came down from heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. This Moses whom they refused, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge? the same did God send to be a ruler and a deliverer by the hand of the angel which appeared to him in the bush. Henry states the type plainly: Moses was an eminent type of Christ as Israel's deliverer, and this Jesus, whom they now refused as their fathers did Moses, even this same has God advanced to be a Prince and Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. He brought them out, after that he had shewed wonders and signs in the land of Egypt, and in the Red sea, and in the wilderness forty years. Henry traces the deliverance to its rise in God's pity for his persecuted people, a deliverance typical of Christ's coming down from heaven for our salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 37. This is that Moses, which said unto the children of Israel, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear. Henry defends Stephen against the blasphemy charge here: in asserting that Jesus should change the customs of the ceremonial law, Stephen was so far from blaspheming Moses that he honoured him, by showing how the prophecy of Moses was come to pass, and God who gave those customs by Moses might no doubt change the custom by his Son Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. To whom our fathers would not obey, but thrust him from them, and in their hearts turned back again into Egypt. Henry's application is direct: Israel thrust Moses from them and would have returned to their bondage, so men in general will not obey Jesus, because they love this present evil world and rejoice in their own works and devices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. Saying unto Aaron, Make us gods to go before us, for as for this Moses, which brought us out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him. Henry reads the demand for gods as the same turning back: they thrust Moses away and chose their own works and devices over obedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. They made a calf in those days, and offered sacrifice unto the idol, and rejoiced in the works of their own hands. Henry joins this to the fathers' idolatry, to which God gave them up as a punishment for their early forsaking of him, and notes men rejoice in their own works and devices instead of obeying.   • Verse 42. Then God turned, and gave them up to worship the host of heaven, as it is written in the book of the prophets. Henry's charge is that Stephen upbraids the Jews with the idolatry of their fathers, to which God gave them up as a punishment for their early forsaking him.   • Verse 43. Yea, ye took up the tabernacle of Moloch, and the star of your god Remphan, figures which ye made to worship them, and I will carry you away beyond Babylon. Henry's point stands over the whole idolatry passage: God gave them up to the worship they chose, as punishment for forsaking him early. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 44. Our fathers had the tabernacle of witness in the wilderness, as he had appointed, speaking unto Moses, that he should make it according to the fashion that he had seen. Henry's succession argument begins here: it was no dishonour but an honour to God that the tabernacle gave way to the temple, so it is now that the earthly temple gives way to the spiritual one.   • Verse 45. Which also our fathers that came after brought in with Jesus into the possession of the Gentiles, whom God drave out before the face of our fathers, unto the days of David. Henry continues the succession: the tabernacle gave way to the temple without dishonour to God, as the earthly temple now gives way to the spiritual. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 47. But Solomon built him an house. Henry's point is the succession itself: the tabernacle gave way to the temple, the earthly temple gives way to the spiritual one, and at last the spiritual shall give way to the eternal one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 48. Howbeit the most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands, as saith the prophet. Henry asks with Stephen: the whole world is God's temple, in which he is everywhere present and fills it with his glory, so what occasion has he for a temple to manifest himself in?   • Verse 49. Heaven is my throne, and earth is my footstool: what house will ye build me? saith the Lord: or what is the place of my rest? Henry adds that as heaven is his throne and the earth his footstool, none of our services can profit him who made all things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 50. Hath not my hand made all these things? Henry closes the temple argument: none of our services can profit him who made all things, and next to the human nature of Christ, the broken and spiritual heart is his most valued temple. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 51. Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost: as your fathers did, so do ye. Henry explains the break in Stephen's defence: perceiving they would not bear the argument about the temple, he broke off and by the Spirit of wisdom, courage and power sharply rebuked his persecutors, for there is in our sinful hearts that which always resists the Holy Ghost, though in God's elect this resistance is overcome when the fulness of time comes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 52. Which of the prophets have not your fathers persecuted? and they have slain them which shewed before of the coming of the Just One, of whom ye have been now the betrayers and murderers. Henry says their guilt stung them to the heart, and they sought relief in murdering their reprover instead of sorrow and supplication for mercy, and when plain truths provoke opposers of the gospel, they should be shown their guilt and danger. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 53. Who have received the law by the disposition of angels, and have not kept it. Henry contrasts the manner of giving: the gospel was offered now, not by angels, but from the Holy Ghost, yet they did not embrace it, for they were resolved not to comply with God either in his law or in his gospel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 54. When they heard these things, they were cut to the heart, and they gnashed on him with their teeth. Henry's diagnosis is that their guilt stung them to the heart, and they sought relief in murdering their reprover instead of sorrow and supplication for mercy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 55. But he, being full of the Holy Ghost, looked up stedfastly into heaven, and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God. Henry says nothing is so comfortable to dying saints, or so encouraging to suffering saints, as to see Jesus at the right hand of God, and blessed be God, by faith we may see him there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 56. And said, Behold, I see the heavens opened, and the Son of man standing on the right hand of God. Henry repeats the comfort: to see Jesus at the right hand of God is the encouragement of suffering saints, and by faith believers may see him there. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 59. And they stoned Stephen, calling upon God, and saying, Lord Jesus, receive my spirit. Henry notes Stephen offered up two short prayers in his dying moments, and that our Lord Jesus is God, to whom we are to seek and in whom we are to trust and comfort ourselves, living and dying. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 60. And he kneeled down, and cried with a loud voice, Lord, lay not this sin to their charge. And when he had said this, he fell asleep. Henry calls this the prayer for his persecutors: though the sin was very great, yet if they would lay it to their hearts, God would not lay it to their charge, and Stephen died with as much composure as if he had been going to sleep, to awake again in the morning of the resurrection into the presence of the Lord, where is fulness of joy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
