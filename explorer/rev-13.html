The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Revelation 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And I stood upon the sand of the sea, and saw a beast rise up out of the sea, having seven heads and ten horns, and upon his horns ten crowns, and upon his heads the name of blasphemy.

2 And the beast which I saw was like unto a leopard, and his feet were as the feet of a bear, and his mouth as the mouth of a lion: and the dragon gave him his power, and his seat, and great authority.

3 And I saw one of his heads as it were wounded to death; and his deadly wound was healed: and all the world wondered after the beast.

4 And they worshipped the dragon which gave power unto the beast: and they worshipped the beast, saying, Who is like unto the beast? who is able to make war with him?

5 And there was given unto him a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies; and power was given unto him to continue forty and two months.

6 And he opened his mouth in blasphemy against God, to blaspheme his name, and his tabernacle, and them that dwell in heaven.

7 And it was given unto him to make war with the saints, and to overcome them: and power was given him over all kindreds, and tongues, and nations.

8 And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.

9 If any man have an ear, let him hear.

10 He that leadeth into captivity shall go into captivity: he that killeth with the sword must be killed with the sword. Here is the patience and the faith of the saints.

11 And I beheld another beast coming up out of the earth; and he had two horns like a lamb, and he spake as a dragon.

12 And he exerciseth all the power of the first beast before him, and causeth the earth and them which dwell therein to worship the first beast, whose deadly wound was healed.

13 And he doeth great wonders, so that he maketh fire come down from heaven on the earth in the sight of men,

14 And deceiveth them that dwell on the earth by the means of those miracles which he had power to do in the sight of the beast; saying to them that dwell on the earth, that they should make an image to the beast, which had the wound by a sword, and did live.

15 And he had power to give life unto the image of the beast, that the image of the beast should both speak, and cause that as many as would not worship the image of the beast should be killed.

16 And he causeth all, both small and great, rich and poor, free and bond, to receive a mark in their right hand, or in their foreheads:

17 And that no man might buy or sell, save he that had the mark, or the name of the beast, or the number of his name.

18 Here is wisdom. Let him that hath understanding count the number of the beast: for it is the number of a man; and his number is Six hundred threescore and six.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A beast rises from the sea with seven heads, ten horns and ten crowns, combining in Henry's reading the forms of Daniel's beasts, its deadly wound healed so that the world wonders and worships both dragon and beast, and it makes war with the saints for forty-two months. A second beast comes up from the earth with two horns like a lamb but speaking as a dragon, working wonders, giving life to the first beast's image, and causing all, small and great, to receive a mark in forehead or hand, without which no one may buy or sell — the number of the beast being six hundred threescore and six, whose precise sense Henry confesses uncertain. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. John stood on the sand of the sea and saw a beast rise out of the sea, having seven heads and ten horns, with ten crowns on his horns, and on his heads the name of blasphemy. Henry notes the monster's form, like Daniel's four beasts combined, proclaims direct enmity to God's glory by promoting idolatry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The beast was like a leopard, with feet as a bear's and a mouth as a lion's, and the dragon gave him his power, and his seat, and great authority. Henry notes the beast combines the fierceness, strength, and swiftness of Daniel's beasts, set up and supported by the devil to do his work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. One of the beast's heads was as it were wounded to death, and his deadly wound was healed, and all the world wondered after the beast. Henry suggests the wound was the abolishing of pagan idolatry, healed by the introducing of popish idolatry, the same in substance in a new dress. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. They worshipped the dragon which gave power to the beast, and they worshipped the beast, saying, "Who is like unto the beast? who is able to make war with him?" Henry notes the world's darkness and madness: they admired the beast's power, policy, and success, and thought no power could withstand him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. There was given to him a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies, and power was given to him to continue forty and two months. Henry notes his reign is limited in time, suited to the other prophetical characters of antichrist's reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. He opened his mouth in blasphemy against God, to blaspheme his name, and his tabernacle, and them that dwell in heaven. Henry notes the malice levels at God in images of the invisible, at the tabernacle, Christ's human nature dishonoured by transubstantiation, and at glorified saints wronged by being prayed to in place of pagan demons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. It was given to him to make war with the saints and to overcome them, and power was given him over all kindreds, tongues, and nations. Henry notes the saints on earth are more exposed to his cruelty, and he is sometimes permitted to triumph over and trample upon them, though never to conquer their souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. All who dwell on the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world. Henry notes the beast is limited as to persons: only those not written in the Lamb's book are entirely subjected, while Christ's chosen remnant, redeemed and sealed, can never be made to forsake their Saviour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. If any man have an ear, let him hear. Henry reads this as a demand of attention to the discovery of the church's great sufferings, with assurance that God will turn his hand against her enemies once his refining work on Zion is accomplished. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. He that leads into captivity shall go into captivity; he that kills with the sword must be killed with the sword. Henry notes this is proper exercise for the patience and faith of the saints: patience under the prospect of such sufferings, and faith in the prospect of so glorious a deliverance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. John saw another beast coming up out of the earth, having two horns like a lamb, but speaking as a dragon. Henry notes the second beast acts under the disguise of religion and charity: the pope shows a lamb's horns, pretending to be Christ's vicar, but his false doctrines and cruel decrees betray him as belonging to the dragon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. He exercises all the power of the first beast before him, and causes the earth and its dwellers to worship the first beast, whose deadly wound was healed. Henry notes the second beast promotes the same design in substance: drawing men from worshipping the true God to worshipping those who by nature are no gods. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. He does great wonders, so that he makes fire come down from heaven on the earth in the sight of men. Henry notes the lying wonders, pretended miracles by which the unwary are deluded, and observes the papal kingdom has long been supported by pretended miracles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. He deceives those dwelling on the earth by the miracles he had power to do, saying they should make an image to the beast which had the wound by a sword and did live. Henry notes the deceit aims at worshipping the former beast in a new image or shape, as God sometimes permits enemies, like Egypt's magicians, to do things that seem wonderful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. He had power to give life to the image of the beast, that the image should both speak, and cause as many as would not worship the image to be killed. Henry counts excommunications and anathemas among the second beast's methods: pretending to cut men off from Christ, they deliver them to the secular power to be put to death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. He causes all, both small and great, rich and poor, free and bond, to receive a mark in their right hand or in their foreheads. Henry notes the mark, the name, and the number may all signify the same thing: open profession of subjection to the papacy in the forehead, and using all one's interest and power to promote papal authority in the right hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. No man might buy or sell, save he that had the mark, or the name of the beast, or the number of his name. Henry notes disfranchisement: allowing none natural, civil, or municipal rights who will not worship the beast, citing pope Martin V's bull prohibiting heretics from dwelling, bargaining, trading, or bearing civil office. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Here is wisdom. Let him that has understanding count the number of the beast: for it is the number of a man, and his number is six hundred threescore and six. Henry confesses uncertainty whether the number is of errors and heresies or of years from rise to fall, but holds this certainty: God has numbered his enemies' days, and they shall be finished, while his kingdom endures for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Mark of the beast. A mark in the right hand or forehead, without which, in the vision, no one may buy or sell. Interpreted Study notes, verses 16–17, Matthew Henry
  • Six hundred threescore and six. The number of the beast: what exactly it counts is not settled — Henry confesses uncertainty whether it is of errors and heresies or of years, holding only that God has numbered his enemies' days. Unresolved Study note, verse 18, Matthew Henry

Connections to other passages

  • Daniel's four beasts combined: the sea-beast's form, like Daniel's four beasts in one, proclaiming enmity to God's glory (verses 1–2, Henry). Interpreted Study note for this chapter
