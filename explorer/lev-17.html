The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 17 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto Aaron, and unto his sons, and unto all the children of Israel, and say unto them; This is the thing which the LORD hath commanded, saying,

3 What man soever there be of the house of Israel, that killeth an ox, or lamb, or goat, in the camp, or that killeth it out of the camp,

4 And bringeth it not unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, to offer an offering unto the LORD before the tabernacle of the LORD; blood shall be imputed unto that man; he hath shed blood; and that man shall be cut off from among his people:

5 To the end that the children of Israel may bring their sacrifices, which they offer in the open field, even that they may bring them unto the LORD, unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, unto the priest, and offer them for peace offerings unto the LORD.

6 And the priest shall sprinkle the blood upon the altar of the LORD at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and burn the fat for a sweet savour unto the LORD.

7 And they shall no more offer their sacrifices unto devils, after whom they have gone a whoring. This shall be a statute for ever unto them throughout their generations.

8 And thou shalt say unto them, Whatsoever man there be of the house of Israel, or of the strangers which sojourn among you, that offereth a burnt offering or sacrifice,

9 And bringeth it not unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, to offer it unto the LORD; even that man shall be cut off from among his people.

10 And whatsoever man there be of the house of Israel, or of the strangers that sojourn among you, that eateth any manner of blood; I will even set my face against that soul that eateth blood, and will cut him off from among his people.

11 For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.

12 Therefore I said unto the children of Israel, No soul of you shall eat blood, neither shall any stranger that sojourneth among you eat blood.

13 And whatsoever man there be of the children of Israel, or of the strangers that sojourn among you, which hunteth and catcheth any beast or fowl that may be eaten; he shall even pour out the blood thereof, and cover it with dust.

14 For it is the life of all flesh; the blood of it is for the life thereof: therefore I said unto the children of Israel, Ye shall eat the blood of no manner of flesh: for the life of all flesh is the blood thereof: whosoever eateth it shall be cut off.

15 And every soul that eateth that which died of itself, or that which was torn with beasts, whether it be one of your own country, or a stranger, he shall both wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even: then shall he be clean.

16 But if he wash them not, nor bathe his flesh; then he shall bear his iniquity.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter fixes one place for sacrifice and one rule for blood. Any ox, lamb, or goat killed for food or offering must be brought to the tabernacle door and offered there, rather than sacrificed in the open field or to goat-demons; failure brings blood-guilt and cutting off. No blood of any flesh may be eaten, because the life of the flesh is in the blood and God has given it on the altar to make atonement. A hunted animal's blood must be poured out and covered with dust, and anyone eating what died of itself or was torn must wash and be unclean until evening. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • The life of the flesh is in the blood. The chapter's own ground for the blood ban: blood is given on the altar to make atonement for souls, and so is reserved to God. Interpreted Chapter text in the Scripture panel above

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the law that blood of beasts must be offered at the tabernacle door. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The command is spoken to Aaron, his sons, and all Israel, so the whole people, not priests alone, are bound by the place and blood laws. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Whoever kills an ox, lamb, or goat in or outside the camp must bring it to the tabernacle, since killing for food in the desert was almost always a feast eaten in company, like a peace offering, and public slaughter prevented private sacrifice to a favourite idol. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. If he does not bring it to the tabernacle door to offer it before the LORD, blood guilt is imputed to him, he has shed blood, and he shall be cut off, the law being observable only while encamped within accessible distance of the tabernacle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The purpose is that Israel bring sacrifices offered in the open field to the priest at the tabernacle door as peace offerings, the law being directed against Egyptian habits of field sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The priest sprinkles the blood on the LORD's altar at the tabernacle door and burns the fat for a sweet savour, the public rite replacing any private field offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They shall no more offer sacrifices to devils, literally goats, after whom they have played the harlot, a reference to goat-symbolled Pan, Faunus, and Saturn worshipped licentiously in Egypt, especially at Mendes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Whatever man of Israel or stranger offers a burnt offering or sacrifice must bring it to the tabernacle, since before the law men worshipped wherever they pleased, but afterward religion could be acceptably performed only at the appointed place, as a preventive of idolatry at grove and field altars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. If he does not bring it to the tabernacle door to offer it to the LORD, that man shall be cut off from his people, the place restriction applying to stranger as well as native. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Whoever eats any blood, God will set His face against him and cut him off, God's face denoting anger, with death for a public crime and vengeance for a secret one, the rite targeted being the Zabian practice of eating flesh and sometimes blood where it was poured out to contract friendship with deities and seek visions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and God has given it on the altar to make atonement for souls, God as sovereign author and proprietor of nature reserving blood to Himself and allowing men only one use of it, in sacrifices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Therefore no one among Israel, nor any stranger dwelling among them, shall eat blood, the prohibition extending beyond natives to sojourners. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Whoever hunts and catches an animal or bird that may be eaten shall pour out its blood and cover it with dust, unlike heathen sportsmen who poured blood as a libation to the god of the chase, Israel being debarred from superstitious uses by covering it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. For it is the life of all flesh, its blood sustains its life, therefore Israel shall not eat the blood of any flesh, for the life of all flesh is its blood, and whoever eats it shall be cut off. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Every person who eats what died of itself or was torn by beasts, native or stranger, shall wash his clothes, bathe in water, and be unclean until evening, then he shall be clean, the uncleanness lasting from discovery of the fault until evening. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. But if he does not wash his clothes or bathe his body, he shall bear his guilt, the washing being the appointed removal of the defilement, binding only on an Israelite in its dietary form. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
