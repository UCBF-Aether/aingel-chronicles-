The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Corinthians 7 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Having therefore these promises, dearly beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God.

2 Receive us; we have wronged no man, we have corrupted no man, we have defrauded no man.

3 I speak not this to condemn you: for I have said before, that ye are in our hearts to die and live with you.

4 Great is my boldness of speech toward you, great is my glorying of you: I am filled with comfort, I am exceeding joyful in all our tribulation.

5 For, when we were come into Macedonia, our flesh had no rest, but we were troubled on every side; without were fightings, within were fears.

6 Nevertheless God, that comforteth those that are cast down, comforted us by the coming of Titus;

7 And not by his coming only, but by the consolation wherewith he was comforted in you, when he told us your earnest desire, your mourning, your fervent mind toward me; so that I rejoiced the more.

8 For though I made you sorry with a letter, I do not repent, though I did repent: for I perceive that the same epistle hath made you sorry, though it were but for a season.

9 Now I rejoice, not that ye were made sorry, but that ye sorrowed to repentance: for ye were made sorry after a godly manner, that ye might receive damage by us in nothing.

10 For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.

11 For behold this selfsame thing, that ye sorrowed after a godly sort, what carefulness it wrought in you, yea, what clearing of yourselves, yea, what indignation, yea, what fear, yea, what vehement desire, yea, what zeal, yea, what revenge! In all things ye have approved yourselves to be clear in this matter.

12 Wherefore, though I wrote unto you, I did it not for his cause that had done the wrong, nor for his cause that suffered wrong, but that our care for you in the sight of God might appear unto you.

13 Therefore we were comforted in your comfort: yea, and exceedingly the more joyed we for the joy of Titus, because his spirit was refreshed by you all.

14 For if I have boasted any thing to him of you, I am not ashamed; but as we spake all things to you in truth, even so our boasting, which I made before Titus, is found a truth.

15 And his inward affection is more abundant toward you, whilst he remembereth the obedience of you all, how with fear and trembling ye received him.

16 I rejoice therefore that I have confidence in you in all things.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Having the promises of God's indwelling, Paul urges cleansing from all filthiness of flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in God's fear, and then rejoices that his painful letter produced godly sorrow leading to repentance, not worldly sorrow leading to death. Titus brings good news of the Corinthians' earnest desire, mourning, and zeal, and Paul closes rejoicing that he has confidence in them in all things. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Having therefore these promises, dearly beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God. JFB notes the promises of chapter 6 are the motive for cleansing: both flesh and spirit are cleansed, and holiness is perfected, not merely begun, in God's fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Receive us; we have wronged no man, we have corrupted no man, we have defrauded no man. Henry notes the threefold denial answers the critics: no wrong, no corruption, no fraud, in Paul's dealings with Corinth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. I speak not this to condemn you: for I have said before, that ye are in our hearts to die and live with you. JFB notes the denial is not a condemnation: Corinth is in Paul's heart for life and death together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Great is my boldness of speech toward you, great is my glorying of you: I am filled with comfort, I am exceeding joyful in all our tribulation. Henry notes boldness and boasting are now joyful: comfort fills Paul even in tribulation, because Corinth has repented. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For, when we were come into Macedonia, our flesh had no rest, but we were troubled on every side; without were fightings, within were fears. JFB notes the Macedonian troubles match chapter 4's pairs: fightings without, fears within, and no rest for the flesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Nevertheless God, that comforts those that are cast down, comforted us by the coming of Titus. Henry notes the comforter of the cast down uses a human coming: Titus's arrival is God's comfort in person. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And not by his coming only, but by the consolation wherewith he was comforted in you, when he told us your earnest desire, your mourning, your fervent mind toward me; so that I rejoiced the more. JFB notes Titus brought a double comfort: his own comfort in Corinth, and the report of their earnest desire, mourning and fervent mind toward Paul. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. For though I made you sorry with a letter, I do not repent, though I did repent: for I perceive that the same epistle has made you sorry, though it were but for a season. Henry notes Paul's double feeling: he did regret the pain, and does not regret it now, because the sorrow lasted only a season and bore fruit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Now I rejoice, not that ye were made sorry, but that ye sorrowed to repentance: for ye were made sorry after a godly manner, that ye might receive damage by us in nothing. JFB notes the joy is not in sorrow but in repentance: godly sorrow damages no one, because it leads back to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. For godly sorrow works repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world works death. Henry notes the two sorrows diverge: godly sorrow works an unregretted repentance unto salvation, worldly sorrow works only death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. For behold this selfsame thing, that ye sorrowed after a godly sort, what carefulness it wrought in you, yea, what clearing of yourselves, yea, what indignation, yea, what fear, yea, what vehement desire, yea, what zeal, yea, what revenge! In all things ye have approved yourselves to be clear in this matter. JFB notes the seven fruits of godly sorrow: carefulness, clearing, indignation, fear, vehement desire, zeal and revenge against the sin itself, proving Corinth clear in the matter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Wherefore, though I wrote unto you, I did it not for his cause that had done the wrong, nor for his cause that suffered wrong, but that our care for you in the sight of God might appear unto you. Henry notes the letter's deepest purpose was neither offender nor offended, but the manifestation of Paul's care for Corinth before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Therefore we were comforted in your comfort: yea, and exceedingly the more joyed we for the joy of Titus, because his spirit was refreshed by you all. JFB notes comfort multiplies: Paul is comforted in Corinth's comfort, and even more in Titus's joy at their refreshing of his spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. For if I have boasted any thing to him of you, I am not ashamed; but as we spake all things to you in truth, even so our boasting, which I made before Titus, is found a truth. Henry notes Paul's boasting before Titus is vindicated: as his preaching to Corinth was truth, so his boasting about Corinth proves true. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And his inward affection is more abundant toward you, whilst he remembers the obedience of you all, how with fear and trembling ye received him. JFB notes Titus's affection grows on remembering their obedience, received with fear and trembling, the reverence of a church toward its minister. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. I rejoice therefore that I have confidence in you in all things. Henry notes the chapter ends where chapter 1's confidence began, but now it is confidence proved: Paul rejoices in full confidence in Corinth in all things. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • godly sorrow. Sorrow according to God, which Paul says works repentance to salvation, unlike the sorrow of the world, which works death. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • filthiness of the flesh and spirit. Defilement of both body and inner life, from which Paul urges cleansing in the fear of God. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • Titus. Paul's fellow worker, whose joyful report of Corinth's repentance comforts Paul. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
