The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Amos 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Hear this word that the LORD hath spoken against you, O children of Israel, against the whole family which I brought up from the land of Egypt, saying,

2 You only have I known of all the families of the earth: therefore I will punish you for all your iniquities.

3 Can two walk together, except they be agreed?

4 Will a lion roar in the forest, when he hath no prey? will a young lion cry out of his den, if he have taken nothing?

5 Can a bird fall in a snare upon the earth, where no gin is for him? shall one take up a snare from the earth, and have taken nothing at all?

6 Shall a trumpet be blown in the city, and the people not be afraid? shall there be evil in a city, and the LORD hath not done it?

7 Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets.

8 The lion hath roared, who will not fear? the Lord GOD hath spoken, who can but prophesy?

9 Publish in the palaces at Ashdod, and in the palaces in the land of Egypt, and say, Assemble yourselves upon the mountains of Samaria, and behold the great tumults in the midst thereof, and the oppressed in the midst thereof.

10 For they know not to do right, saith the LORD, who store up violence and robbery in their palaces.

11 Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD; An adversary there shall be even round about the land; and he shall bring down thy strength from thee, and thy palaces shall be spoiled.

12 Thus saith the LORD; As the shepherd taketh out of the mouth of the lion two legs, or a piece of an ear; so shall the children of Israel be taken out that dwell in Samaria in the corner of a bed, and in Damascus in a couch.

13 Hear ye, and testify in the house of Jacob, saith the Lord GOD, the God of hosts,

14 That in the day that I shall visit the transgressions of Israel upon him I will also visit the altars of Bethel: and the horns of the altar shall be cut off, and fall to the ground.

15 And I will smite the winter house with the summer house; and the houses of ivory shall perish, and the great houses shall have an end, saith the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Hear this word against the whole family brought up from Egypt: you only have I known of all the families of the earth, therefore I will punish you for all your iniquities. As a lion roars when he has prey, so the prophet cannot but speak when the LORD has spoken, and the chapter warns that the palaces of Samaria and the altars of Bethel will fall, with only a fragment of Israel rescued like two legs or a piece of an ear from a lion's mouth. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hear this word the Lord has spoken against you, O children of Israel, against the whole family brought up from Egypt, a word of reproof and threatening now that words of counsel and comfort were disregarded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. You only have God known of all the earth's families, therefore he will punish them for all their iniquities, distinguishing favors not restraining from sin and therefore not exempting from punishment, the nearer to God in profession, the more surely he reckons. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Can two walk together unless agreed, for they could not expect communion with God unless they first sought peace with him, where there is no friendship there can be no fellowship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Will a lion roar in the forest when he has no prey, or a young lion cry from his den if he has taken nothing, the prophet's warnings having a cause as certain as a lion's roar has prey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Can a bird fall in a snare where no gin is, or a snare spring up having taken nothing, effects not arising without causes, so judgment does not come uncaused. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Shall a trumpet be blown in a city and the people not be afraid, or shall evil be in a city and the Lord not have done it, the evil of trouble being from God, whoever the instruments, while the evil of sin is from ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Surely the Lord God will do nothing but reveal his secret to his servants the prophets, warnings preceding judgments so that none can say they were unwarned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The lion has roared, who will not fear, and the Lord God has spoken, who can but prophesy, the prophet compelled to speak once God has spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Publish in Ashdod's and Egypt's palaces and say, assemble on Samaria's mountains and behold the great tumults and oppression in her midst, inviting even foreign witnesses to see Israel's disorders. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. For they know not to do right, says the Lord, storing up violence and robbery in their palaces, power used as an instrument of unrighteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Therefore an adversary shall be round about the land, bringing down Israel's strength and spoiling her palaces, power got and kept wrongfully not being kept long. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. As a shepherd takes from a lion's mouth two legs or a piece of an ear, so shall Samaria's children be taken out, in a bed's corner and in Damascus on a couch, yet a remnant according to election of grace will be secured by the great Shepherd from destruction's jaws. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Hear and testify in the house of Jacob, says the Lord God of hosts, the charge formally witnessed before judgment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. In the day God visits Israel's transgressions he will also visit Bethel's altars, and the altar's horns shall be cut off and fall to the ground, the idolatrous sanctuary judged with the people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. God will smite winter house with summer house, houses of ivory shall perish, and great houses shall have an end, says the Lord, the pomp and pleasantness of houses not fortifying against judgment but making sufferings more grievous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Samaria. the capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, standing in the prophets for the ten tribes as Jerusalem stands for Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
