The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 36 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then the people of the land took Jehoahaz the son of Josiah, and made him king in his father’s stead in Jerusalem.

2 Jehoahaz was twenty and three years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem.

3 And the king of Egypt put him down at Jerusalem, and condemned the land in an hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold.

4 And the king of Egypt made Eliakim his brother king over Judah and Jerusalem, and turned his name to Jehoiakim. And Necho took Jehoahaz his brother, and carried him to Egypt.

5 Jehoiakim was twenty and five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem: and he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD his God.

6 Against him came up Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, and bound him in fetters, to carry him to Babylon.

7 Nebuchadnezzar also carried of the vessels of the house of the LORD to Babylon, and put them in his temple at Babylon.

8 Now the rest of the acts of Jehoiakim, and his abominations which he did, and that which was found in him, behold, they are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah: and Jehoiachin his son reigned in his stead.

9 Jehoiachin was eight years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months and ten days in Jerusalem: and he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD.

10 And when the year was expired, king Nebuchadnezzar sent, and brought him to Babylon, with the goodly vessels of the house of the LORD, and made Zedekiah his brother king over Judah and Jerusalem.

11 Zedekiah was one and twenty years old when he began to reign, and reigned eleven years in Jerusalem.

12 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD his God, and humbled not himself before Jeremiah the prophet speaking from the mouth of the LORD.

13 And he also rebelled against king Nebuchadnezzar, who had made him swear by God: but he stiffened his neck, and hardened his heart from turning unto the LORD God of Israel.

14 Moreover all the chief of the priests, and the people, transgressed very much after all the abominations of the heathen; and polluted the house of the LORD which he had hallowed in Jerusalem.

15 And the LORD God of their fathers sent to them by his messengers, rising up betimes, and sending; because he had compassion on his people, and on his dwelling place:

16 But they mocked the messengers of God, and despised his words, and misused his prophets, until the wrath of the LORD arose against his people, till there was no remedy.

17 Therefore he brought upon them the king of the Chaldees, who slew their young men with the sword in the house of their sanctuary, and had no compassion upon young man or maiden, old man, or him that stooped for age: he gave them all into his hand.

18 And all the vessels of the house of God, great and small, and the treasures of the house of the LORD, and the treasures of the king, and of his princes; all these he brought to Babylon.

19 And they burnt the house of God, and brake down the wall of Jerusalem, and burnt all the palaces thereof with fire, and destroyed all the goodly vessels thereof.

20 And them that had escaped from the sword carried he away to Babylon; where they were servants to him and his sons until the reign of the kingdom of Persia:

21 To fulfil the word of the LORD by the mouth of Jeremiah, until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths: for as long as she lay desolate she kept sabbath, to fulfil threescore and ten years.

22 Now in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, that the word of the LORD spoken by the mouth of Jeremiah might be accomplished, the LORD stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, that he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom, and put it also in writing, saying,

23 Thus saith Cyrus king of Persia, All the kingdoms of the earth hath the LORD God of heaven given me; and he hath charged me to build him an house in Jerusalem, which is in Judah. Who is there among you of all his people? The LORD his God be with him, and let him go up.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the last four kings of Judah — Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim, Jehoiachin, and Zedekiah — the fall of Jerusalem, the exile to Babylon, and Cyrus's decree to rebuild the house at Jerusalem. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then the people of the land took Jehoahaz son of Josiah, and made him king in his father's stead in Jerusalem. Henry notes Jehoahaz was set up by the people, but the destruction of Judah and Jerusalem is here coming on by degrees, for God ordered it so to show he has no pleasure in sinners' ruin but would rather they turn and live, giving time and inducement to repent and waiting to be gracious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Jehoahaz was twenty and three years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. Henry notes in one quarter of a year he was deposed by Pharaoh-necho and carried prisoner to Egypt, for had he trodden in his father's piety he might have reigned long and prospered, but he did evil in the Lord's sight, so his triumphing was short and his joy but for a moment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And the king of Egypt put him down at Jerusalem, and condemned the land in an hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold. Henry notes the land was fined for setting him up, 100 talents of silver and a talent of gold, the price of a people's hasty choice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And the king of Egypt made Eliakim his brother king over Judah and Jerusalem, and turned his name to Jehoiakim. And Necho took Jehoahaz his brother, and carried him to Egypt. Henry notes Egypt's king, an old enemy, gave what king he pleased to the kingdom and what name he pleased to the king, making Eliakim king and calling him Jehoiakim in token of authority over him, and carrying Jehoahaz to Egypt, of whom we hear no more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Jehoiakim was twenty and five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem, and he did evil in the sight of the Lord his God. Henry notes Jehoiakim did evil, nay, we read of the abominations which he did, for he was very wild and wicked, and idolatries generally go under the name of abominations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Against him came up Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, and bound him in fetters, to carry him to Babylon. Henry notes Babylon's king came up against him, seized him and bound him with design to carry him to Babylon, though it seems he either changed his mind and suffered him to reign as vassal, or death released the prisoner before he was carried away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Nebuchadnezzar also carried of the vessels of the house of the Lord to Babylon, and put them in his temple at Babylon. Henry notes the best and most valuable temple vessels were now carried away and used in Nebuchadnezzar's temple, for the sin of Judah was bringing heathen idols into God's temple, and now their punishment was temple vessels carried to the service of nations' gods, and if men profane God's institutions by sins, it is just with God to suffer them profaned by enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Now the rest of the acts of Jehoiakim, and his abominations which he did, and that which was found in him, behold, they are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah, and Jehoiachin his son reigned in his stead. Henry notes in the reference to Kings, mention is made of that which was found in him, meant of treachery toward Babylon's king, though some Jewish writers understand private marks found in his dead body in honour of his idol, cuttings God had forbidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Jehoiachin was eight years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months and ten days in Jerusalem, and he did evil in the sight of the Lord. Henry notes Jehoiachin, or Jeconiah, son of Jehoiakim, attempted to reign and reigned long enough to show his evil inclination, and here he is said to be eight, but in Kings eighteen, so this seems a transcriber's mistake unless his father took him at eight to join in government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And when the year was expired, king Nebuchadnezzar sent, and brought him to Babylon, with the goodly vessels of the house of the Lord, and made Zedekiah his brother king over Judah and Jerusalem. Henry notes after three months and ten days Babylon's king sent and fetched him away captive, with more goodly temple vessels, and made Zedekiah his brother king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Zedekiah was one and twenty years old when he began to reign, and reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. Henry notes Zedekiah, the king in whose days destruction came, reigned eleven years, with additions made to national guilt and advances toward national destruction in each of these reigns. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And he did evil in the sight of the Lord his God, and humbled not himself before Jeremiah the prophet speaking from the mouth of the Lord. Henry notes Zedekiah brought ruin on himself by folly toward God and Babylon's king: Jeremiah brought him messages which, if regarded, might have lengthened tranquillity, but he humbled not himself before Jeremiah, though expected that a mighty prince should humble himself before a poor prophet speaking from the Lord's mouth and submit to his admonitions, and because he would not make himself God's servant he was made his enemies' slave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. And he also rebelled against king Nebuchadnezzar, who had made him swear by God, but he stiffened his neck, and hardened his heart from turning unto the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes if he had but been true to his covenant with Babylon's king, that would have prevented ruin, but he rebelled though sworn to be faithful tributary, perfidiously violating engagements, and all nations looked upon an oath as sacred and oath-breakers as worst of men, while the thing that ruined him was not only turning not to the Lord but stiffening neck and hardening heart from turning to him, obstinately resolving not to return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Moreover all the chief of the priests, and the people, transgressed very much after all the abominations of the heathen, and polluted the house of the Lord which he had hallowed in Jerusalem. Henry notes the great sin bringing destruction was idolatry: priests and people went after heathen abominations, forsook pure worship for pagan superstition's lewd rites, and polluted the Lord's house, and the priests, chief of the priests, who should have opposed idolatry, were ring-leaders in it, for that place is not far from ruin in which religion is already ruined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And the Lord God of their fathers sent to them by his messengers, rising up betimes, and sending, because he had compassion on his people, and on his dwelling place. Henry notes God's tender compassion in sending prophets: because he was their fathers' God in covenant, he sent messengers to convince of sin and warn of ruin, rising up betimes and sending, denoting greatest care and concern, and upon their first deviation, if they took but one step toward idols, God immediately sent to reprove them, for the methods God takes to reclaim sinners are all instances of compassion and unwillingness that any should perish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. But they mocked the messengers of God, and despised his words, and misused his prophets, until the wrath of the Lord arose against his people, till there was no remedy. Henry notes their base carriage: they mocked God's messengers, despised his word in their mouths, and misused prophets, treating them as enemies, evidence of implacable enmity to God and invincible resolution to go on in sins, bringing wrath without remedy, for it was sinning against the remedy, and nothing is more provoking than abuses given to faithful ministers, for a wrong to an ambassador is construed as done to the prince who sends him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Therefore he brought upon them the king of the Chaldees, who slew their young men with the sword in the house of their sanctuary, and had no compassion upon young man or maiden, old man, or him that stooped for age: he gave them all into his hand. Henry notes multitudes were put to the sword even in the sanctuary, whither they fled hoping its holiness would protect them, but how could they expect it when they themselves had polluted it, and Chaldeans showed no pity to tender sex or venerable age, for they forsook God who had compassion on them and would have none of him, so justly they were given to cruel men with no compassion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And all the vessels of the house of God, great and small, and the treasures of the house of the Lord, and the treasures of the king, and of his princes, all these he brought to Babylon. Henry notes all remaining temple vessels great and small, and all treasures sacred and secular, of God's house and king and princes, were seized and brought to Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And they burnt the house of God, and brake down the wall of Jerusalem, and burnt all the palaces thereof with fire, and destroyed all the goodly vessels thereof. Henry notes the temple was burnt, Jerusalem's walls demolished, houses called palaces, so stately and rich, laid in ashes, and all goodly vessels destroyed, showing what woeful havock sin makes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And them that had escaped from the sword carried he away to Babylon, where they were servants to him and his sons until the reign of the kingdom of Persia. Henry notes the remainder escaping the sword were carried captives to Babylon, impoverished, enslaved, insulted, exposed to miseries of a strange and enemy's land, where haters bore rule, and there by Babylon's rivers they mingled tears with streams, yet there, it seems, they were cured of idolatry, though not of mocking prophets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. To fulfil the word of the Lord by the mouth of Jeremiah, until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths, for as long as she lay desolate she kept sabbath, to fulfil threescore and ten years. Henry notes the land lay desolate while captives were in Babylon, that fruitful land turned desert, not tilled nor husbanded, and this was just punishment for former abuse, for they had served Baal with its fruits, so now the land enjoyed her sabbaths as God threatened by Moses, because it did not rest on their sabbaths, and God will be no loser in glory at last by men's disobedience. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Now in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, that the word of the Lord spoken by the mouth of Jeremiah might be accomplished, the Lord stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, that he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom, and put it also in writing, saying. Henry notes these last two verses look back to Jeremiah's prophecy and show its accomplishment: God had promised restoring captives and rebuilding Jerusalem at seventy years' end, and that time to favour Zion, that set time, came at last, for after long dark night the day-spring from on high visited them, and God will be found true to every word spoken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Thus saith Cyrus king of Persia, All the kingdoms of the earth hath the Lord God of heaven given me, and he hath charged me to build him an house in Jerusalem, which is in Judah. Who is there among you of all his people? The Lord his God be with him, and let him go up. Henry notes they look forward to Ezra's history, beginning with repetition of these verses, here the conclusion of a melancholy story and there introduction to a pleasant one, teaching that though God's church be cast down it is not cast off, though his people corrected they are not abandoned, though thrown into the furnace yet not lost there, and though God contend long he will not contend always, for the vision is for an appointed time and at the end shall speak and not lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Investigation (editorial — a question, not a conclusion)

This section investigates a question; it does not conclude it. The study note for verse 9 observes that Jehoiachin is said here to be eight when he began to reign, but in Kings eighteen.

Parallels. Both accounts give Jehoiachin a reign of three months and ten days at Jerusalem. Differences. The age at accession differs by ten years: Henry allows a transcriber's mistake, unless his father took him at eight to share in the government.

Which explanation is correct is not established by the sources consulted for this page. Unresolved Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
