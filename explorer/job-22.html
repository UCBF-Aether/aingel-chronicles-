The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said,

2 Can a man be profitable unto God, as he that is wise may be profitable unto himself?

3 Is it any pleasure to the Almighty, that thou art righteous? or is it gain to him, that thou makest thy ways perfect?

4 Will he reprove thee for fear of thee? will he enter with thee into judgment?

5 Is not thy wickedness great? and thine iniquities infinite?

6 For thou hast taken a pledge from thy brother for nought, and stripped the naked of their clothing.

7 Thou hast not given water to the weary to drink, and thou hast withholden bread from the hungry.

8 But as for the mighty man, he had the earth; and the honourable man dwelt in it.

9 Thou hast sent widows away empty, and the arms of the fatherless have been broken.

10 Therefore snares are round about thee, and sudden fear troubleth thee;

11 Or darkness, that thou canst not see; and abundance of waters cover thee.

12 Is not God in the height of heaven? and behold the height of the stars, how high they are!

13 And thou sayest, How doth God know? can he judge through the dark cloud?

14 Thick clouds are a covering to him, that he seeth not; and he walketh in the circuit of heaven.

15 Hast thou marked the old way which wicked men have trodden?

16 Which were cut down out of time, whose foundation was overflown with a flood:

17 Which said unto God, Depart from us: and what can the Almighty do for them?

18 Yet he filled their houses with good things: but the counsel of the wicked is far from me.

19 The righteous see it, and are glad: and the innocent laugh them to scorn.

20 Whereas our substance is not cut down, but the remnant of them the fire consumeth.

21 Acquaint now thyself with him, and be at peace: thereby good shall come unto thee.

22 Receive, I pray thee, the law from his mouth, and lay up his words in thine heart.

23 If thou return to the Almighty, thou shalt be built up, thou shalt put away iniquity far from thy tabernacles.

24 Then shalt thou lay up gold as dust, and the gold of Ophir as the stones of the brooks.

25 Yea, the Almighty shall be thy defence, and thou shalt have plenty of silver.

26 For then shalt thou have thy delight in the Almighty, and shalt lift up thy face unto God.

27 Thou shalt make thy prayer unto him, and he shall hear thee, and thou shalt pay thy vows.

28 Thou shalt also decree a thing, and it shall be established unto thee: and the light shall shine upon thy ways.

29 When men are cast down, then thou shalt say, There is lifting up; and he shall save the humble person.

30 He shall deliver the island of the innocent: and it is delivered by the pureness of thine hands.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Eliphaz answers a third time, and insinuation hardens into plain charge: Job, he says, took a pledge from his brother for nothing, stripped the naked, withheld bread from the hungry and water from the weary, and sent widows away empty. Man cannot be profitable to God, he argues, and God’s judgment is not far off — but if Job returns to the Almighty and puts away iniquity, he will be built up, delight in God, and see his ways established. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Eliphaz the Temanite answers a third time, beginning the third series as before. The speech moves from insinuation to plain assertion of Job's guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Can a man be profitable unto God, as he that is wise may be profitable unto himself? JFB explains that man's goodness adds nothing to God's happiness, so God cannot send prosperity and calamity for his own advantage: the cause must lie in the men themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Is it any pleasure to the Almighty that Job is righteous, or gain to him that Job makes his ways perfect? JFB notes pleasure is accession of happiness, but God, though pleased by righteousness, is not dependent on man's character for happiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Will God reprove Job for fear of him, or enter with him into judgment? JFB asks whether punishment is inflicted to disarm Job, as Job implied, and notes Job desired judgment but ought rather to have spoken as in Psalm 143. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Is not Job's wickedness great, and his iniquities infinite? JFB notes Eliphaz, who only insinuated before, now plainly asserts guilt merely on the ground of sufferings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. For Job has taken a pledge from his brother for nought, and stripped the naked of their clothing. JFB explains the crimes as likely rich-man sins inferred harshly: taking a pledge with no just claim, the outer garment that served the poor as day covering and night bed, from one poorly clad, naked in the sense of lacking changes of raiment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Job has not given water to the weary to drink, and has withheld bread from the hungry. JFB notes hospitality to the weary traveller is a primary Eastern duty, which Job is charged with refusing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. But as for the mighty man, he had the earth, and the honourable man dwelt in it. JFB explains mighty as man of arm, namely Job, and honourable as eminent and possessing authority, repeating the charge that Job wrung houses and lands from the poor by violence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Job has sent widows away empty, and the arms of the fatherless have been broken. JFB explains empty as wants unrelieved, and arms as supports on which one leans, robbed from those the law especially protected. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Therefore snares are round about Job, and sudden fear troubles him. JFB notes snares allude to Job's own admission of being netted, in chapter 19 and Bildad's snares in chapter 18. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Or darkness, that Job cannot see, and abundance of waters cover him. JFB explains darkness as that Job cannot see through it, and abundance as floods covering him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Is not God in the height of heaven? And behold the height of the stars, how high they are! JFB notes Eliphaz proves God can behold all things from his height, gratuitously inferring Job denied it because he denied the wicked are punished here. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. And Job says, how does God know? Can he judge through the dark cloud? JFB renders it as and yet you say God does not concern himself with human affairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Thick clouds are a covering to God, that he sees not, and he walks in the circuit of heaven. JFB explains the charge as an Epicurean sentiment: God only walks heaven's circuit, taking no part in earthly affairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Hast thou marked the old way which wicked men have trodden? JFB renders marked as dost thou keep to or wish to follow, warning Job against sharing their end, with the old way as the degenerate ways before the flood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Which were cut down out of time, whose foundation was overflown with a flood. JFB renders cut down as fettered, arrested by death prematurely, with foundations poured out as a stream, the solid earth passing into flood as in Genesis 7. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Which said unto God, depart from us, and what can the Almighty do for them? JFB notes Eliphaz designedly uses Job's own words from chapter 21, with do for them meaning they think they can do everything for themselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Yet he filled their houses with good, but the counsel of the wicked is far from Eliphaz. JFB notes Job's words are sarcastically quoted in continuation: after uttering the godless sentiment, Job hypocritically adds may the counsel be far, in Eliphaz's reading. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. The righteous see it and are glad, and the innocent laugh them to scorn. JFB explains the triumph of the pious at the fall of recent followers of antediluvian sinners, cut off while denying God can do them good or harm, as vindication of God's ways, not derision of the wretched. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Whereas our substance is not cut down, but the remnant of them the fire consumes. JFB gives the triumphant speech of the pious, with adversary cut down and remnant as all left of the sinner, and fire alluding to Job's fire, after destruction by water. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Acquaint now thyself with God, and be at peace, thereby good shall come unto thee. JFB explains acquaint as becoming a companion of God with familiar confidence, taking for granted Job is not yet acquainted, with peace as prosperity and restoration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Receive the law from God's mouth, and lay up his words in your heart. The counsel is to take God's word inwardly, not merely hear it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. If Job returns to the Almighty, he shall be built up, putting iniquity far from his tabernacles. JFB explains built up as restored like a house rebuilt, with put away as if thou put away iniquity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Then shall Job lay up gold as dust, and the gold of Ophir as stones of the brooks. JFB explains the protasis as regarding glittering metal as dust, laying it on dust, with Ophir gold washed down by torrents and lodged among brook stones, regarded as little worth as the stones. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Yea, the Almighty shall be Job's defence, and he shall have plenty of silver. JFB gives the apodosis: then God shall be his precious metals in place of riches, and in place of laboriously obtained silver treasures, implying it is less labour to find God than hidden metals. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. For then shall Job have his delight in the Almighty, and lift up his face unto God. JFB notes lift up the face is repeated from Zophar, the posture of the accepted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. Job shall make his prayer unto God, and he shall hear him, and Job shall pay his vows. JFB explains paying vows as God giving occasion to pay former vows by hearing present prayers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Job shall also decree a thing, and it shall be established unto him, and light shall shine upon his ways. JFB explains light as success shining on his ways. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. When men are cast down, Job shall say there is lifting up, and God shall save the humble person. JFB explains cast down as ways cast down for a time, soon followed by joyful lifting up, with humble as him of low eyes, contrasting pride with grace to the humble. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. He shall deliver the island of the innocent, and it is delivered by the pureness of Job's hands. JFB explains island as dwelling, and the Hebrew negative as God delivering him who was not guiltless, saved not because guiltless but because humbled, delivered through purity of hands by intercession — unconsciously answering the final deliverance by Job's intercession for his friends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
