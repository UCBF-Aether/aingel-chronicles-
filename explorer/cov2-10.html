The Text — 2 Book of the Covenant 10 (J. Cooper & A. J. Maclean, The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1902), public domain (archive.org cu31924029296170))

1 Thenceforward let them pray together with all the people. Let the oblation be offered by the deacon. And so let the shepherd give thanks. But the bread is offered for a type of My body.

2 Let the cup be mixed with wine-—mixed with wine and water, for it is a sign of blood and of the laver; so that also the inner man, that is to say, that which is of the soul, may be counted worthy of those things which are like [them], that is to say, those things of the body also.

3 And let all the people, according to what hath been said before, receive with Amen of the Eucharist’ which is offered. Let the deacons hover over [them], as hath before been said.

4 Let him who giveth [the sacrament] say: The Body of Jesus Christ, the Holy Ghost, for the healing of soul and body. And let him who receweth say: Amen.

5 He who spilleth of the cup gathereth up judgment to himself. Similarly also he who seeth and is silent and doth not reprove him, whoever he may be. Let those who take the Offering be exhorted by the priests to be careful to do good works, to love strangers, to labour in fasting, and in every good work to engage” in servitude.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Thenceforward they pray together with all the people. The oblation is offered by the deacon, and the shepherd gives thanks; the bread is offered for a type of His body. The cup is mixed with wine and water, for it is a sign of blood and of the laver, so that the inner man — that is to say, that which is of the soul — may be counted worthy of those things which are like them, that is to say, those things of the body also. All the people, according to what has been said before, receive with Amen of the Eucharist which is offered, the deacons hovering over them as has been said before. He who gives the sacrament says, The Body of Jesus Christ, the Holy Ghost, for the healing of soul and body; and he who receives says Amen. He who spills of the cup gathers up judgment to himself, and similarly he who sees and is silent and does not reprove him, whoever he may be. Those who take the Offering are to be exhorted by the priests to be careful to do good works, to love strangers, to labour in fasting, and in every good work to engage in servitude. They are also to be taught about the resurrection of the body; before anyone receives baptism no one is to know the word about the resurrection, for this is the new decree, which has a new name that none knows but he who receives it. The deacon does not give the Offering to a presbyter; he opens the disc or paten, and the presbyter receives. The deacon gives the Eucharist to the people in their hands. When the presbyter is not present, the deacon of necessity baptizes. Interpreted Chapter text above; the study-notes file for this chapter is silent, so no commentary is cited
