The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 52 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Why boastest thou thyself in mischief, O mighty man? the goodness of God endureth continually.

2 The tongue deviseth mischiefs; like a sharp razor, working deceitfully.

3 Thou lovest evil more than good; and lying rather than to speak righteousness. Selah.

4 Thou lovest all devouring words, O thou deceitful tongue.

5 God shall likewise destroy thee for ever, he shall take thee away, and pluck thee out of thy dwelling place, and root thee out of the land of the living. Selah.

6 The righteous also shall see, and fear, and shall laugh at him:

7 Lo, this is the man that made not God his strength; but trusted in the abundance of his riches, and strengthened himself in his wickedness.

8 But I am like a green olive tree in the house of God: I trust in the mercy of God for ever and ever.

9 I will praise thee for ever, because thou hast done it: and I will wait on thy name; for it is good before thy saints.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Against the boasting mighty man whose razor tongue devises deceit, loving evil more than good: God will destroy, pluck out, and root him up, and the righteous shall see, fear, and laugh. But the speaker is a green olive tree in God's house, trusting mercy for ever and waiting on God's good name. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Why do you boast yourself in mischief, O mighty man? The goodness of God endures continually: boasting in mischief is questioned, and God's continual goodness — against Doeg's brief cruelty — is the answer. The occasion, as the title records, is Doeg the Edomite's betrayal of Ahimelech's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The tongue devises mischiefs, like a sharp razor, working deceitfully: a tongue like a sharp razor cuts with deceit — Doeg's tongue cut down the priests. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. You love evil more than good, and lying rather than to speak righteousness: love of evil over good, and lying over righteous speech, is the character exposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. You love all devouring words, O deceitful tongue: devouring words are loved by the deceitful tongue — speech that swallows others whole. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. God shall likewise destroy you for ever, take you away, pluck you out of your dwelling place, and root you out of the land of the living: destruction for ever, taking away, plucking out, rooting out — the mighty man's end mirrors his uprooting of others. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The righteous also shall see, and fear, and shall laugh at him: seeing, fearing, and laughing — reverent fear at God's judgment joined to laughter at folly's end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Lo, this is the man that made not God his strength, but trusted in the abundance of his riches, and strengthened himself in his wickedness: the epitaph is stated — not God as strength, but riches trusted, and wickedness as strength. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. But I am like a green olive tree in the house of God; I trust in the mercy of God for ever and ever: against the uprooted mighty man stands the green olive in God's house, trusting mercy for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. I will praise you for ever, because you have done it, and I will wait on your name, for it is good before your saints: praise for ever for what God has done, and waiting on his good name before his saints, close the psalm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
