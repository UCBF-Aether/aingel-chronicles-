The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Enoch 6 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Enoch (London: SPCK, 1917; reprint of his 2nd edition, 1912); Project Gutenberg no. 77935)

*The Fall of the Angels: the Demoralisation of Mankind: the Intercession of the Angels on behalf of Mankind. The Dooms pronounced by God on the Angels: the Messianic Kingdom*

1 And it came to pass when the children of men had multiplied that in those days were born unto them beautiful and comely daughters.

2 And the angels, the children of the heaven, saw and lusted after them, and said to one another: ‘Come, let us choose us wives from among the children of men and beget us children.’

3 And Semjâzâ, who was their leader, said unto them: ‘I fear ye will not indeed agree to do this deed, and I alone shall have to pay the penalty of a great sin.’

4 And they all answered him and said: ‘Let us all swear an oath, and all bind ourselves by mutual imprecations not to abandon this plan but to do this thing.’

5 Then sware they all together and bound themselves by mutual imprecations upon it.

6 And they were in all two hundred; who descended ⌜in the days⌝ of Jared on the summit of Mount Hermon, and they called it Mount Hermon, because they had sworn and bound themselves by mutual imprecations upon it.

7 And these are the names of their leaders: Sêmîazâz, their leader, Arâkîba, Râmêêl, Kôkabîêl, Tâmîêl, Râmîêl, Dânêl, Êzêqêêl, Barâqîjâl, Asâêl, Armârôs, Batârêl, Anânêl, Zaqîêl, Samsâpêêl, Satarêl, Tûrêl, Jômjâêl, Sariêl.

8 These are their chiefs of tens.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Enoch – Genesis: an investigation, not a conclusion (editorial investigation — not a conclusion)

This section investigates a possible connection. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

Parallel or differenceWhat each text saysLabel
Men multiply; daughters are bornGenesis 6:1 says men began to multiply on the face of the earth and daughters were born unto them; 1 Enoch 6:1 says the same, that when the children of men had multiplied, beautiful and comely daughters were born unto them.Documented Standard corpus, gen.json 6:1; 1en.json 6:1
Heavenly beings take wivesGenesis 6:2 says the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair and took them wives of all which they chose; 1 Enoch 6:2 says the angels, the children of the heaven, saw and lusted after them and said, let us choose us wives from among the children of men. The identification of the “sons of God” with these angels is a reading of the parallel, not a wording both texts share.Interpreted Standard corpus, gen.json 6:2; 1en.json 6:2
A named leader, a number, an oath, a mountainGenesis 6 names no leader, number, oath, or mountain for this taking of wives. 1 Enoch 6 names Semjâzâ as leader, counts two hundred angels, has them swear an oath and bind themselves by mutual imprecations, and says they descended on Mount Hermon, named for that oath (verses 3–7).Documented Standard corpus, 1en.json 6:3–7; gen.json 6:1–2 silent on these details
A limit Genesis states, Enoch 6 does notGenesis 6:3 sets a limit of a hundred and twenty years and says the Lord’s spirit shall not always strive with man. Chapter 6 of 1 Enoch states no such limit; its concern is the oath, the descent, and the leaders' names.Documented Standard corpus, gen.json 6:3; 1en.json chapter 6

Genesis 6:1–2, in the King James text of the Standard corpus, reads:

1 And it came to pass, when men began to multiply on the face of the earth, and daughters were born unto them,

2 That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose.

Genesis 6:1–2, King James Version, Standard corpus (gen.json).

Interpreted The parallel is close at the level of story shape — multiplication, daughters, heavenly beings taking wives — and wide at the level of detail, where 1 Enoch 6 supplies names, a number, an oath, and a mountain that Genesis 6 does not. Genesis goes on to mention giants in verse 4; this chapter of 1 Enoch does not — the giants belong to the next chapter, outside this page.

Unresolved Whether any historical relationship exists between 1 Enoch 6 and Genesis 6 is Unresolved on the evidence presented here.
