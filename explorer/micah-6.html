The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Micah 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Hear ye now what the LORD saith; Arise, contend thou before the mountains, and let the hills hear thy voice.

2 Hear ye, O mountains, the LORD’s controversy, and ye strong foundations of the earth: for the LORD hath a controversy with his people, and he will plead with Israel.

3 O my people, what have I done unto thee? and wherein have I wearied thee? testify against me.

4 For I brought thee up out of the land of Egypt, and redeemed thee out of the house of servants; and I sent before thee Moses, Aaron, and Miriam.

5 O my people, remember now what Balak king of Moab consulted, and what Balaam the son of Beor answered him from Shittim unto Gilgal; that ye may know the righteousness of the LORD.

6 Wherewith shall I come before the LORD, and bow myself before the high God? shall I come before him with burnt offerings, with calves of a year old?

7 Will the LORD be pleased with thousands of rams, or with ten thousands of rivers of oil? shall I give my firstborn for my transgression, the fruit of my body for the sin of my soul?

8 He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?

9 The LORD’s voice crieth unto the city, and the man of wisdom shall see thy name: hear ye the rod, and who hath appointed it.

10 Are there yet the treasures of wickedness in the house of the wicked, and the scant measure that is abominable?

11 Shall I count them pure with the wicked balances, and with the bag of deceitful weights?

12 For the rich men thereof are full of violence, and the inhabitants thereof have spoken lies, and their tongue is deceitful in their mouth.

13 Therefore also will I make thee sick in smiting thee, in making thee desolate because of thy sins.

14 Thou shalt eat, but not be satisfied; and thy casting down shall be in the midst of thee; and thou shalt take hold, but shalt not deliver; and that which thou deliverest will I give up to the sword.

15 Thou shalt sow, but thou shalt not reap; thou shalt tread the olives, but thou shalt not anoint thee with oil; and sweet wine, but shalt not drink wine.

16 For the statutes of Omri are kept, and all the works of the house of Ahab, and ye walk in their counsels; that I should make thee a desolation, and the inhabitants thereof an hissing: therefore ye shall bear the reproach of my people.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

The LORD's controversy is set before the mountains and the strong foundations of the earth: what has God done to his people, and with what shall they come before him, with thousands of rams or ten thousands of rivers of oil, or their firstborn? The answer is the chapter's plainest line: do justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with God, set against dishonest measures, violence, and deceit that will leave the city desolate. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hear what the Lord says: arise and contend before the mountains, and let the hills hear. The controversy is pleaded openly, before the most enduring witnesses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Mountains and strong foundations of the earth are called to hear the Lord's controversy, for he will plead with Israel. Sin causes the controversy between God and man, and God reasons with us to teach us to reason with ourselves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. O my people, what have I done to you, and how have I wearied you? Testify against me. The people are called to declare why they were weary of God's worship and prone to idolatry, when he has given them no cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. He brought them up out of Egypt and redeemed them from the house of servants, sending Moses, Aaron and Miriam before them. Let them remember God's many favours to them and their fathers, and compare their unworthy, ungrateful conduct toward him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Remember what Balak king of Moab consulted and what Balaam answered him, from Shittim to Gilgal, that they may know the righteousness of the Lord. The whole journey from the wilderness to the promised land testifies to God's righteous dealings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. With what shall I come before the Lord and bow before the high God: with burnt offerings, with calves a year old? Henry reads this as the substance of Balak's consultation with Balaam about how to obtain Israel's God's favour, now asked by a conscience convicted of guilt and seeking peace and pardon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Will the Lord be pleased with thousands of rams, or ten thousands of rivers of oil, or with a firstborn given for transgression? The proposals betray ignorance, though they show zeal: they offer what is rich and costly, and a convicted sinner would give all the world for peace and pardon, yet they do not offer aright, for the blood of bulls and goats could never take away sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. He has shown you, O man, what is good: what the Lord requires is to do justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God. The good God requires is not paying a price for pardon, Henry says, but love to himself; moral duties are commanded because they are good for man, and must be kept as penitent sinners depending on the Redeemer and his atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. The Lord's voice cries to the city, and the man of wisdom will see his name: hear the rod, and who appointed it. Henry's rule fits here: hear the rod when it is coming, before you feel it, and hear it when it has come; the voice of God is to be heard in the rod of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Are there yet treasures of wickedness in the house of the wicked, and the scant, abominable measure? Having shown how necessary it is to do justly, God now shows how plain it is that they have done unjustly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Shall he count them pure with wicked balances and a bag of deceitful weights? Those dishonest in their dealings will never be reckoned pure, whatever shows of devotion they make. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The rich men of the city are full of violence, its inhabitants speak lies, and their tongue is deceitful in their mouth. What is got by fraud and oppression cannot be kept or enjoyed with satisfaction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Therefore God will make them sick in smiting them, making them desolate because of their sins. What they hold closest they commonly lose soonest, and sin is a root of bitterness soon planted but not soon plucked up. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. They will eat but not be satisfied, their casting down will be in their midst, they will seize but not deliver, and what they deliver will be given to the sword. Gain got by wickedness slips away in the very keeping. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. They will sow but not reap, tread olives but not anoint themselves with oil, and make sweet wine but not drink it. The fruit of their labour is denied them, as the fruit of fraud cannot be enjoyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The statutes of Omri are kept and all the works of Ahab's house, and they walk in their counsels, so God will make them a desolation and their inhabitants a hissing, and they will bear the reproach of his people. Their having once been God's people, Henry notes, now that they are backsliders, turns to their reproach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

Unfamiliar terms

  • Balak and Balaam. Balak, king of Moab, who consulted against Israel, and Balaam, son of Beor, who answered him; Micah 6 cites them as part of the journey from Shittim to Gilgal that testifies to God's righteous dealings.
