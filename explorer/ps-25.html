The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 25 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Unto thee, O LORD, do I lift up my soul.

2 O my God, I trust in thee: let me not be ashamed, let not mine enemies triumph over me.

3 Yea, let none that wait on thee be ashamed: let them be ashamed which transgress without cause.

4 Shew me thy ways, O LORD; teach me thy paths.

5 Lead me in thy truth, and teach me: for thou art the God of my salvation; on thee do I wait all the day.

6 Remember, O LORD, thy tender mercies and thy lovingkindnesses; for they have been ever of old.

7 Remember not the sins of my youth, nor my transgressions: according to thy mercy remember thou me for thy goodness’ sake, O LORD.

8 Good and upright is the LORD: therefore will he teach sinners in the way.

9 The meek will he guide in judgment: and the meek will he teach his way.

10 All the paths of the LORD are mercy and truth unto such as keep his covenant and his testimonies.

11 For thy name’s sake, O LORD, pardon mine iniquity; for it is great.

12 What man is he that feareth the LORD? him shall he teach in the way that he shall choose.

13 His soul shall dwell at ease; and his seed shall inherit the earth.

14 The secret of the LORD is with them that fear him; and he will shew them his covenant.

15 Mine eyes are ever toward the LORD; for he shall pluck my feet out of the net.

16 Turn thee unto me, and have mercy upon me; for I am desolate and afflicted.

17 The troubles of my heart are enlarged: O bring thou me out of my distresses.

18 Look upon mine affliction and my pain; and forgive all my sins.

19 Consider mine enemies; for they are many; and they hate me with cruel hatred.

20 O keep my soul, and deliver me: let me not be ashamed; for I put my trust in thee.

21 Let integrity and uprightness preserve me; for I wait on thee.

22 Redeem Israel, O God, out of all his troubles.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A psalm of waiting and guidance: the soul lifted up, trust against shame, prayer to be shown God's ways and led in truth, ancient mercies pleaded against the sins of youth, forgiveness sought alongside affliction, enemies many and cruel, closing by widening the personal prayer to redeem Israel out of all troubles. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Unto you, O Lord, I lift up my soul: the psalm begins with the whole self offered upward in prayer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. O my God, I trust in you; let me not be ashamed, nor my enemies triumph over me: shame before enemies is the feared outcome, and trust is the plea against it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Let none who wait on you be ashamed; let the causeless transgressors be ashamed: waiting on God is never put to shame, while groundless treachery is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Show me your ways, Lord; teach me your paths: guidance is sought as a pupil seeks a teacher, not as a map alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Lead me in your truth and teach me, for you are the God of my salvation; on you I wait all the day: truth, teaching, and waiting belong together, and salvation is the teacher's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Remember your tender mercies and lovingkindnesses, for they have been ever of old: God's ancient mercies are pleaded against present need. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Remember not the sins of my youth nor my transgressions; according to your mercy remember me for your goodness' sake: he asks God to remember mercy and forget sin — two opposite rememberings, both grounded in God's goodness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Good and upright is the Lord; therefore he teaches sinners in the way: God's goodness is the reason he teaches even sinners, rather than abandoning them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The meek he guides in judgment, and the meek he teaches his way: teachability — meekness — is the condition of guidance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. All the paths of the Lord are mercy and truth to those who keep his covenant and testimonies: every road God takes with his covenant people is paved with mercy and truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. For your name's sake, Lord, pardon my iniquity, for it is great: the greatness of the sin becomes an argument for pardon, because pardon magnifies God's name. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. What man fears the Lord? Him God shall teach in the way he shall choose: the fear of God is the school, and God chooses the curriculum. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. His soul shall dwell at ease, and his seed shall inherit the earth: quiet of soul now, and inheritance afterwards, follow the fear of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The secret of the Lord is with those who fear him, and he will show them his covenant: intimacy — God's secret counsel — is given to the fearing, and it takes the form of covenant knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. My eyes are ever toward the Lord, for he shall pluck my feet out of the net: steady looking to God is the way out of the snare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Turn to me and have mercy on me, for I am desolate and afflicted: desolation and affliction together plead for God's turning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The troubles of my heart are enlarged; bring me out of my distresses: inward troubles grow, and only God's bringing-out suffices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Look on my affliction and pain, and forgive all my sins: affliction and sin are joined in one prayer, for forgiveness is the deepest relief of suffering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Consider my enemies, for they are many, and hate me with cruel hatred: many enemies and cruel hatred make the case urgent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Keep my soul and deliver me; let me not be ashamed, for I trust in you: keeping, deliverance, and freedom from shame are the threefold request of trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Let integrity and uprightness preserve me, for I wait on you: the character he has kept becomes, under God, his preservation while he waits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Redeem Israel, O God, out of all his troubles: the personal prayer widens at last to the whole people, whose troubles are the psalmist's own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
