The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 John 4 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world.

2 Hereby know ye the Spirit of God: Every spirit that confesseth that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is of God:

3 And every spirit that confesseth not that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is not of God: and this is that spirit of antichrist, whereof ye have heard that it should come; and even now already is it in the world.

4 Ye are of God, little children, and have overcome them: because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.

5 They are of the world: therefore speak they of the world, and the world heareth them.

6 We are of God: he that knoweth God heareth us; he that is not of God heareth not us. Hereby know we the spirit of truth, and the spirit of error.

7 Beloved, let us love one another: for love is of God; and every one that loveth is born of God, and knoweth God.

8 He that loveth not knoweth not God; for God is love.

9 In this was manifested the love of God toward us, because that God sent his only begotten Son into the world, that we might live through him.

10 Herein is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us, and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins.

11 Beloved, if God so loved us, we ought also to love one another.

12 No man hath seen God at any time. If we love one another, God dwelleth in us, and his love is perfected in us.

13 Hereby know we that we dwell in him, and he in us, because he hath given us of his Spirit.

14 And we have seen and do testify that the Father sent the Son to be the Saviour of the world.

15 Whosoever shall confess that Jesus is the Son of God, God dwelleth in him, and he in God.

16 And we have known and believed the love that God hath to us. God is love; and he that dwelleth in love dwelleth in God, and God in him.

17 Herein is our love made perfect, that we may have boldness in the day of judgment: because as he is, so are we in this world.

18 There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment. He that feareth is not made perfect in love.

19 We love him, because he first loved us.

20 If a man say, I love God, and hateth his brother, he is a liar: for he that loveth not his brother whom he hath seen, how can he love God whom he hath not seen?

21 And this commandment have we from him, That he who loveth God love his brother also.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Do not believe every spirit, the writer warns, but test them: every spirit confessing Jesus Christ come in the flesh is of God, and every spirit denying him is the spirit of antichrist. Believers have overcome because greater is he in them than he in the world. Then the chapter's centre: God is love, shown in sending his only Son as the atoning sacrifice for sins, so that those loved in this way must love one another, and perfect love casts out fear, while claiming to love God and hating a brother is false. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Do not believe every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world. Henry notes that Christians well acquainted with Scripture may, in humble dependence on divine teaching, discern teachers who follow the apostles from those who contradict them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The Spirit of God is known by this: every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ come in the flesh is of God. The sum of revealed religion lies in the doctrine concerning Christ, his person and office, and confession of his true incarnation is the mark of the spirit of truth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Every spirit that does not confess Jesus Christ come in the flesh is not of God, and this is the spirit of antichrist, already in the world. Henry warns that false teachers speak according to the world's maxims and tastes so as not to offend carnal people, which is why the world approves and follows them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Little children, you are of God and have overcome them, because greater is he who is in you than he who is in the world. The indwelling Spirit is stronger than the world's spirit, and that is the ground of the believers' victory over false teaching. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. False teachers are of the world, so they speak of the world and the world hears them. Their rapid progress and many followers are no proof of truth: the world loves its own, and its own love it, suiting schemes and discourses to corrupt taste. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. We are of God: whoever knows God hears us, and whoever is not of God does not hear us, and hereby we know the spirit of truth and the spirit of error. Henry's rule is that the purer and holier a doctrine is, the more likely it is of God, and no other rule can try the spirits. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Let us love one another, for love is of God, and everyone who loves is born of God and knows God. The Spirit of God is the Spirit of love, and one who does not love the image of God in his people has no saving knowledge of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Whoever does not love does not know God, for God is love. It is God's nature to be kind and to give happiness, and the law of God is love, so that lovelessness betrays ignorance of him, whatever is professed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. In this the love of God was manifested toward us: God sent his only begotten Son into the world that we might live through him. The provision of the gospel, forgiving sin and saving sinners consistently with God's glory and justice, is itself the display that God is love. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Herein is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins. The origin of love between God and sinners is not our love for him but his free love for us, shown in the method by which we were saved when strict justice would have condemned us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Beloved, if God so loved us, we ought also to love one another. His love is not designed to be fruitless in us, and when its proper end is gained it may be said to be perfected, as faith is perfected by its works. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. No one has seen God at any time, yet if we love one another God dwells in us and his love is perfected in us. A loving Christian is, in Henry's phrase, a perfect Christian for any good duty, expert at it, because love oils the wheels of his affections and sets him on what helps his brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Hereby we know that we dwell in him and he in us, because he has given us of his Spirit. The test is inward and practical: those truly persuaded they are sons of God call him Father, hate sin and whatever disagrees with his will, and desire heartily to do his will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. We have seen and testify that the Father sent the Son to be the Saviour of the world. The apostle attests this sending as an eyewitness truth: the Father willed the Son's coming into this world, and the Son came for the world's salvation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God has God dwelling in him, and he in God. Henry explains this confession as faith in the heart as foundation, acknowledgment with the mouth to the glory of God and Christ, and profession in life and conduct against the flatteries and frowns of the world. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. We have known and believed the love God has toward us, and God is love, so whoever dwells in love dwells in God and God in him. That God dwells in us and we in him would be words too high for mortals, had not God himself put them before us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Herein is our love made perfect, that we may have boldness in the day of judgment, because as he is, so are we in this world. Happy are those with holy boldness before the Judge at that day, knowing he is their Friend and Advocate, and looking and waiting for his appearance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear, because fear has torment, and whoever fears is not made perfect in love. Henry distinguishes the fear of God, high regard and veneration, from being afraid of him: obedience from love is a dutiful child's service to a beloved father, not a slave's toil from dread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. We love him because he first loved us. This is the whole ground stated in one line: our love is response, not origin, and it is proved by its effects on temper and conduct toward the brethren. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. If a person says he loves God and hates his brother, he is a liar, for how can he love God whom he has not seen if he does not love his brother whom he has seen? Henry is blunt: professed love of God joined to anger, revenge, or selfishness gives the profession the lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. This commandment we have from him: whoever loves God must love his brother also. Love of God and love of brother are commanded together, and the change from natural enmity into affection and gratitude is the seal and earnest of eternal happiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
