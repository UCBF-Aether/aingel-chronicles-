The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 13 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Send thou men, that they may search the land of Canaan, which I give unto the children of Israel: of every tribe of their fathers shall ye send a man, every one a ruler among them.

3 And Moses by the commandment of the LORD sent them from the wilderness of Paran: all those men were heads of the children of Israel.

4 And these were their names: of the tribe of Reuben, Shammua the son of Zaccur.

5 Of the tribe of Simeon, Shaphat the son of Hori.

6 Of the tribe of Judah, Caleb the son of Jephunneh.

7 Of the tribe of Issachar, Igal the son of Joseph.

8 Of the tribe of Ephraim, Oshea the son of Nun.

9 Of the tribe of Benjamin, Palti the son of Raphu.

10 Of the tribe of Zebulun, Gaddiel the son of Sodi.

11 Of the tribe of Joseph, namely, of the tribe of Manasseh, Gaddi the son of Susi.

12 Of the tribe of Dan, Ammiel the son of Gemalli.

13 Of the tribe of Asher, Sethur the son of Michael.

14 Of the tribe of Naphtali, Nahbi the son of Vophsi.

15 Of the tribe of Gad, Geuel the son of Machi.

16 These are the names of the men which Moses sent to spy out the land. And Moses called Oshea the son of Nun Jehoshua.

17 And Moses sent them to spy out the land of Canaan, and said unto them, Get you up this way southward, and go up into the mountain:

18 And see the land, what it is, and the people that dwelleth therein, whether they be strong or weak, few or many;

19 And what the land is that they dwell in, whether it be good or bad; and what cities they be that they dwell in, whether in tents, or in strong holds;

20 And what the land is, whether it be fat or lean, whether there be wood therein, or not. And be ye of good courage, and bring of the fruit of the land. Now the time was the time of the firstripe grapes.

21 So they went up, and searched the land from the wilderness of Zin unto Rehob, as men come to Hamath.

22 And they ascended by the south, and came unto Hebron; where Ahiman, Sheshai, and Talmai, the children of Anak, were. (Now Hebron was built seven years before Zoan in Egypt.)

23 And they came unto the brook of Eshcol, and cut down from thence a branch with one cluster of grapes, and they bare it between two upon a staff; and they brought of the pomegranates, and of the figs.

24 The place was called the brook Eshcol, because of the cluster of grapes which the children of Israel cut down from thence.

25 And they returned from searching of the land after forty days.

26 And they went and came to Moses, and to Aaron, and to all the congregation of the children of Israel, unto the wilderness of Paran, to Kadesh; and brought back word unto them, and unto all the congregation, and shewed them the fruit of the land.

27 And they told him, and said, We came unto the land whither thou sentest us, and surely it floweth with milk and honey; and this is the fruit of it.

28 Nevertheless the people be strong that dwell in the land, and the cities are walled, and very great: and moreover we saw the children of Anak there.

29 The Amalekites dwell in the land of the south: and the Hittites, and the Jebusites, and the Amorites, dwell in the mountains: and the Canaanites dwell by the sea, and by the coast of Jordan.

30 And Caleb stilled the people before Moses, and said, Let us go up at once, and possess it; for we are well able to overcome it.

31 But the men that went up with him said, We be not able to go up against the people; for they are stronger than we.

32 And they brought up an evil report of the land which they had searched unto the children of Israel, saying, The land, through which we have gone to search it, is a land that eateth up the inhabitants thereof; and all the people that we saw in it are men of a great stature.

33 And there we saw the giants, the sons of Anak, which come of the giants: and we were in our own sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight.

How to read the labels

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter sends twelve spies, one ruler from each tribe, from the wilderness of Paran to search the land of Canaan. They spend forty days, reach the brook of Eshcol, and bring back a cluster of grapes so large it is borne between two men on a staff, with pomegranates and figs, and report that the land flows with milk and honey. But ten of them report walled cities and giants, the sons of Anak, and say Israel are as grasshoppers beside them; only Caleb stills the people and urges them to go up at once and possess it. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. Moses was told to send men to search the land of Canaan, which God gave to Israel. JFB notes from Deuteronomy that the proposal came from the people who petitioned for it, and God granted it at once as a trial and a punishment of their distrust, while Henry adds they had a better opinion of their own policy than of God's wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Moses sent them from the wilderness of Paran by the Lord's commandment, all rulers of Israel. JFB clarifies these were heads and leading men, though not the first-rank princes named in chapter 10, a distinction that matters when their report later carries the people's trust. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Of Judah, Caleb son of Jephunneh was sent. Caleb is named here among the twelve, but Henry already singles him out with Joshua as the only two who possessed the strong faith from which courage in such an undertaking can spring. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. These are the names of the men Moses sent to spy out the land, and Moses called Oshea son of Nun, Jehoshua. JFB explains Oshea means a desire of salvation, while Jehoshua, by prefixing God's name, means divinely appointed, head of salvation, Saviour, the same as Jesus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Moses sent them to spy out Canaan, saying, get up southward and go up into the mountain, and be of good courage. JFB identifies the route over Mount Seir, northeast across Paran into the southern promised land, and Henry stresses the charge: courage in a great undertaking and a great trust can only spring from strong faith. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. They were to see the land, what it was, and the people dwelling in it, whether strong or weak, few or many. The commission asked for honest observation, not for a verdict on whether God could give the land, a distinction the ten spies would soon forget. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. They were to see what land the people dwelt in, whether good or bad, and what cities, whether in tents or strong holds. The questions about dwellings measured how the land could be taken and held, information for a campaign, not an excuse for retreat. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. They were to see whether the land was fat or lean, whether wood was in it, and to bring of its fruit, for the time was the time of the firstripe grapes. JFB dates the season to August for first clusters, with second in September and third in October, and the forty days' absence fixes their grapes to the second gathering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. They went up and searched the land from the wilderness of Zin to Rehob, as men come to Hamath. JFB traces the route south to north along the Jordan, with the return by the western border through Sidonian and Philistine territory, and describes Zin as a long sandy plain continuing the Jordan valley from the Dead Sea to the Gulf of Akaba. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. They ascended by the south and came to Hebron, where Ahiman, Sheshai and Talmai, children of Anak, were. JFB describes Hebron, in the heart of Judah's mountains, as one of the oldest cities in the world, its Anakite chiefs ruling distinct townships, and notes its building seven years before Zoan in Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. They came to the brook of Eshcol and cut down a branch with one cluster of grapes, borne between two on a staff, with pomegranates and figs. JFB notes the grapes of that valley are still magnificent, a cluster sometimes weighing ten or twelve pounds, and the two-man carrying preserved the specimen entire for a people familiar only with Egypt's scanty vines. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The place was called the brook Eshcol, because of the cluster of grapes the children of Israel cut down there. JFB renders Eshcol as the torrent of the cluster, a little southwest of Hebron, whose sloping hills are still covered with vineyards matching its ancient celebrity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. They returned from searching the land after forty days. Henry wonders that Israel waited forty days for the spies when ready to enter under every assurance of success, and reads the waiting itself as distrust of God's power and promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. They came to Moses, Aaron and all the congregation in the wilderness of Paran, to Kadesh, and brought back word and showed them the land's fruit. JFB notes Kadesh was an important encampment, though its exact site is uncertain, supposed to be Ain-el-Weibeh or Petra. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. They told him, we came to the land you sent us to, and surely it flows with milk and honey, and this is its fruit. JFB observes the report was given publicly and artfully arranged to begin with commendations of the country's fertility, so that their subsequent slanders might more readily receive credit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Nevertheless the people are strong that dwell in the land, and the cities are walled and very great, and we saw the children of Anak there. Henry's diagnosis is that though they found the land as good as God said, they would not believe it as sure as he said, but despaired of having it though Eternal Truth had engaged it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. The Amalekites dwell in the south, the Hittites, Jebusites and Amorites in the mountains, and the Canaanites by the sea and by Jordan. JFB maps them: Amalek between the Dead and Red Seas skirting Canaan, the mountain peoples in southern Palestine, and the Canaanite remnant divided between the Jordan side and the Mediterranean coast. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Caleb stilled the people before Moses and said, let us go up at once and possess it, for we are well able to overcome it. Henry notes Caleb does not say, let us go up and conquer it, but let us go and possess it: difficulties in the way of salvation dwindle and vanish before a lively, active faith in God's power and promise. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. But the men who went up with him said, we are not able to go up against the people, for they are stronger than we. Henry's rule is that unbelief overlooks God's promises and power, magnifies every danger and difficulty, and fills the heart with discouragement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. They brought up an evil report of the land, saying it eats up its inhabitants, and all the people we saw in it are men of great stature. JFB calls this a false and exaggerated report, taking what was true of a few as descriptive of all, and explains the eating-up charge as an unhealthy climate, with Jewish writers noting many funerals from plague or hornet in God's providence at that time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. There we saw the giants, sons of Anak, who come of the giants, and we were in our own sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight. JFB traces Anak to Arba, a great man whose name suggests a splendid collar, and reads the Anakim as a distinguished family or select body of warriors chosen for size, with grasshoppers as an Oriental exaggeration by treacherous spies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
