The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Chronicles 9 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And when the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon, she came to prove Solomon with hard questions at Jerusalem, with a very great company, and camels that bare spices, and gold in abundance, and precious stones: and when she was come to Solomon, she communed with him of all that was in her heart.

2 And Solomon told her all her questions: and there was nothing hid from Solomon which he told her not.

3 And when the queen of Sheba had seen the wisdom of Solomon, and the house that he had built,

4 And the meat of his table, and the sitting of his servants, and the attendance of his ministers, and their apparel; his cupbearers also, and their apparel; and his ascent by which he went up into the house of the LORD; there was no more spirit in her.

5 And she said to the king, It was a true report which I heard in mine own land of thine acts, and of thy wisdom:

6 Howbeit I believed not their words, until I came, and mine eyes had seen it: and, behold, the one half of the greatness of thy wisdom was not told me: for thou exceedest the fame that I heard.

7 Happy are thy men, and happy are these thy servants, which stand continually before thee, and hear thy wisdom.

8 Blessed be the LORD thy God, which delighted in thee to set thee on his throne, to be king for the LORD thy God: because thy God loved Israel, to establish them for ever, therefore made he thee king over them, to do judgment and justice.

9 And she gave the king an hundred and twenty talents of gold, and of spices great abundance, and precious stones: neither was there any such spice as the queen of Sheba gave king Solomon.

10 And the servants also of Huram, and the servants of Solomon, which brought gold from Ophir, brought algum trees and precious stones.

11 And the king made of the algum trees terraces to the house of the LORD, and to the king’s palace, and harps and psalteries for singers: and there were none such seen before in the land of Judah.

12 And king Solomon gave to the queen of Sheba all her desire, whatsoever she asked, beside that which she had brought unto the king. So she turned, and went away to her own land, she and her servants.

13 Now the weight of gold that came to Solomon in one year was six hundred and threescore and six talents of gold;

14 Beside that which chapmen and merchants brought. And all the kings of Arabia and governors of the country brought gold and silver to Solomon.

15 And king Solomon made two hundred targets of beaten gold: six hundred shekels of beaten gold went to one target.

16 And three hundred shields made he of beaten gold: three hundred shekels of gold went to one shield. And the king put them in the house of the forest of Lebanon.

17 Moreover the king made a great throne of ivory, and overlaid it with pure gold.

18 And there were six steps to the throne, with a footstool of gold, which were fastened to the throne, and stays on each side of the sitting place, and two lions standing by the stays:

19 And twelve lions stood there on the one side and on the other upon the six steps. There was not the like made in any kingdom.

20 And all the drinking vessels of king Solomon were of gold, and all the vessels of the house of the forest of Lebanon were of pure gold: none were of silver; it was not any thing accounted of in the days of Solomon.

21 For the king’s ships went to Tarshish with the servants of Huram: every three years once came the ships of Tarshish bringing gold, and silver, ivory, and apes, and peacocks.

22 And king Solomon passed all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom.

23 And all the kings of the earth sought the presence of Solomon, to hear his wisdom, that God had put in his heart.

24 And they brought every man his present, vessels of silver, and vessels of gold, and raiment, harness, and spices, horses, and mules, a rate year by year.

25 And Solomon had four thousand stalls for horses and chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen; whom he bestowed in the chariot cities, and with the king at Jerusalem.

26 And he reigned over all the kings from the river even unto the land of the Philistines, and to the border of Egypt.

27 And the king made silver in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar trees made he as the sycomore trees that are in the low plains in abundance.

28 And they brought unto Solomon horses out of Egypt, and out of all lands.

29 Now the rest of the acts of Solomon, first and last, are they not written in the book of Nathan the prophet, and in the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and in the visions of Iddo the seer against Jeroboam the son of Nebat?

30 And Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years.

31 And Solomon slept with his fathers, and he was buried in the city of David his father: and Rehoboam his son reigned in his stead.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the queen of Sheba's visit to test Solomon with hard questions, the splendour she found, Solomon's wealth and trade, and his death, with Rehoboam succeeding him. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. And when the queen of Sheba heard of Solomon's fame, she came to prove him with hard questions at Jerusalem, with a very great company, camels bearing spices, gold in abundance and precious stones, and communed with him of all in her heart. Henry notes those who know the worth of true wisdom grudge no pains nor cost to obtain it, and the queen, learning from Solomon to serve God, thought herself well paid for her pains. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. And Solomon told her all her questions, and there was nothing hid from Solomon which he told her not. Henry notes Solomon was communicative of his wisdom and willing to teach what he knew, having freely received and freely giving, as those rich in wisdom should be ready to distribute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. And when the queen of Sheba had seen Solomon's wisdom, and the house he had built. Henry notes good order in a great family, especially in the things of God and regular worship, is highly expedient and much to be admired wherever found, and the queen was exceedingly affected by the propriety of Solomon's household and temple service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And the meat of his table, the sitting of his servants, the attendance of his ministers and their apparel, his cupbearers and their apparel, and his ascent to the house of the Lord, left no more spirit in her. Henry notes the queen was affected by the order with which Solomon's servants attended him and with which he and they attended in God's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And she said to the king, It was a true report I heard in my own land of thine acts and thy wisdom. Henry's visit account applies: the report of Solomon's wisdom, consecrated to God in building and dedicating the temple, proved true on sight, for God made his wisdom and wealth redound to his reputation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Howbeit I believed not their words until I came and mine eyes had seen it, and behold, the one half of the greatness of thy wisdom was not told me, for thou exceedest the fame I heard. Henry notes the queen's experience as an example in enquiring after wisdom: the reality exceeded report, as heavenly wisdom is a pearl of great price well purchased at any cost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Happy are thy men, and happy are these thy servants, which stand continually before thee and hear thy wisdom. Henry notes those are happy who have constant converse with the knowing, wise and good, and the queen thought Solomon's servants happy who continually heard his wisdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Blessed be the Lord thy God, which delighted in thee to set thee on his throne, to be king for the Lord thy God, because thy God loved Israel to establish them for ever, therefore made he thee king to do judgment and justice. Henry notes we ought to rejoice and give God thanks for the gifts and usefulness of others, tracing king and kingdom's happiness to divine favour, because God delighted in him and loved Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And she gave the king a hundred and twenty talents of gold, and spices in great abundance, and precious stones, and there was no such spice as the queen of Sheba gave. Henry notes it becomes the wise and good to be generous according to place and power, and queen and king confirmed their friendship by mutual presents, neither covetous of money. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And the servants of Huram and of Solomon, which brought gold from Ophir, brought algum trees and precious stones. Henry's riches account applies: Solomon's trade brought gold and precious materials from afar, and no gold or silver mines were in his own kingdom, yet plenty came by export and exchange. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And the king made of the algum trees terraces to the house of the Lord and to the king's palace, and harps and psalteries for singers, and there were none such seen before in Judah. Henry notes the temple service's music and order were part of Solomon's splendour, with instruments for singers in God's house. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. And King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba all her desire, whatsoever she asked, beside what she had brought, so she turned and went to her own land, she and her servants. Henry notes Solomon was generous to her as she to him, and both knew how to value wisdom, cultivating acquaintance by mutual presents. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Now the weight of gold that came to Solomon in one year was six hundred and sixty-six talents of gold. Henry notes Solomon reigned in wealth and power, ease and fullness, such as could never since be paralleled, fulfilling the promise of riches, wealth and honour such as no kings had or shall have. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Beside what chapmen and merchants brought, all the kings of Arabia and governors of the country brought gold and silver to Solomon. Henry notes presents came not as extorted tribute but as freewill offerings to procure his favour, or in exchange for husbandry produce, because neighbours coveted his acquaintance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. And King Solomon made two hundred targets of beaten gold, six hundred shekels of beaten gold going to one target. Henry notes Solomon appeared in public with great splendour, with 200 targets and 300 shields of beaten gold carried before him, a lustre typical of the spiritual glory of Messiah's kingdom, though artificial beside the lilies' natural beauty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And three hundred shields made he of beaten gold, three hundred shekels to one shield, and the king put them in the house of the forest of Lebanon. Henry groups the shields with the targets as the public splendour carried before Solomon, recommending him to eyes that judge by sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Moreover the king made a great throne of ivory, and overlaid it with pure gold. Henry notes Solomon sat upon a most stately throne, with no like in any kingdom, a faint representation of Messiah's throne above every throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And there were six steps to the throne, with a footstool of gold fastened to it, and stays on each side of the sitting place, and two lions standing by the stays. Henry's throne description applies: the stately throne with its steps and lions was unmatched in any kingdom. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. And twelve lions stood on the one side and the other upon the six steps, and there was not the like made in any kingdom. Henry notes again there was not the like in any kingdom, Solomon's throne standing alone in splendour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And all Solomon's drinking vessels were of gold, and all vessels of the house of the forest of Lebanon were of pure gold, none of silver, for silver was not anything accounted of in Solomon's days. Henry notes Solomon had greater plenty of gold and silver than any prince, though no mines were in his kingdom, fetched home by trade and export. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. For the king's ships went to Tarshish with Huram's servants, and every three years came the ships of Tarshish bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes and peacocks. Henry notes Solomon's merchandise brought these riches by sea, with Tyrian seamen, showing that grace, not gold, is the best riches. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And King Solomon passed all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom. Henry notes the promise was fulfilled: riches, wealth and honour such as no kings have had or shall have, with Solomon reigning forty years in profound peace, unlike princes famed for wars. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. And all the kings of the earth sought Solomon's presence, to hear his wisdom that God had put in his heart. Henry notes no prince was so renowned for wisdom, so courted and consulted, whether in natural philosophy, physic, state policy, prudence, or religion, and their seeking Solomon will condemn men's contempt of Christ, in whom all treasures of wisdom are hidden. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. And they brought every man his present, vessels of silver and gold, raiment, harness, spices, horses and mules, a rate year by year. Henry notes all kings of the earth in those parts brought presents, because they coveted his acquaintance and friendship, as a type of Christ, to whom wise men brought gold, frankincense and myrrh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And Solomon had four thousand stalls for horses and chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen, whom he bestowed in the chariot cities and with the king at Jerusalem. Henry's account of Solomon's splendour and strength applies: his chariot cities and horsemen were part of the unrivalled ease and fullness of his reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. And he reigned over all kings from the river even unto the Philistines' land, and to the border of Egypt. Henry notes Solomon's dominion and peace were part of the fulfilled promise, reigning over a wide empire without the wars that famed other princes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. And the king made silver in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar trees as the sycamore trees of the low plains in abundance. Henry notes the plenty of gold and silver in Solomon's days, when silver was not accounted of, and the kingdom's wealth was so abundant that common things became plentiful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. And they brought unto Solomon horses out of Egypt, and out of all lands. Henry notes all kings brought presents and trade to Solomon, and horses from Egypt and every land were part of the yearly traffic that made his court renowned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Now the rest of Solomon's acts, first and last, are they not written in the book of Nathan the prophet, and in the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and in the visions of Iddo the seer against Jeroboam? Henry notes the close brings Solomon to the grave with unstained reputation, for Chronicles omits his latter defection, and his acts stand recorded by prophets and seers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years. Henry notes Solomon reigned forty years in profound peace, a reign of wealth and wisdom unmatched, yet the throne could not secure him from the grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. And Solomon slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David his father, and Rehoboam his son reigned in his stead. Henry notes Solomon dying, stripped of pomp, left all to a son he knew would be a fool, vanity and vexation of spirit, yet Scripture silence on his sin speaks pardon: when God pardons, he casts sin behind his back and remembers it no more. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
