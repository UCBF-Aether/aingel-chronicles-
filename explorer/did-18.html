The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Didascalia 18 (J. M. Harden, The Ethiopic Didascalia (London: SPCK, 1920), public domain (Cornell University Library scan, archive.org cu31924096083336))

*OF ORPHANS*

1 CHRISTIAN people who have no children ought to educate orphans, whether youths or virgins, and adopt them as their own children, and love them yet more. And he that hath a son who hath attained the due age, let him marry the orphan virgin.

2 And if ye do thus, ye shall accomplish a great work and be the fathers of the orphans, (and) obtain a reward from the Lord God for the service ye have done. But if there be one that is proud, and despiseth the orphan, and saith, I will not marry a poor woman,’ let him understand then who is the father of the orphan, and the protector of the widow.

3 For he that thinketh to do thus shall scatter his wealth to another, and shall fulfil in himself that which the Scripture saith, “ What the saints have not eaten, the Syrians‘ shall eat,” even as Isaiah saith, “Your land a foreign enemy shall devour it in your sight.”

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In plain language: this short chapter is about orphans. Christian people without children of their own should educate orphans, boys and girls alike, adopt them as their own children and love them all the more; a man whose son has come of age should marry the orphan girl. Despising an orphan, it warns, misunderstands who the orphan's father and protector is, and wealth so hoarded will be scattered to another.
