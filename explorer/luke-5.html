The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Luke 5 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, that, as the people pressed upon him to hear the word of God, he stood by the lake of Gennesaret,

2 And saw two ships standing by the lake: but the fishermen were gone out of them, and were washing their nets.

3 And he entered into one of the ships, which was Simon’s, and prayed him that he would thrust out a little from the land. And he sat down, and taught the people out of the ship.

4 Now when he had left speaking, he said unto Simon, Launch out into the deep, and let down your nets for a draught.

5 And Simon answering said unto him, Master, we have toiled all the night, and have taken nothing: nevertheless at thy word I will let down the net.

6 And when they had this done, they inclosed a great multitude of fishes: and their net brake.

7 And they beckoned unto their partners, which were in the other ship, that they should come and help them. And they came, and filled both the ships, so that they began to sink.

8 When Simon Peter saw it, he fell down at Jesus’ knees, saying, Depart from me; for I am a sinful man, O Lord.

9 For he was astonished, and all that were with him, at the draught of the fishes which they had taken:

10 And so was also James, and John, the sons of Zebedee, which were partners with Simon. And Jesus said unto Simon, Fear not; from henceforth thou shalt catch men.

11 And when they had brought their ships to land, they forsook all, and followed him.

12 And it came to pass, when he was in a certain city, behold a man full of leprosy: who seeing Jesus fell on his face, and besought him, saying, Lord, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean.

13 And he put forth his hand, and touched him, saying, I will: be thou clean. And immediately the leprosy departed from him.

14 And he charged him to tell no man: but go, and shew thyself to the priest, and offer for thy cleansing, according as Moses commanded, for a testimony unto them.

15 But so much the more went there a fame abroad of him: and great multitudes came together to hear, and to be healed by him of their infirmities.

16 And he withdrew himself into the wilderness, and prayed.

17 And it came to pass on a certain day, as he was teaching, that there were Pharisees and doctors of the law sitting by, which were come out of every town of Galilee, and Judaea, and Jerusalem: and the power of the Lord was present to heal them.

18 And, behold, men brought in a bed a man which was taken with a palsy: and they sought means to bring him in, and to lay him before him.

19 And when they could not find by what way they might bring him in because of the multitude, they went upon the housetop, and let him down through the tiling with his couch into the midst before Jesus.

20 And when he saw their faith, he said unto him, Man, thy sins are forgiven thee.

21 And the scribes and the Pharisees began to reason, saying, Who is this which speaketh blasphemies? Who can forgive sins, but God alone?

22 But when Jesus perceived their thoughts, he answering said unto them, What reason ye in your hearts?

23 Whether is easier, to say, Thy sins be forgiven thee; or to say, Rise up and walk?

24 But that ye may know that the Son of man hath power upon earth to forgive sins, (he said unto the sick of the palsy,) I say unto thee, Arise, and take up thy couch, and go into thine house.

25 And immediately he rose up before them, and took up that whereon he lay, and departed to his own house, glorifying God.

26 And they were all amazed, and they glorified God, and were filled with fear, saying, We have seen strange things to day.

27 And after these things he went forth, and saw a publican, named Levi, sitting at the receipt of custom: and he said unto him, Follow me.

28 And he left all, rose up, and followed him.

29 And Levi made him a great feast in his own house: and there was a great company of publicans and of others that sat down with them.

30 But their scribes and Pharisees murmured against his disciples, saying, Why do ye eat and drink with publicans and sinners?

31 And Jesus answering said unto them, They that are whole need not a physician; but they that are sick.

32 I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.

33 And they said unto him, Why do the disciples of John fast often, and make prayers, and likewise the disciples of the Pharisees; but thine eat and drink?

34 And he said unto them, Can ye make the children of the bridechamber fast, while the bridegroom is with them?

35 But the days will come, when the bridegroom shall be taken away from them, and then shall they fast in those days.

36 And he spake also a parable unto them; No man putteth a piece of a new garment upon an old; if otherwise, then both the new maketh a rent, and the piece that was taken out of the new agreeth not with the old.

37 And no man putteth new wine into old bottles; else the new wine will burst the bottles, and be spilled, and the bottles shall perish.

38 But new wine must be put into new bottles; and both are preserved.

39 No man also having drunk old wine straightway desireth new: for he saith, The old is better.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

After a night of empty nets, Simon’s catch at Jesus’ word is so great the nets break, and Simon, James, and John leave all to follow him (vv. 1–11). A leper is cleansed, and Jesus withdraws to pray even as his fame spreads (vv. 12–16). A paralyzed man is let down through the roof before him; Jesus first forgives his sins and then, to prove the Son of man’s authority, tells him to rise and walk home (vv. 17–26). Levi the publican is called and feasts with publicans and sinners, and questions about fasting draw the answers of the bridegroom, the new garment, and the new wine (vv. 27–39). Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes; Standard corpus, Luke 5

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. As people pressed upon him to hear the word of God, Jesus stood by the lake of Gennesaret. Gill notes the crowds were eager only to hear, and the lake, honoured by Christ's preaching, miracles, and resurrection appearances, became famous through him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, John Gill
  • Verse 2. He saw two ships by the lake, but the fishermen were out washing their nets. Gill notes the boats belonged to Peter and Andrew and to Zebedee's sons, and the men were cleansing nets after a fruitless night, which makes the coming miracle more illustrious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill
  • Verse 3. He entered Simon's ship, asked him to thrust out a little, and sat down and taught the people from it. Gill notes sitting to teach was the Jewish custom, and JFB notes the boat was a platform for teaching, as in Matthew 13. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, John Gill, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. When he had left speaking, he told Simon to launch into the deep and let down the nets for a draught. Henry's lesson is that when preaching is done, Christ sends Peter back to his calling, and time spent in public religion need not hinder worldly business but may further it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, John Gill, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Master, we have toiled all night and taken nothing; nevertheless at thy word I will let down the net. Gill notes the objection is honest — night was the best fishing time, and they were wearied and empty — yet faith obeys at Christ's word; JFB adds that letting down again was a mere act of faith, since as a fisherman Peter knew how hopeless it was. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, John Gill, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They enclosed a great multitude of fishes, and their net brake. Gill explains the net was beginning to break under weight and number, yet not so as to lose the fish, gathered there by Christ's secret divine power; JFB likewise reads "was breaking." Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. They beckoned to partners in the other ship, who came and filled both ships so that they began to sink. Gill identifies the partners as James and John, who lay at anchor near shore and had to be signalled by hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill
  • Verse 8. Simon Peter fell at Jesus' knees, saying, Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, O Lord. Henry notes we must all, like Peter, own ourselves sinful men, yet beseech Christ not to depart, for woe if the Saviour departs from sinners; JFB adds Peter did not wish Christ to leave, but cried, in Isaiah's manner, that a sinner is not fit company for such glory. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, John Gill, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. He was astonished, and all with him, at the draught taken. Gill notes the astonishment covered Andrew, the servants, and all in the boat, at a catch the like of which they had never seen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill
  • Verse 10. James and John, Zebedee's sons and Simon's partners, were likewise astonished; and Jesus said, Fear not, from henceforth thou shalt catch men. JFB notes "fear not" shows the Lord read Peter's speech aright, and "from henceforth" marks a new stage: the net of the gospel will draw men out of sin's depths into life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing John Gill, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. They brought their ships to land, forsook all, and followed him. Henry stresses the point: they forsook all when their calling prospered, and to quit riches for Christ when riches increase is thankworthy; JFB notes they had forsaken all before, do it again now, and after Pentecost finally abandon the trade. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, John Gill, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. In a certain city a man full of leprosy fell on his face, beseeching, Lord, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean. Henry notes he had the distemper in a high degree, representing our natural pollution by sin, with no soundness from crown to sole; Gill draws the same likeness at length. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, John Gill
  • Verse 13. He put forth his hand, touched him, and said, I will: be thou clean; immediately the leprosy departed. Investigation (editorial — a parallel examined, not concluded)

This section investigates a possible parallel. It does not conclude one. No parallel below implies direct dependence, shared authorship, or historical certainty.

The paralytic and Levi — Luke 5 and Mark 2

Luke 5:17–32 and Mark 2:1–17 tell the same two episodes in the same order: a paralyzed man brought before Jesus, forgiven and then healed to prove the Son of man’s authority on earth to forgive sins, followed by the call of Levi at the receipt of custom and a feast with publicans and sinners. The differences are small and vivid: Luke’s bearers let the man down through the tiling into the midst before Jesus, while Mark’s bearers uncover the roof and are four in number; Luke names Levi a publican and gives the feast in Levi’s house, Mark adds the scribes’ question about eating with publicans and sinners. The sources consulted for this page do not settle how the two accounts relate historically.
