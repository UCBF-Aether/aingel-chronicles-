If the convert does this, he may draw near to the sanctuary of God after his faith has been examined. The verse then turns, in broken wording, to the sacrifice of the believers and the holy one who appeared to Peter in bright raiment at Joppa. Investigation (editorial — investigating a parallel, not concluding)

Disclaimer. This section investigates a question raised by a shared place and shared imagery; it does not conclude that one text depends on the other or that the two scenes are the same event. Interpreted Editorial framing for this section

Parallels

Both passages place Peter at Joppa and use bright raiment. Qalementos 31 says the holy one who appeared to Peter in bright raiment came down upon him in the city of Joppa. Acts 10 is set in motion from Joppa, where Peter lodged, and describes a man standing in bright clothing. Documented Qalementos 31 (corpus qal.json); Acts 10:5-9, 30 (corpus acts.json)

Differences

The recipients differ. In Acts 10 the man in bright clothing appears to Cornelius at Caesarea, telling him to send to Joppa for Peter, while Peter's own experience at Joppa is a trance vision of a vessel like a great sheet, not a bright-clad figure appearing to him. In Qalementos 31 the bright-clad holy one appears to Peter himself at Joppa, in a broken passage about approaching the sanctuary, with no Cornelius and no sheet. Documented Acts 10:9-16, 30-32 (corpus acts.json); Qalementos 31 (corpus qal.json)
