The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Genesis 23 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And Sarah was an hundred and seven and twenty years old: these were the years of the life of Sarah.

2 And Sarah died in Kirjatharba; the same is Hebron in the land of Canaan: and Abraham came to mourn for Sarah, and to weep for her.

3 And Abraham stood up from before his dead, and spake unto the sons of Heth, saying,

4 I am a stranger and a sojourner with you: give me a possession of a buryingplace with you, that I may bury my dead out of my sight.

5 And the children of Heth answered Abraham, saying unto him,

6 Hear us, my lord: thou art a mighty prince among us: in the choice of our sepulchres bury thy dead; none of us shall withhold from thee his sepulchre, but that thou mayest bury thy dead.

7 And Abraham stood up, and bowed himself to the people of the land, even to the children of Heth.

8 And he communed with them, saying, If it be your mind that I should bury my dead out of my sight; hear me, and intreat for me to Ephron the son of Zohar,

9 That he may give me the cave of Machpelah, which he hath, which is in the end of his field; for as much money as it is worth he shall give it me for a possession of a buryingplace amongst you.

10 And Ephron dwelt among the children of Heth: and Ephron the Hittite answered Abraham in the audience of the children of Heth, even of all that went in at the gate of his city, saying,

11 Nay, my lord, hear me: the field give I thee, and the cave that is therein, I give it thee; in the presence of the sons of my people give I it thee: bury thy dead.

12 And Abraham bowed down himself before the people of the land.

13 And he spake unto Ephron in the audience of the people of the land, saying, But if thou wilt give it, I pray thee, hear me: I will give thee money for the field; take it of me, and I will bury my dead there.

14 And Ephron answered Abraham, saying unto him,

15 My lord, hearken unto me: the land is worth four hundred shekels of silver; what is that betwixt me and thee? bury therefore thy dead.

16 And Abraham hearkened unto Ephron; and Abraham weighed to Ephron the silver, which he had named in the audience of the sons of Heth, four hundred shekels of silver, current money with the merchant.

17 And the field of Ephron which was in Machpelah, which was before Mamre, the field, and the cave which was therein, and all the trees that were in the field, that were in all the borders round about, were made sure

18 Unto Abraham for a possession in the presence of the children of Heth, before all that went in at the gate of his city.

19 And after this, Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Machpelah before Mamre: the same is Hebron in the land of Canaan.

20 And the field, and the cave that is therein, were made sure unto Abraham for a possession of a buryingplace by the sons of Heth.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Sarah dies at a hundred and twenty-seven, and Abraham mourns her. He buys the field and cave of Machpelah from Ephron the Hittite, before witnesses and at full price, as a burial possession, the first owned ground of the promised land, and buries Sarah there. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Sarah lived 127 years; JFB notes she is the only woman in Scripture whose age, death and burial are all recorded, an honour to the mother of the Hebrew people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Sarah died at Kiriath-arba, that is Hebron, and Abraham came to mourn and weep for her, first sitting in customary mourning and then giving way to natural grief. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Abraham rose from before his dead and spoke to the sons of Heth, seeking their help to obtain a burial place, since his pilgrim life had left him owning no grave plot. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Abraham described himself as a stranger and sojourner among them, asking for a burial possession so he could bury his dead out of his sight. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The children of Heth answered Abraham courteously, ready to hear his request. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. They called Abraham a mighty prince among them and offered him the choice of their tombs, saying none would withhold his sepulchre, a generous public welcome. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Abraham stood and bowed to the people of the land, showing respectful courtesy in return before stating his particular request. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Abraham asked them to intercede with Ephron son of Zohar on his behalf, so that the negotiation would come through a respected intermediary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Abraham asked specifically for the cave of Machpelah at the end of Ephron's field, offering to pay its full worth for a permanent burial possession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Ephron was sitting among the Hittites at the city gate, where all business was transacted, and answered Abraham in the hearing of all who entered the gate. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Ephron offered Abraham both field and cave as a gift before witnesses, a show of Eastern generosity that, JFB warns, still expected a costly return gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Abraham bowed again before the people of the land, acknowledging the offer with the same public courtesy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Abraham insisted on paying money for the field, asking Ephron to accept payment so that his burial of Sarah would rest on clear ownership, not on favour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Ephron answered Abraham, beginning to name terms after the offer of a gift had been politely set aside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Ephron named the land at four hundred shekels of silver, adding that such a sum was a trifle between them, a polite form that still stated a real price. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Abraham agreed to Ephron's price and weighed out four hundred shekels of silver, current merchant money, in the hearing of the sons of Heth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The field at Machpelah before Mamre, with its cave and all its trees within its borders, was thus made over to Abraham in a formal, witnessed transfer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The purchase was confirmed to Abraham as a possession before all who went in at the gate of Ephron's city, so the title was public and beyond dispute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Abraham then buried Sarah in the cave of Machpelah before Mamre, that is Hebron, the only spot of ground he owned in the promised land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Field and cave were made sure to Abraham by the sons of Heth as a burial possession, securing a family grave for generations to come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
