The Text — Baruch 5 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 Put off, O Jerusalem, the garment of mourning and affliction, and put on the comeliness of the glory that cometh from God for ever.

2 Cast about thee a double garment of the righteousness which cometh from God; and set a diadem on thine head of the glory of the Everlasting.

3 For God will shew thy brightness unto every country under heaven.

4 For thy name shall be called of God for ever The peace of righteousness, and The glory of God's worship.

5 Arise, O Jerusalem, and stand on high, and look about toward the east, and behold thy children gathered from the west unto the east by the word of the Holy One, rejoicing in the remembrance of God.

6 For they departed from thee on foot, and were led away of their enemies: but God bringeth them unto thee exalted with glory, as children of the kingdom.

7 For God hath appointed that every high hill, and banks of long continuance, should be cast down, and valleys filled up, to make even the ground, that Israel may go safely in the glory of God,

8 Moreover even the woods and every sweetsmelling tree shall overshadow Israel by the commandment of God.

9 For God shall lead Israel with joy in the light of his glory with the mercy and righteousness that cometh from him. Go back to the Bible

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Jerusalem is told to put off the garment of mourning and affliction and put on the comeliness of God's glory for ever, with a double garment of righteousness and a diadem of his glory. Her sons, sent away on foot by their enemies, will be brought back carried in glory, and God will lead Israel with joy in the light of his glory, with mercy and righteousness. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. The garment is in the Hebrew a cloak, such as were worn by people under no disgrace, as Thamar's costly robe, and justice or mercy means God is bound to fulfil his gracious promises, so the double garment is righteousness that comes from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. His is in the Greek thy, and Christ, the sun of justice, would effect this showing of brightness to every one under heaven, so the glory shown in Jerusalem is the brightness of the Sun of justice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. Piety is the distinction Jerusalem shall bear after the captivity, but still more when Christ shall appear and give peace to his Church after the ages of persecution, so the name, peace of justice and honour of piety, looks beyond the return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 5. Arise repeats the earlier call to stand on high, and God means they remember his law with pleasure, so the gathering from east to west is a gathering of those who rejoice in remembrance of God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 6. The children are in the Greek the throne, and the return of the captives was a triumph, as Isaias describes, with abundance of horses, and Darius giving a convoy of a thousand horsemen, so the exalted return is a royal procession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. Diligently means without stumbling under the pillar of God's protection, as the way is made straight in Isaias, so the levelled mountains and filled valleys make a road on which Israel walks without stumbling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. The woods are a poetical description, for the returning people shall pass through the sands of Arabia as if under delightful shade, since the trees overshadow Israel by God's commandment. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
