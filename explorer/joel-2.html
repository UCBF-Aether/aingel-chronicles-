The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Joel 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Blow ye the trumpet in Zion, and sound an alarm in my holy mountain: let all the inhabitants of the land tremble: for the day of the LORD cometh, for it is nigh at hand;

2 A day of darkness and of gloominess, a day of clouds and of thick darkness, as the morning spread upon the mountains: a great people and a strong; there hath not been ever the like, neither shall be any more after it, even to the years of many generations.

3 A fire devoureth before them; and behind them a flame burneth: the land is as the garden of Eden before them, and behind them a desolate wilderness; yea, and nothing shall escape them.

4 The appearance of them is as the appearance of horses; and as horsemen, so shall they run.

5 Like the noise of chariots on the tops of mountains shall they leap, like the noise of a flame of fire that devoureth the stubble, as a strong people set in battle array.

6 Before their face the people shall be much pained: all faces shall gather blackness.

7 They shall run like mighty men; they shall climb the wall like men of war; and they shall march every one on his ways, and they shall not break their ranks:

8 Neither shall one thrust another; they shall walk every one in his path: and when they fall upon the sword, they shall not be wounded.

9 They shall run to and fro in the city; they shall run upon the wall, they shall climb up upon the houses; they shall enter in at the windows like a thief.

10 The earth shall quake before them; the heavens shall tremble: the sun and the moon shall be dark, and the stars shall withdraw their shining:

11 And the LORD shall utter his voice before his army: for his camp is very great: for he is strong that executeth his word: for the day of the LORD is great and very terrible; and who can abide it?

12 Therefore also now, saith the LORD, turn ye even to me with all your heart, and with fasting, and with weeping, and with mourning:

13 And rend your heart, and not your garments, and turn unto the LORD your God: for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repenteth him of the evil.

14 Who knoweth if he will return and repent, and leave a blessing behind him; even a meat offering and a drink offering unto the LORD your God?

15 Blow the trumpet in Zion, sanctify a fast, call a solemn assembly:

16 Gather the people, sanctify the congregation, assemble the elders, gather the children, and those that suck the breasts: let the bridegroom go forth of his chamber, and the bride out of her closet.

17 Let the priests, the ministers of the LORD, weep between the porch and the altar, and let them say, Spare thy people, O LORD, and give not thine heritage to reproach, that the heathen should rule over them: wherefore should they say among the people, Where is their God?

18 Then will the LORD be jealous for his land, and pity his people.

19 Yea, the LORD will answer and say unto his people, Behold, I will send you corn, and wine, and oil, and ye shall be satisfied therewith: and I will no more make you a reproach among the heathen:

20 But I will remove far off from you the northern army, and will drive him into a land barren and desolate, with his face toward the east sea, and his hinder part toward the utmost sea, and his stink shall come up, and his ill savour shall come up, because he hath done great things.

21 Fear not, O land; be glad and rejoice: for the LORD will do great things.

22 Be not afraid, ye beasts of the field: for the pastures of the wilderness do spring, for the tree beareth her fruit, the fig tree and the vine do yield their strength.

23 Be glad then, ye children of Zion, and rejoice in the LORD your God: for he hath given you the former rain moderately, and he will cause to come down for you the rain, the former rain, and the latter rain in the first month.

24 And the floors shall be full of wheat, and the vats shall overflow with wine and oil.

25 And I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten, the cankerworm, and the caterpiller, and the palmerworm, my great army which I sent among you.

26 And ye shall eat in plenty, and be satisfied, and praise the name of the LORD your God, that hath dealt wondrously with you: and my people shall never be ashamed.

27 And ye shall know that I am in the midst of Israel, and that I am the LORD your God, and none else: and my people shall never be ashamed.

28 And it shall come to pass afterward, that I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh; and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, your young men shall see visions:

29 And also upon the servants and upon the handmaids in those days will I pour out my spirit.

30 And I will shew wonders in the heavens and in the earth, blood, and fire, and pillars of smoke.

31 The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the great and terrible day of the LORD come.

32 And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the LORD shall be delivered: for in mount Zion and in Jerusalem shall be deliverance, as the LORD hath said, and in the remnant whom the LORD shall call.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Blow the trumpet in Zion, for the day of the LORD comes as a day of darkness and gloominess, with a great and strong people like horses and chariots running over walls and entering windows like a thief, before whom the earth quakes and the sun and moon go dark. Yet the turn of the chapter is repentance: rend your heart and not your garments, for God is gracious and merciful, slow to anger and of great kindness. Then the LORD pities his people, restores the years the locust has eaten, and promises to pour out his Spirit upon all flesh before the great and terrible day of the LORD, when whosoever calls on the name of the LORD will be delivered. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The trumpet must be blown in Zion and an alarm sounded in the holy mountain, and Henry notes it is the work of ministers to warn of the fatal consequences of sin and reveal the wrath from heaven against ungodliness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The day of the Lord is a day of darkness and gloominess, with a great and strong people like none before or after, and Henry reads the description as both the locust devastation and the ravaging of the land by the Chaldeans. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. A fire devours before them and a flame burns behind them, the land as Eden before them and a desolate wilderness behind, with nothing escaping, so that the army leaves ruin in both directions. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 5. Like the noise of chariots on the mountains and the crackling of flame devouring stubble, they advance as a strong people set in battle array, sound and fire joined in one image. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Before their face the people are much pained and all faces gather blackness, the fear of the invader written on the faces of the invaded. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They run like mighty men and climb the wall like men of war, each marching in his ways without breaking ranks, an army whose discipline makes it more dreadful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. None thrusts another, each walks in his path, and falling on the sword they are not wounded, a host that cannot be disordered or stopped by ordinary resistance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. They run to and fro in the city, run upon the wall, climb into houses, and enter windows like a thief, so that no wall, house, or shut door keeps them out. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. The earth quakes before them, the heavens tremble, the sun and moon are dark, and the stars withdraw their shining, creation itself darkening at the army's advance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. The Lord utters his voice before his army, for his camp is very great and strong to execute his word, and the day of the Lord is great and very terrible, and Henry asks with the prophet who can abide it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 24. The floors will be full of wheat and the fats overflow with wine and oil, a picture of plenty that some understand figuratively of gospel grace and the abundant comforts treasured up in the covenant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. God will restore the years the locust, cankerworm, caterpillar, and palmerworm have eaten, his great army which he sent, so that time itself, devoured by judgment, is given back. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. The people will eat in plenty and be satisfied and praise the name of the Lord who has dealt wondrously with them, and his people shall never be ashamed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

  • Verse 28. Afterward God will pour out his Spirit upon all flesh, sons and daughters prophesying, old men dreaming dreams, and young men seeing visions, and Henry notes this promise began to be fulfilled on the day of Pentecost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. Upon servants and handmaids also God will pour out his Spirit in those days, so that the gift falls without regard to rank or station. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. Wonders will appear in heaven and earth, blood, fire, and pillars of smoke, signs that accompany the great movements of God's judgment and grace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. The sun will turn to darkness and the moon to blood before the great and terrible day of the Lord, and Henry notes that God's judgments on a sinful world only go before the judgment of the world in the last day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. Whosoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be delivered, with deliverance in Mount Zion and Jerusalem and among the remnant whom the Lord calls, and Henry notes that calling on God supposes knowledge, faith, desire, dependence, and conscientious obedience as evidence of sincerity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • The day of the LORD. the day of God's visitation and judgment, pictured in the prophets as darkness, trumpet alarm, and distress, and also as deliverance for those who call on his name. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Palmerworm, locust, cankerworm, caterpillar. four names for successive waves of locusts in Joel 1, each eating what the last left, an image the study note also applies to destruction by a foreign enemy. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
