The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, when the LORD would take up Elijah into heaven by a whirlwind, that Elijah went with Elisha from Gilgal.

2 And Elijah said unto Elisha, Tarry here, I pray thee; for the LORD hath sent me to Bethel. And Elisha said unto him, As the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee. So they went down to Bethel.

3 And the sons of the prophets that were at Bethel came forth to Elisha, and said unto him, Knowest thou that the LORD will take away thy master from thy head to day? And he said, Yea, I know it; hold ye your peace.

4 And Elijah said unto him, Elisha, tarry here, I pray thee; for the LORD hath sent me to Jericho. And he said, As the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee. So they came to Jericho.

5 And the sons of the prophets that were at Jericho came to Elisha, and said unto him, Knowest thou that the LORD will take away thy master from thy head to day? And he answered, Yea, I know it; hold ye your peace.

6 And Elijah said unto him, Tarry, I pray thee, here; for the LORD hath sent me to Jordan. And he said, As the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee. And they two went on.

7 And fifty men of the sons of the prophets went, and stood to view afar off: and they two stood by Jordan.

8 And Elijah took his mantle, and wrapped it together, and smote the waters, and they were divided hither and thither, so that they two went over on dry ground.

9 And it came to pass, when they were gone over, that Elijah said unto Elisha, Ask what I shall do for thee, before I be taken away from thee. And Elisha said, I pray thee, let a double portion of thy spirit be upon me.

10 And he said, Thou hast asked a hard thing: nevertheless, if thou see me when I am taken from thee, it shall be so unto thee; but if not, it shall not be so.

11 And it came to pass, as they still went on, and talked, that, behold, there appeared a chariot of fire, and horses of fire, and parted them both asunder; and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven.

12 And Elisha saw it, and he cried, My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof. And he saw him no more: and he took hold of his own clothes, and rent them in two pieces.

13 He took up also the mantle of Elijah that fell from him, and went back, and stood by the bank of Jordan;

14 And he took the mantle of Elijah that fell from him, and smote the waters, and said, Where is the LORD God of Elijah? and when he also had smitten the waters, they parted hither and thither: and Elisha went over.

15 And when the sons of the prophets which were to view at Jericho saw him, they said, The spirit of Elijah doth rest on Elisha. And they came to meet him, and bowed themselves to the ground before him.

16 And they said unto him, Behold now, there be with thy servants fifty strong men; let them go, we pray thee, and seek thy master: lest peradventure the Spirit of the LORD hath taken him up, and cast him upon some mountain, or into some valley. And he said, Ye shall not send.

17 And when they urged him till he was ashamed, he said, Send. They sent therefore fifty men; and they sought three days, but found him not.

18 And when they came again to him, (for he tarried at Jericho,) he said unto them, Did I not say unto you, Go not?

19 And the men of the city said unto Elisha, Behold, I pray thee, the situation of this city is pleasant, as my lord seeth: but the water is naught, and the ground barren.

20 And he said, Bring me a new cruse, and put salt therein. And they brought it to him.

21 And he went forth unto the spring of the waters, and cast the salt in there, and said, Thus saith the LORD, I have healed these waters; there shall not be from thence any more death or barren land.

22 So the waters were healed unto this day, according to the saying of Elisha which he spake.

23 And he went up from thence unto Bethel: and as he was going up by the way, there came forth little children out of the city, and mocked him, and said unto him, Go up, thou bald head; go up, thou bald head.

24 And he turned back, and looked on them, and cursed them in the name of the LORD. And there came forth two she bears out of the wood, and tare forty and two children of them.

25 And he went from thence to mount Carmel, and from thence he returned to Samaria.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Elijah and Elisha travel from Gilgal to the Jordan, where Elijah parts the waters with his mantle and is taken up in a whirlwind with chariots of fire; Elisha takes the fallen mantle and parts the waters in return. Elisha heals Jericho's waters, and on the road to Bethel mocking youths are mauled by bears. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When the LORD would take up Elijah into heaven by a whirlwind, Elijah went with Elisha from Gilgal. Henry notes the LORD had let Elijah know his time was at hand, so he went to the schools of the prophets to give his last exhortations and blessing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Elijah told Elisha to tarry at Gilgal, but Elisha swore by the LORD and by Elijah's life that he would not leave him. Henry urges followers of Christ not to come short by tiring at last, as Elisha refused to leave when the parting blessing was near. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. The sons of the prophets at Bethel told Elisha the LORD would take away his master that day, and he answered that he knew it and they should hold their peace. Henry frames this journey as Elijah's farewell to the prophetic schools. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. Elijah again told Elisha to tarry, this time at Bethel, for the LORD had sent him to Jericho, but Elisha again refused to leave him. Henry presents Elisha's steadfast attendance as the proper means of obtaining much of his master's spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The sons of the prophets at Jericho likewise told Elisha his master would be taken away that day, and he again answered that he knew it and they should hold their peace. Henry notes the removal of Elijah was a type of Christ's ascension and the opening of heaven to believers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Elijah told Elisha to tarry at Jericho, for the LORD had sent him to Jordan, but Elisha swore once more he would not leave him, so they both went on. Henry's lesson is perseverance to the end of the journey. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Fifty men of the sons of the prophets stood to view afar off while Elijah and Elisha stood by Jordan. Henry notes these witnesses watched the parting that would confirm Elisha's succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Elijah wrapped his mantle together, smote the waters, and they divided so that the two went over on dry ground. Henry observes the waters of Jordan, which once yielded to the ark, now yielded to the prophet's mantle as a token of God's presence, and death itself is the Jordan the faithful must pass through. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Once over, Elijah asked Elisha what he should do for him before being taken away, and Elisha asked for a double portion of his spirit. Henry notes that fulness from which prophets and apostles had all their supply still exists, and we are told to ask large supplies from it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Elijah said Elisha had asked a hard thing, but if he saw him when taken, it would be so, and if not, not. Henry treats diligent attendance on Elijah, particularly in his last hours, as the proper means for Elisha to obtain much of his spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. As they went on talking, a chariot of fire and horses of fire appeared and parted them, and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven. Henry notes the chariot appeared like fire — glorious, not for burning but brightness — and Elijah was found engaged in serious discourse about God's kingdom when his Lord came for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Elisha cried, "My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof," then rent his clothes in two pieces. Henry observes Elijah was to Israel, by his counsels, reproofs, and prayers, better than the strongest force of chariot and horse, and the world could ill spare him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Elisha took up Elijah's mantle that had fallen from him and went back to stand by the bank of Jordan. Henry says Elijah left his mantle as a token of the Spirit's descent, more than thousands of gold and silver, and Christ likewise bequeathed his gospel to his disciples. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Elisha smote the waters with the mantle, asking, "Where is the LORD God of Elijah?" and the waters divided, so he went over. Henry notes Elisha inquired after God when creature-comforts were removed, and asks what it avails us to have the mantles of the departed if we have not their spirit and their God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. The sons of the prophets at Jericho saw him and said the spirit of Elijah rested on Elisha, then bowed to the ground before him. Henry presents the divided river as proof God's people need not fear passing through the Jordan of death as on dry ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. They offered fifty strong men to go seek Elijah, lest the Spirit had cast him on some mountain or valley. Henry calls their proposed search needless: traversing hills and valleys will never bring us to Elijah, but following his holy faith and zeal will in due time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. When they urged Elisha till he was ashamed, he said to send, and fifty men sought three days but found Elijah not. Henry notes wise men may yield for peace's sake to what their judgment counts needless and fruitless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. When they returned to Elisha at Jericho, he said, "Did I not say unto you, Go not?" Henry's point stands: the search confirmed what Elisha already knew, that Elijah had been taken to heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. The men of Jericho told Elisha the city's situation was pleasant but its water was naught and the ground barren. Henry observes prophets should make every place they come to better for them, sweetening bitter spirits and making barren souls fruitful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Elisha asked for a new cruse with salt in it, and they brought it to him. Henry treats the salt cast into the water as an apt emblem of grace's effect on the sinful heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Elisha cast the salt into the spring, saying the LORD had healed the waters so there would be no more death or barren land from there. Henry notes the word of God, like that salt, sweetens bitter spirits and makes barren souls fruitful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. The waters were healed to this day according to Elisha's word. Henry adds that whole families, towns, and cities sometimes take a new appearance through the preaching of the gospel, wickedness changed into fruitfulness in works of righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Elisha went up to Bethel, and little children came out of the city mocking him, saying, "Go up, thou bald head." Henry notes they bade him "go up" in reflection on Elijah's taking up into heaven, abusing Elisha's character as God's prophet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Elisha turned, looked on them, and cursed them in the name of the LORD, and two she bears came out of the wood and tare forty-two of them. Henry stresses the curse was not causeless and Elisha acted by divine impulse, for the LORD must be glorified as a righteous God who hates sin and will reckon for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. Elisha went from there to Mount Carmel, and from there returned to Samaria. Henry closes with the warning to young persons not to mock any for defects, especially not to scoff at any for well-doing, and to parents to train children betimes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
