The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Esther 4 (King James Version (1611) for chs 1–10; King James Version, Apocrypha (the Rest of Esther) for the Greek additions)

1 When Mordecai perceived all that was done, Mordecai rent his clothes, and put on sackcloth with ashes, and went out into the midst of the city, and cried with a loud and a bitter cry;

2 And came even before the king’s gate: for none might enter into the king’s gate clothed with sackcloth.

3 And in every province, whithersoever the king’s commandment and his decree came, there was great mourning among the Jews, and fasting, and weeping, and wailing; and many lay in sackcloth and ashes.

4 So Esther’s maids and her chamberlains came and told it her. Then was the queen exceedingly grieved; and she sent raiment to clothe Mordecai, and to take away his sackcloth from him: but he received it not.

5 Then called Esther for Hatach, one of the king’s chamberlains, whom he had appointed to attend upon her, and gave him a commandment to Mordecai, to know what it was, and why it was.

6 So Hatach went forth to Mordecai unto the street of the city, which was before the king’s gate.

7 And Mordecai told him of all that had happened unto him, and of the sum of the money that Haman had promised to pay to the king’s treasuries for the Jews, to destroy them.

8 Also he gave him the copy of the writing of the decree that was given at Shushan to destroy them, to shew it unto Esther, and to declare it unto her, and to charge her that she should go in unto the king, to make supplication unto him, and to make request before him for her people.

9 And Hatach came and told Esther the words of Mordecai.

10 Again Esther spake unto Hatach, and gave him commandment unto Mordecai;

11 All the king’s servants, and the people of the king’s provinces, do know, that whosoever, whether man or woman, shall come unto the king into the inner court, who is not called, there is one law of his to put him to death, except such to whom the king shall hold out the golden sceptre, that he may live: but I have not been called to come in unto the king these thirty days.

12 And they told to Mordecai Esther’s words.

13 Then Mordecai commanded to answer Esther, Think not with thyself that thou shalt escape in the king’s house, more than all the Jews.

14 For if thou altogether holdest thy peace at this time, then shall there enlargement and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place; but thou and thy father’s house shall be destroyed: and who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?

15 Then Esther bade them return Mordecai this answer,

16 Go, gather together all the Jews that are present in Shushan, and fast ye for me, and neither eat nor drink three days, night or day: I also and my maidens will fast likewise; and so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law: and if I perish, I perish.

17 So Mordecai went his way, and did according to all that Esther had commanded him.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

When the decree reaches them, Mordecai and Jews throughout the provinces mourn in sackcloth and ashes, and Esther, learning of it through Hatach, first pleads the danger of approaching the king uncalled, since any who enter the inner court uncalled face death unless the king holds out the golden sceptre. Mordecai answers that silence will not save her in the king's house, and that she may have come to the kingdom for such a time as this; Esther then calls for three days of fasting in Shushan and resolves to go to the king: and if I perish, I perish. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. When Mordecai perceived all that was done, Mordecai rent his clothes, and put on sackcloth with ashes, and went out into the midst of the city, and cried with a loud and bitter cry. Henry notes Mordecai mourns in public, not in private, so that the city hears what the decree means. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. And came even before the king's gate: for none might enter into the king's gate clothed with sackcloth. JFB notes the limit of grief: sackcloth may cry in the street, but it may not enter the gate, so Mordecai stops where Esther can hear of him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And in every province, whithersoever the king's commandment and decree came, there was great mourning among the Jews, and fasting, and weeping, and wailing; and many lay in sackcloth and ashes. Henry notes the mourning is empire-wide, matching the decree's empire-wide reach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. So Esther's maids and chamberlains came and told it her. Then was the queen exceedingly grieved; and she sent raiment to clothe Mordecai, and to take away his sackcloth from him: but he received it not. JFB notes Esther first tries to comfort Mordecai by removing the sign, but he refuses: the cause of grief must be removed, not its clothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Then called Esther for Hatach, one of the king's chamberlains, whom he had appointed to attend upon her, and gave him a commandment to Mordecai, to know what it was, and why it was. Henry notes Esther, shut in the palace, must learn through a messenger what the whole city already knows. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. So Hatach went forth to Mordecai unto the street of the city, which was before the king's gate. The street before the gate becomes the meeting place of palace and people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. And Mordecai told him of all that had happened unto him, and of the sum of the money that Haman had promised to pay to the king's treasuries for the Jews, to destroy them. JFB notes Mordecai names the price: the Jews are told they have been sold, not merely condemned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Also he gave him the copy of the writing of the decree given at Shushan to destroy them, to show it unto Esther, and to declare it unto her, and to charge her that she should go in unto the king, to make supplication unto him, and to make request before him for her people. Henry notes Mordecai sends not only news but the document, and with it a charge: Esther must go to the king herself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. And Hatach came and told Esther the words of Mordecai. The message is delivered whole, charge and all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Again Esther spake unto Hatach, and gave him commandment unto Mordecai. Esther answers, but her answer is a difficulty, not a refusal. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. All the king's servants, and the people of the provinces, do know, that whosoever, whether man or woman, shall come unto the king into the inner court, who is not called, there is one law of his to put him to death, except such to whom the king shall hold out the golden sceptre, that he may live: but I have not been called to come in unto the king these thirty days. JFB notes the law is stated exactly: uncalled entry means death unless the sceptre is held out, and thirty days without summons makes the risk real. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And they told to Mordecai Esther's words. The difficulty is carried back faithfully, without softening. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Then Mordecai commanded to answer Esther, Think not with thyself that thou shalt escape in the king's house, more than all the Jews. Henry notes the warning strips away palace safety: the decree names all Jews, and a queen's chamber is no refuge from it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. For if thou altogether holdest thy peace at this time, then shall there enlargement and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place; but thou and thy father's house shall be destroyed: and who knows whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this? JFB notes Mordecai's faith: deliverance does not depend on Esther, but her place in it may be the very reason she is queen. Henry calls this the hinge of the book: providence appoints the person to the hour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Then Esther bade them return Mordecai this answer. Esther's answer comes after Mordecai's challenge, not before it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Go, gather together all the Jews that are present in Shushan, and fast ye for me, and neither eat nor drink three days, night or day: I also and my maidens will fast likewise; and so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law: and if I perish, I perish. Henry notes the resolution joins fasting and action: the Jews fast, Esther goes, and the risk is accepted in the same breath as the resolve. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. So Mordecai went his way, and did according to all that Esther had commanded him. JFB notes the reversal is complete: Mordecai, who charged Esther, now obeys Esther's command, and the fast begins. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Sackcloth and ashes. The outward signs of mourning in this chapter: Mordecai rents his clothes, puts on sackcloth with ashes, and cries in the midst of the city, while Jews in every province fast, weep and wail. Documented Chapter text, verses 1-3
  • Golden sceptre. The king's token of favour and life: whoever enters the inner court uncalled dies, except the one to whom the king holds out the golden sceptre. Documented Chapter text, verse 11
