The Text — Baruch 1 (King James Version, Apocrypha; public-domain text as received)

1 And these are the words of the book, which Baruch the son of Nerias, the son of Maasias, the son of Sedecias, the son of Asadias, the son of Chelcias, wrote in Babylon,

2 In the fifth year, and in the seventh day of the month, what time as the Chaldeans took Jerusalem, and burnt it with fire.

3 And Baruch did read the words of this book in the hearing of Jechonias the son of Joachim king of Juda, and in the ears of all the people that came to hear the book,

4 And in the hearing of the nobles, and of the king's sons, and in the hearing of the elders, and of all the people, from the lowest unto the highest, even of all them that dwelt at Babylon by the river Sud.

5 Whereupon they wept, fasted, and prayed before the Lord.

6 They made also a collection of money according to every man's power:

7 And they sent it to Jerusalem unto Joachim the high priest, the son of Chelcias, son of Salom, and to the priests, and to all the people which were found with him at Jerusalem,

8 At the same time when he received the vessels of the house of the Lord, that were carried out of the temple, to return them into the land of Juda, the tenth day of the month Sivan, namely, silver vessels, which Sedecias the son of Josias king of Jada had made,

9 After that Nabuchodonosor king of Babylon had carried away Jechonias, and the princes, and the captives, and the mighty men, and the people of the land, from Jerusalem, and brought them unto Babylon.

10 And they said, Behold, we have sent you money to buy you burnt offerings, and sin offerings, and incense, and prepare ye manna, and offer upon the altar of the Lord our God;

11 And pray for the life of Nabuchodonosor king of Babylon, and for the life of Balthasar his son, that their days may be upon earth as the days of heaven:

12 And the Lord will give us strength, and lighten our eyes, and we shall live under the shadow of Nabuchodonosor king of Babylon, and under the shadow of Balthasar his son, and we shall serve them many days, and find favour in their sight.

13 Pray for us also unto the Lord our God, for we have sinned against the Lord our God; and unto this day the fury of the Lord and his wrath is not turned from us.

14 And ye shall read this book which we have sent unto you, to make confession in the house of the Lord, upon the feasts and solemn days.

15 And ye shall say, To the Lord our God belongeth righteousness, but unto us the confusion of faces, as it is come to pass this day, unto them of Juda, and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem,

16 And to our kings, and to our princes, and to our priests, and to our prophets, and to our fathers:

17 For we have sinned before the Lord,

18 And disobeyed him, and have not hearkened unto the voice of the Lord our God, to walk in the commandments that he gave us openly:

19 Since the day that the Lord brought our forefathers out of the land of Egypt, unto this present day, we have been disobedient unto the Lord our God, and we have been negligent in not hearing his voice.

20 Wherefore the evils cleaved unto us, and the curse, which the Lord appointed by Moses his servant at the time that he brought our fathers out of the land of Egypt, to give us a land that floweth with milk and honey, like as it is to see this day.

21 Nevertheless we have not hearkened unto the voice of the Lord our God, according unto all the words of the prophets, whom he sent unto us:

22 But every man followed the imagination of his own wicked heart, to serve strange gods, and to do evil in the sight of the Lord our God.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Baruch's book, written in Babylon in the fifth year after Jerusalem was taken and burned, is read before Jechonias and the exiles, who weep, fast and pray, and send money to Jerusalem for offerings. The chapter then sends with the money a confession to be read there: the people have sinned, and every man has followed the imagination of his own wicked heart to serve strange gods. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The book was written in Babylon, where Jechonias was in prison, in the fifth year, and Haydock notes that Baruch accompanied his brother Saraias, so the letter comes from the exiles themselves, not from distant onlookers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 2. The fifth year is counted from the taking of the king, and the taking of the city was effected at intervals over eleven years, in the fifth of which this book was written, while priests and a temple still stood at Jerusalem. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 3. Jechonias was in prison, where the Jews met to hear the words of Jeremias, or the king might hear the letter in private, so the reading reached both the captive king and the people gathered to hear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 4. The river Sodi is read as the great river Euphrates, the Hebrew word denoting pride, with other readings naming the city Sura there, so the exiles are placed by the river of Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 7. Joakim, or Eliacim, is the high priest named here, and Haydock notes the dispute whether there was a pontiff then, since the text only says that he was priest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 8. Sivan is a Syriac month corresponding to May, and the vessels are silver vessels, which Sedecias had made to supply the place of the golden vessels taken away, so the return of vessels is a partial restoration, not the full treasure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 9. The Greek reads prisoners where the Latin reads all, so the carrying away is of bound prisoners, princes and mighty men and people of the land, brought bound to Babylon. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 10. The meat offering is literally manna in the Septuagint rendering, or mincha, an oblation of bread, wine and the like, and the Greek adds for sin before frankincense, so the money is sent to buy the full range of offerings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 11. Prayer for the king is prompted by gratitude and duty, as Jeremias commanded prayer for Babylon, and Baltassar is named as the king's son, with the prayer that their days may be as the days of heaven, as long and prosperous as possible, in the customary salutation, O king, live for ever. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 12. Enlightened eyes mean either an end to the captivity or a hatred of sin, and the days are those of God's decree, to which the exiles submit, preferring service under the Chaldeans to service under other foreign nations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 14. The proper days are the festival days, from the Hebrew word for appointed times, which were still observed in the temple, so the book is to be read publicly at the appointed feasts. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 20. The curses against transgressors are those of Leviticus and Daniel, and the day means that the people already feel the effects of sin, though the land of Juda was not yet laid waste, so the confession is made while ruin is still incomplete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
  • Verse 22. To serve is here to serve or sacrifice unto God, or to worship, as in the second chapter, so going after strange gods is described as service given to the wrong master. Interpreted Study note, summarizing George Leo Haydock
