The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 25 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 These are also proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out.

2 It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter.

3 The heaven for height, and the earth for depth, and the heart of kings is unsearchable.

4 Take away the dross from the silver, and there shall come forth a vessel for the finer.

5 Take away the wicked from before the king, and his throne shall be established in righteousness.

6 Put not forth thyself in the presence of the king, and stand not in the place of great men:

7 For better it is that it be said unto thee, Come up hither; than that thou shouldest be put lower in the presence of the prince whom thine eyes have seen.

8 Go not forth hastily to strive, lest thou know not what to do in the end thereof, when thy neighbour hath put thee to shame.

9 Debate thy cause with thy neighbour himself; and discover not a secret to another:

10 Lest he that heareth it put thee to shame, and thine infamy turn not away.

11 A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver.

12 As an earring of gold, and an ornament of fine gold, so is a wise reprover upon an obedient ear.

13 As the cold of snow in the time of harvest, so is a faithful messenger to them that send him: for he refresheth the soul of his masters.

14 Whoso boasteth himself of a false gift is like clouds and wind without rain.

15 By long forbearing is a prince persuaded, and a soft tongue breaketh the bone.

16 Hast thou found honey? eat so much as is sufficient for thee, lest thou be filled therewith, and vomit it.

17 Withdraw thy foot from thy neighbour’s house; lest he be weary of thee, and so hate thee.

18 A man that beareth false witness against his neighbour is a maul, and a sword, and a sharp arrow.

19 Confidence in an unfaithful man in time of trouble is like a broken tooth, and a foot out of joint.

20 As he that taketh away a garment in cold weather, and as vinegar upon nitre, so is he that singeth songs to an heavy heart.

21 If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat; and if he be thirsty, give him water to drink:

22 For thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head, and the LORD shall reward thee.

23 The north wind driveth away rain: so doth an angry countenance a backbiting tongue.

24 It is better to dwell in the corner of the housetop, than with a brawling woman and in a wide house.

25 As cold waters to a thirsty soul, so is good news from a far country.

26 A righteous man falling down before the wicked is as a troubled fountain, and a corrupt spring.

27 It is not good to eat much honey: so for men to search their own glory is not glory.

28 He that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down, and without walls.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A second Solomon collection, copied out by Hezekiah's men, opens on kings and glory: God's glory to conceal a thing, a king's honour to search it out, and a throne established when the wicked are removed like dross from silver. Its similes run through court conduct, fit speech like apples of gold in silver pictures, a faithful messenger like snow in harvest, feeding a hungry enemy, and self-rule like a city without walls. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

  • Verse 4. Take away the dross from the silver, and there shall come forth a vessel for the finer, for a prince suppressing vice and reforming his people best supports his government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Take away the wicked from before the king, and his throne shall be established in righteousness, for a prince suppressing vice and reforming his people best supports his government. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Put not forth thyself in the presence of the king, and stand not in the place of great men, for religion teaches humility and self-denial, and he who has seen the Lord's glory in Christ will feel his own unworthiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. For better it is that it be said unto thee, Come up hither, than that thou shouldest be put lower in the prince's presence, for religion teaches humility and self-denial before the Lord's glory in Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. Go not forth hastily to strive, lest thou know not what to do in the end, when thy neighbour has put thee to shame, for hasty strife brings difficulties, and war must at length end and might better be prevented. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Debate thy cause with thy neighbour himself, and discover not a secret to another, for hasty strife brings difficulties, and in private quarrels we should do all we can to settle the matter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Lest he who hears it put thee to shame, and thine infamy turn not away, for hasty strife brings difficulties, and private quarrels should be settled rather than spread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver, for a word of counsel or reproof rightly spoken is especially beautiful, as fine fruit is still more beautiful in silver baskets. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. As an earring of gold, and an ornament of fine gold, so is a wise reprover upon an obedient ear, for a word of counsel or reproof rightly spoken is especially beautiful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. As the cold of snow in harvest time, so is a faithful messenger to them that send him, for he refreshes his masters' souls, and faithfulness is the aim of one trusted with business, as a faithful minister, Christ's messenger, should be acceptable to us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Whoso boasts himself of a false gift is like clouds and wind without rain, for he who pretends to have received or given what he never had is like a morning cloud disappointing those who look for rain. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. By long forbearing is a prince persuaded, and a soft tongue breaks the bone, for be patient to bear a present hurt and mild to speak without passion, since persuasive language most effectually prevails over the hardened mind. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Hast thou found honey, eat so much as is sufficient, lest thou be filled and vomit it, for God has given leave to use grateful things, but we are cautioned against excess. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Withdraw thy foot from thy neighbour's house, lest he be weary of thee and hate thee, for we cannot be on good terms with neighbours without discretion as well as sincerity, while God, a far better Friend, welcomes us the oftener we come. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. A man bearing false witness against his neighbour is a maul, a sword, and a sharp arrow, for a false testimony is dangerous in everything. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Confidence in an unfaithful man in time of trouble is like a broken tooth and a foot out of joint, for such confidence is painful and vexatious: he not only fails but makes us feel for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. As he who takes away a garment in cold weather, and as vinegar upon nitre, so is he who sings songs to a heavy heart, for we take a wrong course if we think to relieve sorrow by endeavouring to make the sorrowful merry. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat, and if thirsty, give him water to drink, for the precept to love even enemies is an Old Testament commandment, shown by our Saviour loving us when we were enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. For thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head, and the LORD shall reward thee, for loving enemies is an Old Testament commandment, exemplified by Christ loving us when enemies. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. The north wind drives away rain, so does an angry countenance a backbiting tongue, for slanders would not be so readily spoken if they were not readily heard, and sin, if checked, becomes cowardly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. It is better to dwell in the corner of the housetop than with a brawling woman in a wide house, for it is better to be alone than joined to one who hinders life's comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. As cold waters to a thirsty soul, so is good news from a far country, for heaven is a country afar off, and how refreshing is good news from thence in the everlasting gospel and the Spirit's witness that we are God's children. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. A righteous man falling down before the wicked is as a troubled fountain and a corrupt spring, for when the righteous are led into sin, it is as hurtful as if public fountains were poisoned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. It is not good to eat much honey, so for men to search their own glory is not glory, for we must be, through grace, dead to the pleasures of sense and also to the praises of men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. He who has no rule over his own spirit is like a city broken down and without walls, for the man with no command over his anger is easily robbed of peace, and we must give ourselves to the Lord, praying his Spirit within us to cause us to walk in his statutes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
