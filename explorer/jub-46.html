Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Jubilees 46 (R. H. Charles, The Book of Jubilees — his translation from the Ethiopic (1902; the text as reprinted in the Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament, 1913))

1 And it came to pass that after Jacob died the children of Israel multiplied in the land of Egypt, and they became a great nation, and they were of one accord in heart, so that brother loved brother and every man helped his brother, and they increased abundantly and multiplied exceedingly, ten weeks of years, all the days of the life of Joseph.

2 And there was no Satan nor any evil all the days of the life of Joseph which he lived after his father Jacob, for all the Egyptians honoured the children of Israel all the days of the life of Joseph.

3 And Joseph died being a hundred and ten years old ; seventeen years he lived in the land of Canaan, and ten years he was a servant, and three years in prison, and eighty years he was under the king, ruling all the land

4 of Egypt.

5 And he died and all his brethren and all that generation.

6 And he commanded the children of Israel before he died that they should carry his bones with them when they went forth from the land of Egypt.

7 And he made them swear regarding his bones, for he knew that the Egyptians would not again bring forth and bury him in the land of Canaan for Makamaron, king of Canaan, while dwelling in the land of Assyria, fought in the valley with the king of Egypt and slew him there, and pursued after the Egyptians to the gates of 'Ermon.

8 But he was not able to enter, for another, a new king, had become king of Egypt and he was stronger than he, and he returned to the land of Canaan, and the gates of Egypt were closed, and none went out and none came into Egypt.

9 And Joseph died

10 in the forty-sixth jubilee, in the sixth week, in the second year, and they buried him in the land of Egypt, and all his brethren died after him.

11 And the king of Egypt went forth to war with the king of

12 Canaan in the forty-seventh jubilee, in the second week in the second year, and the children of Israel brought forth all the bones of the children of Jacob save the bones of Joseph, and they buried them in the field in the double cave in the mountain. 10.

13 And the most (of them) returned to Egypt, but a few of them remained in the mountains of Hebron, and

14 Amram thy father remained with them.

15 And the king of Canaan was victorious over the king of Egypt, and he closed the gates of Egypt.

16 And he devised an evil device against the children of Israel of afflicting them ; and he said unto the people of Egypt : " Behold the people of the children of Israel have increased and multiplied more than we.

17 Come and let us deal wisely with them before they become too many, and let us afflict them with slavery before war come upon us and before they too fight against us ; else they will join themselves unto our enemies and get them up out of our land, for their hearts and faces are towards the land of Canaan." 14.

18 And he set over them taskmasters to afflict them with slavery; and they built strong cities for Pharaoh, Pithom and Raamses, and they built all the walls and all the fortifications which had fallen in the cities of Egypt.

19 And they made them serve with rigour, and the more they dealt evilly with them, the more they increased and multiplied.

20 And the people of Egypt abominated the children of Israel.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

In plain language: After Jacob died, the children of Israel multiplied in Egypt, became a great nation, and were of one accord in heart, brother loving brother and every man helping his brother, increasing abundantly and multiplying exceedingly for ten weeks of years, all the days of Joseph's life. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators. Where a note is credited to Matthew Henry or Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, it is on the parallel Genesis passage; see the note on voices in Perspectives.

  • Verse 1. After Jacob died, the children of Israel multiplied in Egypt, became a great nation, and were of one accord in heart, brother loving brother and every man helping his brother, increasing abundantly and multiplying exceedingly for ten weeks of years, all the days of Joseph's life. Henry notes that only about seventy persons went down into Egypt, and there, in about the same number of years, though under cruel bondage, they became a large nation, according to the promise long before made to the fathers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. There was no Satan nor any evil all the days of Joseph's life after his father Jacob, for all the Egyptians honoured the children of Israel all Joseph's days. Henry notes that all who knew Joseph loved him and were kind to his brethren for his sake, though the best services a man does are soon forgotten after his death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Joseph died at one hundred and ten years old: seventeen years he lived in Canaan, ten years he was a servant, three years in prison, and eighty years under the king, ruling all the land of Egypt. The sum of his life is the sum of Henry's Joseph history: humbled in Canaan, service, and prison, then exalted to rule, and Henry calls both his death and his life truly excellent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The account of Joseph's rule is completed: eighty years under the king, ruling all the land of Egypt. The long rule, longer than the slavery and prison together, is the exaltation that followed the humiliation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. Joseph died, and all his brethren and all that generation died. Henry notes that the best and most useful services are soon forgotten after death, and the generation that knew Joseph passes with him, opening the house of bondage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Before he died Joseph commanded the children of Israel to carry his bones with them when they went forth from Egypt. Henry explains the commandment: by faith in the resurrection and the promise of Canaan, Joseph charged them to keep his remains unburied till the day they were settled in the promised land, keeping Canaan continually in their minds. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Joseph made them swear regarding his bones, for he knew the Egyptians would not again bring forth and bury him in Canaan, since Makamaron, king of Canaan, dwelling in Assyria, had fought the king of Egypt in the valley, slain him, and pursued the Egyptians to the gates of Ermon. The oath answers the closed gates: only Israel itself, going forth, could carry Joseph's bones home, and Henry notes the commandment was meant to keep up their expectation of a speedy departure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Joseph died in the forty-sixth jubilee, in the sixth week, in the second year. The date fixes the end of the age of favour, after which the Egyptians who knew Joseph were gone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Joseph died in the forty-sixth jubilee, was buried in Egypt, and all his brethren died after him. Henry notes that Joseph's death, like his life, furnishes strong encouragement to persevere in God's service, and that his bones waited in Egypt for the exodus he had foreseen. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The children of Israel brought forth all the bones of Jacob's children except Joseph's, and buried them in the field in the double cave in the mountain. The patriarchs' bones, carried to Hebron even while Egypt's gates were closing, show the same faith as Joseph's commandment: the dead belong to Canaan, not to Egypt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The king of Canaan devised an evil device against Israel, afflicting them, and said to the Egyptians that the children of Israel had increased and multiplied more than they. Henry notes that the offence of Israel was that he prospered, and that there is no sight more hateful to a wicked man than the prosperity of the righteous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. The Egyptians said to deal wisely with Israel before they became too many, afflicting them with slavery before war came, lest they join their enemies and go up out of the land, for their hearts and faces were toward Canaan. Henry notes that the Egyptians feared Israel would join their enemies and get up out of the land, and that wickedness is ever cowardly, fearing where no fear is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The Egyptians set taskmasters over Israel to afflict them with slavery, and they built strong cities for Pharaoh, Pithom and Raamses, and built all the walls and fortifications that had fallen in Egypt's cities. Henry notes that taskmasters were set not only to burden Israel but to afflict them with their burdens, so that their lives became bitter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The Egyptians made Israel serve with rigour, and the more they dealt evilly with them, the more Israel increased and multiplied. Henry observes that Israel wonderfully increased, and that Christianity spread most when persecuted: the blood of the martyrs was the seed of the church, and those who take counsel against the Lord imagine a vain thing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. The people of Egypt abominated the children of Israel. The abomination is the hatred Henry names as the offence of prosperity: the righteous prospering under affliction is hateful to the wicked, and the hatred only fed the increase. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
