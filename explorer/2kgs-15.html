The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Kings 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 In the twenty and seventh year of Jeroboam king of Israel began Azariah son of Amaziah king of Judah to reign.

2 Sixteen years old was he when he began to reign, and he reigned two and fifty years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Jecholiah of Jerusalem.

3 And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, according to all that his father Amaziah had done;

4 Save that the high places were not removed: the people sacrificed and burnt incense still on the high places.

5 And the LORD smote the king, so that he was a leper unto the day of his death, and dwelt in a several house. And Jotham the king’s son was over the house, judging the people of the land.

6 And the rest of the acts of Azariah, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

7 So Azariah slept with his fathers; and they buried him with his fathers in the city of David: and Jotham his son reigned in his stead.

8 In the thirty and eighth year of Azariah king of Judah did Zachariah the son of Jeroboam reign over Israel in Samaria six months.

9 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, as his fathers had done: he departed not from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin.

10 And Shallum the son of Jabesh conspired against him, and smote him before the people, and slew him, and reigned in his stead.

11 And the rest of the acts of Zachariah, behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.

12 This was the word of the LORD which he spake unto Jehu, saying, Thy sons shall sit on the throne of Israel unto the fourth generation. And so it came to pass.

13 Shallum the son of Jabesh began to reign in the nine and thirtieth year of Uzziah king of Judah; and he reigned a full month in Samaria.

14 For Menahem the son of Gadi went up from Tirzah, and came to Samaria, and smote Shallum the son of Jabesh in Samaria, and slew him, and reigned in his stead.

15 And the rest of the acts of Shallum, and his conspiracy which he made, behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.

16 Then Menahem smote Tiphsah, and all that were therein, and the coasts thereof from Tirzah: because they opened not to him, therefore he smote it; and all the women therein that were with child he ripped up.

17 In the nine and thirtieth year of Azariah king of Judah began Menahem the son of Gadi to reign over Israel, and reigned ten years in Samaria.

18 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD: he departed not all his days from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin.

19 And Pul the king of Assyria came against the land: and Menahem gave Pul a thousand talents of silver, that his hand might be with him to confirm the kingdom in his hand.

20 And Menahem exacted the money of Israel, even of all the mighty men of wealth, of each man fifty shekels of silver, to give to the king of Assyria. So the king of Assyria turned back, and stayed not there in the land.

21 And the rest of the acts of Menahem, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

22 And Menahem slept with his fathers; and Pekahiah his son reigned in his stead.

23 In the fiftieth year of Azariah king of Judah Pekahiah the son of Menahem began to reign over Israel in Samaria, and reigned two years.

24 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD: he departed not from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin.

25 But Pekah the son of Remaliah, a captain of his, conspired against him, and smote him in Samaria, in the palace of the king’s house, with Argob and Arieh, and with him fifty men of the Gileadites: and he killed him, and reigned in his room.

26 And the rest of the acts of Pekahiah, and all that he did, behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.

27 In the two and fiftieth year of Azariah king of Judah Pekah the son of Remaliah began to reign over Israel in Samaria, and reigned twenty years.

28 And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD: he departed not from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin.

29 In the days of Pekah king of Israel came Tiglathpileser king of Assyria, and took Ijon, and Abelbethmaachah, and Janoah, and Kedesh, and Hazor, and Gilead, and Galilee, all the land of Naphtali, and carried them captive to Assyria.

30 And Hoshea the son of Elah made a conspiracy against Pekah the son of Remaliah, and smote him, and slew him, and reigned in his stead, in the twentieth year of Jotham the son of Uzziah.

31 And the rest of the acts of Pekah, and all that he did, behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.

32 In the second year of Pekah the son of Remaliah king of Israel began Jotham the son of Uzziah king of Judah to reign.

33 Five and twenty years old was he when he began to reign, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. And his mother’s name was Jerusha, the daughter of Zadok.

34 And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD: he did according to all that his father Uzziah had done.

35 Howbeit the high places were not removed: the people sacrificed and burned incense still in the high places. He built the higher gate of the house of the LORD.

36 Now the rest of the acts of Jotham, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

37 In those days the LORD began to send against Judah Rezin the king of Syria, and Pekah the son of Remaliah.

38 And Jotham slept with his fathers, and was buried with his fathers in the city of David his father: and Ahaz his son reigned in his stead.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A rapid succession of kings passes: Azariah's long reign in Judah, and in Israel a chain of assassinations, Zechariah, Shallum, Menahem, Pekahiah, Pekah, as Assyria under Tiglath-pileser first takes territory of Israel and carries captives away. The kingdom is visibly coming apart. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the twenty-seventh year of Jeroboam king of Israel, Azariah son of Amaziah king of Judah began to reign. JFB notes Judah's throne may have stood vacant eleven or twelve years after Amaziah's murder, since Azariah was only four when his father died and a regency governed his minority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Azariah was sixteen when he began to reign and reigned fifty-two years in Jerusalem, his mother Jecholiah of Jerusalem. JFB describes his reign as very active and eventful, fully related in Chronicles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Azariah did right in the LORD's sight, according to all his father Amaziah had done. JFB notes the brief formula records his religious policy, though his reign's full character appears in Chronicles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Save that the high places were not removed, and the people still sacrificed and burnt incense there. JFB's formula discussion covers this standing exception to Azariah's good reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The LORD smote the king so he was a leper to his death, dwelling in a separate house, while Jotham his son judged the people. JFB explains Azariah, elated by great power, presumptuously arrogated priestly functions, and was punished with leprosy, equivalent to death since it excluded him from society, while his son Jotham administered the kingdom as viceroy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Azariah slept with his fathers, was buried with them in the city of David, and Jotham his son reigned in his stead. JFB notes after his long reign he died and was buried in the royal burying-field, though not in the royal cemetery of the city of David. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. In the thirty-eighth year of Azariah, Zachariah son of Jeroboam reigned over Israel in Samaria for six months. JFB notes an interregnum of ten, twelve, or even twenty-two years may have preceded his accession, and he pursued the calf-worship policy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Zachariah did evil in the LORD's sight as his fathers had done, departing not from Jeroboam's sins. JFB notes his short reign pursued the religious policy of calf worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Shallum son of Jabesh conspired against Zachariah, smote him before the people, slew him, and reigned in his stead. JFB notes his reign was abruptly terminated by violence, fulfilling the prophecy to Jehu that his family would hold Israel's throne for four generations. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. This was the LORD's word spoken to Jehu, that his sons would sit on Israel's throne to the fourth generation, and so it came to pass. JFB lists the four generations — Jehoahaz, Joash, Jeroboam, and Zechariah — and notes public knowledge of this prediction may have prompted Shallum's murderous design. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Shallum son of Jabesh began to reign in the thirty-ninth year of Uzziah king of Judah, reigning a full month in Samaria. JFB notes he was opposed and slain by Menahem, who, according to Josephus, commanded forces besieging Tirzah and marched directly against the usurper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Menahem son of Gadi went up from Tirzah to Samaria, smote Shallum son of Jabesh, slew him, and reigned in his stead. JFB explains Menahem raised the siege of Tirzah, twelve miles east of Samaria, and marched against Shallum. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. Menahem smote Tiphsah and all within it and its coasts from Tirzah, because they opened not to him, ripping up all its women with child. JFB identifies Tiphsah as Thapsacus on the Euphrates, Solomon's border city, which, refusing to open its gates, was taken by storm and spoiled with barbarous excesses regardless of age or sex. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. In the thirty-ninth year of Azariah, Menahem son of Gadi began to reign over Israel in Samaria for ten years. JFB notes his government followed his predecessors' religious policy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Menahem did evil in the LORD's sight, departing not all his days from Jeroboam's sins. JFB notes his reign was conducted on the religious policy of his predecessors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Pul king of Assyria came against the land, and Menahem gave him a thousand talents of silver, that his hand might be with him to confirm the kingdom in his hand. JFB identifies Pul as the first Assyrian king after Nimrod mentioned in biblical history, and explains Menahem turned the marching enemy into a confederate for his usurped dominion by this tribute. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Menahem exacted the money from Israel, from all mighty men of wealth, fifty shekels of silver each, to give to Assyria's king, and Assyria's king turned back and stayed not in the land. JFB notes the tribute, equal to £262,200, was raised by a tax on Israel's grandees and bribed Pul to return home. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Menahem slept with his fathers, and Pekahiah his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section on Pekahiah notes several months intervened between Menahem's death and Pekahiah's accession, probably owing to a contest about the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. In the fiftieth year of Azariah, Pekahiah son of Menahem began to reign over Israel in Samaria for two years. JFB notes the date suggests months intervened after Menahem's death, probably from a contest about the throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. Pekahiah did evil in the LORD's sight, departing not from Jeroboam's sins. JFB's section on his reign frames it within the continuing calf-worship policy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. Pekah son of Remaliah, a captain of Pekahiah, conspired against him, smote him in Samaria in the palace of the king's house, with Argob and Arieh and fifty Gileadites, and reigned in his stead. JFB notes Argob and Arieh were more probably influential friends of the king murdered along with him than the captain's accomplices. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. In the fifty-second year of Azariah, Pekah son of Remaliah began to reign over Israel in Samaria for twenty years. JFB's section on Pekah's reign opens with this accession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Pekah did evil in the LORD's sight, departing not from Jeroboam's sins. JFB's section frames his reign within the same apostasy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. In Pekah's days Tiglathpileser king of Assyria came and took Ijon, Abel-beth-maachah, Janoah, Kedesh, Hazor, Gilead, and Galilee, all Naphtali, carrying them captive to Assyria. JFB notes Tiglathpileser, who succeeded Pul and is supposed an usurper since he gives no genealogy, invaded Syria, and the places are listed as conquered in the invasion's progress, his annals having been found in the Nimroud mound. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Hoshea son of Elah conspired against Pekah son of Remaliah, smote him, slew him, and reigned in his stead in the twentieth year of Jotham son of Uzziah. JFB notes Hoshea did not obtain the kingdom until about nine or ten years after the crime, and the date means the twentieth year after Jotham's reign began, since the historian had not yet introduced Ahaz. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. In the second year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Jotham son of Uzziah king of Judah began to reign. JFB's section on Jotham's reign opens with this accession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. Jotham was twenty-five when he began to reign and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem, his mother Jerusha daughter of Zadok. JFB notes he began to reign alone at twenty-five, having already ruled as his father's viceroy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. Jotham did right in the LORD's sight, according to all his father Uzziah had done. JFB's section presents his reign as following his father's good pattern. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. Howbeit the high places were not removed, and the people still sacrificed and burned incense there, though Jotham built the higher gate of the LORD's house. JFB notes the higher gate was not the temple itself but one of its courts, probably leading into the palace. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 37. In those days the LORD began to send against Judah Rezin king of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah. JFB notes this is the first intimation of the hostile feelings of Israel and Syria toward Judah, leading to their alliance and joint war preparations, though war was not actually waged until Ahaz's reign. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 38. Jotham slept with his fathers, was buried with them in the city of David his father, and Ahaz his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section closes Jotham's reign with this burial and succession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
