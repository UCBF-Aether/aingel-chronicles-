The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 8 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Take Aaron and his sons with him, and the garments, and the anointing oil, and a bullock for the sin offering, and two rams, and a basket of unleavened bread;

3 And gather thou all the congregation together unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

4 And Moses did as the LORD commanded him; and the assembly was gathered together unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

5 And Moses said unto the congregation, This is the thing which the LORD commanded to be done.

6 And Moses brought Aaron and his sons, and washed them with water.

7 And he put upon him the coat, and girded him with the girdle, and clothed him with the robe, and put the ephod upon him, and he girded him with the curious girdle of the ephod, and bound it unto him therewith.

8 And he put the breastplate upon him: also he put in the breastplate the Urim and the Thummim.

9 And he put the mitre upon his head; also upon the mitre, even upon his forefront, did he put the golden plate, the holy crown; as the LORD commanded Moses.

10 And Moses took the anointing oil, and anointed the tabernacle and all that was therein, and sanctified them.

11 And he sprinkled thereof upon the altar seven times, and anointed the altar and all his vessels, both the laver and his foot, to sanctify them.

12 And he poured of the anointing oil upon Aaron’s head, and anointed him, to sanctify him.

13 And Moses brought Aaron’s sons, and put coats upon them, and girded them with girdles, and put bonnets upon them; as the LORD commanded Moses.

14 And he brought the bullock for the sin offering: and Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head of the bullock for the sin offering.

15 And he slew it; and Moses took the blood, and put it upon the horns of the altar round about with his finger, and purified the altar, and poured the blood at the bottom of the altar, and sanctified it, to make reconciliation upon it.

16 And he took all the fat that was upon the inwards, and the caul above the liver, and the two kidneys, and their fat, and Moses burned it upon the altar.

17 But the bullock, and his hide, his flesh, and his dung, he burnt with fire without the camp; as the LORD commanded Moses.

18 And he brought the ram for the burnt offering: and Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head of the ram.

19 And he killed it; and Moses sprinkled the blood upon the altar round about.

20 And he cut the ram into pieces; and Moses burnt the head, and the pieces, and the fat.

21 And he washed the inwards and the legs in water; and Moses burnt the whole ram upon the altar: it was a burnt sacrifice for a sweet savour, and an offering made by fire unto the LORD; as the LORD commanded Moses.

22 And he brought the other ram, the ram of consecration: and Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head of the ram.

23 And he slew it; and Moses took of the blood of it, and put it upon the tip of Aaron’s right ear, and upon the thumb of his right hand, and upon the great toe of his right foot.

24 And he brought Aaron’s sons, and Moses put of the blood upon the tip of their right ear, and upon the thumbs of their right hands, and upon the great toes of their right feet: and Moses sprinkled the blood upon the altar round about.

25 And he took the fat, and the rump, and all the fat that was upon the inwards, and the caul above the liver, and the two kidneys, and their fat, and the right shoulder:

26 And out of the basket of unleavened bread, that was before the LORD, he took one unleavened cake, and a cake of oiled bread, and one wafer, and put them on the fat, and upon the right shoulder:

27 And he put all upon Aaron’s hands, and upon his sons’ hands, and waved them for a wave offering before the LORD.

28 And Moses took them from off their hands, and burnt them on the altar upon the burnt offering: they were consecrations for a sweet savour: it is an offering made by fire unto the LORD.

29 And Moses took the breast, and waved it for a wave offering before the LORD: for of the ram of consecration it was Moses’ part; as the LORD commanded Moses.

30 And Moses took of the anointing oil, and of the blood which was upon the altar, and sprinkled it upon Aaron, and upon his garments, and upon his sons, and upon his sons’ garments with him; and sanctified Aaron, and his garments, and his sons, and his sons’ garments with him.

31 And Moses said unto Aaron and to his sons, Boil the flesh at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation: and there eat it with the bread that is in the basket of consecrations, as I commanded, saying, Aaron and his sons shall eat it.

32 And that which remaineth of the flesh and of the bread shall ye burn with fire.

33 And ye shall not go out of the door of the tabernacle of the congregation in seven days, until the days of your consecration be at an end: for seven days shall he consecrate you.

34 As he hath done this day, so the LORD hath commanded to do, to make an atonement for you.

35 Therefore shall ye abide at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation day and night seven days, and keep the charge of the LORD, that ye die not: for so I am commanded.

36 So Aaron and his sons did all things which the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses.

  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter carries out the consecration of Aaron and his sons as priests. Before the whole congregation Moses washes them, clothes Aaron in the high-priestly garments including the breastplate, anoints the tabernacle and Aaron with oil, and offers a sin offering, a burnt offering, and the ram of consecration, putting its blood on the right ear, thumb, and great toe of each priest. Aaron and his sons then remain at the tabernacle door for seven days, keeping the LORD's charge. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. The consecration now described in full detail had been ordered long before in Exodus 29, but is carried out only after the tabernacle is completed and the sacrifice laws are enacted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Aaron and his sons are taken with the garments, anointing oil, sin-offering bullock, two rams, and basket of unleavened bread, the full provision for the consecration ceremonial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. All the congregation is gathered at the tabernacle door, so the people may be satisfied that Aaron's appointment is not a personal intrusion or a family arrangement between him and Moses. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Moses does as commanded and the congregation gathers, the public assembly itself impressing the divine origin and authority of the priestly institution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Moses tells the congregation that this is what the LORD commanded to be done, framing the whole inauguration as divinely prescribed, not humanly devised. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Moses washes Aaron and his sons wholly with water, whereas on ordinary occasions only hands and feet are washed, the entire ablution teaching inward purity and the obligation of sanctuary ministers to be holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. The coat, girdle, robe, and ephod with its curious girdle are put on Aaron, the splendour of the vestments producing respect for the ministry and the predominant linen inculcating unspotted righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The breastplate is put on him with the Urim and Thummim in it, completing the high priest's vestments whose splendour and linen alike point to dignity and righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. The mitre is set on his head with the golden plate, the holy crown, on its front, the gorgeous tiara completing the official vestments intended to command respect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Moses anoints the tabernacle and all in it with the anointing oil and consecrates them, the anointing intimating that leaders in worship should have the unction of the Holy One in gifts and graces. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Oil is sprinkled seven times on the altar and the altar, its utensils, the laver, and its base are anointed, consecrating the whole place of service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Oil is poured on Aaron's head and he is anointed to consecrate him, marking the high priest himself as set apart by the same unction as the sanctuary. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Aaron's sons are brought, clothed in coats, girded with girdles, and given bonnets, sharing in the linen vestments that inculcate unspotted righteousness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The bullock for the sin offering is brought and Aaron and his sons lay hands on its head, a timely public confession of personal sin and transference of guilt to the typical victim. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Moses kills the bullock, puts blood on the altar horns with his finger, purifies the altar, and pours the rest at its base, consecrating the altar to make atonement for it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The fat, the caul, and the kidneys are burnt on the altar, the altar portion of the sin offering, while the rest of the victim is dealt with outside. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The bullock's skin, flesh, and dung are burnt with fire without the camp, as with a sin offering whose blood is carried into the sanctuary, it is not eaten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. The ram for a burnt offering is brought, a token of the priests' entire dedication to the service of God after their sin has been confessed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Moses kills the ram and sprinkles its blood round about the altar, the burnt offering following the sin offering in the consecration order. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. The ram is cut into pieces, its head and fat laid in order, and its inwards and legs washed, preparing the whole burnt offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Moses burns the whole ram on the altar as a burnt sacrifice of a sweet savour, the token of entire dedication being wholly consumed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. The other ram, the ram of consecration, is brought after sin and burnt offerings, as their peace offering declaring pleasure in entering God's service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. Moses kills the consecration ram and puts its blood on Aaron's right ear, right thumb, and right great toe, marking hearing, doing, and walking as consecrated. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 24. The same blood is put on Aaron's sons' right ears, thumbs, and toes, and the rest sprinkled round about the altar, bringing them into the same consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 25. The fat, rump, inward fat, caul, kidneys, and right shoulder are taken, the altar and priestly portions of the consecration ram. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 26. From the basket of consecration, unleavened cakes, a cake of oiled bread, and a wafer are taken, joining bread to the peace offering of consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. All these are put in Aaron's and his sons' hands and waved as a wave offering before the LORD, declaring their pleasure in being brought into close communion with God as sanctuary ministers. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 28. Moses takes them from their hands and burns them on the altar upon the burnt offering, as consecration offerings for a sweet savour made by fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 29. Moses takes the breast and waves it before the LORD as his part of the ram of consecration, the officiant receiving the wave portion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 30. Moses sprinkles anointing oil and altar blood on Aaron, his garments, his sons, and their garments, consecrating persons and vestments together for service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 31. Aaron and his sons boil the flesh at the tabernacle door and eat it there with the basket bread, as commanded, sharing the peace offering of their own consecration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 32. What remains of flesh and bread shall be burnt with fire, so no part of the consecration meal is kept beyond its appointed use. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 33. They shall not go out of the tabernacle door for seven days, undergoing a week's probation in the court before entering the interior, with the same rites observed each day. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 34. As has been done this day, so the LORD has commanded to be done to make atonement for them, the whole consecration being a divinely appointed atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 35. They must stay at the tabernacle door day and night for seven days and keep the LORD's charge, lest they die, the smallest breach forfeiting their lives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 36. Aaron and his sons do all things the LORD commanded by Moses, completing the public consecration that vindicates the priesthood's divine origin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
