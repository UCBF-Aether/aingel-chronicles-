The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Then came the children of Israel, even the whole congregation, into the desert of Zin in the first month: and the people abode in Kadesh; and Miriam died there, and was buried there.

2 And there was no water for the congregation: and they gathered themselves together against Moses and against Aaron.

3 And the people chode with Moses, and spake, saying, Would God that we had died when our brethren died before the LORD!

4 And why have ye brought up the congregation of the LORD into this wilderness, that we and our cattle should die there?

5 And wherefore have ye made us to come up out of Egypt, to bring us in unto this evil place? it is no place of seed, or of figs, or of vines, or of pomegranates; neither is there any water to drink.

6 And Moses and Aaron went from the presence of the assembly unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and they fell upon their faces: and the glory of the LORD appeared unto them.

7 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

8 Take the rod, and gather thou the assembly together, thou, and Aaron thy brother, and speak ye unto the rock before their eyes; and it shall give forth his water, and thou shalt bring forth to them water out of the rock: so thou shalt give the congregation and their beasts drink.

9 And Moses took the rod from before the LORD, as he commanded him.

10 And Moses and Aaron gathered the congregation together before the rock, and he said unto them, Hear now, ye rebels; must we fetch you water out of this rock?

11 And Moses lifted up his hand, and with his rod he smote the rock twice: and the water came out abundantly, and the congregation drank, and their beasts also.

12 And the LORD spake unto Moses and Aaron, Because ye believed me not, to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel, therefore ye shall not bring this congregation into the land which I have given them.

13 This is the water of Meribah; because the children of Israel strove with the LORD, and he was sanctified in them.

14 And Moses sent messengers from Kadesh unto the king of Edom, Thus saith thy brother Israel, Thou knowest all the travail that hath befallen us:

15 How our fathers went down into Egypt, and we have dwelt in Egypt a long time; and the Egyptians vexed us, and our fathers:

16 And when we cried unto the LORD, he heard our voice, and sent an angel, and hath brought us forth out of Egypt: and, behold, we are in Kadesh, a city in the uttermost of thy border:

17 Let us pass, I pray thee, through thy country: we will not pass through the fields, or through the vineyards, neither will we drink of the water of the wells: we will go by the king’s high way, we will not turn to the right hand nor to the left, until we have passed thy borders.

18 And Edom said unto him, Thou shalt not pass by me, lest I come out against thee with the sword.

19 And the children of Israel said unto him, We will go by the high way: and if I and my cattle drink of thy water, then I will pay for it: I will only, without doing anything else, go through on my feet.

20 And he said, Thou shalt not go through. And Edom came out against him with much people, and with a strong hand.

21 Thus Edom refused to give Israel passage through his border: wherefore Israel turned away from him.

22 And the children of Israel, even the whole congregation, journeyed from Kadesh, and came unto mount Hor.

23 And the LORD spake unto Moses and Aaron in mount Hor, by the coast of the land of Edom, saying,

24 Aaron shall be gathered unto his people: for he shall not enter into the land which I have given unto the children of Israel, because ye rebelled against my word at the water of Meribah.

25 Take Aaron and Eleazar his son, and bring them up unto mount Hor:

26 And strip Aaron of his garments, and put them upon Eleazar his son: and Aaron shall be gathered unto his people, and shall die there.

27 And Moses did as the LORD commanded: and they went up into mount Hor in the sight of all the congregation.

28 And Moses stripped Aaron of his garments, and put them upon Eleazar his son; and Aaron died there in the top of the mount: and Moses and Eleazar came down from the mount.

29 And when all the congregation saw that Aaron was dead, they mourned for Aaron thirty days, even all the house of Israel.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter marks the end of the old generation's leadership. Israel comes to the desert of Zin, Miriam dies there and is buried, and the people contend for water at Meribah. The LORD tells Moses to speak to the rock, but Moses strikes it twice with his rod, calling the people rebels; water flows abundantly, yet for this failure to sanctify God before Israel, Moses and Aaron are told they will not bring the congregation into the land. Edom refuses Israel passage through his border, so they turn away, and on Mount Hor Aaron dies, Eleazar his son is clothed in his garments, and all Israel mourns thirty days. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Then Israel, the whole congregation, came into the desert of Zin in the first month, and the people abode in Kadesh, and Miriam died there and was buried there. Henry notes that after thirty-eight years' tedious abode in the wilderness, the armies of Israel advanced toward Canaan again, and the generation's turning point opened with Miriam's death at Kadesh. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. There was no water for the congregation, and they gathered themselves together against Moses and against Aaron. Henry notes we live in a wanting world, and wherever we are must expect something to put us out, and plenty of water is a great mercy whose worth we should own more when we feel its want. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The people chode with Moses, saying, would God that we had died when our brethren died before the Lord! Henry notes they spoke the same absurd and brutish language their fathers had done, and their crime was worse because they had smarted so long for their fathers' discontent and distrust, yet ventured in the same steps. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. And why have you brought up the congregation of the Lord into this wilderness, that we and our cattle should die there? The complaint charged the leaders with a murderous purpose, as if the wilderness journey were designed to kill the congregation rather than to bring it to Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And wherefore have you made us come up out of Egypt, to bring us into this evil place, a place of no seed, figs, vines or pomegranates, neither is there any water to drink? The catalogue of absences measured Canaan's promised plenty against the desert's bareness, and turned a present want into a verdict on the whole exodus. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Moses and Aaron went from the presence of the assembly to the door of the tabernacle, fell upon their faces, and the glory of the Lord appeared to them. As in every crisis, the leaders turned from the assembly's face to God's presence, and glory answered their prostration. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Take the rod, gather the assembly together, you and Aaron your brother, and speak to the rock before their eyes, and it shall give forth his water, and you shall bring forth water for them out of the rock, for the congregation and their cattle to drink. Henry notes God is as able as ever to supply his people with what is needful, and the command was to speak to the rock, not to smite it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. Moses took the rod from before the Lord, as he commanded him. The rod, the same instrument of former wonders, was taken from before the Lord's presence, so that the wonder would be visibly God's, not Moses'. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Moses and Aaron gathered the congregation before the rock, and he said to them, hear now, you rebels, must we fetch you water out of this rock? Henry's charge is exact: they took much of the glory of the wonder to themselves, as if it were done by some power or worthiness of their own, asking, must we fetch water? Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Moses lifted up his hand, and with his rod he smote the rock twice, and the water came out abundantly, and the congregation drank, and their beasts also. The water came despite the disobedience, abundantly and for cattle as well as people, so that God's mercy was not measured by his servants' failure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. The Lord spoke to Moses and Aaron, because you believed me not, to sanctify me in the eyes of Israel, therefore you shall not bring this congregation into the land which I have given them. Henry explains they did not sanctify God, that is, they did not give to him alone the glory of this miracle due to his name, and, provoked by the people, Moses spoke unadvisedly with his lips. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. This is the water of Meribah, because the children of Israel strove with the Lord, and he was sanctified in them. The name fixed the place as strife, yet God was sanctified in it: even through his servants' failure, the wonder declared his holiness to Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. Moses sent messengers from Kadesh to the king of Edom, saying, thus says your brother Israel, you know all the travail that has befallen us. Henry notes the nearest way to Canaan from Israel's encampment was through Edom, so the embassy was a reasonable request for the nearest road, addressed brother to brother. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. How our fathers went down into Egypt, and we have dwelt in Egypt a long time, and the Egyptians vexed us and our fathers. The embassy rehearsed the shared history first: Israel's long sojourn and affliction were the ground for asking a brother's kindness on the road. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. And when we cried to the Lord, he heard our voice, and sent an angel, and has brought us forth out of Egypt, and behold, we are in Kadesh, a city in the uttermost of your border. The deliverance was God's work by his angel, and Kadesh on Edom's border was the proof that the journey had reached its neighbour's door. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Let us pass, I pray you, through your country: we will not pass through fields or vineyards, neither will we drink of the water of the wells: we will go by the king's high way, not turning to the right hand or the left, until we have passed your borders. The terms removed every ground of fear: no fields, no vineyards, no wells, only the highway, straight through. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. And Edom said to him, you shall not pass by me, lest I come out against you with the sword. Henry notes the Edomites feared damage from Israel, and under any other discipline than God's own there might have been cause for jealousy, but the refusal was still a brother's door shut in Israel's face. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. The children of Israel said to him, we will go by the high way, and if I and my cattle drink of your water, then I will pay for it: I will only, without doing anything else, go through on my feet. Israel answered the sword with a second, humbler offer: foot passage only, and payment for any water drunk. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. And he said, you shall not go through, and Edom came out against him with much people and with a strong hand. The second refusal came with an army on the border, turning a denied request into an armed confrontation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Thus Edom refused to give Israel passage through his border, wherefore Israel turned away from him. Henry reads the old hatred revived: Esau hated Jacob because of the blessing, and now, when the blessing was about to be inherited, the hatred revived, and we must not think it strange if reasonable requests are denied by unreasonable men. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. The children of Israel, the whole congregation, journeyed from Kadesh and came to mount Hor. The turn away from Edom brought the whole congregation to the mountain where the priesthood itself would change hands. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. Aaron shall be gathered to his people, for he shall not enter into the land which I have given to Israel, because you rebelled against my word at the water of Meribah. Henry finds displeasure, mercy and significance together: Aaron died for his transgression at Meribah, yet died with ease and honour, and his exclusion showed the Levitical priesthood could make nothing perfect, which must be done by bringing in a better hope. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. Take Aaron and Eleazar his son, and bring them up to mount Hor. The succession was arranged before the death, in the open sight of the congregation, so that the priesthood passed without a gap. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Strip Aaron of his garments, and put them upon Eleazar his son, and Aaron shall be gathered to his people and shall die there. Henry notes it was a great satisfaction to Aaron to see his dear son preferred and his office preserved and secured, and to see in this a figure of Christ's everlasting priesthood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. Moses did as the Lord commanded, and they went up into mount Hor in the sight of all the congregation. The ascent was public: the whole congregation watched the old priest climb to die and the new priest climb to succeed him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. Moses stripped Aaron of his garments, put them upon Eleazar his son, and Aaron died there in the top of the mount, and Moses and Eleazar came down from the mount. Henry notes Aaron submitted and died in the method and manner appointed, for all that appears with as much cheerfulness as if he had been going to bed, and a good man would not desire to outlive his usefulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. When all the congregation saw that Aaron was dead, they mourned for Aaron thirty days, even all the house of Israel. The thirty days' mourning, by the whole house, honoured the priest who had borne their iniquities for forty years, even though he died outside the land. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
