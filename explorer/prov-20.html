The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Proverbs 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise.

2 The fear of a king is as the roaring of a lion: whoso provoketh him to anger sinneth against his own soul.

3 It is an honour for a man to cease from strife: but every fool will be meddling.

4 The sluggard will not plow by reason of the cold; therefore shall he beg in harvest, and have nothing.

5 Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water; but a man of understanding will draw it out.

6 Most men will proclaim every one his own goodness: but a faithful man who can find?

7 The just man walketh in his integrity: his children are blessed after him.

8 A king that sitteth in the throne of judgment scattereth away all evil with his eyes.

9 Who can say, I have made my heart clean, I am pure from my sin?

10 Divers weights, and divers measures, both of them are alike abomination to the LORD.

11 Even a child is known by his doings, whether his work be pure, and whether it be right.

12 The hearing ear, and the seeing eye, the LORD hath made even both of them.

13 Love not sleep, lest thou come to poverty; open thine eyes, and thou shalt be satisfied with bread.

14 It is naught, it is naught, saith the buyer: but when he is gone his way, then he boasteth.

15 There is gold, and a multitude of rubies: but the lips of knowledge are a precious jewel.

16 Take his garment that is surety for a stranger: and take a pledge of him for a strange woman.

17 Bread of deceit is sweet to a man; but afterwards his mouth shall be filled with gravel.

18 Every purpose is established by counsel: and with good advice make war.

19 He that goeth about as a talebearer revealeth secrets: therefore meddle not with him that flattereth with his lips.

20 Whoso curseth his father or his mother, his lamp shall be put out in obscure darkness.

21 An inheritance may be gotten hastily at the beginning; but the end thereof shall not be blessed.

22 Say not thou, I will recompense evil; but wait on the LORD, and he shall save thee.

23 Divers weights are an abomination unto the LORD; and a false balance is not good.

24 Man’s goings are of the LORD; how can a man then understand his own way?

25 It is a snare to the man who devoureth that which is holy, and after vows to make enquiry.

26 A wise king scattereth the wicked, and bringeth the wheel over them.

27 The spirit of man is the candle of the LORD, searching all the inward parts of the belly.

28 Mercy and truth preserve the king: and his throne is upholden by mercy.

29 The glory of young men is their strength: and the beauty of old men is the grey head.

30 The blueness of a wound cleanseth away evil: so do stripes the inward parts of the belly.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Wine is a mocker and strong drink raging, and the chapter moves through kings, strife, honest weights and the snare of rash vows. Divers weights are abomination, a buyer haggles and then boasts, bread of deceit turns to gravel, and the counsel is to wait on the LORD rather than repay evil. The spirit of man is the LORD's candle, searching the inward parts, and mercy and truth preserve the king. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging, and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise, for it seems hard to believe that men of greatest abilities, as well as the ignorant, render themselves fools and madmen merely for the taste of strong liquors. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. The fear of a king is as the roaring of a lion, and whoso provokes him sins against his own soul, for how formidable kings are to provokers, and how much more foolish to provoke the King of kings. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. It is an honour for a man to cease from strife, but every fool will be meddling, for engaging in quarrels is the greatest folly: yield, and even give up just demands, for peace's sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. The sluggard will not plow by reason of the cold, therefore he shall beg in harvest and have nothing, for he who labours and endures hardship in his seed-time for eternity will be properly diligent in earthly business. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water, but a man of understanding will draw it out, for though many capable of wise counsel are silent, something may be drawn from them rewarding those who obtain it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. Most men will proclaim every one his own goodness, but a faithful man who can find, for it is hard to find those who have done, and will do, more good than they speak or care to hear spoken of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. The just man walks in his integrity, and his children are blessed after him, for a good man is not liable to uneasiness in contriving or reflecting, as deceitful walkers are, and his family fares better for his sake. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. A king sitting in the throne of judgment scatters away all evil with his eyes, for if great men are good men, they may do much good and prevent very much evil. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Who can say, I have made my heart clean, I am pure from my sin, for some can say, through grace, we are cleaner than we have been, but it was the work of the Holy Spirit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. Divers weights and divers measures are both abomination to the LORD, for see the various deceits men use, of which love of money is the root, and the Lord will not bless what is thus gotten. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Even a child is known by his doings, whether his work be pure and right, for parents should observe their children, that they may manage them accordingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. The hearing ear and the seeing eye, the LORD has made even both of them, for all our powers and faculties are from God and are to be employed for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Love not sleep, lest thou come to poverty, open thine eyes and be satisfied with bread, for those indulging themselves may expect to want necessaries which should have been gotten by honest labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. It is naught, it is naught, says the buyer, but when gone his way, then he boasts, for men use arts to get a good bargain and buy cheap, whereas a man ought to be ashamed of a fraud and a lie. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. There is gold and a multitude of rubies, but the lips of knowledge are a precious jewel, for he who prefers true knowledge to riches follows the ways of religion and happiness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. Take his garment who is surety for a stranger, and take a pledge of him for a strange woman, for those ruin themselves who entangle themselves in rash suretiship, and those in league with abandoned women, and no confidence should be placed in either. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. Bread of deceit is sweet to a man, but afterwards his mouth shall be filled with gravel, for wealth gotten by fraud may be sweet, since the carnal mind takes pleasure in wicked devices' success, but it will be bitter in reflection. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. Every purpose is established by counsel, and with good advice make war, for especially we need advice in spiritual warfare, and God's word and Spirit are the best counsellors in every point. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. He who goes about as a talebearer reveals secrets, therefore meddle not with him who flatters with his lips, for those dearly buy their own praise who put confidence in a man because he speaks fairly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. Whoso curses his father or mother, his lamp shall be put out in obscure darkness, for an undutiful child will become very miserable and must never expect peace or comfort. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. An inheritance may be gotten hastily at the beginning, but its end shall not be blessed, for an estate suddenly raised is often as suddenly ruined. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. Say not, I will recompense evil, but wait on the LORD, and he shall save thee, for we must wait on the Lord, attend his pleasure, and he will protect us. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Divers weights are abomination unto the LORD, and a false balance is not good, for a bargain made by fraud will prove a losing bargain in the end. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 24. Man's goings are of the LORD, how can a man then understand his own way, for how can we form plans and conduct business independently of the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 25. It is a snare to the man who devours that which is holy, and after vows to make enquiry, for the evasions men often use with their own consciences show how false and deceitful man is. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 26. A wise king scatters the wicked and brings the wheel over them, for justice should crush the wicked and separate them from the virtuous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 27. The spirit of man is the candle of the LORD, searching all the inward parts of the belly, for the rational soul and conscience are as a lamp within us, to examine dispositions and motives by God's revealed will. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 28. Mercy and truth preserve the king, and his throne is upheld by mercy, for mercy and truth are the glories of God's throne. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 29. The glory of young men is their strength, and the beauty of old men is the grey head, for both young and old have their advantages, and neither should despise or envy the other. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 30. The blueness of a wound cleanses away evil, so do stripes the inward parts of the belly, for severe rebukes sometimes do a great deal of good, and if God uses severe afflictions to purify hearts and fit us for service, we have cause to be thankful. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
