The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 2 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Again there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them to present himself before the LORD.

2 And the LORD said unto Satan, From whence comest thou? And Satan answered the LORD, and said, From going to and fro in the earth, and from walking up and down in it.

3 And the LORD said unto Satan, Hast thou considered my servant Job, that there is none like him in the earth, a perfect and an upright man, one that feareth God, and escheweth evil? and still he holdeth fast his integrity, although thou movedst me against him, to destroy him without cause.

4 And Satan answered the LORD, and said, Skin for skin, yea, all that a man hath will he give for his life.

5 But put forth thine hand now, and touch his bone and his flesh, and he will curse thee to thy face.

6 And the LORD said unto Satan, Behold, he is in thine hand; but save his life.

7 So went Satan forth from the presence of the LORD, and smote Job with sore boils from the sole of his foot unto his crown.

8 And he took him a potsherd to scrape himself withal; and he sat down among the ashes.

9 Then said his wife unto him, Dost thou still retain thine integrity? curse God, and die.

10 But he said unto her, Thou speakest as one of the foolish women speaketh. What? shall we receive good at the hand of God, and shall we not receive evil? In all this did not Job sin with his lips.

11 Now when Job’s three friends heard of all this evil that was come upon him, they came every one from his own place; Eliphaz the Temanite, and Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite: for they had made an appointment together to come to mourn with him and to comfort him.

12 And when they lifted up their eyes afar off, and knew him not, they lifted up their voice, and wept; and they rent every one his mantle, and sprinkled dust upon their heads toward heaven.

13 So they sat down with him upon the ground seven days and seven nights, and none spake a word unto him: for they saw that his grief was very great.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

In a second heavenly scene Satan wagers that Job bore the loss of his property only because his own body was untouched, and is permitted to strike Job himself while sparing his life. Job is covered with sore boils, scrapes himself with a potsherd in the ashes, and refuses his wife’s urging to curse God and die. His three friends — Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar — come to mourn with him and sit with him on the ground in silence for seven days, because his grief is very great. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Again there is a day when the sons of God present themselves, and Satan comes among them. JFB notes this is an appointed day for the angels to give account of their ministry, and Satan now has a special report to make about Job. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The LORD asks Satan where he has come from, and Satan again answers that he has been going to and fro on the earth. The repetition frames a second, sharper trial. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. God again commends Job as perfect and upright, fearing God and shunning evil, and adds that he still holds fast his integrity although God was moved against him without cause. JFB explains integrity as completeness, the same word elsewhere rendered perfect. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Satan answers with the proverb skin for skin: a man will trade any outward good for another, but life itself cannot be replaced. His sneer is that Job bore outward losses calmly only because his own person was untouched, and will give up even religion to save his life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. Satan dares God to touch Job's bone and flesh, predicting Job will curse him to his face. The wager moves from property to the body itself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. The LORD puts Job in Satan's hand but commands him to spare his life. JFB notes Satan's ingenuity in inflicting pain, and his knowledge of what the body can bear without vital injury. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Satan strikes Job with sore boils from sole to crown. JFB explains the Hebrew as a singular burning sore, a universal inflammation, of the black leprous kind called elephantiasis, because the feet swell like an elephant's. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. Job takes a potsherd to scrape himself and sits among the ashes. JFB notes the potsherd was an instrument made for scratching, since the sore was too disgusting to touch, and sitting in ashes marks the deepest mourning and humility, as dust and ashes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Job's wife urges him to curse God and die. JFB reads curse as renounce: take farewell of God and so die, since, in her bitter view, no good is to be got from religion either here or hereafter. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Job answers that she speaks as one of the foolish women speaks, and asks whether we should receive good from God and not also receive evil. JFB notes that sin and folly are allied in Scripture, and that receiving evil means bearing it willingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Job's three friends — Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite — hear of his evil and agree together to come mourn with him and comfort him. JFB traces Eliphaz to Idumea, where Teman in the south was famed for wisdom, and Bildad to Shuah, a son of Abraham and Keturah. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Seeing Job from afar, the friends do not recognize him, lift up their voices and weep, tear their mantles, and sprinkle dust toward heaven. JFB notes the dust thrown upward so it falls on their heads is the deepest mourning. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The friends sit with Job on the ground seven days and seven nights, and no one speaks a word, for his grief is very great. JFB notes seven days was the usual length of mourning, and that extraordinary griefs are too great for utterance, while ordinary griefs find vent in language. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

  • Skin for skin. Satan’s proverb in verse 4: a man will trade any outward good for another, but life itself cannot be replaced — his argument that Job bore property losses calmly only because his own person was untouched. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Potsherd and ashes. Job scrapes his sores with a broken piece of pottery because they are too sore to touch, and sits among ashes, the mark of the deepest mourning and humility. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
