The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Exodus 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Now Moses kept the flock of Jethro his father in law, the priest of Midian: and he led the flock to the backside of the desert, and came to the mountain of God, even to Horeb.

2 And the angel of the LORD appeared unto him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush: and he looked, and, behold, the bush burned with fire, and the bush was not consumed.

3 And Moses said, I will now turn aside, and see this great sight, why the bush is not burnt.

4 And when the LORD saw that he turned aside to see, God called unto him out of the midst of the bush, and said, Moses, Moses. And he said, Here am I.

5 And he said, Draw not nigh hither: put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground.

6 Moreover he said, I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. And Moses hid his face; for he was afraid to look upon God.

7 And the LORD said, I have surely seen the affliction of my people which are in Egypt, and have heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters; for I know their sorrows;

8 And I am come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up out of that land unto a good land and a large, unto a land flowing with milk and honey; unto the place of the Canaanites, and the Hittites, and the Amorites, and the Perizzites, and the Hivites, and the Jebusites.

9 Now therefore, behold, the cry of the children of Israel is come unto me: and I have also seen the oppression wherewith the Egyptians oppress them.

10 Come now therefore, and I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people the children of Israel out of Egypt.

11 And Moses said unto God, Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh, and that I should bring forth the children of Israel out of Egypt?

12 And he said, Certainly I will be with thee; and this shall be a token unto thee, that I have sent thee: When thou hast brought forth the people out of Egypt, ye shall serve God upon this mountain.

13 And Moses said unto God, Behold, when I come unto the children of Israel, and shall say unto them, The God of your fathers hath sent me unto you; and they shall say to me, What is his name? what shall I say unto them?

14 And God said unto Moses, I AM THAT I AM: and he said, Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, I AM hath sent me unto you.

15 And God said moreover unto Moses, Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, the LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, hath sent me unto you: this is my name for ever, and this is my memorial unto all generations.

16 Go, and gather the elders of Israel together, and say unto them, The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob, appeared unto me, saying, I have surely visited you, and seen that which is done to you in Egypt:

17 And I have said, I will bring you up out of the affliction of Egypt unto the land of the Canaanites, and the Hittites, and the Amorites, and the Perizzites, and the Hivites, and the Jebusites, unto a land flowing with milk and honey.

18 And they shall hearken to thy voice: and thou shalt come, thou and the elders of Israel, unto the king of Egypt, and ye shall say unto him, The LORD God of the Hebrews hath met with us: and now let us go, we beseech thee, three days’ journey into the wilderness, that we may sacrifice to the LORD our God.

19 And I am sure that the king of Egypt will not let you go, no, not by a mighty hand.

20 And I will stretch out my hand, and smite Egypt with all my wonders which I will do in the midst thereof: and after that he will let you go.

21 And I will give this people favour in the sight of the Egyptians: and it shall come to pass, that, when ye go, ye shall not go empty.

22 But every woman shall borrow of her neighbour, and of her that sojourneth in her house, jewels of silver, and jewels of gold, and raiment: and ye shall put them upon your sons, and upon your daughters; and ye shall spoil the Egyptians.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

At Horeb, the mountain of God, Moses sees a bush burning without being consumed, and God calls him from it. God names himself I AM and the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and sends Moses to bring Israel out of Egypt to a land flowing with milk and honey. Moses protests his unfitness, and God promises signs and gives Aaron as his spokesman. Interpreted Plain-language summary of the chapter text, checked against the study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Moses kept the flock of Jethro to the backside of the desert, to Horeb, the mountain of God. Henry divides Moses's life into three forties, and notes that God found him employed as a shepherd, learning the meekness for which he is most noted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. The angel of the LORD appeared in a flame of fire from a bush that burned yet was not consumed. Henry reads the bush as an emblem of the church in Egypt: under severest persecution, kept by God's presence from being destroyed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. Moses turned aside to see why the bush was not burnt. Henry notes that those who would have communion with God must attend upon him even in so lowly a manifestation as a bush. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. When the LORD saw Moses turn aside, God called to him out of the bush, and Moses answered, Here am I. Henry observes God gave a gracious call and Moses returned a ready answer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. God bade Moses not draw nigh, but loose his shoes, for the place was holy ground. Henry explains putting off the shoe as a token of respect and submission, and a call to solemn preparation before God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. God said, I am the God of thy father, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and Moses hid his face in fear. Henry stresses God says I am, not I was: the patriarchs still live, and no length of time separates the souls of the just from their Maker. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. The LORD said he had surely seen Israel's affliction and heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters. Henry notes even the secret sorrows of God's people are known to him, and their highest oppressors are not above him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. God was come down to deliver Israel from Egypt and bring them to a good land flowing with milk and honey, the place of the Canaanites and other nations. Henry reads the earthly Canaan as the figure of the heavenly Canaan to which grace brings its redeemed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. The cry of Israel was come unto God, and he had also seen the oppression wherewith the Egyptians oppressed them. Henry joins the two: God both hears the cry and sees the wrong. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. God said, Come now, I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people Israel. Henry notes the commission came only after Moses had learned his own weakness in forty years of obscurity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Moses asked, Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh and bring forth Israel? Henry contrasts this with his earlier haste: former self-confidence has given way to a sense of weakness, though sinful distrust can creep in under the garb of humility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. God answered, Certainly I will be with thee, and gave the token that Israel would serve God upon this mountain. Henry says all objections are answered in that one promise: God's presence is enough. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. Moses asked what he should say when Israel asked the name of the God who sent him. Henry notes Moses must revive among them the religion of their fathers, which was almost lost. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. God said, I AM THAT I AM, and bade Moses tell Israel, I AM hath sent me. Henry expounds the name: God is self-existent, eternal and unchangeable, incomprehensible, and faithful and true to all his promises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. God gave his memorial name, the LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, to be remembered unto all generations. Henry distinguishes the two names: I AM denotes what God is in himself, and the God of your fathers what he is to his people. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. Moses was to gather the elders and tell them the LORD God of their fathers had appeared and had surely visited them and seen what was done to them in Egypt. Henry notes God could promise beforehand that they would hearken, for he would make them willing in the day of power. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. God promised to bring Israel up out of Egyptian affliction unto the land of Canaan, flowing with milk and honey. Henry reads this as the pattern of redemption: those delivered from a spiritual Egypt are brought to a heavenly Canaan. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The elders would hearken, and Moses and they were to ask Pharaoh for three days' journey into the wilderness to sacrifice. Henry observes that petitions and humble complaints would not prevail with Pharaoh, but God's mighty hand would break him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. God told Moses he was sure Pharaoh would not let them go, no, not by a mighty hand. Henry notes those who will not bow to the power of God's word will certainly be broken by the power of his hand. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. God would stretch out his hand and smite Egypt with wonders, and after that Pharaoh would let them go. Henry stresses deliverance would come by methods out of the common ways of providence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. God would give the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians, so they would not go out empty. Henry sees in this the reversal of tyranny: the oppressors would furnish the oppressed with riches at their departure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. Every woman would borrow jewels of silver and gold and raiment of her neighbour, put them on her children, and spoil the Egyptians. Henry notes the redeemed come forth with all they have, applying all to the glory of God and the service of his church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • I AM. Henry distinguishes the two names: I AM denotes what God is in himself, while the God of your fathers is what he is to his people. Interpreted Study note on verse 15, summarizing Matthew Henry
