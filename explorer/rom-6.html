The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Romans 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound?

2 God forbid. How shall we, that are dead to sin, live any longer therein?

3 Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death?

4 Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.

5 For if we have been planted together in the likeness of his death, we shall be also in the likeness of his resurrection:

6 Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin.

7 For he that is dead is freed from sin.

8 Now if we be dead with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with him:

9 Knowing that Christ being raised from the dead dieth no more; death hath no more dominion over him.

10 For in that he died, he died unto sin once: but in that he liveth, he liveth unto God.

11 Likewise reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God through Jesus Christ our Lord.

12 Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body, that ye should obey it in the lusts thereof.

13 Neither yield ye your members as instruments of unrighteousness unto sin: but yield yourselves unto God, as those that are alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness unto God.

14 For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace.

15 What then? shall we sin, because we are not under the law, but under grace? God forbid.

16 Know ye not, that to whom ye yield yourselves servants to obey, his servants ye are to whom ye obey; whether of sin unto death, or of obedience unto righteousness?

17 But God be thanked, that ye were the servants of sin, but ye have obeyed from the heart that form of doctrine which was delivered you.

18 Being then made free from sin, ye became the servants of righteousness.

19 I speak after the manner of men because of the infirmity of your flesh: for as ye have yielded your members servants to uncleanness and to iniquity unto iniquity; even so now yield your members servants to righteousness unto holiness.

20 For when ye were the servants of sin, ye were free from righteousness.

21 What fruit had ye then in those things whereof ye are now ashamed? for the end of those things is death.

22 But now being made free from sin, and become servants to God, ye have your fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life.

23 For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul answers the objection that grace might license sin: believers baptized into Christ were baptized into his death, buried with him, and raised to walk in newness of life, so sin must no longer reign in their mortal bodies. No longer slaves of sin, they are servants of righteousness, and the chapter closes with its sharp contrast: the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? Henry notes that the question exposes a perversion of grace, not a consequence of it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. God forbid. How shall we who are dead to sin live any longer in it? JFB stresses that death to sin is the believer's new identity, not a mere resolution. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. As many as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death. Henry notes that baptism pictures union with Christ in his death. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. We are buried with him by baptism into death, that as Christ was raised by the Father's glory, so we should walk in newness of life. JFB stresses that burial implies a resurrection life, not a return to the grave. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. If we have been planted together in the likeness of his death, we shall also be in the likeness of his resurrection. Henry observes that union with Christ covers both halves of his work. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, so we should no longer serve sin. JFB notes that the old self is executed, not merely reformed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He that is dead is freed from sin. Henry stresses that death ends sin's legal claim, as it ends a master's claim over a servant. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. If we are dead with Christ, we believe we shall also live with him. JFB notes that death with Christ is the pledge of life with him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Christ, being raised from the dead, dies no more; death has no more dominion over him. Henry observes that a risen Christ can never die again, and neither can the life he gives. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. In that he died, he died unto sin once; in that he lives, he lives unto God. JFB stresses the once-for-all character of his death and the Godward direction of his life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Likewise reckon yourselves dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God through Jesus Christ our Lord. Henry notes that reckoning is faith acting on the fact of union with Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body, that you should obey its lusts. JFB stresses that sin may remain as a rebel, but must not reign as a king. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Neither yield your members as instruments of unrighteousness to sin, but yield yourselves to God as alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness. Henry observes that the body is a weapon for whichever master it serves. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace. JFB notes that grace, not law, is the power that breaks sin's dominion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. What then? Shall we sin because we are not under law but under grace? God forbid. Henry observes that freedom from law as a covenant is not freedom to sin. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. To whom you yield yourselves servants to obey, his servants you are, whether of sin unto death or of obedience unto righteousness. JFB stresses that every man serves some master; neutrality is impossible. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. God be thanked that you were servants of sin, but have obeyed from the heart the form of doctrine delivered to you. Henry notes that the change is inward obedience, not outward profession alone. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. Being made free from sin, you became servants of righteousness. JFB observes that Christian freedom is a change of masters, not an absence of one. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Paul speaks after the manner of men because of the infirmity of their flesh: as they yielded members to uncleanness, so now they are to yield them to holiness. Henry notes that the analogy of slavery is used because human weakness needs plain speech. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. When you were servants of sin, you were free from righteousness. JFB notes the irony: freedom from righteousness is the worst bondage. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. What fruit had you then in things you are now ashamed of? For their end is death. Henry observes that shame over past sin is itself evidence of a changed heart. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. Now, made free from sin and become servants of God, you have your fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life. JFB notes that holiness is both the fruit and the road to life. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 23. The wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord. Henry stresses the contrast: death is earned wages, life is a free gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Unfamiliar terms

Plain-language glosses for words and phrases in this chapter that a modern reader may find unfamiliar. They are editorial aids, not Scripture.

  • baptized into his death. Paul's picture of baptism as union with Christ's death and burial, so that the old self is crucified with him. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • instruments. Weapons or tools; Paul urges believers to yield their members as instruments of righteousness to God, not of unrighteousness to sin. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
  • wages. Pay earned by service; Paul contrasts sin's wages, death, with God's free gift, eternal life. Interpreted Chapter text and study notes for this chapter
