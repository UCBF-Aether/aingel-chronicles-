The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Leviticus 1 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD called unto Moses, and spake unto him out of the tabernacle of the congregation, saying,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, If any man of you bring an offering unto the LORD, ye shall bring your offering of the cattle, even of the herd, and of the flock.

3 If his offering be a burnt sacrifice of the herd, let him offer a male without blemish: he shall offer it of his own voluntary will at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the LORD.

4 And he shall put his hand upon the head of the burnt offering; and it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him.

5 And he shall kill the bullock before the LORD: and the priests, Aaron’s sons, shall bring the blood, and sprinkle the blood round about upon the altar that is by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.

6 And he shall flay the burnt offering, and cut it into his pieces.

7 And the sons of Aaron the priest shall put fire upon the altar, and lay the wood in order upon the fire:

8 And the priests, Aaron’s sons, shall lay the parts, the head, and the fat, in order upon the wood that is on the fire which is upon the altar:

9 But his inwards and his legs shall he wash in water: and the priest shall burn all on the altar, to be a burnt sacrifice, an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.

10 And if his offering be of the flocks, namely, of the sheep, or of the goats, for a burnt sacrifice; he shall bring it a male without blemish.

11 And he shall kill it on the side of the altar northward before the LORD: and the priests, Aaron’s sons, shall sprinkle his blood round about upon the altar.

12 And he shall cut it into his pieces, with his head and his fat: and the priest shall lay them in order on the wood that is on the fire which is upon the altar:

13 But he shall wash the inwards and the legs with water: and the priest shall bring it all, and burn it upon the altar: it is a burnt sacrifice, an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.

14 And if the burnt sacrifice for his offering to the LORD be of fowls, then he shall bring his offering of turtledoves, or of young pigeons.

15 And the priest shall bring it unto the altar, and wring off his head, and burn it on the altar; and the blood thereof shall be wrung out at the side of the altar:

16 And he shall pluck away his crop with his feathers, and cast it beside the altar on the east part, by the place of the ashes:

17 And he shall cleave it with the wings thereof, but shall not divide it asunder: and the priest shall burn it upon the altar, upon the wood that is upon the fire: it is a burnt sacrifice, an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter gives the law of the burnt offering, the offering that is wholly consumed on the altar. It works down a scale of means: a bullock from the herd, a sheep or goat from the flock, or turtledoves or young pigeons for the poor. In each case the offerer lays a hand on the animal, it is killed and its blood sprinkled by the priests, and the whole is burnt; the notes stress that what is looked to is the disposition of the offerer, not the costliness of the gift. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Burnt offering. An offering wholly consumed by fire on the altar, with no part eaten by priest or offerer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Atonement. The making of the offerer acceptable before the LORD through the sacrifice, here linked in the notes to the laying on of hands and the sprinkling of blood. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Sweet savour. The phrase used in the chapter for an offering accepted with favour, expressing, in the notes, the offerer's piety. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. With the tabernacle completed, the rest of the law is announced to Moses by an audible voice from the divine glory over the mercy seat, rather than publicly from Sinai or privately on its summit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Where the matter is spiritual, “children of Israel” embraces all the tribes, not the Levites alone. The offerings directed here are voluntary freewill offerings, over and above the standing obligations that could not be commuted. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. A burnt sacrifice is wholly consumed on the altar, with no part eaten by priest or offerer, signifying the offerer's whole self-dedication, body and soul. The animal must be a male without blemish, offered willingly at the door of the tabernacle. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Laying a hand on the victim's head both devotes the animal to God and confesses sin, praying that the offerer's guilt and its punishment might be transferred to the victim, so that it may be an acceptable atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For a voluntary sacrifice the offerer, not the priest, kills the bullock, on the north side of the altar. The priests sprinkle the blood, because the blood, as the life, is the essential part of the sacrifice, and its sprinkling applies the atonement and makes the offerer acceptable. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Flaying the victim and cutting it into pieces is part of the same ceremony: once the skin is stripped off and the carcass cut up, the pieces are disposed on the altar in the way best fitted for their being consumed by the fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Aaron's sons, the priests, put fire on the altar and lay the wood in order, preparing the altar for the whole burnt offering to be consumed upon it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. The pieces, with the head and the fat, are laid in order on the wood over the fire. The fat named here is especially that about the kidneys, called suet. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Washing the inwards and legs in water symbolises the inward purity and the holy walk that become acceptable worshippers. The “sweet savour” expresses the offerer's piety, and is especially a sacrificial type of Christ. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. One who cannot afford a bullock may offer a ram or a he-goat from the flocks, with the same ceremonies observed in the act of offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. The flock offering follows the herd pattern: it is killed on the north side of the altar before the LORD, and Aaron's sons sprinkle its blood round about upon the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. As with the herd offering, the victim is cut into pieces and, with its head and fat, laid in order on the wood that is on the altar fire. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. The inwards and legs are washed with water and the whole is burnt on the altar, a burnt sacrifice of a sweet savour, just as in the herd offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. The offering of fowls is appointed for the poor. The dove is chosen for its gentle nature and cleanly habits, while fiercer or grosser fowls are rejected, and the birds are offered in pairs, turtledoves or young pigeons according to season. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Because the bird is so small there is no blood for waste, so the priest prepares it at the altar itself, wringing off its head, burning it on the altar, and wringing out its blood at the side of the altar. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The crop with its feathers is plucked away and cast beside the altar on the east side, by the place of the ashes, removing the refuse before the bird itself is burnt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The bird is cleft by its wings but not divided asunder, then burnt on the altar on the wood over the fire, a whole burnt sacrifice of a sweet savour unto the LORD. Throughout the varying scale of these offerings, the disposition of the offerer is what is looked to, not the costliness of the gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
