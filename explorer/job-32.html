The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Job 32 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 So these three men ceased to answer Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes.

2 Then was kindled the wrath of Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite, of the kindred of Ram: against Job was his wrath kindled, because he justified himself rather than God.

3 Also against his three friends was his wrath kindled, because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned Job.

4 Now Elihu had waited till Job had spoken, because they were elder than he.

5 When Elihu saw that there was no answer in the mouth of these three men, then his wrath was kindled.

6 And Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite answered and said, I am young, and ye are very old; wherefore I was afraid, and durst not shew you mine opinion.

7 I said, Days should speak, and multitude of years should teach wisdom.

8 But there is a spirit in man: and the inspiration of the Almighty giveth them understanding.

9 Great men are not always wise: neither do the aged understand judgment.

10 Therefore I said, Hearken to me; I also will shew mine opinion.

11 Behold, I waited for your words; I gave ear to your reasons, whilst ye searched out what to say.

12 Yea, I attended unto you, and, behold, there was none of you that convinced Job, or that answered his words:

13 Lest ye should say, We have found out wisdom: God thrusteth him down, not man.

14 Now he hath not directed his words against me: neither will I answer him with your speeches.

15 They were amazed, they answered no more: they left off speaking.

16 When I had waited, (for they spake not, but stood still, and answered no more;)

17 I said, I will answer also my part, I also will shew mine opinion.

18 For I am full of matter, the spirit within me constraineth me.

19 Behold, my belly is as wine which hath no vent; it is ready to burst like new bottles.

20 I will speak, that I may be refreshed: I will open my lips and answer.

21 Let me not, I pray you, accept any man’s person, neither let me give flattering titles unto man.

22 For I know not to give flattering titles; in so doing my maker would soon take me away.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

With the three friends silenced — they ceased to answer Job because he was righteous in his own eyes — a new voice enters: Elihu the Buzite, young where they are old. His wrath is kindled against Job because he justified himself rather than God, and against the friends because they found no answer yet condemned him. He waited in deference to age, he says, but there is a spirit in man, and the inspiration of the Almighty gives understanding, so he will speak and be refreshed. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. So these three men ceased to answer Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes. JFB notes the prose opening and the reason: they could not prove him unrighteous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. Then was kindled the wrath of Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite, of the kindred of Ram, against Job, because he justified himself rather than God. JFB explains Elihu as God is Jehovah, foreshadowing Christ as messenger between God and Job, Barachel as God blesses, Buzite from Buz son of Nahor, and Ram as Aram, with Job more eager to vindicate himself than God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. Also against his three friends was Elihu's wrath kindled, because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned Job. JFB notes that though silenced in argument, they still held their opinion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Now Elihu had waited till Job had spoken, because they were elder than he. JFB explains had spoken in Hebrew as in words, referring to his own long-ready reply, kept back in deference to the friends' seniority. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. When Elihu saw there was no answer in the mouth of these three men, then his wrath was kindled. The silence that ended the debate is what finally moves him to speak. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. And Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite answered and said, I am young, and you are very old, wherefore I was afraid, and dared not show you my opinion. JFB notes poetry begins with I am young, and was afraid from a Hebrew root meaning to crawl. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. Elihu said, days should speak, and multitude of years should teach wisdom. JFB explains days as the aged, who by years should teach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. But there is a spirit in man, and the inspiration of the Almighty gives them understanding. JFB notes Elihu claims inspiration as a divinely commissioned messenger, uncontradicted in chapter 42, and renders it as the spirit God puts in man and inspiration giving understanding, not mere years. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Great men are not always wise, neither do the aged understand judgment. JFB renders great as old, with greater and less for older and younger, and judgment as what is right. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. Therefore Elihu says, hearken to me, I also will show my opinion. JFB renders it as I say, with opinion as knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. Behold, Elihu waited for the friends' words, gave ear to their reasons, whilst they searched out what to say. JFB notes Elihu was therefore present from the first, with reasons as understandings, the meaning intended by words, waiting until they should discover a suitable reply. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Yea, Elihu attended unto them, and behold, there was none of them that convinced Job, or answered his words. The long listening ends in a verdict of failure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Lest the friends should say, we have found out wisdom, God thrusts him down, not man. JFB explains this ordering: lest they pride themselves on overcoming Job by wisdom, God alone can confute him, not man, and Elihu grounds his confutation not on sages' maxims but on special commission from God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. Now Job has not directed his words against Elihu, neither will Elihu answer him with the friends' speeches. JFB notes Elihu is unprejudiced, since Job did not address him, and the friends' speeches were influenced by irritation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. They were amazed, they answered no more, they left off speaking. JFB notes Elihu turns from the friends to Job, passing to the third person as in rebuke, with words taken from them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. When Elihu had waited, for they spoke not, but stood still and answered no more. The waiting is over; silence from the friends is complete. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. Elihu says, I will answer also my part, I also will show my opinion. JFB renders my part as for my part, with opinion as knowledge. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. For Elihu is full of matter, and the spirit within him constrains him. JFB contrasts his fullness of words with the friends who have not a word more, with spirit as in chapter 33 and the constraining spirit of prophecy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 19. Behold, Elihu's belly is as wine which has no vent, like new bottles ready to burst. JFB explains belly as bosom, from which Oriental speech seems to come, like new wine in fermentation without a vent, kept in new goatskin bottles, fitting the young Elihu against the old friends. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 20. Elihu will speak, that he may be refreshed, opening his lips and answering. JFB renders refreshed literally as that there may be air to me. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 21. Let Elihu not accept any man's person, neither give flattering titles unto man. JFB notes Elihu alludes to Job's complaint that the friends plead for God partially, accepting his person, and promises impartiality between God and Job, without flattery. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 22. For Elihu knows not to give flattering titles; in so doing his maker would soon take him away. JFB explains take away as punishment, the maker removing the flatterer. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

Where the speakers disagree

Elihu disagrees with both sides at once: with Job, because he justified himself rather than God, and with the three friends, because they found no answer yet condemned him. His claim is that age alone does not give wisdom — there is a spirit in man, and the inspiration of the Almighty gives understanding — so a younger man may speak where the older have fallen silent.
